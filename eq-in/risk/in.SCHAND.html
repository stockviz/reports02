Headwinds and Challenges

  • Middle East collections: Geo-political instability in the Middle East continues to hamper collections from the region. Management expects this to normalize going ahead.
  • Paper costs and supply: Imported paper prices have risen, with the dollar price moving from around Rs85 last year to around Rs95. Management cited dollar movement, logistics costs, and Middle East tensions as drivers. Paper prices are expected to firm up in the next quarter or so. Shipping timelines for imported paper are elongated.
  • Working capital pressure: The decision to advance paper purchases led to higher raw material and finished goods inventory versus previous years. Combined with Middle East collection issues, this led to slightly higher net working capital days than last year. Management noted that pushing K-12 growth beyond a certain level would bring working capital inefficiency.
  • Seasonality: The business sees higher book sales in January, February, and March because academic sessions start in April. Revenue is generally lower in other quarters. These trends are expected to continue.
  • Tax-related PAT impact: Higher tax expenses due to a one-time adjustment of the tax rate to a lower rate led to increased PAT losses in Q1FY27 versus last year. The company intends to opt for the lower tax regime under Section 115BAA from FY2026-27, with deferred tax recalculated accordingly.
  • Goodwill on balance sheet: A large amount of goodwill sits on the balance sheet, which depresses return ratios. Management noted the only way to address it is through the P&L, which would mean reporting losses if taken over one year. Impairment would likely be considered only in a year with an exceptional income event.
  • CPD Singapore scaling timeline: The acquisition is at a very early stage. Management said it will take at least 2–3 years to shape up, and 1–2 academic cycles for the product to be received by target customers. The brand is unknown in India and the Middle East.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • School Education segment: Q1FY27 was a strong quarter for the School Education segment. Refreshed curriculum businesses including Mylestone, My Zen, SmartK, and Solid Steps got multiple repeat adoptions from across the country.
  • New partnerships: Partnerships with Allied, Jump Maths, Penguin, and Speedlabs strengthened the product offering. Mylestone curriculum was adopted by 650+ schools and the Zen curriculum by over 500 schools. Combined revenues from these were around Rs55–60 crores for the full year last year, on an uptrend.
  • Content licensing: Rs91 million revenues from content licensing during the quarter. Management is diversifying the dataset base to include non-academic data licensing. As of the call, the company was sitting on Rs14 crores of content licensing revenues, of which Q1 contributed Rs9 crore. Management hopes to cross Rs40 crores, possibly by a good margin, though contracts are not yet committed.
  • NCERT new syllabus: Management looks forward to NCERT releasing the remaining books on the new syllabus over the course of the year and expects full adoption of the new syllabus books in FY27.
  • Regional boards: The new syllabus is expected in West Bengal, which should give a good leg up for two years from next year onwards. Last year’s regional board growth (Chhaya Prakashani) was lumpy because West Bengal elections in April shifted some revenue into Q4.
  • International board sales: CPD Singapore is expected to contribute to K-12 international board sales. The company has started marketing in India, hired a team, and is targeting over 1,000 international schools in India. Management expects it could cross SGD 1 million (approx Rs7.5 crore) by the end of the year. Singapore and Malaysia sales continue as normal.
  • IIT Madras partnership: Madhubun Educational Books partnered with IIT Madras Pravartak Technologies Foundation and BodhBridge Education to offer future-ready skills programs for students from Class 7 to Class 12 and educators across India. Most courses are online with live interactive sessions. Management said revenue from this is not much; it is more about branding and school connect.
  • Printing and binding facility: A new state-of-the-art printing and binding facility is in process. Half will be completed this year, with partial binding and printing starting from next week, operations beginning around September–October, and the rest next year. Management expects improved productivity, quality, and quantity efficiency, and believes the infrastructure can meet requirements for 10–15 years.
  • M&A: The company is evaluating M&A opportunities that fill portfolio gaps. Conversations are ongoing in the test prep space and the school space, with total investment size expected around Rs40–50 crores at maximum.
  • Buyback consideration: Management said it is actively considering a market buyback and expects to decide by October, once acquisition opportunities fructify over the next two months.

Key Risks

  • Continued Middle East instability affecting collections and potentially sales.
  • Paper price increases beyond the 10–12% factored into guidance; management noted that if prices go up 40%, it would be a different case.
  • Only 20–25% of annual paper requirements had been purchased, about 3–4 months earlier than usual. The company cannot buy all requirements due to space constraints and cash flow considerations.
  • Working capital inefficiency from pushing K-12 growth beyond a certain level.
  • Goodwill impairment risk, though management sees intrinsic value in the subsidiaries and does not believe impairment is required at this point.
  • CPD Singapore brand-building risk in India and the Middle East, with a 2–3 year timeline before meaningful contribution.
  • Content licensing revenue is not committed until contracts are signed.
  • Seasonality concentrating revenue in Q4.

Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Revenue growth guidance: Management reiterated a target to grow operating revenues by 10%–15% for FY27. Observed: Q1FY27 consolidated revenues were Rs1,145 million, up 12% YOY, within the guided range.
  • EBITDA margin guidance: Management gave an EBITDA margin band of 17%–19% for FY27, factoring in a 10%–12% paper price increase. Observed: Q1FY27 EBITDA loss of Rs97 million, with PAT loss of Rs187 million. Management noted Q1 marketing expenses related to Q2 activities and higher tax expenses from a one-time adjustment to a lower tax rate increased PAT losses versus last year.
  • Content licensing guidance: Management targets revenues in excess of Rs400 million for FY27 and growing clients from 5 to 10. Observed: Rs91 million in Q1FY27, with Rs14 crores total as of the call. Management hopes to cross Rs40 crores, possibly by a good margin, but cannot commit until contracts are in.
  • Cash flow and balance sheet: Management reiterated focus on margins, working capital metrics, and cash flows. Observed: The company remained cash surplus at the end of the quarter with the highest net cash balance at the end of Q1 in its history at Rs1,182 million (vs. Q4FY26: Rs1,160 million) after distributing a dividend of Rs141 million. Receivable days were stable. Net working capital days were slightly higher than last year due to advanced paper purchases and Middle East collection issues.
  • M&A guidance: Management said it is evaluating M&A opportunities. Observed: A couple of conversations have taken place, one in test prep and one in school space, not moved beyond a certain point, with total investment size expected around Rs40–50 crores at maximum.
  • Steady-state growth: Management indicated steady-state growth of 10%–12% for the core business, driven by 3%–4% student increase, 4%–5% volume growth, and 4%–5% pricing growth, with acquisitions potentially adding more.
  • Buyback: Management said it is actively considering a market buyback and expects to decide by October.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative stayed persistently bullish: both the first (May-2023) and last (Aug-2025) reports carry Buy calls and repeatedly lean on curriculum/NCF-driven volume growth, stable/benign paper prices, and a stronger balance sheet/working capital. The emphasis shifted from NEP/NCF rollout, dividend restoration, cash-flow generation, and a Mylestone turnaround in 2023 to AI content licensing, new NCERT syllabus books, and a new press facility in 2025, while still retaining the FY26 revenue/EBITDA guidance. Despite the sustained optimism, every rated call in the history ended in a DISAPPOINTMENT, and the last report itself acknowledged revenue decline, EBITDA loss, and a gross-margin fall.

Fears that came true

  • The feared NCF/curriculum roll-out delay/slowdown materialized as a weak volume trajectory: by Q1FY26 total revenue was down 7.3% YoY despite the new NCERT syllabus cycle for grades 4,5,7 and 8.
  • The FY23 top-line miss foreshadowed persistent revenue/profit shortfalls: Q1FY26 revenue fell to Rs1,026mn with an EBITDA loss of Rs91mn and a reported loss of Rs141mn.

Optimism that failed

  • NEP/NCF implementation driving strong FY24-FY26 volume growth failed to show up in results: Q1FY26 total revenue declined 7.3% YoY.
  • The expected margin expansion from benign paper prices and 6-8% price hikes did not materialize: Q1FY26 gross margin fell to 67.1% from 72.5% YoY.
  • The projected strong cash-flow generation did not prevent persistent shareholder disappointment: actual returns were negative on every call, including -26.5% on the last Buy.
