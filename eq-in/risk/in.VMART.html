V-Mart Retail: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Gross margin pressure. In Q1 FY’27, gross profit grew 20% year-on-year to ₹3,752 Mn, lagging revenue growth of 23%. Gross margin declined to 34.5% from 35.3% in Q1 FY’26.

Cost base expanding with the network. Employee expenses rose to ₹1,112 Mn from ₹950 Mn, and other expenses (post-Ind AS 116) rose to ₹1,033 Mn from ₹912 Mn. Manpower, advertisement, power and fuel, and other expense lines all moved in line with store expansion, indicating that operating costs continue to scale with the footprint.

Dilution of pre-Ind AS EBITDA margin versus post-Ind AS optics. On a pre-Ind AS 116 basis, EBITDA was ₹832 Mn (7.6% of revenue) versus ₹1,606 Mn (14.8%) post-Ind AS 116, reflecting the significant lease-related adjustments embedded in reported profitability.

Free cash flow decline. Net free cash flow (pre-Ind AS 116) fell to ₹760 Mn in Q1 FY’27 from ₹1,097 Mn in Q1 FY’26, despite higher operating profit, as net capex rose to ₹389 Mn from ₹300 Mn and working capital changes contributed less (₹420 Mn versus ₹829 Mn).

Cash position and borrowings movement. Cash and cash equivalents declined to ₹143 Mn as at June 30, 2026 from ₹195 Mn as at March 31, 2026. Borrowings fell sharply to ₹12 Mn from ₹1,000 Mn, with financing activities absorbing ₹1,788 Mn during the quarter.

LimeRoad losses. The LimeRoad marketplace continued to report losses of ₹28 Mn in Q1 FY’27, though these were down 39% year-on-year.

Governance-related dissent. At the 24th AGM held July 30, 2026, the ordinary resolution appointing Mr. Lalit Agarwal as a Director liable to retire by rotation passed with 96.0014% of valid votes in favour and 3.9986% against — the highest dissent among the resolutions tabled. The financial statements adoption and dividend declaration resolutions each received 99.9999% and 99.9931% support respectively.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Broad-based revenue and profit growth. Q1 FY’27 revenue from operations rose 23% year-on-year to ₹10,888 Mn. EBITDA grew 27% to ₹1,606 Mn (14.8% of revenue). Pre-Ind AS PAT rose 90% to ₹410 Mn (3.8% of revenue), and post-Ind AS PAT rose 41% to ₹472 Mn (4.3% of revenue).

Customer traction. Footfall grew 39% and memo (transaction volume) grew 18%. Same-store sales growth was 9% overall, with V-Mart at 8% and Unlimited at 13%.

Tier 4 momentum. Tier 4 markets delivered the highest sales per square foot growth at +15% year-on-year, which the company describes as reinforcing the potential of these markets.

Inventory efficiency. Inventory days of sales improved from 93 to 86 days, a reduction of 8% year-on-year, attributed to improved merchandise assortment and supply chain efficiencies.

Network expansion. The company operates 591 total stores (490 V-Mart, 101 Unlimited) across 335 cities and 5.1 million sq. ft. In Q1 FY’27 it opened 15 stores and closed 1, a net addition of 14. The store count rose from 510 in Q1 FY’26 to 591 in Q1 FY’27. New stores were opened in Karnataka, Gujarat, and Uttar Pradesh. Uttar Pradesh remains the largest state by store count at 183, followed by Bihar at 82.

LimeRoad marketplace. Net Merchandise Value rose 18% year-on-year while losses shrank 39% to ₹28 Mn. LimeRoad commission income was ₹104 Mn in Q1 FY’27 versus ₹81 Mn in Q1 FY’26.

Private labels. Private labels contribute over 66% of the apparel business.

Supply chain infrastructure. The company operates two warehouses — Palwal (near Delhi, 6 lakh sq. ft. owned) and Bengaluru — with 600+ vendors, handling over 190 Mn pieces per year and bi-weekly fashion drops across 250+ routes and 591 stores.

Debt reduction. Borrowings declined from ₹1,000 Mn as at March 31, 2026 to ₹12 Mn as at June 30, 2026, which the company characterizes as debt-free expansion.

ESG recognition. Crisil ESG Ratings & Analytics assigned V-Mart a ‘Crisil ESG 62’ (Strong Category) rating based on FY 2025-26 data, in a report received by the company on August 12, 2026. The company noted it did not engage Crisil for this rating; the report was prepared independently based on public domain data and issued voluntarily.

Awards and recognition. These include a Silver Shield at the ICAI Awards for Excellence in Financial Reporting in 2026, Retail Brand of the Year 2024 by Great India Retail Awards, the 24th ICSI National Awards for Excellence in Corporate Governance in 2025, a Golden Peacock Award for excellence in corporate governance by the Institute of Directors in 2022, a global rank of 19th best Integrated Annual Report in 2026, and ‘India’s Best Workplaces in Retail 2022, 2021 & 2020’ by Great Places to Work.

3. Key Risks

Margin sensitivity. The decline in gross margin to 34.5% from 35.3%, alongside rising employee and other expenses, indicates that profitability remains sensitive to input costs and operating cost discipline as the network scales.

Working capital and cash generation. The reduction in working capital contribution (₹420 Mn versus ₹829 Mn) and lower free cash flow despite higher operating profit point to variability in cash conversion.

Lease and Ind AS 116 exposure. Lease liabilities stood at ₹6,048 Mn (non-current) and ₹2,539 Mn (current) as at June 30, 2026, making reported profitability and EBITDA sensitive to lease accounting treatment and rental obligations.

LimeRoad drag. The marketplace continues to report losses, albeit reduced, and remains a drag on consolidated profitability.

Execution risk in expansion. The company added 14 net stores in Q1 FY’27 and operates across 335 cities; continued expansion requires execution across supply chain, manpower, and store-level productivity.

Governance dissent. The 3.9986% votes against the director appointment resolution at the 24th AGM represents a notable minority dissent that investors may monitor.

Forward-looking statement risk. The company’s own disclaimer notes that certain statements are forward-looking and involve risks and uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ materially, including risks related to its fashion, FMCG, and online marketplace businesses.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance on growth. Management emphasized a commitment to disciplined, sustainable, and profitable growth, continuing to scale pan-India, and maintaining a sharp focus on operational excellence and customer satisfaction.

Observed performance against this guidance. Q1 FY’27 results show revenue growth of 23%, EBITDA growth of 27%, and post-Ind AS PAT growth of 41%, with pre-Ind AS PAT growth of 90%. The company added 14 net stores in the quarter and improved inventory days from 93 to 86. LimeRoad losses narrowed 39% year-on-year. These outcomes are consistent with the stated emphasis on profitable growth and operational excellence.

Guidance on footprint. The company has added 100 stores over the last one year, opened 24 new stores year-to-date in FY27, spans 338+ cities across 29 states and Union Territories, and operates over 5 million+ sq. ft. of total retail space. It launched its 600th store in Patna, Bihar, maintains an average store size of 8,000 sq. ft., and was founded in 2002. The Q1 FY’27 presentation reports 591 stores across 335 cities and 5.1 million sq. ft. as at June 30, 2026, with 15 stores opened and 1 closed during the quarter.

Leadership and governance actions. The Board appointed Mr. Suraj Rathor as Head of Finance and Senior Management Personnel with effect from July 24, 2026, based on the recommendation of the Nomination and Remuneration Committee. Mr. Rathor joined V-Mart in 2023 as Head of FP&A and has over 15 years of experience across Retail, F&B, and FMCG, including business controllership roles at Devyani International and an eight-year stint at KPMG.

Investor engagement. The company scheduled a one-on-one investor call with SBI MF on August 12, 2026, in virtual mode from 17:00 to 18:00 IST, and circulated its Q1 FY’27 investor presentation on July 24, 2026.

AGM outcomes. The 24th AGM was held on July 30, 2026 at 11:00 AM IST through video conferencing. The financial statements for FY 2025-26, the appointment of Mr. Lalit Agarwal as a rotating director, and the declaration of final dividend for FY 2025-26 were all passed with the requisite majority. Remote e-voting ran from July 26 to July 29, 2026, with a cut-off date of July 22, 2026.

Broker Narrative

Both reports centered store expansion, Unlimited brand turnaround, and LimeRoad integration as structural growth levers, while raw material inflation and competitive intensity persisted as recurring risks. The early narrative fixated on acute margin compression, customer conversion decline, and Unlimited underperformance as immediate headwinds, whereas the latest report reframed risks as near-term supply chain disruptions and geopolitical input cost pressures against a backdrop of operating leverage and margin recovery. The Unlimited story flipped from underperformance to outperformance over the period, and LimeRoad evolved from a cash-burn liability to a gradually improving asset.

Fears that came true

  • Raw material cost inflation, flagged as a key downside risk in the first report, materialized in 4QFY26 with yarn costs up 8–9% and garment costs up 3–4%, coinciding with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes such as Anand Rathi’s predicted +52.9% actual -21.2% and predicted +50.4% actual -11.0% in 2025.
  • Gross margin contraction, initially 300bps YoY in 2023, further compressed by ~95bp YoY in 4QFY26 due to higher inventory provisioning, aligning with periods of negative actual returns like -25.3% in Oct 2024 and -3.2% in Jun 2025.
  • LimeRoad’s EBITDA loss of Rs215mn caused prolonged cash burn and near-term profitability pressure that persisted for years before losses were reduced ~56% YoY, correlating with multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes across 2024–2025.

Optimism that failed

  • The optimism that LimeRoad integration would track well and quickly strengthen omni-channel capabilities failed in the near term, as the platform continued to burn cash for years before losses were meaningfully reduced ~56% YoY by 2026.
  • Customer footfalls increased 40% YoY to 11mn, but the simultaneous 5% YoY decline in customer conversion rate to 58% for four consecutive quarters meant footfall growth did not translate into sustained revenue outperformance, undermining the optimism around demand recovery.
  • The expectation that new Unlimited stores would quickly record V-Mart equivalent economics was delayed, as 20% of Unlimited stores still carried negative EBITDA at the time of the first report, contributing to underperformance during periods like the -26.5% actual return in Oct 2024.
