Headwinds and Challenges

  • Global economic fluctuations, supply chain disruptions, failures of global banks, inflation, geopolitical conflicts, unforeseen market shifts, hikes in interest rates, rising operating costs, and a challenging business environment are cited as headwinds.
  • Risks and uncertainties span research and development, competitive developments, regulatory actions, the extent and duration of COVID-19 pandemic effects, litigation and investigations, business development transactions, economic conditions, and changes in laws and regulations.
  • Management describes the present scenario as making it extremely difficult to estimate profits in measurable terms, even while it remains focused on productivity improvement.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Rising demand for technologically advanced, environment-friendly catalysts; a push for greener chemistry in organic synthesis; stricter emission norms; the China+1 policy and the trust deficit between China and the US; a shift toward sustainable chemistry and green products; and declining Chinese competitiveness are cited as tailwinds.
  • The company is pursuing expansion, diversification, cost optimization, productivity improvement, and new avenues for sustainable growth and value creation.
  • On 17 July 2026, the Board approved a capacity addition at a new greenfield unit at Dahej–III, Dahej Industrial Estate, Bharuch, Gujarat: installation of aggregate reactor capacity of 344 KL, to be added over approximately 21 months, at an investment of approximately ₹200 crore, financed through a combination of internal accruals and debt. The rationale is the projected growth of the chemical industry and anticipated increase in demand for specialty chemicals.
  • The company is an integrated specialty chemical company established in 1996, with plants at Ankleshwar and Dahej SEZ, offering a combined reactor capacity of 791 KL and 39 assembly lines as of 31 March 2026. It describes itself as a leading global producer of Phase Transfer Catalysts (PTC), the second largest global manufacturer of Structure Directing Agents (SDA) for zeolites and the largest in India, the largest Indian producer of electrolyte salts for super capacitor batteries, and the largest Indian producer of Glymes (third largest in the world).

Key Risks

  • The headwinds listed above—macroeconomic, supply chain, banking-sector, inflationary, geopolitical, interest-rate, and cost-related—represent the principal external risks highlighted.
  • R&D, competitive developments, regulatory actions, pandemic duration and effects, litigation and investigations, business development transactions, and legal/regulatory changes are also flagged as risks and uncertainties.
  • The Dahej–III greenfield expansion carries execution considerations: it is a new facility (no existing capacity or utilization applies), requires approximately 21 months to complete, and is to be funded partly by debt.
  • The Board approved an increase in borrowing limits from ₹300 crore to ₹1,000 crore under Section 180(1)(c) of the Companies Act, 2013, and the creation of charges on assets up to ₹1,000 crore, both subject to member approval at the ensuing Annual General Meeting.

Broker Narrative

The narrative flipped from a cautious Sell in 2023, citing pricing/margin pressure, delayed PASC commercialization, and a sharp electrolyte-salts decline, to a Buy by 2026 that highlights commercial validation in PASC, semiconductor chemicals, and Euro-7 supply. Persistent themes were electrolyte-salts vulnerability and external/China demand, while the big change was PASC moving from a 12-15 month delay to a source of repeat orders and a qualified semiconductor molecule. The earlier focus on SDA recovering to FY22 levels faded, replaced by new catalysts like PTC adoption and geographic expansion.

Fears that came true

  • The electrolyte-salts decline flagged in the first report persisted: the last report still cites production disruptions in that segment, and the prior -54% YoY drop correlated with negative returns around the 2023-09 and 2024-05 Buy disappointments.
  • The external demand risk for SDA materialized as China remained subdued due to EV penetration in passenger vehicles, contributing to disappointment in multiple Buy/Hold calls (e.g., 2023-09, 2024-05, 2025-04).
  • The delayed PASC commercialization pushed contribution to FY25 as feared, and the 12-15 month scaling time likely contributed to the 2023-09 (-35%) and 2024-05 (-43%) actual returns.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected SDA recovery to FY22 levels in FY24 did not materialize as a durable growth driver; by the last report external demand in China was still subdued and no SDA recovery tailwind was cited.
