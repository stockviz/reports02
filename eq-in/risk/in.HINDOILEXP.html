HINDOILEXP — Q1 FY27 Developments, Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

B-80 (offshore flagship): Production during the quarter was impacted by a higher water cut from one producing well, resulting in lower output. The water cut continued to increase, though the wells continued to produce. The company reconfigured the compressor trains on the MOPU so they can operate at lower suction pressure, allowing wells to flow as flowing pressures decline; this was in test mode. A workover of two existing wells (D1 and D2) was planned, with the rig award expected in August and mobilisation by October 2026.

HPCL crude offtake dispute: The prior quarter was impacted by the reversal of the HPCL sale of INR 259 crore. The company agreed with HPCL to reverse the sale and resell the crude to third parties. Offtake by road was slower than expected. Management indicated the company would incur a loss on the remaining crude, with roughly 15% sold at the time of the call and a loss in the range of INR 4–6 crore incurred so far; the eventual loss was put at around 7%–10%, explicitly not given as guidance. The full crude inventory at HPCL’s Chembur refinery was hoped to be cleared by end-October/early-November 2026. A conciliation process was underway, with the High Court Chief Justice having appointed a conciliator.

PY-1 (East Coast): The platform and three wells suffered serious production loss. Drilling of two new wells, originally planned for Q4 of the following year, became contingent on the ability of gas buyers IOCL/GAIL to lift gas, because wells drilled and left shut could water out again. Management stated that, as far as possible, a take-or-pay agreement with GAIL or IOCL would be secured before spudding. Historically, the wells had come on at 50 mmscfd but were shut due to lack of buyers, and water overwhelmed the gas zones in the basement reservoir.

Dirok (Assam): The field was producing only 50%–70% of the capability of the existing well stock due to lack of an evacuation route. Degraded sections of the DNPL line had reduced its capacity, and tie-back required hot tapping. Earlier, tie-back depended on an NRL shutdown, which had been delayed. Floods in Assam caused disruption. Management noted that the Assam connectivity had been described as “a few months away” for around two years, and an investor questioned the confidence in the December timeline.

Kharsang (Assam): If gas is found, there is no buyer and no pipeline. Virgin gas was found in two wells, which delays monetisation of reserves. The pipeline route goes through a forest area, so regulatory clearances may be an issue.

Cambay assets: New belt technology at Balol was in the trial phase. Palej required additional new wells. A new exploration block near Palej was in the final stages of approval.

Tamil Nadu drilling protest: There were protests around drilling two wells. Management stated the fields are approved by the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, that the central government is the final arbiter on drilling approval, and that the state government is kept informed. The matter was taken up with the state government.

Tax orders: The company received orders from the State Tax Officer (Legal & Adjudication), Chengalpattu Intelligence Division, imposing penalties of Rs. 317,358 (order received July 15, 2026, pertaining to 2022-23) and Rs. 268,956 (order received July 13, 2026, pertaining to 2020-21), along with tax demand and applicable interest, relating to non-payment of IGST under Reverse Charge Mechanism on legal services received outside India. The company stated the financial implications are not material and do not affect its financial position or operations, and that it is filing revised returns.

Financing: Delivering the B-80 programme requires debt. The company was in the market to raise funds from investors and banks. Management noted that if funds are not obtained, there could be a challenge, though part would be funded from own accruals. The board approved an increase in borrowing limits to not exceeding Rs. 1,000 crores (from Rs. 750 crores) under Section 180(1)(c), and an increase in the Section 186 threshold for loans/guarantees/securities/investments to not exceeding Rs. 300 crores, both subject to shareholder approval at the AGM.

Investor credibility concern: An investor noted that production had been guided to increase for three years but had drastically reduced from 2024 levels, and questioned confidence in the 11,000 bopd target. Management attributed the delay largely to the stuck HPCL invoice (INR 260 crore plus interest, roughly INR 300 crore), which otherwise would have allowed workovers in November of the prior year.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Sector backdrop: Continued focus on energy security, domestic hydrocarbon production and infrastructure development, with long-term fundamentals supported by the country’s demand growth of 7%–8%.

Kharsang: The nine-well programme was successfully completed and production doubled from the prior year. Production increased from approximately 12,300 barrels of oil to 17,400 BOE. Five or six workovers of the 23 additional non-producing wells were completed, with incremental production increases. The second phase of nine wells was being prepared, with the rig identified, award imminent, and tubulars being ordered. Oil is being sold to Digboi Refinery and various buyers, mostly IOCL.

Dirok: Described as a highly prosperous field with a production header pressure of 3,000 PSI from six wells. PNGRB made the DNPL line a common carrier (previously a captive of NRL/Oil India, operated by AGCL). Replacement sections were laid, and hot tapping was planned to restore capacity to 2.5 mmscmd from the degraded 1–1.5 mmscmd. Management expected completion by December 2026. Once the pipeline is a common carrier, NRL can also act as a re-seller, and gas can be sold on the exchange within about three days without a firm buyer. Locked-in volumes of 0.5–0.7 mmscmd (gross) could be ramped up, and management expected to be able to sell all well-stock gas once capacity is restored. A revised FDP approved for Dirok secures the block till 2035, with the revised PSC extension likely signed by August/September 2026 and the North Dirok-1 exploration well likely spud in Q4 FY27.

B-80: The compressor reconfiguration was expected to bring production to 100% by end of the month. The workover campaign (two wells, 10–20 days each) was targeted for Q3 FY27, followed by three new wells (30–40 days each) in Q4 FY27, with a jacket and production deck installation, targeting three wells on production by June of the following year. Expected workover output was 500–800 barrels per well and 3–5 mmscfd. New wells were expected at 1,000–1,500 barrels per well based on reservoir simulation. 2P reserves were stated at 26 million, of which 1.5–2 million had been produced. Estimated FY27 capex for B-80 was USD 45 million. Crude stored on the FSO (around 120,000–130,000 barrels) was planned for sale in October using smaller tankers to avoid demurrage.

B-15: Newly awarded block with reserves of 16 MMBOE, subject to reappraisal after processing and the first well. FDP under preparation, evaluating three or four concepts, including connecting to ONGC’s existing platform to reduce capital costs. Drilling planned in FY28.

PY-1: A rig-less intervention contract was awarded to increase production from existing wells in the short term. PetroVietnam analysis indicated 15–20 mmscfd could be achieved from two new wells, to be drilled only after a firm gas sales agreement. A booster compressor installation was targeted for October 2026.

Cambay (Asjol, Balol, Palej): New belt technology implemented at Balol (trial phase). Palej facility debottlenecked using thermionic heaters, with an expected 20%–30% production increase going forward, after a 5%–6% increase already seen in the last quarter. Sucker rod pumps ordered. A new exploration block near Palej was in final approval stages, with new exploratory wells planned. Palej full production assessment was targeted at ~600 bopd by FY27-28.

Kharsang gas monetisation: A 24 km pipeline to tie into Oil India’s existing pipeline at Bordumsa (which connects to NRL and the national gas grid) was planned. The route survey tender was to close the day after the call. Management estimated 6–8 months for right of way and 14–18 months total to lay the pipeline, with completion possibly by December of the following year.

Capital programme: The company is embarking on a comprehensive near-to-medium-term upstream capital programme across B-80, B-15, PY-1, Dirok, and North Dirok & Block-19, directed at scaling production, enhancing recovery, and expanding the reserve base.

3. Key Risks

  • Reservoir and subsurface uncertainty: Management stated that in offshore reservoirs there are always uncertainties, and the 11,000 bopd figure could be 8,900 or 13,000. New B-80 wells could target 1,000 barrels but deliver 700 or 1,400. Wells are about 4 km below the main mud line.
  • Offshore operational risk: Tubing can get stuck or fish can be left in wells, causing delays. The B-80 campaign was described as a single campaign with workovers followed by three wells, extending toward end-March/early-April, followed by jacket and production deck installation.
  • Funding risk: The B-80 programme depends on debt raising. Management acknowledged a challenge if funds are not obtained.
  • Evacuation and offtake risk: Dirok depends on the DNPL hot tap and the ~100–200 metre NRL interconnection; PY-1 new wells depend on a firm gas sales agreement; Kharsang gas depends on a new pipeline and forest clearances.
  • Regulatory and local risk: Forest clearances for the Kharsang pipeline; protests in Tamil Nadu over drilling two wells.
  • Commodity price exposure: The loss on the HPCL crude resale depends on Brent price; the company has sold roughly 15% and expects a loss of around 7%–10% on the remainder.
  • Litigation/conciliation risk: The HPCL matter is in conciliation, with the High Court Chief Justice having appointed a conciliator.
  • Tax orders: Two IGST reverse-charge penalty orders, stated as not material.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance provided: - B-80 workover of two subsea wells targeted in Q3 FY27, followed by drilling three new wells in Q4 FY27; three wells on production by June of the following year; two worked-over wells on production by November–December 2026. - B-80 crude fully sold off by end-October/early-November 2026; FSO crude (~120,000–130,000 barrels) to be sold in October. - Dirok: hot taps and connectivity targeted by December 2026; revised PSC extension likely signed by August/September 2026; North Dirok-1 exploration well likely spud in Q4 FY27. - PY-1: booster compressor installation targeted October 2026; two new wells contingent on a firm gas sales agreement. - Kharsang: second phase of nine wells with rig award imminent; pipeline route survey tender closing, with 14–18 months to lay the pipeline. - Palej: full production assessment targeted at ~600 bopd by FY27-28; 20%–30% production increase expected going forward. - B-15: drilling in FY28. - Cash flows expected to be sufficient to fund B-15 growth by November–December 2027; B-80 to be funded by debt. - Borrowing limits to increase to Rs. 1,000 crores and Section 186 threshold to Rs. 300 crores, subject to AGM approval; actual borrowings to be drawn progressively based on financial discipline and project needs.

Observed performance versus guidance: - The 11,000 bopd production target was questioned by an investor given three years of guided increases that had not materialised and production that had drastically reduced from 2024 levels. Management attributed the shortfall largely to the HPCL invoice blockage (INR 260 crore plus interest, roughly INR 300 crore), which it said otherwise would have allowed workovers in November of the prior year. - The Assam connectivity timeline had been described as “a few months away” for around two years, and an investor explicitly questioned the basis for confidence in the December timeline. Management cited PNGRB’s common-carrier designation, laid replacement sections, the hot-tap solution avoiding an NRL shutdown, and stakeholder pressure from energy security, while noting floods had caused disruption and hot taps have a 16–20 week lead time. - B-80 workovers had been previously discussed (chemical water shut-off attempts) with mixed success; management stated confidence was higher now because a rig would allow physical intervention, re-perforation, and zone changes in subsea-completed wells. - Kharsang delivered observed production growth (12,300 to 17,400 BOE) and doubled production from the prior year, consistent with the completed nine-well programme. - Q1 FY27 standalone revenue from operations was INR 117.5 crores (versus negative INR 194 crores in the prior quarter), net revenue INR 107.6 crores after profit petroleum and revenue share of INR 9.83 crores; consolidated revenue INR 124 crores, net consolidated revenue INR 114.17 crores. Standalone PBT before exceptional items was INR 12.54 crores (versus INR 30.4 crores prior quarter); consolidated PBT before exceptional items was INR 6.5 crores (versus INR 9.01 crores). Other income was INR 19.37 crores standalone and INR 20 crores consolidated, including release of a long-due escrow balance of approximately INR 8 crores, an insurance claim of INR 2 crores, and topping up of the Adbhoot acquisition of approximately INR 2.3 crores. Standalone total expenses were INR 114 crores (depletion INR 51 crores, royalty and statutory dues INR 13 crores, stock adjustments INR 41 crores); consolidated total expenses were INR 128 crores (depletion INR 58 crores, royalty/cess/statutory dues INR 15 crores, stock adjustments INR 41 crores). Consolidated statements include the 10% GeoEnpro sales from Kharsang. - Average crude and condensate realisation improved to about $95.5/barrel from about $70.8/barrel prior quarter (standalone); average gas realisation improved to $12/MMBtu from $9.8/MMBtu. B-80 gas realisation was approximately $15–16/MMBtu (average $16.5/MMBtu), Dirok $12.5/MMBtu. - Gearing was stated at 0.04, with a INR 20 crores bank loan on the books. - The company committed to disclosing asset-wise (offshore and onshore) realisations in the next presentation, following an investor request.
