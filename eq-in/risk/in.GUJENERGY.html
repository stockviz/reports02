Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical disruption and supply-side constraints: The quarter saw significant disruption in global energy markets from geopolitical developments and supply-side constraints. Management noted that the timeline for reasonably priced gas keeps shifting because of successive shocks (Russia-Ukraine, then the Middle East), pushing the expected arrival of a new wave of LNG supply from 2026-27 to later.
  • Middle East conflict and LPG supply: Constrained LPG supplies caused by the conflict in the Middle East prompted government policy measures to boost domestic PNG connections.
  • Alternate fuel competition in Morbi: Propane availability was adversely impacted during the quarter, but post-July it gradually improved as buyers sourced from non-Middle East countries such as the USA and Venezuela. With spot prices near $20, gas prices sit at a premium to propane (Morbi gas around Rs 78/scm versus propane around Rs 65/scm), making it difficult to regain Morbi volume. Current Morbi run rate is about 3 mmscmd of gas, with roughly 5.3-5.4 mmscmd equivalent delivered as propane. Management expects to sustain about 3 mmscmd this quarter, with a floor of about 1.8-2 mmscmd from smaller units lacking propane infrastructure or space.
  • Third-party gas trading volume decline: External trading volume fell to about 3.3 mmscmd from close to 5 mmscmd earlier, attributed to volumes cut off from the Middle East, high prices, and absent power volumes.
  • Non-Morbi industrial sluggishness: Industrial sales outside Morbi remained around 2-2.2 mmscmd. Management cited infrastructure still under development (Ahmedabad rural, Thane, Dahej, Kutch, DNH) alongside pricing, with competition from steamhouse, briquettes, and solid fuels.
  • Low gas-based power utilisation: PLFs were about 6% for the 51 MW GSEG plant and about 1% for Pipavav, with utilisation largely limited to summer peak demand.
  • CGD margin below guidance: CGD EBITDA margin was Rs 5.18/scm in Q1, below the Rs 5.5-6.5/scm guidance, attributed to a larger volume base.
  • GTL listing delay: The GSPL Transmission Limited listing, expected by end-July/mid-August, is now expected in early September because it requires an exemption for listing without a public issue, involving coordination with BSE, NSE, and SEBI.
  • Tax refund timing: Refunds of about INR 900-odd crores tied to merger unabsorbed losses require revised returns for FY24-25 and FY25-26 and are expected to take at least a year.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Post-merger integrated model: The quarter included completion of listing and trading of additional equity shares allotted under the scheme. GEL is positioned as an integrated energy company across gas trading, CGD, exploration and production, and renewable energy.
  • Gas trading profitability: EBT rose to INR 726 crores from INR 237 crores a year earlier, a 206% increase, with 10 LNG cargoes sourced during the quarter despite the disruption.
  • CNG momentum: CNG volume reached a new benchmark of 3.76 mmscmd versus 3.33 mmscmd, up 13%, with Gujarat up 12% and areas outside Gujarat up 19%. Infrastructure expanded to 844 stations, with 6 added and 9 upgraded in the quarter. The company plans more than 75 new CNG stations and about 70 upgrades this financial year. CNG is cited as roughly 46% cheaper than petrol and 25% cheaper than diesel.
  • Domestic PNG drive: About 59,000 new domestic PNG customers were added in the quarter, taking the base above 24.77 lakh households. From January to June 2026, GEL connected about 91,000 domestic and more than 1,000 commercial PNG connections under the government-backed PNG drive, supported by digital and print campaigns, additional contractors, and outreach to hospitals and community kitchens.
  • Industrial volumes: Industrial sales rose to 7.17 mmscmd from 4.71 mmscmd, up 64%. Morbi averaged 5.67 mmscmd and non-Morbi 2.04 mmscmd; Morbi rose 181% versus Q4 FY26. Morbi gas volumes rose from about 0.4 mmscmd in April 2026 to around 8 mmscmd during May-June 2026. Non-Morbi industrial volumes are expected to reach at least 3 mmscmd in 1.5-2 years, helped by 86 new industries added from April to June.
  • Long-term LNG sourcing: Contracts have been signed with Total, Uniper, and Qatar, and the company is in the market for two more, targeting supply from 2028. Term-contract volumes are about 28%, or 2 million tonnes LNG equivalent, with expectations of at least 4 million tonnes by 2030. Long-term contracts are generally sold back-to-back, and greater contracted LNG should reduce spot exposure and support the CGD business with cheaper, more stable pricing.
  • Propane import facility: Sites in Gujarat near the Morbi market have been shortlisted for an own propane import and storage terminal; studies are ongoing and details are expected shortly.
  • Financial scale: Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was INR 9,670 crores versus INR 5,924 crores, up 63%; EBITDA INR 1,482 crores versus INR 896 crores, up 65%; PAT INR 998 crores versus INR 561 crores, up 78%. Overall sales volume was 15.66 mmscmd, with gas trading at 12.22 mmscmd (8.9 mmscmd allocated to CGD) and CGD at 12.34 mmscmd. Pipeline network spans about 45,900 km across six states and one union territory, with INR 127 crores invested in CGD infrastructure in the quarter.
  • Emissions impact: Average Q1 PNG industrial sales helped reduce about 1.07 crore kg of CO2 per day versus coal, and average Q1 CNG sales about 21 lakh kg per day versus petrol or diesel.
  • Balance sheet capacity: Cash of about INR 7,200 crores, gross block close to INR 12,000 crores, and a dividend of about INR 835 crores paid, with deployment plans for strengthening CNG and pipelines and diversification to be detailed by Q3.

Key Risks

  • Price sensitivity of LNG and volatility in gas prices.
  • Tightening supply of cheaper domestic gas alongside reduced APM gas allocation and increased reliance on expensive imported LNG.
  • High capital expenditure for infrastructure expansion.
  • Stiff competition from alternate fuels and multiple gas marketers.
  • Potential supply chain disruptions and price spikes from Middle East conflicts.
  • Regulatory and approval dependencies, including the GSEG reclassification approvals and the GTL listing exemption.
  • Execution risk on new infrastructure and propane terminal development.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Gas trading profit: Guidance of about INR 1,100 crores (with a range of INR 1,100-1,200 crores cited) was maintained despite a very strong first quarter; management said there would be no negative reversal in Q2. Q1 gas trading EBT of INR 726 crores was well above the prior-year quarter.
  • Gas trading margin: Guidance of about 4-5% margin was reiterated, with acknowledgment that exceptional quarters will occur.
  • CGD EBITDA margin: Guidance of Rs 5.5-6.5/scm was reiterated even though Q1 came in at Rs 5.18/scm, attributed to the larger volume base.
  • CGD capex: Guidance of close to INR 1,000 crores was maintained; this excludes the propane infrastructure capex, which is at an early stage and expected over the next two years.
  • CNG growth: Management expects to sustain about 12-13% growth for the next couple of years, supported by station additions; Q1 delivered 13%.
  • Third-party trading volume: Long-term expectation of 4.5-5 mmscmd was maintained, with good volumes expected in 2028-29 when pricing is seen as more reasonable; Q1 external volume was about 3.3 mmscmd.
  • Morbi volumes: Management expects about 3 mmscmd this quarter, with a floor of about 1.8-2 mmscmd, versus the May-June run rate of about 8 mmscmd.
  • Non-Morbi industrial volumes: Expected to reach at least 3 mmscmd in 1.5-2 years from the current 2-2.2 mmscmd.
  • Long-term sourcing: Term-contract share of about 28% today, targeting at least 4 million tonnes by 2030, with new sourcing targeted from 2028.
  • Tax refunds: About INR 900-odd crores expected, but at least a year away pending revised returns.
  • GTL listing: Slipped from end-July/mid-August to early September.
  • Cash deployment: A concrete plan for deploying the roughly INR 7,200 crores cash balance is expected by Q3.

Broker Narrative

The first report was deeply cautious on the sector, flagging severe OMC losses from US-Iran conflict, SAED export duties, RTP caps, and CGD margin compression, yet maintained an Overweight call on upstream benefits and CGD volume growth. By the last report, the narrative narrowed to GUJENERGY-specific tailwinds — strong CGD volume growth (39.1% QoQ/YoY), a Trading EBITDA beat (INR7.3bn), and retained EBITDA/scm guidance — but persistent concerns about PNG-D volume declines, Morbi operational disruptions, and GTL listing delays led to downgraded calls (Overweight → Hold/Buy) with negative actual returns. The shift from broad sector fear to company-specific optimism was insufficient to offset unresolved CGD margin and volume concerns.

Fears that came true

  • The first report flagged that elevated gas input costs would outpace CGD price hikes and pressure EBITDA/scm — this margin compression persisted as a key concern through the last report, correlating with the -11.6% return on the Overweight call.
  • The last report flagged Morbi volumes dropping to ~3mmscmd from an ~8mmscmd run-rate and PNG-D volumes declining 23.1% QoQ — these volume shortfalls materialized alongside the -7.6% actual return.
  • The last report flagged that GTL listing has been delayed to FY28-FY29 pending regulatory exemption — this delay materialized, contributing to disappointment reflected in the -7.6% return.

Optimism that failed

  • The first report expected strong CNG/PNG volume growth to accelerate as gas availability normalized and consumers shifted from LPG to natural gas — despite 39.1% QoQ/YoY CGD volume growth in the last report, the stock fell -11.6% and -7.6%, meaning volume momentum did not translate into positive returns.
  • Motilal Oswal’s Buy call predicted +31.4% returns supported by a Trading business EBITDA beat of INR7.3bn (2x YoY) — the stock returned -7.6%, so the Trading-driven upside failed to materialize.
  • The first report expected upstream benefits from elevated Brent prices (averaged US$96.9/bbl) to meaningfully offset downstream OMC losses — the stock still fell -11.6%, indicating upstream tailwinds were insufficient to rescue the overall thesis.
