Radiant Cash Management Services — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Manpower cost inflation. Employee costs, driver and gunman costs, and service charges towards cash executives have risen sharply. Minimum wages have increased in many states, directly pressuring margins.
  • Shortage of licensed gunmen in several southern states, and more broadly a shortage of armed guards in a few states, constrains cash van operations.
  • Segment sluggishness. Growth has been sluggish in Railways, Petroleum, and E-commerce segments, and revenue from some banks has declined.
  • Margin compression. Standalone EBITDA margins for Q1 FY27 fell to 13.5% from 15.9% in the same quarter last year, even as standalone revenues grew 7% year-over-year. Consolidated PAT declined to Rs. 52 million in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 57 million a year earlier.
  • Continued losses at RVL. Radiant Valuable Logistics has been loss-making, though management expects it to reach breakeven.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • IDBI Bank mandate. A recent large mandate with IDBI Bank is cited as a growth driver, alongside growth in cash van operations.
  • Ecom Logistics is showing positive growth.
  • RVL breakeven is expected, which would remove a drag on consolidated performance.
  • Value-added services cross-selling, including network currency management and cash processing.
  • Geographic expansion into Tier 2 and Tier 3+ locations, with coverage of 14,997 pin codes, 77,014 touch points, and 9,183 locations.
  • Fintech and digital payments scale-up: Soundbox and QR code deployments with rural and urban banks, a corporate BC network for banks, white-label UPI solutions, and fintech software solutions for smaller banks and NBFCs.
  • Acemoney is expected to turn EBITDA positive and contribute meaningfully to full-year profitability.
  • Price revisions sought from customers are expected to materialize.

3. Key Risks

  • Cost pass-through risk. The benefit of price revisions depends on customers accepting them; management expects these to materialize in the current quarter.
  • Contingent liability from subsidiary support. The Board has recommended revised limits for loans to Aceware of up to ₹300 million outstanding at any point, and corporate guarantees to Banks/FIs in favour of Aceware of up to ₹400 million outstanding at any point. These require shareholder approval at the ensuing AGM as they materially modify earlier approvals. The corporate guarantee will be disclosed as a contingent liability in the standalone financial statements, and Aceware’s actual borrowings as on the reporting date will be recorded as borrowing in the consolidated financial statements.
  • Related party interest. Promoters/Promoter Group are interested in the Aceware transaction to the extent of equity shares held as registered owners, directorships in the subsidiary, and nominee directorships; the guarantee is stated to be at arm’s length.
  • Execution risk on new initiatives. The fintech, BC network, and digital payments scale-up, as well as the turnaround of RVL and Acemoney, are forward-looking and depend on delivery.
  • Dependence on labour availability, given the licensed gunman shortage and minimum wage increases.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Revenue growth. Management expects current-year revenue growth to cross double digits, and FY28 top-line growth in the mid-teens (12%–13%). Observed: standalone revenues grew 7% year-over-year in Q1 FY27.
  • Margins. Management hopes FY28 consolidated EBITDA margins will reach about 19%–20%. Observed: standalone EBITDA margin was 13.5% in Q1 FY27, down from 15.9% a year earlier.
  • Price revisions. Management expects significant price revisions with all customers to materialize in the current quarter. This remains to be demonstrated in reported results.
  • RVL. Management expects RVL to reach breakeven in the current quarter. Observed: RVL has been reporting continued losses.
  • Acemoney. Management expects Acemoney to turn EBITDA positive in the current quarter and contribute meaningfully to full-year profitability; a separate statement indicates Acemoney achieving EBITDA breakeven in Q3 FY27. Observed performance is not yet reflected in the reported numbers.
  • Profitability trend. Consolidated PAT fell to Rs. 52 million in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 57 million a year earlier, while total income for Q1 FY27 stood at ₹1,077 million, EBITDA at ₹145 million, and PAT at ₹80 million on the stated basis. Cash volume handled was Rs. 0.43 trillion, up about 2.3%, with gross cash losses of Rs. 2 million (Rs. 2.06 million) and a cash balance of Rs. 2.1 billion as of June 30, 2026, with free cash flow of Rs. 644 million.

5. Governance and Capital Actions

  • The Board, at its meeting on August 12, 2026 (commenced 03:15 PM IST, concluded 04:35 PM IST), approved unaudited standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, with an unmodified limited review opinion from the statutory auditors.
  • The Board recommended the re-appointment of M/s ASA & Associates LLP, Chartered Accountants, as Statutory Auditors for a second term of five consecutive years, from the conclusion of the 21st AGM until the conclusion of the 26th AGM to be held in calendar year 2031, subject to shareholder approval. ASA & Associates LLP is the exclusive India member firm of Baker Tilly International.
  • The 21st AGM was held on September 16, 2026. All proposed resolutions were passed with the requisite majority, including adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements, declaration of dividend, re-appointment of directors and statutory auditors, authorization to advance loans to Aceware Fintech Services Private Limited, and approval of material related party transactions.
