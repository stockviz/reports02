Century Plyboards (India) Limited — Q1 FY27 Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Volatile external environment: Management stated it has stopped giving guidance because “the situation is very volatile,” citing week-to-week shifts in geopolitical conditions (war/peace) that affect pricing and demand visibility.
  • Input cost pressure: Higher chemical input costs arising from geopolitical developments prompted a ~7% plywood price increase in April 2026, largely to pass through raw material cost increases.
  • Price hike rollbacks: In MDF, a price increase of close to 15% was taken but a large part was already rolled back as raw material prices kept moving; laminate increases were ~10% domestic and slightly lower for exports.
  • MDF sequential decline: A planned shutdown at the Andhra Pradesh MDF plant for capacity expansion (700 to 950 CBM/day) caused lower production and a 6.2% QoQ revenue decline; MDF EBITDA margin (excluding forex) fell to 7.0% in Q1 FY27 from 14.3% in Q1 FY26.
  • Particle board margins at an early stage: Despite 155.7% YoY revenue growth, PB EBITDA margin (excluding forex) was only 3.7%, reflecting recent commissioning of capacity.
  • Working capital and leverage: Growing at 33% in an industry with ~60 days of working capital has increased debt; Total Debt/EBITDA stood at 2.20 and Total Debt/Equity at 0.65 in Q1 FY27. ROCE has been under pressure over recent years (30.91% in FY23 down to 13.43% in FY25-26, recovering to 14.39% in Q1 FY27).
  • Laminate volume decline: Laminate volumes fell 8.4% YoY and 9.8% QoQ in Q1 FY27, attributed to product mix/size adjustments, with higher realizations partially offsetting.
  • Land and project delays: The UP greenfield project awaits land possession (targeted before year-end); the Odisha project is still in talks with the government with no land selected. The UP MDF project is expected to lag the plywood unit.
  • Logistics business non-core: Management noted logistics is not a core segment and may involve a strategic partner at a valuation beneficial to the company.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record quarterly revenue: Highest-ever consolidated revenue of INR 1,561 crores in Q1 FY27, up 33.5% YoY; PAT up 57% YoY to INR 83.3 crores; EBITDA margin (excluding forex losses) improved to 13.0% from 12.5%.
  • Plywood momentum: Revenue grew 32.4% YoY and 8.9% QoQ; EBITDA margin (excluding forex) at 16.9%, described as the highest in the industry. Plywood volumes rose 28.8% YoY.
  • Market share ambition: Management estimates it ended last year at ~9.5–10% of the overall plywood industry and aims to reach 15% in five years, implying 12–15% annual growth.
  • Capacity expansion: The 60,000 CBM/annum greenfield plywood plant at Hoshiarpur is expected to commence operations in Q3 FY27; Chennai brownfield capacity rose from 8,000 CBM/month in Q1 to 10,000 CBM/month in Q2, rising to 12,500 CBM/month from Q3 FY27.
  • MDF expansion: Andhra Pradesh MDF capacity expanded from 700 to 950 CBM/day; MDF revenue still grew 28.9% YoY despite the shutdown.
  • Particle board ramp-up: Revenue up 29% sequentially and 155.7% YoY; management expects steady margin improvement with utilization, targeting close to 15% PB margins next year.
  • Laminates: Revenue grew 14.7% YoY with 10.2% EBITDA margin (excluding forex); a new home press for export-grade manufacturing was commissioned end-July 2026; new LookBook launched.
  • Logistics turnaround: Khidderpore Docks terminal operations stabilized and contribute positively; 9,000+ containers handled in July; double-digit growth expected in the CFS business.
  • Brand and product initiatives: Total Cover Assurance Program for Club Prime Plywood (first of its kind, reimbursing furniture cost plus related expenses within 10 years); campaigns featuring Rahul Dravid, Saurabh Shukla, Aamir Khan and Chatur; Prime Ply Day with ~1,000 dealers.
  • Return ratios improving: ROE rose from 13.1% to 13.5% and ROCE from 13.4% to 14.4%, attributed to sweating existing assets and operating leverage.
  • Capital allocation discipline: Focus on utilization and asset productivity rather than aggressive capex; no large capex currently planned beyond Hoshiarpur completion and modest brownfield spends.

3. Key Risks

  • Guidance withdrawal: Management explicitly refrained from providing revenue, margin, or volume guidance due to volatility, limiting forward visibility.
  • Raw material and price volatility: Price increases have been partially rolled back in MDF; further movement in raw material prices could pressure margins.
  • Execution and land risk: UP and Odisha projects depend on land acquisition and government negotiations; timelines could slip.
  • Leverage trajectory: Working capital debt rises with high growth; management targets long-term debt within one time EBITDA, but this is not yet achieved.
  • Segment margin risk: MDF and particle board margins remain below the stated 20% ROCE hurdle rate; particle board margins are at an early stage.
  • Claim/contingency exposure: The Total Cover program carries a claim ratio of 0.06%; any increase in claims could affect costs, though management expects limited impact.
  • Non-core business uncertainty: Logistics may be divested or partnered depending on valuation, creating strategic uncertainty.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Guidance stance: Management has stopped giving guidance due to volatility, but stated objectives include heading toward 15%+ MDF margins, ~15% particle board margins next year, 20% ROCE in both MDF and particle board, and 15% plywood market share in five years.
  • Observed performance vs. objectives:
    • MDF EBITDA margin (excluding forex) was 7.0% in Q1 FY27, below the 15%+ objective; management noted Q1 was impacted by the planned shutdown.
    • Particle board EBITDA margin (excluding forex) was 3.7%, below the ~15% target; management expects steady quarterly improvement.
    • Plywood market share was estimated at ~9.5–10% at end of last year, with Q1 FY27 growth of 28.8% in volumes, consistent with the 12–15% annual growth ambition.
    • ROCE improved to 14.39% in Q1 FY27 from 13.43% in FY25-26, moving toward the 20% hurdle rate but not yet there.
  • Capex guidance: Management indicated no large capex planned; Hoshiarpur plywood plant (~INR 200 crores) and a laminate press (~INR 25 crores) are the main items. The capex table shows total spend of INR 3,900.6 crores to date, with INR 113.4 crores in Q1 FY27 and INR 307.4 crores planned for the 9 months of 2026-27.
  • Balance sheet guidance: Management expects balance sheet strengthening in H2 FY27, with cash flows directed toward debt repayment, while acknowledging working capital needs at 30%+ growth.
  • Dividend: A final dividend of Re. 1/- per equity share for FY 2025-26 was proposed, with record date 9th September 2026 and AGM on 16th September 2026; all 11 resolutions passed with no auditor qualifications.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from an aggressive Buy on FY24 volume growth, softening chemical prices, and margin recovery to a cautious Hold in 2026, citing MDF margin compression, missing FY27 guidance, and full valuations. Persistent themes were raw-material/timber costs, regulatory/sourcing risks, housing/macro slowdown, and MDF import dumping. The changed emphasis was from celebrating capacity expansion and market-share gains to flagging MDF plant shutdown/expansion costs, logistics cash flow, and waiting for a better entry point.

Fears that came true

  • Elevated raw-material costs persisted: MDF EBITDA margin fell 723 bps YoY to 7.0% in Q1FY27, and the stock saw disappointment outcomes such as the 2026-02-06 Accumulate call with -6.2% actual return.
  • The AP MDF capex/expansion risk materialized as the plant was shut for capacity expansion, contributing to the MDF margin drag and weak price action around the last report.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that softening chemical prices would drive durable margin recovery failed, as later raw-material and chemical resin costs rose and MDF EBITDA margin declined sharply by Q1FY27.
