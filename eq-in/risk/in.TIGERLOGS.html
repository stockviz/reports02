Tiger Logistics (India) Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin compression despite revenue growth. In Q1 FY27 (quarter ended 30 June 2026), revenue rose 48.8% year-on-year to ₹15,252.5 lakh, yet EBITDA fell 40.5% to ₹438.2 lakh and PAT fell 53.9% to ₹217.0 lakh. EBITDA margin declined to 2.8% from 7.1% a year earlier, EBIT margin to 2.7% from 6.9%, and PAT margin to 1.4% from 4.5%. The company attributes this to freight-rate volatility, higher transportation costs, and competitive pricing pressure.

Elevated operating costs. Increased freight, fuel, insurance, and related logistics costs are described as putting pressure on gross and operating margins. Operating expenses rose to ₹14,165.3 lakh in Q1 FY27 from ₹8,934.1 lakh in Q1 FY26, and finance cost rose to ₹123.6 lakh from ₹84.5 lakh.

Subdued global trade environment. The broader trade environment remained uncertain, with geopolitical developments, tariff uncertainty, and changing trade routes affecting customer decisions and shipment patterns.

Sequential softness. On a quarter-on-quarter basis, revenue declined 6.2% (from ₹16,254.7 lakh in Q4 FY26), total income declined 6.8%, EBITDA declined 4.5%, and PAT declined 2.3%.

Sector-level challenges. The investor presentation cites poor road and highway quality raising delivery days and operating costs, intensifying competition from global and domestic players, a lack of trained staff slowing technology adoption, and transit loss from goods lost or stolen in transit.

Regulatory matter. On 17 August 2026, the company disclosed a RBI compounding order under Section 15 of FEMA, 1999, relating to contravention of Regulation 16(1)(iii) of the Foreign Exchange Management (Transfer or Issue of Any Foreign Security) Regulations, 2004 — specifically non-compliance with post-disinvestment valuation reporting under FEMA Notification No. 120/RB-2004 dated 7 July 2004. The contravention was compounded on payment of ₹59,981. The company states the financial impact is limited to that amount and that there is no material impact on operations or other activities. This follows an earlier disclosure made on 4 February 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong volume and revenue momentum. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations grew 48.8% YoY to ₹152.5 crore, with TEU volumes up 28.7% compared with Q1 FY26. Management describes this as a strong start to the year driven by healthy business volumes, customer confidence, and new business opportunities.

New contract wins and renewals. During the quarter, the company renewed its logistics mandate with Bank Note Paper Mill India and added a new break-bulk logistics contract from BHEL.

Major HPCL air charter contracts. Announced 13 August 2026, Tiger Logistics secured two air import contracts from Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited for approximately 310 MT of cargo to Visakhapatnam, with a business value of approximately ₹8.8 crore, to be executed over one year through three chartered flights — approximately 80 MT from France and 210 MT from Saudi Arabia. Scope covers end-to-end ex-works handling: air freight, customs clearance, and local transportation. The company frames this as strengthening its air cargo capabilities for PSUs, building on prior work with BHEL.

Market opportunity. The presentation cites an Indian freight and logistics market estimated to grow at 8.8% annually to USD 484 billion by 2029 from USD 317 billion in 2024, with logistics cost at 13% of GDP (versus 18% for China and 8.5% for the USA), and government reforms in transportation, digitalisation, port capacity, and railway traffic.

Growth pillars and new verticals. Stated pillars include digital play, new geographies, acquisitions, and green logistics. New business verticals include FreightJar (digital freight booking and management), TiGreen (renewable energy logistics across solar, wind, and hydropower), and CUBOX (LCL consolidation and B2B solutions). The company operates an asset-light model with partner networks.

Recognition. Awards cited include “25 Years of Logistics Excellence” at Conquest 2026, “Logistics Champion Mid-Sized Company” by ISCM Logistics Ranking 2026, “Logistics Company of the Year” at the All India Maritime and Logistics Awards 2026, and “Business Leader of the Year” at the Northern India Multimodal Logistics Awards.

3. Key Risks

  • Margin and pricing risk: Freight-rate volatility, competitive pricing pressure, and rising freight, fuel, insurance, and logistics costs could continue to compress margins, as seen in the Q1 FY27 decline in EBITDA and PAT despite revenue growth.
  • Geopolitical and trade-policy risk: Geopolitical developments, tariff uncertainty, and shifting trade routes may affect customer decisions and shipment patterns.
  • Execution risk on specialized cargo: The HPCL contracts involve time-sensitive, specialized project cargo moved via chartered aircraft, requiring stringent planning, regulatory compliance, and coordination across multiple touchpoints.
  • Regulatory and compliance risk: The FEMA compounding order illustrates exposure to foreign-exchange compliance requirements; the matter was compounded for ₹59,981 with no stated material operational impact.
  • Sector-level risks: Infrastructure quality, competition from global and domestic players, shortage of trained staff slowing technology adoption, and transit loss from theft or damage.
  • Customer and contract concentration considerations: The presentation includes top-five customer revenue composition disclosures, indicating dependence on a limited set of customers.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements. Management stated it is confident of building on momentum by expanding into new markets, strengthening presence across existing trade lanes, and capturing emerging opportunities. It said the focus going forward is on sustaining the growth trajectory while improving operational efficiency, strengthening margins, and converting expanding business volumes into sustainable profitability, and expressed confidence about the year ahead given deeper market penetration, a growing customer base, new market opportunities, and improving pricing prospects. The company also states it remains focused on growing responsibly, strengthening market position, and creating sustainable long-term value for shareholders. The safe harbor notes these are forward-looking statements subject to risks and uncertainties and not necessarily predictive of future results.

Observed performance against guidance. The stated intent to improve operational efficiency and strengthen margins is set against Q1 FY27 results where EBITDA margin fell to 2.8% from 7.1% YoY and PAT margin fell to 1.4% from 4.5%, with EBITDA down 40.5% and PAT down 53.9% YoY. The growth objective is supported by observed results: revenue up 48.8% YoY and TEU volumes up 28.7% YoY. The cited “improving pricing prospects” contrasts with the reported competitive pricing pressure and margin decline in the same quarter. The new contract momentum cited by management (Bank Note Paper Mill India renewal, BHEL break-bulk contract, and the HPCL air charter contracts valued at approximately ₹8.8 crore over one year) provides evidence of continued order inflows, though the HPCL contracts were announced after the Q1 FY27 results and their contribution is not reflected in the reported quarter.

Other corporate actions. The Board meeting on 12 August 2026 approved the unaudited quarterly results for the period ended 30 June 2026 with the statutory auditors’ limited review report, the Directors’ Report for FY ended 31 March 2026, and the notice of the 26th Annual General Meeting to be held Thursday, 24 September 2026 at 1:00 PM via video conferencing. The register of members will be closed from 17 September 2026 to 24 September 2026 (both days inclusive).
