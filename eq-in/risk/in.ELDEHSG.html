ELDEHSG — Q1 FY27 Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Bookings normalized sharply after a launch-heavy Q4 FY26. Booking value fell to ₹105.7 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹382.7 crore in Q4 FY26 (down 72.4% QoQ) and from ₹221.1 crore in Q1 FY26 (down 52.2% YoY). Area booked fell 62.6% YoY to 1,25,917 sq. ft. Management attributed this to the exceptional launch-led performance in the prior quarter rather than a demand problem, describing customer engagement as healthy.

Deliveries declined. 52 homes (49,405 sq. ft.) were delivered in Q1 FY27 versus 101 homes (84,636 sq. ft.) in Q1 FY26 — a 48.5% drop in units.

External dislocation. Management cited “war dislocation between April, May” and a Middle East situation that caused commodity pressure and labor displacement, with all projects receiving a four-month extension under RERA. This is directly relevant to the timing of revenue recognition at Latitude 27.

Approval risk. The launch of forthcoming projects (Shaheed Path, Eldeco Palm Springs, LDA GH-7/GH-8, Eldeco City Courtyard) depends on approvals, which management acknowledged “can be a bit up and down.”

Legacy inventory drag. Around ₹75 crore of unsold ready inventory sits in legacy projects, which management noted are “not really at top of mind for brokers” and the wider channel.

Macro headwinds (annual report extract). Geopolitical tensions, elevated energy prices, moderating growth expectations, inflationary pressures, and global uncertainties. The company also manages credit, liquidity, capital, interest rate, market exposure, and foreign currency risks.

Concentration and market perception. An investor questioned whether the market’s valuation reflects a terminal-value discount because the business is limited to Lucknow. Management did not provide a diversification roadmap in response.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Collections outpaced bookings. Collections rose 68.2% YoY to ₹131.2 crore and 36.0% QoQ, which management framed as evidence of construction-linked monetization working.

Execution momentum. Construction spend rose 47.2% YoY to ₹57.8 crore, reflecting steady progress across ongoing projects.

Margin mix shift toward horizontal development. EBITDA margin expanded to 37.1% in Q1 FY27 from 17.6% in Q1 FY26. Management explained that Q1 FY26 was dominated by a vertical development with lower margins, while Q1 FY27 revenue was predominantly from Imperia, a high-margin horizontal development, with roughly 85% of quarterly revenue attributable to Imperia Phase 2 at gross margins of approximately 60%. Management expects horizontal formats — including the 50-acre project — to deliver higher margins and intends to balance vertical and horizontal development opportunistically.

Land pipeline expansion. During Q1 FY27 the company executed a legally binding contract for 50.3 acres of contiguous land at a prime Lucknow location, and completed aggregation of approximately 15.7 acres. The land bank for forthcoming projects totals 95.5 acres, including undisclosed locations of 32.0 acres and 8.3 acres, the 50.3-acre contracted parcel, and 4.9 acres of further aggregation at Solano Gardens.

Forthcoming launch pipeline. Of the 3.4 million sq. ft. forthcoming pipeline, management stated that almost all of serial numbers 4 through 7 (LDA GH-7, LDA GH-8, Shaheed Path, Eldeco Palm Springs) will be launched within FY27, with the caveat that some are under approval.

New launches. Eldeco Imperia Avenue launched with 44 units booked (18,495 sq. ft., ₹14.6 crore). Faith Tower, the third and final tower at Eldeco Trinity (146,955 sq. ft.), launched in mid-June 2026.

Solano Gardens. Three elements remain: a 9.5-acre group housing project, tail unsold inventory in the horizontal format, and a five-acre extension. Management expects much of the remaining inventory to be liquidated this year and hopes to launch the group housing within FY27, with no commitment on the extension.

Market tailwinds (annual report extract). Strong domestic demand, sustained public infrastructure investment, favourable urban growth trends in Lucknow, growing interest in premium housing, and rising per capita income. The three prime land parcels are cited as adding nearly ₹2,000 crore in GDV.

Lucknow demand drivers. Management cited inbound migration and interest in the Lucknow housing market following the opening of the Kanpur-Lucknow Expressway, and noted that unorganized commercial supply is shifting into more organized developments with rentals on an upward incline.

3. Key Risks

  • Revenue timing risk at Latitude 27. The project’s completion certificate is expected November 2027. Management intends to recognize revenue tower-wise as towers complete, targeting 15–20% recognition between March and May 2027 — meaning a slip of even a month moves revenue from FY27 into FY28. RERA extensions and commodity/labor disruption are explicit factors.
  • Booking volatility. The sharp QoQ and YoY declines in bookings illustrate sensitivity to launch timing rather than a steady run-rate.
  • Inventory overhang. ₹75 crore of legacy ready inventory and ₹9,82,408 sq. ft. of area available across ongoing projects (against ₹1,015.8 crore value of area allotted and ₹613.2 crore balance pending) represent monetization risk if absorption slows.
  • Approval dependency. Multiple forthcoming projects await final approval or layout approval.
  • Geographic concentration. The portfolio and pipeline are concentrated in Lucknow.
  • Macro and financial risks. As noted in the annual report extract: geopolitical tensions, energy prices, inflation, and credit, liquidity, capital, interest rate, market exposure, and foreign currency risks.
  • Governance/administrative items. Shareholders in physical form are reminded to update KYC, PAN, nomination, contact, bank account, and specimen signature details pursuant to the SEBI Master Circular dated February 6, 2026.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY26 outcome vs. FY27 framing. FY26 delivered record booking value of ₹743.9 crore (up 120% YoY), collections of ₹352.1 crore (up 39%), total consolidated income of ₹175.7 crore, PAT of ₹24.3 crore, and a recommended final dividend of 450% (₹9 per equity share). Against that base, management guided that FY27 booking value should show “strong growth” and that FY27 will show “reasonably strong growth” in sales from FY26. Q1 FY27 bookings of ₹105.7 crore annualize well below the FY26 run-rate, so the guidance implies a substantial pickup over the remaining three quarters.

FY28 as the inflection point. Management stated that FY28 onwards represents “a pivotal change in the trajectory” for both presales and sales, with FY28–FY29 showing “a much larger uptick,” while declining to quantify. This is consistent with the Latitude 27 recognition window and the FY27 launch pipeline.

Imperia Phase 2 monetization. Of the roughly ₹300 crore GDV, approximately ₹170–180 crore of inventory remains available. Management said this would “predominantly” be realized in the current financial year, while declining to commit to timing.

Legacy inventory. Management guided that 40–60% of the ₹75 crore legacy inventory would be liquidated in the current year, describing an internal push to bring legacy projects to closure.

Latitude 27. Guidance of 15–20% recognition between March and May 2027, with the outcome dependent on whether completion lands in March (FY27) or April–May (FY28).

Forthcoming launches. Management stated almost all of the serial 4–7 pipeline will launch within FY27, with approval timing as the caveat.

Longer-term pipeline monetization (annual report extract). Management expects to monetise the newly secured pipeline over five to seven years as projects move through design, approval, and launch, supported by execution, improved cash conversion, and disciplined capital allocation.

Capital allocation. In response to an investor question on buybacks, management said open market buybacks and similar instruments are “under active consideration,” without committing to any action, and reiterated that capital would be deployed where it best optimizes the capital structure or expands future development.

Observed vs. guided on Trinity. The Faith Tower launch occurred in mid-June 2026, with the sample flat ready around June 10–15 and first intermediary and customer engagement in the third and fourth weeks of June. Only two or three bookings were made initially, with a pipeline of 20–25 bookings in conversion during July and August. Management asked investors to defer judgment on Trinity traction to the next board meeting.

Reporting basis change. The company replaced “area booked” with “area allotted” in the ongoing projects disclosure. Management explained that allotment follows booking and reflects customers already in the system, whereas booking is a presales measure — the two are not directly comparable.
