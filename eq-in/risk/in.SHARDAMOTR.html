Sharda Motor Industries (SHARDAMOTR) — Q1 FY27 Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Customer supplier fire: A fire at a supplier of one of Sharda Motor’s key customers caused a production impact during Q1 FY27, weighing on gross profit growth.
  • Geopolitical disruption and premium raw material procurement: Onetime premium freight and premium-priced RM procurement were incurred in April and May 2026 to keep supply chains running. Management indicated these costs were absorbed and had stabilized by July 2026, though they were not quantified as significant.
  • Growth below reported industry pace: While domestic automobile production rose over 22% year-on-year to 93.6 lakh units in Q1 FY27, Sharda Motor’s gross profit grew 8%. Management attributed this to the customer supplier fire, the onetime RM impact, and the company’s absence from Japanese OEMs; adjusting for these, the addressable market grew about 8–10%, broadly in line with the company’s 8% gross profit growth.
  • Margin compression: Gross profit margin fell to 20.2% in Q1 FY27 from 25.1% in Q1 FY26 and 22.2% in Q4 FY26. EBITDA margin declined to 10.2% from 13.0% a year earlier and 11.6% sequentially.
  • Revenue mix distortion: Reported revenue growth of 34% was inflated by catalyst pass-through elements and product mix; management emphasized gross profit as the better performance indicator.
  • Regulatory uncertainty: BS7 has not been officially notified; the eventual content opportunity depends on notified fuel specifications, regulatory requirements, and OEM platform design. CAFE III (draft issued 16 July 2026, proposed from 1 April 2027) moderates super-credit benefits for strong hybrids and flex fuel vehicles and reduces the compliance advantage previously available to heavier fleets.
  • Export and trade environment: Evolving geopolitical conditions, trade dynamics, and macroeconomic volatility continue to create uncertainty; export SOP timing and ramp-ups remain tied to customer schedules.
  • JV contribution constraints: In the Purem JV (CV segment above 4 litres), Sharda Motor does not have management control and receives only 50% of PAT after deductions, limiting financial contribution despite attractive industry unit economics.
  • Limited opportunity in TREM3: The integrated muffler opportunity from TREM3 is estimated at only INR60–100 crores, treated as normal business development rather than a major growth driver.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong industry momentum: Domestic automobile production grew over 22% year-on-year to 93.6 lakh units in Q1 FY27. Passenger vehicle production rose 16.8% to 14.53 lakh units, led by utility vehicles (+21.2%); LCV production rose 20.8% to 1.91 lakh units.
  • Multi-powertrain strategy: Sharda Motor positions itself across ICE, CNG, hybrid, flex fuel, and electric vehicles, with a powertrain-agnostic product portfolio. Except for pure EVs, all powertrains require engineered emission systems.
  • Lightweighting expansion: Control arm and link programs are ramping up; the Chakan 3 lightweighting facility commenced SOP. The Donghee technology licensing agreement extends the portfolio into subframes and torsion beams, increasing content per vehicle from INR2,000–8,000 to INR6,000–18,000. Management assesses the broader lightweighting portfolio at INR8,000–9,000 crores in five years, targeting a mid-teen to high-teen percentage share.
  • Export order book: Three orders from a North American engine and genset manufacturer with combined annual value of approximately US$10.7 million and lifetime value of approximately US$58.5 million remain aligned to revised customer schedules, with SOPs expected in Q3 and Q4 FY27. Additional FY26 orders include a US$4.8 million annual emission adjacency order (SOP Q4 FY27), a US$3 million annual control arm order (SOP Q3 FY27), a US$3 million annual emission components order (SOP Q3 FY27), and a US$2.3 million annual order from a leading North American agriculture equipment OEM (SOP Q1 FY28).
  • Regulatory-driven content growth: BS6.3/WLTP effective 1 April 2027 for M1 passenger vehicles increases focus on catalyst efficiency, calibration, thermal management, and durability. BS7, if aligned with Euro-7, is expected to increase content in hot-end and aftertreatment systems through high catalyst requirements, gasoline particulate filters, and additional catalytic applications. Multiple WLTP replacement business orders were secured from leading PV OEMs in Q1 FY27.
  • New adjacencies: Temperature-controlled tube business is ramping up, with SOP commenced for a leading off-highway equipment manufacturer. Entry into domestic premium 2-wheeler emission systems is being explored. Export focus areas include CV emission components, agri and large genset emission components, temperature-controlled tubes, and small tractor and genset exhaust systems.
  • China+1 opportunity: Trade disruptions and new emission norms in the EU and US create export opportunities from India. Target market sizes cited include CV emission components (US$1,100 million), tractor emission and muffler systems (US$85 million), genset emission and muffler systems (US$225 million), heat shields (US$100 million), and temperature-controlled tubes (US$310 million).
  • Capacity investments: New Uttarakhand facility (approximately INR20 crores investment) is progressing to support relocation and co-location of existing business, with potential for additional share of business and new customers in North India.
  • R&D and IP: Two additional patents filed in Q1 FY27, taking total filings to 24 with 4 granted. R&D supports WLTP, Euro-7 benchmarking, hybrid solutions, flex fuel readiness, temperature control tubes, and lightweighting localization.
  • M&A readiness: Management stated the company is ready to be more assertive in pursuing strategic acquisitions, with filters of strategic fit, customer and technology relevance, valuation, integration feasibility, and ROCE. The strong balance sheet provides capacity, with capital deployment balanced between organic growth, technology partnerships, acquisitions, and shareholder returns.
  • Financial capacity: Total equity stood at INR1,313.0 crores as of March 2026; investments of INR896.0 crores (current) and INR205.0 crores (non-current); cash and cash equivalents of INR91.6 crores. Net cash from operating activities was INR362.9 crores in FY26.

3. Key Risks

  • Customer concentration and production schedules: Growth trajectory depends on customer production schedules, SOP timing, and program ramp-ups, which are outside the company’s control.
  • Commodity and pass-through timing: While catalyst and steel are largely back-to-back pass-through, timing differs by customer and can create quarterly lag effects. Substrate prices are customer-directed with no lag.
  • Geopolitical and trade uncertainty: Evolving geopolitical conditions and trade dynamics could affect export orders, supply chains, and raw material costs.
  • Regulatory dependence: BS7 timing and content scope remain uncertain; CAFE III implementation from FY28 introduces fleet-level CO2 targets tightening from approximately 94.76 gCO/km in 2027-28 to approximately 78.9 gCO/km in 2031-32.
  • JV limited control: Purem JV operates in a segment where Sharda Motor is not directly present; lack of management control limits influence and financial contribution.
  • Ramp-up uncertainty: New platform ramp-ups typically take a year or two to reach peak volumes; management declined to provide specific revenue guidance for export orders.
  • Acquisition execution risk: While capabilities have been strengthened, acquisitions must meet strategic, valuation, integration, and ROCE filters; no specific targets or timelines were disclosed.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • No formal guidance: Management explicitly stated it does not provide guidance. Growth factors cited are existing order ramp-ups, conversion of RFQs into orders, and organic industry growth.
  • Gross profit as key indicator: Management guided that gross profit, not revenue, is the better indicator of underlying operating performance. Observed: gross profit grew 8% year-on-year in Q1 FY27, in line with the 8–10% addressable market growth management cited after adjusting for the customer supplier fire and one-time RM impact.
  • Export SOP timing: Management stated SOPs for North American orders are expected in Q3 FY27 and Q4 FY27, aligned to customer schedules, with no changes seen as of the call date. Execution activities including sampling, validation, and SOP readiness are progressing in line with customer requirements.
  • Lightweighting market share: Management stated the 14% market share (as of FY26) will increase with SOPs and order visibility, but declined to quantify, indicating calculation at financial year-end.
  • Ramp-up expectations: Management indicated new platform ramp-ups typically take a year or two to reach peak volumes, based on experience.
  • Donghee TLA progress: Management stated the collaboration has generated RFQ opportunities across multiple product platforms through joint showcasing of Korean products to key OEM customers; commercial timelines were described as early to comment on.
  • Uttarakhand facility: Progressing in line with customer implementation plan; first phase supports relocation and co-location of existing business.
  • Chakan 3 facility: Commenced SOP and ramping up in line with customer schedules; modular capacity linked to confirmed programs.
  • Temperature-controlled tube order: SOP commenced and ramping up as planned for a leading off-highway equipment manufacturer.
  • Purem JV program: The previously announced program started in Q4 FY26 and will gradually ramp up during FY27; described as on track.
  • Financial performance vs. prior periods: Q1 FY27 revenue was INR1,011.1 crores (+34% YoY), EBITDA INR103.2 crores (+5% YoY), PBT before exceptional items INR115.0 crores (vs. INR107.7 crores in Q1 FY26 excluding exceptional gains of INR22.4 crores), PAT INR86.5 crores (vs. INR99.9 crores in Q1 FY26 including the exceptional gain). Sequentially, revenue rose 4% while EBITDA fell 9% and PAT fell 3%.
  • Dividend: A final dividend of Rs. 20/- per equity share (1000% of face value) was declared for FY 2025-26, with a record date of Friday, September 18, 2026.
  • AGM: The 41st Annual General Meeting is scheduled for Thursday, September 24, 2026, at 12:00 Noon IST through video conference or other audio-visual means. The Annual Report for FY 2025-26 was dispatched on August 31, 2026.
  • Board changes: Shri Ajay Relan is proposed for re-appointment as Whole-time Director designated as Executive Chairperson for five years starting September 1, 2026. Remuneration approval is sought for Non-Executive Independent Director Shri Navin Paul and Cost Auditors Gurdeep Singh & Associates.
