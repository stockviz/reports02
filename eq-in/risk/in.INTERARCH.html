Interarch Building Solutions — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Income tax search and subsequent proceedings. An Income Tax Department search and survey was conducted from August 18, 2025 to August 22, 2025 under Sections 132 and 133A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, covering certain offices, manufacturing locations and other premises of the Company, as well as residences of certain employees/key managerial personnel. The Department took documents, data backups and other information for further investigation. The Company filed a block assessment return on February 26, 2026, pursuant to a notice under Section 115BC dated January 29, 2026, requiring a revised income tax return for the period April 1, 2019 to October 17, 2025. Separately, a notice under Section 142(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 was received from the Office of the Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax, Central Circle 7, Delhi, in connection with the assessment for the block period April 1, 2019 to October 17, 2025, requiring production of accounts, documents and information by September 29, 2026. The notice does not determine any undisclosed income and does not raise any demand for tax, interest or penalty; the financial impact cannot be ascertained at this stage. As of the date of the Q1 FY27 results, no assessment order or demand order had been received, and management stated that no material adjustments were required to those results on this account. The Company stated it is examining the notice in consultation with tax advisors and legal counsel and will file its response with supporting documents within the time allowed.

GST show cause notice. On August 26, 2026, the Company received a Show Cause Notice / DRC-01 under Section 74 on the GST Portal from the Excise and Taxation Officer, Faridabad (North) Ward 4, Haryana, regarding a difference in Input Tax Credit availed and utilised between GSTR 3B and GSTR 2A. The notice involves a differential Input Tax Credit of Rs. 9,88,926 and interest of Rs. 11,42,208, totalling Rs. 21,31,134. The Company stated it will file a proper reply in due course.

Near-term operating environment. Management described a “dynamic operating environment” and noted that near-term challenges arising from external market conditions may persist.

Sequential moderation in profitability. Revenue declined 8.7% QoQ in Q1 FY27 (Rs. 459.6 crore versus Rs. 503.6 crore in Q4 FY26), EBITDA fell 25.3% QoQ and EBITDA margin contracted 191 bps QoQ to 8.6%. PAT declined 22.8% QoQ. On a YoY basis, PAT was flat at Rs. 28.2 crore (versus Rs. 28.4 crore), with PAT margin down 131 bps YoY to 6.1%, partly reflecting lower other income (Rs. 3.0 crore versus Rs. 9.9 crore in Q1 FY26).

Working capital and cash flow. FY26 net cash from operating activities was negative Rs. 18.8 crore, with changes in working capital of negative Rs. 164.6 crore. Cash and cash equivalents fell from Rs. 84.7 crore at the start of FY26 to Rs. 20.6 crore at year-end. Trade receivables rose to Rs. 288.2 crore (current) plus Rs. 111.0 crore (non-current) in FY26, and contract assets increased to Rs. 85.0 crore.

Execution and project risks. The Company’s own disclosures identify risks including time and cost overruns on contracts, competition (domestic and international), fluctuations in earnings, ability to manage growth and international operations, and regulatory and policy changes.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order book and order wins. Total order book as of July 31, 2026 stood at Rs. 1,864 crore. Orders booked between May 1, 2026 and July 31, 2026 totalled Rs. 609 crore, with key customers including Ahluwalia Contracts (India), International Tractors, L&T Construction, Suraj Buildcon, Torrent Electricals and Waree Transpower. During the quarter, the Company secured a major order worth Rs. 165 crore in Gujarat. On August 18, 2026, the Company announced an order for design, engineering, manufacturing, supply and erection of a Pre-Engineered Steel Building System for a major FMCG player of international repute, for a manufacturing facility for homecare/beauty and wellness products. The order is domestic, valued at approximately Rs. 128 crore including taxes, with an approximately 10-month completion period and 20% advance; the customer’s name was withheld due to confidentiality and commercial considerations.

Capacity expansion. Phase 1 of the new PEB manufacturing facility at Kheda, Gujarat was commissioned in July 2026 with capex of Rs. 60 crore, adding 20,000 MT and taking total capacity to 2,21,000 MT. Phase 2 of the Gujarat plant is targeted for completion by Q2 FY27. Phase 1 of the Heavy Steel Structures facility is expected to be commissioned by Q2 FY27. The Company operates six plants, including Phase 1 of the Gujarat facility, and describes itself as the second-largest integrated PEB player in India with a 6.5% market share among integrated PEB players.

Joint venture with ER Steel Inc., Canada. The Board approved entering into a joint venture agreement with ER Steel Inc. and incorporation of a JV company. Interarch will hold 76% and ER Steel 24%. Total funding required to procure land and operationalise the business is expected to be Rs. 80 crore. Engineering, detailing, manufacturing and quality compliance activities are proposed to be led by Interarch from India, while ER Steel will support market development, customer relationships, sales and marketing in the USA and Canadian markets. ER Steel must enter into a 100% offtake agreement with the JV company to purchase the total quantity of OWSJ products for distribution and sale in Canada and the USA.

Collaboration with Mold-Tek Technologies (MTTL). The collaboration focuses on global markets with emphasis on driving export orders. Interarch will handle manufacturing and logistics, while MTTL will provide detailing for PEB and structural steel projects. Interarch will pay MTTL a commission on export orders generated through MTTL’s efforts, with rates adjustable by mutual agreement. Both companies will work exclusively with each other on projects introduced by MTTL under a two-year business plan, extendable by mutual consent.

Industry fundamentals. The India PEB market is projected to grow from approximately Rs. 195 billion in FY24E to Rs. 330–340 billion by FY29P, a CAGR of 10–14%. The organised sector is expected to grow faster than the unorganised sector, with top six players having grown at an 11.4% FY19–23 CAGR versus 8.5% for the total industry and 7.0% for the rest of the industry. Growth drivers cited include increasing investments in manufacturing, industrial infrastructure, warehousing, data centres and commercial real estate; rising private sector capex; government infrastructure focus; the PLI scheme; low per-capita steel consumption in India; and a shift from RCC to PEB construction.

Financial position. The Company describes itself as debt-free with high cash reserves. Net debt was negative Rs. 182 crore in FY26. FY26 revenue from operations was Rs. 1,898.0 crore with EBITDA of Rs. 176.3 crore (9.3% margin) and PAT of Rs. 134.5 crore (7.1% margin). Repeat orders were 72% of revenue in FY26, and 3 of the top 5 customer groups have been associated with the Company for over five years.

Fundraising and corporate actions. The Board approved a stock split of existing equity shares of face value Rs. 10 each into equity shares of face value Rs. 2 each (1:5 ratio), subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals, with completion tentatively within three months of receipt of all approvals. The Board approved raising funds through QIP for an aggregate amount not exceeding Rs. 250 crore, superseding the earlier shareholder approval of March 27, 2026 for up to Rs. 100 crore. The Board also approved allotment of 45,415 equity shares pursuant to exercise of stock options under the Interarch ESOP Scheme-2023.

Dividend. The Board recommended a final dividend of Rs. 12.50 per fully paid-up ordinary share of face value Rs. 10 each (125%) for FY ended March 31, 2026, subject to shareholder approval at the 43rd AGM scheduled for September 10, 2026, with distribution on or before October 9, 2026. The record date is September 3, 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Tax proceedings: The income tax search and survey, the block assessment proceedings for April 1, 2019 to October 17, 2025, and the Section 142(1) notice create uncertainty; no demand has been raised and financial impact cannot be ascertained at this stage.
  • GST demand: The DRC-01 notice involves a tax and interest demand of Rs. 21,31,134, which the Company intends to contest.
  • Working capital intensity: Negative operating cash flow in FY26 and significant working capital consumption, alongside rising receivables and contract assets.
  • Execution risk: Time and cost overruns on contracts, and the ability to manage growth and expanded manufacturing footprint.
  • Competition: Both domestic and international competition in the PEB industry.
  • Macro and policy sensitivity: Economic growth in India and abroad, government policies and actions, interest and other fiscal costs, and fluctuations in earnings.
  • Concentration and customer risk: Revenue from top customer groups and reliance on repeat orders.
  • International expansion risk: The JV with ER Steel and the MTTL collaboration involve new geographies and partnership execution.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Capacity commissioning. Management stated Phase 1 of the Kheda, Gujarat PEB facility was commissioned in July 2026 and Phase 2 remains on track for completion by Q2 FY27; Phase 1 of the Heavy Steel Structures facility is expected to be commissioned by Q2 FY27. The Q1 FY27 presentation confirms Phase 1 commissioning and lists Phase 2 of Gujarat and the Andhra Pradesh heavy steel structures plant as under construction with Q2 FY27 targets.

Order book visibility. Management described the order book as healthy at Rs. 1,864 crore, providing strong revenue visibility. This is corroborated by the disclosed order book as of July 31, 2026 and the subsequent Rs. 128 crore order announcement of August 18, 2026.

Growth trajectory. Management expressed confidence in the long-term growth trajectory supported by industry fundamentals, expanding capacities and sustained customer demand, while noting near-term challenges from external market conditions may persist. Observed Q1 FY27 performance showed 20.7% YoY revenue growth and 24.6% YoY EBITDA growth, but flat PAT YoY and sequential declines across revenue, EBITDA and PAT.

Order selection discipline. Management stated it maintains a disciplined approach to order selection, prioritising projects aligned with execution capabilities, strategic objectives and profitability thresholds. The disclosed order wins and the Rs. 128 crore FMCG order are consistent with this stated approach, though the sequential margin contraction in Q1 FY27 (8.6% versus 10.5% in Q4 FY26) is an observed data point against the profitability emphasis.

Fundraising. The Board’s approval of a QIP of up to Rs. 250 crore supersedes the earlier approval of up to Rs. 100 crore, reflecting revised funding requirements and proposed growth initiatives. The IPO proceeds position as of June 30, 2026 showed Rs. 1,498.31 lakh unutilised, comprising Rs. 571.93 lakh towards IT infrastructure upgradation and the balance towards capital expenditure.

Tax matters. Management stated that based on the search and survey proceedings and the matters of inquiry, no material adjustments were required to the Q1 FY27 financial results, and that it will respond to the Section 142(1) notice with supporting documents within the allowed time. No assessment or demand order had been received as of the relevant dates.

Shareholder approvals. All 11 resolutions presented at the 43rd AGM held on September 10, 2026 were passed successfully, per the Scrutinizer Report and voting results.

Broker Narrative

The broker never wavered from Buy and constantly repeated the same structural risks—private-sector capex dependence, steel price volatility, and low entry barriers—but the growth narrative became more conservative. The first report’s QIP-driven ₹3,400–3,500 crore revenue potential by Q1FY28 was replaced by a ₹2,700 crore FY28 guidance in the last report, with the story shifting to a record ₹1,864 crore order book, a ₹250 crore QIP, the ER Steel JV, and new-age industry diversification. A new near-term execution headwind (Q1FY27 seasonality/monsoon/clearances) was acknowledged, and actual returns were consistently far below the predicted +35% levels.

Fears that came true

  • The flagged dependence on private-sector project execution was borne out when Q1FY27 revenue was hit by seasonality, monsoon conditions and project/site clearances, coinciding with actual returns of -17.9%, -2.4%, and +1.7% versus predicted gains of roughly +35%.

Optimism that failed

  • The early expectation that the ₹100 crore QIP would enable revenue potential of ₹3,400–3,500 crore by Q1FY28 failed to materialize, as the last report guided FY28 revenue to ₹2,700 crore instead.
