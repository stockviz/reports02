Clean Science and Technology (CLEAN) — Q1 FY27 Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical disruption to raw material supply chains. Management described the quarter as operating against “geopolitical headwinds impacting raw material supply chain and its cost.” Raw material costs in China were not impacted as much as in India.
  • Shipping vessel non-availability. Non-availability of shipping vessels delayed timely export of goods, creating supply-side challenges.
  • Production stoppage from supplier shutdown. The primary Indian supplier had to shut its facility due to non-availability of its key raw material, propylene. This caused roughly two weeks of shutdown for Clean Science, which management cited as the main reason for the year-on-year volume decline.
  • Labour and gas-related issues. Management referred to “labour issues” and “gas issues” that created another challenging period during the quarter.
  • Raw material price volatility. Management stated that while availability has improved, price volatility remains an issue, driven by crude oil price movements linked to geopolitical tensions. This is expected to affect profitability even if supply is secure.
  • Inability to fully pass on cost increases. Where long-term contracts exist, the company is respecting them and has not been able to pass on 100% of input cost increases; in some cases only a percentage was passed through.
  • Standalone revenue moderation. Standalone sales declined 6% year-on-year, attributed to lower sales volumes from supply-side headwinds, even as demand remained steady.
  • Margin compression. Standalone EBITDA margin fell to 42.7% in Q1 FY27 from 45.6% in Q4 FY26 and 46.4% in Q1 FY26. Consolidated EBITDA margin fell to 36.5% from 38.9% QoQ and 41.6% YoY. Raw material expense as a percentage of sales rose to 34.6% standalone (from 33.3% QoQ) and 39.7% consolidated (from 37.0% QoQ).
  • Capex delay. Performance Chemical 2, previously expected around September, is now expected to start in November due to labour issues. Commercialisation is guided for Q3 FY27, with revenues expected only from Q1 of the next financial year.
  • Competitive landscape in HALS. Management named BASF and Sabo in Europe and Rianlon and Suqian (Unitechem) in China as major competitors. The NOR HALS segment is currently dominated by BASF.
  • Uncontrollable supply chain risk. When asked whether supply chain continuity was assured, management stated it could not be certain, as “nobody can control” these factors.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record consolidated sales. Q1 FY27 consolidated sales reached approximately INR 264 crores, the highest in the company’s history, up 7% QoQ and 10% YoY.
  • HALS scale-up. HALS now accounts for 22% of sales. The top four legacy products’ share fell from ~85% in Q4 FY23 to ~60% in Q1 FY27, reducing product concentration. HALS volumes were ~1,000 tons in the quarter, with product mix shifting toward higher grades (HALS 770 share fell from ~50% to ~35%), improving gross margins at the subsidiary level. Average realisations moved from ~440 to ~550.
  • HALS export diversification. First-year HALS sales were entirely domestic; in Q1 FY27, exports contributed nearly 50% of HALS sales. Management stated all grades of HALS produced are now fully approved and at par quality with competitors.
  • Geneus Chem collaboration. A long-term strategic collaboration with Swiss partner Geneus Chem AG (agreement dated July 16, 2026) for manufacturing and supply of advanced-grade NOR HALS on a worldwide exclusive basis, with minimum offtake commitments and a pricing mechanism. Geneus Chem will receive warrants in Clean Fino-Chem exercisable within four years. The arrangement includes joint marketing, co-branding, defined geographies, and a technology transfer with no transfer fees or royalties cited. Plants expected to start in Q3 FY27, with capex of ~INR 25 crores. Management guided INR 300–350 crores of cumulative additional revenue over 3–4 years, with peak annual revenue potentially INR 100 crores-plus in year four. NOR HALS are described as a new market segment for the company, applicable to agricultural films in harsher environments.
  • Kemin agreement. A five-year minimum contract with existing customer Kemin (US-based, pet food formulations) covering BHA, BHT, TBHQ, and AP across its global locations. Management described Kemin as the largest buyer of some of these ingredients and expects offtake to increase 20–40%, starting within two to three months. Additional capacity will be required, and the process has been started.
  • CFCL reaching operational self-sustainability. Clean Fino-Chem has fully recovered its operating expenses and is shifting from an investing phase to a monetising phase. Capital infusion of ~INR 100 crores during the quarter took total investment in the subsidiary to ~INR 850 crores.
  • Hydroquinone and catechol stabilisation. Plant stabilisation is largely complete, customer approvals received, and commercial supply ramp-up underway, with revenue expected to build gradually from August–September.
  • European subsidiary. A wholly owned foreign subsidiary is being incorporated in the Netherlands under Clean Fino-Chem, with initial capital equivalent to EUR 50,000, for selling/distribution/trading/job work of specialty chemicals. Management indicated it would start in mid-September and cater to European demand.
  • Margin improvement expectation. Management expressed confidence that EBITDA margins will keep improving as higher-grade HALS sales grow and operating efficiencies kick in. Gross margin above 40% was described as sustainable, with no benefit from cheap raw material cited.
  • Demand environment. Management repeatedly described demand as steady, with volumes not an issue.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material price volatility flowing from crude oil and geopolitical tensions, affecting profitability even when supply is available.
  • Supply chain and shipping availability, which management stated it cannot control and which caused a two-week production stoppage in the quarter.
  • Contractual constraints on pricing, limiting the ability to pass through cost increases where long-term contracts apply.
  • Execution and timing risk on capex, evidenced by the Performance Chemical 2 delay from September to November and revenue recognition only from Q1 of the next financial year.
  • Dependence on collaboration partners for advanced HALS technology, co-branding, and geographic market allocation under the Geneus Chem arrangement.
  • Competitive intensity from established European and Chinese HALS producers, with BASF dominating the NOR segment.
  • Concentration in the customer relationship with Kemin, described as the largest buyer of some ingredients, under a five-year exclusive-style supply arrangement.
  • New product ramp risk in hydroquinone and catechol, where revenue is only expected to build gradually as production scales.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • HALS volume guidance. Management had previously indicated close to 3,000 metric tons for the year. Q1 FY27 delivered ~1,000 tons, and management stated it is “committed” to the target. Annualising the quarter implies INR 250–300 crores of HALS revenue, which management described as comfortable.
  • HALS revenue share. Management indicated HALS could be around 30–35% of overall revenue this year, against the 22% of sales reported in Q1 FY27.
  • Gross margin sustainability. Management stated the 43–45% gross margin range should be sustainable, attributing it to higher-grade mix and operational efficiencies rather than cheap raw material. Observed standalone EBITDA margin of 42.7% was below the prior-year 46.4%, with raw material cost pressure cited.
  • Performance Chemical 2 timing. Previously guided for September production; revised to November start, with commercialisation in Q3 FY27 and revenue from Q1 next year. This represents a slippage against earlier expectations.
  • Hydroquinone and catechol. Trials began around October–December, restarted in April, and are now stabilised. Management guided revenue from August–September, consistent with the stated ramp-up timeline.
  • Geneus Chem revenue. Guided at INR 300–350 crores cumulative over 3–4 years, with peak annual revenue potentially INR 100 crores-plus in year four. Plants to start Q3 FY27.
  • Kemin offtake. Guided 20–40% increase in offtake, starting within two to three months, with additional capacity already being planned.
  • Other expenses. Management stated Q1 FY27 other expenses should be treated as a sustainable number, with the Q4 FY26 elevation attributed to CSR spend and one-off provisioning.
  • Capex guidance. Management stated it has not worked out an annual capex run rate in detail and would provide it in due course.
  • Supply chain outlook. Management stated supply issues have come down and it hopes to avoid stoppages in coming quarters, while acknowledging it cannot be certain and that raw material volatility will persist.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from early optimism on low raw material costs and HALS commercialization to a later focus on West Asia-driven raw material inflation and subsidy-dependent realizations, while agrochemical demand weakness and competition persisted. The sharp reversal in raw material costs—from tailwind to headwind—was the dominant theme change, whereas sector cyclicality remained. By 2026, the thesis shifted to government NBS support and inventory-led price hikes as the primary catalysts after years of destocking-driven losses.

Fears that came true

  • Soft demand from agrochemical industry resulting in unused capacities of PBQ materialized as CLEAN suffered sustained deep losses (e.g., -43.5% to -45.7%), reflecting persistent demand weakness and capacity underutilization.
  • Customers holding onto inventory due to fear of lockdowns, leading to destocking contributed to prolonged negative returns through 2025, indicating ongoing inventory destocking and demand suppression.
  • Stiffer competition as the global economy opens up with short-term demand pressures to sustain persisted, evidenced by repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and negative actuals on bullish calls.

Optimism that failed

  • Lower raw material costs expanding gross margin to 70.5% reversed sharply, as sulfur surged 3.6x and raw material costs rose, squeezing margins.
  • Commercialization of HALS701 and HALS770 with commercial orders initiated failed to drive recovery, with the stock falling >40% repeatedly and HALS absent from the later thesis.
  • Envisaged capex for HALS expansion in Unit IV and new products did not translate into sustained volume growth, given the prolonged slump until 2026 subsidy support.
  • Strong volume growth and increased realization in Pharma & Agro Intermediates proved unsustainable, as actual returns remained deeply negative for years until price hikes aided later realizations.
