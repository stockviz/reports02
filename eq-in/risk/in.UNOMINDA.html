Uno Minda Limited — 34th Annual General Meeting Voting Analysis

Headwinds and Challenges

The 34th AGM, held on July 31, 2026 through video conference, saw all nine resolutions passed, but voting patterns reveal areas of shareholder dissent that warrant attention.

Director Re-appointment (Resolution 4): The re-appointment of Mr. Nirmal Kumar Minda as a director retiring by rotation drew the most significant opposition. While the resolution passed with 98.66% of votes in favour, public institutions holding 148,331,625 shares recorded 7,055,002 votes against — representing 5.43% of their votes polled. This was the highest level of institutional dissent across all resolutions.

Statutory Auditor Re-appointment (Resolution 6): The re-appointment of M/s S.R. Batliboi & Co. LLP as Statutory Auditors for a second tenure of five years also faced notable institutional opposition. Public institutions cast 7,215,042 votes against, amounting to 5.55% of their votes polled. Overall, the resolution passed with 98.63% support, but the concentration of dissent among institutional investors is evident.

Fundraising Resolution (Resolution 8): The approval to raise funds of up to Rs. 2,500 Crores through issue of securities saw public institutions record 517,843 votes against — 0.40% of their votes polled. While significantly lower than the dissent on Resolutions 4 and 6, it still represents institutional caution on the proposed capital raise.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Dividend Declaration (Resolution 3): Shareholders approved a final dividend of Rs. 1.75 per equity share (87.5%) and an interim dividend of Rs. 0.90 per equity share (45%, already paid) for the financial year ended March 31, 2026. This resolution received near-unanimous support, with 100% of promoter votes and 100% of public institution votes in favour, and 99.9952% of public non-institution votes in favour. The total votes against were only 152.

Fundraising Approval (Resolution 8): The approval to raise up to Rs. 2,500 Crores through issue of securities in one or more tranches signals the company’s intent to pursue growth opportunities. The resolution passed with 99.9994% of votes in favour overall, with promoters voting 100% in favour and public institutions recording 99.6015% support.

Auditor Continuity (Resolution 6): The re-appointment of M/s S.R. Batliboi & Co. LLP for a second five-year tenure provides continuity in financial oversight.

Cost Auditor Ratification (Resolution 7): The ratification of M/s. Jitender Navneet & Co. as Cost Auditors passed with 99.9994% support, with only 3,292 votes against.

Key Risks

Institutional Dissent Concentration: The 5.43% and 5.55% institutional votes against Resolutions 4 and 6 respectively indicate that a meaningful minority of institutional shareholders have reservations about certain governance or operational matters. While these levels are not sufficient to block ordinary resolutions, they represent a governance signal.

Capital Raise Execution Risk: The approval to raise up to Rs. 2,500 Crores introduces execution and dilution considerations. The resolution passed with 99.6015% institutional support, but the 0.40% institutional dissent suggests some caution about the timing, structure, or terms of the proposed fundraising.

Shareholder Participation: Of 197,732 total shareholders on the cut-off date (July 24, 2026), only 10 promoters/promoter group members and 471 public shareholders attended the meeting through video conferencing. Total votes polled represented 91.41% of outstanding shares, with public non-institutions participating at only 9.24% of their holdings.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from an initial cautious Hold focused on near-term FAME subsidy disruption, QoQ margin/PAT declines and capex drag, to a Buy driven by structural growth, order momentum and capacity commercialisation. Persisting themes are cost/margin pressure, capacity expansion and EV/order-book optimism, while the stance shifted to confidence in 1.5x-2x industry outperformance, premiumisation and a formal FY27 margin guidance of 11%.

Fears that came true

  • The early margin-contraction risk recurred: Q1FY27 input cost headwinds cut EBITDA margin ~100 bps QoQ to 10.3%, and recent actual returns were disappointing (e.g. 2026-08-05 Buy predicted +16.0% but actual +1.8%).
  • The short-term capex/fixed-cost drag materialised as expansion and commercialisation costs kept margins below the earlier 12% aspiration, coinciding with weak returns such as the 2025-11-14 Hold actual of -2.5%.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of stabilised commodity prices and easing chip constraints failed: the last report still flags volatile raw material prices, commodity inflation and domestic auto slowdown risk.
  • The long-term ~12% EBITDA margin aspiration failed to materialise, as management guided to only 11% ±50 bps for FY27 and Q1FY27 actual margin was 10.3%.
