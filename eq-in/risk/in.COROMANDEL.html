Headwinds and Challenges

  • Monsoon deficit and delayed sowing: India saw a 23% deficit in rainfall against the long period average in the quarter, with June described as one of the driest months. The deficit moderated to 17% as of the call, with recovery in Gujarat and Central regions. Reservoir levels stood at 34% of capacity versus 57% a year earlier, and southern reservoirs at 28% versus 65%. Pulses acreage dropped 15%, oilseeds 6% and cotton 6%, while paddy acreage returned to normal. Fertilizer offtake moderated, and farmer sentiment remained cautious.
  • Elevated raw material costs: Global prices of phosphoric acid, ammonia and sulphur stayed elevated due to geopolitical uncertainty and supply disruptions. Phosphoric acid for Q2 settled at $1,700 versus $1,360 in Q1. Sulphur held high against initial expectations, with structural demand shifts (including EV battery-related nickel leaching demand from Indonesia) cited. Management stated that at a phosphoric acid price of $1,700, sulphur at around $800 would be needed for optimal backward integration economics.
  • Subsidy rates not fully compensating costs: The government announced roughly a 10% increase in subsidy rates across Nitrogen, Phosphatic and Sulphur nutrients under the NBS regime, but these rates did not factor in the Middle East crisis and do not fully compensate for the subsequent rise in global raw material costs. Industry is engaging with the Department of Fertilizers for a review. Management noted that for N and P, significant correction is required, and that the six-month average price prevailing prior to April has no relevance to what was received under NBS.
  • Impact on industry production and imports: Subdued subsidy rates for NP/NPK impacted industry production and imports during the quarter, which were down 21% and 38% respectively. Management stated that if subsidy rates are not revised, it does not make economic sense to produce, and this could impact Q2 as well.
  • Deliberate production moderation: Coromandel moderated fertilizer production to 6.9 lakh tons versus 8.4 lakh tons (about 72% capacity utilization), prioritizing inventory optimization amid volatile raw material markets. Phos acid production was moderated to 1.2 lakh tons, in line with last year, despite the new plant commissioning, because higher sulphur prices led to optimized sulphur usage and moderate sulphuric acid plant operations to extend sulphur availability.
  • Volume moderation and margin pressure: Fertilizer sales volumes moderated 9% to 10 lakh tons versus the prior year period. Fertilizer business margins were under pressure from the sudden spurt in ammonia and sulphur prices not factored into announced subsidy rates.
  • Muted crop protection domestic demand: The domestic crop protection market was muted in the early kharif season due to delayed sowings. Global agri commodity prices are softening, farmer affordability has declined, and farmers are seeking cheaper alternatives, with specialties facing pushback.
  • NACL performance drag: NACL Industries saw lower export volumes and price pressure, with revenues moderate at INR 383 crores. Price moderation in molecules sold to global MNCs moderated revenue.
  • Nano product bans: Some state governments, including UP and Maharashtra, banned certain nano products from fertilizer companies, moderating growth despite Coromandel holding nearly 60% market share in Nano DAP.
  • GST order: A Joint Commissioner (Appeals) order dated August 20, 2026 confirmed a demand for FY 2018-19 under Section 73 of CGST/JKGST for tax of Rs. 1,23,51,441 along with penalty of Rs. 12,35,144 and applicable interest, mainly on alleged excess ITC availment due to mismatch between GSTR-3B and GSTR-2A. The company believes it has a strong case and has appealed before the GST Appellate Tribunal, Jammu, stating no significant impact on financials, operations or other activities.
  • Higher depreciation: Depreciation rose on account of mining overburden amortization (about INR 30-odd crores), PA-SA capex (INR 16-18 crores) and NACL intangible amortization (about INR 20-21 crores), not from additional capex alone.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Policy initiatives: The government approved a national investment policy for urea, potentially facilitating 9-10 million tons of additional domestic urea capacity over 8 years across 7 proposed projects. An INR 37,500 crores coal and lignite gasification scheme is expected to support domestic syngas-based feedstocks and progressively reduce dependence on imported ammonia and other energy inputs over the medium term. A pilot of the National Fertilizer sales framework introduced QR code-enabled traceability integrating farmer, land and sales data, which management described as potentially transformative, enabling a shift to transparent, data-driven, nutrient stewardship-based farming and potentially direct benefit transfer.
  • Market share gains: Despite lower volumes, Coromandel increased primary market share to 22% from 18% a year earlier. Point-of-sales volume rose 13% to 7.9 lakh tons, raising market share to 16%. Consumption was encouraging due to lower MRP stocks in the channel.
  • SSP premiumization: SSP volumes grew 19%, with differentiated products GroPlus and Urea-SSP constituting more than 50% of total SSP sales. Steady-state contribution from SSP was indicated at INR 2,500-3,000 per metric ton.
  • Newly commissioned backward integration: The sulphuric acid plant and phos acid plant at Kakinada were commissioned and stabilized. Sulphuric acid achieved rated capacity and generated power as envisaged. With the new PA-SA plant, 60% of acid requirements are covered. Management indicated average EBITDA in the nutrient business should rise from INR 5,000 to INR 6,500 per metric ton in a steady state once normalcy restores.
  • Granulation and capacity projects: The brownfield fertilizer granulation project adding 7.5 lakh tons annual capacity is on track for Q4 FY27 commissioning. Management plans to sell 50% of that volume through own retail outlets, with markets seeded in UP, Rajasthan, MP and Chhattisgarh for the balance. Post-commissioning, the company plans to go up to 4 million tons of fertilizers. The T-MAP plant at Kakinada and seaweed granulation plant are progressing.
  • Senegal/BMCC: Rock phosphate mining produced over 1.1 lakh tons in the quarter, broadly in line with plan, operating close to target volumes of 5 lakh tons. The company is evaluating an SSP facility in Senegal (estimated $5-6 million for 100,000-150,000 tons) using reject rocks for domestic and export markets. BMCC provides raw material security for Kakinada plus value capture on rock and conversion to acid.
  • Crop Protection record performance: Revenue grew 20% year-on-year to INR 870 crores and EBIT increased 44% to INR 159 crores, driven by exports and B2B sales, improved product mix, traction for key molecules and ability to pass on higher raw material costs in export markets. New products were 32% of domestic B2C sales versus 23% last year, with 3 new products introduced in the quarter. Capacity expansion of key molecules is on track for September commissioning. Fluoro chemistry capabilities are being advanced with a launch facility at Ankleshwar. CDMO engagement with leading global innovators continued, with focus on Latin American markets.
  • Retail business: Revenue grew 85% year-on-year, with 76% of stores profitable versus 61% a year earlier. 22 new stores were added, taking the network to over 1,200 outlets, alongside digital and omnichannel expansion.
  • Specialty nutrients and organics: Strong Q1 performance aided by higher offtake in organics and micronutrients. Three new products launched across water-soluble and sulphur nutrition. The business has been growing top line 25-30% with 20% EBITDA margin. Bentonite sulphur capacity was doubled, taking total specialty nutrients capacity to about 88,000 tons.
  • Bio products: Steady performance with growth in domestic and export markets. Two new products developed in biostimulant and microbial categories. White label products previously sourced externally will now be manufactured in-house, capturing the full value chain. Direct retailing trade team established; feed additives and plant extracts/microbial spaces being explored.
  • Nano business: Sales volume grew 2% year-on-year, with nearly 60% market share in Nano DAP. Exports were initiated during the quarter with registrations secured in a few markets.
  • Drones: Drone spraying services gained traction. Coromandel purchased close to 100-150 drones in the quarter and aims to expand its fleet to 500 during the year. Dhaksha focused on agri-drone variants and applied for type certifications of new drones. Higher-capacity drones capable of fertilizer application are being evaluated.
  • Sustainability and R&D: Responsible Care Certification received. A corporate research center is being set up at IIT Chennai Research Park focused on Nano, Biologicals and Advanced Chemistries.
  • Subsidy receipts: The company received INR 1,392 crores towards subsidy claims versus INR 1,300 crores in Q1 last year, with government prompt in clearing dues. Subsidy outstanding as of June 30 was about INR 3,254 crores, with an additional INR 568 crores received in July.
  • Capital allocation: About INR 7,000 crores invested over the last 3-4 years across organic and inorganic opportunities. Normal sustainable capex indicated at INR 300 crores, with flexibility for good opportunities. No immediate fertilizer capacity addition planned; focus on generating cash from recent investments. Crop protection investment to be directed toward marketing, brand building and channel network rather than asset creation, leveraging spare AI capacity at NACL. Purified phos acid for battery chemicals is under evaluation. Additional land is being sought with the Andhra Pradesh government for future expansion.

Key Risks

  • Continued shortfall or uneven distribution of monsoon rainfall affecting acreages, offtake and farmer purchasing power.
  • Sustained elevation of phosphoric acid, ammonia and sulphur prices, with sulphur described as holding high against expectations and potentially taking a while to soften.
  • Delay or inadequacy of NBS subsidy rate revision for kharif, with management stating production may not be economically viable otherwise and Q2 could be impacted.
  • Demand destruction if NPK prices remain high relative to DAP, with management noting that low-P products like 20:20 priced at INR 2,100 per bag versus DAP at INR 1,350 per bag does not reflect balanced nutrition needs.
  • Pressure on the overall government subsidy bill, with higher urea subsidy due to gas prices and full DAP compensation creating pushback on NP and NPK.
  • State-level bans on nano products in UP and Maharashtra.
  • Price moderation and export pressure in NACL’s molecules sold to global MNCs.
  • Reliance on continued production and imports over the next two months to augment carryover inventory for a smooth rabi season.
  • GST demand of Rs. 1,23,51,441 plus penalty and interest for FY 2018-19, currently under appeal.
  • Foreign currency exposure, with management indicating not more than 5-6% of overall EBITDA attributable to foreign currency on a netting principle.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • EBITDA per ton: Management guided that average EBITDA in the nutrient business should rise from INR 5,000 to INR 6,500 per metric ton in a steady state once the new plants are commercialized and normalcy restores, while explicitly stating this may not happen in the current quarter due to the spurt in input prices. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated EBITDA was INR 761 crores, a 3% degrowth from INR 782 crores, with the decrease attributed to higher input costs from the West Asia crisis not fully commensurate with NBS rate revisions.
  • NACL margins: At acquisition, management discussed EBITDA moving from 3-4% in the first stage to 7-8% and then 10-11%. Observed Q1 FY27 NACL EBITDA rose 9% to INR 41 crores with margin improving to 11% from 8%, which management said is in line with what was envisaged at acquisition. Management added that with the current product portfolio, a reasonable EBITDA level has been reached, and sustaining this level is the best achievable for NACL unless new products are introduced, which would take 2-3 years; efficiency initiatives could add 1-2%.
  • Crop Protection capacity expansion: Guided for commissioning by September (Q2 FY27). The press release confirmed the Sarigam key molecule capacity expansion remains on track for commissioning during Q2 FY27.
  • Granulation project: Guided for Q4 FY27 commissioning, stated as progressing well and on track.
  • Phos acid and sulphuric acid plants: Commissioned with operations stabilized; sulphuric acid achieved rated capacity and generated power as envisaged, while phosphoric acid operations are getting stabilized with new technology being implemented for optimum rock utilization.
  • Subsidy rate review: Management expressed hope for updated NBS rates for kharif and a positive outcome from engagement with the Department of Fertilizers. As of the call, no revision had been received, and management stated the correction sought is for the ongoing kharif season.
  • Price increases: Management stated 25-30% price increases across grades had already been taken, and that beyond this, subsidy correction is required. Management stated it does not want to increase MRP beyond the current level.
  • Capex: Normal sustainable capex guided at INR 300 crores, with openness to opportunities offering reasonable returns. No immediate fertilizer capacity addition planned; focus on realizing value from the approximately INR 7,000 crores invested over the last 3-4 years.
  • Drones: Fleet expansion target of 500 drones during the year, against 100-150 purchased in the quarter.
  • Financial performance: Consolidated total income grew 15% to INR 8,215 crores from INR 7,126 crores, with the increase mainly from higher fertilizer realizations and growth in the non-subsidy business. Consolidated EBITDA declined 3% to INR 761 crores. Net profit was INR 382 crores versus INR 502 crores. Standalone total income grew 10% to INR 7,792 crores, while standalone EBITDA declined 15% to INR 626 crores and PAT declined 26% to INR 377 crores. The subsidy business accounted for 77% of revenue and 48% of EBITDA in the quarter. Last year’s Q1 results did not include NACL, which became a subsidiary effective August 8, 2025.
  • AGM: The 64th Annual General Meeting was held on July 23, 2026, where all resolutions regarding financial statements, dividend declarations, director re-appointment, auditor appointments and cost auditor remuneration were approved by shareholders with the requisite majority.
  • Board approvals: On July 23, 2026, the Board approved conversion of loan amounting to about Rs. 108 crores to equity shares of Coromandel Chemicals Limited at an issue price of Rs. 39.95 per share, and restructuring of loans amounting to USD 9.70 million given to BMCC into equity or preference shares with optionality or such other instruments as may be mutually agreed.

Broker Narrative

The initial thesis was built on stable fertilizer margins, backward integration, product-mix upgrades, and market-share gains, with near-term worries about subsidy cuts, export weakness, and high inventory. By the 2026 report, the narrative had shifted to raw-material inflation and subsidy lags squeezing fertilizer margins, while growth was increasingly driven by crop protection, NACL/CDMO, retail, nano/specialty, and mining. Backward integration remained a persistent theme but was reframed as a long-term structural hedge rather than an immediate margin stabilizer. Broker calls stayed largely Buy despite intermittent Holds and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning on NBS subsidy-rate reductions materialized, as later NBS revisions lagged raw-material cost spikes and compressed fertilizer EBITDA/mt and overall operating margins, causing an earnings miss.
  • Lower profitability recurred: after Q4FY23 PAT declined 9.5% YoY, 1QFY27 adjusted PAT fell 25% YoY and consolidated EBITDA declined 3%, missing broker estimates.
  • The early risk of rising finance costs was echoed by working-capital stress caused by elevated input costs and delayed subsidy support, pressuring near-term earnings.

Optimism that failed

  • The management’s stable-margin guidance of Rs 5,500–6,000 EBITDA/MT did not hold, as subsidy compensation lagged raw-material inflation and compressed fertilizer margins.
  • The aggressive capex plan was only partly delivered: unusually high sulphur prices delayed capacity expansion in bentonite sulphur and specialty-nutrient granulation.
