RICOAUTO — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Cost pressures in Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) - Profitability was impacted by elevated operating costs. Other operating expenses were higher by approximately INR24 crores, primarily due to air freight and sorting costs of around INR13 crores, along with inflationary pressures across manpower, power, fuel and gas, tools and consumables. - A lag in raw material price settlements — particularly for aluminum, where prices increased significantly — resulted in an estimated impact of approximately INR10 crores during the quarter. - Aluminum prices rose sharply: from around INR222 per kg in Q1 FY26 to approximately INR349 per kg in Q1 FY27 for a specific grade (an increase of about 57%). Management noted the raw material settlement lag for the quarter was INR3.3 crores on a standalone basis, down from INR12 crores in the prior quarter, as 75% of customer value has converted to real-time settlements.

Shipping disruption and air freight - Disruption in global shipping routes increased ocean freight transit time from 5 weeks to 9 weeks (sometimes 8–10 weeks), forcing greater reliance on air shipments to maintain supply continuity. - Because Rico is a single source supplier for new programs with customers including BMW and Toyota, it resorted to air freight to keep customer lines running. Quality correlation issues with customer measuring instruments also put some parts on hold, and longer time at sea caused rust issues requiring packing changes and sorting in the U.S. - Management stated the war situation escalated after the early-June call, which is why the market was not pre-informed of the air freight impact.

Margin and profit impact - Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue was INR755 crores versus INR543 crores in Q1 FY26 (growth of around 39%). EBITDA was INR34.8 crores, an EBITDA margin of 4.6%. PAT was a loss of INR3.4 crores versus a profit of INR16.7 crores in the corresponding quarter last year. - Management acknowledged the current cost pressure and timing of customer settlements are likely to keep margins under pressure in the near term.

Other challenges - Labor cost in Haryana increased by almost 40% following a government revision, described as a major impact. - Management noted railways progress is slow, though surely progressing; RDSO approvals are in a 2–3 month cycle. - The company is curbing investments after its current project set, choosing to stabilize and run assets to full capacity before taking up larger projects.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record revenue and growth momentum - Rico delivered its highest ever quarterly revenue in Q1 FY27, with continued momentum across core automotive businesses. - Revenue outlook was raised: management guided to surpassing INR3,200 crores in FY27, with a stated target of around INR3,250 crores (up from the previously stated around INR3,000 crores). July 2026 turnover was close to INR300 crores. - Quarterly revenue trajectory indicated: Q2 crossing approximately INR840 crores, Q3 crossing INR850 crores, and Q4 about INR900 crores.

New programs and platforms - Around 55 new programs are in the launch phase, of which 28 are already launched and in ramp-up. These are long-term programs for Toyota, Ford and BMW with program life of more than 7–8 years, described as highly profitable, with Rico as single source supplier. - Businesses participate across ICE, hybrid and EV platforms. The Hosur facility supports hybrid and EV-related programs with key OEMs and is expected to begin commercial production in September 2026. New launches are also taking place in Chennai, Pathredi, Bawal and North India, with some starting in February–March next year.

Diversification: railways, defense, machine tools - Railway and defense businesses are progressing, supported by increasing localization, infrastructure investments and government focus. RDSO approvals are progressing and supplies have started. Defense work includes computerized shooting/practice ranges in containers (30, 40 and 60 footers) for training, with 200 ranges to be supplied this year, plus consultancy for baffle ranges. - The company has begun selling CNC machines externally (previously made only for captive use), targeting 100 machines this year, with expected revenue of about INR35 crores to INR40 crores. This revenue is not included in the INR3,250 crores guidance, nor is railway revenue.

Cost recovery and normalization - Air freight costs are expected to peak in Q2 as inventory reaches desired levels; the temporary air shipment requirement is expected to cease with shipments returning to normal sea freight from Q3 onwards. - One customer has agreed to pay freight amounting to almost 50% of what was incurred, which will be accounted for as and when received. Price increase requests have been made to overseas and domestic customers for inflation, rupee weakness, oil and gas, imported materials like carbides, and labor costs. - Progressive realization of customer price revisions and cost pass-throughs, together with efficiency initiatives, are expected to support progressive improvement in profitability from Q3 onwards. Management remains confident of exiting the year near targeted full-year margins and of achieving a 12% margin target.

Capacity and efficiency - Focus on high-tonnage aluminum die casting (machines above 1,000 tons, up to 2,700 tons), with about 7–8 such machines. Productivity improvements, redeployment of CNC machines into newer projects, and reduced rejections are contributing. - Management stated approximately INR4,000 crores of revenue is achievable with the existing setup without taking up a major project. - Digital transformation initiatives include AI use for design, die design, manufacturing capability and productivity, and connecting equipment for monitoring and efficient machine utilization, already implemented successfully on a couple of lines.

3. Key Risks

  • Shipping and geopolitical risk: Continuation or escalation of the war and shipping disruption could sustain elevated air freight and freight rates; management noted that if something else happens in Iran, it would not be in their control.
  • Commodity price risk: Aluminum price volatility; management does not hedge aluminum prices because prices are decided and announced by customers. A U.S. decision to continue the war could cause another spike.
  • Settlement timing risk: The timing of customer price revisions and cost pass-throughs varies by customer and program; recovery of air freight and sorting costs is under negotiation and not assured in full.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk: Flawless execution and progressive scaling of the 55 new programs per customer ramp-up plans is a stated focus area.
  • Margin risk: Cost pressures and settlement timing are likely to keep margins under pressure in the near term; achieving full-year margin targets depends on customer revisions and efficiency gains.
  • Regulatory/approval risk: Railway business depends on RDSO approvals, which are in a 2–3 month cycle.
  • Capex discipline risk: After the current 54 projects, the company plans to invest only where absolutely necessary and stabilize before larger projects.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Guidance given and performance observed - In the prior call (early June 2026), management had guided to 10%+ EBITDA. Q1 FY27 actual EBITDA margin was 4.6%. Management explained that a major portion of the air freight occurred after that call, in the later part of June, when the situation escalated, and that the increase in transit time from 7 weeks to 9 weeks (sometimes 8–10 weeks) was not factored. - Management guided to around INR3,000 crores for the year, then raised it to surpassing INR3,200 crores and stated around INR3,250 crores. Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue of INR755 crores and July 2026 turnover close to INR300 crores are consistent with this trajectory. - Management guided that Q2 margins would be better than Q1 but Q2 would still include some air freight; Q3 onwards expected return to normal, with Q2 PAT better and Q3 back to normal. Q2 revenue was guided to approximately INR840 crores. - Management stated it expects to exit the current year near targeted full-year margins through customer price revisions and operating efficiency, and reaffirmed a 12% margin target. - On the 75% customer conversion to real-time raw material settlements, the observed lag impact fell to INR3.3 crores in Q1 FY27 from INR12 crores in the prior quarter, consistent with the stated progress; the balance 25% is still to be onboarded. - On railways, management had previously guided to a potential INR100 crores revenue segment; in Q1 FY27, railway and defense revenue was indicated in the region of about INR5 crores to INR7 crores, with exact figures to be shared later. Management described progress as slow but surely there. - On CNC machine sales, management stated it has already started delivering, has sold more than it is making, and targets 100 machines this year (about INR35 crores to INR40 crores revenue), not factored into the INR3,250 crores guidance. - On land held for sale (Haridwar, approximately INR10 crores, 2-acre land), management stated complete payment would be received by December 30, to be used for repayment. Other land bank discussions are active but not yet concluded. - Capex guidance for FY27 and FY28 was not quantified; management said it would share the figures later, and stated that for one year it will invest only where absolutely necessary.

Annual General Meeting (held September 8, 2026) - All 4 resolutions set out in the notice of the 43rd AGM were passed by members with the requisite majority, including adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements, declaration of a dividend of Re. 0.55 per equity share, appointment of Shri Samarth Kapur, and ratification of remuneration for cost auditors.

Q1 FY27 results approval - The Board of Directors approved the unaudited standalone and consolidated financial results for the first quarter ended June 30, 2026, along with limited review reports from auditors B S R & Co. LLP, at its meeting held on August 12, 2026. The results documents include a clarification letter regarding minor submission deficiencies.
