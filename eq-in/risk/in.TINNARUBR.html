TINNARUBR — Q1 FY27 Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

West Asia conflict and bitumen supply: The West Asia conflict caused bitumen supply shortages and elevated prices. This impacted the Bitumen Emulsion business through temporary supply shortages and subdued market demand. It also raised raw material costs across segments.

Consumer segment decline: Consumer segment volumes declined 20% during the quarter due to three factors: a sharp increase in raw material prices following the West Asia conflict; import-export disruptions delaying availability of imported raw materials to the turfing industry; and a nearly threefold increase in binder and synthetic grass prices, which slowed track and turfing project execution.

Subdued road construction: Overall road construction activity remained subdued, though contractors accelerated bituminous road surfacing layer works.

Oman operations: Tyre crushing volumes in Oman remained temporarily subdued due to the West Asia conflict. Higher inventory at Global Recycle was driven by elevated shipping freight costs impacting exports and lower raw material consumption by GCC customers due to subdued export activity. Performance was impacted by elevated raw material costs, putting pressure on revenue and margins in the last few quarters.

Sequential volume moderation: India’s tyre crushing volumes moderated 10% sequentially due to lower crumb requirements following softer consumer segment demand.

International project delays: The second phase of the South Africa project (full-scale tyre recycling) had timelines impacted by supply chain disruptions arising from the West Asia conflict. The Saudi Arabia project’s construction start (mid-FY27) is subject to normalization of the situation in the Middle East, and management revised the project implementation timeline in light of evolving geopolitical uncertainties in West Asia and the GCC region.

Regulatory observations: NSE sought clarification on deficiencies in the financial results filed May 22, 2026 — segment details not submitted for consolidated results, and the financial results not being in the SEBI-prescribed format with the signing Director’s name and DIN not mentioned. The company clarified it operates as a single reportable segment and that Mr. Subodh Kumar Sharma (DIN: 08947098) signed the results.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Rubberized bitumen demand: The West Asia conflict causing bitumen supply shortages and elevated prices created stronger demand for rubberized bitumen in road infrastructure projects, as rubberized bitumen is an inexpensive option. The long-term outlook for Rubberized Bitumen remains strong, supported by the Government’s focus on reducing import dependence.

Infrastructure segment: Recorded 33% volume growth and 7% revenue growth YoY in Q1 FY27. Secured a 15,000 MT rubberized bitumen processing order scheduled for execution during FY27.

Industrial segment: Achieved 27% YoY volume growth and 58% revenue growth in Q1 FY27, driven by value-added products MRP and RR. Delivered 46% export volume growth despite global economic headwinds, supported by a healthy order pipeline. Rising raw material costs and increasing ESG focus within the tyre manufacturing industry continue to drive demand.

MRP and RR production: MRP and RR volumes grew 28% and 37% YoY respectively. The ongoing 3,500 MTPA MRP capacity expansion is on track for commissioning by Q3 FY27, increasing total MRP capacity to 20,000 MTPA.

Feedstock flexibility: Over the last two years, Tinna expanded capability to process all types of ELT tyres, enhancing feedstock flexibility and resulting in 10–15% raw material cost savings.

TPO and rCB: The Tyre Pyrolysis Oil facility at Varale commenced trial runs in Q1 FY27, with commercial sales expected in Q2 FY27 and stabilization by Q3 FY27. Recovered Carbon Black production is scheduled to commence in Q3 FY27, with commercial sales in Q4 FY27.

PCMB business: With initial capacity of 6,000 TPA operating at 82% utilization, additional capacity of 12,000 TPA at Gannaur (Sonipat), Haryana, was commissioned in Q1 FY27. Revenue grew threefold from Rs. 4 Cr. in Q1 FY26 to Rs. 12 Cr. in Q1 FY27. The division contributed around 8% to Q1 FY27 turnover and targets approximately 8–10% annual revenue contribution in FY27. Registered on the Government portal as an authorized recycler, enabling EPR credit generation from plastic recycling from 1st April 2026.

Steel segment: Revenue remained stable YoY despite a slight decline in volumes, supported by improved realizations. Appointed as Exclusive Authorized Distributor for Zibo TAA Metal Technology Co., Ltd., China for steel abrasives distribution across India, with customer transition starting Q4 FY27. Steel abrasives business expected to achieve ~30% volume growth in FY27.

Global expansion: Established wholly owned subsidiary Tinna Rubber Chile SpA in Santiago, Chile, to strengthen global ELT sourcing. South Africa Phase 1 capex completed with breakeven expected by end of Q2 FY27; initial crumb rubber production line planned at 9,000 MTPA. Saudi Arabia plans for 24,000 MT per annum tyre recycling capacity, with a 13,000-square-metre plot allocated. Oman plant running at 78% capacity utilization, contributing approx INR 9 Cr revenue in Q1 FY27, with improved EBITDA margin of 8.53%.

Sustainability and ESG: FY26 TÜV-verified Life Cycle Assessment validated over 10.37 million kg of CO₂ emissions reduction. Renewable energy contributed 51% of total power consumption in Q1 FY27, backed by 3x+ expansion in renewable capacity (1.23 MW to 4.48 MW). Rooftop solar commissioned at Gummidipoondi (999 kWp, June 2026) and Varale (2,218 kWp, July 2026).

Vision 2029: Targets 10 locations, revenue of INR 1,000 Cr, revenue CAGR of 25%+, profitability growth CAGR of 33%+, EBITDA margin of 18%+, and ROCE of 30%+.

TP Buildtech: Tinna owns 49.42% in TP Buildtech, which specializes in concrete waterproofing admixture, cement admixture, and superplasticizer admixture. Manufacturing at Kolkata commenced July 2025 and stabilized by end of FY26. New products introduced including curing compound, shuttering oil, and SNF admixtures, with 3 new product lines being added.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical uncertainty in West Asia and the GCC region impacting supply chains, logistics, raw material availability, and project timelines.
  • Bitumen supply shortages and elevated prices affecting the Bitumen Emulsion business and raw material costs.
  • Import-export disruptions delaying availability of imported raw materials.
  • Elevated shipping freight costs impacting exports.
  • Subdued road construction activity and subdued market demand.
  • Global economic headwinds affecting export markets.
  • Rising raw material costs pressuring revenue and margins.
  • Project execution timelines subject to normalization of Middle East situation.
  • Regulatory compliance deficiencies observed by NSE in financial results filings.
