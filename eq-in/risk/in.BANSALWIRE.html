BANSALWIRE — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Geopolitical disruption and gas supply: The quarter began with added complexity from ongoing geopolitical tension in West Asia, which created near-term volatility across global supply chains and caused a temporary disruption in the availability of natural gas. Profitability in the first half of the quarter was impacted by higher input costs and operational disruptions.

Input cost escalation: In the first 45 days of the quarter, consumable prices — mainly gas, packaging and other consumables — rose sharply, by about INR 5,000 per tonne on a blended basis. Gas costs tripled at some plants and rose by almost 100% at others, averaging about 1.5x higher. Management noted that gas prices remained escalated even at the time of the call, with no decrease seen.

Margin absorption on committed orders: Because the company carries 30–40 days of fixed-rate orders, the sudden cost increase could not be passed through immediately. Management consciously chose not to renegotiate committed orders, absorbing the cost. Blended EBITDA fell from about INR 7 per kg to INR 2 per kg during this period, a hit of roughly INR 2 per kg. Blended margin for the quarter came to about INR 4–4.5 per kg.

Subdued demand and de-stocking: The quarter witnessed subdued demand and some de-stocking at the customer end, which prevented volume from growing at the 20% rate. Q1 is also seasonally a softer quarter, and Q2 and Q3 are generally weaker in infrastructure.

Raw material cost disadvantage versus China: For speciality products, Indian raw material prices are INR 10–15 per kg higher than those available to Chinese manufacturers. Management stated this represents about 50% of the margin and makes exports non-lucrative, confining the target market for these products to India.

Greenfield ramp-up hiccups: The Dadri facility, a greenfield project, faced initial hiccups, though management stated these are now cleared and a good ramp-up is underway.

GST matter (now closed): A wholly owned subsidiary, Bansal Steel & Power Limited, had received a GST notice in Form GST DRC-07 imposing a penalty of Rs. 50,99,895, tax liability of Rs. 57,77,896 and interest of Rs. 89,94,666 for FY 2017-18. A subsequent rectification reduced the GST liability to NIL and raised an interest demand of Rs. 16,88,804. By order dated July 25, 2026, the Joint Commissioner of State Tax (Appeals), Rohtak, accepted BSPL’s appeal and reduced the interest demand to NIL, closing the case with no financial, operational or other business impact.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Recovery within the quarter: Conditions improved meaningfully in the latter half of the quarter. Through operational recalibration and increased reliance on alternative energy sources, production levels were progressively restored. From about May 15, the company was back to INR 7–8 per kg margins, and new orders booked from April 1 onward carried escalated prices while maintaining margins.

Return to normal operations: Operating capacity stands at around 680,000 tonnes, and the company stated it is back to normal levels of operations with steady and comfortable margins. Demand is back and the company is operating at around 75% utilization.

Steel Cord milestone: The Steel Cord business received its first trial order from one of India’s leading tyre manufacturers. Domestic demand in this segment is largely met through imports. The customer skipped the field trial stage and moved directly to bulk trial after being pleased with sample approval results. Four trials are envisaged, each taking about two to three months, implying a possible six-to-eight-month timeline. More trial orders are expected within the same quarter from other customers. A pilot project of 20,000 tonnes per year has been put up, and the company describes itself as the first Indian company to get started in this product.

IHT and OHT wires: The IHT wire business has been ahead of initial expectations, with customer approvals progressing well — 80% of customers have already given approvals, with the remaining 20% in advanced stages. IHT and OHT combined have 9,000 tonnes of capacity, rising to 15,000 tonnes with OHT commissioning, expected shortly/by the end of the year. The company is targeting 50% capacity utilization in IHT next month and optimum utilization within the year. Break-even is expected around 60% utilization, with decent margins at 70–80%. Targeted EBITDA is INR 10–20 per kg once optimum utilization is reached.

B2C expansion: A new range of steel wire products for the Farming, Fencing and Poultry segment was introduced, contributing approximately 10% of sales in the quarter. The B2C segment sits within the low-carbon side, which is 50–55% of total volume. The ambition is for 50% or more of the low-carbon business to come from B2C, equivalent to about 25% of total sales on a blended basis. B2C margins are 20–30% higher EBITDA per tonne than B2B in the low carbon front. B2C sales doubled last year, though from a lower base. Q2 and Q3 are seasonally soft for B2C, with Q1 and Q4 better.

Speciality portfolio vision: The company’s vision is to reach 2 lakh tonnes of production in speciality wire within four to five years. Current speciality capacity is about 35,000 tonnes (20,000 tonnes Steel Cord pilot, 9,000 tonnes IHT, rising to 15,000 tonnes IHT+OHT). On 2 lakh tonnes, management indicated an ability to maintain INR 600–800 crore of EBITDA on an investment of INR 2,000–2,500 crore. Even at 2 lakh tonnes, the company does not expect more than 45–50% market share, with 60–65% of the market currently served by imports.

Diversification and market share: No segment accounts for more than 25% of total sales and no customer more than 3–4%. The industry grows at 7–8% while the company has grown at 20% over 20 years, with growth split roughly into three equal parts: growing with regular customers, existing customers, and new products. The R&D team develops 20–25 new SKUs monthly, with a target of 250 new products annually.

Demand across sectors: Demand is visible from almost all sectors, exports have started doing well, and automotive remains strong.

Capacity runway: The company started the year with 6.8 lakh tonnes of capacity and 60,000 tonnes that can be commissioned whenever required. The strategy is to start each year with 25% excess capacity to support 20–25% growth. Historically the company has maintained 85–90% capacity utilization, described as its sweet spot for capital efficiency. More than 50% of equipment is manufactured in-house, giving flexibility to plan capacity only six months in advance.

Sanand expansion: Expansion has already begun at the Sanand facility with wire capex of about 90,000 tonnes. The facility will not be disposed of; excess land may lead to a call on partial sale.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical and energy risk: Continued West Asia tension and elevated oil prices could sustain gas cost escalation and supply chain volatility. Management was asked directly how growth and margins would fare if the war persisted for several more quarters.
  • Demand risk: Subdued demand and customer de-stocking weighed on volume growth in Q1; Q2 and Q3 are seasonally weaker in infrastructure.
  • Margin timing risk: The natural hedge of 30–40 days of inventory covered by fixed-rate orders protects against commodity cycles but exposed the company to a sudden cost spike. A sharp, sudden price movement in either direction could produce one-time gains or losses, though management stated any inventory loss is passed on to customers and quarterly timing differences even out.
  • Speciality approval and commercialization risk: Steel Cord qualification is rigorous and typically takes time; the trial order is not a confirmed order book. The timeline depends on trial results, with each stage taking two to three months.
  • Raw material competitiveness risk: The INR 10–15 per kg higher Indian raw material price versus Chinese producers limits export viability in speciality products.
  • Capacity and capex risk: A participant noted that aggressive capex over the past two years has seen utilization suffer, and that if infrastructure growth slows, excess capacity could hurt financials. Management responded that in-house equipment manufacturing allows six-month lead times and that capex will be capped at INR 200–250 crore annually.
  • Working capital structure risk: A participant observed that improvement in trade payable days via payable financing substitutes one liability for another. Management cited inventory days reduction, receivable reduction, channel financing and renegotiation with customers as additional levers, and stated it does not wish to negotiate payable days with suppliers because paying vendors in advance preserves purchasing leverage as the country’s largest wire rod purchaser.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Volume growth guidance: Management is working towards 20% growth for the rest of the year, anchored on market share gains across end markets, the speciality portfolio contributing more meaningfully, and available capacity. Observed: Q1 sales volume was 112,000 MT (1,11,962 MT per the press release) versus 104,000 MT in Q1 FY26, with the shortfall against the 20% trajectory attributed to the demand dip and gas disruption. A participant calculated that 20% growth implies roughly 145,000 MT per quarter, or about 85% utilization; management confirmed 85–90% utilization is its historical sweet spot.

EBITDA guidance: Management maintained a 20% EBITDA guidance alongside 20% volume growth, while acknowledging the higher EBITDA base at the start of the quarter and noting uncertainty. Observed: Q1 FY27 EBITDA was Rs. 567.1 Mn against Rs. 744.6 Mn in Q1 FY26, a decline of 23.8%, with margin at 4.9% versus 7.9%. Management attributed the gap to the deliberate absorption of INR 5,000 per tonne of consumable cost on committed orders, taking blended EBITDA to INR 2 per kg for the first 45 days, before returning to INR 7–8 per kg from mid-May.

Cash flow guidance: The original combined FY26 and FY27 target was about INR 600 crore. Observed: FY26 target of INR 250 crore was exceeded at about INR 330 crore; the FY27 target of INR 350 crore was tracked with INR 115–121 crore generated in Q1 despite weak EBITDA and PAT. Management expressed confidence in reaching the combined INR 800 crore figure, stating that targeting is necessary to achieve it.

Capex guidance: Management guided to INR 200–250 crore of capex every year to support 20–25% volume growth, with no single year of very high capex other than for the speciality segment. Observed: this is presented as a change from the company’s prior approach over the last year.

ROCE guidance: Management targets 25% ROCE in the current business in a very short while, supported by increased low-carbon wires, B2C and cost-cutting initiatives, and noted that the total capital base decreased in Q1. A participant observed that the company delivered about 16% ROCE in the last two quarters even without speciality, and questioned whether speciality adds ROCE given the indicated asset turn; management responded that per-tonne EBITDA and quantity are the stable metrics, with INR 600–800 crore EBITDA expected on INR 2,000–2,500 crore investment in speciality.

Speciality utilization guidance: Management targets optimum utilization in IHT within the year and 60–80% capacity utilization in IHT and OHT by year-end, with OHT commissioning by the end of the year and more OHT numbers expected next year. Observed: IHT is currently operating at 35–40% utilization, with 50% targeted next month.

Market share guidance: Management reiterated that continuing to gain market share remains at the core of its growth philosophy, with the industry growing at 7–8% against the company’s 20% historical growth.

Broker Narrative

The broker’s narrative evolved from a broad growth story centered on becoming India’s largest steel wire manufacturer to a more concrete focus on capacity milestones and margin improvement through value-added products. While the optimism on capacity expansion and high-margin products persisted, the emphasis shifted to specific commissioning events and EBITDA/tonne targets. Concerns about raw material price volatility and project delays remained constant, reflecting the disappointment in the first call.

Fears that came true

  • The fear of raw material price volatility materialized, as the last report still flags it as a key headwind.
  • The fear of delays in ramping up Dadri/Sanand plants materialized, as the last report notes delays in planned capacity expansion.
  • The fear of non-receipt of approvals from customers materialized, as the last report mentions delays in approvals for steel cord wire.
  • The fear of a substantial increase in debt materialized, as the last report cites higher depreciation and finance costs.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that the ‘cost plus’ model would mitigate raw material price volatility failed, as raw material price volatility remains a prominent risk.
