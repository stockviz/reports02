Aurum PropTech: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Housing.com integration and profitability. The acquisition of Housing.com brings a business that has historically operated at a loss. Management noted that listing platforms in Indian real estate “have historically not been profitable,” and that Housing.com’s FY2025 expenditure (employee benefits, advertising, payment gateways, software data) stood at approximately INR 531 crores against revenue from operations of about INR 343 crores. The company has guided that making Housing profitable will take four to six quarters, with an internal target of three to four quarters, requiring product innovation, business synergies, AI-led efficiencies, and “relentless execution.”

Competitive backdrop. A participant observed that the largest competitor in the portal space has spent heavily over many years and still operated at a loss for a long period, raising the question of long-term profitability in the segment.

Quarter-on-quarter softness. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was INR 111.84 crores versus INR 123.85 crores in the previous quarter, and total income was INR 119.01 crores versus INR 132.03 crores. Profit before tax declined to INR 2.31 crores from INR 3.69 crores sequentially. The distribution segment revenue fell to INR 55.94 crores from INR 66.87 crores, and the capital segment to INR 1.55 crores from INR 4.28 crores.

Dilution concerns. A participant raised that the PropTiger and Housing.com acquisitions came “at a serious cost of dilution,” prompting management to explain its thinking on per-share value creation.

Rental segment losses in FY26. In the audited consolidated results for the year ended March 31, 2026, the Rental segment reported a loss of INR 1,854 lakhs, and the Capital segment a loss of INR 527 lakhs, partly offset by Distribution segment profit of INR 3,230 lakhs.

Unpaid rights issue calls. As of March 31, 2025, an aggregate of INR 764 lakhs (including premium of INR 716 lakhs) remained unpaid on the first call of the rights issue. Certain call money remained unpaid even after the second and final call, with the company receiving interest of INR 56.87 lakhs for late payment of call money during FY26.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Third consecutive profitable quarter. Q1 FY27 was described as the third consecutive profitable quarter, with PBT margins expanding 1,590 basis points and adjusted EBITDA margins improving 1,320 basis points. Management characterized the shift to profitability as “structural and enduring,” driven by stronger business fundamentals, improved productivity, and disciplined capital allocation rather than one-off cost actions.

Rental and distribution scale. The rental business delivered INR 56 crores of revenue in Q1 FY27, maintaining 81% occupancy across 21 cities in student housing, co-living, and family rentals, with an average customer rating of 4.3. Both rental businesses achieved EBITDA profitability. The developer and distribution business also delivered INR 56 crores of revenue, enabling over 1,200 developers across 1,50,000 projects in 40+ cities.

Housing.com as a platform expansion. Housing.com commands approximately 24% of India’s online portal market, with over 58 million average monthly visits and 12 million monthly active users on its app. Management framed the acquisition as expanding the addressable market and monetization opportunity: India sees roughly 2.5 crore property transactions annually, of which nearly 1.2 crore are expected to become digitally addressable, while only 7–10 lakh residential transactions are actively intermediated through online portals. India’s annual residential transaction value exceeds INR 15,00,000 crores, with approximately INR 38,000 crores spent annually on property marketing and mortgage, yet online portals currently capture only about INR 1,200 crores.

Monetization beyond listings. Management stated that while traditional portals monetize listings and advertisements, “our monetization begins when the portals end” — Housing.com captures consumer intent, and Aurum monetizes that intent through developer technology, transaction fulfillment, rentals, financing, and AI-enabled services.

AI-native transformation. AI is deployed across lead qualification, sales conversion, customer engagement, transaction workflows, customer service, and developer operations, with revenue per team member as a key success metric. The company is developing “Aurum’s Unified Brain,” a single intelligence layer intended to activate monetization opportunities across the ecosystem.

PropTiger turnaround precedent. Management cited the acquisition of PropTiger from REA in July 2025 as a loss-making business that was turned around to deliver its first two quarters of profitability, using a playbook it intends to apply to Housing.com.

REA as strategic shareholder. REA became a significant shareholder (described as a 24.9% holder), which management views as opening opportunities to learn from a large global PropTech and media group.

ARR target. Management indicated the INR 1,000 crores ARR target would be achieved “a lot faster” with the Housing.com transaction and surpassed.

3. Key Risks

Execution risk on Housing.com turnaround. Management acknowledged that driving profitability in Housing.com in a time-bound manner requires product innovation, business synergies, AI-led efficiencies, and relentless execution, and that the timeline has been extended to four to six quarters from an internal target of three to four.

Integration complexity. Management noted that internationally invested, corporately managed businesses tend to become “management-heavy, top-heavy,” and that businesses built by tech teams may lack understanding of real estate practice — both identified as areas requiring correction.

Cannibalization question. A participant asked whether Housing.com’s organic listing traffic could cannibalize Aurum Analytica’s paid lead generation model for the same developer clients; management stated it does not see cannibalization and instead sees synergy.

Tax structure uncertainty. Management said the team is still evaluating the financial and tax structures of Housing.com together with its existing finance and operations team, and will articulate clear positions in Q3, starting H2.

Regulatory and labour code developments. The Government of India notified provisions of the Code on Wages, 2019, the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, and the Code on Social Security, 2020 on November 21, 2025. The company assessed no material financial implication but continues to monitor developments.

Discontinued operations and sale completion. The sale of buildings at Navi Mumbai for approximately INR 11,200 lakhs was approved on March 12, 2026; possession of one floor was transferred in the quarter ended March 31, 2026, with a gain of INR 1,772 lakhs recognized. The balance transaction is subject to receipt of clearances from statutory authorities and was expected to close by June 30, 2026.

Associate losses. The consolidated audited results include the Group’s share of total comprehensive income of INR (185) lakhs and INR (388) lakhs for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026 respectively, in respect of one associate.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Profitability trajectory. Management stated Q1 FY27 was the third consecutive profitable quarter and described profitability as structural. Observed: Q1 FY27 PBT of INR 2.31 crores (2.1% margin) versus INR 3.69 crores (2.9%) in the previous quarter, and versus a loss before tax of INR 10.81 crores in the quarter ended June 2025. Sequentially, PBT declined even as the year-on-year comparison improved sharply.

Housing.com profitability timeline. Management guided to making Housing.com profitable within four to six quarters, with an internal target of three to four quarters. Observed: no Housing.com financials are consolidated in the supplied results; management cited FY2025 revenue from operations of approximately INR 343 crores and expenditure of approximately INR 531 crores, with FY2026 numbers still being audited.

PropTiger turnaround. Management stated PropTiger was turned around to deliver its first two quarters of profitability after being acquired in July 2025. Observed: the consolidated results show the Distribution segment (which includes PropTiger) delivering profit of INR 3,230 lakhs for FY26 versus INR 1,101 lakhs in FY25, and quarterly Distribution segment profit rising from INR 589 lakhs in Q4 FY25 to INR 1,137 lakhs in Q3 FY26 and INR 1,363 lakhs in Q4 FY26.

ARR target. Management indicated the INR 1,000 crores ARR target would be achieved faster and surpassed with Housing.com, but did not provide exact numerical guidance. Observed: FY26 revenue from operations was INR 38,109 lakhs (continuing operations), with Q1 FY27 revenue from operations at INR 111.84 crores.

Rental segment profitability. Management stated both rental businesses achieved EBITDA profitability in Q1 FY27. Observed: in the FY26 audited consolidated segment results, the Rental segment reported a loss of INR 1,854 lakhs for the year, though the quarterly trend improved from a loss of INR 655 lakhs in Q4 FY25 to a loss of INR 527 lakhs in Q3 FY26 and INR 361 lakhs in Q4 FY26.

Merger effect. The Scheme of Merger of Aurum Softwares and Solutions Private Limited into Liv Real Solutions Private Limited, with an appointed date of April 1, 2025, received confirmation from the Regional Director on May 15, 2026 and became effective June 12, 2026. The company stated there is no financial impact on the results and balances of the consolidated or standalone financial statements for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026 and comparative periods on account of this, and the previously filed results dated April 23, 2026 were updated accordingly.

Broker Narrative

The first report framed AURUM as a high-growth proptech story, citing a $1tn real-estate market, product diversification, and a 46% revenue CAGR, while acknowledging an investment phase with PAT losses. By the last report, the narrative had shifted to near-term macro and cost headwinds—tariffs, Middle East tensions, visa/wage inflation—and muted growth/margin constraints. The persistent theme was growth and margin strain: early fears of scale limitations and losses evolved into the final report’s restricted margin expansion, while the original real-estate/proptech optimism faded.

Fears that came true

  • The investment-phase/PAT-loss risk was real: the stock saw negative actual returns after the Oct-2024 Buy (-9.6%) and Jan-2025 Reduce (-13.1%), and the last report still cited restrictive margin expansion.
  • The early concern about limited near-term scale from smaller products and the K2V2 transition appeared to feed into the muted growth that the last report flagged as a margin headwind.

Optimism that failed

  • The projected 46% revenue CAGR and strong growth from Nestaway/HelloWorld did not prevent negative actual returns in Oct-2024 and Jan-2025, and the final report still described growth as muted.
  • The broad real-estate/proptech adoption and co-living/fractional-ownership tailwinds did not translate into sustained positive returns, as the narrative shifted to tariff, visa, and geopolitical pressures.
