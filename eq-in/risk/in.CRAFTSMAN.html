CRAFTSMAN AUTOMATION — RECENT CORPORATE ANNOUNCEMENTS SUMMARY

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Capacity constraints in Aluminium Products. The Board-approved Hosur Unit-3 expansion is explicitly justified by existing aluminium products capacity “nearing optimal utilisation levels.” The disclosure pegs existing capacity utilisation at approximately 85%, indicating limited headroom to serve incremental demand without new capacity.

Rising finance costs. Consolidated finance cost for Q1 FY27 stood at ₹8,616 lakhs, up from ₹6,630 lakhs in Q1 FY25, reflecting a heavier borrowing base following acquisitions and expansion. Standalone finance cost also rose to ₹6,763 lakhs from ₹5,283 lakhs a year earlier.

Comparability disruption from acquisitions. The company states that, on account of acquisitions completed during FY26, consolidated results for Q1 FY27 are not comparable with Q1 FY25. The consolidation now spans a wider set of entities, including DR Axion India, Suprash Developers, Srikara Technologies, Sunbeam Lightweighting Solutions, and the German entities.

Unreviewed components within consolidated numbers. The limited review report notes that consolidated results include one subsidiary reviewed by other auditors (revenue ₹47,261 lakhs, PAT ₹5,127 lakhs), three subsidiaries not reviewed/audited (revenue ₹1,008 lakhs, PAT ₹290 lakhs), and a joint venture whose results were not reviewed/audited (Group share of PAT ₹62 lakhs). Management has stated these are not material to the Group.

Execution and approval dependencies. The Hosur Unit-3 facility is expected to be commissioned within 6–8 months “subject to receipt of requisite approvals and completion of project execution activities.” The composite scheme of arrangement to amalgamate DR Axion India (with its subsidiaries) into Sunbeam Lightweighting Solutions remains subject to National Company Law Tribunal approval.

Currency and hedging movements. Translation reserve movements swung from a positive ₹1,597 lakhs in Q1 FY25 to negative ₹284 lakhs in Q1 FY27 on a consolidated basis, indicating exposure to foreign exchange translation volatility.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record scale. FY26 consolidated revenue from operations was the highest ever at ₹8,069 crores, with EBITDA of ₹1,299 crores. Market capitalisation has grown from ₹2,852 crores at listing to ₹24,734 crores.

Strong Q1 FY27 momentum. Consolidated revenue from operations rose to ₹2,43,158 lakhs in Q1 FY27 from ₹1,78,400 lakhs in Q1 FY25. Consolidated profit for the period rose to ₹15,055 lakhs from ₹6,960 lakhs. All three reporting segments — Powertrain, Aluminium Products, and Industrial & Engineering — recorded higher segment revenue and higher segment results year-on-year.

Aluminium Products leadership. Aluminium Products is the largest segment by revenue (₹1,47,934 lakhs consolidated in Q1 FY27) and segment result (₹14,988 lakhs), and its segment assets grew to ₹5,69,928 lakhs from ₹3,95,530 lakhs a year earlier.

Capacity expansion. The Board on 29 July 2026 approved a new plant at Hosur Unit-3 (Komaranapalli Village, Denkanikottai Taluk, Krishnagiri District, Tamil Nadu) for the Aluminium Products Division, with Phase I investment estimated at ₹100 crores and Phase II at ₹150 crores, financed substantially through term loans with the balance from internal accruals. The rationale cites growing demand from two-wheeler and passenger vehicle OEMs in Southern India and strategic proximity to key customers.

Germany machining capability. Craftsman Fronberg Precision GmbH was incorporated in Germany on 27 August 2026 as a wholly-owned subsidiary of Craftsman Germany GmbH (and step-down wholly-owned subsidiary of Craftsman Automation Limited), with initial share capital of EUR 25,000. The incorporation is intended to support development of in-house machining capabilities, strengthen operational flexibility, and support future growth of the Group’s Germany operations.

Balance sheet strengthening via QIP. During Q1 FY27, the company issued 22,98,850 equity shares at ₹8,700 per share through a Qualified Institutions Placement, aggregating ₹2,00,000 lakhs. Proceeds are earmarked for repayment/pre-payment of certain borrowings and general corporate purposes; ₹91,208 lakhs remained unutilised at quarter-end for use in subsequent periods.

Dividend. A final dividend of ₹11.25 per equity share (225% on face value of ₹5) for FY26 was approved by shareholders at the 40th AGM held on 23 July 2026.

Leadership continuity. Shareholders approved the re-appointment of Mr. Srinivasan Ravi as Chairman and Managing Director and Mr. Ravi Gauthamram as Whole-time Director, each for five years effective 1 October 2026.

Operational depth. Mr. Santosh Kumar Singh was appointed Senior Management Personnel (Senior Vice President – Operations) on 29 July 2026, bringing over three decades of manufacturing, engineering, and operations experience, including senior roles with Indian Railways.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution risk on expansion: Hosur Unit-3 commissioning is contingent on approvals and project execution within the stated 6–8 month window.
  • Regulatory/legal risk: The DR Axion–Sunbeam composite scheme requires NCLT approval.
  • Leverage and interest cost risk: Finance costs are elevated and rising year-on-year; the Hosur project is to be substantially term-loan funded.
  • Customer concentration in end markets: The business serves commercial vehicles, passenger vehicles, two-wheelers, farm equipment, off-highway vehicles, power transmission, genset/gas engines, material handling, and warehousing — exposing it to cyclicality in these sectors.
  • Foreign exchange and translation risk: Evidenced by translation reserve volatility and the German operations.
  • Integration risk: Multiple recent acquisitions (DR Axion, Suprash, Srikara, Sunbeam, German entities) create consolidation and integration complexity, with results not comparable year-on-year.
  • Assurance scope risk: Certain consolidated components were not reviewed/audited by their auditors, though management deems them immaterial.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance/forward-looking elements disclosed: - Hosur Unit-3 to be commissioned within 6–8 months, subject to approvals and execution. - Phase I investment of ₹100 crores and Phase II of ₹150 crores for Hosur Unit-3. - QIP balance proceeds of ₹91,208 lakhs to be utilised in subsequent periods. - Craftsman Fronberg Precision GmbH intended to support in-house machining capability and future growth of Germany operations. - Composite scheme subject to NCLT approval.

Observed performance against prior positioning: - The company had previously flagged capacity expansion at Bhiwadi, Kothavadi, and Hosur (2025–2026 journey milestones); the Hosur Unit-3 approval on 29 July 2026 is consistent with that stated expansion path. - The 85% capacity utilisation disclosed for Aluminium Products corroborates the stated rationale that existing capacities are nearing optimal levels. - Q1 FY27 delivered year-on-year growth across revenue, segment results, and profit, alongside the QIP completion and dividend declaration, indicating the expansion and financing programme is progressing in line with the disclosed trajectory. - The company held a virtual one-on-one meeting with Allianz Global Investors on 2 September 2026, at which no Unpublished Price Sensitive Information was discussed.
