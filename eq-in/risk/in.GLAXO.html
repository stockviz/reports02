GLAXO — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Supply chain disruption and base effects. The company experienced CMO-related supply disruptions in the prior-year quarter, which created a softer comparable base. Management stated this favourable base effect improved headline growth by roughly 4–5%, with underlying sustainable growth at about 9–10%. On Calpol supply, management said “the worst is behind us” and that learnings were used to stress-test the entire supply chain, with business continuity plans being strengthened.

Cost pressure from currency. Imported purchase costs hardened as the rupee depreciated, partially offsetting gross margin gains.

Acute-season dependence and portfolio skew. The portfolio remains heavily skewed toward acute therapies, and the acute segment still contributes about 60% of the overall Indian pharma market. Q2 (July–September) is the peak acute season; management noted the last two years saw weak acute seasons, though current trends from June are tracking in line with expectations.

Price control exposure. Approximately 44–45% of the General Medicines portfolio is under price control, limiting pricing flexibility.

Competitive pressure in respiratory. Trelegy Ellipta faces generic launches of the Umeclidinium molecule, though the Ellipta device continues to be positioned as an innovation.

Parent restructuring uncertainty. GSK plc announced a restructuring focused on unlocking inefficiencies and improving ways of working across functions and geographies. Management acknowledged the question of whether this affects India given its General Medicines-heavy portfolio, while noting India is one of the few markets with double-digit General Medicines growth and that the company has already gone through several restructuring programs.

Tax demand. The company received a Final Order from ACIT Cir 7(1)(1) with a demand liability of Rs 23.21 Crores. Separately, a refund of Rs 52.00 Cr was received in bank on 6 Aug 2026 following an OGE to CIT-Appeal for AY 1984-85, with interest income of Rs 42.09 Cr to be credited to the P/L account as income.

Opex spike. Opex rose 36% year-on-year, driven by deliberate front-loading of investment, including nearly 3,800 targeted HCP events and international speaker meets concentrated in Q1 ahead of the peak September quarter.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue was INR 924 crores, up 15% Y-o-Y. EBITDA grew 17% to INR 293 crores with a 31.7% margin (press release cites EBITDA margins at 32%). PAT grew 24% to INR 253 crores with a 27.6% margin. EPS was INR 14.95 (+24%). Excluding a one-off dividend payout from 100% subsidiary Biddle Sawyer, PAT growth would have been about 17%.

Portfolio transformation and innovation contribution. Innovation (IP-protected) sales reached 7% of total revenues, versus about 4% a year earlier — nearly doubling. About four percentage points of headline growth came from innovation products. These products did not exist three years ago.

General Medicines strength. The General Medicines business grew double digit, with top five promoted brands outperforming the market (EI >100; IQVIA). Augmentin, Ceftum and T-Bact reinforced leadership positions. Management’s stated vision is to transform the General Medicines business to reach over 1 billion patients this decade and double the sales of General Medicines’ growth engines by 2030.

Vaccines leadership. Continued leadership in the self-pay private paediatric vaccines market, led by Infanrix Hexa and Fluarix Tetra. In adult vaccination, Shingrix grew 65% for the quarter, with 45,000–50,000 doses sold quarterly and the brand crossing INR 100 crores on a MAT basis. Focus on elderly cohorts with underlying CVMD drove stronger HCP endorsement.

Oncology ramp-up. Jemperli (dostarlimab) and Zejula (niraparib), launched in August last year, have grown significantly. Jemperli is now the leading immunotherapy for first-line primary advanced and recurrent endometrial cancer; Zejula is among the top PARP inhibitors in maintenance treatment of recurrent ovarian cancer (IPSOS MAT December 2025). Together they touched 260–270 patients in Q1, with 600–700 patients over the first six months of the calendar year.

Respiratory portfolio. Nucala (mepolizumab) new patients doubled to 100–120 per month versus 50–60 a year earlier, with at least 600 patients on active treatment. Trelegy Ellipta holds 10,000–12,000 units monthly despite generic competition.

Upcoming launches. Blenrep (belantamab mafodotin), an anti-BCMA ADC for second-line relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, has received CDSCO/CDSCO approval and is set for launch within this financial year (Q2 or Q3), with a team in place and training underway. Arexvy, an RSV vaccine for adults over 60 and high-risk adults 50–59, has received marketing authorisation. Bepirovirsen (Hibsago), a potential functional cure for chronic hepatitis B, is expected to launch in India in the next three quarters if all goes well. Ojjaara is listed among upcoming launches expected in the next 18–24 months.

Market environment. The industry grew about 13.5% in Q1, with balanced growth across volume, price and new introductions, and roughly 2% volume growth. GSK’s represented market grew better than the external market, with balanced growth across geographies (metros, Tier 1, Tier 2, rural) and across specialists, super-specialists and general practitioners. Oncology was the fastest-growing segment at 27% versus the industry average of 12–13%.

Distribution reach. A super distributor arrangement (GoApptiv) manages tail-end products, providing access to mofussil interiors.

Local manufacturing. Bulk of the General Medicines business is locally manufactured at the Nashik facility and by 20 contract manufacturers, supporting a local-for-local model.

3. Key Risks

  • Parent restructuring: GSK plc’s global restructuring could affect investment priorities in India given the General Medicines-heavy portfolio, though management noted alignment through faster global clinical trial inclusion in India.
  • Supply chain concentration: The prior CMO incident exposed vulnerability; mitigation via diversified sourcing, contingency planning and end-to-end visibility is ongoing.
  • Currency and input costs: Rupee depreciation hardening imported purchase costs; management sees no immediate impact from externalities given embedded global supply chains.
  • Competitive/generic erosion: Generic launches in the Umeclidinium molecule and a fragmented PARP inhibitor market.
  • Seasonal dependence: Performance tied to the acute season, particularly Q2; weak acute seasons in the last two years are a reminder of this sensitivity.
  • New category creation risk: Adult vaccination (Shingrix, Arexvy) and hepatitis B functional cure require building categories and patient funnels; Bepirovirsen commercial model remains under evaluation, including the question of chronic versus acute therapy for cured versus non-cured patients.
  • Tax/regulatory: The ACIT final order demand of Rs 23.21 Crores.
  • Pricing regulation: 44–45% of General Medicines portfolio under price control.
  • Oncology access/pricing: Management noted oncology is “never about the money” but about patients impacted, implying access and affordability considerations.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Guidance on growth. Management has guided for INR 8,000 crores of top line in four to five years, implying at least 13–14% CAGR. Management stated the 2x vision “remains intact and undiluted,” with established business expected to grow 8–10% and new launches as the arrowhead. Management declined to give specific forward-looking guidance on sustainable growth rate but stated the overriding objective is sustaining double-digit momentum.

Observed performance. Q1 FY27 revenue grew 15% (9–10% on a comparable basis excluding the base effect), within the trajectory implied by the long-term guidance. Innovation contributed 7% of revenues versus 4% a year earlier, supporting the portfolio transformation thesis.

Guidance on margins. Management guided that S&A ratios should normalize to historical levels through the year, with EBITDA margins roughly in the 34% range as last year. Observed Q1 EBITDA margin was 31.7% (press release: 32%), with the opex spike described as a deliberate phasing rather than a pull-forward. Management stated the primary objective is to drive growth while maintaining current levels of profitability.

Guidance on opex. Management stated the Q1 investment ratio spike is a phasing phenomenon and expects moderation to historical trends in following quarters. Observed opex rose 36% Y-o-Y, with nearly 3,800 HCP events concentrated in Q1.

Guidance on launches. Blenrep was guided for launch within this financial year (Q2 or Q3); observed status is CDSCO approval received with team in place and training underway. Bepirovirsen was guided for launch in the next three quarters; management deferred detailed commercial model answers to Q2. Arexvy has received marketing authorisation. Ojjaara and Arexvy were listed among launches expected in the next 18–24 months.

Guidance on Shingrix. Management stated Shingrix crossed INR 100 crores on a MAT basis; observed Q1 growth was 65% with 45,000–50,000 doses quarterly.

Guidance on Nucala. Management stated the business is “almost doubling” and patients on active treatment are at least 600; observed new patients doubled to 100–120 per month.

Guidance on MR productivity. Management stated blended productivity of INR 15–16 lakhs per rep, improving 5–6% this quarter. Oncology has about 40–45 people, with incremental hiring focused on areas of little or no presence (medical oncology for solid tumors done last year, hematology done a few weeks ago, liver disease identified as a future area).

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a defensive, acute-heavy/NLEM-exposed portfolio with a declining vaccines business to a more optimistic story led by vaccines recovery, oncology/respiratory innovation, and rising innovative contribution. Persistent themes were NLEM-linked portfolio constraints, reliance on new launches for accelerated growth, and a Neutral stance with limited upside. The last report still flagged slower 8-10% base-business growth and elevated investments, but acknowledged strong vaccines and specialty momentum.

Fears that came true

  • The early NLEM drag persisted, with ~40-45% of the DF general medicine category still NLEM-linked by the last report, and the May 2025 Reduce call was followed by a -15.0% actual return.
  • The post-FY22 volume growth downtrend/acute underperformance continued as a slower base business by the last report (8-10% annual growth), coinciding with the Aug 2025 Neutral call’s -7.3% actual DISAPPOINTMENT.
