DIACABS — Recent Corporate Developments: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Capacity underutilisation remains the central challenge. The Q1 FY27 investor presentation notes that the company is “ramping off the FY26 base (cables 34% / conductors 20% utilisation)” and describes capacity headroom as “the core turnaround lever.” This indicates that a large portion of installed capacity was idle through FY26 and is only now being brought into production.

Cost and margin pressure at the gross level. In Q1 FY27, cost of goods sold rose 133% YoY against revenue growth of 129%, and contribution margin declined from 19.4% to 17.7% of revenue. Finance cost rose 286% YoY (₹37 mn to ₹142 mn), a materially faster increase than revenue, reflecting the cost of the expanded balance sheet.

Governance and compliance gaps in the recent past. A July 7, 2025 clarification to the NSE addressed a query that the financial results submitted were “not as per format prescribed by SEBI” and that the CEO signature was missing from the Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications. The company explained that the Whole-time Director position had been vacant since May 30, 2025 following Mr. Vinod Jain’s resignation, and that it was actively searching for a suitable candidate, undertaking to fill the vacancy within the stipulated three-month period under Regulation 26A(1).

Execution and project-timing risk. Several growth initiatives are still at approval or commissioning stages: the 6th CCV line has only “in-principle approval” with final approval pending on the detailed project report; the LV cables expansion is targeted for commercial production only in FY2027-28; and the corrugation lines are targeted for June 2027. The order book itself carries timing risk — ₹845 Cr of conductors are noted as executable only in FY28.

Commodity and market risk. The company’s own forward-looking statement flags risks including economic conditions, commodity prices, regulatory developments, and competitive dynamics. The order book is described as being on a “variable-price basis,” and metal (Al ₹400/kg, Cu ₹1,400/kg) is characterised as a passthrough under IEEMA/customer price-variation clauses — meaning revenue and working capital are sensitive to metal price movements even where margin is protected.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

A decisive turnaround in reported performance. FY26 revenue was ₹1,910 Cr with EBITDA of ₹232 Cr (12.1% margin) and PAT of ₹158 Cr, described as ~136% L2Y revenue CAGR and ~205% L2Y PAT CAGR. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹6,899 mn, up 129% YoY; EBITDA ₹846 mn, up 172%; PAT ₹585 mn, up 191%; EPS ₹1.11 versus ₹0.38. The presentation characterises this as a “breakout quarter” with “operating leverage at work.”

A large outstanding order book. As on 11-Aug-2026, the outstanding order book stood at ₹3,687.55 Cr (₹36,876 mn), up from ₹32,404 mn at March 2026, spanning 12 product lines across cables (₹1,791.42 Cr, 48.6%) and conductors (₹1,896.13 Cr, 51.4%), including ₹435 Cr of data-centre MV cable orders.

New order wins. The company received a Letter of Award valued at ₹179.43 crore inclusive of GST from Adani Electricity Mumbai Limited for specialised MV and low voltage underground power cables covering approximately 871 kms, marking the third consecutive year DPIL has been awarded this contract.

Balance-sheet reset and exit from NCLT. Diamond Power has completed a full exit from the NCLT resolution framework a year ahead of schedule, having fully prepaid its ₹2,401 crore Resolution Plan consideration (₹501 crore upfront cash and ₹1,900 crore in redeemable bonds), with all legacy CBI and ED criminal proceedings cleared by Hon’ble Courts. The company is described as debt-light, rateable and bankable, with its entire gross block free for bank financing.

Capital raise and broader shareholder base. A QIP of 7,11,00,000 equity shares at ₹227 per share (including a ₹11.92, or 4.99%, discount to floor price) aggregating ₹1,613.97 crore was allotted on July 28, 2026. Paid-up capital rose from ₹52,69,71,060 to ₹59,80,71,060. Post-QIP, promoter holding is 74.03% with zero promoter pledge, 14.24% held by institutions, and 66,795 shareholders. Marquee names on the register include Motilal Oswal MF (3.43%), HDFC MF (2.21%) and Smallcap World Fund – Capital Group (1.21%).

Capacity expansion and technology initiatives. Four CCV and four Silane lines are commissioned, with one CCV and one Silane line under commissioning before March 2027 and a new CCV line by December 2027. The company is a licensed manufacturing partner for TS Conductor’s Aluminium-Encapsulated Carbon-Fibre Core (AECC) technology, with product type-tested at Kinectrics and participation in tenders worth ₹1,200 Cr including ₹500 Cr with PGCIL. It has secured and executed an 850 km ECO/HTLS conductor order for a marquee Indian buyer and expects 2,000 km in the current FY. Export certification work (BASEC/CPR/UL) is underway for Europe, USA, Middle East and Africa.

Three underwritten capex projects. Project A (LV cables expansion, ₹133.59 Cr) targets 3,502 km/month capacity, ₹1,880 Cr revenue at 100% utilisation, 25.8% unlevered IRR, NPV of ₹293 Cr at 12%, and payback of ~2.5 years on project cost. Project B (corrugation lines, ₹17.03 Cr) is expected to add ₹375 Cr of annual revenue with a 2–3 month simple payback on a contribution basis. Project C (6th CCV line) is expected to unlock ₹652.9 Cr of FY28 HV/EHV plan, with ~18 months indicative payback.

Industry demand backdrop. The presentation cites electricity consumption of 1,623 TWh (FY25E), per-capita power of 1,395 kWh, power-sector investment of ₹37–42 Tn for FY27–31P, and ~10→13% cables & wires CAGR. Demand drivers cited include transmission capex, distribution capex (RDSS, ₹45 Tn over FY27–FY31), the Green Energy Corridor (~9,700 ckm and 22,600 MVA across eight RE-rich states), the 500 GW non-fossil target by 2030, ₹2.4 Tn of transmission build-out, smart-city underground cabling mandates across 650+ cities, and data-centre load growth from 1.4 GW to ~9 GW by 2030.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution risk on capacity ramp: The growth thesis depends on converting idle capacity (34% cables / 20% conductors in FY26) into production, with several lines still under commissioning or awaiting final approval.
  • Order book timing risk: ₹845 Cr of conductor orders are executable only in FY28, and deliveries under the Adani Electricity award are to commence upon manufacturing clearance.
  • Commodity price and working-capital risk: Metal is a passthrough, but working capital at Project A is estimated at ~₹428 Cr at 90% utilisation (≈3.3× machinery), with peak funds of ~₹558 Cr.
  • Competitive and regulatory risk: Explicitly flagged in the company’s forward-looking statement, alongside economic conditions.
  • Governance/key-personnel risk: The recent vacancy in the Whole-time Director position and the resulting non-compliant filing illustrate dependence on a small leadership group. The appointment of Mr. Umeshkumar Chhaya as Additional and Whole-time Director (13.08.2026 to 30.09.2027, subject to shareholder approval) addresses this, but the term is short and subject to approval.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The company’s safe harbour notes that actual results may differ materially due to risks including the competitive environment, ability to implement strategies, technological changes, and sociopolitical, economic and regulatory conditions in India.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Guidance on profitability and productivity. Management expects further improvement in productivity, revenues and profitability through enhanced capacity utilisation, operational efficiencies, cost optimisation, and strengthening of business operations. This is consistent with the observed Q1 FY27 result: revenue up 129% YoY, EBITDA margin up ~196 bps to 12.3%, PAT up 191%, and the operating-cost ratio falling from 9.1% to 6.6% — evidence of the operating leverage management has pointed to.

Guidance on order execution. For the Adani Electricity award, deliveries are to commence upon manufacturing clearance at a scheduled rate of approximately 50 km per month, to be advanced in line with project requirements. No execution data for this specific order is available in the supplied material.

Guidance on capacity and capex timelines. Management has guided to: 1 CCV + 1 Silane line under commissioning before March 2027; LT cable plant expansion and conductor debottlenecking by June 2027; a new CCV line by December 2027; corrugation lines commercial production June 2027; Project C PO in August 2026 with commissioning before December 2027; and Project A commercial production in FY2027-28. These are forward commitments against which no completion evidence is yet provided in the material.

Guidance on HTLS/ECO conductors. Management expects 2,000 km of ECO/HTLS conductor volume in the current FY, against 850 km already secured and executed for a marquee Indian buyer.

Guidance on strategic direction. Management states its strategic focus now shifts wholly to scaling MV/EHV cable capacity, deepening backward integration, and expanding its customer base as India undertakes the largest grid build-out in its history, with an ambition to build Diamond Power into a global cables and conductors major. The Q1 FY27 presentation operationalises this through six workstreams (TS Conductor AECC, capacity additions, data-centre products, export certifications, ECO/HTLS conductors, and EHV range up to 400 kV).

Observed versus guided — where evidence exists. The FY26 outcome (revenue ₹1,910 Cr, EBITDA ₹232 Cr at 12.1%, PAT ₹158 Cr) and the Q1 FY27 outcome (revenue ₹6,899 mn, EBITDA ₹846 mn at 12.3%, PAT ₹585 mn) both show margin expansion and profit growing faster than sales, matching management’s stated expectation of operating leverage. The QIP (₹1,613.97 Cr allotted 28-Jul-2026) and the NCLT exit (₹2,401 Cr Resolution Plan consideration prepaid a year ahead of schedule) provide the funding and balance-sheet conditions management has cited as prerequisites for the capacity ramp. The order book grew from ₹32,404 mn at March 2026 to ₹36,876 mn at 11-Aug-2026, consistent with the stated demand environment.
