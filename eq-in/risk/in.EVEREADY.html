Eveready Industries India — Q1 FY27 Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Commodity inflation: Zinc prices remained elevated at around $3,500 per ton in Q1 FY27, versus below $3,000 per ton for much of the prior year. Electrolytic manganese dioxide, electrodes, acetylene black, and lighting products/components also saw inflationary trends, pressuring the cost structure.
  • Macroeconomic uncertainty: Persistent geopolitical uncertainty, supply chain disruptions, inflationary pressures, and currency fluctuations added uncertainty to input costs.
  • Flashlight weakness: Overall flashlight demand was soft due to the delayed onset of monsoon, which deferred seasonal demand and lowered volumes of conventional battery-operated flashlights. Segment revenue declined 6.7% year-on-year.
  • Lighting price erosion: The lighting segment experienced a prolonged period of price erosion, though the pricing environment showed signs of stabilization in the quarter.
  • Regulatory/compliance matters: BIS implementation for flashlights is complete on paper, but absorption and adoption among non-branded Indian organizations is still pending. On Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR), operational and pricing-mechanism challenges remain, with the company engaged with the Ministry of Environment and Climate Change and the Pollution Control Board.
  • CCI penalty: A penalty of Rs. 171.55 crores was levied by the Competition Commission of India for non-compliance with the Competition Act, 2002; an appeal has been filed with the NCLAT. The next hearing is expected in the last week of September. Management stated it cannot currently estimate the outcome.
  • GST incentive pending: No formal approval has yet been received on the Jammu GST incentive; discussions with the industries department are described as advanced.
  • Demand caution: A delayed monsoon and a cautious near-term outlook are expected to keep demand under pressure. The company also cites risks including competitive intensity, pricing environment, economic conditions affecting demand and supply, input cost changes, key customer and supply chain relationships, trade priorities, political stability, government regulations and taxation, climatic conditions, natural calamities, commodity and currency fluctuations, and litigation.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Revenue momentum: Q1 FY27 revenue was INR 407.7 crores, up 9.0% year-on-year — the seventh consecutive quarter of year-on-year revenue growth. EBITDA was INR 61.5 crores (15.1% margin), PBT INR 50.9 crores, and PAT INR 37.0 crores (9.1% margin), with PAT up 22.3% year-on-year.
  • Battery segment strength: Battery revenue grew 11.9% year-on-year to INR 267.6 crores (64% of revenue). Alkaline volumes grew close to 48%, and alkaline market share expanded to 18%. Carbon zinc performance was stable, with volume growth in line with the industry.
  • Jammu facility: Commercial production at the Jammu alkaline battery plant commenced on 29 May 2026, with an installed capacity of 456 million alkaline batteries per annum. Management expects the plant to improve operating leverage, support alkaline expansion, and open white-labelling and export opportunities. It is described as the only alkaline facility in India at present.
  • Premiumization and device trade-up: Management expects alkaline chemistry growth to be significant over the decade, driven by rising penetration of high-drain devices (smart remotes, toys, medical devices, optical mice). Alkaline is growing at roughly 20%–22% CAGR by volume and value.
  • Flashlight innovation: Rechargeable flashlights grew over 20% in revenue. New launches include a hybrid flashlight (first patent-applied flashlight product) and a rechargeable SHOR flashlight with a 100-decibel animal alarm and red UV strobe for farm protection. The company is also targeting urban and everyday use cases beyond rural/seasonal applications.
  • Lighting growth: Lighting revenue grew 13.7% to INR 87.5 crores (21% of revenue), with healthy volume growth in higher-margin emergency LED bulbs and electrical accessories. The segment broke even in Q1 FY27. Electrical accessories momentum is led by insulation tapes, with wires and MCBs introduced.
  • Digital channels: Strong growth in Q-commerce, quick commerce, and e-commerce reflects an omnichannel distribution strategy.
  • Innovation pipeline: Launch of India’s first portable liquid mosquito vaporizer (patent-applied) and the Xtrabright emergency LED bulb offering double the illumination during power cuts.
  • Distribution advantage: The company cites FMCG-comparable general trade distribution plus an evolving electrical channel as a unique positioning in lighting.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and currency volatility: Zinc and other input costs remain key watch areas; further price or margin corrections may be required if inflation becomes overwhelming.
  • Competitive intensity: Panasonic and Nippo are close followers in zinc; Duracell holds the larger share in alkaline. Amazon Basics has launched battery SKUs, and a news report suggested a Japanese competitor may close Indian plants (management declined to comment, calling it speculative).
  • Regulatory and compliance risk: BIS adoption by non-branded players, EPR pricing/regulatory clarity, and GST incentive approval remain unresolved.
  • Litigation risk: The CCI penalty of Rs. 171.55 crores is under appeal at NCLAT, with a hearing expected in late September; outcome and cash outflow cannot currently be estimated.
  • Demand risk: Delayed monsoon and cautious near-term demand outlook, particularly for conventional flashlights.
  • Cannibalization risk: Management notes cannibalization between alkaline and zinc has not yet appeared, but could emerge once alkaline crosses roughly 20% saliency of the overall battery market.
  • Execution risk on new categories: Wires and MCBs are sourced, not manufactured; the company aims to more than double the business in FY27 but expects only 1%–2% market share, described as preliminary.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Margin sustainability: Management stated it aims to sustain the delivered performance, with disciplined execution and calibrated pricing, while acknowledging external exigencies cannot be contained. CFO confirmed the Q1 numbers contained no one-offs and were purely operational, driven by calibrated price actions and efficiencies.
  • Jammu margin guidance: Management expects a 10% gross margin increment from the Jammu plant once fully stabilized (after about a year), noting alkaline is currently 6%–7% of the total portfolio. Pre-operative expenses through 29 May 2026 were capitalized; post-commencement expenses are charged to P&L.
  • Alkaline market share target: Management guided to an exit share of 25%–30% in alkaline within two years, from 18% currently (up from about 3% over the last 30 months).
  • Debt-free timeline: With debt around INR 165 crores and over INR 200 crores invested in Jammu, management expects the company to be like-to-like debt-free in the next 4–5 quarters.
  • Long-term forecast: Management requested another 1–2 quarters before providing a 3-year EBITDA or turnover CAGR forecast, citing the need for a calculated, executable plan.
  • Noida asset sale: One of two plots has been executed; the second is expected to close shortly. Employee costs were nearly flat year-on-year partly due to the Noida plant closure last year.
  • Wires/MCBs: Management aims to more than double the business in FY27 versus the close of last year, with sourcing capacity sufficient for 2x–3x growth.
  • Lighting: Management expects growth in Q2 and Q3, with accessories, emergency LED bulbs, and wires contributing, while regular GLS bulbs may not grow. Breakeven is expected to hold through the year.
  • GST incentive scheme: Under the Jammu investment scheme, eligible plant and machinery investment (around INR 90–95 crores) is eligible for a 3x refund in GST paid in the state over 10 years; formal approval is still pending.
  • M&A: Any acquisition decision will be part of a long-term vision to be shared in the next 2–3 quarters; no immediate fund infusion is currently needed.
