Headwinds and Challenges

US countervailing duty exposure. On 27 July 2026, HEG received notice of a preliminary affirmative determination by the U.S. Department of Commerce in the Countervailing Duty investigation into imports of large diameter graphite electrodes from India. A preliminary CVD rate of 6.99% was set for HEG, with cash deposits potentially required on imports of the subject merchandise into the United States. The company stated that the financial impact was not ascertainable at that stage and that the preliminary determination is subject to verification and final determination, currently scheduled alongside the final Anti-Dumping Duty determination on 8 December 2026, unless postponed.

ESG gaps flagged by an external rating. NSE Sustainability Ratings and Analytics Limited assigned HEG an ESG score of 60 (Adequate) for FY 26, down from 63 in FY 25, with a Core ESG Rating of 55. The rating was not commissioned by the company and was prepared from publicly available data. Identified weaknesses included greenhouse gas emissions from direct operations and energy consumption exceeding industry averages, Scope 3 carbon footprint above standard practices, energy intensity significantly higher than peers, minimal reliance on renewable energy, waste intensity above industry average, underrepresentation of women, and a combined Chairman and CEO structure indicating potential lack of board independence alongside executive compensation disparity. The rating pertains to FY 2026, prior to the Composite Scheme becoming effective.

Execution and approval dependencies in the Greentech platform. The investor presentation describes the restructuring and the Greentech businesses as subject to requisite approvals, funding and market conditions. The Scheme had received board approval and no-objection/observation letters from BSE, NSE and SEBI, but as of 10 July 2026 the NCLT Indore Bench had reserved its order, and the Scheme remained subject to NCLT sanction and other statutory, regulatory and third-party approvals, with no assurance of sanction in its present form or within any particular timeframe. Capacity, capex and timeline figures are described as targets based on management estimates and subject to change.

Early-stage commercialisation risk. Offtake discussions for anode materials are described as MOUs and ongoing discussions that are non-binding, with no assurance that definitive agreements will be concluded or that indicated volumes will materialise. The graphene order pipeline is described as opportunities under discussion rather than confirmed orders. Performance improvements cited for silicon-doped and graphene-based anodes are based on internal lab-scale results, with commercial-scale performance potentially varying.

Scheduling and disclosure constraints. Investor meeting schedules are subject to change due to exigencies on the part of investors or the company, and no Unpublished Price Sensitive Information is to be shared in such interactions.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Restructuring into two listed entities. The NCLT Indore Bench sanctioned the Composite Scheme of Arrangement involving HEG Limited, HEG Graphite Limited and Bhilwara Energy Limited on 13 August 2026. The Scheme became effective 1 September 2026, with a Record Date of 7 September 2026. HEG shareholders receive 1 fully paid equity share of INR 2 in HEG Graphite Limited for every 1 share held; Bhilwara Energy shareholders receive 8 fully paid equity shares of INR 2 in HEG Limited for every 7 shares of INR 10 held. HEG Limited was renamed HEG Advanced Materials Limited effective 2 September 2026, and HEG Graphite Limited is proposed to be renamed HEG Limited subject to statutory approvals. The graphite electrodes business is expected to list on BSE and NSE in the second half of October 2026, approximately 45 days from the demerger. Management states the reorganisation gives each business an independent listing, focused management and flexibility to pursue its own growth path, serving the evolving EV, BESS and energy-storage ecosystem.

Anode materials scale-up. TACC operates a 200 MT anode demo plant at Mandideep, running the same process flow and technology as the planned commercial plant, and has supplied samples qualified with global cell makers. Construction is underway for Phase I of 20,000 MT with SOP targeted for Q1 FY 28; 85% procurement and 100% engineering are complete, and the facility is expandable to 30,000 MT. Production scale-up is targeted to reach 60,000 MT by FY 32, which management describes as positioning the company among the largest global players outside China. The company cites 50+ years of graphitisation experience, synthetic graphite as the preferred route for fast-charging, and an innovation hub with 37 PhDs and Masters, 5+ patents filed and 20+ R&D projects.

Offtake and validation momentum. Advanced offtake discussions are underway for quantities exceeding 40,000 MT, including a legacy European automotive OEM (>40,000 MT), a North American global automotive company (>70,000 MT), APAC ESS materials (27,000 MT), Asian cell makers (15,000 t over three years), a top-5 North American cell company (50,000 MT by 2030), and Indian majors at scale testing with initial product approvals received.

Graphene commercialisation. Graphene is described as a platform material with applications in construction, textiles, paints and coatings, tires, and energy storage. The company cites an 85–95 MT firm-plus-likely pipeline with 60% firm capacity, an MOU with Nobel laureate Prof. K. Novoselov, validation of graphene-enhanced concrete at NCCBM, road infrastructure validation at CSIR–CRRI, completed commercial-scale textile trials, paint testing with global manufacturers, and a partnership with IIT-Madras on mechanical design and thermal management.

Battery energy solutions. REPlus operates a 1 GWh facility with dual prismatic and cylindrical lines, expanding to 6 GWh by H2 FY27, with a 5 GWh line under construction. The company reports 100 MWh commissioned, 1,500 MWh under execution and a 5,000 MWh order pipeline, with deployments across India and beyond (including Saudi Arabia, D.R. Congo and Zanzibar). EV battery packs are ICAT certified for LCVs, trucks, buses and AGVs, with TSEC approval for telecom and home residential applications, and certification targeted by Q3 FY27.

Green power cash flows and pipeline. Existing hydro assets (86 MW Hydro 1 and 192 MW Hydro 2 in Himachal Pradesh) and 14 MW wind in Maharashtra are described as debt-free, run-of-river with a 3.5–4 hour reservoir enabling peaking generation, eligible for renewable energy certificates, and generating steady free cash flows of approximately ₹300+ crore annually. The pipeline includes a 200 MWh BESS IPP in Gujarat (Q3 FY27), 300 MWp solar in Chhattisgarh (Q2 FY28) and 76 MW Hydro 3 in Uttarakhand (Q4 FY31). Management targets equity IRR of 18–20% and cites a 175-km, 220-kV double-circuit transmission backbone and a power-engineering JV with Canada’s RSW/AECOM.

Capex and funding plan. The Greentech platform has an illustrative capex plan of approximately ₹5,500 crore, split into approximately ₹1,500 crore equity (~27%) and approximately ₹4,000 crore debt (~73%), with net worth of ₹2,700 crore (FY 27) and steady-state ROCE of approximately 17% (FY30), supplemented by hydro cash flows.

ESG strengths. The NSE sustainability assessment noted high waste recycling and recovery versus peers, an LTIR approaching zero or significantly lower than peers, 100% health insurance coverage for permanent employees, zero employee and worker fatalities, low frequency of customer complaints, zero product recalls, CSR spending significantly exceeding obligations, and stronger independent director presence than the minimum required.

Key Risks

  • The US CVD preliminary rate of 6.99% for HEG, with cash deposit requirements potentially applying to US imports, and final determinations scheduled for 8 December 2026 subject to verification and possible postponement; financial impact not yet ascertainable.
  • The Scheme’s benefits depend on NCLT sanction and other approvals; as of 10 July 2026 the NCLT had reserved its order, and there was no assurance of sanction in its present form or within any particular timeframe.
  • Anode offtake discussions and graphene pipeline volumes are non-binding and may not convert into definitive agreements.
  • Capacity, capex, timeline, IRR and ROCE figures are management targets and estimates subject to approvals, funding and market conditions.
  • ESG rating weaknesses include above-peer emissions, energy intensity and waste intensity, minimal renewable energy reliance, low female representation, and a combined Chairman and CEO structure.
  • Investor meeting schedules are subject to change without prior notice.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Restructuring timeline. Management guided that the demerged/resulting entity, HEG Graphite Limited, would list on BSE and NSE in approximately 45 days, in the second half of October 2026. The Scheme was sanctioned by NCLT on 13 August 2026, became effective 1 September 2026, and the Record Date was set at 7 September 2026, consistent with the guided timeline.

Anode capacity build. Management targets Phase I 20,000 MT with SOP in Q1 FY 28 and scale-up to 60,000 MT by FY 32. Observed progress as of the July 2026 presentation: 85% procurement complete, 100% engineering complete, and a 200 MT demo plant operational for the past 12 months supplying qualified samples to global cell makers.

Battery capacity expansion. Management targets expansion from 1 GWh (FY26) to 6 GWh by H2 FY27. Observed position: 1 GWh operational with a 5 GWh line under construction, and reported 100 MWh commissioned, 1,500 MWh under execution and a 5,000 MWh pipeline.

Green power capacity. Management targets growth from 292 MW (FY 26) to 754 MW (FY 28). Observed operational assets total 292 MW (86 MW + 192 MW hydro + 14 MW wind), with the BESS IPP, solar and Hydro 3 assets still under development.

Dividend and governance outcomes. The 54th AGM on 29 July 2026 approved a final dividend of ₹3.40 per equity share (170% of face value ₹2) for FY 2025-26, with all five resolutions passed by requisite majority. Voting participation was approximately 71.3% of shares held, and the special resolution on Shri Shekhar Agarwal’s continuation received the highest proportion of votes against (0.4873%) among the resolutions.

Broker Narrative

The broker kept a Buy rating and nudged its predicted return from +15.5% to +16.8%, but the narrative became more cautious: the first report centred on muted pricing, tariff absorption and needle coke inflation, while the last report added China-led oversupply, Middle East shipment/freight disruptions and a one-time GrafTech MTM loss. The core bullish thesis stayed intact—EAF decarbonisation, capacity expansion, price hikes and the HEG Greentech demerger—and both calls so far have over-delivered (+34.3% and +31.2% vs. +15.5% and +16.8%).

Optimism that failed

  • The supply-side rationalisation tailwind did not materialise as a market-tightening force: the last report still flags industry oversupply and China’s export-led pressure on global graphite electrode prices.
