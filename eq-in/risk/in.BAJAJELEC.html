Bajaj Electricals — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost inflation and pricing pressure. The quarter played out against continued input cost inflation, uneven summer demand across categories, and industry-wide calibrated price hikes. Cost inflation across key categories ranged from 6% to 10%, reaching as high as 13% in some categories, with coolers the highest. Where raw material inflation was significant, the company took selective price corrections depending on category elasticity and competitive intensity.

Fans business weakness. Fans saw a decline and the company is losing share in this large category. Management attributed this more to operational issues than anything else, including external factors such as gas shortages and PCB availability constraints following the war. The company had not anticipated the level of demand for BLDC fans, which use PCBs, and is now planning longer production cycles and stock coverage. BLDC penetration is estimated at 30%–35% industry-wide, with Bajaj Electricals slightly under-indexed versus the industry.

Summer products contraction. Consumer Products was described as a story of two halves: cooling products contracted while non-summer products delivered double-digit growth across categories. Morphy Richards also grew double-digit following its acquisition in the last quarter.

Professional lighting margin drag. In professional lighting, legacy projects were contracted at rates signed before the war, while commodity prices subsequently rose, forcing the company to absorb a margin hit. This was reflected in the overall lighting business margin and was described as a temporary blip expected to unwind as these legacy contracts are exited over a quarter or two.

Lighting Solutions EBIT decline. Lighting Solutions recorded an EBIT of Rs. 18 Cr in Q1 FY27 versus Rs. 27 Cr in the corresponding quarter of the previous year, despite 4.4% revenue growth.

Working capital and cash flow. Overall cash flow was negative in the quarter, largely due to tax compliances related to the Morphy Richards acquisition (TDS and GST). Inventories were slightly up, attributed to seasonal build-up and locking in good inventory; working capital was described as hovering around 50–60 days, with debtors improving.

Competitive intensity. Management acknowledged competition is heating up with more players entering categories, though it framed this as market expansion rather than purely a threat.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Return to growth with margin expansion. Overall revenue grew 2.3% year-on-year in Q1 FY27, with Consumer Products growing 1.7% after declines over multiple quarters, and Lighting Solutions growing 4.4% on a strong base. EBIT margin improved to 6.6% from 2.5%. Profit before tax rose to Rs. 66 Cr from Rs. 2 Cr a year earlier.

Consumer Products turnaround. Consumer Products returned to positive EBIT at 3.9% versus negative 1.7% year-on-year, driven by gross margin expansion (up 220 bps in the segment; 130 bps at company level) and operating leverage. Gross margin improvement was attributed to premiumization, productivity-led initiatives, better mix management, ROI-led investments, and commercial discipline.

Lighting Solutions momentum. Lighting Solutions continued as the vertical carrying momentum, with double-digit growth in consumer lighting. Wires and switchgears are part of the lighting segment, and progress in wires was described as better than expected, with potential to play a significant role. Management reiterated a path back to double-digit margins in overall lighting once legacy projects are exited.

Alternate channels and exports. E-commerce contributes approximately 15% (varying 15%–18% by quarter) and grew double-digit in the quarter; quick commerce is approximately 8%–10% of e-commerce and growing rapidly. Alternate channels overall contribute around 45% (roughly a 55-45 split with general trade). Exports almost doubled.

Distribution strategy. The company continues to expand numeric reach and counter share in a calibrated manner, and has begun direct dealer models in parts of South and West, with plans to accelerate depending on geography. Stock correction is an ongoing calibrated journey, with stocks in a much healthier position than a quarter or two earlier.

New business and leadership. Krishnan Sundaram was appointed Chief Growth & New Business Officer effective August 11, 2026, tasked with identifying, evaluating and scaling new business opportunities. Management flagged opportunities in infrastructure-linked areas, professional lighting, solar, wires, and cables (being seriously looked at), with the ambition to build a company of a different size over five years beyond consumer products and lighting.

Balance sheet strength. Cash equivalents and surplus investments stood at Rs. 884 crores.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity volatility: The external environment remains volatile; future pricing decisions will depend on how commodity prices move. Management noted that at this stage no major price increase is seen as needed to meet margin requirements for the rest of the year, but things could change within two months.
  • Category concentration in fans: Continued share loss in fans, a large category, with corrective actions expected to take two to three quarters to claw back share.
  • Legacy contract exposure: Professional lighting margins remain exposed until legacy pre-war contracts are exited.
  • Competitive intensity: Rising competition across categories could affect market share dynamics, though management views category under-penetration as an offsetting factor.
  • Execution risk on new businesses: New categories (solar, wires, cables, and others) are at an early stage, and the new growth agenda depends on the incoming Chief Growth & New Business Officer.
  • Margin trajectory risk: Quarterly margin movements will depend on commodity prices, category mix, and competitive intensity.
  • Regulatory/accounting items: The company continues to monitor further regulatory developments related to the new Labour Codes and will account for additional impact if required. Prior exceptional items included a Rs. 2,872 lakh labour codes provision, Rs. 2,644 lakh goodwill impairment (Nirlep/Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar unit), and Rs. 2,931 lakh moulds and dies impairment, all recognized in FY26.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Growth guidance. Management targets 8%–10% growth quarter-on-quarter across all businesses, assuming industry growth of at least 6%–7%, with the aim of growing ahead of the industry. This was explicitly not framed as a forward-looking commitment. Observed Q1 FY27 performance was 2.3% overall revenue growth (Consumer Products 1.7%, Lighting Solutions 4.4%) — below the stated target range, with management attributing the gap largely to fans and cooling products.

Consumer Products margin guidance. Management intends to reach a 10% EBIT margin, with the first phase of improvement expected to be quick and large (low-hanging fruit) and the second phase slower and longer. Guidance is for margins to stabilize between 6%–7% over the next two years while investing more behind brands, with steady improvement toward 10% thereafter; the timeline of three to five years depends on multiple factors. Observed Q1 FY27 Consumer Products EBIT margin was 3.9% (versus negative 1.7% year-on-year), showing improvement but still below the guided stabilization band.

Lighting margin guidance. Management expects to return to double-digit margins in overall lighting once legacy professional lighting contracts are exited, describing the current margin as a temporary blip. Observed Q1 FY27 Lighting Solutions EBIT was Rs. 18 Cr versus Rs. 27 Cr a year earlier, with the segment margin below double digits.

Pricing guidance. Management stated that, at this stage, no major price increase is seen as needed to meet margin requirements for the rest of the year, with focus shifting to driving growth since other levers are in place — while cautioning that conditions are volatile and could change.

Share recovery guidance. Management intends to claw back fan share loss over the next two to three quarters, citing corrective actions and knowledge of the causes.

Working capital. Management described working capital as tracking healthy at around 50–60 days, with some seasonal build-up expected; observed cash flow from operations was negative in the quarter due to Morphy acquisition-related tax compliances, which management said was otherwise healthy excluding that element.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from micro-level consumer and weather-driven demand fears in ECD appliances to a macro competitive threat as UltraTech entered the wire and cable segment. Early optimism about C&W outperformance and margin normalisation faded as the stock fell ~69% and bullish calls repeatedly ended in DISAPPOINTMENT, while later reports reframed W&C sector growth as a tailwind but flagged UltraTech’s 5-7% market share ambition as a new headwind. The broker tone evolved from mixed Hold/Buy calls to a final Overweight, reflecting a late-cycle bullish pivot that has yet to prove sustained.

Fears that came true

  • Inflation-led weak consumer sentiment hurt ECD demand, and the stock’s ~69% decline plus repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes confirmed sustained demand weakness.
  • Unseasonal rains impacting cooling products contributed to a prolonged downturn, reflected in consistent negative actual returns across multiple reports.
  • High channel inventory of non-star rated fans and aggressive pricing pressured margins, aligning with the stock’s sustained decline and failed bullish calls.
  • Significant LED price correction and weak kitchen appliance demand undermined revenue growth, correlating with the long streak of DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • RAC volumes being hit and intensifying white goods competition hurt margins, matching the negative actual returns seen through 2025.

Optimism that failed

  • Cable & wire segment outperformance did not sustain, as later reports flagged UltraTech’s entry as a headwind for wire heavy companies.
  • Expected ~13% YoY C&W revenue growth failed amid the sector’s downturn, with the stock falling ~69% and most bullish predictions wrong.
  • Low-base margin normalisation in 1QFY24 did not materialise, as competition intensified and profitability remained under pressure.
  • Healthy B2C lighting and higher B2B/B2G ordering were insufficient to drive recovery, with the stock continuing to fall for years.
