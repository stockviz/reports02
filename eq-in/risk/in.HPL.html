HPL Electric & Power — Q1 FY27 Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Input-cost volatility. The dominant headwind in the quarter was a sharp rise in material costs, particularly metals and industrial plastics, attributed to geopolitical disruption and described as intensifying from February onwards. This drove COGS up 51.27% YoY against revenue growth of 34.52%, compressing gross margin to 30.31% from 38.03% and EBITDA margin to 12.26% from 15.14%. Metering EBIT margin fell to 14.18% from 17.67%, and C&I EBIT margin fell to 8.40% from 11.25%.

Fixed-price metering contracts. In smart metering, sales prices are fixed at the time of order, leaving cost as the variable factor. Semiconductors and critical active/passive components are booked in advance, with some supplier lead times running up to 52 weeks, and exchange-rate fluctuation adds further uncertainty. Industrial plastics are imported, with lead times of up to two months, and pass-through of crude-linked input movements into PVC and similar materials carries a 30–50 day lag.

Limited hedging ability. Management stated that metals cannot be hedged, and that the company sometimes gains when prices fall and sometimes loses when they rise.

Depreciation drag. Depreciation rose 56.55% YoY, reflecting capacity investments of the past two to three years, moderating the translation of operating growth into PAT. PAT grew only 1.15% YoY to ₹18.69 crore despite EBITDA growth of 8.94%.

Wage cost inflation. Minimum wages in Haryana rose approximately 40% in May, with neighbouring states such as UP also moving in that direction. Most of HPL’s manufacturing is located in Haryana, so this added to first-quarter costs.

Execution dependence on AMISPs. Smart metering revenue is execution-led, with quarterly variability linked to AMISP procurement and dispatch schedules. Management noted that execution challenges have been faced by AMISPs rather than by HPL, but HPL’s sales pace depends on them.

Tender and policy environment. The risk set includes working capital intensiveness and dependence on tender-based government and institutional business, alongside policy and regulatory changes, competition, interest-rate and forex movements, and financial-market volatility.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record Q1 scale. Revenue from operations grew 34.52% YoY to ₹515.24 crore, the highest-ever first-quarter revenue, and remained above the ₹500 crore level achieved in Q4 FY26 despite Q1 typically being seasonally light.

C&I as a broadening platform. Consumer & Industrial revenue grew 55.00% YoY to ₹277.59 crore, its highest-ever quarterly revenue, lifting its share of consolidated revenue to approximately 54% from 47%. Growth was broad-based: Wires & Cables grew 78.6% YoY to ₹145.75 crore, Lighting & Electronics grew 78.1% to ₹56.19 crore, Industrial Switchgear grew 19.0%, and Domestic Switchgear grew 6.9%. Wires & Cables achieved more than 40% of its full FY26 revenue of ₹340.74 crore in a single quarter, with demand diversified across builders, industrial OEMs, solar OEMs, telecom, retail and institutional channels. Management noted C&I has now grown for five consecutive quarters, with both absolute sales and growth rates increasing.

Distribution reach. HPL reaches the market through 90+ branch and representative offices, 900+ authorised dealers and 85,000+ retailers. The “one consumer one family” concept incentivises dealers to cross-sell multiple products through the same channel, and new-channel sales are monitored separately from existing-channel sales.

New product launches. A new-generation switch range under the name Cairo was launched, with three more state launches lined up for August. The ATS 370/390 products extend the company’s changeover segment leadership into automatic transfer, with electronics and controllers positioned as a global product launch. Management also flagged ongoing work on integrating electronics and communication into existing switchgear, with results expected over two to three years.

Smart metering visibility. Metering, Systems & Services revenue grew 16.53% YoY to ₹237.65 crore with strong sequential pick-up. The order book stood at ₹3,200+ crore as of 7 August 2026, with more than 96% from Metering, Systems & Services, providing medium-term visibility. Management expects typical execution over one-and-a-half to two years, with visibility of about two years. Approximately 7 crore meters have been installed in India, and a large part of the first phase of 25 crore meters has already been tendered out.

Data centres. HPL has studied the data-centre cable segment and plans to enter with cables specific to data centres, targeting international certifications, with products expected by May–June of next year. The current portfolio does not cover the full range, so the company is not yet focused on it.

Exports and new categories. A Middle East utility recently approved HPL’s metering, and the company is pursuing international certifications. A water meter has been introduced, with gas meters discussed internally as a possible later category, potentially via partnership, acquisition or in-house development.

Backward integration. HPL operates seven factories with strong backward integration: three tool rooms, over 100 injection moulding machines, large in-house electronics manufacturing expanded two years ago, and in-house sheet metal and copper component manufacturing. Relays and certain critical components not historically made in India are being addressed, with government directives pushing localisation of smart metering components. Management indicated actions have been taken but did not confirm a current relay manufacturing setup.

Automation. A new machine for MCBs was commissioned that performs the work of 44 workers, can run nearly 24 hours without a person, and manufactures almost 18,000 MCBs. Seven to eight such machines have been installed across factories, with ROI of three to four years.

Certifications. Most switchgear and certain wire and cable products are IEC compliant. MCBs are DECA certified, and the factory lab is DECA certified, enabling sales to almost 55 countries. Management views this as positioning for an EU FTA opportunity, subject to country-specific testing costs and timelines.

Sector tailwinds. Government-led power sector reforms including RDSS and smart meter rollouts, urbanisation and infrastructure development, energy transition and grid modernisation, export growth potential, expanding digital connectivity and the 5G ecosystem, growing housing demand, and Make in India localisation.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and geopolitical risk: Input-cost volatility in metals and industrial plastics tied to geopolitical conditions, with limited hedging ability and pass-through subject to time lags.
  • Customer concentration dynamics in metering: Adani’s acquisition of Intellis Smart was described as positive because HPL was a preferred and approved vendor with both entities. Management declined to comment on reports of Adani acquiring a meter-manufacturing OEM, but noted HPL’s business is spread across independent AMISPs and is not dependent on one or two.
  • Execution risk: Smart metering revenue depends on AMISP schedules, and management acknowledged that execution pace is outside HPL’s control.
  • Margin risk: Management indicated the current margin level could be treated as a baseline on a conservative basis, with improvement potentially taking an extra quarter or more.
  • Competitive and pricing risk in metering: Metering tender prices are open, and competition is described as changing, with a trade-off for customers between lower prices and experienced partners.
  • Working capital and tender dependence: Working capital intensiveness and dependence on tender-based government and institutional business are identified risks.
  • Macro and market risks: Macroeconomic slowdown, policy and regulatory changes, interest-rate and forex movements, and financial-market volatility.
  • Depreciation and capacity translation: Higher depreciation from prior capacity investments continues to moderate PAT growth.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Revenue growth. Management’s FY27 focus is to scale both verticals. Observed: Q1 FY27 revenue grew 34.52% YoY to ₹515.24 crore, with C&I up 55.00% and Metering up 16.53%. Management stated the next 12–18 months look very strong for C&I, with high double-digit growth expected, and steady revenue growth in smart metering consistent with the last four quarters.

Margin restoration. Management said pricing and product-mix actions are underway to progressively restore margin performance, and that near-term EBITDA margin moderation is expected to be temporary. Observed: EBITDA margin was 12.26% versus 15.14% YoY. Management stated that if the Middle East situation eases, a return to 16–17% margins is possible, and that C&I margins could improve toward Q3 if copper and other prices remain at current levels. Management also said the current margin level could be treated as a conservative baseline, with improvement possibly taking an extra quarter.

Order book execution. Management cited the ₹3,200+ crore order book as providing medium-term visibility, with typical execution over one-and-a-half to two years. Observed: Metering revenue grew 16.53% YoY in Q1 FY27, described as steady momentum with strong sequential pick-up.

Capex. Management stated that from a metering perspective, capex is now mainly maintenance, since capacity is in place, with tools and dies being a substantial component. Additional projects under consideration would have separate revenue streams, and details would be disclosed as information becomes available. Management declined to confirm a specific capex range for FY27–FY29.

Working capital and cash. Management’s FY27 priorities include working capital discipline, calibrated capacity utilisation, and continued R&D investment. Observed: Cash profit increased 21% YoY, finance cost declined 2.32% YoY to ₹22.08 crore, and other expenses were nearly flat YoY (up 0.91%) despite 34.52% revenue growth.

Backward integration. Management said certain components have been identified for backward integration and that work on components and technology is ongoing, with more information to be shared as it becomes available. No relay manufacturing setup exists as of today.

FY26 baseline referenced in the annual report material. For FY25-26, revenue from operations was ₹1,811.10 crore (up 6.52% YoY), EBITDA was ₹281.43 crore (up 10.52% YoY) at a 15.54% margin, and PAT was ₹91.25 crore, with an order book of over ₹3,200 crore as of 22 May 2026.

Broker Narrative

The broker kept a Buy rating throughout, with the smart-meter order book and C&I growth remaining the core pillars of the bull case. However, the narrative shifted from early optimism about margin expansion and rapid smart-meter scale-up to explicit acknowledgment of persistent execution delays, input-cost pressure, and sharp margin contraction. By the last report, C&I was recast as the main growth engine and smart metering was framed as a mature, stable-growth industry rather than a large untapped opportunity.

Fears that came true

  • Execution and dispatch delays persisted: the Q2FY26 metering revenue decline was followed by continued execution delays in the last report, and every subsequent actual return was negative (-30.6%, -12.9%, -17.9%, -3.6%).
  • Raw material price volatility materialized: the last report attributed a 283 bps YoY EBITDA margin decline to 12.3% and gross margin contraction to 30.3% from 38.0% to input-cost volatility and adverse revenue mix.

Optimism that failed

  • The margin-improvement optimism failed: the first report’s 83 bps YoY EBITDA margin expansion reversed into a 283 bps YoY decline, undermining the earnings recovery case.
  • The expected smart-meter scale-up from the large sanctioned-market opportunity did not translate into positive returns: the order book only moved from ₹3,300+ crore to ₹3,200+ crore, and the industry was described as mature/stable growth instead of rapid ramp-up.
