Gravita India Ltd. — Recent Corporate Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Commodity and currency volatility: Volatility in commodity prices and LME cash price fluctuations, along with foreign exchange and currency volatility, are identified as material headwinds. The company operates a back-to-back hedging mechanism intended to lock margins against these movements.
  • Scrap procurement competition: Intensifying competition for quality scrap from both organized and unorganized players is a stated challenge, relevant given the company’s reliance on a global procurement network of 2,200+ touchpoints and 39 own yards.
  • Regulatory and compliance burden: Evolving and more demanding environmental and EPR compliance regulations are flagged as a headwind, even as stricter EPR targets and Battery Waste Management Rules act as tailwinds.
  • Cost inflation: Logistics cost inflation and energy cost inflation are identified as pressures.
  • Geopolitical and trade disruption: Geopolitical risks and trade policy disruptions are cited as headwinds.
  • Integration and execution risk in new verticals: The company flags integration and execution challenges associated with expanding into new verticals such as lithium-ion and copper recycling.
  • Customs demand order: A demand order from the Office of the Commissioner of Customs (Preventive), Jodhpur amounting to Rs. 70.10 crore (excluding interest, fine, and penalty) was received for alleged violation of pre-import conditions under an advance authorisation licence for October 2017–January 2019. The company has appealed and, based on legal and tax opinion, believes it has a case on merit and that the order will not have a material impact; no adjustments have been made in the financial results.
  • Subsidiary closures: The Board approved closure of operations of Gravita Metal Inc. (subsidiary) effective 1 August 2026, as the same line of business is carried out at the Jaipur facility with greater operational and cost efficiencies. Gravita Metal Inc. contributed 2.65% of turnover, 0.05% of net worth, and 0.55% of PAT as on 31 March 2026. A step-down subsidiary in South Africa (Recyclers South Africa (PTY) Ltd) was also closed during the quarter ended 30 June 2026, with no material impact as it had no business activity.
  • Foreign currency translation drag: Other comprehensive income for Q1 FY27 includes a foreign currency translation loss of Rs. 11.89 crore, contributing to total other comprehensive loss of Rs. 11.16 crore.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Macro and policy tailwinds: Strong macroeconomic growth in India (estimated real GDP growth of 7.6% for FY 2025-26 and 6.9% for FY 2026-27), increasing government focus on public infrastructure capital expenditure, stricter EPR targets, Battery Waste Management Rules mandating recycled content, and removal of import duties on copper scrap.
  • Demand for recycled materials: Surging demand for secondary/recycled materials across lead, aluminium, copper, plastic, rubber, and lithium-ion batteries, alongside the global transition towards clean energy and circular economies.
  • Copper capacity expansion at Mundra: On 19 August 2026, the company announced plans to add 59,200 MTPA of copper recycling capacity at its Mundra, Gujarat facility in phases, with capex of approximately Rs. 64 crore funded through internal accruals, expected to be commissioned in phases by 31 March 2029. The capacity is intended to serve as backward integration for the proposed Mandvi plant in Gujarat.
  • RMIL acquisition and copper entry: Gravita acquired a 99.44% stake in Rashtriya Metal Industries Limited (RMIL) for Rs. 561.84 crore, entering the copper and copper alloys segment. RMIL, founded in 1946, operates a facility in Sarigram, Gujarat with 31,200 MTPA capacity and reported FY26 revenue of Rs. 1,040 crore and EBITDA of Rs. 82 crore. During Q1 FY27, Gravita acquired an additional 0.62% stake for Rs. 3.48 crore, raising its shareholding to 99.57%. Stated synergies include portfolio diversification, margin enhancement, access to electrical and defence segments, cross-selling, and backward integration.
  • LME brand listing: The Mundra plant received London Metal Exchange (LME) Brand Listing for its lead metal under the brand name “GRAVITA M,” enabling worldwide LME warehouse deliverability.
  • Credit rating upgrade: ICRA Limited upgraded the company’s credit rating, reflecting its financial profile and capital management. The company carries an AA- external credit rating from ICRA and India Ratings.
  • Capacity expansion and Vision 2030: A capex plan of approximately Rs. 1,700 crore up to FY 2029 aims to more than double processing capacity to over 8,00,000 MTPA. Vision 2030 targets ~20–25% volume CAGR, sustained high ROIC (~25%), 30–35% profitability growth, increasing share of value-added products (~45–50%), expanding non-lead contribution (~35–40%), higher renewable power usage (~25–30%), and 8–10% reduction in energy consumption.
  • Phagi capacity expansion: During Q1 FY27, the company expanded Phagi lead recycling capacity by 40,500 MTPA to 75,819 MTPA and incurred capex of ~Rs. 30 crore.
  • Pilot lithium-ion battery recycling: A pilot lithium-ion battery recycling project was commissioned.
  • Renewable energy: Term sheet signed for 4.7 MWe of hybrid RE power for RMIL; 1.16 million units of RE power generated in Q1 FY27 with a 26% YoY jump in RE power generation.

3. Key Risks

  • Contingent liability from corporate guarantee: On 20 August 2026, the Finance Committee approved a corporate guarantee not exceeding Rs. 100 crore in favour of lenders for credit facilities of up to Rs. 600 crore availed/to be availed by RMIL, a material subsidiary. The guarantee constitutes a contingent liability; there is no immediate impact except to the extent of the guaranteed amount if RMIL is unable to meet repayment obligations. The transaction is at arm’s length. Mr. Rajat Agrawal (Promoter and Chairman cum Managing Director) is interested, and his daughter, Ms. Karvi Agrawal, is Managing Director of RMIL; the promoter and immediate family collectively hold 6 equity shares in RMIL.
  • Commodity price and currency risk: As above, LME cash price fluctuations and FX volatility remain core risks, mitigated by back-to-back hedging.
  • Execution risk on expansion: The Mundra copper capacity addition and the broader Rs. 1,700 crore capex plan carry execution and commissioning risk; the Mundra capacity is expected to be commissioned in phases only by 31 March 2029.
  • Integration risk on acquisitions: The RMIL acquisition and entry into copper, lithium-ion, and planned steel diversification carry integration and execution challenges.
  • Regulatory risk: Evolving EPR and environmental compliance requirements, plus the ongoing customs appeal (Rs. 70.10 crore demand), represent regulatory risk.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The company’s own press release notes that forward-looking statements are subject to risks and uncertainties including regulatory approvals, market conditions, and execution challenges, and actual results may differ materially.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • Q1 FY27 performance vs. commentary: Management stated that Q1 FY27 delivered YoY growth of 42% in revenue, 29% in EBITDA, and 14% in PAT. The consolidated results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 show revenue from operations of Rs. 1,475.06 crore (vs. Rs. 1,039.94 crore in Q1 FY26), profit before tax of Rs. 131.28 crore (vs. Rs. 115.93 crore), and profit after tax of Rs. 106.37 crore (vs. Rs. 93.06 crore), consistent with the stated growth trajectory. Volume growth was 4% YoY.
  • Segment performance: Copper segment revenue was Rs. 376.05 crore in Q1 FY27 (vs. Rs. 51.78 crore in the preceding quarter and nil in Q1 FY26), reflecting the RMIL acquisition consolidated from 12 March 2026. Lead segment revenue was Rs. 954.75 crore (vs. Rs. 928.17 crore in Q1 FY26). Aluminium was Rs. 110.52 crore (vs. Rs. 94.41 crore). Plastics was Rs. 26.63 crore (vs. Rs. 16.41 crore).
  • FY 2025-26 reported performance: Consolidated revenue of approximately Rs. 4,265 crore (10% YoY growth), EBITDA of Rs. 452 crore (12% growth), and PAT of Rs. 379 crore (21% growth). The audited consolidated results for the year ended 31 March 2026 show revenue from operations of Rs. 4,265.27 crore, profit before tax of Rs. 448.25 crore, and profit after tax of Rs. 378.33 crore.
  • Vision 2030 targets: Management targets ~20–25% volume CAGR, sustained ROIC of ~25%+, 30–35% profitability growth, value-added products at ~45–50% of mix, non-lead businesses at ~35–40%, renewable power at ~25–30%, and 8–10% energy efficiency improvement. These are long-term aspirational targets; observed Q1 FY27 volume growth of 4% YoY is below the stated volume CAGR range, while revenue, EBITDA, and PAT growth were above the profitability growth range on a YoY basis.
  • Capex guidance vs. actual: Management guides a cumulative capex plan of approximately Rs. 1,700 crore up to FY 2029. In Q1 FY27, actual capex incurred was ~Rs. 30 crore, alongside the Mundra copper expansion announced at ~Rs. 64 crore and the RMIL acquisition at Rs. 561.84 crore (plus Rs. 3.48 crore for the additional 0.62% stake).
  • Dividend history: The company reports a 15-year consistent dividend payout history.
  • Capacity and footprint: 14 manufacturing plants, 7 business verticals, 4.76 lakh+ MT production capacity, 3.30 lakh+ MT scrap collection, 2,200+ touch points globally, 39 own yards, 400+ customers, and 2,13,000 MT+ recycled products delivered.

Broker Narrative

The narrative stayed structurally bullish on recycling growth, policy-driven formalisation, and capacity expansion, but shifted from broad macro/ESG optimism to a more cautious, execution-focused stance. The last report acknowledges near-term EBITDA misses, geopolitical/logistics disruptions, scrap scarcity, and rising debt, while elevating copper and value-addition as the main growth engines. Earlier themes such as ADC-12 aluminium hedging and lithium-ion battery recycling faded, and the balance-sheet assumption flipped from positive FCF strength to projected net debt of INR20bn+ in FY28.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning about geopolitical exposure in Sri Lanka/Africa materialised: the last report blames geopolitical tensions for logistics disruption, a ~5% YoY lead-volume decline, and the Q1FY27 EBITDA miss.
  • The initial concern about lower scrap availability was realised beyond aluminium: the last report cites lower scrap availability as a contributor to lead-volume decline and supply-chain challenges.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of positive free cash flow and a strong balance sheet during peak capex has not panned out; the last report now cautions that aggressive capex and working capital needs could push net debt to INR20bn+ in FY28.
