Headwinds and Challenges

Electrotherm (India) Limited is navigating a set of material headwinds that span both external market conditions and company-specific legal and financial matters.

On the operational and market side, the company has flagged adverse global tensions and challenging market conditions, including a fall in demand for its products. It has also cited high costs of raw material and production, which weigh on margins. Separately, the company remains in default on repayment of dues to financial institutions, a continuing financial strain.

On the legal and regulatory side, the company has been dealing with a search conducted by the Directorate of Enforcement, Zonal Office, Ahmedabad (ED), related to a complaint by Bank of India. This led to the freezing of bank accounts of the company and of Mr. Shailesh Bhandari. The company has since been permitted to operate its bank accounts as per orders passed by the Hon’ble Gujarat High Court, and a seized car was released on submission of a Fixed Deposit Receipt (FDR) in favour of the ED. These developments were communicated to the stock exchanges through letters dated 17th January, 2025, 31st January, 2025, 18th June, 2025, and 5th February, 2026.

More recently, the ED has filed a complaint under section 44(1)(b) read with Section 45(1) of the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA) before the Hon’ble Special Court for PMLA Cases at Ahmedabad against the company, Mr. Shailesh Bhandari (Promoter & Executive Vice Chairman), and Mr. Mukesh Bhandari (Promoter & Ex-Chairman). Based on notices issued under section 223 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023 (BNSS) for hearing on cognizance, the Hon’ble Court provided a copy of the complaint to the company and to Mr. Shailesh Bhandari on 3rd August, 2026.

The company and Mr. Shailesh Bhandari had already challenged the freezing of bank accounts before the Hon’ble Gujarat High Court and the Hon’ble PMLA Appellate Tribunal, New Delhi, and had sought quashing of the Enforcement Case Information Report (ECIR) registered by the ED before the Hon’ble Gujarat High Court. These matters remain pending for further hearings.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

The company identifies several operational levers as tailwinds and growth prospects. These include operations synergy and operations efficiency, reducing supply chain and procurement risks, ensuring continuous supply, improving inventory management, and maintaining uninterrupted business operations. It also points to a secured payment mechanism as a means of generating liquidity.

On the governance and leadership front, the Board of Directors approved the appointment of Mr. Davesh Khandelwal as an Additional Director and Whole Time Director for a period of three years effective from 26th August, 2026 to 25th August, 2029. The Board also approved the appointment of Mr. Rajesh Bhalchandra Patel as an Additional Director in the category of Non-Executive Non-Independent Director, liable to retire by rotation. Both appointments are subject to shareholder approval.

Key Risks

The material risks facing the company include adverse global tensions and market conditions, specifically a fall in demand for its products. High raw material and production costs pose a continuing risk to profitability. The company also faces the risk associated with default in repayment of dues to financial institutions.

The ongoing ED proceedings represent a significant legal and regulatory risk. The complaint filed under the PMLA against the company and two of its promoters, along with the pending challenges to the freezing of bank accounts and the quashing of the ECIR, introduce uncertainty. The outcome of these proceedings and the related litigation before the Hon’ble Gujarat High Court and the Hon’ble PMLA Appellate Tribunal remain open.

Additionally, the company is seeking shareholder approval for material related party transactions during FY 2026-27, which is a matter requiring investor attention.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Management has stated that it expects profitability to increase drastically with continuous efforts. This forward-looking guidance stands alongside the observed performance of a net loss of Rs. 16.18 Crore for FY 2025-26. The company also continues to report ongoing defaults in repayment of dues to financial institutions, and it is seeking shareholder approval for material related party transactions during FY 2026-27. The gap between the guidance of a drastic increase in profitability and the reported net loss for FY 2025-26, combined with the continuing defaults, is a key area where guidance and observed performance diverge.
