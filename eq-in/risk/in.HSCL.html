HSCL Q1FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical disruption: The quarter was marked by a difficult geopolitical backdrop (referenced as the West Asia situation in April and May). Management stated that despite this, supply-chain resilience built over the years meant there was no dependence on West Asia for functioning.
  • Forex volatility: Standalone results show a foreign exchange fluctuation loss of Rs. 25.27 Cr in Q1FY27, versus a gain of Rs. 9.80 Cr a year ago and a loss of Rs. 38.33 Cr in Q4FY26. Management attributed this to severe currency volatility and indicated the FX drag was expected to end after this quarter.
  • Mining operations paused: The “other” segment EBIT fell sharply from about Rs. 25 Cr in the prior quarter to around Rs. 1 Cr, because mining did not operate in the quarter. Management said operations are awaiting licensing/environmental clearance, with no clear visibility — potentially three to six months.
  • Anode customer approval cycle: Commercial qualification for anode material runs through sample stages A–D and typically takes 1.5 to 2 years. Management said Himadri has advanced the process and expects approvals in place by the time its plant is operational, but the timeline was not disclosed.
  • Birla Tyres not yet EBITDA positive: Although quarterly sales reached about Rs. 127 Cr, the business is not yet EBITDA positive. Management expects EBITDA breakeven and cash positivity within the current financial year.
  • LFP competitive intensity: Global LFP capacity is entirely in China, dominated by the top five to six players holding 75–80% of capacity. An analyst questioned whether LFP economics could work at China-parity pricing; management countered that Himadri is not dependent on China for raw materials and has cost advantages, without disclosing sourcing or margins.
  • CNT scale is small initially: The 200 MTPA CNT plant is described as a first step to stabilise product and build a global customer base; revenue from it will be less than Rs. 100 Cr (roughly Rs. 50–70 Cr), with meaningful revenue only after a future scale-up.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record quarterly performance: Consolidated revenue of Rs. 1,432 Cr (up 28% YoY), EBITDA of Rs. 313 Cr (up 33% YoY, 22% margin) and PAT of Rs. 228 Cr (up 27% YoY, 16% margin) — the highest-ever quarterly revenue, EBITDA and PAT. Standalone revenue was Rs. 1,274 Cr (up 16%), EBITDA Rs. 301 Cr (24% margin) and PAT Rs. 223 Cr (18% margin).
  • Product mix shift: Performance was supported by product mix changes in the core business and continued ramp-up in speciality materials, with earnings growing faster than revenue.
  • Carbon Nanotube (CNT): Indigenous CNT technology developed in-house; India’s first CNT plant, 200 MTPA, capex about Rs. 70 Cr, targeted commissioning Q4FY27. Applications span lithium-ion batteries, semiconductors, electronics, sensors, polymers, coatings and aerospace. Global CNT market cited at around 30,000 metric tons and growing.
  • Super Speciality Carbon Black (SSCB): Conversion of 6,000 MTPA of existing carbon black capacity, capex about Rs. 170 Cr, targeted Q4FY28. Niche market cited at around 300,000 metric tons with premium realisations and significantly higher margins than speciality carbon black; expected revenue potential around 3x the capital investment (roughly Rs. 500 Cr).
  • New Energy Materials: Global lithium-ion battery demand projected to rise from about 1.6 TWh in 2025 to nearly 6.8 TWh by 2035.
    • Anode: 200 MTPA facility at Mahistikry commissioned April 2026, backed by 10+ years of in-house R&D, feedstock flexibility and backward integration. Revenue potential cited at Rs. 120–130 Cr; Rs. 120 Cr capex already spent, with no further capex announced yet.
    • LFP cathode: 2,000 MTPA demo capacity targeted Q3FY27; Phase 1 of 40,000 MTPA targeted FY28 with capex of Rs. 1,125 Cr and revenue potential of about Rs. 3,000 Cr at today’s values; long-term ambition of 200,000 MTPA with announced capex of Rs. 4,800 Cr, targeting 2–3% of the global LFP market in five to six years. Positioned as the first commercial LFP CAM facility globally outside China.
  • Anthraquinone and Carbazole: Total planned capacity 5,300 MTPA; Phase 1 of 2,600 MTPA targeted Q2FY27 and Phase 2 of 2,700 MTPA targeted Q2FY28. India’s first commercial plant of its kind, reducing import dependence; revenue potential cited at Rs. 250–300 Cr.
  • Coal Tar Pitch core: World’s largest single-location fully integrated carbon complex with 600,000 MTPA distillation capacity, over 65% domestic market share, and Haldia and Mangalore liquid pitch terminals strengthening global reach. Capacity utilisation was 80%, expected to exceed 90% during the year.
  • Birla Tyres turnaround: Commercial operations began 29 May 2025; network of 49 distributors (40 domestic, 9 international) and 1,000+ dealers; three new agriculture product lines launched (AGRIWIN, AGRIPLUS, AGRILEAP T40); over 400 new SKUs under development; phased conversion of Truck and Bus Bias capacity to OTR over three years; PCR facility targeted FY28 for EV and SUV entry; target of Rs. 3,000 Cr top line in four to five years.
  • Strategic investments: Sicona secured AUD 45 million in ARENA funding for silicon-carbon anode commercialisation; Himadri raised its IBC stake from 17.29% to 19.44%, with Himadri LFP and anode materials to be used in IBC’s Prabal cells.
  • Capital discipline: Announced capex of about Rs. 2,000 Cr (Rs. 1,125 Cr LFP, Rs. 368 Cr for CNT and SSCB, Rs. 500 Cr Birla Tyres), split roughly Rs. 1,000 Cr this year and Rs. 1,000 Cr next year, to be funded from free cash flow with no incremental debt.
  • ESG recognition: EcoVadis Platinum reaffirmed (top 1% globally), CDP Climate B and Supplier Engagement A, ICRA ESG combined rating of 82, plus multiple safety and sustainability awards.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory/clearance delays: Mining restart depends on environmental clearance with no visibility; management indicated it could take three to six months.
  • Approval timelines in battery materials: Anode qualification takes 1.5–2 years through sample stages; LFP ramp-up is linked to customer approvals.
  • Competition from China: LFP capacity is concentrated in China with top players holding 75–80%; CNT has only a few global manufacturers. Pricing and margin sustainability in these segments face external competitive pressure.
  • Execution and scale-up risk: Multiple simultaneous projects (CNT, SSCB, LFP, anthraquinone/carbazole, Birla Tyres modernisation and OTR conversion) carry commissioning timelines of Q2FY27 through FY29.
  • Forex and commodity volatility: Currency swings drove a Rs. 25.27 Cr standalone FX loss; raw material price increases lifted finished product prices, affecting revenue composition.
  • Margin disclosure gaps: Management declined to disclose margins for CNT and SSCB, and contested an analyst’s calculation suggesting negative gross spread in LFP, citing confidential cost and sourcing.
  • Birla Tyres profitability: Not yet EBITDA positive; breakeven is guided for the current financial year, with continued capex and modernisation over three years.
  • Other income variability: Other income is expected to vary 30–40% quarter-on-quarter, driven by treasury deployment, mutual fund investments and mark-to-market on investments in IBC, Sicona and Birla Tyres NCDs.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

  • Record results delivered: Management reported the highest-ever quarterly revenue, EBITDA and PAT, with consolidated revenue up 28% YoY, EBITDA up 33% and PAT up 27%. Standalone revenue rose 16%, EBITDA 29% and PAT 22%.
  • PAT guidance maintained: The previously stated target of Rs. 1,100 Cr PAT by FY28 was reaffirmed, with management stating nothing negative had emerged and that things were moving positively. No quarterly or annual guidance is provided by policy.
  • Capex guidance: Total announced capex of about Rs. 2,000 Cr, with roughly Rs. 1,000 Cr in the current year and Rs. 1,000 Cr next year, to be self-funded from free cash flow with no incremental debt.
  • Capacity utilisation: Core business utilisation was 80%, with management guiding to 90%+ during the year.
  • Birla Tyres: Guided to EBITDA breakeven and cash positivity within the current financial year, with a Rs. 3,000 Cr top-line target in four to five years; currently not EBITDA positive, with quarterly sales of about Rs. 127 Cr.
  • Anode: 200 MTPA commissioned April 2026; sample B supplied to customers in India and globally; management expects approvals to be in place by plant operation, though the timeline was not disclosed.
  • LFP: 2,000 MTPA demo targeted Q3FY27 and 40,000 MTPA Phase 1 in FY28, with 200,000 MTPA targeted in five years at 2–3% global market share.
  • CNT and SSCB: Commissioning targets of Q4FY27 and Q4FY28 respectively, with revenue potential of under Rs. 100 Cr for CNT and around Rs. 500 Cr for SSCB at 3x asset ratio.
  • Anthraquinone and Carbazole: Phase 1 (2,600 MTPA) targeted Q2FY27 and Phase 2 (2,700 MTPA) Q2FY28, with revenue potential of Rs. 250–300 Cr.
  • Cost optimisation: Other expenses declined quarter-on-quarter, helped by lower outbound freight and ongoing cost optimisation, partly offset by the FX loss.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from an uncomplicated bullish stance in the first report (no headwinds or tailwinds flagged, Buy call) to a cautiously mixed assessment in the last report (Hold call), where concrete operational headwinds such as sales decline, sand mining revenue pause, and rising D&A were explicitly acknowledged alongside growth optimism around new projects, value-added portfolio expansion, and Birla Tyres ramp-up. The persistent theme of growth optimism through Birla Tyres and project pipeline endured, but the dominant shift was toward risk awareness that culminated in a Hold rating.

Fears that came true

  • Sales volume declined 5.9% YoY as flagged, directly materializing and correlated with the -10.3% actual return on 2026-07-17.
  • Sand mining revenue paused pending licence renewal, dragging the other segment’s EBIT, materialized as a current headwind.
  • D&A rose 45.5% YoY due to commissioning of the new specialty carbon black plant, pressuring profitability and correlating with the negative actual return.
  • Downside risk of coal tar price volatility (drop in coal tar prices or rise in crude coal tar prices) impacting legacy business profitability materialized, evidenced by the -10.3% actual return.
  • Rise in competitive intensity from China for the coal chemical value chain contributed to the disappointing -10.3% actual return and the DISAPPOINTMENT pattern across recent reports.

Optimism that failed

  • Potential benefit from realisation jump post Middle East crisis failed to materialize, as the last report showed a -10.3% actual return rather than any price realization uplift.
  • Birla Tyres ramp-up to EBITDA positive during FY27 appears behind schedule, with persistent DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and a -10.3% actual return suggesting weaker-than-expected progress.
  • Faster-than-expected battery chemicals ramp-up (listed as an upside risk) failed to deliver, with the negative -10.3% actual return indicating the new project pipeline has not yet offset legacy headwinds.
