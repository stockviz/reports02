AARTIIND — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

West Asia conflict and supply chain disruption (Q1 FY27) - The West Asia crisis impacted the supply chain, leading to significant volatility in raw material prices. - Volumes of various key products were lower on a quarter-on-quarter basis. - The Middle East, which previously accounted for about 15% of revenues, was significantly impacted; volumes from the region were down to 2% from the earlier ~15% due to the ongoing conflict. - Volumes recovered compared to Q1 FY26 but were sequentially lower in Q1 FY27 on account of supply chain issues linked to the West Asia conflict.

Working capital, debt and finance costs - Working capital increased due to a rise in input prices and exports, leading to higher debt and finance costs. - Net Debt-Equity Ratio (consolidated) rose to 0.80 as of June 30, 2026, from 0.72 as of March 31, 2026 and 0.66 as of June 30, 2025. - Finance costs (consolidated) were ₹83 Crs in Q1 FY27, compared with ₹112 Crs in Q4 FY26 and ₹60 Crs in Q1 FY26.

Segment-specific pressures - PDA: Capacity utilisation impacted by subdued US demand and competition from China. - Polymer & Additives: Softness in the DCB and PDA chains impacted Q1 performance. - Agrochemicals & Fertilizers: Stable demand continues across application segments, though margin and pricing pressure remains persistent in select products; volumes marginally lower due to subdued demand and resistance to source amid elevated RM prices. - Dyes, Pigments & Printing Inks: Segment facing demand headwinds due to soft demand in a high price environment and seasonality. - Pharma: Fluoro products margins continue to remain under pressure driven by high competitive intensity. - Energy & Additives: Feedstock, refining product margins, and Gasoline-Naphtha cracks pose a challenge.

Project execution constraints - Labour constraints impacted the chemical recycling of plastics project timeline by about 3 months; commissioning now expected in H2 FY27. - Zone IV projects were delayed by 2 quarters due to labour constraints and war-related issues.

Other - The company recognised a provision of ₹7 Crs in an earlier period (FY 2025-26) for an advance paid to an Infrastructure NBFC/developer for an industrial land parcel in Gujarat, as the NBFC ran into financial stress and the land parcels are stuck under dispute. - An incremental provision of ₹15 Crs was made in the March 2026 audited results under the statutory impact of new labour codes. - The company’s Long Term Issuers & Bank Facilities credit rating is AA/Negative from CRISIL and India Ratings.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Q1 FY27 performance and mix optimisation - AIL took steps to optimise product and geography mix to manage the West Asia situation, resulting in improved EBITDA. - Monetisation of low-cost inventory and FX gains supported EBITDA growth. - Revenue increase was primarily driven by higher input prices passed on to customers. - Alternate markets absorbed part of the volume impact; volume recovery expected in Q2 FY27.

Capacity expansion - Fuel additives capacity expansion from 290 to 360 KTPA completed in July 2026. - DCB volumes increased, supported by PDCB and downstream demand; further capacity debottlenecking to 140 KTPA is underway. - NT and Ethylation capacity utilisation driven by MEA, DEA demand; expected to improve in CY26 with DEA downstream integration investment (PEDA) and increasing demand outlook for MEA.

Partnerships and joint ventures - Existing long-term contracts progressing well and remain stable. - JV with Superform (DCA downstream) at near-commissioning stage, expected commercialisation in Q2 FY27; tailwinds in one end application may support quicker capacity utilisation; marketing efforts initiated. - Re Aarti (chemical recycling of plastics): critical equipment delivered, on-ground execution underway, engagement with potential pyrolysis oil customers continues; expected to commission in H2 FY27.

Cost savings and digital/AI - Successful cost savings drive across all value chains generating ideas on product yield; energy efficiency catalysed through digital and advanced analytics and best-in-class engineering solutions, with 70% of ideas implemented and generating value. - Building in-house digital and analytics capability; 40+ GenAI use cases executed over the last quarter.

Sustainability recognition - Ecovadis Platinum rating with a score of 87 out of 100, placing AIL among the top 1% of companies.

Three-year EBITDA growth drivers (FY25–FY28) - Cost Optimisation (₹150–200 crore): switching to back pressure turbine to improve cogen, renewable power phase 2, waste energy stream utilisation, ETP cost optimisation, fixed cost optimisation, yield improvement, digital and advanced analytics-led cost excellence — majorly completed. - Volume and margin ramp-up (₹350–550 crore): acid, DCB & NCB value chain ramp-up; ethylation & NT volume ramp-up with downstream integration for select ethylation product; MMA capacity expansion completed with volume ramp-up to follow; fluorination and speciality chemicals ramp-up — on track as planned. - CAPEX-led growth (₹300–450 crore): pilot commissioned to fuel new product development; MPP commissioning and ramp-up; Zone 4 commissioning and ramp-up; Augene & Re Aarti JV commissioning and ramp-up — all projects to commission in FY27.

Long-term growth focus areas - MPP and Zone 4 commercialisation: R&D and MPP to support quick development, qualification and commercialisation of new advanced chemistries; chlorotoluene commissioning and ramp-up to open new opportunities in agro and pharma. - Entry into adjacent markets and new platforms: leverage current capabilities into advanced materials, battery materials, defense and coatings; develop newer growth platforms in sustainability/circularity. - Strategic alliances and CDMO: promote India as a manufacturing destination of choice and partner with customers for new India-based investments; leverage R&D strength to provide CDMO services.

Zone IV Phase I commissioning - Phase I of the Zone IV project at Jhagadia, Gujarat, was commissioned, bringing Calcium Chloride, PEDA, and part of the Multipurpose Plant on stream to strengthen downstream integration, application diversification, and flexible manufacturing capabilities. - The project supports commercialisation of high-value and niche products, several expected to be manufactured in India for the first time. - Phase I broadens applications across agrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, coatings, polymers, and energy and additives.

FY2026 context - In FY2026, revenue from operations reached ₹9,018 Crs, EBITDA stood at ₹1,172 Crs, and PAT was ₹419 Crs. - The company completed a peak capital expenditure cycle of ₹1,125 Crs primarily directed toward the Zone IV expansion. - Aarti Industries serves over 1,100 customers across 60+ countries and holds leading market positions, including #1 globally in Di Chloro Benzene (DCB). - The company received the 2026 Gallup Exceptional Workplace Award and achieved an EcoVadis Platinum rating.

Favourable external developments - China’s VAT removal policy from April 2026 improved margins in select value chains; China removed the export VAT rebate for major NCB chain products, supporting improved margins. - Depreciation of ₹ versus ¥ expected to support margin improvement. - Favorable trade agreements (India-EU FTA and US-India trade dynamics) and structural shifts favouring India as a preferred specialty chemicals manufacturing hub. - China’s anti-involution measures improving pricing discipline; expansion into sunrise sectors (EVs, renewable energy, electronics, semiconductors, advanced materials). - Long-term strategic partnerships including a USD 150 million agrochemical supply agreement.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical friction and global volatility, including the West Asia conflict, tariff uncertainties and new U.S. tariffs.
  • Raw material price volatility (such as price corrections in benzene and anilene) and inventory valuation losses.
  • Intense competition and capacity additions, particularly from China.
  • Supply chain disruptions, logistics bottlenecks, elevated freight costs, and foreign exchange fluctuations.
  • Project execution risk: labour constraints and war-related issues delayed Zone IV projects by 2 quarters; labour constraints impacted the chemical recycling project timeline by about 3 months.
  • Demand softness in key end markets (US and China downstream demand softened in Q1; recovery expected in Q2).
  • Margin pressure in select products (agrochemicals, fluoro products) and competitive intensity.
  • Working capital and leverage risk: higher debt and finance costs; net debt-equity rose to 0.80 (consolidated) as of June 30, 2026.
  • Regulatory/statutory risk: impact of new labour codes (₹15 Crs incremental provision in March 2026 results).
  • Counterparty risk: provision of ₹7 Crs for advance paid to an Infrastructure NBFC/developer that ran into financial stress.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from a cautious Hold centered on domestic demand weakness, slow capacity ramps, and cost absorption issues in textiles and agrochemicals, to an Accumulate rating dominated by West Asia-driven raw material inflation and subsidy viability gaps. Persistent themes included margin pressure and operational volatility, while optimism shifted from internal capacity utilization and specialty margins to government NBS allocations and DAP support. Early fears about demand, Chinese competition, and balance sheet stress correlated with 2024 DISAPPOINTMENTs and deep negative returns, whereas the initial volume-growth and margin tailwinds largely failed to materialize.

Fears that came true

  • Persisting demand weakness in key end-use segments materialized as the stock delivered -43.4%, -37.5%, and -33.5% returns in 2024, driving DISAPPOINTMENTs on bullish calls.
  • Slower-than-expected ramp-up of capacities and plant shutdowns caused elevated operating expenses, correlating with -27.5% and -27.8% actual returns on Accumulate/Add calls.
  • Elevated cost pressures from lower absorption of expansion-led costs squeezed margins, aligning with the -24.3% and -29.4% returns on Hold/Overweight calls.
  • Weak geographic mix and weak volumes linked to textile/dyes dragged down results, matching DISAPPOINTMENTs when calls predicted gains.
  • Demand slowdown in discretionary markets contributed to negative returns and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes on Buy/Hold calls through 2023-2024.
  • Competition from Chinese producers in agrochemicals eroded segment profitability, correlating with -37.5% and -17.5% actual returns.
  • Stretched balance sheet limited recovery options, contributing to DISAPPOINTMENTs on Add/Buy calls as the stock fell sharply.

Optimism that failed

  • Expected 20% EBITDA and 12% PAT CAGR over FY23-25E failed as the stock posted -43.4% and -37.5% returns in 2024, contradicting the growth thesis.
  • Management’s 25% volume growth guidance for FY24 did not materialize, with DISAPPOINTMENTs on Hold/Buy calls showing -27.5% and -24.3% actuals.
  • Long-term contracts ramp-up targets of ~70%, optimal, and ~60-70% were not achieved, correlating with negative returns on Accumulate/Add calls.
  • NCB plant commissioning in Q1FY24 and specialty chemical margin potential of 25-30% failed to drive upside, as the stock fell -33.5% and -29.4%.
  • Chloro toluene plant reaching ~20% of global capacity did not offset losses, with actual returns of -27.8% and -24.3% on relevant calls.
