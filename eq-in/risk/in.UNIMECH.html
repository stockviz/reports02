UNIMECH: Q1 FY27 Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Tariff uncertainty. Management flagged the potential imposition of 100% US tariffs on India as a “very grey” and fluid situation that is difficult to quantify. The company’s response has been to build mitigants rather than forecast impact.

Working capital stretch. Working capital days stood at approximately 130 days, up slightly due to the Hobel acquisition. Management expects this to trend upward to 160 days-plus by end-FY27, as new long-cycle aerospace programs and nuclear tenders require higher inventory commitments and longer production and customer acceptance cycles before billing.

Qualification costs. The precision manufacturing business continues to invest in customer qualification, first articles, and new product introduction, which carry higher initial costs before transitioning into serial production. Nearly 10% of available capacity is committed to these non-revenue-yielding programs.

Order book dip. The consolidated order book (including Hobel) stood at approximately INR280 crores as of 30 June 2026, marginally lower than the previously indicated consolidated level. Management attributed this to strong execution during the quarter and customer pull-ins driven by robust end-market demand.

Other income normalisation. Other income fell to INR7 crores in Q1 FY27, roughly half of Q4 FY26, because treasury surplus was deployed toward the Hobel acquisition. Management guided that Q2 other income would be about half of Q1, and that FY26’s ~INR46 crores level would not repeat.

Dilution risk at Dheya. Dheya is looking to raise approximately USD10 million through equity and debt. Unimech intends to participate, but depending on final structure, its percentage ownership may see modest dilution.

Capacity commitment ahead of demand. A portion of existing capacity is being committed to prove-outs and qualifications, and additional capacity investment is being advanced earlier than originally planned.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record quarterly performance. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was INR1,076.2 mn, up 71% YoY and 32% QoQ. EBITDA (excluding other income) was INR392.5 mn, up 98% YoY and 11% QoQ. PAT was INR278.6 mn, up 46% YoY and 7% QoQ. EBITDA margin was approximately 36.5%; gross margin 65%.

Hobel Bellows integration. The acquisition completed on 27 April 2026, so Q1 included only two months of Hobel revenue (~INR22 crores, 21% of total revenue). A full three-month contribution is expected in Q2. Hobel adds metallic bellows, flexible tubing, and tubular structures, broadening exposure to power generation, locomotive, and advanced industrial markets. AS9100 certification for the Vizag facility is targeted for completion by Q4 FY27.

FACC long-term supply agreement. Signed with FACC Austria (aerospace Tier-1), with an initial value of USD7.5 million over five years and scope for expansion. Management described it as Unimech’s entry into recurring aerospace component supplies under a long-term program, covering precision-engineered aerospace components and flying parts.

Advanced-stage engine Tier-1 discussions. Qualification programs and commercial discussions are ongoing with several airframe Tier-1s and an engine Tier-1, with one leading engine Tier-1 at an advanced stage.

Nuclear order wins. Cumulative nuclear order wins stand at approximately INR87 crores, with execution planned across H2 FY27 and into next year. Management noted four new nuclear reactors coming up and is eyeing that opportunity.

Qualification pipeline. 165 FAIs were completed during the quarter, and engagement was initiated with six additional prospective customers. Management targets a meaningful increase in qualification rates versus the prior year. Roughly 80% of qualified PCA parts are moving into serial production.

Saudi JV (Dheya/Kanoo). The joint venture with Yusuf Bin Ahmed Kanoo Group in Saudi Arabia continues toward operationalisation. Unimech is infusing approximately USD10 million into the JV during the month. The venture establishes Unimech’s first manufacturing footprint outside India, targeting energy and advanced manufacturing localization in Saudi Arabia.

Dheya deep-tech progress. Dheya was selected among the top 100 deep-tech companies at the Bharat Innovates platform in Nice, France, and participated in the Indian Prime Minister’s delegation to France. Combustor development with IISc Bangalore continues, along with next-generation higher-power-rated engines. Unimech’s exclusive manufacturing arrangement for Dheya’s engine platforms remains unchanged.

Semiconductor and energy traction. An RFQ was received from a large semiconductor equipment OEM. Management sees strong engagement in semiconductor and aerospace, and described the business as Tier-1 in semiconductor and nuclear (working with OEMs/government programs, sometimes Tier-2 where EPC is involved).

Capacity and capex. Facilities are operating at approximately 58% utilisation. Gross block by end-FY27 is expected to be roughly double the current value, primarily driven by the Saudi JV investment. Management indicated openness to further capacity investment to capture the demand cycle.

QIP enabling resolution. The Board approved raising up to INR750 crores via QIP, subject to shareholder approval. Management characterised this as an enabling resolution providing strategic flexibility, primarily toward achieving minimum public shareholding due in the next 18 months, and not an immediate fundraising. Shareholders approved the QIP resolution at the 10th AGM on 28 August 2026.

Dheya stake increase. Unimech acquired 850 equity shares of Dheya Engineering Technologies for Rs. 2,99,82,364.50 via a secondary purchase under a Share Purchase Agreement dated 7 August 2026, taking its total shareholding to 35.07% (a 5.08% incremental acquisition). The Board also approved further investment in Dheya of up to Rs. 5 crores.

3. Key Risks

  • Tariff and trade policy volatility, particularly potential US tariffs on India, which management said is difficult to quantify and evolving.
  • Working capital expansion to 160 days-plus, tied to long-cycle aerospace programs and nuclear tenders.
  • Qualification conversion uncertainty — management explicitly noted that not all qualifications will necessarily convert into production orders.
  • Dilution at Dheya depending on the final structure of its ~USD10 million raise.
  • Margin mix risk — precision manufacturing qualification programs carry higher initial costs before serial production.
  • General business risks cited in the company’s forward-looking statement: Indian and international economic performance, industry performance, competition, strategy implementation, future growth and expansion, technological changes, revenue or cash flow changes, market preferences, and market risk exposure.
  • Customer concentration and confidentiality constraints — management declined to discuss specific OEMs, citing confidentiality agreements.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Prior guidance met. On the previous earnings call, management indicated that improvement in Q4 FY26 should support a stronger Q1 FY27. Q1 FY27 revenue of INR1,076.2 mn (up 71% YoY, 32% QoQ) progressed in line with that direction.

Margin guidance. Management guided to 30%–32%-plus consolidated EBITDA margins historically, but stated that for FY27 it expects to deliver close to 34%–35%. Q1 FY27 actual EBITDA margin was approximately 36.5%, above that guided range. Gross margin guidance of 65% as a sustainable blended number for the year was met in Q1 (65%).

Other income guidance. Management guided that Q2 other income would be roughly half of Q1’s INR7 crores, and that FY27 other income would be materially lower than FY26’s ~INR46 crores.

Order book guidance. Management expects the order book to continue building as tooling and precision component opportunities convert into commercial orders, and expects Q2 to be stronger with higher revenue and robust EBITDA margins, supported by a full quarter of Hobel contribution.

Capacity utilisation guidance. Management expects utilisation to improve as qualification programs transition into serial production, and expects H2 FY27 to see heavier PCA-led revenue contribution.

Asset turn and ROCE guidance. Management indicated current investments can deliver asset turns of up to three times, with precision components expected at around two times, and a two-to-three-year blended expectation of roughly 2.5 times. ROCE, currently ~15%–16%, is expected to remain at that level for the calendar year and could rise to or beyond 20%–21% as utilisation improves.

Hobel growth guidance. Management indicated Hobel growth could be closer to 15%–20%, while noting this may be a premature indication given ongoing integration.

Nuclear execution guidance. Approximately 50% of the INR87 crores nuclear order book is expected to be executed in FY27, largely in H2, with the remainder into next year.

FY27 outlook. Management remains highly confident of delivering meaningful growth in FY27 on a consolidated basis, describing it as a year of strong growth and value creation, underpinned by healthy tooling demand visibility, a full quarter of Hobel contribution, and a growing precision component pipeline.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a detailed, optimistic outlook emphasizing a 36.5% revenue CAGR, order book expansion, and diversification across aerospace, nuclear, and semiconductor sectors, to a more cautious stance in August 2026 that highlighted slow ramp-up in new business and margin contraction as key risks. While order book strength and strategic positioning remained persistent themes, earlier detailed tailwinds like tariff mitigation via FTWZ and specific technological milestones faded from emphasis, replaced by concerns over execution and margin pressure.

Fears that came true

  • The feared margin contraction materialized, with gross margin down 493 bps y/y in Q2 and the last report still flagging margin contraction as a key risk.
  • The fear of muted near-term growth due to slow ordering and lower customer offtake materialized, as the last report cites slow ramp-up in new business.

Optimism that failed

  • The forecast of a 36.5% revenue CAGR over FY25–28 appears overly optimistic given the slow ramp-up in new business noted in the August 2026 report.
  • The expectation of EBITDA margin recovery to FY25 levels failed to materialize, as margins continued to contract and were cited as a key risk in the last report.
