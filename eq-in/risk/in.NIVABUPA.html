Headwinds and Challenges

  • Regulatory action on Expense of Management (EoM): IRDAI issued an order on August 19, 2026, warning the company for not complying with applicable EoM limits for FY 2024-25 and directing it not to open new places of business for six months from the date of the order. The company had made submissions to IRDAI after being asked for an explanation. The company states the order has nil quantifiable financial or operational impact.
  • Pending forbearance application: The company has filed an application with IRDAI for forbearance of expenses exceeding the allowable limit under the EoM Regulations, 2024 for FY 2024-25; approval is yet to be received. The auditors note that pending grant of forbearance, the consequential impact, if any, on the financial statements cannot presently be determined.
  • Challenging group/B2B pricing environment: Management stated the group business saw flat growth in Q1 FY27, with no growth on the group side. The pricing environment on the B2B side remained challenging, with the market operating at “claims minus” pricing, which management described as making no economic sense. The company lost some B2B renewals largely on account of pricing.
  • Expense ratio movement: The expense ratio increased by 90 basis points in Q1 FY27, primarily due to mix change toward retail health. The net reinsurance expense line saw a sharp pickup, though management attributed this to timing of profit commission bookings rather than structural change.
  • GST base effect: Management noted that the GST-related benefit will normalize from Q3, after which results will be comparable year-on-year.
  • Seasonal and weather-related risks: The infection season was expected to start, and management noted floods and heavy rains in some areas, though no adversities had been seen so far and July claims were in line with plan.
  • Solvency moderation: The solvency ratio stood at 2.25x as of June 30, 2026, against a regulatory minimum of 1.50x, down from 2.86x in Q1 FY26 and 2.49x in FY2026.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Industry growth and GST tailwinds: Retail health at the industry level grew 31.6% and overall health grew 22.4% on a reported basis. The company continues to experience GST tailwinds.
  • Outperformance versus market: Niva Bupa grew 31.7% year-on-year on a 1/n basis in Q1 FY27, with GWP of ₹2,150 crore. Retail health grew 47.1% on a reported basis and 35.5% on a like-to-like basis. Management aims to sustain 8–10 percentage points faster than market growth on retail health.
  • Retail market share gains: Retail health market share rose to 11.1% in Q1 FY27 from 10.0% in Q1 FY26.
  • Profitability improvement: PAT grew 93% year-on-year to ₹137.8 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹71.4 crore in Q1 FY26. CISR improved to 100.2% from 103.2% in Q1 FY26.
  • Loss ratio improvement: Retail loss ratio improved 90 basis points to 67.5%. The claims ratio improved to 63.0% from 66.8% in Q1 FY26. Improvement was driven by new retail business and a decision not to write large group accounts outside underwriting philosophy, with focus on affinity groups and SMEs.
  • Distribution expansion: The company continues to add advisors and expand into smaller markets (Tier 2, Tier 3 towns and beyond) through its “Bharat initiative.” Direct Business was the fastest-growing channel, followed by Agency. The company had 251,834 individual agents and 134 banca and other corporate agents as of Q1 FY27.
  • Product innovation: The Reassure 3.0 product launched in August 2025 has seen good market success.
  • Preferred Provider Network (PPN): PPN is live in 49 cities encompassing more than 1,000 hospitals, with 22% of claims flowing through these. Average claim size differential between tertiary/quaternary and secondary care setups ranges 15–30 percentage points, providing pricing flexibility.
  • Health and wellness ecosystem: The company has 5,70,000 monthly active users, with over 62,000 monthly health checkups/diagnostics and over 6,600 monthly doctor consults on its app.
  • Common empanelment initiative: MOUs with more than 3,000 hospitals.
  • Customer experience: Weighted Episodal NPS improved to 62 from 57 last year. Claims settlement ratio improved to 95.6%. The company was ranked 41st among India’s Top 100 Great Places to Work 2026, up from 67th.
  • Investment portfolio: AUM of ₹9,963 crore on book value basis and over ₹10,000 crore on mark-to-market basis. Annualized investment yield of 7.2% for Q1. The company is investing in AIFs yielding 12–15% (at ~4% of AUM against a 5% regulatory limit) and increased Nifty ETF allocation to ~3.5% of the investment book.
  • Debt raising capacity: The Board approved raising up to ₹500 crore via NCDs in one or more tranches, with a Debt Raising Committee constituted. The company’s issuer rating is AAA by ICRA. An existing ₹250 crore NCD (raised 5 years ago at 10.7% coupon) has a call option due this financial year.
  • Ind AS transition: The company transitioned to Ind AS from Q1 FY27, which management views as enhancing financial transparency.

Key Risks

  • Regulatory compliance risk: The IRDAI order warning on EoM non-compliance for FY 2024-25 and the six-month restriction on opening new places of business. The company is evaluating the order and will take appropriate steps to safeguard stakeholder interests.
  • Pending regulatory approval risk: The forbearance application outcome is uncertain, and the consequential impact on financial statements cannot presently be determined.
  • Pricing/underwriting risk in group business: Continued challenging B2B pricing environment with market pricing below claims, leading to lost renewals and flat group growth.
  • Catastrophe/seasonal risk: Infection season and weather events (floods, heavy rains) could affect claims experience.
  • Mix-related margin risk: Shift toward retail health increases expense ratio, though offset by loss ratio reduction.
  • Reinsurance accounting volatility: Profit commission timing on obligatory/voluntary quota share can cause quarter-to-quarter fluctuations in the net reinsurance expense line.
  • Solvency moderation: Solvency ratio declined to 2.25x from 2.86x year-on-year, though still above the 1.50x regulatory minimum.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • EoM compliance: Management states the company is in compliance with EoM Regulations, 2024 for the full financial year ended March 31, 2026, for Q1 ended June 30, 2026, and is on track to ensure compliance for the full financial year ending March 31, 2027. In Q1 FY27, the EoM ratio improved to 35.2% from 38.0% in Q1 FY26, with allowable EoM including additional allowances at 36.2%, leaving a headroom of 100 basis points. Management expects the EoM ratio to stabilize closer to 32–33%.
  • Growth guidance: Management aims to sustain 8–10 percentage points faster than market growth on retail health. In Q1 FY27, retail health grew 47.1% on a reported basis versus industry retail health growth of 31.6%, and 35.5% on a like-to-like basis versus industry overall health growth of 22.4%. July was described as looking very good.
  • Retail/group mix: Management indicated an internal mix of approximately 70% retail and 30% group. In Q1 FY27, retail health GWP was ₹1,607.1 crore (reported) out of total GWP of ₹2,150.0 crore.
  • Loss ratio trajectory: Management stated July trends were in line with expectations and claims were in line with plan. Q1 FY27 retail loss ratio improved 90 basis points to 67.5%. Renewal book loss ratio in retail is around 75%, which management considers acceptable given low renewal expenses (under 20%). The company takes high single-digit annual price increases on the renewal book.
  • CISR: Management stated no normalization is required on CISR as it is already amortized, and does not expect material change between H1 and H2. Q1 FY27 CISR was 100.2%.
  • ROE guidance: Management guided mid-to-high teens ROE by FY29, with a smooth trajectory from the current rolling four-quarter ROE of 11.8%. Q1 FY27 return on average net worth was 3.7% (quarterly).
  • Expense ratio: Management expects the expense ratio to stabilize closer to 33% (32–33% range). Q1 FY27 expense ratio was 37.2%.
  • Fresh vs. renewal mix: Management stated approximately 35% of retail business is fresh and 65% is renewal. In Q1 FY27, fresh grew 41% and both fresh and renewal grew.
  • Investment strategy: Management stated no major change in conservative investment strategy, with continued AIF investments (12–15% yield) and increased Nifty ETF allocation. No discussion on direct equity investment at this stage.
  • Debt raising: Management stated the company is evaluating options for the ₹500 crore NCD raise, with instrument rating to be obtained soon, and will decide on timing and amount based on growth plans.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from broad structural optimism in 2024 — emphasizing underpenetration, IFRS-driven RoE improvement, composite license potential, and SAHI advantages — to a more cautious, near-term operational focus by mid-2026. Persistent themes included retail health growth and SAHI market-share gains, but the early regulatory and structural catalysts (IFRS, composite license) dropped out of the narrative, replaced by concerns over rising operating expense ratios, competitive pricing pressure on group health, and seasonal infection claims watch.

Fears that came true

  • Increased competition from multi-line general insurers and potential life-insurer entry pressured SAHIs, as evidenced by group health business growth being subdued due to a highly competitive pricing environment.
  • Inaccurate estimation of medical expenses or claims frequency hurt Star Health, reflected in claims trends being placed under watch during the seasonal infection period with multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Catastrophic events or natural disasters raised claim liabilities, as seasonal infection-related claims concerns weighed on sentiment across several recent reports with negative actual returns.

Optimism that failed

  • IFRS implementation in FY26 was expected to improve RoEs by allowing amortization of acquisition costs, but this benefit was dropped from the narrative by the last report.
  • The composite license was expected to create value by allowing SAHIs to sell life insurance products, yet this catalyst disappeared from the latest narrative entirely.
  • EOM regulations were expected to improve efficiency, but EoM at 35.2% in 1QFY27 is near the 35% regulatory threshold, indicating efficiency gains have stalled or reversed.
