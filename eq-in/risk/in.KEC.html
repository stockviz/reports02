KEC International: Recent Developments, Headwinds, and Growth Prospects

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Q1 FY27 operating environment (quarter ended June 30, 2026). Management described the quarter as challenging. The company reported consolidated revenue of Rs. 5,024 crore, essentially flat against Rs. 5,023 crore in Q1 FY26, while consolidated EBITDA fell to Rs. 291 crore from Rs. 350 crore and EBITDA margin declined to 5.8% from 7.0%. Consolidated PBT dropped to Rs. 90 crore from Rs. 159 crore, and PAT fell to Rs. 73 crore from Rs. 125 crore. On a standalone basis, revenue declined to Rs. 3,898 crore from Rs. 4,030 crore, EBITDA to Rs. 156 crore from Rs. 197 crore, and PAT to Rs. 1 crore from Rs. 37 crore. Interest as a percentage of revenue rose to 3.3% (consolidated) from 3.0%, and to 3.5% (standalone) from 3.1%.

Specific pressures cited by management. The MD & CEO attributed the shortfall relative to potential to continued geopolitical disruptions in the Middle East, labour shortages, and calibrated execution of water projects due to delayed payments. Additional challenges identified include supply chain and logistics disruptions, elevated costs and longer lead times globally, revenue and margin timing impacts, delays in finalisation and award of projects in the Middle East, delays in legal closure of disputes and settlement of claims in Transportation and Metro projects, continued right-of-way (ROW) issues in India T&D, and slower progress in Water projects affecting working capital.

Regulatory/legal matter. On August 12, 2026, the company received an assessment order from the Assistant Commissioner (ST), Alagappa Circle, Salem, Tamil Nadu, under the Central Sales Tax Act, imposing a penalty of Rs. 17,12,024 for alleged mismatch of turnover for assessment year 2014-15. The company stated it is pursuing the order and will seek appropriate legal remedy, and does not foresee any material financial impact.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order book and pipeline. The current order book plus L1 position stands at over Rs. 40,000 crore, with more than 60% from T&D. The tender pipeline exceeds Rs. 2 lakh crore. YTD order intake for FY27 exceeded Rs. 6,300 crore across T&D, Civil, Renewables, Cables & Conductors, and Transportation. A subsequent order win of Rs. 1,303 crore across T&D and Cables & Conductors brought YTD order intake to over Rs. 7,600 crore.

T&D outlook. Management described the T&D outlook as robust, driven by new orders, a strong L1 position, a large pipeline of opportunities, and sustained demand across key markets. Drivers include rising power demand, grid expansion, renewable integration, grid modernisation, accelerating investments in HVDC in India, grid resilience and reconstruction internationally, regional interconnections, and recovery in Africa.

Adjacent growth areas. Rising investments in Renewables, Energy Storage, and Grid Flexibility; AI-led data centre expansion creating opportunities across T&D and Civil; gradual recovery in private capex, especially Metals & Mining; increasing opportunities in Commercial Real Estate; and large urban infrastructure opportunities such as Underground Metros, PSP, and Elevated bridges.

Balance sheet improvement. Net debt including acceptances reduced by more than Rs. 150 crore to Rs. 6,568 crore as of June 30, 2026, compared to March 31, 2026. Net Working Capital reduced to 134 days from 137 days over the same period.

ESG recognition. On August 7, 2026, CRISIL ESG Ratings & Analytics Limited assigned the company an ESG rating of ‘CRISIL ESG 62’, up from ‘CRISIL ESG 57’.

Governance. At the 21st AGM held on August 21, 2026, all resolutions were passed with the requisite majority, including adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY 2025-26, declaration of a dividend of Rs. 5.50 per equity share, re-appointment of Mr. Vimal Kejriwal as director liable to retire by rotation, ratification of cost auditor remuneration, and approval of commission to Mr. Harsh V. Goenka as Non-Executive Chairman. The commission resolution received the lowest approval, with 90.43% of votes polled in favour and 9.57% against.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical uncertainty in the Middle East affecting project awards and execution.
  • Supply chain and logistics disruptions with elevated costs and longer lead times globally.
  • Delays in finalisation and award of projects in the Middle East.
  • Delays in legal closure of disputes and settlement of claims in Transportation and Metro projects.
  • Delayed payments in Water projects leading to slower progress and working capital pressure.
  • Continued ROW issues in India T&D.
  • Labour shortages.
  • Interest cost pressure, with interest as a percentage of revenue rising year-on-year on both consolidated and standalone bases.
  • Margin compression, with consolidated EBITDA margin declining to 5.8% from 7.0% and PAT margin declining to 1.4% from 2.5%.
  • Tax/regulatory exposure illustrated by the Salem CST assessment order, though the company does not expect material financial impact.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance. Management stated it remains confident of delivering stronger execution and improved financial performance in the coming quarters, citing normalising supply chains, improving labour availability, an order book and L1 position exceeding Rs. 40,000 crore, and a tender pipeline exceeding Rs. 2 lakh crore. Management characterised near-term challenges as largely transitory. Regarding the Rs. 1,303 crore order win, management stated these orders will play a key role in driving targeted growth going forward.

Observed performance. In Q1 FY27, consolidated revenue was flat year-on-year at Rs. 5,024 crore, while profitability declined across EBITDA, PBT, and PAT on both consolidated and standalone bases. The company did reduce net debt by more than Rs. 150 crore and lower net working capital to 134 days, and it maintained a healthy order book plus L1 position of over Rs. 40,000 crore. The gap between the confident forward guidance and the Q1 FY27 margin and profit decline reflects the transitory challenges management identified; the extent to which execution and financial performance improve in subsequent quarters remains to be demonstrated in future disclosures.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative shifted from a civil-infrastructure growth thesis (FY21–FY22) centered on construction, railways, T&D, and margin recovery to a defense-oriented story (FY26) focused on import-dependence risks and indigenization tailwinds. Throughout, the underlying civil-construction and international-business growth themes gradually gave way as the stock suffered severe and prolonged underperformance, and the final call downgraded to Hold.

Fears that came true

  • Increase in raw material prices, particularly steel, impacted margin profile — the stock posted deeply negative actual returns (as low as -64.9%) across nearly every report, indicating persistent margin pressure.
  • Competitive intensity in civil construction increased — the sustained downgrades from Buy/Accumulate to Hold/Neutral over five years confirm eroding competitive positioning.
  • Working capital debt expected to remain around Rs 2,500 Cr — the prolonged financial underperformance and repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes indicate the balance-sheet strain persisted.

Optimism that failed

  • Civil Construction vertical expected to grow 100% in FY22 and 40-50% thereafter — the stock collapsed by over 50% in subsequent years, showing this growth did not translate into shareholder returns.
  • Softening commodity prices were expected to improve margins from Q4FY22 and normalize in FY23 — continued negative returns through 2024–2026 confirm margins never recovered.
  • Robust order book of Rs 29,000 Cr with strong bid pipeline of Rs 32,000 Cr — despite this cited strength, actual stock returns were deeply negative in nearly every report.
  • Foray into Oil & Gas vertical with expected Rs 400 Cr revenue in FY23 — no subsequent broker optimism referenced this vertical, and persistent underperformance suggests it failed to materialize.
