Amber Enterprises India — Q1FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Commodity and currency pressure. Management states that the business continues to face pressure from elevated commodity prices, currency depreciation and minimum wage revision, and expects this to persist through H1, describing it as temporary in nature and expected to normalize as the macro environment improves. Copper is specifically called out in the Railway Sub-systems & Defense division, alongside currency depreciation and minimum wage revisions in Haryana (where minimum wage rose 35% in one go).

Bare PCB margin compression. During the quarter, the bare printed circuit board business witnessed margin compression amid a steep rise in copper clad laminate (CCL) cost. As a second-tier supplier, the PCB business experiences a time lag of around two quarters for price pass-on. Management quantified the effect: standard PCB margins were earlier about 16%, currently hovering around 12%, with impact expected to continue for the next quarter and margins returning to normalized levels from quarter 3 onwards — subject to no further CCL price increases. CCL prices continue to increase, and management notes there is a shortage of CCL, with strategic inventory positioning occurring; getting price revisions from customers has become a regular event.

Consumer Durables cost headwinds. The division’s operating EBITDA grew 12% despite cost headwinds from commodity prices and minimum wage revision. Growth of 8% was delivered on a large prior-year base.

Railway Sub-systems & Defense margin decline. Operating EBITDA declined 26% (from INR22 crores to INR16 crores), impacted by product mix, continued commodity inflation (particularly copper), currency depreciation and minimum wage revisions in Haryana. Indian Railway segment contracts are fixed-price, with no commodity or currency escalation available. In addition, the first two to three trains of new products were supplied directly by the principal as a pass-through (trading), which lowered margins, while factory approvals are under process.

Electronics EMS growth moderation. Within the Electronics division, EMS segment growth was stunted because customers in the consumer durable segment were carrying considerable inventory, reducing the order book; some customers shifted from sales to job-work, impacting revenue. The energy (smart meter) segment degrew, and the smart watch segment also degrew.

ILJIN fire disruption. A fire incident occurred at ILJIN; permission to reconstruct the facility was received from departments the day before the call. The assessment of loss is ongoing, with an exact amount to be communicated within the coming week. Management states the company is adequately insured (building, inventory, machines) and that multi-geographic locations allowed immediate shifting of business to other locations.

Market inventory confusion in RAC. From 1 July, regulation changed so that tonnage must be mentioned on the BEE label; previously tonnage was not mandatory. Mixed inventory exists in the market (e.g., 1.5-tonne machines ranging from 4,400W to 5,200W). Management expects this inventory to be liquidated by Diwali, with a strong summer expected from November, and does not expect very high demand in the coming festive season.

Minority interest swing. Minority interest turned negative (approximately minus INR19 crores versus INR28 crores positive in the prior quarter), attributed to the exceptional loss of INR123 crores in the Electronics division being allocated to minority interest in entities where minority interests exist (ILJIN, Stelltek, Unitronics, ILJIN Technologies). The CFO stated the adjusted minority interest number would be calculated and updated.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Mobile manufacturing entry via Oppo collaboration. Amber Group signed a manufacturing collaboration agreement with Oppo Mobiles India, covering the Oppo, OnePlus and Realme brands — expanding into the largest segment of the electronics industry. Initial phase is progressing well through joint working sessions; a Chief Operating Officer for the mobile vertical has been onboarded. Trial production is targeted for Q4FY27, with commercial production from Q1FY28. Scale is expected to begin at around 8 million units in the first year, doubling to approximately 15–16 million in the second year. Management views this as the beginning of a longer relationship, with additional collaboration avenues to be explored aligned with Atmanirbhar Bharat and increasing local value addition. The business is described as de-seasonalised, asset-light and capital-efficient with healthy ROCE and minimum incremental capex.

PCB expansion and import substitution. The groundbreaking ceremony was conducted for the HDI PCB facility at Jewar (YEIDA, near Noida Airport, Uttar Pradesh), a JV between ILJIN and Korea Circuit bringing complementary strengths to localize HDI PCB production, which is currently heavily import dependent. Construction for the multilayer PCB facility at Hosur (Tamil Nadu) is progressing well. ECMS approvals have been received for three applications: INR3,200 crores at Jewar Airport, about INR1,000 crores at Hosur, and INR500 crores at Shogini, Pune. Hosur is expected to be up and running this financial year; Jewar trial production expected about 18 months out. Asset turns in this business are generally 0.8:1 depending on PCB complexity.

Electronics division momentum. Revenue grew 29% to INR985 crores, operating EBITDA more than doubled to INR107 crores, and margin expanded to 10.8% (which would have been 12% if PCB were at normalized levels). The journey from 2.8% margin in 2018 (when the company was acquired) to 10.8% now is cited as evidence of the value-oriented strategy. All three verticals — PCBA, PCB and industrials/automation — are growing. Power electronics (PLCs, HMIs, solar inverters, UPS, BESS) is seeing 35–40% growth. Management cites a total addressable market of about USD16–17 billion across the three verticals, potentially rising to USD35–40 billion by FY30 in a conservative USD300 billion electronics consumption scenario.

Railway and defense expansion. Sidwal’s greenfield facility for HVAC, pantry, doors and gangways in Faridabad is now operational, positioning the company for scalable growth. The Yujin JV facility is ready, with product development and design underway and commercial production anticipated in H2FY27 following requisite RDSO approval. Non-railway businesses (metro, defense, bus air conditioning, data center air conditioning) carry price variation clauses.

Consumer Durables premiumization. New customers were added during the quarter, strengthening the customer base, with reinforced presence in the Light Commercial AC segment. The strategy focuses on the high-value/premium side, and the growing light commercial air conditioning portfolio improves product realization. The prior-year base is described as normalized, with customers expected to remain for the longer term.

CCL localization. The first domestic CCL plant (Wipro’s) is almost operational and meeting customers; two more CCL plants are being planned. Amber is in discussion at group level with prospective JV partners for its own CCL plant, expected by FY29–30, which management views as important backward integration given PCB growth.

Fundraising capacity. The Board approved an enabling resolution at ILJIN level to raise up to INR5,000 crores; quantum and form to be communicated once finalized.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and currency volatility, particularly copper and CCL, with pass-through lags (one quarter for tier-1 consumer durable and PCBA; two quarters for tier-2 PCB). Management notes that if commodity prices fall, the benefit must also be passed back at a quarterly lag.
  • Fixed-price railway contracts where commodity, currency and wage changes cannot be passed on, requiring fulfilment of tender obligations as submitted.
  • Seasonality and product mix in consumer durables, which management cites as reasons guidance on margins is difficult; quarterly margins are not representative of the full year.
  • Regulatory transition risk in RAC from the BEE tonnage labelling change from 1 July, creating mixed market inventory and uncertain festive demand.
  • Customer inventory and business-model shifts in EMS (inventory build-up at consumer durable customers; shift from sales to job work).
  • Fire/operational disruption at ILJIN, with loss assessment pending; insurance is stated to cover refurbishment and extra capex.
  • Dependence on timely approvals — RDSO approval for the Yujin JV, factory approvals for railway products, and pending draft guidelines on PLI eligibility for mobile backward integration (management declined to comment until draft guidelines are issued).
  • Revenue recognition and entity placement for the Oppo business (Amber versus ILJIN) still being finalized, pending ERP integration, with formalization targeted before mid-September.
  • Minority interest allocation complexity, with the CFO stating a standard percentage cannot be used to calculate group-level minority interest allocation.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from a balanced view in 2023 — flagging real headwinds like margin misses, unseasonal rains, customer in-sourcing risk, and rising finance costs alongside strong tailwinds like revenue beats, market share gains, and peak capex completion — to an entirely bullish posture by 2026 with no headwinds acknowledged and only the MPMS government scheme cited as a tailwind. Over time, broker calls became overwhelmingly Buy-oriented despite the stock’s actual returns deteriorating from triple-digit gains in 2023 to deeply negative figures by 2025–2026. The earlier cautious themes around demand, margins, and structural risks were progressively dropped even as the stock underperformed.

Fears that came true

  • Customer In-sourcing Risk — multiple AC brands setting up in-house manufacturing likely eroded component demand and market share, contributing to the string of DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes from 2025 onward.
  • Rising Finance Costs — the 101.7% YoY jump in interest expenses persisted and squeezed profitability, correlating with the deep negative actual returns (-4.6% to -11.4%) seen in mid-2025 DISAPPOINTMENT reports.
  • Unseasonal Rains & Muted Q1 Demand — soft RAC demand and high channel inventory issues likely persisted into subsequent quarters, aligning with the cluster of DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in 2024–2025.
  • Slow Export Conversion — export expansion failing to accelerate left a critical growth pillar underdeveloped, as reflected in the sustained negative actual returns and DISAPPOINTMENT calls from 2025–2026.

Optimism that failed

  • Revenue Beats Expectations — the +55% YoY and +64.7% YoY revenue growth in FY23 did not sustain, with actual returns turning deeply negative from 2025 onward.
  • Market Share & Wallet Share Gains — the increase from 26% to 29% market share did not hold, as the stock suffered multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes through 2025–2026.
  • Peak Capex Completed / RoCE expansion — the anticipated RoCE expansion and strong FCF generation failed to translate into positive stock returns, with consecutive DISAPPOINTMENT and FLAT outcomes in 2025–2026.
  • Debt Reduction — the projected Rs 1.5–2.0bn net debt decline in FY24 appears to have faltered given the persistent financial pressure evident in negative returns through 2026.
