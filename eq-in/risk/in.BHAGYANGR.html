Bhagyanagar India Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Volume loss from trade-route disruption. Management stated that in the first two months of Q1 FY27 (April and May 2026), disruption of trade routes and a nationwide material shortage pulled turnover down. Q1 FY27 sales volume came in at 5,278 MT, down 9.45% quarter-on-quarter and 6.52% year-on-year. Management attributed the lower full-year volume guidance (revised to 12–15% from an earlier 15–20%) directly to volume lost in those two months.

Sourcing concentration in imported scrap. Roughly 75% of scrap is imported, which exposed the business to the April–May 2026 route disruptions. Management said sourcing normalized from June 2026.

GST input-credit dispute. A GST impact of ₹17–18 crore relating to FY2022-23 was recognized, arising from retrospective cancellation of a local scrap supplier’s registration. The company deposited ₹17 crore. The CFO stated the company holds lorry tracking, video, photographic and payment proof and is confident of winning at the tribunal, but the matter remains unresolved.

Primary copper market distortion. Management noted refining charges in primary copper have turned negative because global ore and concentrate is flowing to China, where sulphuric acid by-product prices are very high, making ore-to-copper conversion elsewhere uncompetitive. Indian primary players including Vedanta, Hindalco and Adani are buying recycled copper for their furnaces.

Data centre architecture risk. A participant raised a shift from 48V DC to 800V DC architecture in data centres, which could reduce copper bus bar demand by around 50%. Management said data centre demand has only just started and that any such reduction would have no material impact on the company.

Margin normalization. The elevated Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 5.43% was attributed to shortage-driven realizations in April–May 2026, not to structural pricing. Management guided to 5–5.5% for the rest of the year and said sustaining 5.5% for the full year may be difficult.

Competitive pressure. Management said competitive pressures will persist and that blended margins are projected at only 5–5.5% through 2030.

Foreign exchange volatility. The company’s own disclosures state that prevalent volatile conditions in foreign exchange make turnover and profitability not precisely predictable.

Real estate inquiry. An inquiry relating to a real estate piece was raised by an investor. Management clarified it concerns Bhagyanagar Properties Limited, a third company with no shareholding relationship to Bhagyanagar India, that the inquiries were closed within 3–6 months of arising in 2024-25 without repercussions, and that the three land parcels held within the company are industrial lands taken mostly from the government with no issue.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record quarterly profitability metrics. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹70,507.54 lakh (over ₹700 crore), with EBITDA (excluding other income) of ₹3,829.20 lakh, PAT of ₹2,024.52 lakh, EBITDA margin of 5.43%, PAT margin of 2.87%, ROE of 29% and ROCE of 18.48%. EBITDA per kg was ₹72.55, described as the highest in recent times. PAT grew 167.38% year-on-year and 9.52% quarter-on-quarter.

Value-added mix at a record. Value-added products were 63% of sales in Q1 FY27, the highest share yet. Management targets 63–64% for FY27 and a gradual rise to 68–69% over three to four years, without exiting commodity sales.

New product traction. Transformer products, tin-coated bus bars and data centre products have seen large dispatches. About 250 tons of tin-coated products were sold in the quarter, and management estimated roughly 600 tons went to data centres overall. More than 100 tons of bus bars were exported to North America in the quarter. CTC wire capacity is around 80 MT per month, planned to rise to 150 MT per month, and a 48-conductor CTC machine has been added, extending coverage to the full copper range of transformers.

Capacity expansion. The 35,000 MT capacity addition is online. Expansion from 35,000 to 45,000 MT is projected for the next financial year, expected between April and June 2027, more likely June 2027. A ₹40 crore capex is proposed across FY27 and FY28. Current capacity utilization is 70%.

Demerger and value unlocking. A composite scheme involves three linked steps: amalgamation of Bhagyanagar Copper Private Limited into Bhagyanagar India, demerger of the copper business into Tieramet Limited, and independent listing of Tieramet on NSE and BSE. Entitlement is 1:1 — one Tieramet share (₹2 FV) for every BIL share (₹2 FV). The appointed date is 01 April 2025; board approval was 20.09.2025; the NCLT order convening the meeting was 29.01.2026; shareholders, banks, creditors and SEBI have approved; ROC, RD and OL reports have been submitted to NCLT; the NCLT hearing is scheduled for 7 August 2026. Post-scheme, Bhagyanagar India retains three land parcels in the heart of the city and the windmill project, while Tieramet holds the copper infrastructure and businesses. Management said the current valuation appears to reflect only the copper business, so any future value assigned to real estate would be total value unlocking.

Fundraise. A preferential issue of ₹52.25 crore to non-promoters was approved by the board on 30 June 2026 and by shareholders via EGM on 23 July 2026, comprising 15,01,434 equity shares at ₹348 per share (₹2 face value plus ₹346 premium) to 7 investors (3 QIB, 4 non-QIB). Management said the first tranche of ₹52 crore is finalized and funds should be received in August 2026, with a second tranche likely around March 2027 after the demerger, in the standalone copper unit. Trading approval for these shares was received, with listing and admission to dealings on NSE and BSE effective 15 September 2026, distinctive numbers 31995001 to 33496434, and a lock-in up to 15 March 2027.

Industry demand. Third-party research cited in the presentation projects India consuming around 2.4 million metric tons of copper by 2030, with secondary copper demand doubling from 0.7 million to 1.4 million metric tons over five years. Management cited recycled copper market growth in India at a 12–13% CAGR, against overall copper product growth of 6–6.5%, implying market share gains at roughly two to two and a half times the market rate. Government mandates cited include a minimum 5% recycled content for new non-ferrous products from FY28, EPR for non-ferrous metals effective April 2026, the National Critical Mineral Mission (2025), the Vehicle Scrappage Policy (2021), the National Non-ferrous Metal Scrap Recycling Framework (2020) and the National Resource Efficiency Policy (2019).

Export expansion. Exports were 18% of Q1 FY27 sales. Management guided to 12–15% export contribution for the year, with a minimum of 10%. The major export component is commodities; the value-added export share was described as relatively small.

Customer additions. Crompton Greaves, TMC and Atlanta were added in the transformer division; Blue Star, Micron, OBO and L&T in the Al data centre bus bar division. The company serves 500+ customers, with over 50 customers in transformer and motor products, mainly based in Chennai.

Plastic recycling. Plastic recycling is expected to contribute around ₹50 crore to turnover (less than 1%), with very high margins of 30–50% since the plastic is obtained free of cost along with cable, adding to the bottom line rather than the top line.

Green copper and EPR. Management acknowledged the company qualifies as a green copper supplier, uses solar power in its factory, and that EPR will arrive in one to two years, but stated no market study has been done and the green copper tag does not currently add material value.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity price and margin translation risk. Margins are managed in percentage terms rather than per-kg terms because raw material purchases range from 86% to 95% of LME and sales are linked to LME. A 20% copper price rise would leave the percentage margin at 5% while raising EBITDA per kg by 20%.
  • Hedging and margin funding. Approximately 85% of inventory is hedged on the exchange, with purchases and sales hedged on a daily basis. MCX margin requirements depend on copper prices; management said if copper prices stay where they are, no funds are required, but if copper prices double, substantial funds would be needed for MCX margin.
  • GST credit recovery risk. The ₹17 crore deposited may not be recovered if the tribunal rules against the company, despite management’s stated confidence.
  • Scheme completion risk. The restructuring remains subject to the NCLT hearing scheduled for 7 August 2026.
  • Volume guidance risk. Guidance was already reduced once, from 15–20% to 12–15%, due to two months of lost volume.
  • Value-added scaling difficulty. Management stated scaling value-added products is materially harder than scaling commodities, and that the company has been building this over roughly 35 years.
  • Debt trajectory. Projections indicate debt of approximately ₹300–350 crore by 2030, with a specific figure of around ₹325 crore cited, alongside working capital requirements. Management characterized these as long-term projections that cannot be stated with certainty.
  • Foreign exchange volatility, as disclosed by the company, making turnover and profitability not precisely predictable.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Volume guidance revised down. Management had earlier guided 15–20% volume growth; this was revised to 12–15% for FY27, attributed to volume lost in April–May 2026. Observed Q1 FY27 volume of 5,278 MT was down 9.45% QoQ and 6.52% YoY. Management noted 2,200 of the 5,200 tons came in June alone, which it cited as supporting the revised run rate.

Margin guidance versus delivered margin. Management had earlier indicated 5–5.5% EBITDA margin. Q1 FY27 delivered 5.43%, which management described as an overachievement driven by shortage conditions in April–May 2026, and reiterated 5–5.5% for the rest of the year, stating 5.5% for the full year may be difficult.

Realization guidance. The average price projected at the start of the year was ₹1,300 per kg. Observed Q1 FY27 sales realization was ₹1,279 per kg. Management said it expects the full-year average to exceed ₹1,300 and that value would be about 30% higher than last year.

Value-added mix guidance. Management had projected an increase from 62% toward 68–69% over three to four years. Observed Q1 FY27 was 63%, and management guided to 63–64% for FY27 with 1–2% annual increments thereafter.

Export guidance. Management guided to 12–15% for the year against observed Q1 FY27 exports of 18%.

Long-term revenue target. Management reiterated confidence in reaching ₹5,000 crore by FY30, describing FY30 as a conservative estimate, and stated it is confident of this “whatever the copper price.” Management said it is predicting 12–15% volume growth this year and 15% from next year onwards, with value growth over 30% this year.

Capex guidance. The ₹40 crore capex across FY27 and FY28 was described as well on track.

Capacity timeline. The 10,000-ton expansion (35,000 to 45,000 MT) was guided to the next financial year, expected April–June 2027, more likely June 2027, and explicitly not this year.

Fundraise timeline. The first tranche of ₹52 crore was guided to reach the bank in August 2026; the second tranche was guided to around March 2027 after the demerger.

Demerger timeline. The NCLT hearing was guided for 7 August 2026, after which management expects to complete the restructuring soon.

Debt guidance. Projections indicate approximately ₹325 crore of debt in 2030, with management stating cash flow from the 5% EBITDA margin will be more than enough to fund the company through 2030, while noting these are long-term projections.

AGM and governance items. The 41st Annual General Meeting is scheduled for Wednesday, 30 September 2026 at 11:00 A.M. IST via video conferencing or other audio-visual means. Shri Devendra Surana is re-appointed as Managing Director for three years effective 17.01.2027 to 16.01.2030, and Shri Venkateswara Rao Nukala is re-appointed as Director. The company proposes material related party transactions with Tieramet Limited and seeks approval for loans, investments and guarantees up to ₹750 crore under Sections 185 and 186 of the Companies Act, 2013. Management stated it believes the company will sustain volatile market conditions and emerge successfully in terms of good turnover and profits, based on current business plans and global copper demand in electric vehicles, solar and wind power sectors.

Standalone segment change. Bhagyanagar India ceased operations in the Copper Segment on a standalone basis with effect from 01.01.2024 pursuant to a slump sale to its wholly owned subsidiary, and now operates only in the Wind Power Segment on a standalone basis.
