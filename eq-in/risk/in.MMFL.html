M M Forgings Limited — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical uncertainties and trade-related challenges are cited as pressures on the business environment.
  • A volatile global environment and inflationary pressures are identified as ongoing challenges.
  • Company Secretary turnover: Shri. Chandrasekar S resigned as Company Secretary and Compliance Officer (CS&CO) and Key Managerial Personnel, citing personal and professional commitments, effective 14 August 2026. The Board took note of the resignation at its meeting on 14 August 2026. Shri. S. Muthukrishnan was appointed as CS&CO and KMP with effect from the same date (14 August 2026), a transition the outgoing secretary described as being managed with full cooperation.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Resilient export market and improving domestic opportunities.
  • Large machined-product programmes are ramping up towards full potential.
  • Increasing focus on Passenger Vehicles and Off-Highway segments.
  • Commissioning of the 16,500-tonne press, expected to strengthen capabilities and create additional export opportunities.
  • Emphasis on operational excellence, productivity, technology upgrades, and automation.
  • A healthy order pipeline and a diversified customer base.
  • FY27 is described as an important phase in MMF’s next stage of transformation, with continued focus on sustainable growth, profitability, and long-term value creation.

3. Key Risks

  • The external risk set — geopolitical uncertainty, trade-related challenges, global volatility, and inflation — as noted above.
  • Governance/leadership continuity risk around the CS&CO role: the resignation and same-day replacement were disclosed to the exchanges on 14 August 2026, with the outgoing officer noting a smooth handover to his successor.
  • Shareholder voting dissent: At the 80th AGM (8 August 2026), resolutions were passed with requisite majority, but notable opposition appeared on some items:
    • Adoption of audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY2025-26: 70.17% of outstanding shares polled; 99.94% of promoter votes in favour; public-institution votes 90.64% in favour; public-non-institution votes 14.87% polled with 100% in favour.
    • Re-appointment of Smt. Sumita Vidyashankar as director retiring by rotation: 99.95% in favour overall, with 0.05% against.
    • Re-appointment of Shri. Ramnath Nagarajan as director retiring by rotation: 98.81% in favour, 1.19% against (402,466 votes against).
    • Ratification of Cost Auditor remuneration for FY2026-27: 100% in favour.
  • Related-party/merger accounting: The wholly-owned subsidiary S V D Industries Private Limited was merged with the Company pursuant to a National Company Law Tribunal (Chennai Bench) order dated 29 June 2026, with an appointed date of 1 April 2024. The merger is a common-control transaction, and prior-period figures have been restated accordingly; the standalone impact is described as immaterial.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Guidance: FY27 is framed as an important transformation phase, with large machined-product programmes ramping to full potential; the 16,500-tonne press is expected to create additional export opportunities; management remains focused on sustainable growth, profitability, and long-term value creation.
  • Observed Q1FY27 performance (quarter ended 30 June 2026):
    • Standalone total revenue: ₹427.01 Cr, up 16% YoY.
    • Standalone EBITDA: ₹82.33 Cr, up 16% YoY.
    • Standalone PBT: ₹36.30 Cr, up 30% YoY.
    • Standalone PAT: ₹91.66 Cr, up 373% YoY, including ₹56.25 Cr net of tax profit on sale of land near Oragadam.
    • Consolidated total revenue: ₹426.76 Cr; EBITDA: ₹81.22 Cr; PBT: ₹35.07 Cr; PAT: ₹90.42 Cr.
    • Geographical revenue split: 63% domestic, 37% export (India 63%, US 18%, South America 4%, Europe 14%, others 1%).
  • Investor engagement: A virtual investor and analyst meeting with Nuvama Professional Clients Group is scheduled for 15 September 2026, with discussions to be based strictly on publicly available information and no UPSI disclosure.
  • Audit status: The statutory auditors (G. Ramesh Kumar & Co.) issued limited review reports on the standalone and consolidated results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, with nothing coming to their attention indicating material misstatement.

Broker Narrative

The initial narrative was bullish, driven by a domestic CV upswing, higher machining mix, and aggressive volume targets, with only operational risks noted. By the final report, tone was more cautious, emphasizing muted segment growth, execution delays, and persistent electricity and raw-material costs, while retaining faith in machining mix, US Class-8 demand, and productivity gains as future levers. The optimism shifted from broad demand recovery to company-specific execution and overseas cycle rebound.

Fears that came true

  • The risk of execution delays materialized, as the last report explicitly flagged ‘Delay in order execution,’ correlating with the stock’s DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes (-28.5% and -42.6%) in June and July 2024.
  • The flagged cost pressures from ‘elevated power & fuel costs’ and high raw material costs persisted, with the last report still citing ‘Rising electricity rates in Tamil Nadu’ and ‘Higher RM prices,’ contributing to negative actual returns in late 2023 (e.g., -20.9% in October 2023).

Optimism that failed

  • The expected ‘domestic CV demand recovery’ with a ‘cyclical upswing’ failed to materialize, as the last report instead noted ‘Muted growth in underlying segments,’ and the stock suffered multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes during 2024.
