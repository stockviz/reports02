MSTC Limited: Recent Developments and Outlook

Headwinds and Challenges

Cyclicality of the scrap business. Management notes that scrap sales, which contribute roughly 50–55% of e-commerce revenue, are inherently cyclical. The steel sector moves up and down, and the volume of scrap available changes from period to period. Large-ticket plant sales do not arrive every quarter, so the business cannot be assumed to grow at a steady 20–25% pace.

Dependence on external approvals. Several new verticals are ready but stalled pending clearances that MSTC does not control: - The EPR certificate exchange platform is fully developed and integrated, but operations await a government notification. Management describes this as a policy decision whose timing cannot be predicted. - The TReDS platform is at an advanced stage, but requires Reserve Bank of India approval. Management states it cannot project revenue until RBI clearance is received. - The travel portal’s B2C rollout awaits IATA empanelment.

Competition in coal exchanges. Multiple entities, including IEX, are planning coal exchanges. The Gazette notification contemplates multiple exchanges, so MSTC will have to compete for volumes. Management acknowledges it cannot yet state what market share it might achieve.

Legacy legal matters. Current borrowings include Rs. 14,361.97 lakhs relating to payments made by Standard Chartered Bank after purchase of export bills raised on foreign buyers against export of gold jewellery in 2008-09. This has led to ongoing legal cases, sub-judice matters, and contingent liabilities.

Policy-driven and lumpy revenue. New ventures such as liquor licence auctions depend on licence validity periods and state government decisions. Management notes that if a licence is valid for three years, no further allocation event occurs in that period. Revenue models across the business vary—some are a percentage of transaction value, others are event-based, and some are software development charges with AMCs—which contributes to lumpiness.

Margin pressure at higher volumes. Management indicates that while it aims to keep overheads in control, scaling beyond certain levels may put some pressure on margins.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue from operations was Rs. 94.25 crores, up nearly 22% year-on-year. E-commerce revenue reached its highest ever Q1 level at Rs. 89.49 crores. EBITDA margin on total income was 69.05%. The company reported its highest ever Q1 PBT and PAT since listing in March 2019. Standalone EBITDA was Rs. 81.49 crores, PBT Rs. 78.53 crores, and PAT Rs. 58.12 crores. Consolidated PAT was Rs. 58.22 crores and consolidated PBT Rs. 78.63 crores. Total revenue was Rs. 118.00 crores.

Completion of the legacy exit. MSTC has fully exited the trading and marketing segment, including discontinuation of the 110% BG backed model, and recovered its dues. Segmental reporting for the marketing division was discontinued from FY 2026-27. The company describes itself as having moved from a canalizing agency trading house to a digital solutions provider.

New verticals in advanced stages. - EPR certificate exchange: Developed for CPCB, with integrations and security testing complete. Initially to cover 5 sectors, expanding later to about 15. MSTC is described as the only exchange for this at present. - TReDS platform: Transaction fee expected to be the primary revenue source. Management views the MSME financing push as a positive ecosystem, with the first couple of years targeted for stabilisation. - Travel portal (mstcsmarttravel.in): B2B segment operational for internal corporate travel, with other PSUs approached. Partnership with EaseMyTrip for aggregation; future operations planned in-house. B2C rollout planned after IATA empanelment. - Coal and mineral exchanges: MSTC is examining feasibility and has had initial discussions with stakeholders.

Core auction business momentum. MSTC continues services for sale of major and minor mineral blocks, including critical minerals. In the quarter: 4 exploration licence blocks and 10 critical mineral blocks allocated; an agreement with the Tamil Nadu Forest Department for sale of about 100 tons of Red Sandalwood through international competitive bidding; first batch of Coal India NRS coal linkage auctions underway; a niche auction for RINL process scrap; liquor licence auctions for the Government of Karnataka (following a prior Rajasthan exercise); land parcel auctions for Telangana (HMDA and TSIIC), including land parcel sales worth Rs. 2259.52 crores for TSIIC; the 15th round of commercial coal block auctions; and a first-time auction for selection of charge point operators for Kerala State Electricity Board under the PM E-DRIVE Scheme. E-commerce goods transacted crossed INR 238.14 bn in Q1 FY27.

Joint venture turnaround. The MMRPL joint venture with Mahindra showed a positive PAT for the first time in several sequential reporting periods, with a profit of Rs. 0.20 crores and MSTC’s share of around Rs. 10 lakhs. This is attributed to higher feedstock inflows driven by extended producer responsibility norms for automobile manufacturers, diversion of end-of-life vehicles from the grey market to RVSFs, and OEM and government incentives for new car buyers trading in old vehicles. Management expects the trend to strengthen.

Private sector expansion. MSTC is expanding private sector clientele for scrap and other commodities, with entities such as Tata, Reliance, Vedanta, and ArcelorMittal transacting scrap, iron ore, and some coal auctions on its platform. The scrap business is spread across roughly 3,000 sellers, reducing concentration risk.

Key Risks

  • Regulatory and approval risk: Operations of the EPR exchange, TReDS platform, and travel portal B2C segment all depend on external approvals (government notification, RBI, IATA) with uncertain timelines.
  • Competitive risk: Multiple coal exchanges are expected, and MSTC’s market share is undetermined.
  • Cyclicality risk: Scrap and steel sector cycles affect volumes and realisations.
  • Policy risk: New business lines such as liquor licence auctions and coal exchanges are policy-driven, with states adopting models at differing paces.
  • Legal and contingent liability risk: The Standard Chartered Bank matter relating to 2008-09 export bills remains sub-judice.
  • Revenue concentration and lumpiness: While scrap is spread across many sellers, large-ticket plant sales and mineral block events cause period-to-period variability.
  • Margin risk: Higher volumes may pressure margins.
  • General economic and market risk: Performance depends on the Indian economy, international markets, industry performance, competition, technological changes, market preferences, and exposure to market risks.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Growth guidance. Management has consistently aimed to sustain double-digit growth, acknowledging that growth will vary between periods—some higher, some lower—and average out over the long run. Observed performance in Q1 FY27 showed nearly 22% year-on-year revenue growth, following 20% year-on-year growth in the e-commerce vertical over the preceding two quarters. Management cautioned that 20–25% growth should not be assumed to continue indefinitely.

Margin guidance. Management indicated an endeavour to maintain average margins in the recent range, while noting that higher volumes could pressure margins. Observed Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 69.05%, nearly 3% higher than the corresponding period of FY26, with total expenses rising 16% against a 22% revenue increase.

New vertical timelines. Management’s internal target is to obtain RBI approval and operationalise TReDS within FY27. For the EPR exchange, management stated the platform is ready and awaiting a government notification, with timing described as unpredictable. For the travel portal, B2B is operational and B2C awaits IATA empanelment. For coal and mineral exchanges, management said it is evaluating possibilities and may provide input after a couple of quarters.

JV outlook. Management described the MMRPL outlook as positive and hoped the positive trend would continue, while declining to speculate. Observed Q1 FY27 showed the JV’s first positive PAT in several periods.

Dividend guidance. Dividend for the year will be guided by DIPAM policy—a minimum of 4% of net worth or 30% of PAT, whichever is higher. A dividend was already declared in the Q4 results, subject to shareholder approval at the AGM.

AGM and record date. The 61st AGM is scheduled for Thursday, September 24, 2026, at 11:00 A.M. via video conferencing. The Register of Members and Share Transfer Books will remain closed from Friday, September 18, 2026, to Thursday, September 24, 2026. Thursday, September 17, 2026, is the Record Date for determining entitlement to the final dividend for FY 2025-26. The agenda includes adopting financial statements, confirming an interim dividend, appointing and re-appointing directors (including Shri Manobendra Ghoshal, Shri Sirajong Singpho, Shri Niwas Mandal, and Smt. Santosh), and fixing auditor remuneration.
