Steel Exchange India Limited — Recent Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Weak sequential demand and pricing pressure in steel. Revenue from operations declined from ₹28,732.75 lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 to ₹26,971.39 lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2026, and was also below the ₹29,999.89 lakhs recorded in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. The steel segment’s revenue fell from ₹28,265.76 lakhs to ₹26,582.16 lakhs sequentially.
  • Profitability compression. Profit before exceptional items and tax fell to ₹1,502.56 lakhs in Q1 FY2027 from ₹2,562.44 lakhs in Q4 FY2026, though it was above the ₹1,022.88 lakhs of Q1 FY2026. Segment results before finance cost, exceptional items and tax declined to ₹2,900.72 lakhs from ₹4,320.98 lakhs sequentially.
  • Power segment losses. The power segment reported a loss of ₹67.44 lakhs in Q1 FY2027, compared with a loss of ₹92.38 lakhs in Q4 FY2026 and a profit of ₹11.22 lakhs in Q1 FY2026.
  • Rising working capital intensity. Debtors turnover lengthened to 91 days in Q1 FY2027 from 72 days in Q4 FY2026 and 71 days in Q1 FY2026. Inventory turnover rose to 173 days from 157 days sequentially and 106 days a year earlier.
  • Higher finance costs year-on-year. Finance costs were ₹1,398.16 lakhs in Q1 FY2027 against ₹1,889.06 lakhs in Q1 FY2026, but above the ₹1,758.55 lakhs of Q4 FY2026.
  • Operating margin moderation. Operating EBITDA margin declined to 13% in Q1 FY2027 from 17% in Q4 FY2026, though it was above the 12% of Q1 FY2026.
  • Raw material and input cost pressure. Raw materials consumed rose to ₹20,251.30 lakhs in Q1 FY2027 from ₹18,619.75 lakhs in Q4 FY2026, even as revenue fell.
  • External risk factors. The company’s forward-looking disclosures cite risks and uncertainties including government actions, local, political or economic developments, and technological risks.
  • Subsidiary not yet operational. SEIL Infra Logistics Limited, incorporated on June 29, 2025 and acquired as a wholly owned subsidiary during the quarter, has not commenced commercial operations and has not undertaken any business or operating transactions, so it contributed no revenue, expenses, assets (other than initial capital), liabilities, or profit/loss.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strategic MoU with NMDC Limited. The company entered into a strategic 4-year MoU with NMDC Limited for the supply and procurement of consistent grade Iron Ore Fines (Fe 61–63% and above) from NMDC’s upcoming Visakhapatnam Buffer Stockpile & Blending Yard. This is expected to secure the raw material pipeline, lower freight costs, and provide logistical advantages.
  • Support for expansion. The MoU is expected to support SEIL’s expansion plans in the near future and establish the operational blueprint for upcoming Long-Term Agreements (LTAs).
  • Reheating Furnace ramp-up. The recently operationalized Reheating Furnace achieved a highest-ever monthly Re-Bar output of 24,823.509 MT.
  • Diversification into specialty steels. The company is diversifying into specialty steels under the PLI scheme to support import substitution and expand value-added offerings.
  • Fundraising completed. In April 2026, the company allotted 2,82,97,870 equity shares on conversion of share warrants at a premium of ₹13.10 per share, raising ₹3,989.99 lakhs through preferential allotment to Gunakala Vijayalakshmi. During the quarter it also issued 36,14,60,300 convertible equity share warrants at a premium of ₹8.45 per warrant for ₹34,157.99 lakhs, receiving ₹14,089.50 lakhs on allotment.
  • Deleveraging. The debt-equity ratio improved to 0.27 times as at June 30, 2026 from 0.42 times as at March 31, 2026 and 0.48 times as at June 30, 2025. Total debts to total assets fell to 0.17 from 0.24 sequentially.
  • Improved coverage and liquidity. Interest service coverage ratio rose to 2.52 times in Q1 FY2027 from 2.27 times in Q4 FY2026 and 2.13 times in Q1 FY2026. Current ratio improved to 2.57 times from 2.26 times sequentially and 1.74 times a year earlier. Asset coverage ratio improved to 3.77 times from 2.91 times.
  • Logistics segment growth. Logistics revenue rose to ₹162.83 lakhs in Q1 FY2027 from ₹64.38 lakhs in Q1 FY2026, with segment results of ₹157.77 lakhs versus ₹56.59 lakhs.
  • NCD part repayments. The 14.06% listed NCDs held by Kotak Credit Opportunities Fund were stated at a reduced face value of ₹3,87,345 each as at June 30, 2026 after mandatory part payments during the quarter; outstanding NCDs declined to ₹14,827.57 lakhs from ₹19,501.27 lakhs.
  • No deviation in use of funds. The company reported no deviation or variation in utilization of funds raised through the preferential issue of equity shares (₹3,990.00 lakhs) and convertible warrants (₹34,158.00 lakhs assumed on full conversion).
  • Governance continuity. The Board approved continuation of Ms. Bhagyam Ramani as Non-Executive Independent Director beyond age 75 until completion of her current first term, and approved remuneration for Whole-time Director Mr. Mohit Sai Kumar Bandi for the remaining two years of his tenure, both subject to member approval. All six ordinary and special resolutions at the 27th AGM held on August 22, 2026 were passed with overwhelming majority.
  • Investor engagement. The company held an investor meeting on June 19, 2026 with several investors and brokerages via Zoom, based on generally available information.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material concentration risk. The NMDC MoU is for a primary 4-year term and is expected to form the blueprint for upcoming LTAs; the benefits depend on execution of those agreements and on the Visakhapatnam Buffer Stockpile & Blending Yard becoming operational.
  • Commodity price and demand cyclicality. Steel segment revenue and profitability declined sequentially, indicating sensitivity to steel market conditions.
  • Working capital risk. Lengthening debtor and inventory turnover days increase the cash conversion cycle and funding requirements.
  • Power segment drag. Continued losses in the power segment weigh on overall results.
  • Subsidiary execution risk. SEIL Infra Logistics Limited has not commenced commercial operations; its future contribution is unproven.
  • External and regulatory risks. Government actions, local, political or economic developments, and technological risks are cited as factors that could affect forward-looking statements.
  • Dilution risk. The large issuance of convertible equity share warrants (36,14,60,300 warrants) could result in significant equity dilution on conversion.
  • Debt servicing obligations. Scheduled principal repayments and finance charges remain material, with debt service coverage ratio at 1.23 times in Q1 FY2027.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Guidance on the NMDC MoU. Management states the MoU is expected to support SEIL’s expansion plans in the near future, optimize input logistics, drive cost efficiencies, and support its commitment to expanding market leadership in quality long steel products. Observed performance in Q1 FY2027 shows raw materials consumed rising sequentially to ₹20,251.30 lakhs while revenue declined, and operating EBITDA margin falling to 13% from 17%; the anticipated cost and logistics benefits are not yet reflected in the reported quarterly numbers.
  • Guidance on the Reheating Furnace. The company highlights the Reheating Furnace achieving a highest-ever monthly Re-Bar output of 24,823.509 MT. Observed steel segment revenue, however, declined to ₹26,582.16 lakhs in Q1 FY2027 from ₹28,265.76 lakhs in Q4 FY2026 and ₹29,486.49 lakhs in Q1 FY2026.
  • Guidance on consolidation. Management stated that the consolidation of SEIL Infra Logistics Limited has no material impact on the Group’s financial performance for the quarter, and the standalone and consolidated figures are substantially the same. This is consistent with the reported consolidated results, which mirror the standalone numbers.
  • Guidance on fund utilization. The company reported no deviation or variation in the use of funds raised. Against the warrant issue, funds utilized during the quarter were ₹5,406.42 lakhs for working capital, ₹7,492.40 lakhs for repayment/servicing of financial facilities, and ₹1,182.58 lakhs for general corporate purposes, against original allocations of ₹14,639.14 lakhs, ₹11,223.34 lakhs, and ₹8,295.51 lakhs respectively.
  • Guidance on expansion and diversification. Management points to expansion plans and diversification into specialty steels under the PLI scheme. The reported quarterly results do not yet show a contribution from specialty steel offerings, and the subsidiary intended to support infrastructure and logistics has not commenced operations.
