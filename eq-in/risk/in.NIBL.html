NRB Industrial Bearings Limited — 15th Annual General Meeting, 14 August 2026

Headwinds and Challenges

  • The 15th AGM was conducted through video conferencing and other audio-visual means, with the proceedings deemed to be conducted at the registered office as the deemed venue.
  • Shareholder participation in the meeting itself was limited: no shareholders were present in person or through proxy, while 3 promoter/promoter group shareholders and 72 public shareholders attended through video conferencing.
  • Voting was concentrated in remote e-voting. For the ordinary resolutions, promoter and promoter group votes polled through remote e-voting were 16,338,693 shares (90.1605% of their holding), while e-voting at the AGM and poll recorded zero votes across categories.
  • Public institutional participation was absent: public institutions held 1,750 shares and polled zero votes on every resolution.
  • Public non-institution participation was modest relative to shares held: 1,183,619 votes polled out of 6,107,106 shares held (19.381%) on the resolutions.
  • On the three related party transaction resolutions (Resolutions 3, 4 and 5) and the remuneration revision resolution (Resolution 6), promoter and promoter group shareholders were interested and recorded zero votes polled, leaving the outcome dependent on public non-institution votes. On these resolutions, total votes polled were 1,183,619 out of 24,230,650 outstanding shares (4.8848%).
  • A small number of votes were cast against: 13 votes against on each of Resolutions 1 through 6, representing 0.0011% of votes polled on those resolutions.

  • Resolution 4 approved material related party transaction limits with NIBL-Korta Engineering Private Limited, passed with 1,183,606 votes in favour and 13 against.

  • Resolution 6, a special resolution, approved revision in the remuneration of Mr. Devesh Singh Sahney, Chairman and Managing Director, for the period from 1 October 2026 to 30 September 2027, passed with 1,183,606 votes in favour and 13 against.

Key Risks

  • Related party transaction approvals (Resolutions 3, 4 and 5) and the remuneration revision (Resolution 6) were carried solely on public non-institution votes, as promoter and promoter group shareholders were interested and did not vote; total participation on these resolutions was 4.8848% of outstanding shares.
  • The narrow margin of participation on interested-party resolutions means outcomes rest on a small proportion of total share capital.
  • Dissenting votes, though minimal at 13 against per resolution, were recorded on every resolution, including the adoption of financial statements and the re-appointment of the managing director.
  • No public institutional votes were polled on any resolution, leaving no institutional endorsement recorded in the voting results.
  • The scrutinizer’s report notes that the management is responsible for compliance with the Companies Act, 2013 and rules on remote e-voting and e-voting, with the scrutinizer’s role restricted to reporting votes cast in favour or against based on CDSL-generated reports.
