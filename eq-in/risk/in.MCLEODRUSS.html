McLeod Russel India Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Financial distress and going-concern stress. The company’s current liabilities exceed its current assets, and operational losses have significantly affected net worth. Borrowings and interest could not be repaid as stipulated, and statutory and employee-related dues (including provident fund arrears demanded by authorities) could not be met, causing financial hardship and constraints. The auditors note that the company has been passing through prolonged financial distress over a considerable period.

Non-recoverable inter-corporate deposits. ICDs given to promoter group and other entities aggregate Rs. 2,86,050 lakhs (including interest accrued till March 31, 2019) as on June 30, 2026. These were largely routed to provide financial support to a promoter group company against which CIRP was initiated; under the NCLT-approved resolution plan, amounts advanced became irrecoverable. A provision of Rs. 2,44,629 lakhs was made in earlier years, leaving Rs. 41,421 lakhs unprovided. The company has filed a legal suit before the Hon’ble Calcutta High Court for recovery from certain promoter group entities.

Unrecognised interest and unascertained liabilities. Interest of Rs. 17,148 lakhs (including Rs. 959 lakhs for the quarter) on ICDs/short-term borrowings of Rs. 32,079 lakhs outstanding as on June 30, 2026 has not been recognised. Penal/compound interest on borrowings from a bank and ICDs has not been recognised. Statutory liabilities aggregate Rs. 24,008 lakhs. Lease rent for the registered/corporate office (agreement expired August 31, 2022) has not been recognised. The impact of the Labour Code notified November 21, 2025 is under evaluation and not yet recognised.

Operational cost pressures. The company notes that while tea realisations improved to some extent, increases in employee costs and remuneration impact overall performance. Remuneration to sub-staff of Assam tea estates was revised w.e.f. June 1, 2025 (impact of Rs. 382 lakhs recognised in the June 2026 quarter), and the Government of Assam increased daily-rated worker wages by Rs. 30 per day w.e.f. April 1, 2026 on an interim basis (impact of Rs. 922 lakhs recognised).

Seasonality and weather. The company states that its tea business is seasonal in character, so quarterly figures should not be construed as indicative of full-year results. The supplied material also references adverse weather conditions and unfavorable weather patterns at the start of the tea season, increased pest incidence, high absenteeism affecting labour availability, and operational constraints from limited working capital.

Arbitral award exposure. An arbitral award dated September 29, 2025 entitles claimants to recover Rs. 50,896 lakhs plus US$ 564,600 (equivalent to Rs. 534 lakhs as on June 30, 2026) and Rs. 20 lakhs in costs, jointly and severally from promoter group entities along with the company. The company has challenged the award before the Hon’ble High Court of Delhi (petition filed January 27, 2026); outcome is awaited.

Pending insolvency proceedings. Certain lenders and creditors filed petitions before the DRT and under the IBC with NCLT Kolkata, which remain pending. NARCL sought to withdraw its Section 7 application (alleged default of approximately Rs. 444.55 crores) in view of the Sanction Letter dated April 2, 2026; NCLT dismissed the application as withdrawn on July 14, 2026, with liberty to revive it if the restructuring process fails.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Debt restructuring through ARCs. Out of borrowings including interest aggregating Rs. 3,35,327 lakhs, Rs. 2,48,330 lakhs was assigned to NARCL and Rs. 74,966 lakhs to JCAF, leaving Rs. 12,031 lakhs payable to one lender bank. NARCL’s resolution plan was sanctioned by letter dated April 2, 2026, restructuring debts with effect from cut-off date December 31, 2025: sustainable debt of Rs. 1,05,000 lakhs (after adjusting Rs. 2,742 lakhs repaid till March 31, 2026) is payable over three years through promoters’ contribution, monetisation of assets, and operational accruals. An amount equivalent to 10% of total equity capital on a diluted basis (estimated Rs. 11,900 lakhs) is to be converted to equity and allotted to NARCL. The remaining unsustainable debt of Rs. 84,648 lakhs was adjusted as an exceptional item in the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026.

JCAF one-time settlement. JCAF issued a letter dated May 13, 2026 sanctioning an OTS with effect from cut-off date March 31, 2026: sustainable debt of Rs. 15,000 lakhs (after adjusting Rs. 62 lakhs repaid till March 31, 2026) is payable, including through monetisation of assets, over 15 months. The remaining unsustainable debt of Rs. 44,247 lakhs was adjusted as an exceptional item in the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026.

Asset monetisation. The Board approved the sale of specified assets of four tea estates (carrying value Rs. 8,485 lakhs as on June 30, 2026) with an MOU/term sheet for aggregate consideration of Rs. 12,305 lakhs, subject to due diligence and approvals; profit of Rs. 3,820 lakhs on such sale has not yet been recognised. Subsequent to the balance sheet date, the Board approved the sale of specified assets of another tea estate (Corramore Tea Estate) with an MOU/term sheet for aggregate consideration of Rs. 2,616 lakhs; the carrying value is Rs. 1,102 lakhs with an expected impact of Rs. 1,514 lakhs on profit and other equity.

Provident fund repayment approval. Assam Tea Employees Provident Fund Organisation, vide letter dated March 12, 2026, granted approval for repayment of provident fund dues of Rs. 19,111 lakhs (for the period up to January 31, 2026) over three years.

Market and demand factors. The supplied material references improving prices in the latter quarters of the year, growing preference for Orthodox teas, increasing demand in export markets such as the UAE, and the implementation of the OTS and restructuring plans with lenders.

Management’s going-concern expectation. Management believes the restructuring will allow adequate cashflow to meet obligations, that repayments and conditions precedent will be fulfilled, and that the company will strengthen its financial position over time. The financial results have been prepared on a going-concern basis on this assumption.

3. Key Risks

  • Revocation risk on restructuring. If there is default in payments or fulfilment of conditions (including regulatory compliances and approvals), the restructuring with NARCL and JCAF shall stand revoked, and outstanding amounts will be restored and payable per original terms.
  • Conditions precedent pending. The NARCL resolution is subject to execution of the Master Restructuring Agreement (MRA), which was pending as on the date of the results. Conditions include selling certain tea estates, promoter fund infusion, a provident fund settlement plan, settlement with one lender bank, and settlement of the arbitral award.
  • Bank settlement pending. The proposal for settlement of the Rs. 12,031 lakhs payable to one lender bank was under consideration as on the date of the results; recovery proceedings are pending.
  • Impairment uncertainty. Fair value of Property, Plant and Equipment, Intangible Assets under development, Capital Work in Progress, and investment in subsidiary (Rs. 15,967 lakhs in Borelli Tea Holdings Limited) required impairment testing; valuers were appointed and assessment was pending. Management envisages no impairment as on June 30, 2026, but impairment, if any, will be considered on determination.
  • Deferred tax asset uncertainty. Deferred tax assets of Rs. 28,525 lakhs created in earlier periods have been carried forward based on expected improvement in operational and financial performance; the auditors flag uncertainty regarding availability.
  • Unreconciled balances. Certain debit and credit balances, including borrowings and interest, statutory liabilities, trade payables, advances, loans and receivables, are subject to reconciliation and confirmation; adjustments and related disclosures (including MSME-related) are not currently ascertainable.
  • Regulatory and judicial matters. The matters relating to ICDs, including utilisation of loan amounts, are under examination and pending before regulatory and judicial authorities; final outcome and directions are awaited.
  • Adverse auditor conclusion. The auditors issued an adverse conclusion on both standalone and consolidated results, citing the significance of the matters above, including recoverability of ICDs, and stated the statements have not been prepared fairly in all material respects under Ind AS and the Listing Regulations.
  • Subsidiary review limitations. For the consolidated results, reports and information from certain subsidiaries’ independent auditors were awaited, and the auditors could not assess the work of those auditors.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance on restructuring and going concern. Management expressed confidence that repayments and compliances with conditions precedent would be in place and obligations restated to sustainable levels would be fulfilled in due course, and that the company would strengthen its financial position over time with sufficient funds for operations. Observed performance: the company continued to report losses, and the auditors noted that the ability to continue as a going concern depends on execution of the MRA by NARCL, fulfilment of conditions precedent, timely implementation of the JCAF OTS, and approval of the settlement proposal pending with a lender bank. The MRA remained unexecuted and the bank proposal remained under consideration as on the date of the results.

Guidance on impairment. Management stated that, considering the debt restructuring, the intrinsic value of the company has improved and no impairment adjustment is envisaged as on June 30, 2026. Observed status: valuation of assets was still pending, with valuers appointed and information submitted; impairment, if any, will be considered on determination.

Guidance on deferred tax assets. Management stated that future taxable income, pending detailed year-end review, will be sufficient to absorb the deferred tax assets over a period of time. Observed performance: the company reported a loss for the period, and the auditors flagged uncertainty regarding availability of the deferred tax assets.

Guidance on asset sales. The Board approved the sale of specified assets of four tea estates and, subsequently, another tea estate. Observed status: the transactions remained subject to due diligence, binding agreements, and statutory/lender approvals; the profit of Rs. 3,820 lakhs on the four-estate sale had not been recognised, and operations of those estates continued to be included in the results.

Guidance on 2026 growth. Management anticipates that 2026 will deliver growth in both business volume and profitability, driven by major sales verticals, large annual contracts in the UAE local market, and resumed shipments to CIS countries and Indonesia. Observed performance: for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, standalone revenue from operations was Rs. 20,445 lakhs (versus Rs. 16,803 lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2025) and the standalone loss for the period was Rs. 1,478 lakhs (versus Rs. 4,013 lakhs in the prior-year quarter); consolidated revenue from operations was Rs. 26,111 lakhs (versus Rs. 21,576 lakhs) and the consolidated loss was Rs. 1,338 lakhs (versus Rs. 4,348 lakhs). For the year ended March 31, 2026, the standalone loss after tax was Rs. 9,086 lakhs and the consolidated loss after tax was Rs. 12,350 lakhs. The company cautions that, due to the seasonal nature of the tea business, quarterly figures should not be construed as indicative of full-year results.
