MRPL: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Board and committee composition non-compliance: MRPL received notices from BSE Limited and National Stock Exchange of India Limited for non-compliance with regulations governing the composition of the Board and its Sub-committees for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. Each exchange levied a fine of Rs. 14,19,540.
  • Independent director shortfall: The company does not have the requisite number of Independent Directors as required under Regulation 17(1) of the SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015, the Companies Act, 2013, and DPE guidelines. As a consequence, the Audit Committee’s functions were carried out by the Board of Directors in the absence of the required quorum.
  • Prior-year loss position: The first quarter of FY 2025-26 had recorded a loss before tax of ₹(403) crore and a loss after tax of ₹(272) crore, against which the current quarter’s profitability represents a turnaround.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Sharp earnings recovery: For Q1 FY 2026-27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026), revenue from operations was ₹41,609 crore, compared with ₹20,989 crore in Q1 FY 2025-26. Profit before tax was ₹1,215 crore and profit after tax was ₹915 crore, compared with losses in the year-earlier quarter.
  • Throughput improvement: Throughput (crude plus others) was 4.43 MMT in Q1 FY 2026-27, against 4.35 MMT in Q4 FY 2025-26 and 3.52 MMT in Q1 FY 2025-26.
  • One-time price revision benefit: An additional income (net of expenses) of INR 471.76 crore was recognized during the quarter pursuant to revision of certain petroleum product prices on supplies made in the previous period.
  • Lower effective tax rate: The company exercised the option for a lower tax rate under Section 200 of the Income Tax Act, 2025, reducing its effective tax rate from 34.944% to 25.168% and allowing it to carry forward accumulated MAT Credit.
  • Logistics and market expansion:
    • PNGRB Authorization received for an ATF pipeline from Devangonthi Terminal to Kempegowda International Airport, Bengaluru.
    • Product loading commenced at Aegis Terminal in Mangaluru, Hindupur depot in Andhra Pradesh, and Ennore terminal in Tamil Nadu.
    • Lease agreements executed for tankages at Jawaharlal Nehru Port Authority (JNPA) in Navi Mumbai, Kakinada, and Krishnapatnam in Andhra Pradesh.
  • Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF): MRPL obtained certification under the ISCC CORSIA framework on April 24, 2026, for co-processing of Used Cooking Oil (UCO), described as a significant milestone in its SAF journey.
  • Awards: Second Prize under Category B in the Swachhta Pakhwada Awards – 2023 and a Consolation Prize in the 2024 edition, both from the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas (MoPNG).
  • Governance actions: The 38th Annual General Meeting was held on August 24, 2026, where all 10 resolutions set out in the notice dated July 15, 2026, were passed with the requisite majority. Shri Sanjib Kumar Mandal was appointed Internal Auditor and M/s Bandyopadhyaya Bhaumik & Co. was appointed Cost Auditor for FY 2026-27.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory and listing compliance risk: The board and sub-committee composition shortfall triggered fines from both BSE and NSE (Rs. 14,19,540 each) for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. MRPL has requested a waiver from the stock exchanges on the basis that, as a Central Public Sector Enterprise, its directors are nominated by the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, Government of India.
  • Governance oversight risk: The absence of the requisite number of Independent Directors, and the resulting conduct of Audit Committee functions by the Board without the required quorum, indicate a gap in the intended governance structure.
  • Dependence on administrative processes: The composition of the Board is tied to nomination by the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, which is the stated basis for the waiver request.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • No forward-looking guidance or projections are provided in the supplied material.
  • Observed performance for Q1 FY 2026-27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026): standalone revenue from operations of ₹41,609 crore; profit before tax of ₹1,215 crore; profit after tax of ₹915 crore; total comprehensive income of ₹912 crore. Consolidated profit after tax attributable to owners was ₹945.68 crore (with consolidated total comprehensive income of ₹943 crore), while standalone net profit for the period was ₹914.82 crore.
  • Comparative observed performance: Q4 FY 2025-26 standalone revenue was ₹28,493 crore, PBT ₹1,235 crore, and PAT ₹119 crore; Q1 FY 2025-26 standalone revenue was ₹20,989 crore, PBT ₹(403) crore, and PAT ₹(272) crore. Full-year FY 2025-26 standalone revenue was ₹1,05,155 crore, EBITDA ₹6,449 crore, PBT ₹4,022 crore, and PAT ₹1,931 crore.
  • Exports were ₹5,012 crore in Q1 FY 2026-27, against ₹7,252 crore in Q4 FY 2025-26, ₹4,767 crore in Q1 FY 2025-26, and ₹29,880 crore for FY 2025-26.
  • The quarter’s results include the ₹471.76 crore additional income from revised petroleum product prices on prior-period supplies and the benefit of the lower effective tax rate, both of which management disclosed as factors affecting the reported figures.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from sector-wide macro risks and optimism to company-specific operational issues. Initially, brokers flagged volatile refining margins, windfall taxes, high LNG prices, and rich valuations as headwinds, while banking on strong consumption, Russian crude discounts, and robust marketing margins. By the final report, these were replaced by concerns over summer water supply, captive power plant delays, and investor skepticism, while tailwinds shifted to geopolitical-driven crack spreads and debt reduction.

Fears that came true

  • Refining margins fell from 2022 peaks, leading to a sharp stock decline in Q2 2024 as evidenced by successful Sell calls.
  • Windfall export taxes on diesel, ATF, and petrol pressured standalone refiners, contributing to DISAPPOINTMENT Buy calls in early 2024.
  • Election-related retail fuel price cuts squeezed refining margins, aligning with the stock’s negative performance in mid-2024.

Optimism that failed

  • Expected modest crude and LNG prices failed to materialize as geopolitical tensions spiked energy prices, causing refining margin volatility.
  • The anticipated GRM premium over the Singapore benchmark did not hold, as refining margins weakened in 2024.
