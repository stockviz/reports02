GPPL: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Middle East conflict is cited as a factor resulting in lower minerals imports for bulk cargo and lower LPG and fuel oil imports for liquid cargo.
  • Cyclone Tauktae (May 2021) disrupted port operations from 1 June 2021 until the port was restored. There was no loss of life and no loss of port infrastructure, but a portion of property, plant and equipment required repairs. The Company incurred INR 847.10 million towards cyclone expenditure and received an interim claim of INR 350.00 million up to 31 March 2025, with no additional expenditure incurred during the previous year as the entire restoration work was completed.
  • Labour Codes transition costs: The Company recognised an incremental impact of INR 43.29 million (comprising past service cost, primarily gratuity) arising from a change in the definition of prescribed wages under the new Labour Codes. This was recognised immediately in the interim statement of profit and loss and presented as an “Exceptional Item” for the quarter and year ended 31 March 2026, being regulatory-driven and non-recurring in nature. The Company continues to monitor finalisation of the Central Labour Code Rules.
  • GMB arbitration and settlement history: The Company had entered into a settlement agreement with GMB in 1998 concerning terms and conditions proposed by GMB for setting up tank farms at the port. One of the conditions was that the Company should provide rail connectivity at Pipavav, which the Company could not meet as rail connectivity was established only by 2003. The customer initiated arbitration proceedings against the Company in 2005 seeking refund of INR 107.30 million paid in 2000 as land premium and development charges. The Arbitrator announced an award against the Company on 12 February 2024, with refund of principal plus interest until the date of payment. The Company filed for rectification of the award; a revised order was issued on 18 May 2024 and a second rectification order on 19 August 2024. The customer filed a petition under Section 34 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 before the Honorable High Court of Mumbai on 14 November 2024. The Company filed a commercial execution application on 5 December 2024, and on 1 April 2025 the Honorable High Court of Mumbai allowed the interim application and granted a conditional stay, subject to submission of a bank guarantee or deposit of the award amount payable under the arbitral award. The Company submitted a Bank Guarantee of INR 601.36 million with the Registrar on 2 July 2025 and has continued accrual of the estimated amount of INR 671.64 million, including interest cost of INR 518.24 million. No further development in the matter during the reporting period.
  • GST and liquidated damages claim: GMB encashed a bank guarantee of INR 185.35 million on 13 February 2019 and asked the Company to pay INR 337.59 million towards liquidated damages plus interest at 18% per annum, along with GST on the aforesaid bank guarantee amounting to INR 33.36 million plus interest at 18% per annum. The Company reviewed the terms and conditions of approval and, based on management assessment and external legal expert advice, believes the bank guarantee is recoverable as well as no liquidated damages are liable to be paid, and had filed a Commercial Suit before the Commercial Court, Rajula.
  • Dispute resolution mechanism: The Commercial Court, Rajula directed both parties to settle the matter through arbitration. The Company sent a legal notice on 28 August 2023 under Section 11 of the Arbitration Act. GMB replied on 27 September 2023 stating the matter should be resolved per the dispute resolution mechanism of the concession agreement. The Company gave its concurrence for the alternative dispute resolution mechanism. GMB received a letter on 12 June 2024 requesting appointment of a Company representative as part of an Expert Committee to resolve the issue. The Company nominated its representative and intimated GMB vide letter dated 08 August 2024. GMB nominated its representative and communicated the same to the Company vide letter dated 28 April 2025. The Expert Committee proposed a final settlement of INR 188.31 million. The Board of Gujarat Pipavav Port Ltd accepted the proposal of the Expert Committee. The Company recognised INR 188.31 million as an exceptional item during the quarter and year ended 31 March 2026, adjusted the receivable of INR 185.35 million recoverable from GMB, and recognised the balance amount payable of INR 2.96 million as a liability in the consolidated financial results for the quarter ended 31 March 2026. GMB’s Board has accepted the recommendations of the expert committee. Subsequently, the Company vide its letter dated 16 June 2026 conveyed its acceptance and concurrence, and now only execution of the settlement agreement remains pending.
  • Key personnel transition: Mr. Amit Bhardwaj, Chief Commercial Officer, resigned with effect from 30 April 2026. Mr. Zeeshan Mukhi has been appointed as Chief Commercial Officer, effective by mid-October 2026. Mr. Mukhi has over 22 years of experience in Container Shipping and Logistics and has held various positions within AP Moller Maersk and DP World, with a leadership role for the last decade.
  • Board transition: Ms. Monica Widhani, Independent Director, ceased to be a Director effective 12 August 2026 after completion of her 5-year tenure. Mrs. Harjeet Kaur Joshi was appointed as Independent Director effective 1 July 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Additional transhipment volume for containers is cited as a tailwind.
  • Higher exports driven by OEMs for RORO is cited as a tailwind.
  • SEIS Duty Credit Scrips: During the current quarter, the Company received approval from the Office of the Additional Director General of Foreign Trade, Mumbai, vide its approval dated 22 April 2026, for issuance of SEIS Duty Credit Scrips aggregating to INR 521.70 million pertaining to FY 2017-18 (INR 223.35 million) and FY 2018-19 (INR 298.35 million). The approval has been considered as an adjusting event as it provides additional evidence of conditions existing as at 31 March 2026. Accordingly, the Duty Credit Scrips were recognised in the standalone financial results for the previous quarter ended 31 March 2026 at their estimated realisable value of INR 495.62 million (95% of face value), which has been included under Other Operating Revenue within Revenue from Operations. During the current quarter, the Company transferred the aforesaid Duty Credit Scrips and realised consideration of INR 502.40 million. The difference between the carrying amount and the consideration received, amounting to INR 6.78 million, has been recognised as Other Operating Revenue within Revenue from Operations in the standalone financial results for the current quarter.
  • Prior SEIS approvals: The Company had received approval on 08 June 2026 for SEIS Duty Credit Scrips pertaining to FY 2015-16 (INR 138.43 million) and FY 2016-17 (INR 180.14 million), having an aggregate face value of INR 318.57 million. During the current quarter, the Duty Credit Scrips were recognised and transferred for an aggregate consideration of INR 309.49 million (97.15% of face value), which has been included under Other Operating Revenue within Revenue from Operations.
  • GMB settlement: The amicable settlement of the long-standing dispute with GMB, with only execution of the settlement agreement now pending, removes a source of litigation and potential further disputes.
  • Cyclone restoration completed: The entire restoration work following Cyclone Tauktae has been completed, with no additional expenditure incurred during the previous year.

3. Key Risks

  • Middle East conflict impacting cargo volumes, specifically lower minerals imports for bulk and lower LPG and fuel oil imports for liquid.
  • Arbitration and litigation risk: The GMB arbitration matter, though now at the settlement execution stage, remains pending execution of the settlement agreement. The Company has submitted a Bank Guarantee of INR 601.36 million with the Registrar and continues to accrue an estimated amount of INR 671.64 million including interest cost of INR 518.24 million.
  • GST and liquidated damages claim: GMB’s claim of INR 337.59 million towards liquidated damages plus interest at 18% per annum, and GST on the bank guarantee of INR 33.36 million plus interest at 18% per annum, remains subject to legal proceedings, though the Company believes the bank guarantee is recoverable and no liquidated damages are liable to be paid.
  • Regulatory changes: The new Labour Codes and finalisation of Central Labour Code Rules could give rise to further incremental costs. The Company continues to monitor finalisation of the Central Labour Code Rules.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: Statements describing the Company’s objectives, projections, estimates, and expectations may be forward-looking statements within the meaning of applicable securities laws and regulations, and actual results could differ materially.
  • Key personnel risk: The Chief Commercial Officer transition, with the outgoing officer resigning effective 30 April 2026 and the incoming officer effective by mid-October 2026, creates a period of leadership transition in the commercial function.
