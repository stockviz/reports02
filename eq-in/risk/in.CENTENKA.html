Century Enka Limited — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Raw material volatility: Caprolactam and chips prices rose sharply and substantially in a short period following the Iran war, and management notes these remain volatile due to geopolitical conditions. The company expects margins to normalize as higher-cost inventory gets consumed.
  • Cheap Chinese imports: Imports of commodity filament yarn from China continued at very low prices. Despite favourable findings by the DGTR on dumping, the Finance Ministry did not notify the anti-dumping duty.
  • Energy costs: Petro-based energy costs increased, though higher renewable power availability partially offset this.
  • Demand uncertainty: Evolving geopolitical developments, volatile crude oil prices, and persistent inflation remain areas to monitor and could impact demand growth in coming quarters. Management flagged risks of deferment of purchases, reduced demand for new vehicles, and demand cuts among value chain partners.
  • Import threat: Different geopolitical situations across countries could lead to a surge in imports into domestic markets, where Century Enka has most of its exposure (export exposure is very little).
  • Margin normalization: The Q1 FY27 profitability benefited from one-time inventory gains (Rs. 46.24 crores, as reported in quarterly results) arising from low-cost opening stock; management expects margins to normalize as higher-cost inventory is consumed.
  • Safety incident legacy: Following the Bharuch incident in February 2025, the company has been spending on safety improvements, particularly fire risk mitigation.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • GST cuts on tyres and automobiles: Tyre cord fabric demand remained robust following GST cuts, with healthy growth across all automotive segments (two-wheelers, three-wheelers, tractors, personal vehicles, heavy commercial vehicles) driving strong demand from OEMs. Management observed a stark improvement from Q3 onwards across auto companies, tyre companies, and value chain partners.
  • Record quarterly performance: Q1 FY27 delivered record revenue growth, profitability growth, and margin performance, driven by healthy volume growth across all business verticals, productivity improvements, and higher operating rates.
  • PTCF approval progress: The PTCF approval process is moving in the desired direction, with commercial sales expected to commence in H2 FY27 (possibly Q3 or Q4).
  • Value-added products (VAPs): New mother yarn and other valuated products continue to support margin expansion. New VAP investments are planned during the year to offer more customer-specific products and improve margins. The share of value-added products is increasing year on year.
  • Renewable power expansion: Renewable power was over 40% of power consumption for the quarter. Expansion at Bharuch (an additional 10–10.5 MW capacity) is expected to commission in H2 or most likely Q3 of FY27, taking the renewable share to around 50%, with a structural reduction in power rates.
  • Capacity addition: A new project commissioning in FY28 may increase capacity by 3,000–4,000 tonnes per annum (mother yarn).
  • Pricing power: The company was able to effectively pass on raw material cost increases through calibrated pricing actions.

3. Key Risks

  • Margin sustainability: Management maintains normalized EBITDA margins in the 7–10% range, noting that anything above this may not be a permanent feature. Sharp raw material price movements (up or down) in a short period can significantly impact gross margins.
  • Import competition: Commodity imports from China at very low prices, with no anti-dumping duty notified, pose a continuing competitive threat, particularly in commodity filament yarn.
  • Demand cyclicality: Auto demand is cyclical; the durability of GST-cut-led demand improvement is uncertain and depends on financing conditions and the general economic environment.
  • Raw material availability: While no current shortage is foreseen, China remains a large share of sourcing; geopolitical developments could affect supply, though the company is diversifying across domestic sources and multiple countries.
  • Inventory exposure: The company holds 25–30 days of raw material inventory (including in-transit) for imported material and around 10–15 days for domestic sources, exposing it to price volatility.
  • Project execution: Renewable power commissioning is weather-dependent and may not operate at constant levels, though yearly performance is expected within forecast ranges.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Margin guidance: Management has guided normalized EBITDA margins of 7–10%, upgraded from a prior 6–8% range. In Q1 FY27, reported EBITDA margin was 15.46% (up 1,050 bps YoY and 400 bps QoQ), significantly above the guided range, but this included one-time inventory gains of Rs. 46.24 crores. Management explicitly stated margins should normalize over the next few quarters.
  • Volume guidance: Management does not provide specific volume guidance, stating focus is on value addition and margin improvement rather than significant capacity addition in the current financial year. Observed Q1 FY27 volume was 19,199 metric tonnes, up 12% YoY from 17,072, but down from 20,711 in Q4 FY26.
  • PTCF commercialization: Management expects commercial sales to start in H2 FY27, with progress expected in the current quarter and some commercial sales possibly from Q3 or Q4. This remains a forward-looking expectation.
  • Renewable power: Management expects the renewable share to rise from over 40% to around 50% once the Bharuch expansion commissions in H2/Q3 FY27. This is yet to be observed.
  • Capex: Management expects to spend over Rs. 100 crores in capex in the current financial year, with a minimum IRR hurdle of 12–15% for projects. Equity investment of about Rs. 8.5 crores has been made in the group captive renewable JV.
  • Capacity utilization: Management indicated normal operating capacity utilization of 85–90%, varying across products depending on market conditions.
  • Financial performance (Q1 FY27): Operating revenue Rs. 554 crores (up 38% YoY, 15% QoQ); EBITDA Rs. 86 crores (up 331% YoY, 55% QoQ); PAT approximately Rs. 62 crores (up 301% YoY, 57% QoQ); PAT margin 11.13%. Tyre cord fabric sales rose 69% to Rs. 306 crores; filament yarn sales rose 20% to Rs. 230 crores. Basic/diluted EPS was Rs. 28.24. The company holds a 24% NFY domestic market share and 25% NTCF domestic market share, operating 2 plants across 4 locations with 39 dealers and approximately 92,000 MTPA capacity.
