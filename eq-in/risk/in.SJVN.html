Headwinds and Challenges

Pending tariff determination for NJHPS. The CERC notified the Tariff Regulations, 2024 (Order dated 15.03.2024) for the 2024–29 period. Tariff for the Rampur Hydro Power Station has been approved, but the tariff order for the Nathpa Jhakri Hydro Power Station (NJHPS) with effect from 01.04.2024 remains pending. Billing to beneficiaries for NJHPS is therefore being done provisionally on the basis of the tariff approved and applicable as on 31.03.2024. This is flagged as an Emphasis of Matter in both the standalone and consolidated limited review reports.

Devasari Hydro Electric Project on hold. Survey and investigation works for the Devasari Hydro Electric Project in Uttarakhand were put on hold per Ministry of Power directions dated 06.07.2021. SJVN has taken the matter up with the Ministry of Power and the Government of Uttarakhand for resumption. Expenditure of ₹251.45 crore (including capital work in progress of ₹200.36 crore) had been incurred up to 30.06.2026, against ₹251.38 crore (including CWIP of ₹200.34 crore) up to 31.03.2026. Management’s view is that the hold will be withdrawn.

Land revenue levy in Himachal Pradesh. A notification dated 03.02.2026 from the Department of Settlement, Government of Himachal Pradesh, levied land revenue in respect of NJHPS and Rampur Hydro Power Station. SJVN has filed petitions before the Himachal Pradesh High Court challenging the levy, and the amount has not been recognised. The company states that even if the liability crystallises, it would be recoverable from beneficiaries/customers as a pass-through item under applicable tariff regulations.

Stock exchange fines for board composition non-compliance. Notices dated 25.08.2026 from BSE and NSE levied fines of ₹13,44,020 each for non-compliance with SEBI Listing Regulations provisions relating to the composition of the Board, Audit Committee, Nomination and Remuneration Committee, Stakeholder Relationship Committee, Risk Management Committee, and quorum requirements, arising from the absence of the requisite number of Independent Directors. SJVN notes it is a Government Company where appointment/removal powers vest with the President of India acting through the Ministry of Power.

Seasonality. The company notes that, given the seasonal nature of the business, financial results may vary from quarter to quarter.

Weak quarterly coverage and liquidity indicators. For the quarter ended 30.06.2026, standalone DSCR was 0.50 (against 2.00 in the quarter ended 30.06.2025 and 2.47 for the year ended 31.03.2026), and the current ratio was 0.62 (against 1.87 a year earlier). Consolidated DSCR was 0.60 (against 2.21 a year earlier), with a consolidated current ratio of 0.54. Standalone finance cost of ₹141.06 crore and depreciation of ₹103.00 crore weighed on the quarter.

Lower standalone revenue and profit year-on-year. Standalone revenue from operations was ₹758.83 crore for Q1 FY2026-27, against ₹822.44 crore for the quarter ended 30.06.2025. Standalone profit after tax was ₹246.88 crore, against ₹258.51 crore a year earlier. Standalone profit before tax fell to ₹376.41 crore from ₹443.33 crore. The company also utilised MAT credit of ₹Nil in the quarter, against ₹60.14 crore in the quarter ended 30.06.2025.

Subsidiary losses within the consolidated results. The consolidated review report notes that two subsidiaries reviewed by other auditors — SJVN Green Energy Limited and SJVN Thermal Power Limited — reflected total revenues (including other income) of ₹644.17 crore, a total net loss after tax of ₹(21.63) crore, and total comprehensive loss of ₹(21.76) crore for the quarter ended 30.06.2026. Consolidated profit after tax was ₹224.74 crore, against ₹227.58 crore a year earlier, and consolidated operating margin was 48.17% against 68.32% a year earlier.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Commissioning of the 75 MW Jamui Solar Power Project. SJVN achieved the Commercial Operation Date of the 75 MW Jamui Solar Power Project in District Jamui, Bihar, executed through wholly owned subsidiary SJVN Green Energy Limited. The project is expected to generate approximately 90 million units in its first year and around 3,522 million units cumulatively over 25 years. It was developed on approximately 300 acres of outright-purchased land near Village Hadhadiya, Kakanchaur, at an estimated project cost of ₹342 crore, with a tariff of ₹2.96 per unit, supplying power to the State of Bihar. It was developed pursuant to a tender floated by the Bihar Renewable Energy Development Agency (BREDA), Government of Bihar. The project is estimated to reduce approximately 82,250.74 tonnes of carbon emissions and created approximately 15–20 direct and 50–100 indirect employment opportunities.

Buxar Thermal Power Project Unit-2 trial run. SJVN completed the 72-hour trial run of the 660 MW Unit-2 of the 1,320 MW Buxar Thermal Power Project in Bihar on 31.08.2026. The project is implemented by SJVN Thermal Private Limited, a wholly owned subsidiary, uses supercritical technology, and comprises two units of 660 MW each with advanced emission control technology. Management stated the company is now all set for Commercial Operation of Unit-2, and that once fully commissioned the project will generate approximately 9,828.72 million units of electricity annually.

Resolution of the independent director shortfall. The Ministry of Power appointed Smt. Arti Kujur as an independent director effective 17.07.2026, leading to reconstitution of the relevant committees and compliance with Regulations 17(2A), 20(2)/(2A), and 21(2) of the SEBI Listing Regulations.

Alignment with national renewable energy targets. The Jamui commissioning is described as a contribution towards the Government of India’s target of 500 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2030 and the country’s decarbonisation objectives.

Consolidated revenue growth year-on-year. Consolidated revenue from operations rose to ₹1,394.38 crore for the quarter ended 30.06.2026, from ₹917.45 crore for the quarter ended 30.06.2025, reflecting the expanded consolidated asset base including thermal and renewable subsidiaries.

Debt-funded expansion reflected in consolidated balance sheet. Consolidated paid-up debt capital stood at ₹32,562.18 crore as at 30.06.2026, against ₹28,125.32 crore as at 30.06.2025, with consolidated net worth of ₹14,460.57 crore.

Key Risks

  • Regulatory and tariff risk: the pending NJHPS tariff order for 2024–29 leaves revenues provisionally billed, with the final determination yet to be settled.
  • Project execution and hold risk: the Devasari Hydro Electric Project remains suspended since the 06.07.2021 Ministry of Power direction, with ₹251.45 crore of expenditure incurred and recovery dependent on resumption.
  • State levy risk: the Himachal Pradesh land revenue notification of 03.02.2026 is under challenge before the High Court; the amount is unrecognised, and recovery is expected as a tariff pass-through.
  • Governance and compliance risk: the BSE and NSE fines of ₹13,44,020 each reflect a historical shortfall in Independent Directors, with a waiver request to be submitted.
  • Leverage and coverage risk: consolidated debt-to-equity was 2.25 as at 30.06.2026, and consolidated DSCR of 0.60 for the quarter indicates tight near-term debt service coverage relative to the full-year figure of 1.91.
  • Seasonality risk: results may vary from quarter to quarter due to the seasonal nature of the business.
  • Dependence on subsidiary performance: consolidated results include subsidiaries with losses in the quarter and entities whose results were not reviewed by their auditors (SJVN Arun-3 Power Development Company, SJVN Lower Arun Power Development Company, SGEL Assam Renewable Energy Limited, and the Cross Border Power Transmission Company joint venture).

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance on Unit-2 commercial operation. Management stated the company is “all set for Commercial Operation of Unit-2” of the Buxar project following the 72-hour trial run completed on 31.08.2026, and guided to annual generation of approximately 9,828.72 million units once fully commissioned. The observed event is the completion of the trial run; commercial operation and the guided generation level are forward-looking.

Guidance on the Devasari hold. Management’s view is that the hold on the Devasari project will be withdrawn. Observed performance: the project remains on hold, with expenditure rising only marginally from ₹251.38 crore as at 31.03.2026 to ₹251.45 crore as at 30.06.2026, indicating no material resumption of activity in the quarter.

Guidance on the land revenue levy. Management states the levy would be recoverable from beneficiaries/customers as a pass-through and would not impact financial results. Observed performance: the amount has not been recognised, and the matter is before the Himachal Pradesh High Court.

Guidance on the exchange fines. The company will submit a request to both stock exchanges for waiver of the fines. Observed performance: the fines were levied on 25.08.2026, and the appointment of Smt. Arti Kujur as independent director effective 17.07.2026 has restored compliance with the cited regulations.

Guidance on Jamui project output. Management guided to approximately 90 million units in the first year and around 3,522 million units cumulatively over 25 years at a tariff of ₹2.96 per unit. Observed performance: the COD has been achieved; generation outcomes are forward-looking.

Guidance on NJHPS tariff. Billing continues provisionally at the tariff applicable as on 31.03.2024 pending the CERC order. Observed performance: the tariff order remains pending as at the date of the results.

Guidance on AGM resolutions. All nine resolutions presented at the 38th Annual General Meeting held on 31.08.2026 were duly approved by shareholders with the requisite majority, including adoption of financial statements, dividend confirmations, director and auditor appointments, and remuneration ratifications.

Observed financial performance versus prior periods. Standalone revenue from operations declined year-on-year (₹758.83 crore versus ₹822.44 crore), and standalone profit after tax declined (₹246.88 crore versus ₹258.51 crore). Standalone operating margin was 68.19% for the quarter, against 76.11% a year earlier, while net profit margin was 32.53% against 31.43%. Consolidated revenue grew year-on-year (₹1,394.38 crore versus ₹917.45 crore), but consolidated operating margin fell to 48.17% from 68.32% and consolidated net profit margin fell to 16.12% from 24.81%. The company has opted for taxation under Section 200 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 with effect from Tax Year 2026-27, and current tax expense for the quarter has been recognised accordingly.
