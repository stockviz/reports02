Premier Energies (PREMIERENE) — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Margin compression in the solar segment. Consolidated operational EBITDA margin slipped to 29.01% in Q1 FY27 from 30.11% in Q1 FY26 and 30.26% in Q4 FY26. The Solar Cells and Modules segment recorded a 29.22% operational EBITDA margin, while Transformers stood at 24.27%. EBITDA margin (including other income) fell to 30.28% from 31.94% a year earlier.
  • Rising cost lines. Cost of materials consumed rose 40.96% YOY to INR 13,470.84 Mn, purchases of stock-in-trade rose sharply to INR 2,536.03 Mn from INR 1,129.66 Mn, employee benefits expense rose 71.62% YOY, and sales, administration and other expenses rose 51.71% YOY. Finance costs increased 19.35% YOY to INR 439.34 Mn.
  • Depreciation step-up. Depreciation and amortisation rose 20.87% QOQ to INR 956.29 Mn, reflecting the expanding asset base.
  • Policy-related uncertainty. The ALMM-II relaxation provides only a temporary exemption until December 2026 for C&I projects and residential projects forgoing government subsidies under PM Surya Ghar Yojana, implying a defined window before norms revert. Countervailing duties on Malaysian solar glass were extended by five years to June 2031, affecting input cost dynamics for imported glass.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk on new capacity. The 7 GW Naidupeta cell plant is still in commissioning — civil work completed, machinery on-site, tool-in and hookup started, with trial runs expected by end of the month. The 10 GVA HV and EHV transformer capacity at Neotrafo is expected to reach COD only by September 2026. The 12 GWh BESS facility’s first 6 GWh phase is targeted for FY27-28. The Q1 FY27 effective capacity utilisation figure excludes the newly commissioned Seetharampur plant, indicating the new module capacity is not yet reflected in utilisation metrics.
  • Order book decline. The order book fell to INR 140 Bn as on 30 June 2026 from INR 150 Bn as on 31 March 2026, with new orders of INR 33 Bn and sales of INR 23 Bn during the quarter. The order book is heavily concentrated in cells (40%) and modules (58%), with transformers at 2% and EPC at 0%.
  • Export concentration. Exports were 12% of revenue in Q1 FY27, down from 22% in Q4 FY26 and 24% in Q3 FY26, with domestic sales at 88%. This reflects a shift toward the domestic market and reduced export contribution.
  • Leverage increase. Total debt to equity rose to 0.86 in Q1 FY27 from 0.79 in Q4 FY26 and 0.49 in Q1 FY26. Net debt increased to INR 16,417 Mn from INR 10,464 Mn in Q4 FY26, reversing the negative net debt position of INR -10,570 Mn in Q1 FY26.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong industry installation momentum. Quarterly solar capacity addition data shows ground-mounted, rooftop, off-grid, and KUSUM installations across Q1 FY25 to Q1 FY27, with PM Surya Ghar and KUSUM component installations tracked across Maharashtra, UP, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Kerala and others.
  • Solar’s rising share in the grid. Solar contributed 25% of incremental power generation in 2017-2025, versus fossil fuels at 55%, wind at 9%, and hydro at 8%. Solar power output has grown steadily from 2019 through H1 2026.
  • Data centre-driven power demand. India’s data centre market is projected to grow from 1.9 GW installed capacity in FY2026 to 4.5 GW, with a development pipeline of 28 GW and potential solar demand of 24 GW by 2030. Policy support includes Gujarat’s data centre policy 2026-29 (7.5 GW AI data centre target, INR 6 Tn investment, minimum 51% renewable energy requirement, 2.5% capital subsidy for Dholera projects, INR 1.00/kWh power subsidy), Rajasthan’s data centre policy 2025 (INR 200 Bn investment target, 5% interest subsidy for 5 years, INR 100-200 Mn per year investment-linked incentive for 10 years), and Andhra Pradesh’s data centre policy 2024-29 (1 GW capacity target, capital subsidy, stamp duty reimbursement, tailor-made incentives for projects > 50 MW).
  • BESS demand pickup. Market opportunity estimated at INR 600 Bn per annum. Tender status as of June 2026: open tenders 44 GWh, awarded 16 GWh, under construction 52 GWh. Monthly capacity addition reached 4,230 MWh and 2,031 MWh in recent periods, with hourly exchange prices in May reaching INR 10.00/kWh.
  • Policy support. ALMM-II relaxation until December 2026, INR 51 Bn incentive programme for 5 GW floating solar plus 10 GWh BESS by 2031, SECI designated as sole nodal agency for central renewable energy procurement, stricter vendor accountability and module warranty norms under PM Surya Ghar, World Bank financing of USD 890 Mn for residential rooftop solar, countervailing duty extension on Malaysian solar glass to June 2031, and additional PLI bids for 10 GWh domestic battery cell manufacturing for grid-scale applications.
  • Capacity expansion. Cells capacity expanding from 3.6 GW to 10.6 GW; modules from 11.1 GW; ingot-wafers at 10 GW; aluminum frames at 18,000 MT; transformers at 570 MVA; BESS containers at 12 GWh under construction. The 5.6 GW Seetharampur module plant was inaugurated, producing 4 modules every 16 seconds using advanced automation. The 7 GW Naidupeta cell plant is nearing completion.
  • Transcon (transformers) growth. Transcon Q1 FY27 revenue was INR 1,100 Mn, EBITDA INR 294 Mn, PAT INR 183 Mn. Operational GVA capacity is 6.75 with 10 GVA under construction. Order book is INR 2.5 Bn. Growth drivers cited: increasing electrification of the economy (mobility, cooking, industry), grid expansion and modernisation, growing demand from AI usage and data centres, and attractive export opportunity. The Neotrafo plant for 10 GVA HV and EHV transformer capacity is expected to reach COD by September 2026.
  • BESS joint venture. Premier Energies and RCT India (part of RCT Group, Germany) entered into a binding term sheet to form a joint venture — Premier Energies Storage Solutions Private Limited (PESSPL) — to establish a 12 GWh BESS manufacturing facility in Telangana, with the first phase planned at 6 GWh, expected to be set up in FY27-28 at Seetharampur, Telangana. PBTPL and RCT India will hold 85% and 15% equity respectively, with an option for RCT India for an additional 5% (resulting in 80% and 20%). The facility is expected to cater to commercial, industrial, and utility-scale applications in India and international markets, envisioned as an export-oriented manufacturing platform.
  • Corporate reorganization and new entity. The Board approved incorporation of PE Horizon Pte. Ltd. in Singapore with ODI up to SGD 1,00,000 to carry on trading, management consulting, and ancillary activities in the clean energy industry. An intra-group shareholding reorganization will transfer the Company’s entire shareholding in PESSPL to PBTPL via a share swap, consolidating battery and energy storage businesses under a single sub-holding structure. Indicative completion is approximately 60 days from disclosure.
  • Capital raising. A proposed capital raise of up to INR 5,000 Crores through issuance of equity shares or other eligible convertible securities, to support organic expansion and inorganic growth, is a special resolution at the 31st AGM scheduled for September 21, 2026.
  • Mission 2028. Target to become India’s leading provider of cleantech solutions with vertically integrated capacity of 10 GW+ across ingots, wafers, cells, modules, EPC services, BESS solutions, inverters, and transformers. Allied products expected to contribute about 25% of group revenues, with strong sales synergies cited for lower customer acquisition cost and better margins.
  • Sustainability credentials. 30% female employees in plants, 12,716 green jobs, 60,000+ hours employee training, Certified Great Place to Work, zero fatalities, India’s first LEED gold rated solar manufacturing facility, 11.6 MW rooftop solar reducing scope 2 emissions by 8% in FY2026, Kiwa Top Performer, amongst top 13 global module manufacturers as rated by Terawatt PV, ALMM certified, India’s first Zero Busbar TOPCon cell, all-black G12R module, 35 Mn litre rain water harvesting capacity, Zero Liquid Discharge, 30.1 MMT CO2e avoided emissions in Q1 FY27.

3. Key Risks

  • Margin sustainability. Operational EBITDA margin declined YOY and QOQ in Q1 FY27, with cost of materials, stock-in-trade purchases, employee costs, and other expenses all rising faster than revenue in several line items.
  • Order book execution. The order book declined from INR 150 Bn to INR 140 Bn, with new orders of INR 33 Bn not fully replacing sales of INR 23 Bn plus the opening backlog drawdown. Concentration in cells and modules (98% combined) leaves limited diversification.
  • Export market dependence. Export share fell to 12% of revenue in Q1 FY27 from 24% in Q3 FY26, exposing the business to domestic demand cycles.
  • Leverage and finance costs. Total debt to equity rose to 0.86 and finance costs increased 19.35% YOY, with net debt at INR 16,417 Mn versus negative net debt a year earlier.
  • Project execution. The Naidupeta cell plant, Neotrafo transformer plant, and BESS facility are all under construction or commissioning, with timelines subject to execution risk.
  • Policy and regulatory changes. The ALMM-II relaxation expires December 2026; countervailing duties on Malaysian solar glass extended to June 2031; PLI and incentive schemes are subject to government implementation.
  • Competition and technology. The disclaimer notes risks including performance of the Indian and international economies, competition in India and worldwide, ability to implement strategy, growth and expansion levels, technological implementation and advancements, changes in revenue, income or cash flows, withdrawal of governmental fiscal incentives, market preferences, and exposure to market risks.
  • JV partner and new business risk. The BESS JV with RCT India is at term sheet stage, with definitive agreements, regulatory approvals, and execution yet to be completed. The first phase is targeted for FY27-28.
  • Capital raise dilution. The proposed INR 5,000 Crores capital raise could dilute existing shareholders, though terms are not yet specified.

Broker Narrative

The narrative started with unambiguously bullish policy-driven demand expectations—ALMM mandates, import duties, PLI incentives, and RE growth—with no risks flagged. By the last report, those structural policy themes remained (ALMM, PLI, duties, 2030 target), but the picture had become more cautious: competition had compressed spreads, cell-to-module spreads had fallen sharply, and policy/execution risks (ALMM-I delays, duty reductions, DCR execution, slower capacity) were explicitly acknowledged. The call stayed Buy, underpinned by strong Q1FY27 results, a record order book, and DCR capacity being sold out.

Optimism that failed

  • The ALMM local-sourcing mandate was expected to secure domestic module/cell demand, but the last report flags delays in ALMM-I applicability, undermining that support.
  • The import-duty protection (40% modules/25% cells) was expected to shield pricing, but the last report flags the risk of reduced duties on Chinese imports, and the first report’s actual outcome was a -21.5% disappointment.
