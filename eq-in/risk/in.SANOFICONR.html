Sanofi Consumer Healthcare India Limited — Q2/H1 2026 Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Export softness in the quarter. While domestic sales grew 12% year-on-year in Q2 2026, export sales declined 9%, which the company attributes to a high base. This export drag limited overall quarterly revenue growth to 7% even as the domestic business expanded at a double-digit pace.

Cost inflation in the quarter. Cost of materials consumed rose to ₹475 million in Q2 2026 from ₹402 million in Q1 2026 and ₹554 million in Q2 2025. Other expenses also increased to ₹588 million from ₹511 million sequentially and ₹575 million a year earlier. Total expenses for the quarter were ₹1,501 million, higher than ₹1,441 million in the preceding quarter.

Working capital absorption. Inventories increased to ₹937 million as at June 30, 2026 from ₹619 million at December 31, 2025, and trade receivables rose to ₹498 million from ₹296 million over the same period. In the cash flow statement, inventories absorbed ₹318 million and trade receivables ₹205 million during H1 2026, contributing to a lower cash conversion relative to profit.

Comparability distortion from the 2024 recall. The company voluntarily recalled Combiflam Suspension, Allegra Suspension and Depura Kids in 2024. Combiflam Suspension was relaunched in the quarter ended June 2025, and Allegra Suspension and Depura Kids in the quarter ended September 2025. As a result, there were no sales of these products in the quarter ended March 31, 2025 and of Allegra Suspension and Depura Kids in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. The company states that results are not fully comparable with the corresponding prior-year periods because of the recall and subsequent relaunches.

New Labour Codes. The Government of India notified four New Labour Codes effective November 21, 2025, replacing 29 existing labour laws. The company recognised an incremental impact of ₹24 million as past service cost in the year ended December 31, 2025, increasing gratuity liability and compensated absences due to the change in the definition of wages. The company continues to monitor finalisation of Central and State Rules and will evaluate any further impact on employee benefit measurement.

Balance sheet movement in other financial liabilities. Other financial liabilities rose sharply to ₹1,772 million as at June 30, 2026 from ₹211 million at December 31, 2025, and other current liabilities increased to ₹169 million from ₹30 million. Current tax liabilities moved from nil to ₹68 million. Total current liabilities increased to ₹3,271 million from ₹1,284 million, while total equity declined to ₹3,479 million from ₹3,840 million.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Revenue growth. Revenue from operations for Q2 2026 was ₹2,357 million, a 7% year-on-year increase over ₹2,209 million in Q2 2025. Sequentially, revenue rose from ₹2,292 million in Q1 2026. For H1 2026, revenue was ₹4,649 million, up 18% from ₹3,935 million in H1 2025.

Domestic sales momentum. Domestic sales grew 12% year-on-year in Q2 2026 and 14% in H1 2026, aided by relaunches. The company’s press release highlights that targeted investments behind brands and go-to-market capabilities are translating into profitable growth and market share gains in most brands.

Export recovery on a half-year basis. Although export sales declined 9% in Q2 2026 on a high base, export sales grew 27% in H1 2026 on a low base.

Profitability improvement. Profit before tax for Q2 2026 was ₹919 million, up 16% year-on-year. For H1 2026, PBT was ₹1,826 million, up 25% from ₹1,457 million in H1 2025. Profit for the period was ₹688 million in Q2 2026 and ₹1,366 million in H1 2026, compared with ₹607 million and ₹1,107 million respectively in the prior-year periods. Basic and diluted EPS was ₹29.87 for Q2 2026 and ₹59.31 for H1 2026, against ₹26.36 and ₹48.07 a year earlier.

Product sales. Sales of products were ₹2,132 million in Q2 2026, ₹2,063 million in Q1 2026 and ₹2,023 million in Q2 2025. For H1 2026, product sales were ₹4,196 million versus ₹3,610 million in H1 2025.

Cash generation. Net cash inflow from operating activities was ₹1,096 million in H1 2026, compared with ₹179 million in H1 2025. Cash and cash equivalents rose to ₹4,902 million as at June 30, 2026 from ₹3,748 million at December 31, 2025. Interest income on fixed deposits contributed ₹109 million in H1 2026 versus ₹83 million a year earlier.

Portfolio and positioning. The company operates as a standalone consumer healthcare entity following its demerger from Sanofi India Limited on June 1, 2024, with brands spanning Allergy, Digestive Wellness, Pain Care and Multivitamins, including Allegra, DePURA, Avil and Combiflam.

3. Key Risks

Recall and relaunch execution risk. The 2024 voluntary recall of Combiflam Suspension, Allegra Suspension and Depura Kids and the subsequent staggered relaunches (Combiflam Suspension in Q2 2025; Allegra Suspension and Depura Kids in Q3 2025) mean reported comparatives are affected by product availability. Any further disruption to these products would affect domestic sales.

Export concentration and base effects. Export sales declined 9% in Q2 2026 on a high base while growing 27% in H1 2026 on a low base, indicating sensitivity to prior-period comparatives and potentially to a limited set of export products or markets.

Input cost and expense volatility. Cost of materials consumed and other expenses both increased sequentially and year-on-year in Q2 2026, which could pressure margins if the trend continues.

Regulatory and labour law uncertainty. The New Labour Codes effective November 21, 2025 require assessment of obligations based on actuarial valuation and best estimates. The company has stated it will continue to monitor finalisation of Central and State Rules and evaluate any impact on employee benefit measurement, with accounting treatment to be provided in the period such rules or clarifications are notified. This leaves scope for further charges.

Working capital and balance sheet risk. The build-up in inventories and trade receivables, alongside the sharp increase in other financial liabilities and other current liabilities, affects liquidity and leverage metrics. Total equity declined from ₹3,840 million to ₹3,479 million between December 31, 2025 and June 30, 2026.

Governance-related dissent. At the 3rd Annual General Meeting held on June 26, 2026, the resolution to appoint M/s. Price Waterhouse & Co Chartered Accountants LLP as Statutory Auditors received 91.2764% votes in favour and 8.7236% against. Public Institutions voted 57.2780% in favour and 42.7220% against. The resolution to appoint the same firm to fill a casual vacancy also saw public institutions vote 57.2780% in favour and 42.7220% against. The resolution to re-appoint Mr. Stanislas Camart as Director saw 99.8768% in favour and 0.1232% against. The dividend resolution of ₹75 per equity share for FY2025 saw 99.9999% in favour and 0.0001% against.

Single segment concentration. The company operates a single business segment, ‘Pharmaceutical Business’, with no subsidiary, associates or joint ventures as at June 30, 2026, concentrating exposure in one line of business.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Management commentary. Managing Director Himanshu Bakshi stated that the quarter’s performance reflects disciplined execution on all fronts, with targeted investments behind brands and go-to-market capabilities translating into profitable growth and market share gains in most brands, reinforcing confidence in the strategy.

Observed performance against that commentary. The reported numbers are consistent with profitable growth: revenue grew 7% year-on-year in Q2 2026 and 18% in H1 2026; PBT grew 16% in Q2 2026 and 25% in H1 2026; profit for the period grew to ₹688 million in Q2 2026 from ₹607 million and to ₹1,366 million in H1 2026 from ₹1,107 million. Domestic sales growth of 12% in Q2 2026 and 14% in H1 2026 supports the reference to brand investment and market share gains, though the company attributes domestic growth to being aided by relaunch. The reference to profitable growth is supported by the increase in PBT and profit, while the export decline of 9% in Q2 2026 indicates that growth was not uniform across all segments.

Guidance on labour codes. The company has not quantified future labour code impact beyond the ₹24 million past service cost recognised in FY2025, stating it will monitor finalisation of rules and evaluate impact, with appropriate accounting treatment in the period rules or clarifications are notified.

Dividend. The AGM approved a final dividend of ₹75 per equity share of ₹10 each for the financial year ended December 31, 2025. The cash flow statement for H1 2026 shows no dividend paid during the period, compared with ₹1,267 million paid in H1 2025.

Auditor transition. The financial results for the quarters ended March 31, 2026 and June 30, 2025 and the year-to-date results for January 1, 2025 to June 30, 2025 were reviewed by another firm of chartered accountants, which issued unmodified conclusions dated April 28, 2026 and August 4, 2025 respectively. The financial statements for the year ended December 31, 2025 were audited by another firm, which issued an unmodified opinion dated February 25, 2026. Price Waterhouse & Co Chartered Accountants LLP conducted the limited review of the Q2/H1 2026 results and issued its report dated July 28, 2026, with the conclusion not modified in respect of the prior-firm matters.
