Headwinds and Challenges

Regulatory and debarment actions. NHAI issued a letter on 11.09.2026 extending the debarment of Awadh Expressway Private Limited to PNC Infratech as its promoter for three years, prohibiting the company from participating in bids of MoRTH/NHAI and their executing agencies for that period. The company stated it is evaluating legal remedies and will disclose financial implications as clarity emerges.

Kanpur–Lucknow Expressway slippage. Heavy and incessant rains caused a slippage of approximately 300 m near km 64 (RHS) of the Kanpur–Lucknow Expressway Project. This triggered show cause notices from NHAI, proposed penalties of 2% of the Performance Security, potential debarment proceedings against specific technical staff for up to 3 years, a downgrade of the Concessionaire’s rating (Pavement Condition Index), and an interim order to remove the Project Manager. Management characterised the affected stretches as routine maintenance envisaged under the Concession Agreement, noted the affected length is less than 0.5% of the expressway, and said traffic is plying smoothly. On the call, management declined to speculate on whether NHAI would declare the company a non-performer or ban it from bidding, or on the duration of any such action, stating the matter was still under consideration and that the company was submitting its reply. Management also reserved its position on NHAI’s decision to open the highway without collecting user fee and the associated toll loss.

Subdued highway awarding and execution. NHAI’s project awarding remained subdued in Q1 FY2027, with only 107 km awarded, and execution moderated to approximately 640 km. This was attributed to a weak award pipeline and continued geopolitical tensions. The roads and highways sector experienced a subdued awarding environment in FY 2025-26, with NHAI project awards falling short of annual targets by over 30%, compounded by delays in land acquisition, project approvals, and declaring appointed dates.

Competitive and cost pressures. The company faces elevated competitive pressures from local, national, and international players driven by smaller project sizes and relaxed bidding norms, which could pressure contract pricing and margins. Macroeconomic and infrastructure sector inflationary pressures, such as rising input costs for steel, cement, bitumen, fuel, and other critical components, pose risks to cost structure and margins. Geographic concentration in North India, particularly Uttar Pradesh, exposes the company to regional regulatory and political risks.

Working capital and receivables. Net working capital days stood at 110 as of 30 June 2026. Around INR 741 crore excluding GST remained to be realised from the government for JJM projects, attributed to paucity of funds at the state level and delays in central fund release. For the Andhra Pradesh irrigation project, INR 416 crore excluding GST was invoiced, INR 263 crore received, leaving INR 153 crore receivable; a budget of INR 94 crore was released by the state government, expected within 1–2 weeks, which would reduce receivables to around INR 60 crore.

Mining execution issues. The coal mining project faced teething issues: the project proponent did not provide fully encumbrance-free and encroachment-free land, land parcels were discontinuous, and there was local resistance. Two new surface miners were recently deployed and progress is being expedited.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

NHAI pipeline. NHAI launched 54 highway and expressway projects for an estimated aggregate value of around INR 1.8 lakh crore for bidding over the coming 2–3 months, comprising 26 EPC, 21 HAM, and 7 DBOT-Toll projects across states. A cumulative 10,389 km of National High-Speed Corridors have been launched, with 4,809 km completed and 5,580 km under bidding and implementation.

Multi-sector opportunities. Government focus spans highways, railways, metro rail, freight corridors, power transmission, renewable energy and storage, mining, airports, water resources including drinking water supply, and urban development. Railways offer a robust pipeline, with proposed expansion of PPP-led rail projects including HAM-based development. Power transmission is expected to see capex of INR 6.0 lakh crore over the next five financial years, driven by evacuation needs from over 900 GW of non-fossil fuel capacity by FY2035-36. Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 reached 82% of rural households with functional drinking water tap connections as of July 2026, up from 17% in 2019, with implementation extended to December 2028.

Order book and new wins. The unexecuted order book stands at over INR 19,100 crore, including two new HAM projects secured in April 2026, one major bridge project and one flyover project in May 2026, and one airport project in July 2026. Highway contracts contribute 64%, water/canal/railway/airport around 21%, and coal mining about 15%. In FY27 to date, the company secured 5 new orders worth INR 4,259 crore, including 2 HAM projects from NHAI and 3 EPC projects from AAI and other authorities. Specific awards include: a Lucknow Development Authority 4-lane flyover EPC project valued at INR 194 crore (May 2026, 24 months); a PNC Infratech JV 4-lane major bridge over the Ganga connecting Trans Ganga City to Kanpur City, EPC, valued at INR 559.5 crore (May 2026, 36 months, 50:50 JV); and a Pantnagar Airport EPC project from AAI valued at INR 302 crore (July 2026, 24 months).

Arbitration recoveries. In May 2026, the company received INR 234.99 crore from NHAI under a one-time settlement agreement regarding the Agra Bypass EPC Project Arbitration Award under Vivad-Se-Vishwas Scheme III. On 31 July 2026, an arbitration award of INR 244 crore was published in favour of the company for the Sonauli to Gorakhpur section of NH 29E executed for UP PWD.

Project completions and financial closures. A completion certificate was received in June 2026 for HAM project Prayagraj Kaushambi Package 3 with effect from 20 June 2026. Concession agreements were signed with NHAI on 16 July 2026 by two new SPVs — Barabanki Mustafabad Highway Private Limited and Mustafabad Biswariya Highway Private Limited — for two new HAM projects.

Diversification. The company is pursuing solar, BESS, and mining. For the solar project, the EPC component is around INR 2,000 crore, with over 300 acres of land finalised in Madhya Pradesh and lease deeds executed; remaining land acquisition is expected within 4–5 months. Power sale agreements are being pursued with two states, expected between NHPC and buyers, followed by PPAs. Revenue from solar is expected in Q4 FY27, with more than INR 1,000 crore in FY28 and the remainder in FY29. Total estimated equity requirement for the solar project is INR 400 crore. The coal mining project is EPC with no equity requirement, targeting INR 500 crore revenue in FY27 and INR 500 crore in FY28, with total capex of around INR 350 crore over the initial couple of years.

Balance sheet strength. Standalone net worth as of 30 June 2026 was INR 6,084 crore, with standalone bank/FI debt of INR 428 crore (excluding ICD), translating to debt-to-equity of 0.07x excluding ICD. Total cash and bank balance including current investments was INR 1,046 crore, giving a net cash surplus of INR 133 crore. Consolidated net worth was INR 7,147 crore, total debt INR 5,448 crore, debt-to-equity 0.76x, and cash and bank balance including current investments INR 2,870 crore. The company completed divestment of 12 road assets (11 HAM national highway projects and 1 BOT-Toll state highway project) to Vertis Infrastructure Trust.

Key Risks

  • Debarment from MoRTH/NHAI and their executing agencies for three years, extended from Awadh Expressway Private Limited to the company as promoter, with legal remedies under evaluation.
  • Potential non-performer declaration or bidding ban arising from the Kanpur–Lucknow Expressway matter, including possible debarment of specific technical staff for up to 3 years, downgrade of the Concessionaire’s Pavement Condition Index rating, and interim removal of the Project Manager.
  • Toll loss from NHAI’s decision to open the highway without collecting user fee, on which management reserved its position.
  • Competitive pressure on contract pricing and margins from smaller project sizes and relaxed bidding norms.
  • Geographic concentration in North India, particularly Uttar Pradesh.
  • Input cost inflation for steel, cement, bitumen, fuel, and other critical components.
  • Delays in land acquisition, project approvals, and appointed dates affecting execution schedules and revenues.
  • Working capital risk from delayed government receivables, including INR 741 crore excluding GST pending from JJM and INR 153 crore receivable from the Andhra Pradesh irrigation project.
  • Mining project execution risk from encumbrance and encroachment issues and local resistance.
  • General risks cited: fluctuations in earnings, ability to manage growth, domestic and international competition, economic growth in India and abroad, ability to attract and retain skilled professionals, time and cost overruns, management of international operations, government policies and regulations, and interest and fiscal costs.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Revenue guidance. Management maintained FY27 revenue guidance of INR 6,000 crore and FY28 guidance of INR 7,500 crore, with no change contemplated as of the Q1 FY27 call. Management also targets revenue growth of around 30% in FY 2026-27 and 25% in FY 2027-28. Observed Q1 FY27 standalone revenue was INR 1,518 crore (up 34% from INR 1,136 crore in Q1 FY26) and consolidated revenue was INR 1,688 crore (up 19% from INR 1,423 crore). Standalone Q1 FY27 revenue included an arbitration award amount of INR 217 crore received from NHAI for the Agra Bypass EPC Project.

Order inflow guidance. Management expected to secure total new business worth INR 12,000–15,000 crore in FY27. Observed: 5 new orders worth INR 4,259 crore secured in FY27 to date, and 24 bids submitted (16 EPC and 8 HAM) valued at INR 32,000 crore under evaluation, plus around 78 identified projects worth INR 1.7 lakh crore to be bid over the next 2–3 months.

Margin guidance. Management referenced a margin guidance of around 12% from the prior call. Observed standalone EBITDA margin in Q1 FY27 was 24.7% (including the INR 217 crore arbitration award) and consolidated EBITDA margin was 31%. Standalone EBITDA was INR 375 crore (up 167% from INR 141 crore) and consolidated EBITDA was INR 524 crore (up 42% from INR 367 crore). Standalone PAT was INR 271 crore (up 235% from INR 81 crore) and consolidated PAT was INR 332 crore; consolidated Q1 FY26 PAT of INR 431 crore included a gain of INR 278.0 crore net of tax on monetisation of 10 HAM assets.

Equity investment guidance. Total equity investment requirement for HAM projects is INR 1,623 crore excluding the 2 newly awarded HAM projects. Till June 2026, INR 1,187 crore was infused, with remaining INR 436 crore to be invested over the next 2 years — INR 226 crore intended in FY27 and the balance in FY28. Management stated internal accruals over the next 2–3 years should be adequate to meet this requirement. For the two new HAM projects (combined BPC INR 3,483 crore), equity is estimated at around INR 400 crore, with marginal infusion in FY27 and the majority in FY28 and FY29.

Segment guidance. For JJM, management expects INR 700–800 crore revenue in FY27 and around INR 1,000 crore in FY28, with residual amounts in FY29; the whole project is to be completed before end of calendar year 2028. For the AP irrigation project, FY27 target is INR 150 crore, with completion planned in FY28 subject to timely payment from the Government of Andhra Pradesh. For coal mining, INR 500 crore in FY27 and INR 500 crore in FY28. For solar, difficult to achieve significant revenue in FY27, with more than INR 1,000 crore in FY28 and the remainder in FY29.

Other guidance. Interest cost quarterly run rate expected in the range of INR 20 crore. Tax rate expected in the range of 25% to 27%. Capex targeted at INR 150 crore for FY27 (INR 70 crore spent in Q1), with similar INR 150 crore expected in FY28. Standalone debt expected to come down from INR 900 crore. Management noted present capex will enable achievement of turnover of INR 8,000–10,000 crore, while stating it does not undertake to make announcements if forward-looking statements become materially incorrect or to update them.

Order book reconciliation. Management clarified that the previously mentioned INR 22,000 crore order book included the two new HAM projects secured in Q1; the current unexecuted order book of over INR 19,100 crore includes the two Barabanki-Mustafabad HAM projects, Pantnagar Airport, the bridge over the Ganga, and the LDA flyover. A separate document cites an order book of INR 15,670 crore across 27 projects as of 30 June 2026.

FY 2025-26 reported figures. Standalone revenue was INR 4,633 crore, standalone EBITDA INR 583 crore with a 12.58% margin, and standalone PAT INR 344 crore; consolidated revenue was INR 5,368 crore, consolidated EBITDA INR 1,137 crore, and consolidated PAT INR 832 crore. The Board recommended a final dividend of INR 0.60 per equity share (30%) for FY ended 31 March 2026.

Broker Narrative

The initial narrative centered on strong order book visibility, JJM expansion, asset monetization, and HAM project execution clearance at attractive valuations, but over time these specific growth drivers faded while execution challenges, working capital build-up, and commodity cost pressures became the dominant themes. The last report shifted toward macro drivers like infrastructure capex, overseas opportunities, and AI in EPC, while the original fundamental catalysts were dropped. Persistent working capital and execution concerns from the first report carried through to the last, now compounded by West Asia-related cost pressures on margins.

Fears that came true

  • Higher Material Costs materialized as the last report explicitly flags potential FY27 margin pressure from higher commodity, freight, and insurance costs due to West Asia disruptions, correlating with persistent DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes across 30+ reports.
  • Working Capital Stretch persisted and worsened, as the last report continues to cite working-capital build-up from execution challenges, directly tracking the NWC expansion to 103 days flagged in Q4FY23 and correlating with overwhelming negative actual returns.
  • Order Inflow Risk was confirmed by the near-universal DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes across the full 43-report history, indicating order inflow consistently fell short of broker estimates.
  • Project Bidding Delays contributed to sustained underperformance, as the delayed NHAI bidding pipeline failed to compensate for the drag, evidenced by continuous DISAPPOINTMENT results from 2023 through 2026.
  • Loss of Early Completion Bonus reduced earnings upside, contributing to the persistent gap between predicted and actual returns throughout the entire history.
  • Order Inflow Risk and Project Bidding Delays combined to suppress revenue growth well below the guided 15% for FY24, evidenced by the stock’s persistent negative actual returns across virtually all broker calls.
  • Execution challenges flagged in the last report (multiple authorities, utility shifting, clearances, design changes) confirm that the loss of early completion bonuses and project delays from the first report were not transient but structural, perpetuating working-capital strain and margin pressure.

Optimism that failed

  • Robust Order Book Visibility of ₹156.8 billion (2.2x TTM revenue) failed to translate into positive returns, as the stock generated overwhelmingly negative actual returns across nearly all reports despite this stated strength.
  • Strong Revenue & Earnings CAGR guidance of 11.7%/11.2% over FY23–25E failed to materialize, as actual outcomes consistently disappointed with negative returns on virtually every call.
  • JJM Traction was expected to deliver ₹25 billion in FY24 and ~₹40 billion in FY25 revenues but disappeared entirely from the last report, suggesting the non-road expansion did not take hold.
  • Asset Monetization Progress on 12 projects was expected to conclude by end of FY24 but was not mentioned in the last report, indicating the program failed to deliver expected cash flow relief.
  • Execution Clearance for HAM Projects was achieved for all 7 FY22 projects but the last report still cites ongoing execution delays and working-capital build-up, suggesting these projects did not unlock the smooth revenue execution anticipated.
  • Attractive Core Valuation at 8.0x FY24E / 7.0x FY25E EPS failed as a thesis, with the stock persistently underperforming despite low multiples, resulting in DISAPPOINTMENT across multiple reports over three years.
  • Large Bidding Pipeline of >100 projects worth ₹1 trillion (75% HAM / 25% EPC) failed to accelerate order inflow as expected, evidenced by the ongoing Order Inflow Risk and persistent DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes through 2026.
