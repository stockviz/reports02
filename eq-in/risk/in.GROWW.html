Groww (Billionbrains Garage Ventures Limited) — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Derivatives softness and volatility correlation: Active derivatives customers showed sequential softness. Management attributed this to market volatility (including geopolitical events such as the Iran war) rather than regulation, noting that F&O volumes dipped in late June and early July before recovering when volatility returned. Q4 FY26 was described as an exception, with Q1 FY27 showing improvement over Q3 FY26.
  • Cash segment order decline: Cash segment orders declined approximately 9% sequentially, which management linked primarily to a drop in gold and silver ETF volumes as those assets underperformed, rather than to core equity activity.
  • MTF momentum flattening: After a strong Q1, MTF trends appeared flattish in early Q2. Management attributed this to market direction (bullish at quarter-end versus volatile subsequently) rather than a change in customer behaviour, and noted MTF active users remained around 0.13 million even as assets grew.
  • Rising customer acquisition cost: CAC per new NTU rose to approximately INR 1,900 from around INR 1,400 in Q1 and INR 1,000 in Q4, driven by a longer IPL window (nearly two months versus one week in Q4).
  • Fisdom integration still in gestation: Management stated that no significant revenue improvement has yet been seen from Fisdom, which remains in a gestation stage.
  • Employee cost increase: Employee benefits expense rose, primarily due to the April appraisal cycle rather than significant headcount additions. ESOPs accounted for roughly 10% of employee cost in the quarter.
  • Regulatory uncertainty: A news article suggested margin requirements on expiry day could increase. Management stated they had not heard anything from SEBI on this and could not comment on news. On the November 2024 additional 2% ELM margin requirement, management said impact could not be attributed to that single change because five regulatory changes occurred in parallel, and that the regulation does not impact Groww’s retail business.
  • DAU/MAU stagnation: MAU and DAU numbers appeared fairly stagnant, with DAUs slightly lower than Q4 FY26, which management again attributed to Q4 being an anomaly due to higher volatility.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • 10-year milestone and strategic evolution: Groww completed 10 years in May 2026. The company is transitioning from an execution and user-experience platform toward becoming a wealth management company, building research-backed products such as MF Prime and W that assist with investment selection, rebalancing, and tax considerations.
  • Commodities traction: Commodities achieved more than 28% retail market share in notional ADTO.
  • MTF growth: MTF continued to grow better than expected, with roughly INR 600–700 crores added to the book quarterly. Management sees significant adoption pending and expects penetration and ticket sizes to expand further.
  • Cash yield improvement: Cash yields improved approximately 5% year-on-year, with management expecting further increases of one to two percentage points per quarter as MTF penetration rises.
  • LAS growth: LAS constitutes 34% of disbursements and is showing good growth, described as relevant for investors.
  • AMC partnership progress: The State Street Global Advisors investment in Groww AMC received CCI approval on 25 March 2026 and SEBI approval on 1 June 2026, with consummation pending remaining closing conditions.
  • New products: Bonds launched last quarter and are scaling well. A new product is expected this quarter. US stocks via GIFT City are in testing, with launch expected soon, starting with US stocks and potentially expanding to other geographies.
  • AI leverage: AI is being applied across product development (faster, higher-quality building) and customer experience (faster query resolution, richer context for support executives, handling edge-case use cases beyond the 80% covered by standard UX).
  • Affluent customer retention: Retention among affluent customers is described as almost 100%, attributed to AUM stickiness and long-standing customer relationships.
  • Improving customer quality: FY26 customer acquisition quality was better than prior years, with first-year ARPU for FY26-acquired customers higher than for previous cohorts.
  • Float income: Float income rose substantially, aided by higher interest rates and yields, though average balances were not significantly higher than the prior quarter.
  • Cost to operate: Cost to operate declined slightly versus the prior quarter, which had one-time risk costs; management expects this cost to continue with slight inflationary increases.
  • IPO proceeds deployment: Of INR 1,015.98 crores net IPO proceeds, INR 862.29 crores was utilised as of 30 June 2026, with INR 153.69 crores unutilised. No deviation or variation in use of funds was reported.

3. Key Risks

  • Market volatility sensitivity: MTF, derivatives, and cash segment volumes are highly correlated with market volatility and direction, creating quarter-to-quarter variability.
  • Regulatory risk: Potential changes to expiry-day margin requirements and the cumulative effect of prior regulatory changes (such as the November 2024 ELM margin increase) create uncertainty, though management stated the regulation does not impact Groww’s retail business.
  • Execution risk across multiple products: The company is simultaneously building commodities, wealth management, AMC, US stocks, and other products. Management addressed this by noting products are at different life stages and are developed through a pod structure using existing teams, with no expectation of significant headcount increases.
  • Fisdom integration risk: Revenue improvement from Fisdom has not yet materialised, and the business remains in gestation.
  • Customer acquisition cost volatility: Marketing spend, particularly IPL-related, can significantly elevate CAC without directly proportional acquisition, as seen in Q1 FY27.
  • Data privacy considerations: Management noted that user privacy is very important and that any use of customer data for personalisation must be handled carefully given customer sensitivities.
  • No identified earnings risks: When asked about risks to the earnings profile over the next couple of years, management stated nothing was top of mind.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Employee costs: Management attributed the increase to the April appraisal cycle, not headcount growth, and guided that headcount will not increase significantly in wealth or Groww. Observed: employee benefits expense rose, with ESOPs at roughly 10% of employee cost.
  • MTF: Management guided that growth can continue as it has for the past year or two, with penetration and ticket sizes expanding. Observed: MTF grew better than expected, with INR 600–700 crores added quarterly, though early Q2 trends appeared flattish due to market conditions.
  • Cash yields: Management guided to continued increases of one to two percentage points per quarter. Observed: cash yields improved approximately 5% year-on-year.
  • Derivatives: Management guided that the segment is stabilising and Q4 FY26 was an exception. Observed: sequential softness in active derivatives customers, with improvement over Q3 FY26.
  • Cost to operate: Management guided that costs will continue as is with slight inflationary increases. Observed: cost to operate declined slightly versus the prior quarter, which had one-time risk costs.
  • Fisdom: Management guided that revenue improvement has not yet been seen and the business is in gestation. Observed: no significant revenue improvement, with Fisdom and AMC together contributing less than 2% of other income.
  • New products (US stocks, Prime, W): Management guided that it is too early to comment on traction and that numbers will be shared in coming quarters. Observed: no specific customer or revenue figures were disclosed.
  • Affluent retention: Management guided to very high retention. Observed: retention described as almost 100%.
  • Customer quality: Management guided that FY26 acquisition quality was better. Observed: first-year ARPU for FY26-acquired customers was higher than for previous cohorts.
  • IPO proceeds: Management reported no deviation or variation in use of funds. Observed: INR 862.29 crores utilised, INR 153.69 crores unutilised as of 30 June 2026.

Broker Narrative

The broker stayed persistently bullish, repeatedly emphasizing market-share leadership, MTF expansion, and product diversification as the core growth story. The risk narrative, however, shifted from macro/regulatory concerns (F&O tightening, credit regulation, market volatility) to company-specific execution issues such as a CAC surge from IPL spending, operating losses at AMC/Fisdom, and sequential declines in derivatives, cash/ETF, and partner credit activity. Earlier optimism about cheap organic acquisition and wealth-management monetization was tempered, while newer growth drivers—commodities, US stocks, and institutional backing for the AMC—were added to support the Buy case.

Fears that came true

  • The flagged vulnerability in derivatives activity materialized as derivatives revenue fell 4% QoQ to ~INR 8.1b, a disappointment consistent with the -8.8% actual return after the last report.
  • The credit-distribution risk materialized as partner institution disbursements declined sequentially to INR 3.7b, undermining earlier assumptions about credit-led growth.
  • The market-volatility/volume risk materialized as cash orders fell ~9% sequentially due to weaker demand and risk-control tightening on MTF and intraday limits.

Optimism that failed

  • The low-CAC/organic acquisition advantage failed to sustain as customer acquisition cost spiked to ~INR 1,900 in 1QFY27 due to IPL marketing spend, versus the earlier $6–10 range.
  • The Fisdom wealth-management monetization optimism did not materialize as Fisdom posted an operating loss of INR 109m in 1QFY27 while still in its gestation phase.
