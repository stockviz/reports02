Shilchar Technologies (SHILCTECH) — Q1FY27 Update

1. Headwinds and Challenges

West Asia crisis and export disruption. The quarter was shaped largely by the continuing effects of the crisis in West Asia, which affected Middle East exports directly and, through commodity prices, the domestic business as well. Management stated that recovery in exports has taken longer than originally expected at the time of the Q4 call.

Elevated shipping costs. Container costs rose between three and five times for certain geographies during Q1 compared to pre-crisis levels. This materially increased the landed cost for customers and led to slower pickup in export dispatches. The increase was not limited to the Middle East — shipping costs to North America also rose significantly on account of ongoing geopolitical uncertainty. Management emphasized this is a cost and logistics issue, not a demand issue, and that most export terms are ex-works, so the higher freight does not directly reduce Shilchar’s margin but does raise procurement cost for customers, causing order deferment. Customers are lifting the bare minimum volume they need rather than stocking.

Domestic commodity pass-through delays. Passing on the sudden escalation in commodity prices for existing orders arising from the West Asia crisis took longer than anticipated. Negotiations with customers extended through much of the quarter, resulting in slower Q1 dispatches. The situation was more severe in April and May and eased off in recent months.

Margin compression. Margins for the quarter reflected a lower export mix and only partial pass-through of higher raw material prices. Management stated that for orders on hand to be executed in Q4 and Q1, roughly 50–60% of the price rise could be passed on to customers.

Underutilization. Q1 capacity utilization was only around 60–65%, against a plan to run close to 100% for FY27.

Annual report versus actual outcome. Management noted the annual report was prepared in early June and circulated in July; at that time, announcements by concerned parties and countries suggested the war was ending and MOUs were being signed, which informed the more positive tone. That did not materialize.

New 220 KV segment margin profile. For the new higher-class transformers, management stated that initially, being new in the market, margins will be lower, mainly to create references and penetrate the market.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong order book. Order book stood at close to ₹500 crores as of the call, over and above shipments already made, split roughly 30% export and 70% domestic. These orders are mostly for Q2, Q3, and some for Q4. Management stated the company is fully booked for Q2 and expects more orders for Q3 and Q4.

Better Q2 momentum. Management expects overall business momentum to be notably better in Q2 compared to Q1, with better visibility on the domestic side supporting the top line. Orders at current raw material prices are described as good orders with reasonable margins, and management expects higher EBITDA than Q1.

Capacity expansion (Phase-3). The Gavasad Expansion #3, adding about 6,500 MVA, remains on track for commissioning in April 2027. Civil foundation work is complete, PEB erection and utility infrastructure work are progressing, and all major production equipment has been ordered. Management indicated the project may complete slightly earlier than projected. The new facility is expected to drive the next leg of growth from FY28 onwards, taking annual capacity to 14,000 MVA.

Full utilization of existing capacity. Management expects to operate the existing 7,500 MVA capacity at almost full utilization in FY27.

Demand environment. Underlying customer demand in both Middle East and North America remains firm. Demand drivers cited include growing global electricity demand, a global transformer supply shortage creating export opportunities, grid infrastructure investment in the US and EU, private sector capex in traditional and new-age sectors (including data centres and captive green energy), energy transition to renewables, and investments in power generation.

Export recovery potential. Management expressed confidence that export revenue will not be permanently lost, citing competitiveness, quality, service, and shorter lead times, and noted customers are not buying from alternative sources but deferring purchases. If the Middle East situation improves and shipping costs fall, the company expects to return to earlier export levels.

Further expansion plans. The company purchased an additional ~4.5 acres of land adjacent to its existing land (common wall) and is planning further expansion there, without disclosing product type, KV class, or capacity.

Domestic pricing stable. Management stated there is no pressure on domestic transformer prices or margins; the domestic market is described as normal relative to prior periods.

3. Key Risks

  • Prolonged West Asia crisis and elevated shipping costs, which could continue to affect export dispatches and the geographic mix.
  • Geographic mix shift toward domestic, which carries lower margins than exports. Management estimated the margin difference between domestic and international clientele at about 10%. If the company relies more on the local market, management indicated there could be a slight dip in profitability versus prior years.
  • Commodity price volatility and the timing of pass-through to customers.
  • Execution and approval timelines for the new 220 KV class products, including customer audits, approval processes, and type testing, which take time; larger-capacity transformer orders are expected to come later. Prototyping for bigger transformers is expected to take around three to four months after production starts.
  • Order deferrals — management stated orders are not being cancelled, only pushed out, and that cancellation does not exist for custom-based products; delivery delays remain a risk to timing.
  • Dependence on the domestic market to compensate for export weakness, with the company concentrating on local customers to offset the export impact.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Revenue ambition. Management maintained its FY27 revenue ambition (referenced in the call as the ₹800 crores target) and stated it does not anticipate problems achieving it, while noting the geographic mix may change if the West Asia situation persists. The investor presentation cites an order pipeline for FY27 of ~₹800 Cr. Observed Q1FY27 revenue from operations was ₹134.60 crores (₹13,460.71 Lakhs per the board approval), with PAT of ₹20.86 crores (₹2,086.48 Lakhs).

Capacity utilization. Management guided to operating existing 7,500 MVA capacity at almost full utilization for FY27. Observed Q1 utilization was only around 60–65%.

Margins. Management stated that if the situation normalizes and exports return as in previous years, it is confident of maintaining the same profitability as prior years; if the company relies more on the local market, there could be a slight dip. Observed Q1FY27 EBITDA margin was 16.4% (EBITDA excluding OI & EI of ₹22.06 crores), versus 33.0% in Q1FY26 and 21.0% in Q4FY26. Management expects Q2 margins to be higher than Q1.

Export recovery timing. Management had expected recovery sooner at the time of the Q4 call; observed recovery took longer due to persistent shipping cost increases through Q1.

Expansion timeline. Management guided to commissioning of the 6,500 MVA Phase-3 expansion in April 2027, with civil foundation complete, PEB and utilities in progress, and all equipment ordered; it also indicated the project may complete slightly earlier. No delay is anticipated.

Revenue shortfall quantification. Management indicated the company could have done around ₹30–35 crores more revenue in Q1 absent the crisis, while declining to characterize it as a revenue loss.
