Hindustan Zinc Limited — Corporate Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Director re-appointment drew significant institutional dissent. At the 60th AGM held on June 29, 2026, Resolution 4 (re-appointment of Mr. Navin Agarwal as a director retiring by rotation) saw 65,012,179 votes cast against within the Public-Institutions category, representing 23.9741% of institutional votes polled. Total votes against the resolution stood at 65,029,284, or 1.6191% of all votes polled — the highest opposition of any resolution tabled.
  • Appointment of an independent director also faced notable institutional opposition. Resolution 7 (appointment of Dr. Aruna Sharma as an Independent Director) recorded 4,694,709 votes against from Public-Institutions, equal to 1.7312% of institutional votes polled, and 4,714,382 votes against overall (0.1174% of total votes polled).
  • Auditor appointment and cost auditor remuneration attracted smaller but non-trivial dissent. Resolution 5 (appointment of M/s MSKA & Associates LLP as Statutory Auditors) recorded 199,826 institutional votes against (0.0737% of institutional votes polled), while Resolution 6 (ratification of Cost Auditors’ remuneration for FY ending March 31, 2027) recorded 16,853 votes against overall.
  • Low physical shareholder participation. No shareholders were present in person or by proxy (0 promoters/promoter group; 0 public). Only 2 promoter/promoter group shareholders and 76 public shareholders attended through video conferencing, against 933,664 shareholders on the record date of June 22, 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • All eight resolutions passed with the requisite majority. The company confirmed that every resolution set out in the notice convening the 60th AGM was duly approved by members.
  • Strong promoter support. Promoter and Promoter Group holding of 2,565,271,353 shares voted 100% in favour on every resolution, providing a stable base of approval.
  • Broad-based approval for financial statements and dividend. Resolution 1 (adoption of Audited Standalone Financial Statements for FY ended March 31, 2026) passed with 4,014,255,559 votes in favour (99.9997% of votes polled); Resolution 2 (Audited Consolidated Financial Statements) passed with 4,014,255,399 votes in favour (99.9997%); Resolution 3 (confirmation of interim dividend for FY ended March 31, 2026) passed with 4,016,491,481 votes in favour (99.9995%).
  • Leadership continuity endorsed. Resolution 8 (re-appointment of Mr. Arun Misra as Whole-time Director designated as Chief Executive Officer with effect from June 01) was tabled as a Special Resolution, with promoter votes recorded 100% in favour.
  • Board refreshment. Dr. Yogesh Attray was appointed as a Non-Executive Independent Director effective August 14, 2026, for a three-year term or until further orders by the Ministry of Mines, whichever is earlier, following Ministry of Mines order no. Met3-10/2/2020Metal III dated August 14, 2026. The Board approved the appointment via circulation on August 15, 2026, at 02:40 PM IST.
  • Governance and audit framework renewed. Shareholders appointed M/s MSKA & Associates LLP as Statutory Auditors and ratified Cost Auditors’ remuneration for the financial year ending March 31, 2027.

3. Key Risks

  • Concentration of promoter voting power. Promoter and Promoter Group held 2,565,271,353 shares and voted 100% in favour on all resolutions, while public institutional and non-institutional participation was comparatively fragmented. This concentration means outcomes on ordinary and special resolutions are heavily influenced by the promoter bloc.
  • Institutional dissent on director-related resolutions. The 23.9741% institutional votes against Resolution 4 and 1.7312% against Resolution 7 indicate governance-related disagreement among institutional investors on board composition matters.
  • Government-linked director appointment terms. Dr. Yogesh Attray’s appointment as a non-official part-time Director is for three years “w.e.f. date of notification or until further orders, whichever is earlier,” meaning the tenure is subject to Ministry of Mines discretion rather than a fixed term.
  • Low retail and physical participation. With zero shareholders present in person or by proxy and only 76 public shareholders attending via video conferencing out of 933,664 shareholders on record, engagement from the broader shareholder base is limited.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Dividend confirmation. Resolution 3 sought confirmation of the interim dividend for the financial year ended March 31, 2026. Shareholders approved it with 4,016,491,481 votes in favour (99.9995% of votes polled), confirming that the interim dividend declared during FY2025-26 was ratified at the AGM.
  • Financial statements adoption. The Audited Standalone and Consolidated Financial Statements for the financial year ended March 31, 2026, together with the reports of the Board of Directors and Auditors, were adopted with 99.9997% approval in each case, indicating no shareholder challenge to the reported financial performance for FY2025-26.
  • CEO re-appointment. The re-appointment of Mr. Arun Misra as Whole-time Director designated as Chief Executive Officer with effect from June 01 was placed before shareholders as a Special Resolution, indicating management’s intent to maintain leadership continuity into the new term.
  • Auditor transition. The appointment of M/s MSKA & Associates LLP as Statutory Auditors was approved, marking a change in the company’s statutory audit arrangement.
  • AGM conduct. The 60th AGM commenced at 11:50 AM IST and concluded at 01:54 PM IST on June 29, 2026, including the time allowed for e-voting. The Scrutinizer’s report was issued to the company on June 30, 2026, by Manoj Maheshwari of V. M. & Associates, appointed at the Board meeting on April 24, 2026.

Broker Narrative

From June 2023 to July 2026 the broker stayed broadly Neutral, and valuation caution was the recurring theme—the first report saw the stock fully valued at 6.1x FY24E EV/EBITDA and the last at 6.9x FY28E EV/EBITDA. The early balance-sheet fears (net debt, dividend stress on liquidity) did not play out; by 2026 HZL was net-cash with strong free cash flow. The narrative shifted to operational/input-cost risks (coal availability, hedge losses, smelter outages) and regulatory delays, while retaining optimism on cost leadership, silver earnings, and the long-term capacity expansion pipeline.

Fears that came true

  • The valuation risk flagged at 6.1x EV/EBITDA persisted—the last report still saw the stock fully priced at 6.9x EV/EBITDA, and later Neutral calls produced negative actual returns (-14.0% in Jan-2026 and -3.5% in Apr-2026).

Optimism that failed

  • Forward integration into the 500kt fertilizer plant was delayed—the DAP plant commissioning slipped to 1QFY28 pending regulatory clearances.
  • The early mine-ramp optimism (1.2mt near-term, 1.35mt by FY25) did not create near-term volume headroom—by 2026 volume growth was still capped until new expansion projects commissioned.
