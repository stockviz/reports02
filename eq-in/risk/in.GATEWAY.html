GATEWAY — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

West Asia conflict and trade disruption. Management attributed the stagnation in ICD volumes year-on-year to the war situation that began around April 2026. Samvid Gupta noted the market itself had de-grown in Gateway’s operating regions, with a ceasefire followed by renewed attacks creating continuing uncertainty. Imports dried up in April and May, with some improvement from June–July onwards.

Volume and margin pressure in the core business. Q1 FY27 throughput was 1,83,867 TEUs versus 1,87,550 TEUs in Q1 FY26, a decline of 1.96%. Rail vertical volumes fell 0.69% and CFS volumes fell 3.22%. Revenue per TEU declined to ₹20,281 from ₹20,739 a year earlier, and EBITDA per TEU fell to ₹5,012 from ₹5,219. Management explained the rail EBITDA decline as a function of volume mix (lower imports, higher exports), port imbalance at Mundra and Pipavav, lower double stacking, higher underframe and higher empty running. Double stacking dipped to 39% from 40–42% in prior quarters.

Cost inflation. Fuel prices rose, and minimum wage revisions were significant — Haryana alone saw a 35% increase in minimum wages. Fuel cost pass-through was largely achieved, but wage pass-through faced a time lag, with the impact expected to be more visible in Q2.

Weather and port congestion. July volumes were impacted by bad weather in Mumbai, with restrictions on double stacking and port operations congested or restricted on multiple days. All CTOs faced these conditions during July.

CFS business weakness. The CFS business was described as not benefiting from the Western DFC and showing weak numbers. A sale exploration about 1.5 years ago did not yield the right valuation, and the company is not actively looking to sell, having reached a net debt-zero position earlier in the year after paying a special dividend.

Tax rate transition. The company transited to the new tax regime under Section 115BAA effective April 1, 2026, with the current year tax rate at 25.17% versus an effective rate of 9.57% in the previous year due to MAT credit availment. Reported PAT declined 17.55% year-on-year to ₹51.3 crores, though cash tax outgo rose only marginally — 18.88% versus 17.47%.

Regulatory and legal matters. Demand orders and notices of ₹18,409.94 lakhs regarding SEIS benefits; income tax disallowances and demands of ₹2,171.65 lakhs (AY 2011-12 to 2014-15) and ₹202.50 lakhs (AY 2020-21) for GDKL; land license fee demands from Northern Railways totaling ₹6,162.30 lakhs; regulatory proceedings and provisional attachment of ₹866.25 lakhs under the Benami Property Transactions Act; and GST demands and show-cause notices for Snowman totaling ₹1,903.95 lakhs.

Jaipur ICD delay. The Jaipur matter remains in litigation, with the last hearing adjourned and final arguments listed for September. No operational update was provided.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Western DFC completion. The 1,506 km WDFC is 100% complete, and Gateway was the first CTO to operate a double stack train on the complete corridor. The final connection to JNPT is complete. Management expects a gradual shift of cargo toward JNPT, though shipping line decisions and port congestion dynamics mean it is “wait and watch” for a couple more months. Rail coefficient at ports is currently low at 14–15%, and ports are in touch with shipping lines on competitive THC charges.

Ankleshwar MMLP. Received customs permission to handle EXIM volumes, with operations expected to commence by September 2026. The existing ICD at Ankleshwar is estimated to handle about 5,000 TEUs, and Gateway estimates it can reach a similar number within 3–4 years. The 15-year exclusive agreement with the Sawariya Group for container train operations provides an asset-light entry. Management noted Ankleshwar enables mixed trains using it as a hub, combining Ankleshwar imports with northern imports — a combination other operators cannot run.

Indore ICD. Construction is ongoing, with an additional 2.5 acres acquired, bringing total land to 26.4 acres. Operations are expected by 2028. Planned capacity is approximately 1.2 lakh TEUs annually with estimated investment of approximately ₹150 crores. Construction slowed due to rains but is expected to be in full swing after September.

Network expansion. The company is looking at new locations for both domestic and EXIM. Domestic volumes are currently about 500 containers per month, with potential to increase substantially as the network expands. Fleet expansion to 37 rakes is planned (35 currently, with 2 more purchased). Addition of 100x40’ domestic containers and 2 high-speed high-capacity trains is in the pipeline.

Snowman Logistics expansion. Snowman is on track to reach 170,000 pallets by middle of next year. This year will see approximately 24,000 additional pallets, with Pune coming up in a couple of months and Patna thereafter. Pricing revisions of 5–7% on average were achieved from most customers, with further revisions expected as renewals occur. 5PL saw a 6% year-on-year hike in distribution, with service margins of 5–6%.

Land bank. The company holds approximately 473–475 acres across India in prime locations, mostly freehold. The Garhi land of 85–90 acres was cited as potentially valued at ₹25–30 crores per acre on an outright purchase basis, with residential towers coming nearby. Management noted the land bank can double ICD capacity at modest incremental capex.

Structural policy support. Government initiatives including PM GatiShakti, the National Logistics Policy, MMLPs, and PLI schemes support growth. The national target of 45% rail share of freight by 2030 works in Gateway’s favour. The company is also pursuing sustainability initiatives with EV trailers and electric reach stackers.

Balance sheet strength. Net debt-zero position achieved earlier in the year. Rated IND AA / Stable with short-term IND A1+ affirmed March 2026. FY26 dividends of ₹3.25 per share including a special dividend. First interim dividend for FY27 of ₹1.25 per share declared.

3. Key Risks

Geopolitical and macro uncertainty. The West Asia conflict remains the primary risk, with management repeatedly caveating guidance as “subject to global macros.” The duration and resolution of the conflict are unknown.

Shipping line decisions. The anticipated shift of volumes to JNPT depends on shipping lines choosing single dip over double dip. Management noted this is still in exploratory stages with no concrete decisions taken. JNPT is currently more expensive for Northern India due to extra distance (6 slabs higher than Mundra), and special haulage rationalization by Indian Railways is only a rumour with nothing concrete.

Competition. CONCOR is the largest player. Intense competition from domestic and global logistics companies, technology startups, and logistics insourcing by large e-commerce players. The transition from unorganized to organized in cold chain is ongoing but competitive intensity remains.

Regulatory and legal exposure. The various tax, regulatory, and legal proceedings described above, including SEIS benefit demands, income tax disallowances, land license fee demands, Benami Act proceedings, and GST demands for Snowman. Management believes no provisions are necessary based on legal opinions and expects full recovery of amounts related to favourable arbitration orders regarding bank guarantees.

Execution risk on expansion. Indore construction has been slowed by rains. Ankleshwar ramp-up will take time as shipping line BL points and other arrangements are added. Jaipur remains stuck in litigation.

Input cost volatility. Fuel, wage, and other input costs remain uncertain, with pass-through to customers subject to time lags.

Tax regime transition. While cash tax outgo is broadly unchanged due to accumulated MAT credit available for 7–8 years, the reported tax rate has increased, affecting reported PAT.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Volume growth guidance. Management had previously guided for 10–15% growth. In the Q1 FY27 call, Samvid Gupta stated the company is “hopeful that for the year, we can still close out on double-digit growth,” contingent on the war situation clearing up. Observed performance: Q1 FY27 throughput declined 1.96% year-on-year, with rail down 0.69% and CFS down 3.22%. June and July volumes were described as similar to Q1 trends, with July impacted by weather. The guidance remains aspirational and explicitly conditional on external factors.

Revenue and EBITDA growth guidance. Management guided for 10–15% growth on top line across all segments. Observed performance: Q1 FY27 total income was flat year-on-year at ₹553.7 crores (down 0.08%), EBITDA declined 1.28% to ₹121.5 crores, and EBITDA margin contracted to 21.9% from 22.2%. The core business (ex-Snowman) saw revenue decline to ₹373 crores from ₹389 crores, and EBITDA decline to ₹92 crores from ₹98 crores.

Margin guidance. Management indicated expectation that rail EBITDA would return to the “old range” as volumes recover. Observed performance: EBITDA per TEU declined to ₹5,012 in Q1 FY27 from ₹5,219 in Q1 FY26 and ₹5,208 in Q4 FY26. The 24–27% EBITDA margin band has been held across 13 quarters, but Q1 FY27 at 21.9% is at the lower end.

Ankleshwar timeline. Management guided for EXIM operations by September 2026. This remains on track per the press release, with customs permission received.

Indore timeline. Management guided for operations by 2028. Construction is ongoing, with tenders awarded and full-scale construction expected after September.

Snowman capacity. Management guided for 170,000 pallets by middle of next year. This remains on track per the press release, with 24,000 pallets to be added this year.

Tax guidance. Management guided that the company will pay at the rate of 18.88% for at least 7–8 years due to accumulated MAT credit. Observed performance: Q1 FY27 cash tax outgo was 18.88% versus 17.47% in the previous year, consistent with guidance.

Dividend. The company declared a first interim dividend of ₹1.25 per share for FY27, with a record date of August 11, 2026.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative remained consistently bullish, with a Buy rating on both the first and last reports, but the emphasis shifted from near-term operational disruptions to structural demand and ramp-up concerns. Persistent themes include EXIM imbalance, double-stacking progress, volume growth, and new-terminal execution. Early optimism about robust capex-led expansion and EBITDA/TEU improvement gave way to acknowledgments of lower-than-expected volume growth and delayed terminal stabilisation, though the broker still saw DFC connectivity and double-stacking as future catalysts.

Fears that came true

  • The early-flagged EXIM imbalance persisted and was still cited as a headwind in the last report, aligning with the disappointing returns on the 2024 Add calls.
  • The early warning about slower-than-anticipated volume growth and multi-year new-terminal ramp-up materialized, as the last report cited lower-than-expected volume growth and delays in stabilising new terminals.
  • The early risk of execution/ramp-up issues at new terminals was confirmed by the last report’s mention of delayed stabilisation, coinciding with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in the middle call history.

Optimism that failed

  • The targeted 50% double-stacking ratio did not materialise, as the last report showed only 41% with a near-term target of 43–45%.
  • The expected robust volume growth from new terminals/capacity did not materialise, with the last report explicitly noting lower-than-expected volume growth despite maintaining a 10–15% FY26 guidance.
  • The expectation that Jaipur ICD would reach full impact by FY26 was undermined by the last report’s delay in stabilisation of new terminals.
