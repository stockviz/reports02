Midwest Limited — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Diesel and fuel cost volatility. Management identified diesel as a major cost factor, noting it accounts for almost 13% of costs and that prices moved from around INR85 to INR125–130 per litre. Despite this, EBITDA margin declined only about 190 basis points in the quarter, which management attributed to proactive fleet electrification and solar back-integration. Fuel prices have since rationalized somewhat but remain above prior-year levels.

Quartz production stabilization. The Phase 1 quartz plant faced technical issues in the prior quarter that constrained output. Q1 FY27 quartz revenue was approximately INR5 crores on volumes of about 5,000 tonnes. Management stated the technical glitch is resolved and commercial operations have begun.

Revised quartz volume guidance. Against a prior call’s guidance of 150,000 tonnes for FY27, management now expects around 120,000 tonnes, attributing the reduction to the production stabilization issues.

Sri Lanka policy delay. The project faced a delay of almost six months due to a pending policy framework. Management indicated no progress for roughly 12 months until the policy was finalized and ratified, after which the government requested confirmation of production, investment, and timelines.

Kerala JV inactivity. No progress occurred during the quarter on the KMML partnership because a new state government was settling in and no new Managing Director had been appointed at KMML. Management could not estimate a timeline, citing government processes.

Phase 2 equipment sourcing. Machinery for the quartz Phase 2 expansion was originally to come from China; the company is now evaluating alternate options.

Seasonal and weather factors. Q4 is historically the strongest quarter (30–38% of annual revenue) due to Chinese New Year restocking, making Q1-Q4 comparisons less meaningful. Delayed rains in Q3 could also affect production.

Sierra Leone subsidiary. A couple of concessions reviewed had local issues; there was no update and minimal expenditure during the quarter.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue grew 35%, EBITDA 25%, and PAT 27% year-on-year, with most of the growth coming from granite. Excluding quartz, granite growth was still above 30%, supported by better pricing realizations.

Quartz ramp-up. Management expects this quarter to show a big jump from Q1, targeting a run rate of 10,000 tonnes per month by the end of the current quarter, 10,000–12,000 tonnes next quarter, and exceeding 15,000 tonnes per month in Q4. The current plant can reach 18,000 tonnes, with 15,000 tonnes considered the optimum base. Average realization is around INR10,000 per tonne. The product mix is roughly 65% engineered stone and 35% solar.

Indonesia MoU with PERMINAS. Signed with Indonesia’s state-owned strategic minerals enterprise, alongside Midwest Energy Limited and NFTDC. Midwest Limited is described as the first private company to have an MoU with PERMINAS, giving access to resources across the country with a focus on rare earths. The MoU covers upstream exploration, mining, processing, extraction, refining, and downstream manufacturing, including rare earth magnets. Two initial projects have been identified. Management highlighted that Indonesian ionic clay deposits contain heavy rare earths (dysprosium and terbium), which are absent from Indian monazite and are essential for defense, aerospace, and critical applications, and command four to five times the price of light rare earths. The MoU is expected to convert to an agreement this quarter, with a JV formed next quarter.

Sri Lanka heavy mineral sands. The policy framework is finalized and ratified, and the company has received formal communication to confirm capacities, timelines, and investment. Management expects documentation to complete this quarter, ground-breaking around October, and plant build-out of 12–15 months. Plant capex is estimated at approximately INR120 crores, expected to be funded from internal accruals. Commercial revenue is conservatively expected in FY29.

Granite demand and pricing. Demand is described as good, with better realizations and a price increase of roughly 3–5% implemented. Volume growth was approximately 10% across Black Galaxy and Absolute Black.

Electrification and solar. Most electrification expenditure is done this year, reducing dependence on diesel fluctuations. Management stated granite margins should be insulated from future diesel volatility going forward.

APMDC Letters of Intent. The company accepted LOIs for granite quarry leases in Andhra Pradesh on a Raising-Cum-Sale Contract basis across three locations in Chittoor and Srikakulam districts.

Longer-term ambitions. Management aims for 2.5x+ revenue growth, 3x+ profitability growth, and 35% ROCE over three to four years, supported by INR300 crores of planned capex, alongside a 15–20% reduction in energy consumption and 10% of energy from renewables.

3. Key Risks

  • Resource and geology risk: variations in mineral structure and block yield affecting realization.
  • Market and demand sensitivity: exposure to construction cycles and real estate activity.
  • Commodity, energy, and freight cost pressures, with diesel specifically identified as a significant cost head.
  • Foreign-exchange risk from currency fluctuations impacting export realizations.
  • Regulatory and approvals risk for mining and processing operations, including the Sri Lankan license and the Kerala JV.
  • Project execution and new-business risk: plant stabilization and technology validation, including rare-earth extraction recovery.
  • Geopolitical and jurisdictional uncertainty, particularly Sri Lankan licensing.
  • Concentration risk in product categories and export destinations, notably China.
  • Capability development risk as the company transitions into critical minerals.
  • Forward-looking statement risks: fiscal policy, competition, inflationary pressures, and general economic conditions affecting demand, supply, and prices in domestic and international markets.
  • Promoter holding overhang: promoter holding stands at 77%, above the 75% threshold, with a three-year timeline indicated and no near-term plan to reduce it.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 revenue guidance. Management estimates approximately INR840 crores for FY27, against INR645 crores in the prior year, split into roughly INR720 crores from granite and INR100–120 crores from quartz. Management characterized this as conservative, stating that if things go as planned, the company should beat it. An analyst noted Q1 alone was around INR190 crores, implying the guidance may be on the lower side.

Granite margin guidance. Management expects granite EBITDA to be in line with prior years, within a range of about 50–100 basis points, unless diesel prices intervene. Q1 FY27 saw a 190 basis point margin decline attributed to diesel, with recovery expected in Q2 given fuel rationalization, currency support, and the price increase.

Quartz break-even guidance. Management expects break-even at 10,000 tonnes per month, targeted for end of the current quarter or next quarter, with profit for the year. Optimum EBITDA is expected next year. FY28 quartz revenue from Phase 1 is guided at INR180–200 crores, with Phase 2 contributing an additional INR40–50 crores, targeting INR200–240 crores overall. Blended EBITDA for FY28 is guided at around 29–30%, with quartz EBITDA close to or slightly above 30%.

Quartz volume guidance revision. The prior call’s 150,000-tonne FY27 target was revised down to approximately 120,000 tonnes, with all current guidance based on the revised figure.

Phase 2 timing. Commissioning is guided within 10–12 months, with revenue expected next year and a buffer of about a quarter for stabilization, implying roughly four months of contribution in FY28. Capex is approximately INR125 crores, split across the current and next year, with INR60–70 crores to be spent over Q2 and Q3.

Sri Lanka revenue timing. Commercial revenue is conservatively guided for FY29, not FY28, reflecting licensing and build-out timelines.

Indonesia revenue timing. Management guided FY29 for potential revenue, describing the project as at a very initial stage and declining to provide precise timelines or numbers.

FY28 overall revenue. Management indicated FY28 revenue could be close to INR950 crores, combining full quartz Phase 1 contribution, partial Phase 2, and 10–12% granite growth.

Capex funding. Phase 2 quartz is funded from fresh equity raised, not debt. Granite replacement capex is met from regular cash flows. Sri Lanka capex of INR120–150 crores is expected from internal accruals. Gearing stands at 0.2 times, providing leverage headroom.
