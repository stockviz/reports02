Headwinds and Challenges

  • Global trade uncertainty and high US import tariffs on automotive components exceeding 45%.
  • Low-cost Chinese import competition causing margin compression in price-sensitive segments.
  • Raw material price volatility tied to global commodity cycles.
  • High revenue concentration in automotive OEM demand.
  • High import dependence for precision and specialty bearings such as ceramic, magnetic, and hybrid.
  • Potential supply chain disruptions or operational continuity risks.
  • Labour availability issues and short-term shortages of workers on the shop floor, acting as a constraint on output conversion.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Global expansion and recovery of European markets.
  • The China+1 supply chain strategy by Western OEMs diversifying procurement toward India.
  • Government initiatives such as ‘Make in India’ and PLI schemes.
  • A growing EV and hybrid vehicle transition driving demand for lighter and stronger forged components.
  • Railway modernization, including Vande Bharat.
  • Expansion into aerospace and defence indigenization.
  • A substantial capacity runway with utilization at 60-65%, allowing growth without major new capital expenditure.
  • Favourable macro tailwinds including growth in global bearings and Indian forging markets, EV and hybrid vehicle boom, renewable energy growth, China+1 supply shift, Make in India and PLI schemes, and aerospace and defence indigenization.

Key Risks

  • Margin compression from low-cost Chinese imports in price-sensitive segments.
  • Exposure to global commodity cycles through raw material price volatility.
  • Dependence on automotive OEM demand, which represents a concentrated revenue base.
  • Reliance on imports for precision and specialty bearings.
  • Vulnerability to supply chain disruptions and operational continuity risks.
  • Labour availability constraints limiting output conversion.

Broker Narrative

The first report was a Buy built on broad tailwinds such as bearing-component outsourcing, preference for non-Chinese suppliers, and demand recovery across geographies. By the August 2024 Hold report, the narrative turned defensive after export revenue fell 35% YoY, ocean freight spiked, and European demand weakened. The persistent themes were bearing localization and healthy domestic bearing-ring demand, but the broker shifted from expecting broad growth to cautioning over Europe and industrial demand.

Fears that came true

  • Macro weakness, inflation, and rising interest rates in the US and Europe materialized as weaker European demand (~15% of revenue), contributing to a 35% YoY export decline and a DISAPPOINTMENT outcome.
  • The earlier higher-input-cost/import-pressure theme was mirrored by the spike in ocean freight rates that hurt Q1FY25 performance.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of an opening agriculture cycle in LATAM and other geographies did not translate into export growth, as export revenue declined 35% YoY.
  • The last report’s expectation of demand recovery in FY25 from existing and new customers failed to materialize in the stock, which returned -41.1% after the Hold call.
