MADHUCON PROJECTS LIMITED — Summary of Recent Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Qualified audit opinions and unresolved accounting matters. The statutory auditor, P. Murali & Co., issued a qualified opinion on both the standalone and consolidated results for the quarter and year ended 31 March 2026 (board meeting of 28 May 2026), and again on the limited review of the quarter ended 30 June 2026 (board meeting of 13 August 2026). The qualifications cover a wide set of unresolved items:

  • Carrying value of equity investments of Rs. 36,657.07 lakhs and other investments of Rs. 6,426.85 lakhs in subsidiaries and other companies, where several entities have eroded net worth or affected going-concern status, with no fair valuation available to determine impairment.
  • No write-off of investments was made during the quarter ended 30 June 2026, in contrast to partial write-offs in earlier periods.
  • Default on dues to Punjab National Bank, classified as NPA, with interest not provided; OTS agreements signed and payments made, but OTS benefits not recognised pending a No Objection Certificate.
  • Recognition of Rs. 15,252.71 lakhs of earlier-year advances against work bills as revenue from operations during FY 2025-26, which the auditor states lacked proper justification.
  • Write-back of trade payables (Rs. 4,613.46 lakhs for the March 2026 quarter; Rs. 14,279.73 lakhs for FY 2025-26; Rs. 565.00 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter) and write-off of advances to other parties.
  • Unpaid dividend of Rs. 4.46 lakhs for FY 2009-10 to 2010-11 not yet transferred to the IEPF.
  • GST turnover and credit reconciliation pending; title deeds for certain immovable properties not produced; dormant bank account balances unconfirmed; trade payable, receivable and advance balances unconfirmed, with some payables netted against receivables.
  • Managerial remuneration paid without prior lender bank approval (Rs. 74.50 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter; Rs. 15.38 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter).
  • Undisputed statutory dues outstanding: dividend distribution tax and interest of Rs. 139.93 lakhs (FY 2011-12 to 2016-17) and provident fund of Rs. 63.95 lakhs (FY 2013-14 to 2020-21).
  • Internal audit not conducted for FY 2025-26 (as noted in the FY26 audit report).

Going-concern pressure at the holding company. The auditor notes that current liabilities exceeded current assets by Rs. 16,809.94 lakhs, and that the pending PNB NOC contributes to material uncertainty. Management nonetheless prepared the accounts on a going-concern basis, citing OTS settlements and continuing discussions with other lenders on a debt resolution plan.

Distressed subsidiary and step-down subsidiary portfolio. Multiple SPVs are in or near insolvency, with adverse conclusions from their respective auditors:

  • Ranchi Expressways Ltd: CBI FIR (12 March 2019) against the company, promoters and directors under the Prevention of Corruption Act and IPC; Enforcement Directorate raid (11 June 2021) and charge sheet (December 2023); ED provisional attachment of 105 immovable properties and 28 other assets worth Rs. 96.21 crore and Rs. 80.65 crore (press releases of 2 July 2022 and 17 October 2022); NCLT admitted CIRP on 22 December 2023 on a State Bank of India petition, with an appeal pending before NCLAT. Madhucon Projects carries Rs. 1.40 lakhs of investment and Rs. 8,114.51 lakhs of trade receivables in this entity with no provision. The auditor also notes a change in accounting policy from 1 April 2026 that stopped amortising deferred revenue expenditure, which would have increased the quarterly loss by Rs. 8,363.29 lakhs had the prior policy continued, and which the auditor states appears to contravene Ind AS 37.
  • Madurai Tuticorin Expressways Ltd: project terminated by NHAI on 17 March 2023; auditor issued an adverse conclusion, noting the financials were prepared other than on a going-concern basis.
  • Rajauli-Bakhtiyarpur Expressways Ltd: project terminated by NHAI on 31 December 2015 and by BSRDC on 2 March 2016; adverse conclusion; arbitration expected to be unfavourable.
  • Vijayawada-Machilipatnam Expressways Ltd: concession agreement terminated by NHAI on 29 October 2013; adverse conclusion; arbitral award of approximately Rs. 26.35 crore in the company’s favour while other claims and NHAI counterclaims were substantially rejected.
  • Chhapra-Hajipur Expressways Ltd: net worth fully eroded, going concern affected; project cost of Rs. 1,19,365.27 lakhs not recognised as prescribed under Ind AS 109; adverse conclusion.
  • Barasat-Krishnagar Expressways Ltd: project terminated by NHAI on 31 December 2015; NCLT admitted CIRP on 28 November 2023 on an SBI petition, appeal pending before NCLAT; adverse conclusion. An arbitral award dated 5 May 2022 requires the company to pay Rs. 396.06 crores to EPC contractor Madhucon Projects Ltd, which has not been recognised in its books.
  • TN (DK) Expressways Ltd: loans classified NPA, interest not recognised; contingent liability of Rs. 475.36 crores for NHAI penalty for delayed periodic maintenance; SBI petition under Section 7 of IBC disposed of by NCLT on 29 July 2025 without initiating CIRP, with liberty to file afresh.
  • Madhucon Infra Ltd (subsidiary): equity investments of Rs. 6,719.83 lakhs and unsecured loans/advances of Rs. 28,771.52 lakhs to subsidiaries/other companies; internal audit not conducted; advances of Rs. 5,000.35 lakhs to Trichy-Thanjavur Expressways and Rs. 26,668.16 lakhs to Ranchi Expressways; IFCI OTS of Rs. 51 crores approved on 19 June 2023, with Rs. 4,250.00 lakhs paid as of 30 June 2026.
  • Madhucon Toll Highways Ltd: equity investments of Rs. 6,347.75 lakhs and unsecured loans/advances of Rs. 2,393.04 lakhs; no investment write-off provision in the June 2026 quarter.
  • Madhucon Heights Pvt Ltd and Nama Hotels Pvt Ltd: capital work-in-progress realisable value not ascertainable in the absence of valuation reports; a case is pending before the Telangana High Court for Madhucon Heights.

Bank guarantee invocation. A bank guarantee of Rs. 480.30 lakhs given by SREI to NHIDCL on the company’s behalf was invoked during the quarter ended 30 June 2026, with the amount charged from SREI and shown as payable to SREI; the company is contesting the invocation.

Regulatory and filing issues. NSE raised deficiencies in the FY26 results filings (email of 14 July 2026), including the Statement of Impact of Audit Qualifications not being in SEBI format and not signed by the MD/CEO and audit committee chairperson, a standalone audit report that referred to a “review,” and a mismatch in consolidated net cash flows from financing activities between XBRL and PDF. The company submitted revised filings on 18 July, 20 July and 22 July 2026.

Revenue and profitability decline. Standalone revenue from operations fell to Rs. 2,778.66 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter from Rs. 13,517.60 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter and Rs. 10,377.85 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter. Standalone FY26 revenue was Rs. 46,407.81 lakhs against Rs. 58,032.49 lakhs in FY25. The June 2026 quarter recorded a standalone loss before tax of Rs. 135.86 lakhs, versus profit before tax of Rs. 43.24 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter. Consolidated revenue for the June 2026 quarter was Rs. 4,994.21 lakhs against Rs. 15,729.26 lakhs a year earlier.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

One Time Settlement progress with lenders. The company has submitted OTS proposals to all working capital banks and financial institutions and is working towards approval with mutually agreed modifications. The board resolution at the 36th AGM seeks approval for non-provision of interest on working capital and unsecured loans for FY 2025-26 on this basis. OTS agreements have been entered into with PNB, with full payments made, though the NOC is awaited. For Madhucon Infra Ltd, IFCI approved a full and final settlement of Rs. 51 crores on 19 June 2023, with Rs. 4,250.00 lakhs paid as of 30 June 2026.

Arbitral receipts. During the quarter ended 30 June 2026, the company received Rs. 5,512.73 lakhs from NHAI-Assam pursuant to an arbitral award, accounted as an advance received rather than revenue pending return of the bank guarantee. In the Vijayawada-Machilipatnam matter, the arbitral tribunal awarded approximately Rs. 26.35 crore towards bid security, interest and costs.

Order book and project activity. The company’s operations continue to consist of construction-project activities, with no other reportable segments under Ind AS 108. The June 2026 standalone results show cost of materials consumed of Rs. 2,802.33 lakhs and other expenses of Rs. 411.98 lakhs, indicating ongoing project execution.

Board and governance refresh. The board appointed Mr. Shankara Rao Kadambala as an additional independent director for five years from 13 August 2026 (subject to shareholder approval at the 36th AGM) and Mr. Prithvi Teja Nama as an additional director from 13 August 2026 (subject to shareholder approval). The board also appointed M/s. B. Narsing Rao & Co LLP as statutory auditors for three years from 1 July 2026, M/s. Ganga Rao & Associates as internal auditors for FY 2026-27, and M/s. AS RAO & CO. as cost auditors for FY 2026-27 at a remuneration of Rs. 1,00,000 inclusive of taxes.

Consolidated profitability in the June 2026 quarter. The consolidated results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 show a profit before tax of Rs. 1,553.72 lakhs and a profit for the period of Rs. 1,726.06 lakhs, compared with a loss of Rs. 12,696.73 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter and a loss of Rs. 9,514.56 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter. Total comprehensive income attributable to shareholders of the parent was Rs. 1,641.91 lakhs. This swing reflects the absence of the depreciation and amortisation charge that was present in prior periods (Rs. 13,069.91 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter and Rs. 13,077.79 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter), consistent with the change in accounting policy at Ranchi Expressways Ltd.

3. Key Risks

  • Going-concern risk at the holding company, given current liabilities exceeding current assets by Rs. 16,809.94 lakhs and the pending PNB NOC.
  • Impairment risk on investments and receivables in distressed subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries, where no provisions have been made and fair values are not ascertainable.
  • Insolvency risk across multiple SPVs, with CIRP admitted at Ranchi Expressways, Barasat-Krishnagar and Trichy-Thanjavur (the latter two currently under NCLAT deferral), and an SBI petition against TN (DK) Expressways disposed of with liberty to refile.
  • Litigation and investigation risk, including the CBI FIR and ED proceedings related to Ranchi Expressways, the ED provisional attachment of properties worth Rs. 96.21 crore and Rs. 80.65 crore, and the arbitral award of Rs. 396.06 crores payable by Barasat-Krishnagar to Madhucon Projects.
  • Accounting and disclosure risk, including the auditor’s observation that the change in accounting policy at Ranchi Expressways appears to contravene Ind AS 37, the recognition of earlier-year advances as revenue, and the qualified opinions.
  • Regulatory compliance risk, evidenced by the NSE queries on FY26 filings and the pending IEPF transfer.
  • Counterparty and confirmation risk, with unconfirmed trade payables, receivables, advances and dormant bank balances.
  • Contingent liability risk, including the Rs. 475.36 crores NHAI penalty exposure at TN (DK) Expressways.
  • Liquidity and funding risk, with interest not provided on NPA-classified loans and reliance on OTS approvals.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Interest non-provision. Management sought shareholder approval at the 36th AGM for non-provision of interest on working capital and unsecured loans for FY 2025-26, on the basis that OTS proposals have been submitted to all working capital banks and financial institutions and approval is expected with suitable modifications. Observed performance: interest on the PNB loan has not been provided, and the auditor notes the loan was classified NPA. OTS benefits have not been recognised despite full payments, pending the NOC. The June 2026 standalone results show nil financial costs, consistent with the non-provision stance.

Going-concern assumption. Management believes the going-concern basis remains appropriate, citing OTS settlements with banks and continuing discussions with other lenders for a debt resolution plan. Observed performance: the auditor records material uncertainty, current liabilities exceeding current assets by Rs. 16,809.94 lakhs, and the pending PNB NOC. The auditor issued qualified conclusions on both the FY26 and June 2026 results.

OTS with PNB. Management states that payments have been made as per the OTS agreement and that no prior lender approval was obtained for managerial remuneration because of complete settlement of dues. Observed performance: the auditor confirms full payments have been made but the NOC has not been received, so OTS benefits remain unrecognised.

Investment write-offs. The company has been writing off investments over past years. Observed performance: no write-off of investments was made during the quarter ended 30 June 2026, and the auditor was unable to comment on the carrying value of investments and whether further impairment is required. In FY26, partial write-offs were made in Madhucon Infra Ltd (75% of investment written off up to 31 March 2026) and Madurai Tuticorin Expressways Ltd (66.66% written off up to 31 March 2026), with the auditor unable to ascertain the basis of the partial write-offs.

Ranchi Expressways accounting policy. The board resolution dated 22 May 2026 changed the policy to stop amortising deferred revenue expenditure from 1 April 2026. Observed performance: the auditor states that had the prior policy continued, the loss for the June 2026 quarter would have increased by Rs. 8,363.29 lakhs, and that the treatment appears to contravene Ind AS 37, overstating current assets by Rs. 766,906.45 lakhs and understating cumulative losses by the same amount. The auditor also notes significant doubt on Ranchi Expressways’ going-concern ability following termination of the concession agreement.

Revenue recognition. Management recognised Rs. 15,252.71 lakhs of earlier-year advances against work bills as revenue from operations in FY 2025-26. Observed performance: the auditor states there is no proper justification for this recognition.

NHAI-Assam arbitral receipt. Management accounted Rs. 5,512.73 lakhs received from NHAI-Assam as an advance received rather than revenue, pending return of the bank guarantee. Observed performance: the auditor records this treatment without adverse comment.

Dividend transfer to IEPF. Management has not transferred Rs. 4.46 lakhs of unpaid dividend for FY 2009-10 to 2010-11 to the IEPF. Observed performance: the auditor notes this as an outstanding matter.

Internal audit. The board appointed M/s. Ganga Rao & Associates as internal auditors for FY 2026-27 on 13 August 2026. Observed performance: the auditor notes internal audit was not conducted for FY 2025-26 at the holding company and at Madhucon Infra Ltd.

Financial performance trajectory. Management’s June 2026 standalone results show a loss before tax of Rs. 135.86 lakhs and a profit for the period of Rs. 36.48 lakhs (after a deferred tax credit of Rs. 172.34 lakhs), compared with a profit before tax of Rs. 43.24 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter. Consolidated results show a profit for the period of Rs. 1,726.06 lakhs, driven by the absence of the depreciation and amortisation charge that was present in prior periods. FY26 standalone profit for the period was Rs. 1,112.23 lakhs, against a loss of Rs. 2,104.41 lakhs in FY25, while FY26 standalone revenue declined to Rs. 46,407.81 lakhs from Rs. 58,032.49 lakhs in FY25.
