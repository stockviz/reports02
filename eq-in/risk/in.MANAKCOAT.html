MANAKCOAT: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Cost inflation from geopolitical disruption. The second half of FY26 was marked by a sharp escalation in fuel, freight and petrochemical-linked costs following the Middle East conflict. Management described LPG prices spiking from roughly INR60/kg to INR200/kg, with the Strait of Hormuz closure causing a nationwide supply-side shock in LPG and propane availability. Prices later cooled to around INR80/kg, leaving costs about 25% above pre-war levels. The company maintained uninterrupted plant operations through buffer stock and by diversifying purchases from PSU to private and PSU suppliers, but at a cost.

Elevated input prices. Aluminium, zinc and paints remained at multi-year highs during Q1 FY27, alongside higher freight and fuel costs.

Volume softness and ramp-up friction. Q1 FY27 sales of ~27,900 MT were marginally below Q1 FY26 (~29,000 MT). Management attributed this entirely to the gradual ramp-up of the Alu-Zinc line, describing “teething troubles” typical of a new, highly automated line in its first 3–4 months of commissioning, and acknowledged running behind on execution plans.

Working capital drag. The working capital cycle stands at about 75 days, reflecting inventories across raw material, WIP and finished goods plus debtors, compounded by the need to stock hundreds of SKUs for customized export and domestic orders.

External uncertainty. Management explicitly noted that the external environment “continues to remain uncertain.”

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Alu-Zinc technology upgrade. Commissioned in December 2025, the upgrade raised capacity from 1,32,000 TPA to 1,80,000 TPA (+36%) with 100% Alu-Zinc capability. Q1 FY27 output was 27,941 MT (+8% QoQ) at 62% utilization, leaving substantial headroom. Management indicated incremental EBITDA of INR1,000–3,000/ton from the shift from galvanized to Alu-Zinc, driven by both cost savings and product premium. Galvanized sales were zero in Q1 FY27.

Second colour coating line. In advanced stages of erection and commissioning, targeted for Q2 FY27. It lifts total Pre-Painted capacity from 86,000 to 2,36,000 TPA (+174%). The existing line ran at 95.4% utilization, indicating demand already waiting for the new capacity. Management expects faster ramp-up than Alu-Zinc given lower complexity, with 50–60% utilization achievable in H2 FY27.

7 MW captive solar plant at Kutch. Targeted for Q2 FY27, expected to offset 50–55% of grid power consumption, a structural reduction in energy cost per unit, with EPC by Prozeal Green Energy.

Export momentum. Exports were 65% of Q1 FY27 volume (18,221 MT), with export revenue up 20% YoY and export Pre-Painted Steel volumes up 25% YoY. Four new markets were added: Latvia, Brazil, Jamaica and Somalia. Order book stood at approximately INR450 crore (±INR20–25 crore), representing 4.5–5 months of execution, with management guiding to a continuous INR350–450 crore order book through the year.

Pricing discipline. All new Q1 orders were priced to fully cover current raw material and freight costs with a margin buffer, with quarterly price resets and pass-through mechanisms agreed with export customers.

Phase 2 expansion blueprint. A planned INR350 crore outlay covering backward integration (cold rolling) and a second Alu-Zinc line, funded by a mix of internal accruals, debt and equity. Management estimates additional debt of roughly INR100 crore, with leverage expected to stay below 1.25x debt-to-equity. Cold rolling is expected to cut inventory roughly in half and compress the working capital cycle substantially. Phase 2 is slated to begin in FY28.

Salesforce CRM implementation is underway to provide centralized customer, order and after-sales visibility across channels.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and energy price volatility, with the steel cycle described as unpredictable; the business model relies on back-to-back selling and quarterly pass-through, which lagged sharply moving costs in Q4 FY26.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk on new lines, evidenced by the Alu-Zinc teething issues and the company running behind on its order book.
  • Geopolitical supply risk to LPG/propane, given India’s import dependence, though sourcing has diversified to the USA, Canada, Australia, Venezuela and Russia.
  • Leverage and funding risk around the INR350 crore Phase 2 capex, whose equity/debt/internal accrual mix is not yet frozen and depends on FY27 earnings.
  • Working capital intensity as revenues scale, with cash flows directed toward capex and working capital until stable utilization is reached.
  • Export concentration, with 65% of volumes exported and a large share of the order book tied to export and OEM long-term customers.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Q1 FY27 delivery against prior commentary. Management had previously guided to H1 recovery and 10–12% consolidated EBITDA margin for FY27. Q1 FY27 delivered EBITDA margin of 11.06% (+422 bps QoQ) and EBITDA of INR29.08 crore (+86% QoQ), which management characterized as reflecting that recovery. Revenue was INR263.07 crore (+15% QoQ, +3.59% YoY); PAT was INR14.10 crore (+162% QoQ); realization per ton rose to INR88,597 from INR79,180; EBITDA per ton reached an all-time high of INR10,401; finance cost fell 11.77% YoY to INR6.86 crore.

Margin sustainability. Management called the Q1 EBITDA per ton sustainable and sees further headroom from new capacity, solar savings and a higher value-added mix, indicating potential improvement of roughly 1–2% in margin, though it declined to commit to a precise FY27 figure or confirm whether the full benefit lands in FY27.

FY27 revenue guidance. Based on new capacities and 50–60% utilization of the new colour coating line, management guided to approximately 150,000 tons and revenue of roughly INR1,300–1,350 crore for FY27.

FY28 and peak-capacity outlook. Management referenced FY28 volumes of 1,80,000–2,10,000 tons and revenue of INR1,700–1,800 crore from the new colour coating line, and peak revenue of INR2,500–2,700 crore at optimum utilization after Phase 2, with tonnages near or above 3 lakh tons. It declined to estimate Phase 2 ramp-up timing as premature.

Alu-Zinc utilization target. Management stated an aim to reach 75–80% utilization within roughly three months.

Debt trajectory. Current debt is about INR115 crore, including term debt for capex. Of the INR140 crore spent on the Alu-Zinc upgrade, second colour coating line and solar plant, INR15–20 crore of debt remains to be deployed. Management expects peak debt of roughly INR125–130 crore rather than INR140 crore, as repayments have begun.

Order book growth. Management noted order book growth from lows of INR100–120 crore over the last couple of years to INR400–450 crore currently, describing it as a gradual process built on demonstrated execution capability.

Dividend and governance events. The 16th AGM was held on 3 September 2026, with 11 resolutions passed by overwhelming majority, including adoption of financial statements, a final dividend of Rs 0.05 per equity share, director and auditor appointments and remunerations, and the appointment of Mr. Devansh Agrawal as Vice-President Business Development. The cut-off date for voting and dividend eligibility was 27 August 2026. Mr. Sushil Kumar Agrawal and Mr. Karan Agrawal were re-appointed as Managing Director and Wholetime Director respectively, and Mr. Devansh Agrawal’s remuneration was set at Rs 8,00,000 per month effective 1 July 2026.

FY26 baseline. Total income for FY26 was Rs. 896.27 crore, with EBITDA of Rs. 92.21 crore and PAT of Rs. 40.69 crore. The company operates one manufacturing plant in Kutch, Gujarat, and targets a FY29 vision of tripling output, revenue and profitability.
