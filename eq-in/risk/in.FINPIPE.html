FINPIPE: Q1 FY27 Earnings Call and Recent Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Polymer price volatility dominated the quarter. Management described Q1 FY27 as marked by volatility in polymer prices, which became the dominant macro factor for the industry. Average PVC prices were higher year-on-year at about USD875 per metric ton versus about USD707 per metric ton a year earlier, but prices saw a sharp intra-quarter correction. This triggered channel destocking and impacted volumes during what is typically a seasonally strong pre-monsoon quarter. On the call, the CFO noted PVC prices moved from INR96 to INR92 and then to INR82 during the quarter.

Volume and revenue decline. Overall sales volume declined 27% year-on-year to around 68,000 metric tons in Q1 FY27 (standalone sales volume of 67,699 MT versus 92,129 MT in Q1 FY26). Revenue declined 27% to ₹884 crore from ₹1,043 crore. Agri volumes declined 27% and non-agri volumes declined around 24%.

Agri-segment concentration amplified the downturn. Management attributed the steeper volume decline relative to peers primarily to Finolex’s larger presence in the agri segment, which was more affected than plumbing/non-agri. Agri was 69% of volumes in the quarter, with fittings at 11% of total volume and CPVC at around 7%.

Market share erosion. Management acknowledged that among the top 5–6 reporting companies, Finolex’s full-year FY26 share was about 22%, down from about 27% in FY23. Management cited a constant push to increase penetration in underrepresented areas and noted there are segments where the company is not present but competitors are.

VCM sourcing constraints. VCM availability remains limited due to issues in the Middle East, and VCM requires specialized logistics. Feedstock from Northeast Asia, the main source for India, is also constrained. The Ratnagiri VCM line does not produce during the monsoon months because VCM cannot be imported then due to jetty limitations, and it cannot be imported at another port and transported to Ratnagiri. Management said efforts to secure the VCM supply chain are ongoing, with the Middle East situation remaining unpredictable.

Realization lag versus peers. On a quarter-on-quarter basis, Finolex’s realization per kg rose about 1%, versus 7% and 9% reported by two peers. The CFO attributed this to the sharp PVC price decline in April and to incentives and discounts in the sector affecting net landed realization per kg.

Inventory gains/losses. Management said inventory gains and losses exist but declined to quantify them, stating they would be utilized over Q1 and Q2.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Regulatory interventions supporting price stability. Two developments were highlighted: the withdrawal of the customs duty exemption on PVC resin in mid-July, and the imposition of a minimum import price (MIP) for PVC resin. Management expects these to support stability in PVC prices and help normalize channel inventories.

Post-MIP volume recovery. The CFO stated that post-MIP, volumes started to pick up, with a good uptick in July. July was described as the best of the four months of the year, with May and June also showing growth after a weak April. Management indicated that if July’s performance holds, a flattish-to-slightly-positive first half year-on-year could be expected, though August and September remain to be seen.

Price pass-through. Management stated that barring INR1–2, most of the recent PVC price increase has been passed on to customers as of the call date.

Seasonal demand pattern. PVC demand in India peaks pre-monsoon (January–May) as farmers prepare for Kharif, moderates in Q2–Q3, and picks up again toward the end of Q2/Q3 with the Rabi season.

Capacity headroom and capex. Total installed capacity is 520 (thousand metric tons), against last year’s volume of 333, leaving headroom to grow at a healthy rate of 10%–12%. Capex plans remain intact, with capital outlay in the INR125–200 crore range. The company is expanding capacity and augmenting it via debottlenecking—replacing older lower-capacity extruders with higher-capacity ones—rather than announcing large greenfield or brownfield expansions. Management said capacity planning is done on a mid- to long-term basis and is not impacted by short-term macro conditions.

CPVC capacity. Management stated the company has enough CPVC extrusion capacity to support growth in the CPVC segment.

Portfolio rebalancing. Agri versus non-agri mix improved from 67% agri two years ago to 63% last year, with a continued directional approach toward better portfolio balance.

JJM 2.0. The government announced an outlay of about INR66,000–67,000 crore for JJM 2.0 for the current financial year. Management noted the distribution mechanism differs from JJM 1.0 and that all of Finolex’s business is through channel partners who participate. Management stated that on an overall basis, JJM 2.0 is not higher than JJM 1.0 in value terms.

Strong liquidity. Cash in hand of ₹2,636 crore was highlighted as a continuing balance sheet strength.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued volatility in PVC prices and the global geopolitical situation, which management repeatedly cited as reasons for withholding full-year guidance.
  • Channel inventory levels: while described as near normal with some room remaining, further stocking behavior could distort demand visibility.
  • VCM availability and logistics constraints, particularly the monsoon-related shutdown of the VCM line at Ratnagiri and dependence on Middle East and Northeast Asian supply.
  • Market share pressure from competitors, particularly in segments where Finolex is not present.
  • Dependence on the agri segment, which was more affected than non-agri during the quarter.
  • Other income sensitivity: the CFO noted other income is driven by bond yields and represents mark-to-market, not realized, gains/losses, so it will remain range-bound only if bond yields are stable.
  • A clerical error in the consolidated XBRL filing for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026, where EPS was inadvertently entered under “discontinued operations” instead of “continuing operations.” The company clarified the PDF figures are correct and the error was typographical, not affecting the board-approved results.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Margin guidance. Management reiterated its prior guidance of sub-15% margins (around 15%) for the full year, unchanged from the Q4 call. When asked why the company was “under-guiding” given a 12% margin delivered in a falling-resin, declining-volume environment, the CFO said the guidance remains unchanged due to the volatility seen in the quarter.

Observed margin performance. Q1 FY27 EBITDA was ₹107 crore (per the CFO on the call) with EBITDA margin improving from 9% to 12% year-on-year (₹94 crore in Q1 FY26 to ₹109 crore per the results summary). PBT was ₹148 crore versus ₹126 crore in Q1 FY26. PAT was around ₹107 crore. The CFO attributed the operating performance largely to top-line realization, with costs remaining range-bound, and cited the company’s backward-integrated position and the timing of raw material procurement as cost advantages.

Volume guidance. Management declined to give full-year volume guidance, stating it is too early and that the PVC industry is surrounded by uncertainty and volatility. The prior Q4 call projection was said to continue. For H1, management expressed hope for flattish-to-slightly-positive year-on-year performance if July’s trend holds.

Spread data. PVC-EDC spread was about $501 in Q1 FY27 versus $522 in Q1 FY26; PVC-VCM spread was INR122 in Q1 FY27 versus INR163 in Q1 FY26. Latest PVC price cited was $795 with a PVC-EDC spread of about $490.

Corporate actions. The Board recommended a final dividend of ₹2 and a special dividend of ₹0.75 per equity share for the year ended March 31, 2026, with a record date of September 11, 2026. The 45th AGM is scheduled for September 22, 2026, at 4:00 p.m. IST via video conferencing/OAVM. The company proposed revising the annual monetary ceiling for Non-Executive Directors’ commission to ₹6,00,00,000.
