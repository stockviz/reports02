BORANA — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Operating cost and input pressures - Raw material price volatility across yarn, polyester, and other fibers. - Rising energy and utility costs. - Rising wages alongside labor shortages. - Currency exchange rate fluctuations.

Competitive and demand environment - Intense global competition from low-cost producing countries including China, Bangladesh, Vietnam, Pakistan, and Turkey. - Rapidly changing consumer preferences. - General economic and market uncertainties. - Potential supply chain disruptions.

Regulatory and compliance - Stringent environmental regulations.

Project execution headwinds (renewable energy) - High wind speeds affecting WTG erection. - Heavy monsoon conditions requiring additional logistics-road rework. - Delays in obtaining shutdowns of existing 33 kV and 11 kV lines for the 33 kV transmission-line work. - These factors contributed to the delay and revision of the commissioning timeline for the 9.9 MW Hybrid Power Project at Kalikanagar, Gujarat, and were described as beyond the project developer’s control.

General forward-looking caution - Actual outcomes may differ due to known and unknown risks. - Operational and renewable-energy commissioning timelines are subject to stabilisation, approvals, grid availability, and other implementation factors.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Demand drivers - Rising global demand for apparel, home textiles, and technical textiles, driven by fast fashion, growing urbanization, and rising disposable incomes. - Increasing demand for sustainable textiles. - Growth of e-commerce.

Policy and export support - Government support through the PM MITRA Parks Scheme and the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme for Textiles. - Growing export opportunities as global brands diversify supply chains beyond China. - The political crisis in Bangladesh has led global buyers to seek stable supply chains in India. - India’s textile roadmap favours scale, MMF (man-made fiber), and value addition, with a USD 100 billion textile export target by FY 2030.

Capacity and technology - Adoption of advanced technology such as water-jet looms. - Unit 4 expansion: +192 water-jet looms and +3 texturizing machines, targeted for commercial production in December 2026. - A 23.30 MW renewable portfolio planned, including a 19.79 MW hybrid renewable-energy project targeted for commissioning during 2026. - A scaled manufacturing platform expected to deliver double-digit revenue growth and operating leverage.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material price volatility and energy cost inflation compressing margins.
  • Competitive pressure from low-cost textile-producing nations.
  • Supply chain disruptions and currency fluctuations.
  • Environmental regulatory compliance obligations.
  • Labor availability and wage inflation.
  • Shifts in consumer preferences and broader economic/market uncertainty.
  • Execution risk on expansion and renewable-energy projects: commissioning timelines depend on stabilisation, approvals, grid availability, and other implementation factors; the Kalikanagar hybrid project has already experienced delays from weather, logistics-road rework, and transmission-line shutdown delays.
  • Forward-looking statements may diverge from actual outcomes due to known and unknown risks.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements - Strengthen the core greige fabric manufacturing business, enhance capacity utilization, improve cost efficiencies, and explore opportunities in value-added and man-made fiber-based textile products. - Unit 4 expansion commercial production targeted for December 2026. - 19.79 MW hybrid renewable-energy project commissioning targeted during 2026. - For the 9.9 MW Hybrid Power Project at Kalikanagar, Gujarat: one WTG location along with the 7.65 MW solar component expected to be commissioned by September 30, 2026; the remaining two WTG locations expected by the end of October 2026. - Management states the company is well-positioned to participate in India’s USD 100 billion textile export target by FY 2030, supported by modern manufacturing infrastructure and technological focus.

Observed performance - FY 2025-26: revenue from operations of Rs. 38,859.31 Lakhs and net profit after tax of Rs. 6,461.20 Lakhs, a year-on-year increase of approximately 60.71%. - FY 2025-26: IPO completed during the year, increasing issued equity shares to 2,66,45,295; no dividend proposed for FY 2025-26 to preserve resources for future growth plans. - Q1 FY27: revenue of Rs 100.84 Cr (+24.5% YoY), EBITDA of Rs 25.84 Cr (+50.7% YoY), PAT of Rs 16.48 Cr (+35.0% YoY), EBITDA margin of 25.63%, and basic and diluted EPS of Rs 6.18. - FY24–FY26 track record: 39.7% revenue CAGR, 49.1% EBITDA CAGR, and 65.5% PAT CAGR. - Manufacturing scale: 1,212 high-speed water-jet looms across 4 manufacturing units, 24 texturizing machines, 20 folding machines, and 9 warping machines.

Guidance versus performance - The reported FY 2025-26 revenue growth of approximately 60.71% in net profit after tax and the FY24–FY26 CAGR track record (39.7% revenue, 49.1% EBITDA, 65.5% PAT) are consistent with management’s stated expectation of double-digit revenue growth and operating leverage from the scaled manufacturing platform. - Q1 FY27 results show continued growth across revenue, EBITDA, and PAT, with EBITDA growing faster than revenue, consistent with the operating leverage narrative. - On the renewable-energy side, guidance has been revised: the Kalikanagar 9.9 MW Hybrid Power Project timeline was delayed, with the 7.65 MW solar component and one WTG now expected by September 30, 2026, and the remaining two WTG locations by end-October 2026. - The Unit 4 expansion and the 19.79 MW hybrid renewable project remain targeted for December 2026 and 2026 respectively, with no completed commissioning reported in the supplied material. - The decision not to declare a dividend for FY 2025-26 is aligned with management’s stated intent to preserve resources for future growth plans, including the capacity and renewable-energy initiatives.
