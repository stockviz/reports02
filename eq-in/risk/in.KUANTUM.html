Kuantum Papers — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Cost inflation from the West Asia conflict. Management stated that the conflict intensified cost pressures across fuel, chemicals and other raw materials, and also affected freight and logistics costs. On the Q1 FY27 call, the CFO noted that an improvement in blended NSR of approximately INR 3,400 per ton was more than offset by a cost increase of around INR 4,200 per ton on a quarter-to-quarter basis. In response to a later question, management attributed roughly 50% of the increased cost to the West Asia crisis, with the remainder linked to local raw material prices, specifically wheat straw.
  • Margin compression despite volume growth. Q1 FY27 operational income was INR 304 crores (up 36% year-on-year) on paper sales volume of 42,922 MT (up 35% year-on-year), yet EBITDA was described as broadly stable year-on-year at INR 40 crores, with a 13.2% margin, and PAT at INR 6 crores.
  • Regional raw material disadvantage. Management said Kuantum is located in a state where raw material input costs rose “a little abnormally,” affecting only operators in Punjab, and that this is where the company faces a competitive landscape in procurement. This was offered as an explanation when asked why a competitor’s EBITDA margin had increased year-on-year while Kuantum’s had decreased.
  • Import competition and the GST inverted duty structure. Management identified competitive intensity from imports as a key industry factor. On Maplitho, the segment was described as severely impacted by the inverted duty structure after the GST rate change, and management said it does not expect government rectification, stating the government is firm in its conviction. Kuantum said it has taken a conscious call to reduce, and eventually eliminate, its notebook paper foray, and that it charged customers for the GST loss incurred.
  • Seasonality. Management described Q2 (July–September) as the leanest quarter for the industry, with improvement only from October onwards as the sector prepares for the next education year.
  • Machine downtime and maintenance. PM3 was shut for a major rebuild during the quarter. Management also noted Q2 involves expansion, modernization, some machines being closed and boiler maintenance, making it “not the right time” for target margins. Separately, management noted paper machines typically run about 330 days a year due to planned downtime for consumables such as wire part, press part and clothings.
  • EC clearance pending. Asked whether EC clearance for the PM3 upgradation had been received, management said it has been filed and is awaited “very shortly.”
  • Debt and interest burden. A shareholder questioned why profits are being consumed by interest given high debt. Management acknowledged the debt burden and framed it as the cost of a growth program.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Healthy demand environment. Management said the paper industry saw healthy demand in Q1 FY27 supported by education, publishing, office consumption and broader economic activity, enabling good volumes and improved net sales realization. Demand is expected to remain supportive despite near-term volatility.
  • Capacity expansion nearing completion. The DDS Double Displacement Digester System for wood pulping was commissioned, expected to optimize pulp quality and yield and reduce utility, chemical and overall pulp costs. An advanced native starch system was commissioned on PM2 and PM3 to improve paper properties and reduce uncooked starch losses. A state-of-the-art Folio Ream Wrapping Machine was installed with integrated labeling and stacking. PM3’s major rebuild was expected to be commissioned within the month of the call, after which management said the entire plant would run at peak capacity.
  • Value-added and specialty products. Kuantum produced oil and grease resistant (OGR) paper for food wrapping on PM2. Management said it is targeting specialty grades yielding above 20% EBITDA, with incremental top-line contribution of around 5% to 6% initially, building a base this year before moving further into specialty grades. Specialty paper was stated to be just under 20% of contribution (about 18–19%), against a stated ambition of 30%. Management also cited customer demand for sustainable alternatives to plastic-based products.
  • Diminishing imports. Management observed a diminishing trend in imports, largely attributed to global shipping costs, container availability and logistics costs, with import pricing described as stable and not reducing. Imported copier and Maplitho prices were cited at USD 610–620 per ton, with volumes described as not large enough to create a negative impact.
  • Anti-dumping and anti-subsidy filings. Management said applications for anti-dumping duty and anti-subsidy duty have been filed and are being keenly looked at by the government, covering the writing and printing paper segment across all GSMs Kuantum makes (40 to 140). Management expects a five-year duration if implemented.
  • Raw material cost relief. Wheat straw prices were described as coming down, with further relief expected in the next month or so as alternate fodder materials (rice straw, corn cobs) become available around September–October. Timber prices were described as largely stable, if not reducing by about 5% to 6%.
  • Forestry and clonal propagation. The company achieved its highest ever quarterly production of 17.28 lakh clonal saplings and added almost 1,300 acres of social farm forestry, taking total plantation area to about 19,650 acres. Management targets 1 crore saplings annually within 3 to 4 years, up from about 40-odd lakh. The Chief Strategy Officer framed this as securing future wood requirements and reducing procurement cost rather than a revenue play.
  • Industry 4.0 / AI integration. Management said AI integration in production and manufacturing is a continuous process under a long-term contract with a supplier, expected to conclude by March 2028, with a targeted 4% to 5% reduction in costs.
  • Channel inventory. Management’s view was that dealer inventories are minimal and pipelines relatively empty, suggesting a potential surge in demand that could positively impact pricing.
  • Fundraise. The Board approved raising up to INR 100 crores via unlisted, unrated, senior, secured, non-convertible debentures on a private placement basis, in one or more series/tranches, within approved borrowing limits.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and input cost volatility. Management said input costs are on a rise and have not bottomed out, while expressing the view that pricing has bottomed out. Chemical prices were described as higher than Q4 levels but having stabilized, with no further rise expected.
  • Pricing risk. Management stated there is no direct correlation between increased costs and increased selling price. The earlier revenue guidance was revised downward because expected sales realizations did not hold at anticipated levels.
  • Import and trade policy uncertainty. The outcome of ADD/CVD filings is not assured; management said it is “very hopeful” of positive feedback. The GST inverted duty structure on notebook paper was described as unlikely to be rectified.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk. Benefits from the PM3 rebuild, DDS digester, starch system and AI integration depend on commissioning, syncing of operations and realization of expected efficiencies. EC clearance for the PM3 upgradation was still awaited at the time of the call.
  • Leverage and interest cost. Peak debt was guided at about INR 760–770 crores, with repayments of about INR 170–175 crores over the next 2 to 3 years and a target of under INR 300 crores in three years. Management noted the possibility of prepayment if profits exceed expectations.
  • Segment demand growth. A questioner cited a CRISIL report indicating writing and paper demand growing only 1% to 3% CAGR up to FY30, versus 6% to 8% for packaging and specialty. Management acknowledged a strategic move toward specialty but said the writing and printing segment cannot be written off given its established market position.
  • Raw material availability. Management said sourcing is primarily within Punjab or neighboring states and that availability is not an issue, with the caveat of pricing vagaries.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Revenue guidance revised downward. On the prior call, management had guided FY27 revenue of INR 1,400–1,500 crores. On the Q1 FY27 call, management said FY27 would be “INR 1,300 crores plus,” with INR 1,400–1,500 crores now expected “next year, for sure.” Asked to explain the gap, management attributed it to sales realizations running below expectations and EBITDA margins being strained by increased operating costs due to the West Asia crisis, describing the revised figures as conservative. Management added that it still stands by the earlier INR 1,600–1,700 crores aspiration for FY28, and separately indicated a conservative FY29–FY30 figure of about INR 1,500 crores top line with INR 300–350 crores EBITDA, potentially INR 1,600–1,650 crores if pricing averages about INR 75,000 per ton.

EBITDA margin guidance. Management guided to “at least between 16% to 18%” by year-end, with Q3 onwards identified as the period when such margins should be visible, given Q2 expansion, modernization and boiler maintenance. This compares with the reported Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 13.2%. Management noted that adding other income (driven by sale of plant and scrap sales) to EBITDA would give a margin of 14.4%, and characterized other income as operational in nature.

Realization guidance. Management said it is maintaining the INR 69,000–71,000 per ton realization indicated in the prior quarter, and that current levels are about INR 68,000–69,000 per ton, with an expectation of reaching INR 72,000–75,000 per ton in the next 4 to 6 months. Management disputed a questioner’s characterization of flat year-on-year pricing, stating NSR rose about INR 3,400 per ton quarter-on-quarter and about INR 4,000 per ton year-on-year (described elsewhere as 5%+ quarter-on-quarter and 7%+ year-on-year).

Volume and capacity. Management said that post commissioning of the fourth machine (PM3), the entire plant would run at peak capacity, with FY27 revenue of INR 1,300 crores plus. Pulp capacity was stated at about 200 tons each of agro and wood, to increase to about 410–415 tons per day, with 150–180 tons of filler and 40–50 tons per day of imported pulp used in paper manufacturing. The raw material mix was described as approximately 50% agro pulp and 50% wood pulp.

Debt guidance. Peak debt guided at about INR 760–770 crores, with annual reduction of about INR 175 crores and a target of under INR 300 crores within three years. Management indicated EBITDA of close to INR 300 crores at 18%–20% margins on INR 1,400–1,500 crores revenue would be sufficient to cover debt and interest repayment.

Specialty mix guidance. Management targets 30% specialty contribution, against the current 18–19%, with incremental top-line contribution of 5% to 6% initially and specialty-grade EBITDA targeted above 20%.

Clonal propagation guidance. Target of 1 crore saplings annually within 3 to 4 years, versus the current level of about 40-odd lakh saplings per year, with the stated purpose of securing wood supply and reducing procurement cost rather than generating revenue.

AI/Industry 4.0 guidance. Cost reduction of 4% to 5% targeted, with the exercise expected to conclude by March 2028.

Observed performance versus guidance. Q1 FY27 operational income of INR 304 crores and 42,922 MT volume represented 36% and 35% year-on-year growth respectively, which management partly attributed to a notional loss of production in the prior-year Q1 due to a machine shutdown for upgradation. Sequentially, production increased slightly versus Q4 FY26 on improved machine efficiency. Despite the volume and NSR gains, EBITDA margin of 13.2% remained below the 16%–18% year-end target, with management pointing to cost inflation and the timing of expansion-related shutdowns.
