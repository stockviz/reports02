Sula Vineyards (SULA) – Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure from grape mix. In Q1 FY27, gross margins fell 550 bps, driven by a higher mix of wine grapes versus table grapes (approximately 150 bps impact on blended grape cost) and an adverse market mix with a lower share of core markets in Maharashtra and Karnataka. Management describes the higher blended grape cost as temporary, expected to subside from Q4 FY27 and fully normalize from Q1 FY28 as the wine and table grape mix rebalances in Harvest 2027.

Soft core markets. Karnataka continued to remain soft in Q1 FY27. The decline in the Economy & Popular portfolio partially offset Own Brands revenue performance.

Flat wine tourism footfalls. Visitor footfalls were flat year-on-year in Q1 FY27 due to a longer summer and scorching heat, even as room revenue and spend per guest rose.

Profitability decline. Q1 FY27 EBITDA was INR 16.6 Cr, down 9.4% YoY, with EBITDA margin at 14.7% (down 197 bps). Gross Profit was INR 77.3 Cr, down 4.7% YoY. Cost actions reduced Opex by 3% YoY, which helped limit the EBITDA impact.

Profitability risk on director remuneration. The company notes the possibility of having no profits or inadequate profits in FY 26-27, FY 27-28, and FY 28-29, which would result in paying proposed commission remuneration as minimum remuneration under Schedule V of the Act.

Seasonality. Revenue, costs, and profits do not accrue evenly over the year, causing quarterly results to vary.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Elite & Premium momentum. Elite & Premium sales grew 6% YoY in Q1 FY27, led by strong double-digit growth in The Source, RASA, and Sula Merlot. Elite & Premium share rose 310 bps YoY to 78%.

Wine tourism growth. Wine Tourism revenue was INR 15.5 Cr in Q1 FY27, up 12% YoY, driven by higher room revenue following the launch of The Haven (the third resort) and higher spend per guest. Room revenue grew 21% YoY.

Regional bright spots. Telangana grew more than 50% YoY, while Haryana, Chandigarh, CSD, and Exports delivered strong double-digit growth.

Chandon estate acquisition. Sula completed the acquisition of the 19-acre Chandon Estate, renamed Domaine Rāsā, for INR 20 crore in Q1 FY27. The tasting room, bottle shop, and banquet facilities have commenced operations, with the intent to create another landmark wine tourism destination in Nashik.

Project pipeline. The amphitheatre expansion at the flagship Nashik campus was completed; the wine shop at Domaine Dindori is scheduled to open later in August 2026; construction of the events pavilion is progressing with launch targeted ahead of the festive season; winery operations at Domaine Rāsā are upcoming.

Leadership addition. The appointment of Mr. Chaitanya Kamal Rathi as a Non-Executive Non-Independent Director is expected to strengthen strategic direction, operational excellence, overall performance, long-term growth, and value creation, given his experience in scaling high-growth businesses and the wine industry.

Asset monetization. The company sold its office property in Mumbai during the quarter for INR 27.00 crores, recognizing a profit of INR 1.79 crores in other income.

3. Key Risks

  • Grape cost and mix risk: Higher blended grape cost from a greater wine grape versus table grape mix, with normalization dependent on Harvest 2027 rebalancing.
  • Market concentration/softness: Continued softness in Karnataka and adverse market mix with lower share of core markets (Maharashtra and Karnataka).
  • Demand/weather sensitivity: Wine tourism footfalls vulnerable to weather conditions such as prolonged summer and extreme heat.
  • Profitability risk on remuneration: Possibility of no or inadequate profits in FY 26-27, FY 27-28, and FY 28-29, triggering minimum remuneration under Schedule V.
  • Seasonality: Uneven accrual of revenue, costs, and profits across the year causing quarterly variability.
  • Portfolio mix risk: Decline in the Economy & Popular portfolio offsetting Own Brands growth.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Karnataka recovery. Management expects Karnataka to turn a corner in H2 FY27. Observed: Karnataka continued to remain soft in Q1 FY27.

Grape cost normalization. Management states the higher blended grape cost is temporary, subsiding from Q4 FY27 and completely normalizing from Q1 FY28 as the wine and table grape mix rebalances in Harvest 2027, supporting improved profitability in H2 FY27. Observed: Q1 FY27 gross margins were lower by 550 bps with a ~150 bps impact from blended grape cost.

Growth strategy. Management’s growth strategy aims at accelerating earnings growth over the next 3 years (FY26–FY29) with improved EBITDA margins and capital efficiency. Observed: Q1 FY27 Net Revenue from Operations was INR 112.9 Cr (up 3.0% YoY), EBITDA was INR 16.6 Cr (down 9.4% YoY), and EBITDA margin was 14.7% (down 197 bps).

Wine tourism trajectory. Management highlights continued double-digit growth in Wine Tourism. Observed: Wine Tourism revenue grew 12.3% YoY in Q1 FY27, with room revenue up 21% YoY.

Director contribution. Management anticipates Mr. Rathi’s industry experience and business acumen will provide valuable guidance in driving sustainable growth and long-term value creation. Observed: The appointment and commission resolutions are subject to postal ballot approval, with voting from 8th September 2026 to 7th October 2026.

Investor engagement. The company will participate in the DAM Capital Alcoholic Beverages Conference on 20th August 2026 in Mumbai, meeting multiple institutional investors across group and one-to-one sessions.
