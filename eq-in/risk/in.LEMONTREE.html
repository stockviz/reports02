Lemon Tree Hotels — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Q1 FY27 demand softness. Management described Q1 FY27 as an aberration, attributing softer demand to the West Asia conflict, which reduced inbound traffic and, as a second-order effect, domestic travel. Corporate demand weakened in high-density CBD locations, with Mumbai and Gurgaon unable to compensate for the decline in corporate traffic, unlike Delhi, Hyderabad and Bengaluru. Mumbai was further affected by roughly 2,000 new rooms opening near the airport over the preceding two years, supply that is still being absorbed.

Margin pressure. Net EBITDA margin for Q1 FY27 stood at 43.8%, 99 basis points lower than 44.8% in Q1 FY26. This was attributed to the Provision for Stock Appreciation Rights and the loss of input credit in the GST levied, which increased expenses by 3.1% of total revenue in Q1 FY27 versus zero in the prior-year quarter. Management noted GST impact is running at roughly 2% of overall revenue.

ARR dilution from tactical strategy. Gross ARR rose only 2% year-on-year to Rs. 6,361 despite 19% RevPAR growth in the Keys portfolio. Management explained that in response to anticipated corporate softness from early March/April, the company pivoted to a volume-based retail strategy, filling rooms through retail channels at lower net ARR due to commissions. This was described as temporary and specific to Q1.

Renovation drag. Renovation spend was Rs. 9.8 crore in Q1 FY27, about 2.25%–2.3% of revenue, versus a normal run rate of roughly 1%–1.2%. The company spent Rs. 15 crore on renovation in the prior-year period. Renovation has been ongoing for three years and is expected to taper.

Portfolio attrition. One management contract (Tarudhan Valley, 70 rooms) was mutually terminated, and a Keys-inherited property (Nestor Hotel, 130 rooms) was removed from inventory and taken to NCLT; the latter was not generating fees. Management noted that as signings accelerate, some terminations due to brand-standard non-adherence should be expected, and net room additions are the relevant metric.

Regulatory/tax matter. Material subsidiary Berggruen Hotels Private Limited received a GST intimation in Form GST DRC-01A from the Office of the Deputy Commissioner of Commercial Taxes, Bengaluru, ascertaining tax of Rs. 2,94,01,042, interest of Rs. 2,31,26,135 and penalty of Rs. 37,65,546 — totaling Rs. 5,62,92,723 — for FY 2022-23. Berggruen intends to file its response/objections in Part B within the prescribed timeline of September 21, 2026, and will take further legal recourse as advised if a Show Cause Notice or adjudication order is issued.

Project approval and gestation risk. Aurika Nehru Place awaits final approvals, with expected capital deployment to be announced in due course and an expected opening on or after FY 2030. Aurika Varanasi has an expected opening in FY29. Aurika Shillong is expected to open in H2 FY28.

Broader industry risks cited. Land cost and scarcity, regulatory complexity, capital intensity, brand entrenchment, financing constraints, seasonality-driven occupancy and RevPAR fluctuation, geopolitical and external disruptions, rising crude oil prices increasing airfare inflation, interest rate volatility, elevated working capital requirements, long project gestation periods, and algorithm-led travel discovery.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Asset-light expansion. In Q1 FY27, Lemon Tree opened 6 managed and franchised hotels with 334 rooms (signed on average about 30 months earlier) and signed 13 managed and franchised hotels with 1,020 rooms — over 3x the inventory opened. Combined operational and pipeline inventory stands at 23,381 rooms across 279 hotels in 170+ cities, of which 135 hotels (11,946 rooms) across 80-plus cities are operational. The managed/franchised pipeline is approximately 13,300 rooms.

Fee income growth. Fees from management and franchise contracts from third-party owned hotels stood at Rs. 22.8 crore in Q1 FY27, up 42% year-on-year. Fees from Fleur Hotels stood at Rs. 22.6 crore, up 6%. Total management fee stood at Rs. 45.4 crore, up 21%. Management described a flywheel effect: signings of 2,000 rooms three years ago, 3,500 rooms two years ago and nearly 5,000 rooms last year translate into openings and fee income roughly three years out. Management indicated that what was signed in 2023 opened this year, what was signed in 2024 will open at the end of this year, and what was signed in 2025 will open a year after that.

Network revenue. Network revenue for the quarter grew 16% year-on-year to Rs. 576 crore, with owned hotels contributing Rs. 320 crore and managed and franchised hotels contributing Rs. 256 crore.

Keys portfolio turnaround. Keys portfolio RevPAR in Q1 FY27 was up 19% year-on-year to Rs. 2,885, on a 350-basis-point improvement in occupancy to 67% and 13% growth in average room rate to Rs. 4,311. Renovation was two-thirds complete as on June 30, 2026, with double-digit growth across all locations. Management targets Rs. 60 crore EBITDA from Keys, with revenue of about Rs. 115–120 crore and Rs. 6.5 lakh EBITDA per key.

Demerger and Warburg Pincus backing. A Composite Scheme of Arrangement would separate Lemon Tree into a pure-play asset-light brand management company and Fleur Hotels into a dedicated hotel ownership and development company. Warburg Pincus has a primary equity line of Rs. 960 crore (USD 100 million for 10% primary) to fund growth, at a valuation fixed at USD 1 billion. Management stated Warburg must deploy the Rs. 960 crore in Fleur before listing. The demerger is expected to complete in the first half of calendar year 2027, with listing thereafter; management conservatively indicated the late second half of next year given approval dependencies (SEBI, shareholder vote, creditors’ approval, GST approval).

Fleur expansion. Fleur currently operates a shade under 6,000 rooms and is building 850–900 rooms. Management is pursuing approximately 2,500 rooms, whether operating or under development, with a mix of brownfield, operating assets at 6–8 multiples, deep-demand greenfield assets, and long-leased buildings. Focus markets are the top 6–7 Indian cities, markets 3–5 hours from India, and upcoming leisure/temple tourism locations. Management stated Fleur will increase by over 50% in rooms by FY30, with more in revenue as incremental rooms are largely Aurika.

Aurika pipeline. Aurika Shimla (owned, 90 rooms) has approximately Rs. 108 crore deployed as on June 30, 2026, with finishing work and operational licenses in final stage and expected to open shortly. Aurika Shillong (leased, 165 rooms) has approximately Rs. 33 crore deployed, expected opening H2 FY28. Aurika Varanasi (47-room leased heritage) work has begun, expected opening FY29. Aurika Nehru Place (5-star hotel on 2.256 acres licensed from DDA) has an expected opening on or after FY 2030; the Letter of Award validity was extended by one year to August 14, 2027.

Joint venture for Aurika Shillong. Carnation Hotels Private Limited (wholly owned subsidiary) and RJ Corp Limited executed a Joint Venture Agreement in respect of Arum Hotels Private Limited, the SPV for the Aurika Shillong project. Carnation holds 51% and RJ Corp 49%, with RJ Corp subscribing to 47,65,394 fresh equity shares of face value Rs. 10 each within 7 days from the effective date of the JVA. Arum ceases to be a step-down wholly owned subsidiary and continues as a step-down subsidiary.

Punjab and Tier-3 expansion. Lemon Tree opened Keys Lite by Lemon Tree Hotels, Moga — its fourth operational hotel in Punjab — with 33 rooms and suites, a multi-cuisine restaurant (Keys Café), a fitness center, and 24-hour in-room dining, operated under a franchise agreement. Nine more properties are in the pipeline in Punjab. The group operates 130+ hotels across 80+ cities with a pipeline of 140+ upcoming properties, totaling 270+ properties operational and upcoming. Management highlighted focus on India’s fast-growing Tier-3 markets.

International expansion. Three international destinations are operational — Nepal, Bhutan and Dubai — with fresh Nepal signings in the quarter. Management’s medium-term interest is in markets preferably 3 hours flying time from India where large numbers of Indians travel, citing UAE, Nepal, Thailand (Bangkok, Phuket), Maldives and South India, with the intent to monetize the loyalty program (2.5 million members; 45% of demand is repeat, of which only half is in the loyalty program).

Industry tailwinds cited. Strong domestic tourism growth, increasing disposable incomes, expanding infrastructure (airports, highways, Vande Bharat rail, UDAN routes), rise in faith-based and event-led tourism (such as Maha Kumbh and live events), growth in destination weddings, decentralising MICE demand, AI-driven travel planning, and expansion in Tier II to IV markets.

3. Key Risks

  • Demand volatility from external disruptions. The West Asia conflict reduced inbound traffic and, as a second-order effect, domestic corporate travel in Q1 FY27, with Mumbai and Gurgaon unable to compensate.
  • Supply absorption. Approximately 2,000 new rooms opened in the Mumbai airport micro-market over two years, creating a temporary supply-demand mismatch.
  • GST input credit loss. The loss of input credit increased expenses by 3.1% of total revenue in Q1 FY27 versus zero in the prior-year quarter; management expects GST impact to run at roughly 2% of revenue and to reduce progressively as more rooms are sold above Rs. 7,500, though it will never be zero.
  • Renovation execution and cost. Renovation is a multi-year program with high-value renovations (Rs. 10–12 lakh per key) and smaller refurbs (Rs. 2.5–3 lakh per room); timing and owner agreement (particularly for managed hotels such as Rishikesh) affect execution.
  • Project approval and timeline risk. Aurika Nehru Place awaits final approvals with opening on or after FY 2030; the LOA validity was extended to August 14, 2027, subject to necessary compliances on the part of DDA.
  • Contract attrition. Management contracts may be terminated for brand-standard non-adherence; net room additions differ from gross openings.
  • Tax litigation. The Berggruen GST intimation for FY 2022-23 totaling Rs. 5,62,92,723; the company intends to respond by September 21, 2026 and pursue legal recourse if a Show Cause Notice or adjudication order is issued.
  • Debt and capital deployment. Management stated debt may briefly cross 2x existing EBITDA when building six large hotels, with a long-term plan for Fleur debt-to-EBITDA to hover around 2x. Group debt was stated at Rs. 1,275 crore (excluding about Rs. 200 crore cash) against EBITDA of Rs. 700 crore.
  • Demerger approval risk. Completion depends on SEBI, shareholder vote, creditors’ approval and GST approval, with limited control over timelines.
  • Industry risks. Land cost and scarcity, regulatory complexity, capital intensity, brand entrenchment, financing constraints, seasonality, geopolitical and external disruptions, rising crude oil prices increasing airfare inflation, interest rate volatility, elevated working capital requirements, long project gestation periods, and algorithm-led travel discovery.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Q1 FY27 margin versus prior guidance. Management had previously guided to a larger margin decline for the year. Observed Q1 FY27 net EBITDA margin was 43.8%, down 99 basis points year-on-year, which management characterized as better than expected. The decline was attributed to the Provision for Stock Appreciation Rights and GST input credit loss (3.1% of total revenue versus zero).

FY27 margin expectation. When asked about full-year FY27 net EBITDA margin, management stated it would be “well better” than the 47% suggested by the analyst, and that FY27 would not be 47% but better. For FY28, management stated that if the company does not do 50%, it would have underperformed. Management also stated there is no reason net EBITDA margin should be less than 50%, citing performing hotel-level EBITDA margins in the late 50s, tapering renovation, and GST mitigation through higher ARR.

FY27 openings guidance. Management had targeted opening 2,000 keys in FY27 (referenced to Slide 63 of the presentation). Q1 FY27 openings were 334 keys. Management stated it is “pretty confident to be around the 2,000 Keys mark,” acknowledging some slippage is normal in the business.

Keys guidance. Management had targeted Keys to achieve Red Fox ARRs (approximately Rs. 4,500) and stated Keys is close to that now. Keys ARR in Q1 FY27 was Rs. 4,311. Management reiterated the target of Rs. 60 crore EBITDA from Keys, with Keys expected to operate at full performance next year, with occupancies close to the Lemon Tree average and continued ARR improvement.

Aurika Mumbai pricing guidance. A couple of quarters prior, management had spoken about significant pricing power for Aurika Mumbai in FY27 given stabilized occupancies. Following the West Asia crisis and Mumbai supply additions, management stated Q1 was an aberration, Q2 will be better, and the focus is now on pricing Aurika, with RevPAR improvement expected going forward. Management declined to give specific RevPAR growth guidance, stating it would become “very guidance oriented.”

Renovation completion guidance. Management had previously indicated a tentative timeline of October for completing the overall renovation exercise. In the Q1 FY27 call, management stated 300 rooms were renovated in Q1, a similar or slightly higher number expected in Q2, with Q3 and Q4 shifting to refurbs and quicker turnarounds. Management stated the full renovation of main hotels and refurbishment of many Keys and other hotels will be finished in FY27, with FY28 reverting to norm (roughly 1.2% of revenue on renovation).

Demerger timeline guidance. Management had previously indicated calendar year 2027 for completion. In the Q1 FY27 call, management stated the first half of calendar year 2027 should see completion and listing of Fleur, but conservatively, given limited control over approvals, it could be the late second half of next year.

Expense reduction guidance. Management anticipates that expense heads impacted by renovation, technology, and GST changes will reduce to approximately 3.7% of revenue by FY 2027-28, leading to EBITDA margin expansion.

Industry forecast cited. For CY2026, industry RevPAR is forecasted to reach approximately Rs. 5,988 with occupancy at approximately 67% and ARR at approximately Rs. 8,931. Brand signings in CY2026 are estimated to accelerate to approximately 690 hotels with 76,000 keys, with scheduled openings projected at approximately 200 hotels with approximately 15,620 keys, increasingly tilted toward Tier II–IV markets and leisure destinations.

Observed FY 2025-26 performance. In FY 2025-26, revenue grew 13% year-on-year to Rs. 1,452.7 crore, net EBITDA increased 10% to Rs. 699.3 crore, and PAT expanded 19% to Rs. 288.3 crore. Gross ARR reached Rs. 6,875 with occupancy at 73.5% for owned/leased hotels. Total debt decreased to Rs. 1,500 crore. The company surpassed its CY 2028 expansion target early, reaching a portfolio of 268 hotels with 22,581 operational and signed pipeline rooms.

Q1 FY27 observed performance. Total revenue stood at Rs. 346.8 crore (up 9% year-on-year), net EBITDA at Rs. 151.9 crore (up 7%), net EBITDA adjusted for GST impact and provision for stock appreciation rights at Rs. 162.5 crore (up 14% versus Rs. 142.1 crore), PAT grew 19% to Rs. 57.3 crore, and cash profit grew 17% to Rs. 96 crore. Gross ARR was Rs. 6,361 (up 2%) and occupancy was 75.7% (up 314 basis points). Gross debt as on June 30, 2026 stood at Rs. 1,475 crore, down 11% from Rs. 1,657.9 crore a year ago, with cost of debt reduced to 7.48%, down 53 basis points.

Pro forma financials upon demerger. For Lemon Tree on a pro forma basis in Q1 FY27, revenue was Rs. 65.7 crore versus Rs. 54 crore (up 22%), net EBITDA before provision for stock appreciation rights was Rs. 38.1 crore versus Rs. 29.3 crore, net EBITDA margin improved to 58.1% from 54.2% (up 383 basis points), PBT after share of profit of associates was Rs. 46.3 crore versus Rs. 38.4 crore (up 21%), PAT was Rs. 33.6 crore versus Rs. 27.5 crore (up 22%), and cash profit was Rs. 40 crore versus Rs. 30.5 crore (up 31%). For Fleur on a pro forma basis, revenue was Rs. 311.4 crore versus Rs. 292 crore (up 7%), net EBITDA without GST impact was Rs. 125.1 crore versus Rs. 114 crore (up 10%) with margin of 40.2% versus 39.1%, PBT was Rs. 46.5 crore versus Rs. 35.5 crore (up 31%), PAT was Rs. 34.7 crore versus Rs. 28.3 crore (up 23%), and cash profit was Rs. 68.4 crore versus Rs. 61 crore (up 12%).

Leadership transition. Mr. Patanjali Govind Keswani, appointed Chairman and Executive Director for 18 months effective October 1, 2025, completes his current tenure on March 31, 2027. The Board approved his appointment as Chairman and Non-Executive Director effective April 1, 2027, subject to shareholder approval, to facilitate a succession plan and continuity of leadership. He would be liable to retire by rotation.

Broker Narrative

The first report built a Buy case on a favourable industry demand-supply gap, ~2,800 key additions, and Aurika Mumbai Airport as an FY25E earnings driver, while treating cost inflation and revamp costs as manageable. The last report shifted to margin repair—tapering renovation spends, pricing actions to offset GST, and a ~50% EBITDA margin target—and to corporate actions: the Fleur demerger with a INR9.6bn Warburg Pincus infusion plus asset-light management-fee growth. Persistent themes were room expansion and renovation-led margin pressure; the changed emphasis was from organic debt reduction and Mumbai Airport-led earnings to external capital and asset-light contracts.

Fears that came true

  • The feared discretionary demand slowdown correlated with repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes from Sep-2023 onwards and the last report’s admission that Middle East geo-political tensions impacted the quarter.
  • Cost inflation materialized as elevated renovation spends, technology investments, GST input-credit loss, and stock-appreciation-rights provisions that weighed on EBITDA margins.
  • The expected FY24E margin hit from the Keys revamp materialized as renovation intensity remained elevated and was still expected to taper only by FY27E/FY28E.

Optimism that failed

  • The anticipated industry demand CAGR tailwind did not prevent sustained negative actual returns—e.g., -15.7%, -26.3%, -25.4%, -21.8%—so the demand-supply gap did not translate into the hoped-for re-rating.
  • The 669-key Aurika Mumbai Airport was projected as a key FY25E earnings driver, but FY25 actual outcomes were consistently negative (e.g., -25.4%, -21.5%, -18.5%), so that earnings driver did not materialize in returns.
