GIC Re: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Underwriting losses persist. GIC Re continues to report underwriting losses across every period shown: from ₹(5,488) Cr in FY20-21 narrowing to ₹(1,763) Cr in FY25-26, and ₹(724) Cr in Q1 FY26-27. The combined ratio, while improving steadily (112.0% in FY20-21 to 106.0% in FY25-26 and 104.9% in Q1 FY26-27), remains above 100%, meaning the core underwriting book is still loss-making and profitability depends on investment income.

Commission ratio pressure. Net commission percentage on net premium rose from 16.7% in FY22-23 to 19.6% in FY25-26, though it moderated to 18.8% in Q1 FY26-27. Management notes commission ratios are expected to remain stable.

Competitive and structural pressures. The Indian reinsurance framework now includes 11 Foreign Reinsurance Branches (FRBs), which booked ₹27,816 Cr of gross reinsurance premium income on Indian business in FY24-25, plus about 291 Cross Border Reinsurers participating in the Indian market. GIC Re’s India market share is cited at ~52% (FY24-25).

Low penetration and retention dynamics. India’s non-life insurance penetration is described as approximately 23% of the global average in 2025, and the industry operates under a “cash before cover” model. Retention ratio dynamics of the general insurance industry are flagged as a relevant factor.

Regulatory/capital transition. The presentation notes that capital requirements post Risk Based Capital implementation could create solvency pressures on Indian insurers, which could lead to higher cessions — framed as an opportunity for reinsurance market size, but also a shifting regulatory landscape.

Tax-related shareholder friction. The TDS communication notes that non-linkage of PAN with Aadhaar results in PAN being deemed invalid/inoperative and tax deducted at 20% under Section 397(2). Shareholders holding shares under multiple accounts with a single PAN face the higher of the applicable tax rates on their entire holding. Non-resident shareholders must submit extensive documentation (TRC, Form 41, no-PE declaration, beneficial ownership declaration) by 7 September 2026, and the Corporation states it is not obligated to apply beneficial DTAA rates at the time of deduction.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Improving profitability trend. Profit After Tax rose from ₹6,497 Cr (FY23-24) to ₹6,701 Cr (FY24-25) to ₹8,392 Cr (FY25-26), with ₹1,922 Cr in Q1 FY26-27. Profit Before Tax improved from ₹7,878 Cr to ₹10,793 Cr over the same two-year span.

Strengthening solvency. Solvency ratio improved from 325% (FY23-24) to 370% (FY24-25) to 421% (FY25-26), reaching 432% in Q1 FY26-27.

Improving claims experience. Incurred claims ratio (on earned premium) declined from 92.3% (FY23-24) to 88.4% (FY24-25) to 85.4% (FY25-26), and 85.0% in Q1 FY26-27.

Growing premium base. Gross Written Premium grew from ₹37,182 Cr (FY23-24) to ₹41,154 Cr (FY24-25) to ₹44,007 Cr (FY25-26), with ₹13,475 Cr in Q1 FY26-27.

Market growth opportunity. India’s general insurance market is estimated to grow at a CAGR of 9.9% during 2021-2026, and reinsurance premiums in India are expected to touch ₹99,000 Cr by 2025-26. GIC Re is expected to maintain market leadership on the strength of its financials, capacity, and expertise.

Global reinsurance market expansion. The global reinsurance market size is expected to grow from ~USD 642 Billion in 2023 to ~USD 2001 Billion in 2034, a robust CAGR of 11%. A.M. Best maintained its Positive outlook on the global reinsurance sector, and hard reinsurance market conditions are expected to continue with potential for further price increases.

Diversification avenues. Growth is expected in Surety bonds, Cyber risk covers, and Parametric covers. GIC Re has begun offering parametric ART covers through its Emerging Solutions initiative. The company plans geographical diversification post credit rating upgrade and intends to leverage its Afro-Asian brand equity.

Investment portfolio strength. As on 31.03.2026, 99.13% of debt instruments were in Sovereign and AAA rated bonds. Investment income grew from ₹11,620 Cr (FY23-24) to ₹12,773 Cr (FY24-25) to ₹13,089 Cr (FY25-26), with ₹3,266 Cr in Q1 FY26-27. 50.53% of the debt portfolio matures in more than 5 years.

Structural advantages. GIC Re is the only Indian reinsurer registered with the regulatory authority and listed on Indian stock exchanges, receives obligatory cessions on every policy by domestic general insurers, and provides reinsurance support to 59 direct general and life insurance companies in India. It has a presence in around 137 countries, a low management expense ratio at 1%, and a credit rating of A- Excellent from AM Best.

3. Key Risks

Catastrophe and climate exposure. The company has started building catastrophe reserves for climate change to better manage volatility. Reinsurers are restricting cover to named perils only on property portfolios, reducing attritional loss expectations. Risk management models such as Moody’s RMS or AIR Worldwide are used for exposure management.

Credit and counterparty risk. The investment portfolio is concentrated in high-quality sovereign and AAA bonds to eliminate credit risk. Retrocession counterparties are described as well-rated.

Underwriting and pricing risk. Management identifies continued focus on underwriting profitability through class-specific evaluation and weeding out contracts with inadequate pricing, and incentivization based on individual company/contract performance.

Regulatory and tax compliance risk. The TDS communication sets a hard deadline of 5:00 PM on 7 September 2026 for submission of documents, with no communication on tax determination entertained after that date. The Corporation states no claim shall lie against it for taxes deducted at higher rates due to incomplete documentation.

Forward-looking statement risk. The presentation’s disclaimer notes that forward-looking statements are based on assumptions and expectations that may not be accurate, and actual results could differ materially.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Combined ratio. Management states it is focused on improving net incurred claims and commission ratios, with commission ratios expected to remain stable. Observed: combined ratio improved from 111.8% (FY23-24) to 108.8% (FY24-25) to 106.0% (FY25-26) and 104.9% in Q1 FY26-27 — consistent with the stated direction, though still above 100%.

Claims ratio. Management states focus on reducing attritional losses. Observed: incurred claims ratio fell from 92.3% to 88.4% to 85.4% to 85.0% across the same periods — consistent with the stated focus.

Commission ratio. Management states commission ratios are expected to remain stable. Observed: net commission percentage moved from 18.4% (FY23-24) to 19.5% (FY24-25) to 19.6% (FY25-26), then 18.8% in Q1 FY26-27 — broadly stable with a modest uptick over two years before easing in Q1.

Credit rating. Management states it will strive to improve its rating by continued focus on balance sheet strength, operating performance, market position, and enterprise risk management. Observed: the company holds A- Excellent from AM Best; the presentation references a “credit rating upgrade” as a basis for planned geographical diversification.

Market share. Management states it expects to sustain and maintain market share, supported by potential higher cessions from Indian insurers facing Risk Based Capital solvency pressures. Observed: ~52% India market share (FY24-25) is cited.

Capital and growth. Management states significant net worth will fuel growth and resilience. Observed: net worth (excluding FVC) and solvency ratio both improved through FY25-26 and into Q1 FY26-27, with solvency at 432%.

Dividend. The Board recommended a dividend of ₹13.25 per equity share (nominal value ₹5) for FY 2025-26 at its meeting on 26 May 2026, subject to shareholder approval at the 54th AGM. The record date is Friday, 4 September 2026, with payment within 30 days of the AGM. The AGM is scheduled for Tuesday, 22 September 2026 at 11:00 a.m. IST via video conference.
