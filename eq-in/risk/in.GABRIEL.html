Gabriel India: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Segment performance divergence. In Q1 FY27, Gabriel’s Passenger Vehicle sales grew 5.5% YoY, materially lagging the PV industry’s 16.8% YoY growth. The company attributes PV traction mainly to small cars, indicating underperformance relative to the broader PV market. Commercial Vehicle sales grew 13.3% YoY, slightly below the CV industry’s 15.2% YoY growth.

JV/associate margin pressure. Anand CY Myutec Automotive saw EBITDA margin decline to 10.2% in FY26 from 12.0% in FY25, with PAT margin falling to 3.0% from 5.8% and RoE dropping to 9.8% from 19.3%. Henkel Anand India also saw margin contraction in FY26 (EBITDA margin 21.5% vs 25.6% in FY25; PAT margin 14.8% vs 18.1%), alongside a rise in debt to ₹23 crore from ₹6 crore.

Balance sheet gearing. Non-current liabilities rose sharply to ₹1,283 Mn at Mar-26 from ₹350 Mn at Mar-25. Debt of Anchemco India Pvt Ltd’s business transferred to the Company stands at ₹844 Mn as on June 2026. Liquidity declined to ₹2,536 Mn at end-Jun-26 from ₹2,974 Mn at end-Mar-26.

Regulatory/compliance observations. NSE raised a deficiency regarding the May 27, 2026 Board Meeting outcome, citing non-submission of segment details and discrepancies in the Consolidated Cash Flow Statement figures between XBRL and PDF filings. The company clarified it operates as a single segment under Ind AS 108 and uploaded revised XBRL.

Event cancellation. The Non-Deal Roadshow scheduled for September 15, 2026 was cancelled due to exigencies.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong 2W/3W momentum. The 2W/3W segment (including aftermarket) contributed 64% of total sales with 32% market share. GIL sales grew 21% YoY, driven by strong demand from key customers, against 2W industry production growth of 22.8% YoY and 3W industry growth of 39.1% YoY. New SOPs commenced with Bajaj Auto, Piaggio, M&M 2W, and Royal Enfield, with LOIs from TVSM, Suzuki, HMSI, and M&M 2W.

EV leadership. GIL holds 57% E2W market share in Q1 FY27, with E2W-E3W contributing 10% to 2W/3W segment revenues. The company is well-entrenched among emerging EV players including Ather, TVS, and Mahindra Electric.

Utility vehicle strength. Utility vehicles account for 66% of GIL’s PV business, with GIL holding a 66% share of business in total utility vehicle sales. SOP commenced for FSD supplies for Sierra EV.

CV and railways. 88% market share in CV (including aftermarket and railways), with SOP commenced for multiple suspension programs of Ashok Leyland and rising demand from cabin dampers.

Aftermarket expansion. 26 SKUs launched in Q1 FY27, focus on B & C class towns, and export growth focus on Latin American, Australian, and African markets with new geographies added in Latin America.

Autonomous driving and electronics. Growing demand for ADAS and automotive electronic products, with increasing importance of autonomous driving technologies as India’s automotive industry advances toward smarter and safer mobility.

Strategic acquisitions and partnerships. Gabriel is acquiring a 30% less one share stake in HL Klemove India Private Limited for US$98.44 million (approximately ₹935 crore), expected to foster deeper OEM engagement and expand participation in future mobility solutions. The company also holds stakes in ACYM (76%), IGSS, GEEC, JGAIPL, DAIPL, and HAIPL.

R&D and innovation. Gabriel Tech Centre has filed 100 patents with 34 granted. R&D centers at Hosur and Chakan are DSIR-recognized. Key focus areas include product reliability, product technology, and product know-how under the “3 Pillars” framework.

Awards. Q1 FY27 accolades include Excellence in Delivery Award from Ather Energy, Innovation Award at Eurobike 2026, Special Award from Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, and Excellence Award from Honda Spare Parts Division.

3. Key Risks

  • Customer concentration: Top customers in 2W/3W include TVS, HMSI, and Yamaha; in PV, MSIL, M&M, and Skoda Volkswagen; in CV, TML, M&M, and AL.
  • Market share vulnerability: PV market share at 23% with sales growth lagging industry.
  • JV dependency: Significant portion of consolidated performance derives from JVs/associates (Dana Anand, Henkel Anand, ACYM) with varying shareholding levels and partner dependencies.
  • Fundraising and leverage: Approval to raise up to ₹1,000 crores through NCDs (8.15% coupon, 36-month tenure, maturing September 10, 2029) and preferential allotment of 1,44,04,204 equity shares to Asia Investments Private Limited (promoter) for consideration other than cash, as part consideration for acquisition of HL Mando ANAND India Private Limited shares for ₹18,81,03,05,962.
  • Regulatory compliance: Prior XBRL/PDF discrepancies and segment disclosure queries from NSE.
  • Scheme accounting: Composite Scheme of Arrangement involving merger of Anchemco India Pvt Ltd with Asia Investments Private Limited applied retrospectively using pooling of interest method.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance/forward-looking statements: - Management stated the HL Klemove collaboration will foster deeper OEM engagement, expand participation in future mobility solutions, create enduring value for all stakeholders, and enable Gabriel India to further diversify its portfolio and strengthen participation in emerging mobility segments. - The company expresses focus on Latin American, Australian, and African markets for aftermarket export growth. - R&D, innovation, and technology are described as integral to continuous improvement.

Observed performance versus guidance: - The 2W/3W segment delivered 21% YoY sales growth, broadly in line with industry growth of 22.8% (2W) and 39.1% (3W), though 3W industry growth outpaced GIL’s overall segment growth. - PV sales growth of 5.5% YoY significantly trailed PV industry growth of 16.8% YoY, indicating execution challenges against market opportunity. - CV sales growth of 13.3% YoY slightly trailed CV industry growth of 15.2% YoY. - JV performance in FY26 showed margin contraction at Henkel Anand and ACYM, while Dana Anand showed stable EBITDA margin (16.1%) but slight PAT margin decline (11.8% vs 12.0%). - The company has demonstrated progress on EV positioning (57% E2W market share) and utility vehicle penetration (66% share of business), supporting its stated diversification and future mobility focus.

Broker Narrative

The first report framed Gabriel as a cyclical margin-recovery story—stable raw material prices and reduced price declines would improve domestic margins, while Q4 demand softness, export inventory overhang and A&P spends were key drags. By the last report, the narrative had shifted to structural growth: premiumisation, higher content per vehicle, sunroof scale, and the HL Mando/Klemove acquisitions positioning Gabriel as ANAND Group’s consolidation platform toward ₹50,000 crore revenue. Margin pressure persisted as a theme, but the cited causes changed from demand/A&P to input cost inflation, delayed OEM cost recoveries and Hyundai production disruptions.

Optimism that failed

  • Stable raw material prices did not hold—the last report cites input cost inflation pressuring EBITDA margins.
  • The expected decent y/y margin improvement for domestic companies failed to materialize—the last report notes EBITDA margins came under pressure.
