Sundram Fasteners — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Input cost inflation: Management flagged pressure on the middle line from a rise in the cost of direct and indirect materials, primarily attributed to the West Asia conflict. Energy-related indirect materials such as LPG also increased. Indirect material cost recovery is being pursued through negotiation rather than contractual pass-through, and management expects the benefit to accrue and expand margins in Q2.
  • Raw material inflation in the top line: Of the 20% revenue growth, roughly INR20–25 crores was attributable to raw material inflation, with volume growth of about 13% in tonnage terms.
  • Working capital cycle: The cash conversion cycle has been elevated above 150 days over the last two years versus 110–130 days previously. Management linked this to the rising share of exports (back to about 30% of revenues) and the inherently longer operating cycle of export business, and expects the cycle to remain around current levels.
  • High base effect in H2: Management noted that the high base of H2 last year may temper percentage growth figures even if activity levels remain robust.
  • Regulatory uncertainty: A dynamic global environment and potential regulatory changes or uncertainties regarding rules for the New Labour Codes were identified as material headwinds.
  • Segment gap: The company has no presence in two-wheelers and three-wheelers, which form 5–6% of the overall auto pie, so its growth will not track overall industry growth on that count.
  • Defence business cycle: The defence business is described as being at a startup stage and subject to long validation and approval cycles.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong Q1 FY27 start: Turnover grew 20% at both standalone and consolidated levels, from INR1,367 crores to INR1,618 crores, with growth across OE, aftermarket and exports. Standalone profit rose from INR138 crores to INR150 crores, aided by control over fixed costs. Consolidated revenue from operations for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was Rs. 1,846.07 crores with consolidated net profit of Rs. 168.69 crores; standalone revenue from operations was Rs. 1,614.76 crores with net profit of Rs. 150.97 crores.
  • Export demand recovery: North American passenger car volumes are picking up as ICE engines return to the fore after the EV project setback. Class 8 truck demand has rebounded, with primary order levels 20–25% above the prior year, backlogs at a 38-month high as of June, and support from construction, fleet replacement and a mild pre-buy ahead of EPA27 norms. High-horsepower demand is being driven by power generation for AI compute and data center loads, with customers seeing full order books for the next 2–3 years. Europe is also improving, particularly with cooling and temperature control customers.
  • Europe expansion: Europe is now sub-20% of exports and growing, supported by machined parts for turbochargers from Garrett Motion (two divisions have acquired business) and ZF work at Sri City, with additional RFQs in the pipeline.
  • EV and hybrid programs: The General Motors EV business is scaling, with INR200–250 crores expected this year versus less than INR50 crores last year, against initial peak projections of INR750 crores. Stellantis contracts cover ICE, PHEV and EV, with ICE and PHEV platforms picking up well; the INR200–250 crores figure comprises both General Motors and Stellantis. EV is 4–5% of Sundram’s revenue overall.
  • New products and pipeline: The company targets at least 20% of revenue from new products on a three-year basis and is almost there. It has a pipeline of over INR1,000 crores of new projects, with an equal magnitude under discussion.
  • Domestic market resilience: Growth matched industry in M&HCV (industry up 20%), passenger cars (industry up 23%, helped by GST rationalization and a small car recovery), and tractors (up 14–15%). The company participates in nearly all recent new projects in India, and its parts are largely fuel- and transmission-agnostic, positioning it for multi-powertrain launches.
  • New OEM penetration: BIS and QCO requirements have opened entry into Hyundai and Kia fasteners, where imports were previously used; management sees INR100 crores plus potential from this customer. Cummins fastener business is also growing well.
  • Non-auto expansion: Key drivers are wind energy fasteners, aerospace fasteners and industrial application fasteners, pursued to counter auto cyclicality as a “twin engine” alongside auto. Aerospace has moved from INR50 crores to a target of INR100 crores plus this year, with a vision of INR500 crores in 2–3 years; customers include Hindustan Aeronautics, ISRO, General Electric and new addition Skyroot Aerospace, with NADCAP and AS9100 certifications. Wind energy fasteners moved from INR25–30 crores per month after last year’s expansion, with a further ~INR100 crores investment to take annualized revenue from INR350 crores to INR500 crores.
  • Subsidiaries: China is leading on the back of construction and commercial vehicle momentum, with close to 20% growth expected for the year versus FY25-26. The U.K. subsidiary is aligned with European truck market growth and is adding U.S. business. TVS Upasana serves two-wheelers with Bajaj, Royal Enfield and TVS Motors and is growing well. All subsidiaries together are expected to grow on lines similar to SFL standalone.
  • Industry 4.0 and digital transformation: IoT has been deployed across all plants, with benefits in data quality, machine downtime and quality control, delivering 5–10% productivity improvement, with an expected margin improvement of 0.2–0.5%. Automatic storage and retrieval systems and AI usage are also being adopted.
  • Other tailwinds cited: Steady recovery in export demand, continued resilience of domestic markets, emerging mobility trends such as hybrids in the U.S. and electric vehicles in Europe, expanding non-automotive presence in aerospace, wind energy, railways and defence, and adoption of Industry 4.0 technologies.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material and indirect cost volatility: Mitigated in the domestic market by contractual pass-through arrangements with OEMs, back-to-back invoicing aligned to steel mill–OEM settlements, and price increases in the aftermarket. Export margins are supported by rupee depreciation. Indirect material recovery remains subject to negotiation.
  • Execution and project timelines: Capital allocation covers non-auto expansion and auto growth in line with customer requirements, with execution reported as per timeline. Imported machinery content is being monitored case-by-case given the West Asia crisis to keep receipt and commissioning of capital equipment on schedule. Financing is not seen as a concern.
  • Balance sheet and liquidity: Debt-equity is about 0.1–0.2, liquidity is available, the repo rate has been held and interest rates are not expected to rise; capex and working capital can be financed from internal accruals or borrowings if necessary. Working capital increases are in line with operations, and both inventory and receivable days have come down in number-of-days terms.
  • Receivables and compliance: No finance-side challenges were cited; temporary spikes in days may reflect payment cycle timing at OEMs. The company states there are no concerns.
  • Cyclicality: The non-auto push is explicitly intended to avoid or beat auto cyclicality in CV and passenger cars.
  • Defence validation risk: Long validation and approval cycles are acknowledged.
  • Inorganic execution: The company scans for inorganic opportunities, including asset purchases in Europe to shorten procurement time versus the typical 12–18 month project expansion, but nothing is in an advanced stage that can be disclosed.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Growth guidance: Management had guided at the start of the year to CV 8%, PV 10% and tractor 7% growth, versus industry CV 4% and tractor flat to 2%. In Q1 FY27, domestic growth was about 16%, with the company matching industry growth in M&HCV (20%), passenger cars (23%) and tractors (14–15%). Management retained its higher assessment for the year, noting credit rating agencies are not talking about such high numbers, and flagged the H2 high base effect as a reason percentages may not look as strong, with possible calibration a quarter later.
  • Run-rate guidance: Management said the 20% run rate should be maintained at similar levels, plus or minus a few percentage points, given festivals and indicative customer schedules.
  • Margin guidance: Reported EBITDA was 16.1%, and management expects it to only go up from here, potentially finishing closer to 16.5%. Direct material pass-throughs are contractual; indirect material negotiations, once accrued and accounted, should expand margins in Q2.
  • EV revenue guidance: INR200–250 crores expected this year from General Motors and Stellantis combined, versus less than INR50 crores last year, with the plan described as on track based on Q1. Management declined to commit to a INR500–600 crores run rate by FY29, noting the initial peak projection of INR750 crores and a preference to take one year at a time.
  • Aerospace guidance: From INR50 crores historically to INR100 crores plus targeted this year, with a INR500 crores aspiration in 2–3 years.
  • Wind energy guidance: From INR350 crores annualized to INR500 crores annualized, supported by close to INR100 crores of further investment.
  • Hyundai/Kia guidance: INR100 crores plus potential over 2–3 years.
  • Subsidiary guidance: Close to 20% growth in China for the current year versus FY25-26; all subsidiaries together expected to grow on lines similar to SFL standalone.
  • Capex guidance: A prior call guided roughly INR250 crores of capex for the year; management referenced a press release about INR400 crores, noting the last three years’ balance sheets have been around those levels, with about 30% for replacement and the balance for growth. The company has earmarked Rs. 400 crores towards capacity expansion, with approximately Rs. 250 crores planned for the Fasteners Division and Rs. 100 crores for the Cast and Machined Assemblies business. Management stated the industry’s inherent nature means continued investment.
  • Working capital guidance: The cash conversion cycle should remain around current elevated levels, with a return to 140–150 days if execution and logistics stay in place.
  • Productivity guidance: Digital transformation has delivered 5–10% productivity improvement, expected to translate to 0.2–0.5% margin improvement, with horizontal deployment across facilities.
  • Strategic guidance: Management remains focused on operational excellence, prudent capital allocation and creating long-term value.
