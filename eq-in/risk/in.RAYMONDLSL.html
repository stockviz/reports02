Raymond Lifestyle Limited — Q1 FY27 Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Macro and input-cost pressure. The company flags a difficult operating environment: the collapse of US-Iran peace talks has pushed Brent crude back to $100/bbl, rising oil costs and anticipated Fed rate hikes are pressuring the rupee toward a record low, and El Niño poses a threat of record-breaking heat and a potentially subpar monsoon. Wool prices are climbing on persistent demand, and cotton prices are rising due to a poor Indian crop and high global demand. The RBI has cut its FY27 growth outlook to 6.6% (a 30 bps reduction) and revised its FY27 CPI inflation projection up to 5.1% from 4.6%.

Margin pressure from costs and mix. Despite an 11% YoY rise in EBITDA to ₹135 Cr and a 40 bps margin improvement to 8.6%, the company states margins were weighed down by a significant increase in raw material prices and freight costs, along with unfavourable sales mix shifts and adverse channel mix.

Segment-level softness. - Branded Textile revenue declined 2% YoY to ₹684 Cr and EBITDA fell 11% to ₹95 Cr (margin 13.9% vs 15.3%), attributed to a strong prior-year base effect and scale deleverage. - Branded Apparel revenue grew 4% YoY to ₹349 Cr, but EBITDA fell 33% to ₹18 Cr (margin 5.1% vs 7.8%) on adverse channel mix, partly mitigated by reduced markdowns and retail network optimization. - High Value Cotton Shirting revenue declined 5% YoY to ₹195 Cr on a strong base effect, though EBITDA was resilient at ₹19 Cr with margin improving to 9.7% from 9.1%. - Emerging Businesses revenue grew 9% YoY to ₹79 Cr but EBITDA loss widened to ₹(19) Cr from ₹(13) Cr, as these businesses remain in investment mode.

Bottom line. PBT before exceptional items worsened to ₹(38) Cr from ₹(25) Cr, and net profit fell to ₹(23) Cr from ₹(20) Cr. On a standalone basis, the company reported a net loss of ₹(3,486) lakhs for the quarter.

Retail rationalization. The company exited 133 underperforming stores and opened 85 new locations, resulting in net closures of 48 stores since June 2025. Store count stood at 1,627 as of June 2026 versus 1,675 a year earlier. Branded Apparel saw 34 net EBO closures (down 9% YoY), and Ethnix optimized its footprint by a net 29 stores since June 2025.

Prior-year exceptional items. During FY26, the company recognized exceptional losses of ₹(11,598) lakhs standalone and ₹(12,854) lakhs consolidated, including loss allowance on receivables and inventory write-downs in Apparel (₹6,996 lakhs), statutory impact of New Labour Codes, and stamp duty on demerger. The New Labour Codes notified on 21 November 2025 had an incremental impact consisting of gratuity and compensated absences, primarily from a change in wage definition.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Garmenting outperformance. The Garmenting segment delivered 50% YoY revenue growth to ₹296 Cr (from ₹197 Cr), with EBITDA swinging to ₹22 Cr from ₹(8) Cr (margin 7.3% vs (4.1%)). This was driven by order book execution following US-India tariff rationalisation and onboarding of new global clients.

Trade tailwinds. The company cites the US-India Tariff rationalisation and the implementation of the UK FTA as supporting a robust order book, with upcoming FTAs with the UK and EU seen as further strategic advantages. International growth is a stated FY27 lever, expanding into EU and UK markets via trade agreements.

Premiumization and casualization. Premiumization across Branded Textile and Apparel is driving higher average selling prices. The company is transitioning its fabric mix toward high-value wool, polywool blends, and pure linen, and accelerating premium product introductions. Casual brands ColorPlus and Parx grew in double digits, with increasing casual mix within formal brands Park Avenue and Raymond RTW. LFS and online channels demonstrated high double-digit growth.

Financial strength. The company remains net-debt free with a net cash position of ₹154 Cr in June 2026, improved from net debt of ₹(55) Cr in June 2025. Net working capital days improved to 75 from 90 a year earlier (and from 87 to 77 days in FY26), attributed to better collections and inventory management. The asset cover ratio for the 9.00% Series ‘P’ NCDs stood at 2.90 times as at 30 June 2026, above the required minimum of 2.00 times.

Distribution depth. The company operates 1,112 TRS outlets, 463 EBOs, 20,000+ trade points, 12.4 million loyalty members, 43 international stores, and 1,600+ retail stores across 600+ towns and cities, supported by 11 manufacturing facilities and 23,000+ employees.

Strategic framework. The FY27 roadmap focuses on premiumization, casualization, and geographical diversification, supported by enablers including supply chain efficiency, talent and culture, ESG, digital and consumer insights, and global growth. Value drivers cited include ~65% worsted suiting fabric share, OTC shirting leadership, pricing power in wedding/celebration wear with EBITDA margins above 18%, FTR above 95%, and capacity utilization above 90%.

ESG commitments. By 2030, targets include 40% female representation, Zero Liquid Discharge, Zero Waste to Landfill, 25% renewable energy usage, and a 15% reduction in Scope 1 and 2 emissions. FY26 achievements cited include a 3.7% reduction in Scope 1 and 2 emissions, 11.26% energy from renewable sources, 24.26% reduction in waste to landfill, and a 37.22% increase in liquid discharge.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and currency risk: Rising wool and cotton prices, elevated crude at $100/bbl, and a rupee under pressure toward record lows.
  • Macro and demand risk: RBI’s lowered FY27 growth outlook (6.6%) and higher inflation projection (5.1%), plus geopolitical tensions and El Niño-related monsoon uncertainty.
  • Margin risk: Adverse channel and sales mix, scale deleverage, and elevated raw material and freight costs.
  • Execution risk in retail: Ongoing network optimization with net store closures; the company notes recently opened stores continue to mature.
  • Investment drag: Emerging Businesses remain in investment mode with widening EBITDA losses.
  • Regulatory risk: The New Labour Codes’ final Central and/or State Rules remain pending, and the company continues to monitor clarifications that could require further accounting effects.
  • Prior-year asset quality signals: Loss allowance on receivables and inventory write-downs in Apparel were recognized in FY26, described as non-recurring operational decisions.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The company notes its statements are subject to regulatory changes, political or economic developments, and technological risks.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 targets. Management set an FY27 target of double-digit top-line growth and higher double-digit EBITDA growth, with a roadmap focused on premiumization, casualization, and geographical diversification.

Observed Q1 FY27 performance. Total income grew 6% YoY to ₹1,560 Cr, which is below the stated double-digit top-line ambition. EBITDA grew 11% YoY to ₹135 Cr with margin at 8.6% (up 40 bps), which is below the “higher double-digit” EBITDA growth target. PBT before exceptional items was ₹(38) Cr versus ₹(25) Cr, and net profit was ₹(23) Cr versus ₹(20) Cr.

Segment versus commentary. The Garmenting segment’s 50% growth and swing to positive EBITDA align with management’s emphasis on trade tailwinds and order book execution. However, Branded Textile and High Value Cotton Shirting both declined YoY on base effects, and Branded Apparel’s EBITDA fell despite revenue growth, indicating that the premiumization and casualization strategy has not yet translated into segment-level margin expansion in Q1.

Balance sheet versus guidance. The company’s stated focus on disciplined working capital management and strong cash flows is reflected in NWC days improving to 75 from 90, and the net cash position of ₹154 Cr versus net debt of ₹(55) Cr a year earlier. The company reiterates remaining debt-free.

CEO commentary. Satyaki Ghosh described Q1 FY27 as “steady performance” marked by strong international traction and sustained domestic demand, acknowledging that short-term macroeconomic pressures and elevated raw material costs weighed on overall margins, while pointing to the resilient product mix, debt-free balance sheet, and ₹154 Cr net cash position as providing operational flexibility.

Governance. At the 8th AGM held on 14 July 2026, all six resolutions were passed with requisite majority, including adoption of FY26 audited financial statements, declaration of a final dividend of Re. 1 per share for FY2025-26, re-appointment of Gautam Hari Singhania as rotating director, ratification of Cost Auditors’ remuneration for FY2026-27, approval of commission to Non-Executive Directors, and appointment of Satyaki Ghosh as Whole-time Director and CEO.

Broker Narrative

The first report was purely optimistic, with no flagged headwinds, and emphasized domestic EBO expansion, wedding/ethnic wear, new categories, and export opportunities. By the last report, the narrative had turned defensive on domestic branded apparel — store closures, consolidation, raw-material inflation, margin declines, and a PAT loss — while shifting optimism to garmenting exports and cost transformation. Export/trade-related themes persisted, but the earlier retail-expansion and wedding-led growth optimism gave way to rationalization and premiumization/casualization.

Optimism that failed

  • The doubling of the EBO network did not materialize, as the last report instead shows store rationalization and closures hurting branded apparel.
  • Wedding wear-led growth and rising ethnic-wear share failed to materialize, with an unfavorable wedding calendar and weaker formalwear demand hitting apparel sales.
  • The asset-light franchisee-led expansion model did not drive the expected growth, as the last report instead shows store rationalization and a consolidation year for branded businesses.
