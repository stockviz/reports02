Asian Hotels (West) Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Operational and financial distress. The company’s principal asset, Hyatt Regency Mumbai, has been at the centre of severe disruption. Operations were suspended in June 2021 following restrictions on lender funding, and the company subsequently entered Corporate Insolvency Resolution Proceedings (CIRP) after a default claim of Rs. 26,407.35 lakhs. The standalone results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 show no revenue from operations, with total income of only Rs. 138.12 lakhs (entirely other income) against total expenses of Rs. 210.56 lakhs, producing a loss before tax of Rs. 72.44 lakhs. The consolidated picture is different: revenue from operations was Rs. 9,771.35 lakhs for the quarter, with profit before tax of Rs. 205.62 lakhs, reflecting the contribution of a subsidiary.

Going concern pressure. The standalone financial statements record current liabilities exceeding current assets by Rs. 42,358.44 lakhs as at June 30, 2026; on a consolidated basis the gap is Rs. 41,865.94 lakhs. The auditors state that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the entity’s ability to continue as a going concern, and that the statements do not adequately disclose this matter.

Disputed lender claims. Novak Hotels Private Limited (identified as Saraf Group or the lender) advanced Rs. 37,100 lakhs as of March 31, 2024 and a further Rs. 1,900 lakhs during the year, aggregating Rs. 39,000 lakhs, which was used to pay creditors and regulatory and necessitated expenses. The company has not recognised interest expense of Rs. 8,616.43 lakhs, certain reimbursement expenses of Rs. 1,783.24 lakhs, and an unreconciled borrowing balance of Rs. 242.64 lakhs claimed by the lender, as these matters are in dispute. The company is still in the process of executing loan documents and finalising terms including security, interest rate, and repayment.

Asset classification and compliance issues. The auditors note that under clause (v) of Schedule 2 to the Framework Agreement, Saraf Group has an option to buy Hyatt Regency Mumbai after successful withdrawal of CIRP and revocation of trading suspension. They state they are unable to determine whether amounts received are a borrowing or an advance for sale of assets, and that presentation as non-current is questionable. They also note that shareholder approval was obtained only for creating security on assets, without informing members of the option granted to Saraf Group; that the company has not yet filed the necessary forms with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs for the charge; and that if the intention is to sell Hyatt Regency Mumbai, Ind AS 105 (non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations) should have been applied.

Record-keeping and verification gaps. The auditors report they were not provided proper records showing full particulars, including quantitative details and situation, of property, plant and equipment, nor information on physical verification, and are therefore unable to comment on the existence of the PPE balance of Rs. 1,467.01 lakhs. Outstanding recoverable/payable balances with government authorities are subject to reconciliation, and in the absence of complete details of statutory dues payable, the auditors cannot comment on the adequacy of interest expense on statutory dues.

Adverse conclusion. For both standalone and consolidated results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, J. C. Bhalla & Co. issued an Adverse Conclusion, based on the matters above. The consolidated review also relies on another auditor for a subsidiary, whose financial information reflected total revenues of Rs. 9,775.01 lakhs, total net profit after tax of Rs. 1,439.39 lakhs, and total comprehensive income of Rs. 1,441.90 lakhs for the quarter.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Closure of insolvency proceedings. The CIRP against the company was closed following NCLAT’s approval of a settlement proposal under Section 12A of the IBC on January 9, 2024. The company has prepared its financial statements on a going concern basis considering this approved settlement proposal and the steps being taken to meet regulatory requirements and reporting obligations.

Lender funding. Under the framework agreement between the promoters and Saraf Group, Novak Hotels Private Limited agreed to advance an aggregate of Rs. 390 crores as a secured loan, of which approximately Rs. 373 crores had been received and utilised to pay creditors and regulatory expenses. The company has recognised interest expense of Rs. 198 lakhs at 9% p.a. on Rs. 2,200 lakhs during FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26, and had recognised Rs. 2,200 lakhs of interest expense in the previous year ended March 31, 2024.

Subsidiary performance. The consolidated results reflect a profitable subsidiary, which recorded total revenues of Rs. 9,775.01 lakhs and net profit after tax of Rs. 1,439.39 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. Consolidated profit for the quarter was Rs. 1,428.29 lakhs, with total comprehensive income of Rs. 1,430.80 lakhs, compared with a consolidated loss position in the standalone entity.

3. Key Risks

  • Going concern risk: Current liabilities exceed current assets by Rs. 42,358.44 lakhs (standalone) and Rs. 41,865.94 lakhs (consolidated) as at June 30, 2026, with the auditors flagging material uncertainty over the entity’s ability to meet its obligations.
  • Disputed liabilities: Unrecognised interest of Rs. 8,616.43 lakhs and reimbursement expenses of Rs. 1,783.24 lakhs claimed by the lender remain in dispute; the auditors note that even at the minimum interest rate stipulated under Section 186(7) of the Companies Act, 2013, the unrecognised interest would be material and represent a substantial proportion of the financial results.
  • Asset ownership uncertainty: The option granted to Saraf Group to acquire Hyatt Regency Mumbai, and the classification of the Rs. 39,000 lakhs received, create uncertainty over whether the principal asset will be sold and how the amounts should be presented.
  • Regulatory non-compliance: Non-filing of charge forms with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, and the failure to inform members of the Saraf Group option when seeking approval only for creating security, are identified compliance concerns.
  • Accounting basis risk: If the intention is to sell Hyatt Regency Mumbai, the financial results should have been prepared under Ind AS 105, which they were not.
  • Verification and reconciliation risk: Inability to verify the existence of PPE of Rs. 1,467.01 lakhs, unreconciled government balances, and incomplete statutory dues details.
  • Historical record limitations: The auditors previously issued a Disclaimer of Opinion for the year ended March 31, 2021 due to limitations in obtaining primary books of accounts and records, inability to verify inventory, and uncertainties regarding subsequent events; the financial statements for that year were approved and presented in June 2024 following closure of CIRP. The company reported a net loss of Rs. 4,760.87 lakhs (standalone) and Rs. 14,885.96 lakhs (consolidated) for the year ended March 31, 2021, with substantial borrowings, defaults in debt repayments, ongoing legal proceedings, property tax demands, and unabsorbed depreciation and business losses.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Management has prepared the financial statements on a going concern basis, assuming the company will continue as a going concern, realise its assets, and discharge its liabilities in the normal course of business, based on the settlement of insolvency proceedings and the financial support received. The company also states it is in the process of executing loan documents with the lender and finalising terms and conditions, including the nature of security, interest rate, and terms of repayment.

Observed performance against this guidance:

  • Going concern assumption vs. balance sheet position: Despite the going concern basis, the standalone entity’s current liabilities exceed current assets by Rs. 42,358.44 lakhs as at June 30, 2026, and the auditors state that a material uncertainty exists and that the statements do not adequately disclose this matter.
  • Loan documentation vs. dispute: While management states it is finalising loan terms, the lender’s claims for interest of Rs. 8,616.43 lakhs, reimbursement expenses of Rs. 1,783.24 lakhs, and the unreconciled balance of Rs. 242.64 lakhs remain unrecognised and in dispute, indicating terms have not been agreed.
  • Settlement and support vs. operational status: Although the CIRP was closed on January 9, 2024 and funding of approximately Rs. 373 crores was received, the standalone entity recorded no revenue from operations in the quarter ended June 30, 2026, with a loss before tax of Rs. 72.44 lakhs and a loss for the period of Rs. 18.69 lakhs. The consolidated entity, supported by its subsidiary, recorded a profit for the period of Rs. 1,428.29 lakhs.
  • Regulatory compliance vs. stated steps: Management states it is taking steps to meet regulatory requirements, but the auditors note the charge forms with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs are yet to be filed and that members were not informed of the Saraf Group option.
