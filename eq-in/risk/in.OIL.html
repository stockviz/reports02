Oil India Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Gas monetisation and evacuation constraints. Gas production faces recurring hiccups from shutdowns of downstream industries, including annual maintenance shutdowns taken by BCPL. Seasonal factors also weigh on demand: from October onwards, tea gardens take less gas, and NEEPCO reportedly consumes less gas when it can substitute hydroelectricity, since it cannot match hydro pricing. The company shut in up to 30 wells on average, and up to 60 wells when consumption is low, because evacuation capacity is the binding constraint rather than production capability. Management stated plainly that ramping up production is not the challenge — the absence of a network is.

Pending infrastructure. A roughly 200-metre stretch of line remains to be laid inside NRL before DNPL’s common-carrier status can be fully utilised. The Duliajan Feeder Line (DFL) by IGGL is still under construction, with about 20% progress reported and a PMC engaged. Until these links are complete, the company remains reliant on a narrow set of North-East customers (BCPL, NEEPCO and others).

Regulatory and tax matters. A GST liability on royalty, provided for since 1 July 2017, amounts to roughly INR2,500 crores excluding interest; the court has given six weeks to settle, and payment is expected to be reflected in the second-quarter financials. On the Assam land tax, the state government’s senior counsel gave an undertaking to the court that the law will be withdrawn and repealed through the legislature; the amount has so far been carried as a contingent liability and has not impacted financials.

Exploration uncertainty. The Andaman campaign has seen mixed results: the first well (Vijayapuram 1) encountered tight sands that could not be tested with the available system, the second well (Vijayapuram 2) flowed only intermittently and was written down, while the third (Vijayapuram 3) flowed continuously and was a technical discovery intimated to the DGH. Spending on Vijayapuram 1 was roughly INR1,000–1,050 crores. The fate of Vijayapuram 1 and 3 depends on ongoing testing and new 3D seismic interpretation.

Cost pressure. Contract costs rose by roughly INR146–150 crores, largely on G&G costs for offshore blocks, with other items broadly in line.

Weather. Floods in nearby areas had no impact on OIL’s operations, which are in the eastern parts of Sivasagar and Charaideo.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record operational and financial performance in Q1 FY27. Crude oil production rose over 11% year-on-year to 0.95 MMT, with daily crude production peaking at 10,921 MT on 27 June 2026 — the highest daily production ever recorded — and rising further to 11,017 MT on 3 August 2026. Standalone operating revenue of INR7,958 crores was the highest since listing in FY2009-10; EBITDA was INR4,605 crores at a margin above 54%; PBT was INR3,742 crores and PAT INR2,870 crores, both highest-ever post-listing. EPS was Rs. 17.65 for the quarter. Consolidated operating revenue was INR12,886 crores with PAT of INR4,026 crores.

Integrated workover and drilling planning. New integrated planning forums — the high-level workover program planning committee (HWPMB) and the Exploration & Development portfolio management board (EDPMB) — have improved workover and well-intervention results. The company drilled 17 wells in the quarter (7 exploratory, 10 development), against 74 wells in FY26, and targets 100 wells in FY27 (42 exploratory, 57 development), with at least a 10% increase planned thereafter.

Deepwater and ultra-deepwater opportunity. Under the new PNG rules and the Samudra Manthan initiative, the government will reimburse seismic activity and up to roughly INR675 crores per well for deepwater and ultra-deepwater drilling, with about INR10,000 crores for common infrastructure hubs. OIL has completed 4,200 line-km of 2D and 5,300 sq km of 3D data in Mahanadi and KG deepwaters within a year of PEL award, with acquisition, processing and interpretation of roughly 40,000 sq km to finish by January. The first deepwater rig arrives June–July 2027 and the second by March 2028. One Mahanadi well next year will be government-sponsored at INR800 crores under the stratigraphic well campaign. TOTAL and Petrobras are providing independent interpretation and de-risking support.

Gas evacuation build-out. DNPL has been mandated by PNGRB as a common carrier. Once the 200-metre NRL link is done in two to three months, gas not consumed by NRL can flow to IGGL and the national grid. DNPL can then be shut down briefly to augment capacity from 1 MMSCMD to 2, and eventually 2.5 MMSCMD. NRL is expected to consume an additional 1.5 MMSCMD, with a spike in offtake when it comes into operation, expected around January. The DFL connection is expected to remove the evacuation bottleneck, with all facilities expected by end-CY2027 and full pipeline readiness by 2028, enabling evacuation of an additional 3.5–4 MMSCMD.

NRL expansion. CDU/VDU has been mechanically completed and passed OISD and PESO inspections; DHDT and SRU are targeted for commissioning by October–November, with remaining units targeted by 31 March 2027. Graded production ramp-up begins next financial year, reaching about 75% of the 9 MMTPA rated capacity by Q4 FY28. The Paradip–Numaligarh pipeline has all ROU acquired except 8 km across four states, with mechanical completion targeted for October and commissioning by December. NRL capex is expected at INR34,000–35,000 crores for the refinery and another INR7,200–7,300 crores for the PPU.

Low-carbon portfolio. OIL Green Energy Limited, a wholly owned subsidiary, signed MoUs with four Haryana municipalities (Gurugram, Faridabad, Hisar and Ambala) for four Integrated Waste-to-Clean-Energy Projects processing 5,000 tonnes per day of municipal solid waste, expected to produce 70–75 TPD of CBG and 50 MW of green power.

Corporate actions. The 67th AGM is scheduled for 17 September 2026, with a proposed final dividend of Rs. 1 per share (10% of paid-up capital) for FY2025-26, an increase in authorised share capital from INR2,000 crores to INR5,000 crores, amendments to the Memorandum and Articles of Association, and the appointment of Shri Bhupinder Kumar as Government Nominee Director. Potential issuance of capital such as bonus issues in compliance with DIPAM guidelines is noted as a growth-related item.

3. Key Risks

  • Evacuation and offtake risk: production capability exceeds network capacity; dependence on BCPL, NEEPCO and other North-East customers, with their shutdowns and price-driven switching to hydro creating volume volatility.
  • Execution and timeline risk: the 200-metre NRL link, DFL construction, DNPL augmentation, NRL unit commissioning and the Paradip–Numaligarh pipeline all carry stated timelines that must be met for the gas ramp-up to materialise.
  • Exploration risk: Andaman wells have shown variable reservoir quality; write-downs have occurred (Vijayapuram 2), and outcomes for Vijayapuram 1 and 3 remain contingent on testing and seismic interpretation.
  • Commodity price and margin risk: realisations are volatile — crude realisation was USD98.73/bbl versus USD66.20/bbl a year earlier, and NRL’s GRM of USD35.95/bbl versus USD5.02/bbl a year earlier included about USD2 of inventory gain, against a normal range of USD7–8. NRL also gave discounts to OMCs under SAED, starting at INR13/litre for petrol and INR10/litre for diesel and falling to INR3/litre and nil respectively by quarter-end, with discounts reappearing thereafter.
  • Tax and regulatory risk: the GST-on-royalty payment of about INR2,500 crores (excluding interest) and the pending repeal of the Assam land tax law.
  • Debt and leverage: group-level total debt of about INR37,233 crores, comprising USD1.4 billion for Mozambique, a USD500 million Singapore subsidiary bond repayable in May 2027, and over INR19,000 crores at NRL.
  • Key personnel risk: Shri Jyotirmoy Bhattacharya, ED (Finance) and a member of the Senior Management, superannuates after close of working hours on 31 August 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Crude oil production. Management guided that the company would achieve close to 1 MMT each quarter, and that with conditions unchanged it expects to reach 1 MMT every quarter going forward. Observed performance: 0.95 MMT in Q1 FY27, with daily production rising from 10,921 MT on 27 June 2026 to 11,017 MT on 3 August 2026. For FY27, management indicated minimum production of about 3.9 MMT, with a possibility of touching 4 MMT, and a FY29 target of around 4.2 MMT from the main producing areas plus Rajasthan ramp-up.

Gas production. Management guided FY28 gas production of 3.8 BCM, with a “big jump” from Q1 FY29 onwards, and a long-term path to 5 BCM. The stated route is: about 1.5 MMSCMD additional from NRL (roughly 0.5 BCM) as NRL commissions and the 200-metre link completes, plus a further 2–2.5 MMSCMD (roughly 1.5 BCM) once the other pipelines are in place, taking volumes from the current 2.93 BCM to about 5 BCM by FY29. Observed performance: natural gas production rose 0.4% quarter-on-quarter, but was down 8% year-on-year, reflecting the offtake constraints described.

NRL GRM. Management characterised the normal GRM range as USD7–8 based on prior annual reports. Observed Q1 FY27 GRM was USD35.95/bbl, of which about USD2 was inventory gain, giving roughly USD33 excluding it — a level management attributed to unusually high diesel/MS-to-crude spreads, net of OMC discounts under SAED.

NRL operating cost. Management guided per-barrel operating cost falling from the current USD4.5–5 to about USD3.5 as plants stabilise, facilities are rationalised and throughput rises.

Capex. Q1 FY27 spend was about INR3,050 crores (INR450 crores survey, INR1,230 crores exploratory drilling, INR700 crores development drilling, INR350 crores capital equipment and projects, INR350 crores subsidiaries/JVs/overseas). The full-year budget estimate is currently INR8,600 crores, subject to revision, against actual FY26 expenditure of INR13,026 crores.

Drilling. FY25: 22 exploratory and 35 development wells. FY26: 22 exploratory and 52 development wells. FY27 target: 42 exploratory and 57 development wells, with at least 10% growth in well count thereafter.

Andaman. Management guided that hydro frac testing at Vijayapuram 1 would take about a month, with the fate of the well to be decided after testing concludes in September; a fourth well is to be drilled by December with a jack-up rig at about 90 metres water depth; 600 sq km of new 3D seismic is being processed by Schlumberger for completion by October, with interpretation by January and a decision by February on additional appraisal wells near Vijayapuram 2 and 3.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from broad macro-driven concerns (Russia-Ukraine conflict, windfall taxes, LNG price spikes, retail fuel margin pressure, EV adoption risk) to company-specific execution risks (NRL delays, gas monetisation delays, regulatory caps on oil realisations). Themes around GRMs and domestic gas production persisted across both reports, while geopolitical risks and retail margin optimism faded. Tailwinds shifted from expectations of moderate crude/LNG prices and infrastructure completion toward stronger oil prices, higher gas production, and better petchem margins.

Fears that came true

  • Windfall taxes limiting gains from pricing strength materialized, correlating with the DISAPPOINTMENT cluster of Buy calls from August to November 2024 (e.g., 2024-09-24 at -15.3%, 2024-10-09 at -12.7%, 2024-11-06 at -4.5%).
  • Unofficial freeze on retail petrol/diesel prices leading to weak OMC marketing margins materialized, correlating with negative actual returns in the Aug–Nov 2024 DISAPPOINTMENT cluster.
  • Sharp spikes in crude and gas prices due to restricted supply materialized, contributing to the DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes clustered in August–September 2024 (e.g., 2024-08-09 actual -21.8%).
  • Execution delays for NRL from the last report materialized, correlating with the DISAPPOINTMENT on 2026-05-14 (actual −7.9%).
  • Regulatory changes capping oil realisations from the last report materialized, correlating with the DISAPPOINTMENT on 2026-08-28 (actual −1.3%).

Optimism that failed

  • Soft crude/product prices taking retail fuel margins to record levels failed to sustain, as DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes from August 2024 onward showed margins compressed and multiple Buy calls posted negative actual returns.
  • Completion of expanded gas transportation infrastructure failed to deliver on schedule, as the last report cites ‘Gas monetisation delays owing to infra delays,’ indicating infrastructure completion was significantly delayed.
  • Comfortable ceiling for domestic priority-sector gas prices and tariff revisions removing uncertainty failed, as the last report lists ‘Regulatory changes capping oil realisations’ as a key headwind.
