Headwinds and Challenges

Execution delays in large projects. Management repeatedly acknowledged that three major projects — MAHAPREIT, Supertech, and Jammu & Kashmir — together involving work orders of around ₹50,000 crore, have faced delays. The J&K project moved forward only after a delay of about one and a half to two years. MAHAPREIT remains stuck on approvals, with management hoping it matures by the third quarter. Supertech went through insolvency proceedings before NBCC regained the mandate following a Supreme Court order.

Approval and funding bottlenecks. Management attributed delays to the nature of self-sustainable redevelopment projects, which require numerous statutory clearances — environmental clearance alone can take at least six months, and in Delhi around 18 to 20 approvals (including airport and traffic clearances) are needed before work can start. Seed money was described as the main constraint alongside approvals. An MOU with HUDCO was signed to provide seed funding where the government client can offer a sovereign guarantee, and Naveen Nagpur arranged around ₹3,500 crore to acquire land.

State government processes. Management noted that state government working processes are slower than central government, and that state agencies are still learning central agency procedures. MAHAPREIT was specifically cited as delayed for this reason.

Subsidiary drag on consolidated revenue. HSCC’s turnover declined due to the merger process and the foreclosure of a Maharashtra government project executed at a very low PMC fee (around 2%), which affected consolidated EBITDA. Management stated it stopped such loss-making practices.

Seasonality and pollution-related stoppages. Management noted Q1 is usually slower, with pickup from Q2 onward. An investor flagged that GRAP restrictions in Delhi (November to January) could slow construction; management said its projections already account for these factors.

Auditor-flagged matters. The review reports draw attention to several items: non-execution of lease/conveyance deeds for land at Naya Raipur and Faridabad; the stuck NBCC Plaza project where MCD demands ₹3,224.45 lakh in additional FAR charges already paid to L&DO; the Kochi project pending environmental clearance; the NBCC Green View, Sector-37D Gurugram project, where cumulative provisions and write-offs total ₹46,882.51 lakh, a ₹75,000 lakh recovery suit has been filed against the contractor, and 18 litigations are pending; and a DVAT demand of ₹40,480.01 lakh set aside and remanded for recalculation, with contingent liability not ascertainable.

Governance and compliance observations. The auditors noted that the Board did not comprise the requisite number of Independent Directors, including an Independent Woman Director, and that the Audit Committee and Nomination and Remuneration Committee compositions were not in compliance during the quarter. Similar non-compliances were noted at HSCL and HSCC.

HSCC contract irregularities. For the “Test to Treat Arogya Yojna” project, procedural and governance-related irregularities by a subcontractor were observed. Revenue and costs for the quarter from April 1, 2026 to June 30, 2026 were not recognized, and previously recognized revenue of ₹24,732.84 lakh and cost of ₹24,220.87 lakh were reversed. The BOCWW Board closed the scheme via letter dated June 25, 2026.

Order inflow slippage versus earlier expectations. Investors noted that prospective orders of ₹60,000–80,000 crore discussed over the prior seven to eight months had been delayed month after month. Management attributed this to redevelopment projects requiring statutory approvals and clearances.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong order book. Standalone order book stands at ₹1,12,000 crore and consolidated at ₹1,27,000 crore. The consolidated order book was cited as ₹1,26,964 crore as on June 30, 2026. The split is roughly 40% PMC and 60% redevelopment at the standalone level.

Q1 FY27 performance. Standalone revenue from operations was ₹1,823 crore (up 10% YoY) and consolidated ₹2,260 crore. Standalone EBITDA rose to ₹160 crore from ₹99 crore, up 62%, with EBITDA margin at 8.77%. Standalone PAT was ₹151 crore, up 32% YoY. New business secured was ₹1,600 crore consolidated in Q1; works awarded to contractors were ₹1,700 crore consolidated.

Business secured and pipeline. Q1 wins included Odisha schools (₹253 crore), J&K campuses (₹171 crore), and New Andhra Pradesh Bhavan at Delhi (₹106 crore). Post-quarter wins included Seychelles housing (around ₹620 crore), RBI Amravati residential and office complex (₹780 crore), and Rajasthan school science laboratories (₹431 crore). Management expects ₹18,000–20,000 crore of new works to be awarded over the next two to three quarters, and ₹50,000–60,000 crore of new business for the year, including around ₹30,000 crore from 5 GPRA colonies (Lodhi Colony, Andrews Ganj, DIZ Connaught Place area, Lakshmi Bai Nagar).

Redevelopment execution. All major packages of redevelopment projects are in running stage, with around ₹10,000 crore of balance work expected to contribute significantly to top and bottom line. The 7 GPRA projects have around ₹10,000 crore of work orders to be completed within two years, with ₹5,000–6,000 crore of top line expected. Amrapali Phase 1 is essentially complete (23 of 24 projects done, the 24th ready), and Phase 2 is in full swing, with one project reaching the 20th floor using Mivan shuttering. Amrapali is expected to contribute ₹5,000–6,000 crore of top line.

Asset monetization. The entire Bharat Business Park was sold in three auctions within three months, generating around ₹10,000 crore, removing any funding crisis for redevelopment projects. Africa Avenue received an overwhelming response (around 2,900 participants for 262 units), with the auction postponed on technical grounds and expected to conclude in August. Vinayak Mandir Marg was targeted for September and Scindia Marg/Palanji area for September–October. Total fund generation of ₹30,000–35,000 crore from redevelopment sales is expected during the year, with money received over two years on a construction-linked basis.

Margin expansion. Management expects margins to increase because redevelopment and Amrapali projects contribute more than 60% of revenue and are high-value PMC projects. Guidance was 6–6.5% PAT margin and 6.5–7% minimum EBITDA margin. The Q1 margin boost was attributed to marketing fees from Bharat Business Park sales, with further marketing fees expected from Africa Avenue, Vinayak Mandir, Sarojini Nagar, and Amrapali inventory sales (8% PMC plus 1% marketing fee).

HSCC merger. The Board approved the merger of HSCC into NBCC. DIPAM conveyed no-objection on July 9, 2026, and a joint first motion application was filed with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs on July 17, 2026. The merger is expected to create operational and financial synergies by combining NBCC’s execution strengths with HSCC’s expertise.

CPSE REIT. The Board accorded in-principle approval to incorporate a wholly owned SPV for the proposed CPSE REIT, subject to approval of the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs/DIPAM. NBCC has been entrusted with developing the structure of the proposed REIT following the Finance Minister’s budget 2026 announcement. Three rentable assets totaling 75,000 square feet will be transferred to the SPV. NBCC would be the ultimate sponsor, with ownership depending on assets contributed.

International expansion. Dubai operations are on track with a developer license obtained, land purchased, around 66 housing units planned, design and local authority approvals obtained, and an RFO floated for broker selection; the construction tender is planned for October. After completing around 2,000 houses in Maldives, Seychelles entrusted NBCC with another 1,000 houses, with hospital construction opportunities there. Opportunities are also expected from Australia, Saudi Arabia, and Fiji — not included in current projections.

Stalled housing opportunity. Management expects to receive several private sector stalled projects, citing Amrapali’s completion (around ₹8,000 crore top line completed with almost 9% bottom line and ₹2,000 crore GST paid) and the Supertech mandate. Around 2.5–3 lakh homebuyers in Delhi-NCR and 4.5 lakh across India are affected by stalled projects.

Other project progress. J&K: one tender called with technical evaluation underway, and five more tenders worth nearly ₹3,500 crore to be published. Naveen Nagpur: 400–450 acres acquired out of 1,000 acres, consultant appointed, DPR in final stage. Goa: four packages ordered, two tenders pending, site mobilized and work started. Gurugram Sector 37-D: contract awarded, contractor mobilized, test pile done, revised plan and environmental clearance pending, RERA registration and sales expected next quarter. Ghitorni: final lease agreement in process, consultant tender to be finalized this quarter.

Key Risks

  • Approval risk: Multiple statutory clearances (environmental, airport, traffic) can delay projects by months or years, as seen with J&K, MAHAPREIT, and Kochi.
  • Funding/seed money risk: Self-sustainable redevelopment projects require seed capital before sales materialize; HUDCO financing depends on sovereign guarantees from government clients.
  • Client-side process risk: State government processes are slower, and MAHAPREIT remains unresolved.
  • Litigation and contingent liabilities: The Green View project has 18 pending litigations, a ₹75,000 lakh recovery suit, and quantifiable claims of ₹6,393.92 lakh in contingent liabilities. The DVAT matter remains unascertained.
  • Regulatory/pollution stoppages: GRAP restrictions in Delhi during November–January can halt construction.
  • Governance compliance: Board and committee composition non-compliances were flagged by auditors.
  • Subsidiary risks: HSCC’s Test to Treat contract has unresolved irregularities, with revenue recognition suspended and prior revenue reversed; HSCL and HSCC have board and committee non-compliances.
  • Execution risk: Investors repeatedly raised concern that order book conversion into revenue has lagged, with one noting that a ₹1,20,000 crore order book implies a run rate of ₹20,000–25,000 crore, and a ₹2 lakh crore order book implies ₹35,000 crore.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: As disclosed, forward-looking statements involve risks and uncertainties, and actual results may differ materially.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance provided: - FY27 revenue: ₹15,000–17,000 crore (later stated precisely as ₹16,000–17,000 crore consolidated); standalone ₹13,000–14,000 crore. - FY28 revenue: around ₹21,000 crore. FY29: ₹24,000–25,000 crore. - FY27 PAT: ₹1,100–1,200 crore consolidated. FY28: ₹1,300–1,400 crore. FY29: around ₹2,000 crore. - Margin guidance: 6–6.5% PAT, 6.5–7% minimum EBITDA. - Order awarding: ₹20,000–25,000 crore this year (against an earlier investor day indication of ₹30,000–32,000 crore); order inflow of ₹50,000–60,000 crore for the year. - Real estate revenue: approximately ₹500 crore from real estate sales this year. - Cash: ₹666 crore as on June 30.

Observed performance: - Q1 FY27 standalone revenue of ₹1,823 crore represents roughly 11% of the ₹16,000–17,000 crore consolidated full-year guidance; management noted Q1 is typically slower with pickup from Q2. - Q1 standalone PAT of ₹151 crore represents roughly 13% of the ₹1,100–1,200 crore consolidated PAT guidance. - Q1 order awarding of ₹1,700 crore consolidated is well below the ₹20,000–25,000 crore annual awarding guidance, though management stated quarter-to-quarter comparisons are not meaningful and expressed confidence in achieving the target. - Management stated that during the last three years, whatever was committed was achieved, and that NBCC received an “Excellent” rating after a gap of four to five years, scoring 97.50 as second-best PSU. - Management stated its projections exclude MAHAPREIT and international opportunities, describing these as potential upside. - Management acknowledged that profits from Ghitorni and Sector 37-D will come after two years, not immediately, and cited other real estate assets (including a Jaipur property in construction stage) as backup for projections.
