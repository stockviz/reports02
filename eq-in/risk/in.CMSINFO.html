CMS Info Systems — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Currency supply disruption. The most significant headwind in the quarter was an industry-wide cash supply shortage. Management had flagged cash supply from banks as “the one visible risk” entering FY27, and this risk materialised in Q1. The company quantified the impact at ₹25 Cr on ATM revenue for the quarter, describing it as the sharpest currency-supply disruption in a decade. Transaction impact varied by fill levels: ATMs that were well supplied (≥90% fill) saw a 1.1% YoY transaction decline, partly supplied ATMs (70–90% fill) saw a 6.7% decline, and under-supplied ATMs (<70% fill) saw a 27.2% decline. The situation was noted as improving from July.

Contracted revenue coverage fell. Entering FY27, about 85% of the year’s services revenue was contracted against a ₹2,000 Cr order book. Following the cash-supply disruption, roughly 70% of the indented amount of FY27 services revenue was contracted as of the Q1 update.

Cost inflation. Steep minimum-wage increases of 6% to 60% across key states, combined with fuel price increases over the last six to eight months (linked in the presentation to the Middle East crisis), raised operating costs. Repricing of contracts is underway to recover these costs: price increases are being secured across private-sector bank and retail contracts with a target of closure by Q2, while for public-sector banks the IBA is constituting a committee to engage with the industry, evaluate the cost impact, and approve revisions across contracts.

Product revenue at a cyclical trough. Product revenue fell to ₹10 Cr in Q1 FY27 versus ₹56 Cr a year earlier, as banks paused the refresh cycle in FY26.

Segment margin pressure. Managed Services EBIT margin fell to 10.3% in Q1 FY27 from 14.1% in both Q1 FY26 and Q4 FY26, impacted by the flow-through of lower BLA transaction revenue and a higher depreciation charge. Cash Logistics EBIT declined 18% YoY to ₹81 Cr even as revenue was broadly flat.

Profit decline. Despite record services revenue, PAT fell 10.6% YoY to ₹84 Cr, with depreciation and amortisation up 63.6% YoY and finance costs up 51.2% YoY. PAT margin contracted 173 bps YoY to 13.19%.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record services revenue. Services revenue reached a highest-ever ₹625 Cr, up ~9% YoY and ~3% QoQ, with EBITDA margin expanding 170 bps QoQ and 190 bps YoY to 27.2%. EBITDA grew 8.9% YoY to ₹173 Cr.

Order wins. New order wins of approximately ₹500 Cr were reported in Q1, led by the HDFC Bank integrated managed services mandate (6,000 ATMs, 5 years, ₹400 Cr TCV), which is now live. Two product mandates for roughly 1,000 currency recyclers were won with PSU banks, and two large PSU bank wins were secured in Technology & Payment Solutions for the HAWKAI Enterprise and ALGO MVS solutions. Large multi-year contracts have now been signed with three of India’s largest banks in 18 months.

Platform mix shift. Technology and Payments scaled from 7% of services revenue in FY22 to 16% in FY26, with HAWKAI scaled to 50,000+ sites and approximately ₹200 Cr revenue (2× in two years), and ALGO MVS deployed across 63,000+ ATMs, expected to ramp to 73,000+ by FY27. HAWKAI is described as India’s largest Vision AI platform for BFSI with 36%+ BFSI market share, expanded into 15 non-BFSI sectors, addressing a total TAM of ₹8,000 Cr (₹3,000 Cr BFSI, ₹5,000 Cr non-BFSI).

Refresh cycle resuming. The product revenue trough is described as cyclical, with the two recycler mandates won in Q1 and the refresh pipeline across large PSU banks in progress.

FY30 aspiration. Management targets services revenue of ₹3,750–3,950 Cr by FY30, with a large TAM of ₹20,000 Cr+, consolidation opportunity with pricing upside, and maintained margin quality and ROCE.

Capital allocation. FY27 capex is guided at ₹100–125 Cr against ₹351 Cr in FY26, described as materially lower, alongside a track record of high cash conversion (average OCF/EBITDA of ~71%), post-tax ROCE of 23.0%+ cumulative, and ₹438 Cr returned to shareholders.

Other developments. The company anticipates future expansion in its manufacturing business (Banking Automation, ATMs, and Kiosks), with potential growth driven by capital expenditure, investments, acquisitions, and strategic initiatives. The AGM agenda includes approval of increased borrowing limits and creation of mortgage or charge on company assets under Sections 180(1)(c) and 180(1)(a) of the Companies Act, 2013.

3. Key Risks

  • Currency supply risk: The Q1 disruption demonstrated that bank cash supply directly affects ATM transaction volumes and revenue; the risk was explicitly identified entering FY27 and materialised with a ₹25 Cr impact.
  • Contract repricing execution risk: Recovery of wage and fuel cost inflation depends on concluding price increases with private-sector banks and retail clients by Q2, and on IBA committee approval for public-sector bank contract revisions.
  • Revenue coverage risk: Contracted coverage of FY27 services revenue fell from about 85% to about 70% of the indented amount, leaving a larger share of the year’s revenue to be converted during the year.
  • Segment margin risk: Managed Services EBIT margin compression from lower BLA transaction revenue and higher depreciation.
  • Cost inflation risk: Minimum-wage increases of 6% to 60% across key states and elevated fuel prices.
  • Cyclical product revenue: Bank refresh cycles can pause, as seen in FY26, depressing product revenue.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The presentation notes known and unknown risks, uncertainties, and other factors that may cause actual results to differ materially from forward-looking statements.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

What management said entering FY27 (May): A ₹2,000 Cr order book into FY27, with about 85% of the year’s services revenue contracted; entering Q1 at a services run rate near ₹650 Cr; cash supply from banks as the one visible risk.

What happened in Q1 FY27: - Services revenue came in at ₹625 Cr — the highest ever, up ~3% QoQ and 9% YoY — below the ~₹650 Cr run-rate reference point, with the cash-supply risk materialising at a ₹25 Cr impact. - EBITDA margin was 27.2%, up 170 bps QoQ and 190 bps YoY. - Contracted coverage of FY27 services revenue fell to ~70% of the indented amount, against about 85% in May. - Total revenue was ₹635 Cr (+1.2% YoY, +0.3% QoQ); EBITDA ₹173 Cr (+8.9% YoY, +6.8% QoQ); PAT ₹84 Cr (−10.6% YoY, +5.8% QoQ).

Revised FY27 outlook: Management trimmed revenue slightly on the currency supply impact while raising the margin outlook, guiding to services revenue of ₹2,650–2,750 Cr, total revenue of ₹2,750–2,850 Cr, EBITDA margin of ~27%, and FY27 capex of ₹100–125 Cr against ₹351 Cr in FY26.

Longer-term guidance: FY30 aspiration of ₹3,750–3,950 Cr services revenue with 20–22% EBITDA margin, versus FY26 services revenue of ₹2,310 Cr at 26–28% margin.

Other forward-looking items: Repricing closure targeted by Q2 for private-sector bank and retail contracts; IBA committee process for public-sector bank contract revisions; ALGO MVS ATM coverage expected to ramp from 63,000+ to 73,000+ by FY27; anticipated expansion in the manufacturing business (Banking Automation, ATMs, and Kiosks) and potential growth from capital expenditure, investments, acquisitions, and strategic initiatives.
