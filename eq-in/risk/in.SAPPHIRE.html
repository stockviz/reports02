Sapphire Foods India — Q1 FY27 Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Weak consumer demand environment. Management stated there has been no material improvement in the demand environment, which has remained similar. April and May were good, June was not, and July benefited from the timing shift of Shravan in the North. The company noted that inflationary pressures continue and the external macro environment is not favouring consumer sentiment.

Pizza Hut India remains loss-making. Restaurant EBITDA was negative at 3.6% in Q1 FY27, down 110 bps YoY, impacted significantly by higher energy costs (majorly gas). This followed a full year FY26 at -3.3% restaurant EBITDA. Average daily sales per restaurant have fallen from ₹46k in FY24 to ₹41k in FY26, recovering only to ₹44k in Q1 FY27. Management stated Pizza Hut cannot expand until the brand proposition is fixed and a unified strategy between both franchisees is agreed, and that without expansion it will be difficult to drive double-digit growth.

Sri Lanka profitability pressure. Despite a healthy 9% SSSG in LKR and 14% revenue growth in LKR, restaurant EBITDA fell 70 bps YoY to 12.0%. Operating leverage from positive SSSG was offset by a minimum wage revision and high energy and food cost inflation arising out of geopolitical conflict. Management indicated normalisation may be at least a couple of quarters away.

Energy cost inflation. High energy cost inflation, mainly gas, was called out as a pressure point across both KFC India and Pizza Hut India, and in Sri Lanka.

ADS dilution from expansion. Management explained that new restaurants come in at 80–85% of brand average ADS, and over the last 2–2.5 years new additions pulled ADS levels down while SSSG was not positive. KFC average daily sales per restaurant declined from ₹125k in FY24 to ₹110k in FY26 before recovering to ₹118k in Q1 FY27.

Historical SSSG weakness. Three quarters prior, the company was generating negative SSSG and various campaigns were not working. KFC FY26 SSSG was challenging, with only 11% growth delivered for the year.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Broad-based positive SSSG. Q1 FY27 delivered positive SSSG across all three verticals — KFC India (+5%), Pizza Hut India (+1%, its first positive after five quarters), and Sri Lanka (+9% in LKR). Management described this as the heartening part of the quarter and the reason growth and EBITDA came through as they did.

Strong consolidated performance. Q1 FY27 was the second consecutive quarter of strong performance, with 15% revenue growth (best in 11 quarters) and 37% adjusted EBITDA growth (best in 15 quarters). Consolidated restaurant EBITDA rose 23% YoY with margin at 13.0% (up 80 bps); adjusted EBITDA margin was 8.4% (up 130 bps); EBITDA margin was 15.8% (up 120 bps); PBT margin was 1.8% (up 200 bps).

KFC value strategy working. The two-pronged consumer recruitment strategy — everyday value (₹99 Chicken Krisper Burger Meal) plus abundant value (BOGO on chicken buckets on select days) backed by marketing — drove dine-in and takeaway transaction growth. KFC dine-in and takeaway mix improved from 57% to 59%. KFC revenue grew 17% with restaurant EBITDA margin at 16.9% (up 120 bps YoY), aided by gross margin improvement, mix shift, and operating leverage.

Pizza Hut Tamil Nadu blueprint. The exclusive Tamil Nadu territory, supported by additional marketing investment and aggressive value dine-in offers (4-course meal at ₹99, Unlimited Pizza Friday, Buy 1 Get 3), continues to deliver significantly superior dine-in and takeaway performance versus the rest of India. Management believes a blueprint is ready, pending CCI approval for both franchisees to agree a common way forward.

Sri Lanka transaction momentum. Management emphasised the Sri Lanka business and team are very strong, with a deliberate strategy to keep driving transaction growth through shocks rather than protect short-term profitability.

Expansion runway. KFC guidance of 60–80 stores per year remains unchanged. Management reiterated a desire to double store count over a five-year horizon and described KFC and Pizza Hut QSR in India as a multi-decade opportunity, with 4,000–5,000 KFC stores in India possible eventually. Smaller-city stores were said to have similar profitability and payback to Tier 1 metro stores, with ~20% lower ADS offset by significantly lower costs.

Digital and channel initiatives. Digital kiosks rolled out across 75%+ of the KFC estate; KFC app downloads at 71.2 Mn+ with 2.7 Mn monthly active users; Pizza Hut app downloads at 29 Mn+ with 0.8 Mn+ MAU. Own delivery continues to deliver healthy growth. Delivery mix rose from 21% in FY19 to 43% in Q1 FY27.

Pizza Hut global sale viewed positively. Management stated the potential global sale of Pizza Hut at the parent level does not impact existing franchise agreements, and views a new brand owner with money on the table as positive for renewed brand focus.

3. Key Risks

  • Demand environment risk: Management explicitly said there has been no material improvement in demand and that the macro environment is not favouring consumer sentiment.
  • Pizza Hut brand risk: Continued negative restaurant EBITDA, cautious/zero expansion, and dependence on CCI approval and franchisee alignment before a unified strategy can proceed.
  • Sri Lanka macro risk: Susceptibility to shocks; cost of sales pressure from currency depreciation, minimum wage revision, and geopolitical-conflict-driven energy and food inflation.
  • Energy and input cost inflation: Gas cost inflation cited as a significant drag in India and Sri Lanka.
  • Expansion execution risk: New stores dilute ADS at 80–85% of brand average; strike rates are tracked internally and could trigger further moderation of expansion if they deteriorate.
  • Pricing power constraints: Management noted the pizza category has seen difficult pricing dynamics for two to three years, with price hikes restricted to 50–60% of inflation and the remainder managed through efficiencies.
  • Merger-related regulatory risk: The merger scheme between Sapphire Foods and Devyani International, approved by both boards in January 2026, remains subject to statutory and regulatory approvals from various authorities.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

KFC expansion guidance — maintained. Guidance of 60–80 KFC stores per year remains unchanged. Observed: 16 net KFC additions in Q1 FY27; KFC restaurant count rose from 575 at FY26 to 591 at June 30, 2026. Management noted some moderation versus two to three years ago when 80–90 stores were added annually, reflecting slow or negative SSSG.

Pizza Hut expansion guidance — maintained. Management reiterated it would remain cautious on Pizza Hut expansion in calendar year 2026, as in calendar year 2025. Observed: 5 net Pizza Hut additions in India in Q1 FY27 and 7 restaurants added in CY26; Pizza Hut India count rose from 341 at FY26 to 346 at June 30, 2026.

SSSG and operating leverage threshold. Management stated that typically 3–5% SSSG is neutral for the P&L against wage and other cost inflation, and that efficiencies this year created leverage even at 3–5% SSSG. Observed: KFC SSSG of 5% delivered 120 bps YoY restaurant EBITDA margin improvement to 16.9%; Pizza Hut SSSG of 1% was below the stated threshold and restaurant EBITDA remained negative at -3.6%.

Margin recovery expectations. Management stated the near-term focus is on driving SSSG rather than returning immediately to 17–19% restaurant EBITDA margins, and described KFC’s 16.9% margin as strong and healthy. Observed: KFC restaurant EBITDA margin of 16.9% in Q1 FY27 versus 16.3% in FY26 and 19.7% in FY24.

Price hike strategy. Management stated price hikes are typically restricted to 50–60% of inflation, with the balance managed through supply chain and P&L efficiencies. Observed: KFC took ~1% in April and 0.5–1% in June (total ~2% or slightly less); Pizza Hut took two instalments of 1% each (2–3% total). Management noted customer bills remained largely similar pre- and post-price hikes as customers readjust baskets, and that price hikes are a gross margin tool rather than a revenue driver.

Sri Lanka normalisation timing. Management indicated normalisation is at least a couple of quarters away. Observed: Q1 FY27 restaurant EBITDA of 12.0%, down 70 bps YoY, despite 9% LKR SSSG and 220 bps gross margin improvement.

Growth trajectory. Management stated that with 5–6% SSSG, KFC should be able to match foodservice growth of 15–20%. Observed: KFC delivered 17% revenue growth in Q1 FY27 with 5% SSSG, consistent with that framing. For Pizza Hut, management stated double-digit or 15% growth would be difficult until expansion resumes; observed Pizza Hut revenue growth was 3% in Q1 FY27.

Store expansion discipline. Management stated expansion decisions are guided by internal strike rates — whether new cohorts deliver expected ADS and whether 75–80% of stores achieve those strike rates — and that poor strike rates would trigger further moderation, as demonstrated by Pizza Hut going from 50–60 stores a period to 30–20 and then to zero in the last calendar year.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from acute concerns about margin erosion, demand slowdown, and Sri Lanka macro stress in 2023 to cautious optimism on revenue growth and margin expansion by 2026, but several core themes persisted in altered forms — demand weakness shifted from domestic QSR slowdown to discretionary spending weakness, input cost inflation migrated from milk/wheat to LPG/energy, and Sri Lanka moved from crisis to temporary pressure. Despite this shift toward bullish calls in the final reports, many of the original risks lingered unresolved.

Fears that came true

  • Industry & demand slowdown flagged in 2023 materialized as persistent weak SSSG and ‘broader demand environment has not materially improved’ in the last report, correlating with multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and sharp negative actual returns (e.g., -49.8% on 2024-04-16, -32.5% on 2024-12-04, -33.1% on 2025-07-23).
  • Input cost inflation in milk and wheat flagged in 2023 persisted and transformed into LPG/energy inflation as the ‘primary margin headwind’ per the last report, sustaining margin compression through multiple reporting periods.
  • Sri Lanka macro challenges flagged in 2023 re-emerged in the last report as profitability ‘temporarily pressured by rupee depreciation, wage inflation, and higher fuel/utility costs from geopolitical developments.’
  • Earnings revision cuts of 21.5% (FY24E) and 24.2% (FY25E) flagged in 2023 proved prescient, evidenced by successive successful Reduce/Sell calls with negative actual returns (-18.7%, -32.6%, -33.1%) across 2024-2025.
  • Higher per-store capex (Rs 200 mn KFC, Rs 150 mn Pizza Hut) and slowed expansion flagged in 2023 constrained operational flexibility, contributing to sustained earnings underperformance.
  • Profitability erosion from reduced operational efficiency flagged in 2023 persisted, with Pizza Hut India restaurant EBITDA margin still negative at -3.6% in the last report.

Optimism that failed

  • Sequential gross margin gains expected as commodity inflation cooled off did not materialize — Pizza Hut India EBITDA margin remained negative at -3.6% (down 110bps y/y) with LPG/energy inflation still the primary margin headwind in the last report.
  • Long-term target of adding 130-160 stores annually and doubling store counts over 3-4 years was not achieved, with the last report guiding only 60-80 annual KFC store additions.
  • Sri Lanka demand recovery anticipated by end of FY24E failed as the last report confirms Sri Lanka profitability was again ‘temporarily pressured’ by macro and geopolitical factors.
  • Robust domestic topline growth of 23% YoY flagged in 2023 did not translate into sustained positive stock returns, with numerous Buy and Add calls resulting in DISAPPOINTMENT (e.g., -26.1%, -24.2%, -32.5%, -28.8%, -23.2%).
