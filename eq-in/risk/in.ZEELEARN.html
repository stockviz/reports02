ZEELEARN: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Corporate guarantee and insolvency legacy. The company and its subsidiary Digital Ventures Private Limited (DVPL) had corporate guarantees invoked by Yes Bank Limited (notices dated 2 August 2021 and 9 August 2021) over non-repayment of credit facilities availed by four trusts/entity, with a demand of ₹44,962.56 lakhs including interest up to 31 July 2021. The credit facilities were assigned to J.C. Flowers Asset Reconstructions Private Limited (30 December 2022), and a settlement agreement dated 7 August 2023 to settle the obligation for ₹28,500 lakhs became effective in the quarter ended 31 March 2024. J.C. Flowers terminated that settlement agreement by letter dated 11 October 2024, and the outstanding credit facilities of the four trusts/entity (₹62,481.28 lakhs as on 11 October 2024) were assigned to Assets Care & Reconstruction Enterprise Limited (ACRE). A Supplemental Facilities Agreement dated 15 November 2024 was entered into by the company, DVPL, the four trusts/entity and promoter/promoter group entities with ACRE.

DVPL insolvency proceedings. Axis Bank Limited invoked a corporate guarantee on 14 February 2022 (₹9,162 lakhs outstanding as at 30 June 2021), and a second lender also invoked a guarantee (₹2,299.59 lakhs). The NCLT Mumbai admitted Axis Bank’s Section 7 application against DVPL on 19 November 2024 and appointed an Interim Resolution Professional; the NCLAT directed on 2 December 2024 that no further steps be taken by the IRP and that the agreed 20% cut-back arrangement continue with Axis Bank. Axis Bank then assigned the total credit facility of ₹13,008 lakhs (outstanding as at 20 March 2025; ₹13,021.19 lakhs as at 31 March 2025) to ACRE via an assignment agreement dated 28 March 2025.

Qualified audit opinion and going-concern uncertainty. The statutory auditors (Ford Rhodes Parks & Co. LLP) issued a qualified opinion on both standalone and consolidated results for the quarter and year ended 31 March 2025. The qualifications relate to: (a) non-assessment of impairment of the ₹69,458.74 lakhs recoverable from four trusts/entity under Ind AS 109; (b) non-provision of liability against corporate guarantee obligations to DVPL; and (c) the carrying value and recoverability of ₹34,388.10 lakhs of net investments/receivables from DVPL. The auditors also flagged a material uncertainty relating to going concern, noting that current liabilities exceeded current assets as at 31 March 2025, resulting in negative working capital.

Regulatory submission deficiency. On 4 July 2025, the company received an NSE communication through the NEAPS portal citing deficiencies in its financial results submission — that the results were not in the SEBI-prescribed format and were not signed by authorized signatory/ies. The company responded that it had submitted in the prescribed format to the best of its understanding, was unable to identify any deviation, and requested specification of the non-compliant sections.

Loss-making subsidiary being divested. Liberium Global Resources Private Limited, a wholly owned material subsidiary engaged in workforce solutions, staffing, payroll management and HR outsourcing, reported FY 2025-26 turnover of ₹10,490 lakhs (24% of consolidated turnover) but a PAT loss of ₹(809) lakhs, i.e. (21%) of consolidated PAT, with net worth of ₹112 lakhs (0.5% of consolidated net worth).

Segment performance. In the consolidated segment information for the year ended 31 March 2025, the “Construction and leasing (for education)” segment recorded a loss of ₹(2,012.62) lakhs, and “Training, manpower and related activities” recorded a loss of ₹(314.37) lakhs, against a profit of ₹9,020.88 lakhs in “Educational services and related activities.”

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Divestment of Liberium. On 29 July 2026, the Board approved divestment of the company’s entire shareholding — 1,000 equity shares of ₹10 each, constituting 100% of paid-up share capital — in Liberium Global Resources Private Limited to Creantum Security Solutions Private Limited, an unrelated third party, subject to shareholder approval. No consideration will be received on transfer of shares after shareholder approval. The agreement is to be entered into after member approval, with completion expected within three months of shareholder approval. The buyer is an active private company incorporated in December 2016, registered in Mumbai, operating in the security and surveillance/business support services sector; it does not belong to the promoter/promoter group, and the transaction is not a related party transaction.

Educational services segment profitability. The consolidated segment results for the year ended 31 March 2025 show the “Educational services and related activities” segment contributing ₹9,020.88 lakhs of segment results, the principal profit driver of the group.

Revenue growth in standalone operations. Standalone revenue from operations rose to ₹27,384.04 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2025 from ₹25,263.15 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2024. Standalone net profit after tax was ₹5,418.56 lakhs for FY 2024-25 versus ₹5,000.60 lakhs for FY 2023-24.

Consolidated revenue growth. Consolidated revenue from operations increased to ₹37,193.75 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2025 from ₹35,626.65 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2024.

Training, manpower segment revenue growth. Segment revenue for “Training, manpower and related activities” rose to ₹8,808.57 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2025 from ₹5,568.45 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2024.

Settlement framework with ACRE. Under the Supplemental Facilities Agreement dated 15 November 2024, the company, DVPL, the four trusts/entity and promoter/promoter group entities agreed additional conditions with ACRE, and certain promoter/promoter group entities created and extended security on their assets to ACRE’s satisfaction. The management believes the outstanding credit facilities will be paid to ACRE through steps including monetization of assets of DVPL along with the four trusts/entity and other security providers.

Board-approved business plan. The company’s business plan for the next financial year, as approved by the Board of Directors, exhibits higher growth in revenues and profits, which the company cites as supporting operational cash flows and the going-concern basis of preparation.

3. Key Risks

Recoverability of amounts from four trusts/entity. The company has not carried out an impairment assessment under Ind AS 109 of the ₹69,458.74 lakhs recoverable from four trusts/entity as at 31 March 2025, disclosed under “other current financial assets.” The auditors were unable to comment on adjustments, if any, required.

Non-provision for corporate guarantee obligations. Despite invocation of corporate guarantees and CIRP proceedings against DVPL, the company has not provided for liability against corporate guarantee obligations as at 31 March 2025 as required by applicable Indian Accounting Standards; the auditors were unable to comment on adjustments, if any.

Carrying value of DVPL investments and receivables. The company considers the net outstanding of ₹34,388.10 lakhs from DVPL as good and recoverable, having provided ₹140 lakhs towards impairment for the year ended 31 March 2025 (cumulative impairment of ₹21,927.05 lakhs till 31 March 2024). The auditors were unable to comment on the appropriateness of the net carrying value and recoverability.

Going-concern dependence on asset monetization. The going-concern basis rests on the total amounts payable to ACRE being settled through monetization of assets of DVPL along with four trusts/entity, and on the Board-approved business plan. The auditors noted these events indicate material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the company’s ability to continue as a going concern.

Pending Supreme Court matter. The J.C. Flowers Special Leave Petition against the NCLAT’s 16 February 2023 order (which set aside the NCLT’s 10 February 2023 admission of CIRP against the company) was allowed by the Supreme Court on 29 March 2023, which stayed further NCLT proceedings; the matter remains pending for hearing before the Supreme Court.

Interest non-provision on ADCB/DCB credit facilities. The company has not provided interest on term loan and overdraft facilities (originally from Abu Dhabi Commercial Bank, assigned to DCB Bank Limited) from 1 April 2023 through 31 March 2025, and continues to show the outstanding amounts as unsecured current borrowings, pending ascertainment of any liability arising out of the Deed of Assignment and Subrogation Agreement.

Divestment execution risk. The Liberium divestment requires shareholder approval, the agreement is to be entered into only after such approval, and completion is expected within three months of approval; no consideration will be received on transfer of shares.

Negative working capital. Current liabilities exceeded current assets as at 31 March 2025 at both standalone and consolidated levels.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Business plan guidance. The Board-approved business plan for the next financial year exhibits higher growth in revenues and profits, thereby increasing operational cash flows. This is cited in support of preparing the financial results on a going-concern basis. The auditors noted the material uncertainty relating to going concern but did not modify their opinion in respect of that matter.

Recoverability guidance versus audit position. Management strongly believes the ₹69,458.74 lakhs receivable from four trusts/entity as at 31 March 2025 is good and recoverable, and that the outstanding credit facility of DVPL will be paid to ACRE through steps including monetization of DVPL’s assets. The auditors were unable to comment on the impairment assessment and the non-recognition of liability, and the qualifications on these matters were noted as appearing for the second time (recoverable amount from four trusts/entity) and the fourth time (DVPL investments/receivables and corporate guarantee liability).

Observed performance versus prior periods. Standalone revenue from operations for the year ended 31 March 2025 was ₹27,384.04 lakhs (prior year ₹25,263.15 lakhs), with net profit after tax of ₹5,418.56 lakhs (prior year ₹5,000.60 lakhs). Consolidated revenue from operations was ₹37,193.75 lakhs (prior year ₹35,626.65 lakhs), with consolidated net profit after tax of ₹1,272.34 lakhs (prior year ₹15,569.39 lakhs, which included an exceptional gain of ₹13,845.88 lakhs from derecognition of MT Educare Limited as a subsidiary). Consolidated profit before tax after exceptional items for FY 2024-25 was ₹3,287.73 lakhs, after recognizing an exceptional item of ₹1,596.65 lakhs — differential interest recognized pursuant to the outstanding balance under the Axis Bank–ACRE assignment agreement.

Liberium performance versus group. Liberium contributed 24% of consolidated turnover in FY 2025-26 but a PAT loss of ₹(809) lakhs, i.e. (21%) of consolidated PAT, and 0.5% of consolidated net worth — the entity being divested for nil consideration.

Subsidiary results. The consolidated results include three subsidiaries whose financial statements (before consolidation adjustments) reflect total assets of ₹68,983.85 lakhs as at 31 March 2025, total revenues of ₹11,121.96 lakhs, total net loss after tax of ₹4,919.77 lakhs, and total cash inflows of ₹176.27 lakhs for the year ended on that date, audited by other auditors.

MT Educare derecognition. MT Educare Limited ceased to be a subsidiary with effect from 1 January 2024, after the Board resolved on 28 May 2024 not to exercise options under the IBC to revive it; the resultant gain of ₹13,845.88 lakhs was recognized as an exceptional item in the consolidated statement of profit and loss for the year ended 31 March 2024.
