Nitin Spinners (NITINSPIN) — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • U.S. tariff-related disruption: The company describes a subdued demand environment in the first half of the prior year following U.S. tariff developments, with prices falling substantially. The knit fabric business, which had a major presence in the U.S., is still affected by frequent changes in tariff rates; utilization is running at about 55–60% versus a pre-tariff norm of 60–65%, and management notes it has not yet returned to the 65% level.
  • Geopolitical and logistics disruption: The West Asia war is cited as causing global supply chain and logistics challenges, including non-availability of vessels, which left some stock undispatched at the end of Q1 FY27 (later dispatched in July). Increased freight costs and extended transit timelines are also flagged.
  • Raw material price volatility: Cotton prices rose about 8–10% during Q1 FY27, driven by lower global production and supply chain issues. Management notes Q2 is seeing further increases in raw material prices relative to the prior six months.
  • Cotton quality issues: Last year’s Indian cotton quality was not good, prompting spinners to import cotton even when international-domestic parity was broadly equal. The coming season’s quality depends on rainfall over the next four weeks.
  • Historical cotton price disadvantage: From end-2023 to mid-2025, Indian cotton was priced about 5–7% above the Cotlook Index, versus a normal position of 1–2% below it — a delta of roughly 7–8% that has only recently corrected.
  • Fabric margin pass-through lag: In the fabric business, only the actual raw material increase is being passed on; unlike yarn, margins cannot be improved upon. Management expects this to improve as cotton prices settle.
  • Structural industry risks: Volatile operating environment, competition (domestic and international), economic growth uncertainty in India and abroad, government policy and regulatory actions, interest and fiscal costs, time and cost overruns, ability to attract and retain skilled professionals, and ability to manage international operations.
  • Other flagged risks: Tariff and non-tariff barriers, technological gaps, labor shortages and skill gaps, increasing environmental compliance requirements, foreign currency exchange risk, customer concentration risk, and reputation risk.
  • Capacity absorption timing: Ramp-up of the new fabric capacity will take time — 6 to 8 months, with processing (fashion-oriented) taking longer; full fabric ramp-up may slip a quarter or two into FY28.
  • No buyback: Management states it is in a growth phase, deploying capital (internal accruals plus loans) for growth, so a buyback would not be prudent at this point.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Demand recovery: FY27 commenced positively, with momentum improving from the prior quarter following removal of additional U.S. tariffs. Yarn prices rose more than cotton prices, supported by healthy domestic and export demand.
  • Cotton price parity correction: Removal of import duty on cotton by the Government of India and improved international-domestic parity (now roughly 1–2% below Cotlook versus 5–7% above in 2023–24) is cited as a factor in industry performance improvement.
  • China yarn demand: Increased Chinese imports of cotton yarn supported demand for Indian spinners. India’s exports rose from roughly 90–100 million kg in calendar 2025 to 110–115 million in calendar 2026. A lower expected Chinese crop supports continuity of these imports.
  • Trade agreements: The recently implemented India–U.K. FTA is expected to open new sourcing and export opportunities; the EU FTA is expected to create a more level playing field against competing textile nations. Management says it is already in discussions with customers in these markets.
  • Industry consolidation: Weaker players exiting and unviable smaller capacities shutting down over the last one to two years have helped balance demand and supply, supporting current price increases. Not much new capacity is coming up in yarn and fabric segments.
  • Capacity expansion: Expansion of fabric capacity by 35 million meters and spinning capacity by 74,000 spindles is progressing on schedule. Weaving is expected to start in a couple of months, processing around Diwali, and spinning on stream by around December. Yarn capacity is expected to be fully ramped by March 31; fabric full ramp-up may take a quarter or two more in FY28.
  • Revenue contribution from new capacity: Management expects the expansion to add roughly Rs. 200–300 crores to revenues, with existing revenue slightly higher than last year. On the fabric side specifically, revenue is expected to rise from about Rs. 700 crores-plus in FY26 to about Rs. 1,200 crores in FY28 (roughly Rs. 500 crores added). Of the new yarn capacity (~22,000 metric tons), about 16,000–18,000 tons will go to fabric production; about 60% of new capacity output will be internally consumed and 40% sold.
  • Margin mix shift: Fabric is currently about 20–21% of top line and is expected to rise to about 30%, adding roughly 100–150 bps to overall margins.
  • Renewable power expansion: Progressing on schedule, expected operational by end of Q3 FY27. About Rs. 1.2–1.5 crores of benefit accrued in Q1 FY27; annual EBITDA-level savings are expected at around Rs. 50 crores, with full quarterly benefit from Q4. Blended power cost is expected around Rs. 5.50 per unit once capacity is fully running (renewables meeting about 60% of requirements). Post-expansion, clean energy is expected to exceed 50% of increased power consumption.
  • Cost savings: Energy savings, automation, and modernization of existing capabilities have produced permanent cost savings; power savings contributed about 0.5% of EBITDA improvement year-on-year.
  • Long-term industry outlook: Supported by India’s large manufacturing base, raw material availability, value-added product focus, innovation, sustainability, favorable government initiatives (PLI Scheme, MITRA Parks, Rajasthan Investment Promotion Scheme 2024), Kasturi Cotton Bharat revival, global sourcing shifts (rising Vietnam labor costs, Bangladesh political unrest, potential U.S. tariffs on Chinese textiles), e-commerce penetration, and expanding industrial/medical/technical textile applications.
  • Downstream ambitions: Management is evaluating garmenting and home textiles as future growth areas, noting both are key growth areas for Indian textiles, with the EU offering substantial opportunity in home textiles. No firm plan or capex is committed beyond FY28.

3. Key Risks

  • Margin sustainability: Management declined to commit to margin improvement, stating only that it expects to maintain current levels absent extraordinary global or geopolitical changes. It reiterated the guided band of 16–20%.
  • Inventory valuation: A small portion of the margin improvement came from inventory gains on cotton; the company does not carry extraordinary inventories and balances inventory with sales.
  • Volume optics: Yarn volumes declined quarter-on-quarter (~14%) and year-on-year (~7%), offset by higher cloth volumes (~7% up). Management attributes this to higher in-house consumption by fabric divisions and logistics-related stock build, not demand weakness.
  • Capex execution and ramp-up risk: Fabric processing ramp-up is fashion-oriented and slower; full utilization may extend into FY28.
  • Geographic concentration: Exports contributed about 65% of Q1 FY27 revenue (61.9% in FY26), exposing the company to tariff, currency, and geopolitical risk.
  • Policy dependence: Growth prospects are tied to FTAs, government incentives, and tariff decisions that are outside the company’s control.
  • Incentive structure: Capital subsidy does not flow through revenues/EBITDA; interest subsidy is part of EBITDA, so margin profile does not change materially from incentives, though net profit increases.
  • Disclosure limitations: Management declined to disclose cotton inventory positions and marketing strategies.

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from ICICI Direct’s bullish Buy (May 2023) — driven by demand recovery, capacity expansion, and margin/cost tailwinds — to Systematix’s Not Rated (Feb 2024) after the stock had already risen sharply. Persisting themes were improving capacity utilisation, export yarn demand, and value-added products; the changed theme was margins, as early spread-expansion optimism gave way to weak yarn pricing and high raw-material costs relative to sales.

Fears that came true

  • The early macro/demand-pressure fear materialised in weak pricing: yarn prices fell ~7% q/q and finished-product prices dipped, with yarn-price improvement elusive.
  • The raw-material/margin fear materialised as raw material costs were 64% of sales vs a normal 60%, keeping operating margins below normal.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of continued spread expansion/cost moderation did not hold — although cotton softened, raw-material costs still weighed on margins and margins remained below normal.
