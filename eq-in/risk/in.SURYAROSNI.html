Surya Roshni — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Steel Pipes & Strips - Galvanised pipes remained soft due to continued delays in government fund releases for state-led projects. - Q1 FY27 volumes came in at ~2.28–2.30 lakh tonnes against an internal expectation of ~2.65 lakh tonnes. Management attributed the shortfall to: ~7,000 tonnes of material stranded at port due to vessel unavailability; lower Middle East orders (down ~8,000–10,000 tonnes) amid an extended regional situation; and an ~8,000–10,000 tonne shortfall in API spiral. - Ocean freight spiked to ~$3,800 per tonne on exports, creating an ~₹800/tonne EBITDA drag in the quarter because older orders had been booked at prior freight rates. Coating material, gas and ORM input cost increases added ~₹200/tonne of impact. - Middle East volumes fell to roughly one-third of the normal 15,000–20,000 tonnes per quarter; API order flow showed almost 50% de-growth. - Spiral pipe EBITDA/tonne fell to ₹3,420 in Q1 FY27, with coating material a significant factor; the API/spiral mix shift between higher-margin API spiral and more competitive water pipe affects realisations. - The order book declined from ~₹1,000 crore at end-March to ~₹800 crore, reflecting Middle East softness and slower API flows. - Steel prices rose ₹3,000–4,000 in April 2026 before declining in May and June; overall the quarter was roughly flat, so no material inventory loss was reported. - Rising copper and steel input costs made distributors cautious about carrying fan inventory, leaving fans relatively flat. - Government-linked project spending domestically was slower than management would like.

Lighting & Consumer Durables - An input cost increase of ~7% (net sales realisation basis; ~10% gross) was passed through during the quarter. - Export markets for lighting were flattish — attributed to logistics economics rather than demand, with freight costs in some cases exceeding material value, causing customers to delay bookings. - LPG availability tightness aided induction cooktop volumes; that tightness has begun to ease, leaving the underlying run-rate unclear over the next couple of months.

Company-level / Other - Net working capital cycle was 72 days; ROCE 12.69% and ROE 8.95% in Q1 FY27. - Working capital increased amid steel price volatility, tying up money. - Management flagged global geopolitical uncertainty as a factor in the timing of corporate actions (buyback/demerger). - The company’s own risk disclosures cite fluctuations in earnings, growth management, domestic and international competition, economic growth in India and abroad, talent retention, time and cost overruns on contracts, international operations management, government policies and regulations, and interest/fiscal costs.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Steel Pipes & Strips - Q1 FY27 was the highest-ever Q1 sales volume: 2.28 lakh tonnes, up 21% YoY; revenue ₹1,590 crore, up 32% YoY; EBITDA ₹84 crore, up 63% YoY; EBITDA/tonne ₹4,006 vs ₹2,922. - Broad-based growth: section pipes +38% YoY, ERW API pipes +207% YoY (export volumes +377% after the US market opened, domestic API +41%), spiral non-API water pipes +36%, GP pipes +53%, cold rolled +30%. - Value-added products reached ~47% of volumes, with a target above 50% over the next two years; exports were ~20% of segment volumes, with a path to ~25%. - Order book of ~₹800 crore, including 78,000 tonnes of export API orders for the US as of July 1, 2026. US business expected at 1,20,000–1,25,000 tonnes in FY27, ~10–11% of segment volumes; total exports seen around 3 lakh tonnes. - Three new DFT mills being commissioned across Anjar, Malanpur and Bahadurgarh between August and December 2026; capacity targeted at ~16 lakh tonnes in FY27 and ~20 lakh tonnes by FY30, adding 2–3 lakh tonnes annually. - New capacity expansion in South (Hindupur): 10 acres of adjacent land taken, ~15,000 tonnes additional setup, first mill commissioning January 2027, four-mill plant with ~₹60 crore investment, adding ~3 lakh tonnes. - Cost reduction programme targeting ₹1,100/tonne via automation (including pipe coating), rooftop solar, energy audits and phased replacement of older mills. Solar: 1 MW commissioned at each of three steel plants (~₹10–11 crore), 1 MW under installation at Bahadurgarh (~₹3 crore), feasibility study at Malanpur; expected savings ~₹3 crore per annum. Electric trucks and trailers under evaluation for logistics. - Break-bulk tie-ups for the US market expected to reduce freight sensitivity. - Marketing budget for the steel business raised to ₹20 crore for FY27 from ₹7 crore, with a specialist agency onboarded. - Unique manufacturing breadth: MS pipes from 0.6 mm to 25 mm thickness and half an inch to 140 inches. - Export order from North America worth US$2.96 million (₹28.21 crore) for lightly oiled, prime hot rolled tube ASTM A500 Grade B/C, to be executed up to October 2026. - Demand outlook cited: government thrust on high investments, CGD, Smart Cities, Nal se Jal, expected ₹5 lakh crore investment over 5 years, public and private capex in airports, metros, urbanisation, Jal Jeevan Mission and river inter-linking; high steel pipe demand across the Gulf, Europe and USA; sustained recovery in urban real estate supporting non-agricultural pipes.

Lighting & Consumer Durables - Strongest-ever first quarter: revenue ₹456 crore, up 15% YoY; EBITDA ₹36 crore, up 17% YoY; margins 7.9% vs 7.7%; PBT ₹24 crore, up 12%. - Broad-based growth: LED bulb volumes +46% YoY, battens +32%, downlighters +16%, induction cooktops +52%, water heaters +26%, appliances overall +10%; BLDC fans posted modest growth. - Professional lighting order book ~₹150 crore, with healthy institutional and tender demand and government spending continuing. - Wires & Cables achieved close to FY26 sales volume within Q1 FY27 alone, supported by the Direct Benefit Transfer electrician loyalty programme (enrolments up from ~18,000 to ~36,000 by early August 2026), emergence of exclusive wire distributors, new 180-metre reel packs, a 200-metre pack for large project/institutional buyers, and brand registrations with housing boards and government bodies. FY26 wire revenue target of ₹250 crore, on track with the three-year ₹500 crore guidance. - 3 MW rooftop solar commissioned across two lighting factories. - Growth plans: deeper distribution penetration in Tier 2/Tier 3 markets, premiumisation, brand-building including the ‘INDIA BOLE SURYA KO YA’ campaign, regional launches, institutional and gifting channels including Gift Expo, and expanding in-house manufacturing at Kashipur. ~80% of lighting production is in-house, ~20% outsourced with company quality control.

3. Key Risks

  • Export profitability is exposed to ocean freight volatility; the Q1 impact was ~₹800/tonne on blended EBITDA, with Middle East freight having risen to ~$3,800/tonne.
  • Middle East demand disruption and geopolitical uncertainty, with volumes down to roughly one-third of normal quarterly levels.
  • Delay in government fund releases affecting galvanised pipes and government-linked project spending.
  • Slow materialisation of API tenders despite tenders being floated.
  • Steel price fluctuation, which management states is not in its control; inventory loss/gain effects persist despite a rising value-added mix.
  • Domestic and international competition, including in the more competitive spiral water pipe segment.
  • Execution and timing risk on capacity expansion: three DFT mills commissioning between August and December 2026, Hindupur first mill January 2027, and annual additions of 2–3 lakh tonnes.
  • Freight economics in lighting exports causing customer booking delays even where demand and enquiries remain strong.
  • Input cost pressure across coating material, gas, ORM, copper and steel.
  • Working capital intensity (72-day cycle) and the drag from steel price swings on cash.
  • The company’s stated risk factors also include growth management, talent attraction and retention, contract time and cost overruns, international operations management, government policy and regulation, and interest/fiscal costs.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Steel EBITDA per tonne - Full-year FY27 guidance reiterated at ₹4,600–4,700/tonne. - Q1 FY27 actual: ₹4,006/tonne, against an internal expectation of ~₹4,700 — a shortfall management attributed to ~₹800/tonne of ocean freight and ~₹200/tonne of input cost increases. - Management expects ~₹4,400–4,500/tonne in Q2 and ₹4,600–4,700/tonne for the full year, with fresh orders booked at higher freight rates expected to add ~₹600–1,000/tonne. In July, the freight impact was described as very low, and expected to be absent in the following two months.

Steel volumes - Full-year FY27 target: 11 lakh tonnes. Q1 actual: 2.28–2.30 lakh tonnes (vs ~2.65 lakh tonnes expected). Management guided Q2 at 2.6–2.65 lakh tonnes, Q3 at 2.8–2.9 lakh tonnes and Q4 at ~3.2 lakh tonnes, and stated the 11 lakh tonne target would be met across the remaining three quarters with ~22–23% growth.

Company-level FY27 - Guidance: revenue ~₹9,400–9,500 crore and EBITDA ~₹670–680 crore. Q1 actual: revenue ₹2,046 crore, EBITDA ₹120 crore. Management guided ~₹150 crore EBITDA in Q2 and ~₹400 crore across H2. - Lighting: FY27 revenue target ~₹2,200 crore (from ₹1,809 crore) with ~20–22% growth and ~₹200 crore EBITDA; separately, FY27 guidance of 22–23% value growth and 25% volume growth was reiterated as fully on track. Q1 actual: ₹456 crore revenue, ₹36 crore EBITDA, 15% revenue growth.

Spiral/API EBITDA per tonne - Management expects improvement from ₹3,420/tonne in Q1 FY27 toward ₹5,000/tonne for the full year, against ₹5,500–5,700/tonne last year.

Exports - Export share of steel segment volumes: 17% in FY26, 20% in Q1 FY27, guided to ~25%. US share expected at ~8–10% of the segment, with total FY27 exports around 3 lakh tonnes.

Capacity - Guidance: ~16 lakh tonnes in FY27 and ~20 lakh tonnes by FY30 (the transcript references ~2 million tonnes by FY28–29), with 2–3 lakh tonnes added annually. Current capacity is 14 lakh tonnes, with three DFT mills commissioning between August and December 2026.

Corporate actions - A buyback was discussed on the prior call; management said work is ongoing, the double-taxation angle has been corrected by the government, and a decision is expected soon, though no material disclosure was made. On demerger, management said both businesses are strong standalone and that the board will decide between buyback and demerger, with no timeline committed. Management also stated it sees no logic in not doing the demerger.

Other - Net working capital cycle: 72 days in Q1 FY27. ROCE 12.69%, ROE 8.95%. Zero-debt with net cash surplus of ₹154–155 crore as of June 30, 2026. - The 53rd AGM was held on September 15, 2026, and all eight resolutions were passed with the requisite majority through remote e-voting and e-voting during the AGM. The Board approved Q1 FY27 results, declared a final dividend of ₹2.50 per share with a record date of August 21, 2026, and approved the re-appointment of Mr. Jai Prakash Agarwal as Whole-time Director designated Executive Chairman and Mr. Vinay Surya as Managing Director.

Broker Narrative

The early narrative was bullish (Buy), focused on strong domestic demand from infrastructure/construction/auto and stable margins despite weak base-metal prices. The last report turned cautious (Hold), citing an earnings miss, FY26 guidance cut, LCD pricing pressure, and inventory losses. Steel pipe volume growth and oil & gas demand persisted as positive themes, but the emphasis shifted from macro demand optimism to margin/execution risk and demand disruptions.

Fears that came true

  • Falling steel prices—an early headwind—materialized again in July, causing ~₹500/ton inventory losses in the steel pipe segment and contributing to the Q2FY26 earnings miss and guidance cut.
  • The weak metal-pricing environment continued to pressure earnings, correlating with the repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and negative actual returns across the call history.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that higher volumes and lower raw-material costs would keep EBITDA margin stable failed to protect earnings: FY26 consolidated EBITDA guidance was cut to ₹620–625 crore from ₹700 crore despite volume growth.
  • The optimism around strong domestic demand and order intake was not reflected in returns: subsequent Buy calls saw actual returns of -14%, -39%, -42%, and -0.2%, with most ending DISAPPOINTMENT or FLAT.
