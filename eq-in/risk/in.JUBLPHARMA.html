JUBLPHARMA — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Radiopharmaceuticals — SPECT product unavailability. The most significant margin drag in the quarter was the unavailability of high-margin SPECT radiopharmaceutical products due to lower production at the CMO Montreal facility. This was the primary reason Radiopharmaceuticals EBITDA margin fell to 34% in Q1 FY27 from 46% in Q1 FY26 (a 1,250 bps decline), even as revenue grew 19% to ₹322 Cr. Management quantified the Q1 FY27 revenue impact at approximately $7 Mn.

CMO Montreal — remediation and warning letter. In Q1 FY27, the Montreal facility received a Warning Letter following USFDA review of the EIR for an inspection conducted October 20 – November 3, 2025. The company has responded to the FDA with requested information and an update on an independent third-party assessment. Incremental remediation costs — primarily additional external oversight — are expected to continue into FY27. The site is expected to run lean operations in FY27 and FY28 until the new isolator-based Line 5 becomes operational in FY29.

CDMO Sterile Injectables margin compression. Despite 34% revenue growth to ₹496 Cr., EBITDA fell 28% to ₹45 Cr. and margins contracted 780 bps to 9%. Drivers cited: negligible third-party revenues and higher operating expenses including incremental remediation cost at Montreal. Revenue was also lower quarter-on-quarter due to a planned maintenance shutdown.

Radiopharmacy — competitive intensity. High competitive intensity persists in the SPECT radiopharmacy segment, with margin pressure expected to continue. Segment EBITDA margin remained flat at 2%.

Allergy Immunotherapy — lower production. Although revenue grew 18% to ₹214 Cr., EBITDA rose only 5% to ₹66 Cr. and margins fell 400 bps to 31% due to lower production.

API — pricing pressure and raw material inflation. Revenue declined 4% to ₹135 Cr. and EBITDA fell 12% to ₹19 Cr. amid industry-wide pricing pressure. Raw material cost increases linked to the Middle East conflict are expected to pressure margins in subsequent quarters.

Generics — product mix. Revenue grew 4% to ₹173 Cr. on two new product launches, but EBITDA fell 65% to ₹4 Cr. and margins dropped 480 bps to 2% due to a change in product mix. Trailing twelve-month margin was approximately 10%.

CRDMO Drug Discovery — competitive intensity. In the short term, competitive intensity is expected to increase in the large-pharma customer segment.

Consolidated profitability. EBITDA declined 11% to ₹268 Cr. with margins down 385 bps to 11.9%. Reported PAT fell 45% to ₹56 Cr., with margins down 285 bps to 2.5%, attributed to lower operating profitability and higher depreciation (including Line 3 at Spokane). Net Debt/EBITDA rose from 1.5x in March 2026 to 1.8x in June 2026 as the company invests in future growth.

Broader industry headwinds referenced. Intense competition, geopolitical uncertainties, large patent expirations, implementation of federal drug price negotiations, and ongoing geopolitical shifts.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Broad-based revenue growth. Q1 FY27 revenue rose 17% year-on-year to ₹2,229 Cr., with growth across all business segments. Total income rose 18% to ₹2,249 Cr.

CDMO Sterile Injectables — Line 3 ramp and capacity expansion. Spokane revenue grew 43% to ₹494 Cr. and EBITDA grew 49% to ₹117 Cr., with margins expanding 100 bps to 24%. More than 10 products are undergoing technology transfer on Line 3, and one of the world’s largest oncology products has been onboarded, with commercial batch production expected in late FY27 subject to FDA approval. Line 4 installation is complete, with technology transfer revenues expected from Q4 FY27. Combined Line 3 and Line 4 peak revenue potential is estimated at USD 160–180 Mn. Line 5 at Montreal (USD 114 Mn capex, USD 35 Mn concessional Canadian government loan) is expected to generate technology transfer revenues from FY29. New US tariffs are pushing innovator companies toward US-based manufacturing, driving strong RFP traction.

Radiopharmaceuticals — supply normalization and pipeline. Commercial batch production for SPECT products started at CMO Montreal in Q1 FY27, with batches expected to be released in Q2 FY27 and all SPECT products available by H2 FY27. Ruby-Fill® install base grew 26% annualized in Q1 FY27, with margin improvement in that category. A new PET/SPECT product pipeline with a TAM of USD 535 Mn is slated for launch from FY28–FY29. MIBG Phase 2 dosing is complete, with NDA filing expected in H2 FY27 and peak sales potential of USD 70–100 Mn.

Radiopharmacy — PET network expansion. Revenue grew 17% to ₹700 Cr. on higher PET product volumes, with EBITDA up 19% to ₹12 Cr. The company is investing USD 60+ Mn to expand from 3 to 9 PET manufacturing sites by FY28, positioning it as the second-largest radiopharmacy network in the US. Partnerships include Life Molecular Imaging’s Neuraceq, Lantheus’ Pylarify, and Novartis’ Pluvicto.

Allergy Immunotherapy. Revenue growth of 18% was driven by both US and outside-US markets. The business is the sole US supplier of Venom and the No. 2 player in US subcutaneous allergy immunotherapy, with a three-pronged strategy: strengthen US position, expand internationally, and increase R&D investment.

CRDMO Drug Discovery. Revenue grew 8% to ₹174 Cr. and EBITDA grew 43% to ₹45 Cr., with margins expanding 630 bps to 26% on better revenue mix and forex gain. The Biosecure Act (enacted December 2025) and “friendshoring” trends favor India-based CROs. Capacity is planned to expand from 1,000 FTEs (FY25) to 4,000 FTEs (FY30), with total capex of USD 150 Mn and expected RoCE above 20%. A strategic partnership with Pierre Fabre added USD 1.4 Bn. in mAbs and ADC TAM.

Generics. Two new products launched in Q1 FY27; 12 ANDA approvals secured since April 2024; 6–8 product launches expected in FY27.

Proprietary Novel Drugs. JBI-802 Phase I/II trial in ET/MPN is ongoing with preliminary data showing rapid, durable, dose-dependent platelet normalization; an investigator-led NSCLC trial is ongoing at Christ Hospital, Ohio. JBI-778 Phase I first-in-human trial has launched in India. Clinical data readouts are expected in CY2026, with monetization through licensing or external fundraising being explored.

USFDA VAI classification at Spokane. The USFDA concluded its review of the EIR for the inspection conducted at Jubilant HollisterStier LLC’s CMO facility in Spokane, Washington, from June 8–17, 2026, and classified the facility as Voluntary Action Indicated (VAI).

Vision 2030. The company aspires to double revenue from ₹6,703 Cr. (FY24) to ₹13,500 Cr. by FY30, with EBITDA margin of 23–25%, zero net debt, and high-teens RoCE. TTM Q1 FY27 revenue stood at ₹8,608 Cr. with 15% EBITDA margin.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory risk: The Montreal Warning Letter and ongoing remediation; FDA approval dependency for commercial batch production on Line 3; the Spokane inspection outcome (VAI) and ongoing observation responses.
  • Supply concentration risk: Dependence on CMO Montreal for SPECT products, with mitigation via technology transfer programs to third-party CMOs and CMO Spokane (18–24 month timelines).
  • Competitive risk: High competitive intensity in SPECT radiopharmacy and in the large-pharma segment of Drug Discovery; industry-wide pricing pressure in API.
  • Input cost and geopolitical risk: Raw material cost inflation linked to the Middle East conflict expected to pressure API margins in subsequent quarters.
  • Tariff and trade risk: US tariffs; however, approximately 78% of goods and services sold in the US originate in the US, 14% from Canada (radiopharmaceuticals exempt under US-Canada-Mexico trade agreement), and 8% from India (generics and APIs, with a US CMO network as mitigation). Management expects an overall positive tariff impact, particularly on CDMO Sterile Injectables, with no material negative impact elsewhere.
  • Leverage risk: Net Debt/EBITDA increased from 1.5x to 1.8x; Net Debt/Equity rose from 0.28 to 0.33; interest coverage declined from 6.3 to 6.0.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: Statements are subject to numerous risks and uncertainties, and actual results may differ materially.
  • Broader industry risks: Large patent expirations, federal drug price negotiations, and geopolitical shifts.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Margin trajectory. Management guides that EBITDA margins will start to expand from H2 FY27 onwards as SPECT production normalizes, describing FY27 as “a story of two halves.” Observed Q1 FY27 performance showed margins contracting 385 bps year-on-year to 11.9%, consistent with the guided first-half weakness.

Radiopharmaceuticals normalization. Management expects revenue and EBITDA to normalize from H2 FY27. Observed Q1 FY27: revenue grew 19% but EBITDA declined 13% with margins down 1,250 bps — the anticipated trough.

CDMO Sterile Injectables. Management guides Line 3 commercial batch production in late FY27 and Line 4 technology transfer revenues from Q4 FY27. Observed Q1 FY27: revenue grew 34% on Line 3 tech transfer ramp, but margins compressed to 9% on negligible third-party revenues and Montreal remediation costs.

Montreal remediation. Management guides incremental remediation costs to continue into FY27 and lean operations through FY28, with Line 5 revenues from FY29. Observed Q1 FY27: remediation costs contributed to CDMO-SI margin decline.

API margins. Management guides margin pressure in FY27 from raw material inflation, with focus on high-margin products to offset. Observed Q1 FY27: revenue declined 4% and EBITDA declined 12%.

Generics. Management guides margins to improve in subsequent quarters, with 6–8 launches in FY27. Observed Q1 FY27: margins fell to 2% on product mix, with TTM margin at ~10%.

Drug Discovery. Management guides healthy revenue growth and steady margins in the medium term. Observed Q1 FY27: revenue grew 8% and margins expanded 630 bps to 26%.

FY28 inflection. Management expects FY28 to be an inflection year driven by new radiopharmaceutical products, commercial production from Line 3, and Line 4 technology-transfer revenues, supporting healthy margin expansion through FY30.

Capex. Management guides a similar capex outlay in FY27 as in FY26.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from a cautious Accumulate centred on 1QFY24 operational misses, margin/tax/interest drags and subdued ROE/ROCE to an aggressive Buy built on CDMO Sterile remediation, radiopharma expansion, strategic capex and a FY30 margin/RoCE vision. Persistent themes were margin pressure, API weakness and radiopharma growth; the change was from US generic/API and tax/interest worries toward regulatory, competitive-radiopharma and near-term supply risks.

Fears that came true

  • The early margin-contraction warning materialized: Q3FY26 EBITDA margin fell 214 bps YoY to 13.6%, and the stock subsequently delivered large negative actual returns (-23.5%, -25.0%, -20.9%).
  • Operational/profitability misses recurred: lower Allergy production and a CDMO Sterile facility shutdown for remediation hit Q3FY26 profits, echoing the first report’s operational-miss warning.
  • The API weakness flagged early persisted: CDMO-API revenue declined 9% YoY in Q3FY26, with the 2025-11-02 Hold call followed by a -20.9% actual return.

Optimism that failed

  • The projected EBITDA margin expansion of 413 bps by FY25E failed to sustain: Q3FY26 EBITDA margin contracted 214 bps YoY to 13.6%, with EBITDA flat at ₹289 crore.
  • The ‘compelling valuation’ support failed: despite 12x Jun’26 EPS, the stock delivered negative actual returns after subsequent Buy calls (-23.5% after 2025-06-05 and -25.0% after 2025-07-11).
