Power Mech Projects — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure in Q1 FY27. Consolidated EBITDA for the quarter was ₹176 crores at a 10.8% margin, a 3% decline year-on-year. Management attributed this to higher material and execution costs from the ongoing Middle East conflict, an increase in royalty cost in the KRBM project following government orders on royalty sharing for seized quantities, lower margins in the KBP mining business due to higher overburden (OB) removal cost from opening new seams, and lower other income. Standalone EBITDA margin, however, improved to 11.3% from 10.2% a year earlier.

KRBM royalty regime. The government introduced a change whereby 50% of the penalty collected on seized quantities of private pattas and river dredging is retained by the government and 50% passed to the company. The government also increased the royalty rate by ₹10 per metric ton as an additional royalty payment, plus MITF charges at 5% of royalty value. KRBM EBITDA margin fell from around 14% annually to 10% in Q1 FY27, with 10–11% expected for the remaining quarters. Increased enforcement has also substantially reduced royalty avoidance and seized quantities, with only around ₹30 crores of seized-quantity revenue recognized in Q1 FY27 versus ₹51 crores in Q1 FY26.

KBP mining cost timing. Production began from new seams where initial OB removal is higher, so Q1 carried higher OB cost and lower coal production. Management stated the overall stripping ratio remains within the agreed ratio and that this normalizes in subsequent quarters.

ETC business weakness. Industrial construction revenue fell 13% year-on-year to ₹217 crores, with no order inflow in the segment during the quarter. An accident at the Athena Vedanta project disrupted activities for three to four months, impacting Q1 revenue; work has resumed. The Udupi FGD project is near completion. Some erection and civil teams were redeployed to in-house BOP EPC activities.

Input cost inflation. Base metal prices raised steel and alloy steel costs; LNG price spikes early in the war period fed into construction gas costs; diesel prices rose over the preceding two months, affecting mining. Price variation clauses (PVC) compensate for most increases but can lag by a quarter when indices update, and public-sector clients follow fixed rules, in extreme cases requiring claims management or dispute resolution.

Middle East disruption. Management described disruptive conditions prevailing in the Middle East as the current focus constraint on international operations, while expressing hope the situation improves.

Execution capacity ceiling in power plant construction. Management stated it believes it has maximized execution potential at 14–15 boilers of 800 MW simultaneously, with only one or two additional boilers per year expected, contributing 5–7% growth in that specific activity.

BHEL tendering delays and packaging. BHEL’s tendering has been delayed for various reasons, including packaging issues; BHEL’s vertical composite/integrated package approach differs from Adani’s end-to-end construction packages, which management prefers.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong topline growth. Q1 FY27 total revenue was ₹1,632 crores, up 26% year-on-year, driven by civil infrastructure, industrial EPC, O&M, and international projects. PAT was ₹89 crores (up 11%), PAT after minority interest was ₹80 crores versus ₹53 crores (up 53%), and EPS was ₹25.23 versus ₹16.61.

Order book. Total order backlog including MDO projects was approximately ₹55,398 crores; excluding MDO, the executable order book was around ₹16,229 crores, up from ₹15,898 crores at the start of the year. Q1 FY27 order inflows were approximately ₹1,864 crores against an annual target of ₹12,000 crores (about 15.5%). O&M order backlog rose 11.8% to ₹3,322 crores after ₹781 crores of first-quarter O&M orders, including O&M of Mumbai Monorail, marking entry into urban mobility.

Power sector opportunity pipeline. Management mapped ₹70,000–75,000 crores of opportunities at the start of the year; as of the call, about ₹20,500 crores in the power sector and about ₹8,500 crores in infrastructure were being tracked. Roughly 58,000 MW of ordering has been tracked across BHEL and L&T over three years; BHEL has orders of about ₹2.41 lakh crores and L&T about ₹42,523 crores. JSW has entered with two plants (Salboni, West Bengal and Assam), four 800 MW units each (6,400 MW total), with investment exceeding ₹70,000 crores. Adani plans to raise capacity from 18,330 MW to 42,000 MW over five to six years; NTPC plans coal-based capacity from 67 GW to 91 GW; JSW plans from 5,658 MW to 30,000 MW over five to seven years. Management expects an immediate opportunity of ₹25,000–30,000 crores, with a power-sector bid strike rate of 60–65%.

Government capacity addition. The government plans to add 63 GW, from 228 GW to 291 GW by 2032, and to 321 GW by 2036 — an average of 8,000–10,000 MW per year. This is expected to open ₹800 crores to ₹1,000 crores per annum of O&M opportunity, in addition to renewals of existing contracts.

MDO ramp-up. KBP revenue was ₹57.54 crores and Tasra ₹26.34 crores in Q1 (mining segment total ₹84 crores, up 223% year-on-year). Management targets 3 million tons at KBP this year (up to 4–4.2 million tons next year) and 1.2–1.3 million tons at Tasra once the washery is hot-commissioned around November–December. Combined MDO revenue target is ₹500 crores this year and ₹1,100–1,200 crores in FY28. MDO EBITDA margin is currently 15%, expected at 15–17% next year and 21–23% once both mines reach peak rated capacity (blended 20–21% per the CFO), with Tasra peak rated capacity of 3.5–4 million tons including 0.5 million tons of Jama coal.

O&M growth. O&M revenue was ₹431 crores in Q1, up 8% year-on-year. Management targets about 28% growth for the year, from ₹1,760 crores to about ₹2,089 crores, with O&M margins around 18% and a 30–35% share of overall business. Five plants are under long-term O&M contracts, indicating public-sector interest in outsourcing.

New order wins and sector expansion. A major JSW order of ₹850 crores for civil and structures at the Salboni project. Vedanta Power Limited awarded an end-to-end O&M contract at the 2x600 MW Sakti Thermal Plant valued at ₹970 crores, to be executed within 60 months from LOI or order confirmation. Management is pursuing NMDC mining tenders (NMDC planning ₹70,000 crores of investment), steel sector expansion (JSW Paradeep and a greenfield Maharashtra plant; ArcelorMittal’s ~10 mtpa, ₹1.5 lakh crore Vizag plant; SAIL Bokaro and Rourkela), and railways (maintenance depot shops, new lines, EPC) and roads.

International. Long-term O&M contracts in Nigeria (Dangote) and nearly ₹300 crores of multiple maintenance jobs in UAE and GCC countries; new opportunities expected in West Africa and the Middle East.

Balance sheet capacity. Management stated working capital limits support up to ₹10,000 crores of revenue, requiring ₹100–120 crores of annual capex. Headcount rose from about 40,000 a year earlier to 50,000, with about 4,000 engineers and supervisors.

Employee alignment. The board approved the Power Mech Projects Limited Employee Stock Option Plan 2026 (PMPL ESOP 2026) for up to 1,000,000 options for eligible employees of the company and subsidiaries, aimed at attracting, retaining, and motivating talent and enabling participation in company growth.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and fuel price risk: steel, alloy steel, construction gases, LNG, and diesel increases, with PVC compensation lagging by a quarter and public-sector clients bound by fixed rules, potentially requiring claims or dispute resolution.
  • Geopolitical risk: the ongoing Middle East conflict raising material and execution costs and disrupting international operations.
  • Regulatory/royalty risk: government changes to royalty sharing and additional royalty levies on KRBM, which reduced project EBITDA margin from 14% to 10%.
  • Mining execution risk: OB removal timing and seam ramp-up at KBP; washery commissioning timing at Tasra (expected November–December) affecting MDO revenue and margin phasing.
  • Project disruption risk: the Athena Vedanta accident halted activities for three to four months.
  • Order inflow concentration/timing risk: Q1 inflows were 15.5% of the annual target; BHEL tendering delays and packaging philosophy could affect award timing.
  • Execution capacity constraints: power plant construction activity described as near maximum execution potential.
  • Customer concentration: Adani (~₹2,400 crores outstanding across ~14 projects), BHEL (~₹5,300 crores across five major projects), and Vedanta (~₹1,551 crores across six projects) are major exposures; NTPC exposure is limited.
  • Receivables/payment cycle: private players pay 70% on bill submission and the balance 30% before month-end (15–30 days); BHEL pays 30 days after bill submission; 5% general retention applies, with bank guarantees used to receive full RA bills without retention deduction.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 EBITDA margin guidance of 12.5%. Q1 FY27 delivered a 10.8% consolidated EBITDA margin, below the guided level. Management expressed confidence in achieving 12.5% for the year, citing KBP normalization, higher-margin O&M and civil infra orders, and MDO ramp-up. The CFO described the current blended margin range as 12–13%, with core business (94–95% of turnover) at 10–11% and MDO at 15% contributing 5–6% of turnover.

KBP mine margin guidance of 15–16% for the year. Q1 was impacted by higher OB removal cost from new seams; management stated the stripping ratio remains within the agreed ratio and that production from these seams ramps up in coming quarters, normalizing margins.

KRBM revenue guidance of ₹700–750 crores for the year. Last year’s revenue was around ₹700 crores; Q1 margin came in at 10% versus 14% annually last year, with 10–11% expected for the remaining quarters.

MDO revenue guidance. Combined ₹500 crores targeted this year (₹350 crores KBP plus ₹150 crores Tasra), unchanged from prior guidance; FY28 targeted at ₹1,100–1,200 crores. Management noted KBP clients are ready to take up to 3 million tons versus the 1.5 million ton agreement, and that ₹350 crores is a conservative projection with potential to exceed.

FY27 revenue guidance of ₹7,300 crores. Management stated it is on track, with O&M targeted at about 28% growth to ₹2,089 crores.

Order inflow guidance of ₹10,000–12,000 crores for the year. Q1 inflows of ₹1,864 crores represent approximately 15.5% of the ₹12,000 crores annual target.

Longer-term margin trajectory. Management targets roughly a 0.5% annual EBITDA margin increase, reaching almost 14% by 2030 from the present 12.5%, contingent on the mix of O&M and mining business.

Segment growth guidance. Power plant construction, erection, testing, and commissioning expected to grow 5–7%; EPC expected to grow 20–22%; combined power construction and EPC growth of 20–22%, averaging 14–15% for the power sector.

Dividend. The board recommended a final dividend of ₹1.50 per equity share of ₹10 each for FY ended March 31, 2026, to be considered at the 27th AGM on September 17, 2026, held through video conferencing or other audio-visual means.

Broker Narrative

The early narrative centered on the SAIL MDO win and management’s aggressive revenue/order-inflow targets, while flagging ex-MDO order softness, working capital, and royalty-related risks. By the last report, the Buy stance remained, but the emphasis had shifted to a record ex-MDO order book and the thermal power capex pipeline, with the royalty/execution concerns becoming concrete Q1FY27 margin drags. Diversification themes narrowed from water/railway/international to thermal power and Mumbai Monorail O&M, while margin expansion was deferred to a FY30 target.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning that execution was a key monitorable materialized when Q1FY27 EBITDA margin fell 160bps YoY due to higher overburden-removal costs as KBP opened new coal seams.
  • The early royalty-burden concern did not ease by FY25; instead, a government-ordered reset of the KRBM royalty-sharing arrangement added to Q1FY27 margin pressure, coinciding with the stock’s later DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that MDO revenue would scale up and improve margins had not materialized by the last report, as mining revenue was still only projected at INR5bn for FY27 and MDO ramp-up costs were a margin headwind.
  • The early international expansion opportunity across West Africa, Nigeria, Ghana, Tanzania, and the Middle East was absent from the last report’s tailwinds and backlog narrative, suggesting it did not become a visible growth driver.
