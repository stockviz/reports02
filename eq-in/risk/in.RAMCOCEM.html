RAMCOCEM: Recent Corporate Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Competition Commission of India (CCI) penalty: A penalty of Rs. 258.63 Crores was imposed by the CCI on 31-08-2016 over alleged cartelisation. The matter remains under legal appeal at the Supreme Court.
  • Foreign operations: The company closed the operations of its foreign branch in Sri Lanka due to un-viability.
  • Regulatory transition: The company continues to monitor the finalisation of State Rules and further clarifications relating to the Labour Codes, and will give effect to such changes in its books as and when required.
  • Cancelled investor meeting: An investor meeting scheduled for 11th September 2026 with Motilal AMC at the Corporate Office in Chennai, organised by 360 ONE Capital Market Private Limited, was cancelled.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Mineral Bearing Land Tax cessation: Following the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2026 (MMDR), the company is no longer required to pay Rs. 160/- per tonne of limestone towards Mineral Bearing Land Tax in Tamil Nadu with effect from 22nd August 2026. Management expects this to reduce operating costs and favourably impact profitability and cash flows.
  • Biodiversity recognition: The Pandalgudi limestone mine restoration received Advanced Certification from The Global Biodiversity Standard, making it the first site in Peninsular India to receive this recognition. The project inspired the Rajapalayam Masterplan and the declaration of Rajapalayam as one of four towns in Tamil Nadu on a fast track to carbon neutrality by 2041.
  • Scale and footprint: Ramco Cements Limited, headquartered in Chennai, Tamil Nadu, was incorporated in 1957 and has an installed capacity of over 20 million tonnes per annum across more than ten manufacturing facilities in southern and eastern India.

3. Key Risks

  • The CCI penalty of Rs. 258.63 Crores remains subject to an ongoing legal appeal at the Supreme Court, leaving the outcome unresolved.
  • The Sri Lanka foreign branch was closed due to un-viability, reflecting challenges in that overseas operation.
  • The company remains exposed to evolving regulatory requirements under the Labour Codes, with State Rules and Government clarifications still pending.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • Mineral Bearing Land Tax: Management stated that the cessation of the levy is expected to reduce operating costs and favourably impact profitability and cash flows. Observed performance shows the company had already paid Rs. 171.78 crores for 2025-2026 and Rs. 79.07 crores for 2026-2027 (till date) towards this tax, indicating the scale of the cost that will no longer be incurred from 22nd August 2026.
  • Pandalgudi mine restoration: Management guidance indicates the restoration work is ongoing and expected to be completed by 2027. Observed progress includes over 500 acres of worked-out mines covered (with a 234-acre site assessed and certified) and over 430,000 native trees and shrubs from 150 species planted since 2018.
  • Quarterly performance: For the quarter ended 30-06-2026, the company reported standalone total income of Rs. 2,276.18 Crores and net profit after tax of Rs. 31.86 Crores. Consolidated total income was Rs. 2,279.79 Crores with a net profit for the period of Rs. 31.24 Crores. The statutory auditors expressed an unmodified audit opinion.
  • AGM outcomes: The 68th Annual General Meeting was held on August 20, 2026, via Video Conferencing. Actions included adoption of separate and consolidated audited financial statements for the year ended March 31, 2026, declaration of a dividend of Rs. 2.50 per share, reappointment of Shri P.R. Venketrama Raja as Director, and ratification of Rs. 7,50,000 remuneration for Cost Auditors M/s. Geeyes & Co. for the financial year 2026-27. All resolutions were passed with the requisite majority.

Broker Narrative

Early reports centered on company-specific headwinds—weak realizations, elevated fuel costs, flat eastern volumes, and surging interest expenses—while emphasizing green power expansion, cost discipline, and capacity additions as growth levers. Later narratives shifted to structural industry issues: competitive intensity, low RoEs, high leverage, and volatile regional pricing. Persistent themes were elevated costs and weak pricing, evolving from fuel/inventory pressures into broader fuel cost resurgence and price rollback volatility.

Fears that came true

  • Elevated fuel and input costs materialized as a ‘resurgence of global fuel costs’ flagged in the last report, correlating with multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Weak realizations and volatile cement prices persisted, evolving into ‘volatile cement prices in the region following the rollback of price hikes’ and corresponding with negative actual returns across numerous calls.
  • Higher debt and interest expenses drove sustained earnings pressure, with high leverage (net debt/EBITDA of 2.4x for FY27) confirmed in the final report and linked to underperformance.
  • Supply disruptions and flat eastern volume growth contributed to disappointing results, reflected in DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes when growth failed to sustain.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that fuel cost per kcal would continue declining and yield further savings failed, as global fuel costs resurged per the last report.
  • Cost savings from green power expansion (estimated INR 70-80/t from shifting 133MW wind energy) did not prevent persistent low RoEs (3-7%) flagged later.
  • Upcoming capacity expansion (Orissa GU Line-2 and Karnataka plant) failed to offset competitive intensity, as the industry faced ‘elevated competitive intensity’ and high leverage instead.
  • Opex discipline and overall cost reductions did not translate into sustained margin improvement, as earnings revisions were repeatedly cut and actual returns disappointed.
