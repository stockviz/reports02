STUDDS Accessories — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Sharp styrene-based raw material inflation. The quarter ended June 30, 2026 was defined by an exceptional spike in styrene-based input prices. The average styrene-based raw material price was around INR135 at the start of Q4 FY26 and rose at its peak to approximately INR225 in Q1 FY27 — an increase of almost 65% in a short period. Management noted the worst point was April, driven by the disruption of Gulf supply routes (India imports about 98% of its styrene through the Gulf), which forced sourcing from alternative locations at a premium. Prices had moderated to roughly INR185 on a weighted-average basis by the time of the call, still about 30% above the earlier level and not fully aligned with softer oil prices.

Lag in price pass-through. A ~9% price hike was undertaken from the FY26 (March 2026) base, but only about 5% effective realisation was achieved in Q1 FY27. Some export orders executed in the quarter had been contracted before the price increase and continued at old prices; revised OEM pricing became effective only from Q2 for some customers, and government channels (CSD, CPC) were expected to see hikes mid-Q2. One large OEM customer’s increase was still awaited at the time of the call.

Wage cost inflation. Minimum wages in Haryana increased 35% effective April 1, raising the unskilled minimum wage from INR11,000–12,000 to INR15,000–16,000. This contributed a 200 bps impact on margins, alongside a 600 bps impact from material costs. Management indicated the manpower cost increase will sustain and can only be offset by better product mix or higher prices.

Margin compression. Consolidated EBITDA fell to INR19.6 crore with an 11.5% margin, versus 20.3% in Q1 FY26 and 18.7% in Q4 FY26. PAT was INR12.3 crore (7.2% margin), against 13.6% in Q1 FY26 and 12.6% in Q4 FY26.

New-market start-up drag. The Italian subsidiary (SMK Helmets Europe SRL, incorporated May 22, 2026) generated no revenue in Q1 and recorded a loss of INR9.00 million, with salaries being incurred ahead of the October 2026 commercial start. Management expects losses of INR2–2.5 crore in FY27 and about INR2 crore in FY28, with profits only from the third year.

High utilisation limiting headroom. Two-wheeler helmet and box production was ~1.95 million units in Q1 FY27 at 81% utilisation, leaving limited spare capacity until the new line starts.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Stable demand and revenue growth. Consolidated revenue grew 13.7% year-on-year to INR169.7 crore, with volume growth of about 8.5% (1.4 lakh units on a base of 17.56 lakh units). Helmet ASP rose to INR845 in Q1 FY27 from INR802 in Q1 FY26.

Moderating input costs. Styrene-based raw material prices began moderating from July 2026, and lower-cost material is expected to progressively flow through inventory.

Capacity expansion. The first phase of 1.5 million additional annual helmet capacity is progressing as planned and is expected to become operational beginning October (and September). Management described the manufacturing setup as highly fungible, allowing throughput optimisation across products and brands.

Decathlon engagement. Commercial production for Decathlon is expected to commence from October 2026, providing access to the organised and institutional segment and building capabilities for other international institutional customers. Bicycle helmet ASPs are about 15–20% lower than motorcycle helmets.

Italy as a European platform. Operations in Italy are expected to be fully functional in October 2026, enabling a dealer-direct model in Italy, Germany and France (markets where STUDDS lacks direct distributors), reducing shipping lead times of 45–60 days, moving distributors toward just-in-time ordering, and supporting deeper presence in Turkey, Colombia, Mexico, USA, Indonesia, Philippines and Sri Lanka. Management expects surplus EBITDA margins of 10–12% from the Italian subsidiary at steady state, with revenue of EUR100,000–125,000 in the start-up year and close to EUR1 million next year.

Product innovation beyond helmets. Two new helmet models, Ares and Raider Youth, were launched in Q1, with four to five more products planned during the year. Two Bluetooth communication products are in development — a standard device and a mesh group-communication system whose prototype is ready, with first commercial production expected in Q3 FY27. Riding jackets are expected to be commercially available around Q2 FY27. These two categories are expected to contribute INR15–20 crore of revenue in the current financial year, produced partly in-house and partly through contract manufacturing.

Export mix as a margin lever. Exports were about 21% of revenue in Q1 versus around 20% for full-year FY26, with an ambition to reach ~30%. Exports on SMK carry 30–35% EBITDA margins versus roughly 16–18% for the domestic STUDDS business. Management indicated that taking exports to 30% could lift PAT margins by at least 200–300 bps.

Subsidiary investments. The Board approved further investment in SMK Helmets Europe SRL of up to EUR 1,000,000 (remittance on or before March 31, 2027, in one or more tranches) and in Bikerz US Inc. of USD 99,936 (completion on or before September 30, 2026), both remaining 100% owned. Bikerz US recorded revenue of INR35.38 million and a net loss of INR1.82 million for the quarter.

Structural industry shift. Stronger regulatory enforcement and premiumisation are accelerating the shift from an unorganised, price-driven market toward an organised, branded, safety-focused market, which management views as favourable given STUDDS’s brands, distribution, manufacturing and portfolio.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity price risk: Further or renewed increases in styrene-based raw materials, or a slower-than-expected decline, would delay margin recovery. Management’s margin path is explicitly subject to raw material prices remaining broadly stable.
  • Pass-through risk: Continued delays in realising price increases from OEMs, government channels and export contracts would extend margin pressure. One large OEM’s increase was still pending at the time of the call.
  • Wage cost persistence: The Haryana minimum wage increase is described as sustaining, with offset possible only through mix improvement or price increases.
  • New-market execution risk: The Italy dealer-direct model is a start-up with expected losses in FY27 and FY28 and profits only from year three; the shift from distributors to dealer-direct in Italy, Germany and France carries execution and cost-absorption uncertainty.
  • Capacity ramp risk: The 1.5 million unit capacity addition and Decathlon commercial production are both scheduled around October 2026; timing slippage would limit the ability to serve incremental demand given 81% utilisation.
  • Export and geopolitical exposure: Global economic conditions remain challenging with geopolitical and trade-related uncertainties creating market volatility; the styrene supply disruption itself illustrates the company’s exposure to Gulf supply routes.
  • Price reduction risk in OEM channel: Management indicated that if raw material prices soften, OEM prices might see a slight decrease, though other channels are not expected to see price reductions.
  • General investment risk: The supplied material notes that investments in equity and equity-related securities involve a degree of risk and investors should not invest unless they can afford to lose their entire investment.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Q1 FY27 guidance versus actuals. Management had previously guided to high-teens revenue growth for the year. In Q1 FY27, revenue growth came in at 13.7% year-on-year, with volume growth of about 8.5% and effective price realisation of about 5%. EBITDA margin was 11.5% and PAT margin 7.2%, reflecting the raw material spike, wage inflation and pass-through lag.

Margin recovery path. Management expects EBITDA margin of 14–15% in Q2 FY27 and a return to the normal 18–20% on a run-rate basis by Q4 FY27, subject to broadly stable commodity prices. The Q1 FY27 outcome of 11.5% is the starting point against which this recovery is guided. Management also stated that at current elevated raw material levels, PAT would be around 11.5–12% versus 13% previously, and that by Q4 FY27 the company should be back to FY26 levels.

Price realisation path. Of the ~9% price hike, ~5% was realised in Q1 FY27; management expects 8–9% realisation in Q2 FY27 and full 9% realisation in the following quarter. On raw material impact, the 600 bps hit in Q1 is expected to soften to close to 300 bps in Q2, with Q3 and Q4 better as material prices soften.

Volume and revenue growth. Full-year FY27 volume growth is guided at close to 10% (between 8% and 10%, “more, actually it is closer to 10%”), with blended realisation growth of 7–8%, supporting the earlier high-teens revenue growth guidance. For FY28, management guided to mid-to-high teens growth on a standalone basis, split roughly 13–14% from volume and 3–4% from price realisation. Management clarified that the FY28 figures cited were standalone, not consolidated, and that Italian subsidiary revenue would be over and above this, with some Italian volumes also distributed to other markets.

Italy guidance versus observed. SMK Helmets Europe SRL was incorporated on May 22, 2026 with initial share capital of INR33.51 million (EUR 0.30 million). In Q1 FY27 it recorded nil revenue and a loss of INR9.00 million. Management expects FY27 losses of INR2–2.5 crore and FY28 losses of about INR2 crore, with profits from the third year and surplus EBITDA margins of 10–12% at steady state.

Capex guidance. Total capex incurred to June 30 was about INR76 crore plus INR10 crore of committed vendor advances; INR7.5 crore was spent in Q1 FY27. The FY27 budget is INR58 crore, and the FY28 plan is INR31 crore, with the second phase of construction also starting.

Other forward-looking items. Commercial production for Decathlon and full functionality of Italy operations are both expected in October 2026; Bluetooth mesh communication first commercial production is expected in Q3 FY27; riding jackets are expected around Q2 FY27. The 44th AGM held on September 5, 2026 approved all five resolutions, including adoption of financial statements, a final dividend of Rupees Three per equity share, re-appointment of Ms. Shilpa Arora, the Studds Accessories Limited Employee Stock Option Scheme 2026, and the grant of ESOP options to eligible employees of subsidiary companies. A special window runs from February 5, 2026 to February 4, 2027 to facilitate transfer and dematerialisation of physical securities sold or purchased prior to April 1, 2019.
