GRP Limited — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material inflation. Raw material costs rose sharply during the quarter, with the investor presentation noting a 31% increase in raw material costs due to higher prices of select rubber grades. Gross margin moderated to 48.3% in Q1 FY27 from roughly 50% a year earlier. Management noted that most pricing contracts run on quarterly, six-monthly or annual cycles, so there is generally a lag before cost pass-through is fully achieved, though a large part of the pass-through had been achieved as of the call. Management declined to forecast how long rubber inflation would last, citing El Niño effects on natural rubber and the link between synthetic rubber prices and oil prices.

Logistics volatility. The quarter saw extreme volatility in global logistics, which the company said it managed through better realizations and manufacturing efficiencies.

Tariff-related disruption and permanent loss. The polymer composite business line was permanently shut down on account of tariffs, described as a real one-time loss to revenue and margins. Separately, GRP lost volume on account of the indirect impact of tariffs — exports to non-U.S. countries that served as a base for goods supplied to North American markets. Some of that volume had not returned, as large global tire companies diverted those volumes to other markets. Management hoped this would be recovered through the rest of the year. There was also a possibility that customers receiving tariff credits could lead to higher margins for GRP in future, but visibility on this was not known at the time.

Pyrova not yet at stand-alone profitability. The Pyrova Energy business was described as shy of achieving stand-alone profitability, with margins expected to build from a single digit during the current ramp-up stage.

rCB approvals and stabilization timeline. The recovered carbon black plant was to begin commissioning the following month, with commissioning expected by October 2026. Management expected at least one to two months before stabilization and productive sales, with several quarters needed before a product could be approved and used by the tire industry. Meaningful contribution from rCB was expected by Q4 of the fiscal, with FY28 seen as the year the business attains maturity.

Plastic recycling on-ground challenges. Management flagged that the on-ground challenges of collecting waste effectively and improving yield remain real and persistent, and that building a dependable, compliant recycled content supply chain will take significant sustained effort. The company chose not to chase short-term volume.

Weak OE segment globally. The global tire industry continued to operate in a mixed environment, with weakness persisting in the OE segment while replacement demand remained relatively resilient across most regions.

Polyolefin volume stagnation. The polyolefin business grew in revenue but volumes were flat, reflecting selectivity in customers, product categories and end applications.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Consolidated total income grew 26% year-on-year to INR1,573 million, driven by 24% volume growth and a 5% improvement in realizations. EBITDA rose 60% to INR174 million, with margin expanding 233 basis points to 11%. Profit after tax more than doubled to INR42 million, up 140% year-on-year. Total EPR income was INR49 million versus INR46 million in Q1 FY26.

Rubber Recycling segment leadership. Segment revenue grew 34% year-on-year on a stand-alone basis. Reclaim volumes grew 12% and reclaim export volumes rebounded 20% as customer order patterns normalized following the easing of U.S. tariffs. Domestic demand stayed healthy with market share intact. Management stated domestic market share grew by about 1% compared to the prior year, while export share in focused geographies increased.

Pyrova Energy milestones. The plant achieved its longest continuous reactor run since commissioning. In July, the company achieved the industry performance threshold of a minimum 25-day run in a month with a 4-day shutdown. Both crumb rubber and continuous pyrolysis facilities had largely stabilized. Cumulative investment under Pyrova stood at approximately INR91 crores as of 30 June 2026. Phase 1 (October 2025) commissioned India’s largest single-line continuous reactor of 15 KTPA and an integrated crumb rubber facility. Phase 2 (under commissioning through February 2027) covers the rCB facility at Solapur and capacity enhancement of the tyre pyrolysis plant to 45 KTPA. Phase 3 (under planning, to be commissioned through October 2027) covers an integrated facility in Gujarat for crumb rubber, tyre pyrolysis oil and rCB.

Plastics turnaround. Engineering Plastics grew volumes 27%, helped by domestic automotive demand and a more favourable virgin nylon pricing environment. The Repurposed Polyolefin business delivered a clear turnaround in profitability. Together, the plastic verticals expanded EBITDA margins by 14 percentage points year-on-year on a 10-point improvement in gross margin, reflecting better product and customer mix and continued cost discipline. Approvals in the appliance sector obtained in Q4 FY26 translated into commercial volumes in the quarter.

Regulatory tailwinds. Plastic EPR norms are getting tighter and brand owners are becoming markedly more serious about implementation. EPR guidelines for tires require manufacturers and importers to recycle 100% of the quantity of new manufactured or imported tires after 2025, or acquire EPR certificates of equivalent quantity, with certificates earned by recyclers based on quantity recycled. Management noted that EPR adds a new revenue stream for recyclers without additional capital expenditure.

Export market expansion. The company invested in setting up a base in Europe for serving the Western Hemisphere, with some payoff starting to show. Management saw likelihood of increasing growth in export geographies in coming years, supported by new product categories deploying newer technologies and higher-performance materials. Tire pyrolysis oil and rCB were also expected to have markets outside India.

Certification and platform breadth. GRP is the first reclaim rubber manufacturer in the world to earn ISCC Plus certification. The company serves 8 of the top 10 global tire manufacturers, with more than half of revenues coming from exports. The platform spans five decades across reclaim rubber, crumb rubber, tire pyrolysis oil, rCB and engineering plastics.

Sustainability progress. Renewable energy accounted for approximately 48% of GRP’s stand-alone power consumption in FY 2026, up from 37% a year ago, ahead of the stated target of 50% by FY 2028, which the company expected to reach in the current fiscal. The first sustainability report for FY 2026 was published during the quarter.

Working capital improvement. The working capital cycle improved by 8 days to 86 days as of 30 June from 31 March.

3. Key Risks

Raw material price and pass-through lag. Continued rubber inflation and the contractual lag before pass-through could pressure gross margins. Management declined to forecast the duration of inflation.

Tariff and trade policy uncertainty. The permanent shutdown of the polymer composite business and the unrecovered indirect tariff impact on non-U.S. export volumes illustrate exposure to trade policy shifts. Visibility on potential tariff credit benefits was not known.

Technology and approval risk at Pyrova. The rCB plant’s commissioning, stabilization and customer approvals are subject to stage gates. Management stated that the 18% to 20% EBITDA potential depends on customer approvals and optimal utilization of reactors and rCB capacity, with FY28 seen as the maturity year.

Execution risk on capacity expansion. The company remains committed to an overall investment of INR250 crores set out about 18 months earlier, with room for about another INR100 crores of expansion. Whether all of it happens in FY28 or spills into FY29 was described as too early to comment. FY28 capex decisions were to be taken only in the second half of the year.

Leverage and deleveraging timing. Borrowings increased year-on-year on the consolidated balance sheet. Management stated that the timing of deleveraging is not entirely in its hands and that there are no firm plans on the table at the moment. If the next phase of expansion in Gujarat plants is to be accelerated, deleveraging would be needed.

Waste collection and yield challenges in plastics. Building a dependable, compliant recycled content supply chain was described as requiring significant sustained effort.

Dependence on customer approvals and market conditions. Growth expectations for reclaim rubber, Pyrova and plastics depend on new product introductions, customer approvals, regulatory maturity and demand conditions.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

FY27 revenue growth. Management guided to close to overall 20%-plus revenue growth for the fiscal, on the back of Pyrova Energy, growth in Reclaim Rubber capacity and order book, and the Plastic division scaling to a newer high. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: consolidated total income grew 26% year-on-year to INR1,573 million, with 24% volume growth and 5% realization improvement. Management also stated it hoped to maintain double-digit volume growth for the year.

EBITDA margin trajectory. Management stated that the Q1 margin was not a one-time margin generation and believed it would continue to improve. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: EBITDA margin expanded 233 basis points to 11% from 8.7%, despite a 2 percentage point decline in gross margin, supported by operating leverage and cost discipline.

Reclaim Rubber volume growth. Management guided to close to 20% volume growth in FY27 over the prior year, and sustained mid-teen volume growth over the next three years on the back of new product introductions, translating into healthy double-digit revenue growth. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: reclaim volumes grew 12% year-on-year, with export volumes rebounding 20%.

Reclaim Rubber EBITDA margin. Management guided to improvement from the roughly 9% to 10% average over the last couple of years towards 10% to 14% as synergies with Pyrova progressively come through. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: the Rubber Recycling segment delivered a healthy expansion in segment margins, with segment results (profit before tax and interest) of INR1,922.86 lakhs stand-alone versus INR1,123.63 lakhs in Q1 FY26.

Pyrova Energy capacity and margin. Management guided to steadily scale capacity to 45,000 tons as the technology stabilizes, followed by a further 30,000 tons capacity addition in FY28. Margins were expected to build from a single digit during the ramp-up stage towards 18% to 20% once the rCB facility is commissioned, operational and mature with customer approvals. On a stand-alone Pyrova basis, management stated the investment would generate in excess of INR250 crores to INR300 crores of revenue and possibly generate an EBITDA margin of between 15% and 20%. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: the plant achieved its longest continuous reactor run since commissioning and, in July, achieved the 25-day run threshold. The business remained shy of stand-alone profitability.

Plastic recycling growth and margin. Management guided to over 20% growth this fiscal in the nylon and polyolefin recycling verticals, followed by mid-teen growth through FY2030, while maintaining a stable EBITDA margin of between 10% and 15%, supported by tightening EPR regulation and focus on value-added products. Management also cited a 12% to 15% EBITDA margin target for the plastic business on an individual BU basis. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: Engineering Plastics volumes grew 27%; the plastic verticals expanded EBITDA margins by 14 percentage points year-on-year on a 10-point improvement in gross margin; the Repurposed Polyolefin business delivered a clear turnaround in profitability.

Capex. Management guided to FY27 capex of between INR90 crores and INR100 crores, covering expansion of pyrolysis lines (adding two more lines to reach 45 KTA), the rCB plant to be commissioned by October 2026, and debottlenecking of reclaim rubber plants. FY28 capex decisions were to be taken in the second half of the year. The company remained committed to the overall INR250 crores investment target, with room for about another INR100 crores of expansion, including another 30,000 tons of pyrolysis capacity and 12,000 tons of rCB capacity in Gujarat, plus further reclaim rubber debottlenecking. Observed: cumulative Pyrova investment stood at approximately INR91 crores as of 30 June 2026; consolidated capex FY24 to FY26 was INR170 crores.

Renewable energy target. Management stated the 50% renewable energy target by FY2028 should be reached in the current fiscal. Observed: renewable energy was approximately 48% of stand-alone power consumption in FY2026, up from 37% a year ago.

Working capital. Management stated continued focus on maintaining a prudent balance sheet with disciplined capital allocation. Observed: working capital cycle improved by 8 days to 86 days as of 30 June from 31 March.
