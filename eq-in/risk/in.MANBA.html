Manba Finance — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Capital adequacy dilution and fresh capital need. On the Q1 FY27 call (28 July 2026), an investor noted that the capital adequacy ratio had declined from 29.81% in FY25 to around 25–26% currently. Management acknowledged this and said it is in the process of raising ₹100 crores, targeted for September or October, to fund further growth and expansion. Management indicated this raise would be via preference shares. The investor presentation shows CRAR at 24.40% as of Q1 FY27.
  • Rising cost of borrowing. An investor observed a 15–20 basis point increase in the cost of borrowing. The CFO attributed this to carrying healthy liquidity: the company held around ₹350 crores of liquidity as of 31 March 2026, borrowed only ₹100–150 crores in the quarter, and kept liquidity of ₹200 crores, which raised the interest burden. Cost of borrowings stood at 10.86% in Q1 FY27.
  • Sequential AUM stagnation despite industry momentum. An investor pointed out that while YoY AUM growth was encouraging, QoQ AUM growth was only about 1%, even as the two-wheeler industry was thriving. Management explained that April–June has few festivals, that the second and third quarters (Raksha Bandhan, Ganpati, Dussehra, Diwali) are seasonally the strongest, and that considerable run-down of the book occurs each quarter.
  • Product concentration in two-wheelers. Two-wheelers accounted for 84.1% of the product mix in Q1 FY27. Management repeatedly acknowledged the need to reduce this dependence, targeting a fall to 75–77% by end of the year and to about 65% within three years.
  • New-vertical ramp-up timelines. The MSME LAP business, launched this year, is expected to contribute only 2–3% of AUM by FY27 end. The Namma Loan (Sreesastha) South India partnership is expected to take 9–12 months to break even, with management noting it could be 6–7 months if early response holds.
  • Geographic white spaces. Management said Maharashtra, Gujarat and Rajasthan are largely stabilised, with 12–14% market share reached, and that focus is now on untapped parts of Rajasthan and on UP and MP. No further new states will be added this year beyond the South India entry.
  • External headwinds cited in the annual report material. Intensifying competition from banks, NBFCs, fintechs and captive finance companies; an evolving regulatory framework for NBFCs; macroeconomic volatility including inflation, interest rate fluctuations and slower economic growth; potential asset quality pressures from adverse economic conditions; and rapid technological change requiring continuous investment in digital capabilities.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong Q1 FY27 momentum. Disbursements grew 36.9% YoY to ₹2,263 million; net interest income grew 35.9% YoY to ₹416 million; PAT grew 35.7% YoY to ₹133 million; total revenue was ₹926 million; diluted EPS was ₹2.64 versus ₹1.94. AUM stood at ₹17,308 million, up 22.3% YoY.
  • Explicit AUM growth guidance. Management stated it remains confident of delivering AUM growth of 35–40% during the current financial year, reiterated in the press release dated 27 July 2026.
  • South India entry. A strategic partnership with Sreesastha, operating as Nammaloan, commenced in Karnataka, to be followed by Tamil Nadu within the financial year and a phased entry into other southern states. Management expects ₹60–75 crores of AUM from this partner in FY27. The arrangement is 100% Manba-funded, with Sreesastha acting as a BC partner and hypothecation remaining with Manba.
  • Product diversification. Commencement of MSME Loan Against Property disbursements (ticket size ₹8–20 lakhs, targeting businessmen with shops); launch of battery replacement financing for electric three-wheelers (₹60,000 ticket size, initially for existing e-three-wheeler customers, with an OEM tie-up involving OEM participation if something goes wrong); focus on top-up loans, personal loans, used two-wheeler loans and three-wheeler loans. Small business loans disburse around ₹6–7 crores per month. Management said MSME LAP and small business loans have disbursed about ₹150 crores so far, with AUM above ₹100 crores.
  • EV and rural partnerships. Partnerships with AMU Leasing and S.H. Finserv (SHFIN) are deepening EV and rural lending reach.
  • Distribution expansion. Locations rose to 134 (from 76 in Q1 FY26) and dealers to 1,784 (from 1,258). The company operates in 130+ locations across seven states, with Karnataka newly added.
  • Secured book and asset quality. Over 95% of the portfolio is secured. Stage 1 assets were 93.55% of gross assets; Stage 2 fell to 3.04% from 3.18% QoQ; GNPA improved to 3.41% from 3.47% YoY; NNPA improved to 2.52% from 2.64% YoY. ECL provisions of ₹25 crores compare with an IRAC norm requirement of ₹7.57 crores, leaving an excess buffer.
  • Low collection cost as a structural advantage. Management said 85% of collections are in-house (around 600 people in the collection team), versus peers who outsource, keeping collection cost much lower and supporting opex. The investor presentation cites 6% versus 18% for peers.
  • Technology-led turnaround. Over 60% of loans are sanctioned in one minute and 92% on the same day; 95%+ digital processing, 20+ integrated APIs and a 100% paperless journey. Management said 90% of tech and software is proprietary, with the MSME LAP software being the first taken from an external vendor whose ownership lies with the vendor. AI voice agents are deployed for onboarding welcome calls, EMI reminders and early-bucket collections.
  • Funding relationships. Funding from about three public sector banks, 10 private sector banks and 25 NBFCs; ₹6.5 billion raised through NCDs over the last two years at 10.5% ROI. Borrowing mix is around 60% term loans, 25% NCDs, with the rest PTC and CC balances.
  • Ratings and capital position. CARE assigned BBB+ with positive outlook; Acuite assigned A−. CRAR was 24.40%, above the regulatory minimum. Net worth was ₹423 crore, up about 12% from ₹379 crore.
  • Shareholder returns. The Board declared a first interim dividend of ₹0.25 per equity share (face value ₹10) for FY27, with a record date of 7 August 2026 and payment on or before 20 August 2026. A final dividend of ₹0.25 per equity share for FY26 was recommended, with the 30th AGM to be held on Saturday, 26 September 2026 via video conferencing and a record date of 18 September 2026.
  • Sector tailwinds cited in the annual report material. Increasing demand for vehicle financing in semi-urban and rural markets; accelerating electric mobility adoption; expansion into new geographies and underserved segments; growing digital adoption; and supportive government initiatives on financial inclusion, entrepreneurship and sustainable mobility. The investor presentation cites CRISIL projecting 14–15% growth for the used car market.

3. Key Risks

  • Asset quality sensitivity. GNPA at 3.41% and NNPA at 2.52% remain the key monitorable, with the annual report material flagging potential asset quality pressures from adverse economic conditions. Stage 3 gross assets rose to ₹590 million in June 2026 from ₹570 million in March 2026, even as the ratio improved YoY.
  • Concentration risk. Two-wheeler dependence at 84.1% of the mix leaves the book exposed to a single product cycle; management’s diversification targets are multi-year.
  • Capital and leverage. Debt-equity stood at 3.44. The planned ₹100 crore preference share raise is needed to support the 35–40% AUM growth ambition; any delay would constrain growth or further dilute CRAR.
  • Funding cost and liquidity management. Carrying high liquidity raised the cost of borrowing in Q1 FY27; the trajectory of borrowing costs depends on how liquidity is managed against disbursement seasonality.
  • New-vertical execution. MSME LAP, battery replacement financing, personal loans and the Namma Loan partnership are early-stage; break-even timelines (9–12 months for Namma Loan) and contribution targets (2–3% of AUM for MSME LAP in FY27) carry execution risk.
  • Competitive and regulatory environment. The annual report material cites intensifying competition from banks, NBFCs, fintechs and captive finance companies, and an evolving NBFC regulatory framework.
  • Technology dependence and investment. Rapid technological advancement requires continuous investment; the MSME LAP software is vendor-owned, unlike the proprietary LOS/LMS/LAS stack, and the company plans considerable spend on personal loan software this year.
  • Geographic expansion risk. Entry into South India is a first move beyond the core six-state base, with leadership hired from TVS Motor Company and TVS Credit; the model is untested in the new region.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • AUM growth. Management guides 35–40% AUM growth for FY27. Observed Q1 FY27 AUM growth was 22.3% YoY, with QoQ growth of about 1%. Management attributes the sequential softness to the absence of festivals in April–June and points to historical second- and third-quarter seasonality, with disbursement growth of 36.9% YoY cited as evidence of underlying momentum.
  • Profitability. Management said PAT grew “almost 30% plus percent” this quarter and expects similar performance in future quarters. Observed PAT growth was 35.7% YoY to ₹133 million, though PAT was up 19.8% QoQ and PBT was down 4.7% QoQ. Management declined to give specific quarterly profit guidance, saying it cannot, but expects growth in line with historical performance.
  • Return ratios. Management targets around 3.5% ROA for FY27, citing a higher-yield mix (personal loans, top-up loans, used two-wheelers) with almost 30% of further lending at higher IRR. Observed Q1 FY27 ROA was 2.63% and ROE 11.65%, indicating the target requires improvement over the remainder of the year.
  • Net interest margin. Management guides NIM in the 13–14% range, citing falling borrowing costs and rising lending yields. Observed Q1 FY27 NIM was 12.13%, below the guided range.
  • Product mix. Management expects two-wheeler share to fall to 75–77% by end of FY27 and to about 65% within three years. Observed Q1 FY27 two-wheeler share was 84.1%, with top-up loans 5.1%, three-wheelers 3.1% and used vehicles 1.5%.
  • MSME LAP. Management expects 2–3% of AUM by FY27 end, having just commenced disbursements this year.
  • Namma Loan. Management expects ₹60–75 crores of AUM in FY27 and break-even in 9–12 months, possibly 6–7 months given early response.
  • Capital raise. Management said ₹100 crores would be raised by September or latest by October via preference shares. This remains a stated intention as of the 28 July 2026 call.
  • Cost of borrowing. Management linked the Q1 FY27 increase to liquidity carry rather than a structural repricing, implying the elevated cost may normalise as liquidity is deployed.
  • Annual report guidance. Management states its focus remains on delivering profitable growth, preserving asset quality, and building a resilient institution capable of creating sustainable value for all stakeholders, and that it will continue to expand reach, strengthen the technology platform and enhance customer experience while maintaining disciplined risk management.
