ORICONENT: Recent Corporate Developments and Outlook

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Losses in discontinued operations and on disposal: The company reported losses in discontinued operations and incurred additional losses on slump sales. This includes an additional loss of Rs. 18.38 lakhs arising from a change in Delta Working Capital.
  • Receivables provisioning: Provisions for doubtful old receivables amounting to Rs. 750.00 lakhs were recognised.
  • Uneven segment performance: Fluctuations in segment performance are cited as a challenge.
  • Losses linked to specific divested businesses: Losses from discontinued operations and from the sale of undertakings such as Metal Crown Seals and Roll On Pilfer Proof Closures.
  • Product substitution risk: End-user industries could shift to newer packaging formats such as tetra packs or sachets.
  • Foreign exchange exposure: The company imports raw materials and exports finished products without hedging activities, leaving it exposed to currency movements.
  • Broader external risks: Valuation risks, regulatory developments, macroeconomic uncertainties, and market volatility.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Debt-light balance sheet and liquidity: Following the divestment of its operating undertakings, the company enters its next phase with a debt-light balance sheet, substantial liquidity, and flexibility to pursue new growth and attractive market opportunities without immediate capital constraints.
  • Deployment of divestment proceeds: Surplus funds arising from the divestment of operating undertakings have been deployed into treasury and other investment avenues in accordance with the Companies Act.
  • Continued trading activity: The company continues its trading activities and is identifying new growth opportunities.
  • Macroeconomic support: Management anticipates GDP growth between 6.3% and 6.8% for FY 2025-26, supported by domestic demand, infrastructure investment, and a consumption-led recovery.

3. Key Risks

  • Valuation risks and regulatory developments.
  • Macroeconomic uncertainties and market volatility.
  • Product substitution risk from alternative packaging formats (tetra packs, sachets).
  • Foreign exchange risk from unhedged imports of raw materials and exports of finished products.
  • Geopolitical trade challenges, which management says it remains vigilant to.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Guidance: Management anticipated GDP growth of 6.3% to 6.8% for FY 2025-26, supported by domestic demand, infrastructure investment, and consumption-led recovery, while flagging geopolitical trade challenges.
  • Observed performance: During FY 2025-26, the company completed the sale and disposal of its entire manufacturing facilities (except the Murbad unit land and building), effectively discontinuing all manufacturing operations and shifting focus to trading activities and deployment of surplus funds into treasury and other investment avenues.
  • Divestment programme: Key transactions include the divestment of several subsidiaries and business units, such as Oriental Containers Limited, United Shippers Logistics Limited, and parts of the Packaging and Petrochemical divisions, classified under discontinued operations in accordance with Ind AS 105.
  • Oriental Containers Limited exit: Further to an earlier intimation dated May 29, 2026, Oricon Enterprises sold/transferred its entire equity shareholding in Oriental Containers Limited, a non-material subsidiary, on July 8, 2026, and the entity ceased to be a subsidiary with effect from that date. The consideration received was Rs. 3,99,950, against a net worth of Rs. 3,37,677 and total revenue including other income of Rs. NIL for the financial year ended March 31, 2026. The buyers are Adarsh Somani, Mridula Somani, Nupur Somani, Yashh Somani, Krrish Somani, Venkatesh Karriers Limited, and Hazarimal Somani (Partnership Firm); all belong to the promoter/promoter group except Yashh Somani and Krrish Somani. The transaction falls within related party transactions and was done at arm’s length.
  • Reporting and governance: The board approved un-audited standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, which were reviewed by statutory auditors S G N & Co. The board convened the 56th Annual General Meeting for September 29, 2026, and re-appointed Mr. Shravan Kumar Malani as an Independent Director.
