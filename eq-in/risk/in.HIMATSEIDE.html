Himatsingka Seide Limited — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical disruption in the Middle East affected Q1 FY27, causing deferrals of shipments and contributing to range-bound consolidated total income of INR634 crores versus INR661 crores.
  • U.S. market pressures: management cited market share challenges in the U.S., pricing challenges on account of tariffs, and client/market concentration in the Home Textile space. Policy “flip-flop” created uncertainty and an inflationary environment for the company’s products, altering demand scenarios.
  • Raw material inflation and product-mix changes weighed on profitability; consolidated EBITDA was INR101 crores at roughly a 16% margin.
  • Low utilization in key divisions: overall facility utilization stood at 99%, but the Sheeting division was at 52% and the Terry division at 63%, unchanged from the prior quarter.
  • Pricing pressure in Home Textiles: management expects continued pricing challenges, especially on the Sheeting front.
  • Leverage: overall leverage remained range-bound at about INR2,550 crores.
  • Transition volatility: management explicitly flagged that the business-model transition will bring volatility in numbers over the short term (a couple of quarters) before stabilizing on new platforms.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Transition to four verticals using existing assets, without expansion or capex: Home Textile Solutions plus three new verticals — Yarn Solutions, Fabric Solutions, and Apparel Solutions.
  • Yarn Solutions: the spinning asset is described as the world’s largest plant under one roof with 211,584 spindles. The business has begun and is being ramped up through the year; over 90% of capacity is expected to be placed externally. Management indicated full-capacity revenue potential of roughly INR1,000 crores.
  • Fabric Solutions: has begun, leveraging combined Sheeting and knitting fabric processing capacity of approximately 90 million meters by year-end (the earlier 61 million meters did not include knitted platforms). Applications initially in lifestyle fabrics, later technical and advanced fabrics. Management indicated full-capacity revenue potential of roughly INR1,000 crores.
  • Apparel Solutions: planned to start in Phase 2, about a couple of quarters down the line, positioned to serve a broad cross-section of demand.
  • Market size: the new verticals address markets management describes as substantially larger than Home Textiles — potentially around 20x the size.
  • FTAs: trade agreements with the U.K. and EU are expected to benefit the company in the medium term; the U.K. piece will kick in, and the EU piece is expected in the short term, though both remain under process.
  • Domestic opportunity: India is expected to become the largest jurisdiction for the company in the medium term, if not the largest then among the top two, supported by domestic consumption of the new verticals.
  • De-risking: the new model is intended to reduce U.S. concentration, client concentration, and broaden jurisdictions, categories, and India presence.
  • Debt profile: NCD issuances are aimed at balancing debt tenors and maturity profiles; management expects net debt to reduce by the end of the fiscal, with leverage range-bound and initiatives underway to strengthen equity.

3. Key Risks

  • Transition execution risk: the shift to new verticals is expected to bring near-term volatility, and Home Textile (Sheeting) revenues are expected to taper down while new streams ramp up.
  • Concentration risk: existing exposure to the U.S. market and to concentrated clients in Home Textiles.
  • Tariff and trade-policy uncertainty: past policy flip-flops created an inflationary environment and altered demand; FTA benefits with the U.K. and EU are still under process.
  • Pricing risk in Home Textiles, particularly Sheeting.
  • Financing terms: the Series E NCDs carry an 11.50% p.a. coupon payable quarterly, with principal repayable in three instalments at 30, 36, and 42 months, and a 2% charge for delay in payment beyond three months from due date. The debentures are secured by a first pari passu charge over immoveable and moveable fixed assets at the Hassan and Doddaballapur (Karnataka) plants, a negative lien over 4.85 acres at the Hassan facility, and an exclusive charge over the subscription escrow account.
  • Capacity allocation risk: Sheeting and Fabric Solutions will share common processing infrastructure, requiring allocation between verticals.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Revenue: Q1 FY27 consolidated total income was INR634 crores versus INR661 crores, which management characterized as range-bound, with hits from Middle East geopolitical issues and capacity utilization movement. Management stated it will attempt to keep revenues range-bound through the transition, with Home Textile Solutions correcting primarily on Sheeting while Terry continues to perform, and new revenue streams expected to compensate for reductions in existing streams.
  • Margins: EBITDA of INR101 crores at about 16% margin, attributed to lower revenues, product-mix tweaks, and raw material inflation.
  • Utilization: overall 99%, but Sheeting at 52% and Terry at 63%, described as unchanged from the last quarter.
  • New verticals: management stated Yarn Solutions and Fabric Solutions have begun generating revenue, with ramp-up over the next few quarters; specific per-vertical revenue contributions for the quarter were not disclosed. Apparel Solutions is guided to start a couple of quarters down the line.
  • Capex: guidance is to keep capex limited to maintenance and organic requirements, with the transition using the existing campus and infrastructure.
  • Leverage and debt: leverage guided to remain range-bound at about INR2,550 crores; NCD raises are for tenor/maturity balancing rather than additional capital; net debt is expected to reduce by the end of the fiscal; equity-strengthening initiatives are underway and will be shared at an appropriate time.
  • Dividend: the Board recommended a final dividend of Rs. 0.25 (5%) per equity share of face value Rs. 5 for FY ended March 31, 2026, with the record date of September 17, 2026, and the 41st AGM scheduled for September 24, 2026, at 12:10 p.m. IST via video conferencing/OAVM; remote e-voting runs from September 20 to September 23, 2026.
  • Debt issuance: the Securities Committee approved re-classification of Series E NCDs on July 10, 2026 — 4,000 NCDs of Rs. 5,00,000 each aggregating to Rs. 200 crores, on a private placement basis in one or more tranches, with a 42-month tenure and 11.50% p.a. coupon. A subsequent allotment of 500 Tranche 5 Series E NCDs of Rs. 5,00,000 each, aggregating to Rs. 25,00,00,000, was made on a private placement basis with the same 42-month tenure and 11.50% p.a. quarterly interest.
