Headwinds and Challenges

  • The company operates in a competitive automotive components industry, with its proposed Vietnam subsidiary explicitly classified under the “Automotive Industry” for manufacturing and assembly of auto components and parts.
  • The subsea joint venture with Singatac Engineering Pte Ltd, Singapore, is subject to receipt of requisite consents, permissions, sanctions, and approvals under applicable laws, and fulfilment of various terms and conditions specified in the Joint Venture Agreement.
  • The incorporation of the wholly owned subsidiary in Vietnam is subject to requisite approvals, permissions, consents, and sanctions under applicable laws in India (including RBI regulations on overseas investments) and in Vietnam.
  • The Mysore residential apartment joint development with Brigade Enterprises Limited is subject to receipt of requisite consents, permissions, sanctions, licenses, no-objections, and approvals under applicable laws, and fulfilment of various terms and conditions in the Memorandum of Agreement.
  • The consolidated financial results include one subsidiary and three step-down subsidiaries whose financial information was unaudited and furnished by the Board of Directors; these entities reflected a Group share of total loss before tax of Rs. 103.70 Lakhs for the quarter ended 31st March 2026.
  • The company’s consolidated results include operations outside India (LGB-USA, GFM Acquisition LLC, GFM LLC, LGB Mexico), exposing it to multi-jurisdictional regulatory and operational complexity.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Subsea joint venture: The Board approved entering into a Joint Venture Agreement with Singatac Engineering Pte Ltd, Singapore, to incorporate SLGB Energy Solutions Private Limited, with LGB holding 51% and SEPL holding 49%. The JVC will undertake precision manufacturing, machining, assembly, testing, and quality assurance of subsea components, modules, connectors, tools, and related equipment for supply to global subsea OEMs and EPC contractors. SEPL will support with engineering and development, while LGB will support operations including procurement and administration.
  • Vietnam wholly owned subsidiary: The Board approved incorporation of a wholly owned subsidiary in Vietnam, tentatively named LGB Private Limited-Vietnam, to engage in manufacturing and assembly of auto components and parts, with aggregate investment not exceeding INR 10 crores.
  • Mysore land monetisation: The Board approved a Memorandum of Agreement with Brigade Enterprises Limited to develop the company’s non-substantial land property in Mysore, Karnataka into a residential apartment project under a joint development arrangement on a revenue-sharing model. Total cost is estimated at INR 120 crores, with constructed area to be shared 29% to the Owner (LGB) and 71% to the Developer.
  • Dividend: Shareholders approved a dividend of Rs. 22/- per equity share at the 70th AGM held on 26th August 2026.
  • Revenue scale: LGB’s standalone turnover for FY2025-26 was INR 2,77,348.40 Lakhs.

Key Risks

  • Execution and approval risk: All three major initiatives (subsea JV, Vietnam subsidiary, Mysore development) are contingent on regulatory and other approvals, and fulfilment of conditions in the respective agreements.
  • Joint venture governance: The JVC board will comprise four directors—two nominated by LGB and two by SEPL. Any alteration in the capital structure requires prior consent of both parties. Transfer of shares is subject to restrictions including right of first refusal. These provisions could constrain flexibility.
  • Related party status: The JVC will be considered a related party since LGB will hold 51% of its paid-up capital post subscription. The proposed investment is to be carried out at arm’s length.
  • Unaudited subsidiary information: The consolidated results include unaudited financial information for one subsidiary and three step-down subsidiaries, which, while not material to the Group according to the Board, introduces a degree of reporting uncertainty.
  • Real estate development risk: The Mysore project is a revenue-sharing arrangement where the Developer bears the entire cost of approval, construction, development, and maintenance, and all decisions regarding the land property will be taken by the Developer with information given to the Owner. This limits LGB’s control over the project.
  • Geographic concentration risk: The Vietnam subsidiary and the subsea JV represent new geographic and business segments where the company has not previously operated, introducing execution and market-entry risks.
