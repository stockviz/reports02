Prostarm Info Systems — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Delayed commencement of relocated operations. The company had informed (via intimation dated June 16, 2026) of the relocation and consolidation of certain business operations. In its August 12, 2026 board meeting outcome, it stated that while the requisite infrastructure and operational facilities are substantially ready, commencement has been delayed because a required regulatory licence/approval is still awaited. The company now expects to commence these operations on or before September 30, 2026.
  • Seasonal order cycle. Q1 typically witnesses lower order activity due to the industry’s seasonal order cycle.
  • Execution timelines on awarded work. The Karnataka State Fire and Emergency Services order (L-1 bidder, INR 4.03 Crores, for supply, installation, testing and commissioning of 3 KVA UPS with battery conforming to IS 16242) carries a 60-day execution period.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Order book and pipeline. Total order in hand stands at INR 10,852 Mn across 117 projects as of June 2026. The company was declared L-1 bidder by Karnataka State Fire and Emergency Services for an INR 4.03 Crores domestic order (intimation dated June 24, 2026, correcting an earlier date error of June 3, 2026 to June 23, 2026).
  • Capacity expansion. The company operates 3 manufacturing units and is expanding BESS and UPS manufacturing capacities. The 1.2 GWh Jhajjar BESS facility is expected to be commissioned by the end of H1-FY27, and the Gujarat expansion for UPS manufacturing is expected to be operational by Q2-FY27.
  • Digital systems rollout. SAP and Sales Force are expected to be operational by the end of H1-FY27.
  • End-market growth projections. India’s power inverter market is expected to reach USD 22 billion by 2034 growing at a 15% CAGR; the uninterrupted power supply market is projected to grow at 8%–10% CAGR during 2023–28; India’s BESS market is expected to grow from under 0.2 GW to 66 GW by 2032; and the BESS market is valued at USD 7.8 billion in 2024 and projected to reach USD 32 billion by 2030.
  • Broadened objects clause. The 19th AGM (September 11, 2026) passed a resolution altering the Objects Clause of the MOA, inserting a sub-clause covering end-to-end system integration, IT infrastructure and digital technology services, and BESS/energy storage projects across grid, commercial, industrial, renewable energy and smart infrastructure applications, for customers in India and abroad.
  • Fundraise for working capital. A preferential issue of fully convertible warrants is intended to meet working capital requirements, fund day-to-day operational needs, and strengthen liquidity.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory approval risk. The awaited licence/approval for the relocated operations has already pushed back commencement, with the company now targeting on or before September 30, 2026.
  • Dilution from warrant conversion. Upon allotment of warrants the paid-up equity share capital remains unchanged; upon full conversion of all warrants into equity shares, paid-up equity share capital would increase from 5,88,74,592 equity shares to 6,18,18,309 equity shares, subject to receipt of balance consideration and exercise of the conversion option within the prescribed period.
  • Conversion and funding uncertainty. Each warrant is convertible into one fully paid-up equity share at the warrant holder’s option, in one or more tranches, within 18 months from allotment. Only 25% of the issue price is payable at subscription, with the balance 75% payable at exercise/conversion — so the full fund inflow is contingent on holders exercising.
  • Execution and counterparty concentration. Order book of INR 10,852 Mn across 117 projects implies dependence on timely execution and on government/public-sector-linked customers (e.g., Karnataka State Fire and Emergency Services).
  • Seasonality. Lower Q1 order activity due to the industry’s seasonal order cycle.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • Guidance on relocated operations: Commencement now expected on or before September 30, 2026. Observed: The June 16, 2026 intimation had flagged relocation/consolidation; as of August 12, 2026, infrastructure was substantially ready but operations had not commenced pending regulatory approval — i.e., the timeline has slipped.
  • Guidance on capacity and systems: 1.2 GWh Jhajjar BESS facility commissioning by end of H1-FY27; Gujarat UPS manufacturing expansion operational by Q2-FY27; SAP and Sales Force operational by end of H1-FY27. Observed: These remain forward expectations; the August 12, 2026 disclosure confirms only that infrastructure for the relocated operations is substantially ready.
  • Guidance on fund utilisation: Management intends to utilise the entire proceeds from the preferential issue within 24 months from receipt of funds for working capital requirements. Observed: The board approved the preferential issue on August 12, 2026 (up to 29,43,717 warrants at Rs. 147/- each, aggregating up to Rs. 43,27,26,399/-), with the relevant date for minimum issue price being August 12, 2026; the 19th AGM on September 11, 2026 passed the resolution for the issue of fully convertible warrants on a preferential basis. A separate disclosure describes a preferential issue of up to 28,98,717 convertible warrants at Rs. 147/- per warrant to non-promoter allottees, raising up to Rs. 42,61,11,399/-, with 25% upfront and 75% on exercise within 18 months.
  • Guidance on revenue visibility: Management notes strong revenue visibility for the coming quarters. Observed: Q1-FY27 Total Income of INR 760 Mn, EBITDA of INR 65 Mn (8.55% margin), PAT of INR 46 Mn (6.05% margin), with order in hand of INR 10,852 Mn across 117 projects as of June 2026.
  • Governance and approvals: The 19th AGM (September 11, 2026) passed all six resolutions — adoption of financial statements, re-appointment of director Mr. Ram Agarwal, appointment of M/s Valawat and Associates as statutory auditors, ratification of cost auditor remuneration for M/s Y R Doshi & Company, alteration of the Objects Clause of the MOA, and the preferential issue of fully convertible warrants. The board also approved M/s Valawat and Associates (FRN 003623C) as statutory auditors for five consecutive years from the conclusion of the 19th AGM until the conclusion of the 24th AGM (FY 2030-31), and appointed Mr. Sandeep P Parekh as scrutinizer for remote e-voting and e-voting at the AGM. Book closure runs from September 5, 2026 to September 11, 2026 (both days inclusive).
  • Investor engagement: Management is scheduled to hold an in-person, one-to-one meeting with various investors organised by Valorem Advisors on Thursday, September 24, 2026, with discussions based strictly on publicly available information and no unpublished price sensitive information intended to be discussed.
