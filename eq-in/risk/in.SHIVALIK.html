Shivalik Rasayan Limited — Business Responsibility & Sustainability Report, FY 2025-26

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Regulatory and tax litigation. The company disclosed a monetary penalty/fine of ₹1,18,34,538 levied by the Assistant Commissioner, Central Goods & Services Tax, Dehradun, relating to an alleged excess input tax credit for FY 2017-18. The matter is pending before the Nainital High Court, and the company has preferred an appeal. Shivalik states the discrepancy arose from a clerical error where input credit (IGST ₹1,15,77,552; CGST ₹1,28,493; SGST ₹1,28,493) was filed under Table 4A(4) instead of Table 4A(5) of GSTR-3B for March 2018.

Elevated workforce turnover. Turnover rates for permanent employees rose to 48.66% in FY 2025-26 from 43.08% in FY 2023-24, though they were below the FY 2024-25 level of 76.40%. Permanent worker turnover was 49.30% in FY 2025-26, down from 93.06% in FY 2024-25 but below the FY 2023-24 figure of 56.54%. Male permanent worker turnover was reported at 69.5% for FY 2025-26.

Safety performance deterioration. The Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate for workers rose to 2.62 per million person-hours in FY 2025-26 from 0.21 in FY 2024-25. No fatalities or high-consequence injuries were reported for either year.

Rising payables cycle. Number of days of accounts payables increased to 142 days in FY 2025-26 from 104 days in FY 2024-25.

Concentrated domestic revenue base. Domestic sales accounted for 98.61% of turnover, with exports at just 1.39%. Agrochemicals contributed 88.85% of turnover, indicating dependence on a single business segment.

Material risks identified by the company. Health & Safety, Business Ethics, and Compliance were flagged as risks with negative financial implications. Health & Safety risk is tied to potential people and community impact and operational disruption in the chemical industry; Business Ethics risk could affect brand and stakeholder trust; Compliance risk is managed through a legal tracking system and internal/external audits.

Governance gaps. The company has not undertaken assessment or assurance of the BRSR core, and no assurance provider was named. No specific commitments, goals, or targets with defined timelines were set for any of the nine NGRBC principles. No cyber security and data privacy policy exists. Independent assessment of policy working was not carried out by an external agency; reviews are conducted internally by senior management.

Environmental data gaps. Scope 1 and Scope 2 GHG emissions were reported as NA for both years, and no project related to reducing GHG emissions exists. Renewable energy consumption was nil in both years.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Employee development as an opportunity. The company identifies employee development as an opportunity, linking encouraged employees to improved operational efficiency and higher customer satisfaction, with learning and development opportunities provided across levels.

Process and product innovation. R&D center and pilot plant facility, a dedicated technology transfer team, and continuous new product process development are cited as supporting growth and relevance to changing customer needs.

Waste management as an opportunity. Efficient waste management is expected to yield resource recovery, cost savings, and reduced environmental impact, with recycling and waste reduction practices optimizing resource utilization and lowering disposal expenses.

Rising R&D capex on sustainability. R&D capex directed at improving environmental and social impacts of products and processes rose to 7.30% of total R&D and capex in FY 2025-26 from 3.01% in FY 2024-25, with the improvement cited as chemistry process enhancement.

Sustainable sourcing. 90% of inputs were sourced sustainably, supported by a structured supplier selection approach covering sustainable procurement, EHS compliance, legal requirements, and ISO certifications.

Improved environmental intensities. Energy intensity per rupee of turnover improved to 0.0000222803 from 0.000024106; energy intensity in physical output terms fell to 12.26 GJ/MT from 13.04 GJ/MT. Water intensity per rupee of turnover improved to 0.0000441687 from 0.0000569861, and water intensity in physical output terms fell to 24.31 KL/MT from 30.82 KL/MT. Total water withdrawal declined to 75,190 KL from 85,038 KL.

Lower air emissions. NOx fell to 114.00 PPM from 139.60 PPM; SOx fell to 37.68 PPM from 96.30 PPM; particulate matter fell to 249.5 MG/NM³ from 463 MG/NM³.

Waste reduction. Total waste generated declined to 16.377 MT from 36.417 MT, with ETP sludge falling to 16.079 MT from 36.261 MT.

Zero Liquid Discharge. ZLD is implemented, with ETP (70 kl/day), RO (50 kl/day), MEE (50 kl/day), and STP (20 KL/day) at the Dahej-II plant, and a Multi Effect Evaporator with ATFD and stripper at the Dehradun plant.

Certifications. ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2015, and ISO 45001:2018 are adopted.

Community and stakeholder engagement. Ongoing engagement channels exist across customers, shareholders, employees, partners, regulators, and communities, with a site-level grievance committee and senior leadership interaction with communities.

Local sourcing. Inputs sourced directly from within the district and neighboring districts rose to 27% from 20%, while direct sourcing from MSMEs/small producers was 18%, down from 20%.

3. Key Risks

  • Health & Safety: Chemical industry operations can directly impact people and the community and disrupt operations; mitigated through a Health & Safety Management Plan, Process Safety & Risk Management, Emergency Mitigation System, regular trainings, mock drills, and an on-site disaster management plan.
  • Business Ethics: Potential impact on brand and stakeholder trust; mitigated through the Code of Conduct and a monitoring mechanism for ethical conduct.
  • Compliance: Managed via a legal tracking system for applicable obligations and renewal requirements, with regular reviews and internal and external audits.
  • Tax litigation: The CGST penalty matter remains pending before the Nainital High Court with an appeal preferred.
  • Safety incidents: LTIFR for workers increased to 2.62 in FY 2025-26; all safety-related accidents are investigated and learnings shared across the organization, with corrective action effectiveness checked during safety audits.
  • Data privacy and cyber security: No cyber security and data privacy policy exists; no data breaches were reported.
  • Product safety: No voluntary or forced recalls; no corrective actions relating to advertising, essential services delivery, cyber security, data privacy, or product safety penalties were reported.
  • Related-party concentration: Share of related-party transactions in sales rose to 1345.40 in FY 2025-26 from 684.49 in FY 2024-25; related-party investments rose to 8,109.13 from 7,446.63; related-party purchases rose to 7.49 from 4.41.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance and commitments. The company states it is committed to integrating ESG principles, addressing a majority of the SDGs, and conducting fair business practices toward labour, human capital, and community. It states it provides clean, safe, healthy, and fair working conditions and strives to be the neighbour of choice in its communities. Dr. Vimal Kumar Shrawat, Managing Director, is responsible for implementation and oversight of Business Responsibility policies under the guidance of the Board and its Committees, with performance of each principle reviewed periodically by Committees led by Management and the Board. Statutory Compliance Certificates on applicable laws are provided by the MD to the Board annually. The company states it is compliant with the Water Act, Air Act, and Environment Protection Act and rules thereunder, with no non-compliances reported.

Observed performance against these positions.

  • Safety: Despite the stated commitment to safe working conditions and ISO 45001:2018 certification, the worker LTIFR rose to 2.62 in FY 2025-26 from 0.21 in FY 2024-25. Health and safety practices and working conditions were assessed at 100% of plants and offices, with no employee or worker complaints on working conditions or health and safety in either year.
  • Training coverage: Health and safety training coverage for employees declined to 79.85% in FY 2025-26 from 91.04% in FY 2024-25, and skill upgradation training coverage declined to 76.19% from 88.68%. Worker health and safety training coverage rose to 95.65% from 91.78%, and worker skill upgradation coverage rose to 98.55% from 95.89%.
  • Human rights training: 80.22% of permanent employees and 84.06% of permanent workers were covered by human rights training in FY 2025-26, versus 80.19% and 89.04% respectively in FY 2024-25.
  • Well-being spending: Cost incurred on well-being measures as a percentage of total revenue was 15.19% in FY 2025-26 versus 15.51% in FY 2024-25.
  • Grievances: No complaints were filed or pending across communities, shareholders, employees and workers, customers, or value chain partners in either FY 2025-26 or FY 2024-25. No POSH complaints were reported in either year. No disciplinary action for bribery or corruption was taken against directors, KMPs, or employees in either year, and no conflict-of-interest complaints were received.
  • Environmental compliance: No fines, penalties, or regulatory action were reported under environmental laws, and no environmental impact assessments were applicable.
  • Targets: No specific commitments, goals, or targets with defined timelines were set for any NGRBC principle, and no performance against such targets was reported.
  • Assurance: No BRSR core assessment or assurance was undertaken, and no assurance provider was engaged.
