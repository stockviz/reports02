Broker Narrative

The narrative remained consistently bullish: both the first and last reports are Buy calls, with near-term weaknesses framed as temporary. Early concerns about macro overhang and delayed approval cycles evolved into specific execution headwinds — delayed project ramp-ups, shipment disruptions, wage hikes, and segment weakness. Optimism shifted from payout yields, cash conversion, and a H2FY24E recovery to AI monetization, large-deal TCV, margin expansion, and the Lakshya ’31 doubling target, while the expected demand recovery kept getting pushed further out.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning of delayed approval cycles and tighter scrutiny materialized as slower-than-expected ramp-up of large deals and specific project/shipment delays (CBDT deal, Middle East hardware) highlighted in the last report.

  • The early fear of clients reprioritizing spends toward cost optimization appeared later as margin pressure from wage hikes, headcount reduction, and reliance on internal cost-efficiency initiatives for margin expansion.

Optimism that failed

  • The projected demand recovery through H2FY24E failed to materialize; by the last report, growth acceleration was still only guided to begin from Q2 FY27, implying the recovery was repeatedly delayed.
  • The early confidence in a good long-term demand outlook did not translate into sustained positive stock performance, as the majority of intervening 100+ reports show Buy/Add calls followed by DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and negative actual returns.
