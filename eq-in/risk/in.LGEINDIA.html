1. Headwinds and Challenges

Sequential Revenue Decline The company reported revenue from operations of INR 72,333.50 million for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, compared to INR 80,535.52 million in the preceding quarter ended March 31, 2026. This represents a sequential decline. The Home Appliances and Air Solution division declined from INR 65,162.89 million to INR 55,766.93 million quarter-on-quarter, while the Home Entertainment division increased from INR 15,372.63 million to INR 16,566.57 million over the same period.

Profitability Pressure Profit before tax declined from INR 9,310.16 million in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 to INR 8,780.72 million in the quarter ended June 30, 2026. Profit for the period similarly declined from INR 6,927.31 million to INR 6,528.63 million. Basic and diluted EPS declined from INR 10.21 to INR 9.62 quarter-on-quarter.

Cost Pressures Cost of materials consumed increased from INR 42,244.95 million in the March 2026 quarter to INR 45,370.72 million in the June 2026 quarter. Purchases of stock-in-trade also increased from INR 5,398.05 million to INR 6,778.82 million over the same period. Employee benefits expense rose from INR 2,348.13 million to INR 2,786.82 million.

ESG and Operational Challenges The Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report identifies climate action as a risk, citing rising GHG emissions globally, regulatory pressures encouraging emission reduction, and adverse impacts on businesses and society affecting cashflows and expenses. Waste management is identified as a risk, with improper disposal potentially leading to environmental damage, regulatory penalties, and reputational damage. Employee health and safety is identified as a risk, with workplace accidents potentially disrupting operations and harming reputation. Quality and customer satisfaction is identified as a risk, with product defects potentially harming brand reputation and leading to recalls.

Customer Grievances The BRSR discloses 85,715 customer complaints filed during FY 2025-26, compared to 71,743 in FY 2024-25. The company notes these include customer concerns, suggestions, and escalations received through social media and the National Consumer Helpline, with several matters pertaining to delivery, packaging, and related service aspects.

Tax Litigation History The company received an ITAT order on July 29, 2026, relating to income tax matters for financial years 2014-15, 2016-17, 2017-18, 2019-20, and 2021-22. The order deleted all additions related to Transfer Pricing and Corporate Income Tax amounting to approximately INR 1,305 Crores. The company notes the Income Tax Department may prefer an appeal in the Hon’ble High Court on Corporate Tax issues.

Governance Gaps The BRSR discloses that the company has not carried out independent assessment or evaluation of the working of its policies by an external agency. The company states it is in the process of institutionalising periodic reviews of its performance against applicable policies through structured business review meetings with senior management. Sustainable sourcing procedures are not in place, with the company stating it is currently undergoing and implementing ESG-related assessments and intends to progressively formalize its sustainable sourcing framework.

Diversity Metrics The BRSR discloses that differently abled employees and workers are Nil across permanent and other than permanent categories. 3. Key Risks

Climate Action Risk The BRSR identifies climate action as a risk, with rising GHG emissions globally resulting in many regulations nationally and globally encouraging GHG emission reduction. Global warming is having adverse impact on businesses and society, resulting in impacts to cashflows and expenses. The approach to adapt or mitigate includes adoption of renewable energy, energy efficiency projects, transitioning to lower carbon fuel, and climate adaptation projects. Financial implications are negative, with potential compliance costs.

Waste Management Risk Improper disposal could lead to damage to environment and may attract regulatory penalties and reputational damage. The approach to mitigate includes implementing waste segregation at source, introducing the 3R principle of reduce, reuse, and recycle, partnering with authorized and capable hazardous waste handling companies, and circular economy practices in operations. Financial implications are negative, with heavy penalties and remediation cost, and operation closure in case of non-conformity.

Employee Health and Safety Risk Workplace accidents can disrupt operations and harm reputation. The approach to mitigate includes safety training, audits, protective equipment, strict compliance, and inculcating a safety culture in the company. Financial implications are negative, with potential compensation and legal cost, and potential closure of operation.

Quality and Customer Satisfaction Risk Product defects can harm brand reputation and lead to recalls. The approach to mitigate includes quality assurance systems and customer feedback loops. Financial implications are negative, with potential decrease in sales volume.

Tax Litigation Risk The company notes the Income Tax Department may prefer an appeal in the Hon’ble High Court on Corporate Tax issues following the ITAT order received on July 29, 2026. The Assessing Officer is yet to issue “Order Giving Effect to ITAT Order.”

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from an appliance-sector bull case built on policy catalysts, localization, and premiumisation in the first report to a wire-and-cable outlook emphasizing ~13% W&C CAGR and UltraTech-driven competitive intensity in the last report, with early optimism around margin expansion and capital returns giving way to concerns over competitive entry and input costs.

Fears that came true

  • Steep Competition: Intense competitive intensity across white goods materialized, correlating with negative returns (-5.1% to -1.5%) in Aug-Sep 2026.
  • Commodity Price Inflation: Input cost inflation risk materialized, pressuring margins as reflected in negative returns.

Optimism that failed

  • Macro & Policy Catalysts: GST, tax, and interest rate cuts failed to lift the stock, which posted negative returns in Aug-Sep 2026.
  • Low Appliance Penetration & Premiumisation: The expected 200-300 bps p.a. value uplift did not prevent negative returns.
  • Localization & Cost Efficiencies: Domestic sourcing expansion and backward integration did not shield the stock from losses.
  • Superior Capital Returns: High RoE and FCF generation failed to support positive performance.
