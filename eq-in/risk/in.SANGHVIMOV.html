Sanghvi Movers — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure in the core business. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin fell to 35% from 40% in Q4 FY26 and 38% in Q1 FY26, even as revenue grew 39% YoY. Management attributes this to a combination of factors: higher Expected Credit Loss (ECL) provisions due to ageing receivables; a revenue mix shift toward hired ancillary equipment and asset-light cross-rental cranes; one-time incentives for frontline workers and the C-suite; the structural cost impact of the new labour codes; and mark-to-market forex restatement on loans. The company notes ECL should rationalise over the course of the year.

Labour code transition. The four Labour Codes notified on 21 November 2025 consolidated 29 existing labour laws. The company recognised incremental provisions (gratuity and long-term compensated absences) as exceptional items, and has taken a conscious call to absorb the impact for employees earning below ₹50,000. It continues to monitor finalisation of Central/State rules and may record further accounting effects.

Working capital stretch in international operations. Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) stood at 201 days for the GCC crane rental business versus 124 days for India and 98 days for Renewables E&C, with a group DSO of 116 days.

GCC profitability still nascent. The GCC crane rental segment reported ROCE of -6% (as on 31-Mar-26), though it turned EBITDA-positive in Q1 FY27 at a 23% margin.

Sector-level headwinds cited in the industry overview: - Refining: EU ban on Russian-crude products (in force 21 Jan 2026) cut India’s EU product exports ~97%; sanctions on Rosneft/Lukoil reduced Russian intake; project cost escalation (HRRL Barmer revised from ₹43,129 cr to ₹79,459 cr; Panipat raised to ₹36,225 cr). - Cement: ICRA expects FY27 EBITDA/t to ease to ₹820–870 from ₹900–950; 70–75 MT of new capacity commissioning this fiscal; EU CBAM covers cement exports from Jan 2026. - Steel: EU CBAM definitive phase live from Jan 2026 (~€270/t on BF-route HRC); EU steel import quotas cut ~47% from 1 Jul 2026 and UK quotas -60%; domestic oversupply pressure. - Wind: ISTS waiver available only for projects commissioned by Jun 2028 with no extension announced; CERC cut the deviation band to ±10% (Apr 2026); land, RoW and grid delays persist; offshore bids yet to be awarded. - Thermal: coal PLF seen at ~61% by FY32; ESG limits on coal funding; ~17 GW of import-coal fleet needs costly boiler rework. - Nuclear: SHANTI Act rules and AERB build-out pending; long gestation and funding cycles; SMR vendor ecosystem still maturing.

Leverage rising. The D/E ceiling guidance moves from 0.47 in FY26 to 0.72 in FY27 and 0.76–0.80 in FY28; actual D/E rose from 0.40 in Q1 FY26 to 0.54 in Q1 FY27.

Insurance claim unresolved. The FY26 crane cabin damage loss (net WDV ₹377.96 lakhs plus ₹59.20 lakhs input tax credit reversal) is subject to an insurance claim; pending final assessment, no claim receivable has been recognised.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong order book momentum. Order book rose 19% to ₹1,253 Cr as on 24 Jul 2026 from ₹1,053 Cr on 14 May 2026 (over 10 weeks), driven by SML and SFRPL. Group enquiry pipeline stands at ₹5,641 Cr, including ₹4,656 Cr from Renewables E&C and ₹631 Cr from India crane rental.

International expansion gaining traction. Operations now span India, KSA, Botswana and Qatar. Botswana cranes were commissioned on schedule with ~$1.1 Mn repatriated to India. KSA delivered cumulative EBITDA-positive performance, with SMMEL inflecting from -23% EBITDA in FY26 to +23% in Q1 FY27. The KSA market shows a 0–24 month pipeline of ~$14–38 Mn across infrastructure, housing, industry, giga projects, oil & gas, wind and mining. The addressable market is framed as a $1B+ crane rental market with 10–12% expected CAGR, supported by a $1.5T+ KSA and Qatar infrastructure pipeline.

Renewables as a strategic pivot. Sangreen Future Renewables (SFRPL) has a 2+ GW current order book and 5 GW enquiry pipeline, with 140+ projects delivered and 125+ clients served. It contributes EBITDA at 14% margins on an asset-light base — optically dilutive to blended margin but accretive to ROCE (77% ROCE) and cash generation. Management describes it as building a defensible competitive moat through integrated capabilities and securing more than 20% of core crane rental business demand.

Capex deployment for scale. Of the ₹652 Cr FY27 capex pool, ₹92 Cr (14%) was deployed in Q1 FY27 across India and Saudi Arabia; ₹560 Cr remains pending. The pool includes ₹36 Cr (India) and ₹125 Cr (KSA) carried forward from FY26.

Fleet and utilisation. Group fleet stands at 492 cranes (443 India + Botswana, 49 GCC). Utilisation is 86% in both India and GCC. GCC yield (4.10%) is materially higher than India (2.29%).

Sector tailwinds cited: - Refining: government target of 300+ MMTPA reaffirmed (India Energy Week, Feb 2026); IOCL Panipat, Koyali and Barauni expansions (+17.3 MMTPA) commissioning Nov–Dec 2026; petchem import-duty relief on 40 inputs suspended Apr–Jun 2026. - Cement: Budget FY27 record ₹12.2 lakh cr capex and new Infrastructure Risk Guarantee Fund; MoRTH up ~8% to ₹3.09 lakh cr; ₹54,917 cr for PMAY-Gramin; FY26 volumes +9.2% YoY. - Steel: 3-year safeguard duty notified Dec 2025 (12% tapering to 11% by Apr 2028); CBIC circular (May 2026) easing customs clearance; 300 MT capacity target for 2030. - Wind: record 6.05 GW added in FY26 (+46%); 29 GW under construction as of Apr 2026; MNRE execution task force (Jan 2026); unified wind RCO framework; ₹6,853 cr offshore VGF and 500 MW CfD pilot. - Thermal: 97 GW coal expansion plan targeting 307 GW by FY35; 38.7 GW under construction and 22.9 GW awarded (Jan 2026); 18.2 GW added since Apr 2023. - Nuclear: SHANTI Act enacted Dec 2025 opening nuclear to private and foreign players; 100 GW by 2047 mission with ₹20,000 cr SMR fund; PFBR Kalpakkam criticality (Apr 2026); Kaiga 5&6 first concrete poured Mar 2026.

Elevate 2030 progress. The transformation roadmap shows FY26 achievements across business structure (India + KSA + Botswana + Qatar), capital model (SFRPL asset-light complement), product portfolio (crane to spider cranes), leadership (complete CXO team), technology (operations bots, ISO 27001 certification) and strategy. FY27 targets include new country activation, pivot to capital allocator, incremental product segments, governance upgrades, and HRMS/Power BI/CRM/AI pilot implementation.

3. Key Risks

  • Margin sustainability: The gap between revenue growth (39%) and EBITDA growth (30%) in Q1 FY27, with margin down 300 bps YoY, raises the question of whether the cited impacts are genuinely one-time.
  • Receivables and credit risk: Rising ECL provisions and elevated GCC DSO (201 days) point to collection risk in newer geographies.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk in new markets: GCC ROCE remains negative; Botswana and Qatar operations are early-stage.
  • Leverage trajectory: Guided D/E ceiling nearly doubling from FY26 to FY28 increases financial risk if growth does not materialise as planned.
  • Policy and regulatory dependence: The business outlook is tied to refinery, cement, steel, wind, thermal and nuclear policy cycles, several of which carry explicit headwinds (CBAM, ISTS waiver cliff, EU quotas).
  • Concentration in renewables order book: SFRPL accounts for ₹686 Cr of the ₹1,253 Cr order book and ₹4,656 Cr of the ₹5,641 Cr enquiry pipeline.
  • Labour code finalisation: Further accounting effects may arise as Central/State rules are clarified.
  • Unresolved insurance claim: No receivable recognised pending insurer assessment.

Broker Narrative

The first report was a Sell with no tailwinds, focused on valuation risk, execution/approval delays, cost/competitive pressures, and policy/geopolitical risks. The last report upgraded to Hold and retained approval/supply-chain and geopolitical concerns, but dropped valuation and cost pressures from the key bear case. The narrative shifted from pure downside risk to a defence-sector growth story, citing indigenisation, order pipeline, Atmanirbhar Bharat, higher defence budgets, exports, and AI/robotics as tailwinds.

Fears that came true

  • The valuation risk materialized: the stock suffered a -56.1% actual return after the first report, suggesting the steep +2SD multiple compressed.
  • Execution/approval delays materialized: the last report still lists delays in government approvals and supply chain difficulties as ongoing headwinds.
  • Geopolitical risk materialized as a persistent issue: the last report continues to flag Russia-Ukraine and Israel-Palestine disputes.
  • Cost/competitive pressure risk was validated by the -56.1% negative actual return, consistent with the feared margin pressure.
