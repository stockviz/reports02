Landmark Cars Limited — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure. Gross profit margin for the June 2026 quarter was lower both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year. The company attributes this to a changed sales and service revenue mix, the accounting of an annual incentive in the previous quarter, and an IndAS gain recorded in the prior year.

Macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty. The company flags developments in West Asia, currency movement, fuel-price volatility, and supply-chain disruptions as external pressures.

Regulatory and policy change. Taxation, emission standards, safety requirements, and a pending legal matter relating to GST compensation cess credits are identified as areas of exposure.

OEM concentration. Dependence on select OEM partners is called out as a structural risk.

Gestation lag on new capacity. Newly added outlets and workshops require a gestation period before reaching optimal productivity, weighing on near-term returns.

Supply constraints. BYD supply improved in the June 2026 quarter, with further improvement expected only in the current quarter — indicating prior availability limitations.

Broader forward-looking risk factors. The company also cites the performance of Indian and international economies, industry performance, competition, strategy implementation, growth and expansion, technological advancements, changes in revenue, income or cash flows, and market preferences.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1FY27 momentum. The company reported its highest-ever first-quarter sales, with growth in both vehicle sales and after-sales. Proforma revenue rose 22.4% year-on-year to ₹17,325 Mn, EBITDA rose 13.7% to ₹752 Mn, and PAT rose 97.5% to ₹145 Mn. The auto industry is described as continuing a strong growth trajectory after GST 2.0 reforms.

New model launches. Deliveries commenced in the June 2026 quarter for the Mercedes-Benz CLA, MG Majestor, and the new Renault Duster. Further launches are expected from Mercedes-Benz, BYD, MG, M&M, Honda, and KIA in upcoming quarters.

After-sales expansion. Ramp-up and stabilisation of newly opened workshops supported solid after-sales performance in line with expectations, and workshop capacity is being expanded across several brands to meet demand in select geographies. After-sales crossed ₹10,000 Million in annual revenue for the first time in FY2025-26.

EV and new-energy positioning. EVs contributed approximately 30% of new-car revenue in Q1FY27. MG is described as a leading player in the new energy vehicle segment with multiple planned launches.

Network expansion. On 24 July 2026, the company received a letter of intent from JSW MG Motor India to open a new MG Experia showroom in Vastral, Ahmedabad, to be established by wholly owned subsidiary Aeromark Cars Private Limited. This would be Landmark’s 17th MG outlet and its 2nd MG sales outlet in Ahmedabad, consistent with its stated objective of going deeper into a brand and a geography.

New revenue stream. A Memorandum of Understanding with Tecso Charge Zone Limited, an EV charging infrastructure company, provides wallet credit and other benefits to eligible customers through a designated referral code.

Industry structure and allied revenue. India’s fragmented automobile dealership industry offers headroom for selective organic expansion and acquisition-led growth. Other tailwinds cited include maturing of recently added outlets providing operating leverage, recent trade agreements (India-EU FTA, India-UK CETA, India-US framework), India-focused product strategies and localisation by OEM partners, continued premiumisation, and growth in finance, insurance, accessories, and pre-owned vehicle revenue.

3. Key Risks

  • Macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty, including West Asia developments, currency movement, fuel-price volatility, and supply-chain disruption.
  • Regulatory and policy changes covering taxation, emission standards, and safety requirements.
  • A pending legal matter relating to GST compensation cess credits.
  • Dependency on select OEM partners.
  • Gestation period required for newly added outlets and workshops to reach optimal productivity.
  • Competition, strategy implementation, growth and expansion execution, technological advancements, and shifts in market preferences.
  • Gross margin sensitivity to revenue mix and to prior-period accounting items such as annual incentives and IndAS gains.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

FY2026-27 to be a consolidation year. Management stated that FY2026-27 will focus on consolidation rather than continued rapid expansion — making existing capacity work harder, allowing newer outlets to stabilise, improving throughput, and restoring profit metrics. Capital expenditure is not expected to remain at the elevated levels seen during the expansion phase. Growth will continue through facilities already under implementation, such as BYD in Pune and additional after-sales capacity for Mahindra & Mahindra, with profitability and capital efficiency central to every decision.

Observed performance against that guidance. The June 2026 quarter showed the highest-ever first-quarter sales, with proforma revenue up 22.4% year-on-year and PAT up 97.5% year-on-year, alongside after-sales performance described as in line with expectations and supported by newly opened workshops ramping up. At the same time, gross profit margin declined on both a quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year basis, reflecting mix and prior-period accounting effects — consistent with the stated emphasis on restoring profit metrics during the consolidation phase.

Near-term outlook. Management describes upcoming quarters as promising, citing strong market momentum, timely maturing of newly set-up outlets, and new model launches, and reiterates a commitment to cost discipline with the goal of further improving profitability.

FY2025-26 baseline. For FY2025-26, the company reported its highest-ever proforma revenue of approximately ₹67,187 Million (up 19.4%) and revenue from operations of ₹48,962 Million (up 21.6%). EBITDA reached a record ₹2,830 Million, PAT rose 119.7% to ₹381 Million, and net cash generated from operations grew 76% to ₹2,675 Million. The network reached 140 outlets across 29 cities and 12 states, and EVs contributed 24% of new-car sales on a proforma revenue basis. The Board recommended a final dividend of ₹1.50 per equity share (30% of face value) for FY2025-26.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from cautious optimism about premiumisation and low luxury-car penetration in 2023 to stronger conviction by 2026, adding EV adoption, operating leverage, and after-sales resilience as key drivers. Persistent themes included India’s low luxury penetration and premiumisation-led ASP growth, while risks shifted from demand cyclicality and discount risk to execution, supply chain, and inventory concerns.

Fears that came true

  • The flagged slowdown in the domestic PV market materialized, as the stock delivered negative returns across multiple reports in 2024-2025 before the eventual recovery.
  • The early risk of higher working capital and discounts during weak demand materialized, reflected in last report’s moderately elevated inventory (36 days vs 28 days) and tactically managed build-up ahead of price hikes.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected same-store sales growth from higher-margin service/spares revenue failed to materialize; last report flags slower-than-built-in profitability gains at existing outlets.
  • The optimism around new OEM additions and launches (e.g., Honda Elevate, BYD) failed to deliver expected uptake; last report cites lower-than-built-in uptake in new vehicle sales.
