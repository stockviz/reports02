Atul Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Cost pressure in the June 2026 quarter. On a standalone basis, cost of materials consumed rose sharply to ₹985.94 crore in Q1 FY2026-27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) from ₹804.79 crore in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 and ₹770.34 crore in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. Power, fuel and water costs also increased to ₹159.57 crore from ₹144.11 crore and ₹138.68 crore respectively. On a consolidated basis, cost of materials consumed rose to ₹1,103.00 crore from ₹889.13 crore and ₹791.09 crore over the same comparison periods.

Employee benefit expenses increased. Standalone employee benefit expenses were ₹109.62 crore in Q1 FY2026-27, up from ₹80.20 crore in the prior quarter and ₹94.92 crore a year earlier. Consolidated employee benefit expenses were ₹132.18 crore, compared with ₹107.70 crore and ₹120.02 crore.

Sequential moderation in profitability. Standalone profit for the period declined to ₹201.03 crore in Q1 FY2026-27 from ₹203.84 crore in the quarter ended March 31, 2026, although it was well above ₹97.74 crore in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. Consolidated profit for the period rose to ₹253.93 crore from ₹211.12 crore sequentially and ₹132.36 crore year-on-year.

Prior-quarter comparatives were flattered by a one-off gain. The company disclosed that employee benefit expenses for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 included the impact of reassessment of past service cost on gratuity and remeasurement of leave encashment following implementation of the new labour code, resulting in a gain from reversal of excess provision of ₹24.38 crore (standalone) and ₹25.41 crore (consolidated). This affects the comparability of the March 2026 quarter.

Other income fell sequentially. Standalone other income was ₹26.31 crore in Q1 FY2026-27, down from ₹74.96 crore in the quarter ended March 31, 2026. Consolidated other income was ₹33.31 crore versus ₹89.57 crore.

Segment mix. Life Science Chemicals segment revenue was ₹469.23 crore in Q1 FY2026-27, with segment results of ₹92.78 crore, compared with segment results of ₹143.06 crore in the quarter ended March 31, 2026. Performance and Other Chemicals segment revenue was ₹1,427.44 crore with segment results of ₹239.83 crore, versus ₹124.94 crore in the prior quarter.

Year-on-year revenue growth. Standalone revenue from operations rose to ₹1,588.60 crore in Q1 FY2026-27 from ₹1,304.56 crore in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. Consolidated revenue from operations rose to ₹1,847.95 crore from ₹1,478.00 crore.

Year-on-year profit growth. Standalone profit for the period nearly doubled to ₹201.03 crore from ₹97.74 crore a year earlier. Consolidated profit for the period rose to ₹253.93 crore from ₹132.36 crore.

Stronger consolidated segment performance. Consolidated segment results for Performance and Other Chemicals were ₹239.83 crore in Q1 FY2026-27, up from ₹100.00 crore in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. Life Science Chemicals segment results were ₹92.78 crore versus ₹68.41 crore.

Total comprehensive income. Standalone total comprehensive income was ₹321.37 crore in Q1 FY2026-27, compared with ₹266.33 crore a year earlier. Consolidated total comprehensive income was ₹375.41 crore versus ₹308.90 crore.

3. Key Risks

Input cost volatility. The sharp sequential increase in cost of materials consumed and power, fuel and water indicates exposure to input cost movements.

Comparability and one-off items. The labour code-related reversal of excess provision in the March 2026 quarter affects period-to-period comparability of employee benefit expenses and profitability.

Segment concentration. Performance and Other Chemicals accounted for the bulk of consolidated segment revenue (₹1,427.44 crore of ₹1,922.65 crore subtotal in Q1 FY2026-27) and segment assets (₹4,166.73 crore of ₹8,528.42 crore total assets), concentrating exposure in this segment.

Unreviewed subsidiary financials. The auditor’s consolidated review report notes that interim financial information of 3 subsidiaries was reviewed by other auditors (total revenues ₹283.27 crore, net profit after tax ₹13.42 crore for the quarter), and that 36 subsidiaries were not reviewed by their auditors (total revenues ₹75.47 crore, net profit after tax ₹10.66 crore). The Group’s share of net profit after tax of an associate and a joint venture of ₹(1.43) crore was based on unreviewed interim financial information. The auditor stated these are not material to the Group.

Foreign operations and currency translation. Certain subsidiaries are located outside India, with financial information converted from local GAAP to Indian GAAP; exchange differences on translation of foreign operations are reported within other comprehensive income.

Governance and approval risk. The appointment of Mr Deshpande as Independent Director requires member approval by special resolution through the postal ballot process; the resolution is proposed, not yet passed.

Broker Narrative

Early narrative was a broad sector China+1/import-substitution story, with aggressive capex and moderating input costs expected to outweigh cyclical demand and freight/cost headwinds. By the last report, the thesis had become much more ATUL-specific: product-price inflation, domestic epoxy demand, project execution, and EBITDA upgrades were the drivers, while export-volume softness and fuel/freight/input-cost inflation were the key risks. The recurring theme was capex-led growth, but the earlier assumption of a quick normalization in costs and demand was replaced by pricing-power optimism and a sharper focus on export volumes.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning that input-cost and freight inflation would squeeze margins proved recurring: the last report again flagged fuel, freight and other input-cost inflation partly due to the West Asia crisis, and later calls such as 2024-07-27 Buy (-11.8%) and 2024-10-11 Hold (-15.5%) saw negative actual returns.
  • The subdued-volumes/de-stocking worry materialized in export volume softness: 2,4-D and anisic aldehyde export volumes fell 21.4% and 12.1% YoY in Q1FY27-TD, consistent with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and negative actual returns in 2024 and 2025.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that input-cost pressures and freight rates would moderate from peaks did not hold: by the last report, fuel, freight and other input-cost inflation had returned, driven partly by the West Asia crisis.
  • The China+1/alternate-supplier-led export optimism did not fully translate for key chemicals: 2,4-D and anisic aldehyde export volumes declined in Q1FY27-TD, though epoxy exports rose strongly.
