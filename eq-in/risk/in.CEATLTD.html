CEAT Limited: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material cost inflation. The company described Q1 FY27 as “a challenging quarter for the industry,” with the continuing West Asia crisis driving significant raw material cost inflation. The RM basket cost increased by high-teens on a sequential basis. This weighed on gross and operating margins: consolidated gross margin contracted 575 bps QoQ and 287 bps YoY, while EBITDA margin fell to 8.6% (−562 bps QoQ, −238 bps YoY). Standalone gross margin contracted 623 bps QoQ and 337 bps YoY, with EBITDA margin at 9.1%.

Profitability compression. Consolidated PAT fell to Rs. 4 crore in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 244 crore in Q4 FY26 and Rs. 112 crore in Q1 FY26. Standalone PAT was Rs. 98 crore, down 65.3% QoQ and 27.3% YoY. Operating PBT on a consolidated basis fell 88.5% QoQ and 75.2% YoY.

Higher finance costs. Consolidated finance costs rose 71.9% QoQ and 77.4% YoY to Rs. 146 crore. This included a reassessment by CEAT OHT Lanka of the classification of an exchange loss of Rs. 48 crore from operating expenses to finance costs, on restatement of a USD 80 million foreign currency borrowing from the holding company at the closing exchange rate.

Leverage and coverage. Consolidated D/E rose to 0.65x from 0.60x at Q4 FY26, and Debt/EBITDA to 1.58x from 1.46x. Interest service coverage fell to 2.37x from 6.12x QoQ; debt service coverage fell to 0.51x from 0.90x.

Working capital and cash flow pressure. Net working capital increased QoQ by approximately Rs. 138 crore. Long-term debt to working capital is marked as not meaningful because working capital is negative.

Subsidiary drag. The limited review noted four subsidiaries not reviewed by the auditors with total revenues of Rs. 135 crore and a net loss after tax of Rs. 79 crore for the quarter, plus ten subsidiaries and one trust with revenues of Rs. 71 crore and a net loss of Rs. 16 crore.

Governance dissent signals. At the 67th AGM on August 17, 2026, the resolution to re-appoint Mr. Anant Vardhan Goenka as a director received 9.3168% of votes polled against, with public institutions voting 25.425% against. The resolution for continuation of Mr. Paras Kumar Chowdhary as a Non-Executive, Non-Independent Director saw 2.4916% against overall, with public institutions at 6.7993% against. Both resolutions passed.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Revenue growth. Consolidated revenue reached Rs. 4,318 crore in Q1 FY27, up 2.4% QoQ and 22.3% YoY. Standalone revenue was Rs. 4,163 crore, up 3.2% QoQ and 18.3% YoY. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs. 15,678 crore.

Volume and mix. Healthy YoY volume growth was reported across segments, with QoQ volume growth led by the Replacement segment. International Business was the fastest-growing segment on a YoY basis and continued to recover. Realizations were higher both sequentially and YoY, primarily from price hikes in domestic and international markets. The market mix was Replacement 51% (versus 53% prior), OEM 30% (versus 28%), and Exports 19%.

Pricing response. Cumulative price increases of 5% were taken. Management stated it would continue a disciplined approach to pricing while focusing on profitable growth.

Capacity expansion. On July 16, 2026, the Board approved approximately Rs. 1,205 crore of investment to add about 53,000 tyres per day of capacity, expected by the end of FY2031 and implemented progressively in phases, funded by a mix of internal accruals and debt. The rationale is that existing two-wheeler tyre capacity at the Nagpur plant is nearing full utilisation. Existing capacity is about 80,000 tyres per day (excluding capacity under implementation), with utilisation at approximately 95%.

Camso acquisition scale-up. The company entered definitive agreements on December 6, 2024 with Michelin Group associate companies to acquire the Camso brand’s Off-Highway construction equipment tyre and tracks business through CEAT OHT Lanka (Private) Limited, valued at USD 225 million including a commitment to acquire the Camso trademark for a pre-determined value of USD 44 million and inventories of approximately USD 44 million at a later date. Results include the acquisition from September 1, 2025. CEAT OHT Lanka’s FY2025-26 turnover was Rs. 4,206 million, versus Nil in FY2023-24 and FY2024-25.

Subsidiary funding structure. On August 17, 2026, the Finance and Banking Committee approved conversion of USD 24.5 million of a pre-existing inter-company loan into equity of CEAT OHT Lanka, out of a total loan of USD 80 million. This converts LKR 8,218,525,000 into 12,788,094 ordinary shares at LKR 642.67 per share, subject to Board of Investment approval in Sri Lanka. No fresh capital infusion is involved, and CEAT OHT Lanka remains a 100% wholly owned subsidiary. The company had previously remitted Rs. 274 crore as equity investment and Rs. 719 crore as loan to the subsidiary.

Balance sheet capacity. The AGM passed a special resolution authorising issuance of Non-Convertible Debentures up to Rs. 1,000 crore on a private placement basis. As at June 30, 2026, listed unsecured NCDs aggregating Rs. 400 crore and commercial papers with face value of Rs. 800 crore were outstanding and not due for repayment.

Recognition. CEAT was awarded the EcoVadis Gold Medal, placing it among the top 5% of companies globally assessed for sustainability performance; ranked #4 among the World’s Strongest Tyre Brands in the 2026 Brand Finance Rankings; and received a Best Practices in Corporate Governance & Compliance award at the VaultCircle GC Summit and Awards 2026. Employee Happiness Quotient improved to 87% in FY26 from 85% in FY25.

Dividend. The Board recommended a dividend of Rs. 35 per equity share of face value Rs. 10 for FY2025-26, approved by shareholders at the AGM on August 17, 2026.

3. Key Risks

Raw material cost persistence. Management expects raw material costs to remain at inflated levels in Q2, and stated it will continue balancing pricing actions and cost prudence to progressively mitigate margin impact.

Geopolitical exposure. The continuing West Asia crisis is identified as the driver of raw material cost inflation.

Regulatory and approval dependency. The CEAT OHT Lanka loan conversion is subject to approval by the Board of Investment in Sri Lanka under local norms.

Labour code obligations. The Government of India notified four Labour Codes effective November 21, 2025. The company recognised incremental estimated obligations aggregating Rs. 58 crore as an exceptional item for the year ended March 31, 2026, based on actuarial valuation.

Foreign currency exposure. CEAT OHT Lanka holds a USD 80 million foreign currency borrowing from the holding company, restated at closing exchange rates, which produced an exchange loss of Rs. 48 crore reclassified to finance costs in the quarter.

Negative working capital. Long-term debt to working capital is not meaningful because working capital is negative, and the current ratio stands at 0.67x consolidated.

VRS-related costs. Voluntary Retirement Scheme compensation was Rs. 7 crore in Q1 FY27, Rs. 10 crore in Q4 FY26, Rs. 3 crore in Q1 FY26, and Rs. 13 crore for FY26.

Auditor scope limitations. The limited review excluded from its review four subsidiaries with a net loss of Rs. 79 crore and ten subsidiaries plus one trust with a net loss of Rs. 16 crore, though management stated these are not material to the Group.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Pricing versus margin outcome. Management stated cumulative price increases of 5% were taken to partly offset raw material inflation. Observed performance: gross margin still contracted 575 bps QoQ and 287 bps YoY on a consolidated basis, and EBITDA margin fell to 8.6%, indicating pricing did not fully offset cost inflation in the quarter.

Raw material outlook. Management expects raw material costs to remain at inflated levels in Q2 and intends to balance pricing actions and cost prudence to progressively mitigate margin impact. This is forward-looking; the Q1 outcome already shows margin contraction despite price actions.

Capex discipline. Management stated it stayed invested in capex to the tune of approximately Rs. 300 crore, largely to enhance capacities, while maintaining tight control over discretionary and routine capex to conserve cash. Observed: capex outflow for the quarter was approximately Rs. 293 crore, and the Board subsequently approved Rs. 1,205 crore of two-wheeler capacity expansion.

Demand and market share focus. Management stated it stayed focused on demand and market share despite pressures. Observed: consolidated revenue grew 22.3% YoY with healthy volume growth across segments and high-capacity utilisation, and International Business was the fastest-growing segment YoY.

Capacity planning. The company stated that existing two-wheeler tyre capacity at Nagpur is nearing full utilisation and that it would augment capacity in greenfield and/or brownfield as per internal assessment. Observed: existing utilisation is approximately 95% of installed capacity, and the Board approved about 53,000 tyres per day of additional capacity by end-FY2031.

Auditor re-appointment. The Board approved re-appointment of M/s. B S R & Co. LLP as Statutory Auditors for a second consecutive five-year term, from the conclusion of the 68th AGM (2027) up to the conclusion of the 73rd AGM (2032), subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from an Accumulate call in May-2023 built on RM-cost softness, margin expansion, and upgraded FY24/FY25 EPS, to a Buy call in Jul-2026 that still sees margin recovery but is battling sharp RM inflation and a Q1FY27 miss. Raw-material costs and margin pressure were the central recurring theme, flipping from a tailwind to the main headwind. Export demand also flipped from an early near-term concern to a positive, while demand moderation remained a persistent risk. Later reports added CAMSO as a drag and leaned on price hikes and market-share gains for the bullish case.

Fears that came true

  • Raw-material cost inflation materialized: the RM basket rose ~15% QoQ and domestic natural rubber climbed to ~INR 280/kg by Q1FY27, squeezing margins.
  • Margin moderation materialized: Q1FY27 EBITDA fell 6% YoY/38% QoQ, EBITDA margin contracted 250/550 bps YoY/QoQ, and adjusted PAT fell ~91% YoY.
  • Volume/demand moderation materialized: later reports flag replacement growth moderation and weaker industry tonnage, with actual returns turning flat/negative by mid-2025, including a -9.9% actual return on the 2025-06-04 ICICI Hold call.
  • Export headwinds partially materialized: Middle East export demand remained subdued, though total exports later recovered.

Optimism that failed

  • The early RM-softness/margin-expansion optimism failed: margins eventually compressed sharply as RM costs reversed higher.
  • The FY24/FY25 EPS upgrades proved premature: by Q1FY27 adjusted PAT collapsed ~91% YoY despite the initial earnings-upgrade narrative.
