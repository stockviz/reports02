Hindustan Foods Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Footwear cost pressures: The footwear business experienced temporary cost pressures from higher raw material prices caused by the Middle East crisis, along with the impact of an exceptional wage revision in Haryana. Management stated it proactively secured alternative raw material sources and is in discussions with customers to pass through these cost increases.
  • Commodity inflation and geopolitics: Management noted continued business traction “in spite of the volatile geopolitical situation and the inflationary effect of the increase in the commodity prices.”
  • GST inversion: Steps were taken to defray the effects of GST inversion, which had affected profitability.
  • Silvassa facility disruption: In July, the Silvassa manufacturing facility producing Home & Personal Care products faced a disruption following record rains and severe waterlogging. Production partially resumed, with the facility targeted to be fully operational by end-August. The extent of damage to plant and machinery, inventory and other assets was being assessed; the financial impact could not be ascertained at that stage. Assets are comprehensively covered under insurance, and no material impact on long-term operations was expected.
  • Revenue recognition transition: Certain businesses will continue transitioning their revenue recognition methodology in Q2 and Q3 FY27. This has no impact on absolute profitability, although reported revenues for these businesses will moderate.
  • Regulatory/legal: The Securities Appellate Tribunal, by order dated August 13, 2026, dismissed Appeal No. 178 of 2024 filed by the Company against the BSE order dated December 10, 2023 imposing a fine of Rs. 52,21,500 for violation of SEBI (LODR) Regulations. The appeal was dismissed without cost. The Company stated there is no material impact on financials, operations, or other activities and that it is in the process of contesting the order.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Q1 FY27 performance: Total Income increased 18% to Rs 1,207.0 Crores from Rs 1,022.2 Crores in Q1 FY26; EBITDA increased 26% to Rs 106.3 Crores from Rs 84.3 Crores; PBT before exceptional increased 33% to Rs 56.6 Crores from Rs 42.7 Crores; PAT increased 33% to Rs 42.8 Crores from Rs 32.2 Crores.
  • Expansion investments: The Board authorized additional investments of Rs. 190 Crores towards new expansion projects, bringing the FY27 total to Rs. 340 Crores, in addition to Rs. 150 Crores of projects carried forward from FY26. Allocation: Food & Beverages Rs. 210 Crores (Coimbatore, Mysuru, Goa, Aurangabad & Hyderabad); Ice Cream Rs. 80 Crores (Panipat); Home & Personal Care Rs. 50 Crores (Lucknow).
  • Capacity ramp-up and operating leverage: Management expects recently commissioned capacities to continue ramping up, with consequent improvement in utilisation expected to drive operating leverage and enhance profitability.
  • Project commercialisation: Management expects to commercialise Rs. 500+ crore worth of projects in FY27, positioning the company to sustain earnings growth in FY28.
  • Operational momentum: All businesses delivered growth; continued ramp-up of recently commissioned capacities, new customer additions, product launches, and successful integration of the latest acquisition strengthened the manufacturing platform. Ice Cream and beverages delivered another strong summer season with record production volumes.
  • Acquisition: On April 1, 2026, the Company completed the acquisition of the manufacturing facility of Ultra Beauty Care Private Limited at Aurangabad, Maharashtra for Rs. 21.81 Crores; fair value of net assets acquired was Rs 24.19 Crores and capital reserve Rs. 2.38 Crores (provisional).
  • Business combination schemes: The Scheme for the business combination of the Nashik Manufacturing Unit of Avalon Cosmetics Private Limited and the Scheme for amalgamation of Vanity Case India Private Limited with the Company were approved by the NCLT (Mumbai Bench) on February 25, 2026; certified copies were filed with the Registrar of Companies on March 31, 2026, with the schemes operative from closing of business hours on March 31, 2026 and effective from April 1, 2024 (appointed date). Prior-period results were restated accordingly.
  • Opportunity size: The company continues to witness a large opportunity size across its business verticals, driven by increasing outsourcing opportunities, strong relationships with marquee customers, and a substantial proportion of projects backed by long-term customer contracts. It operates across five business segments with 42 factories in 12 states, using dedicated and shared manufacturing models, with expanding markets in home care, personal care, packaged foods, beverages, ice cream, healthcare, and footwear.
  • Balance sheet strength: Management stated the balance sheet remains strong, providing financial flexibility to support future growth while maintaining disciplined capital allocation.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material price inflation, including Middle East crisis-led increases affecting footwear.
  • Wage revisions (including the exceptional wage revision in Haryana and New Labour Code wage increases effective April 1) affecting costs.
  • Volatile geopolitical situation and commodity price inflation.
  • Weather-related operational disruption, as demonstrated by the Silvassa flooding; the financial impact of the damage was not ascertainable at the time of reporting.
  • Regulatory and legal exposure, as illustrated by the SAT dismissal of the Company’s appeal and the BSE fine of Rs. 52,21,500.
  • Transition in revenue recognition methodology moderating reported revenues in certain businesses in Q2 and Q3 FY27.
  • Dependence on customer pass-through discussions to offset cost increases in the footwear business.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • Guidance: Management reaffirmed FY27 PAT guidance of Rs. 200–220 crore (described as 34–48% growth over FY26). Management also stated it expects EBITDA and PAT growth to be higher than sales growth in the coming quarters, and expects to commercialise Rs. 500+ crore worth of projects in FY27, positioning the company to sustain earnings growth in FY28.
  • Observed performance: In Q1 FY27, Total Income grew 18% year-on-year to Rs 1,207 crore, while EBITDA and PAT increased by 26% and 33% respectively — consistent with the stated expectation that EBITDA and PAT growth would exceed sales growth. PAT of Rs 42.8 Crores was described as the highest-ever quarterly profit. The company also reported a healthy project pipeline, improving asset utilisation, and continued execution supporting its growth trajectory.
  • Context for guidance: The FY27 PAT guidance is supported by the ramp-up of recently commissioned capacity and a healthy new-project pipeline. Management noted the Q1 performance was achieved despite headwinds in the shoe business, and expressed confidence in protecting margins in that business over the coming quarters.

5. Other Key Facts

  • Non-Deal Roadshows: Officials are participating in Non-Deal Roadshows organized by Nuvama Institutional Equities on September 8, 2026, and September 15, 2026, in Mumbai for 1x1 or group meetings starting from 9:00 am onwards. Discussions will be based on publicly available information and no unpublished price sensitive information is intended to be discussed.
  • 41st Annual General Meeting: Scheduled for Wednesday, September 23, 2026, at 11:30 A.M. (IST) through Video Conferencing / Other Audio Visual Means. Key proposals include adoption of financial statements, re-appointment of Mr. Shrinivas V. Dempo as a Director, ratification of cost auditor remuneration (M/s. Poddar & Co. at Rs. 5,25,000/- plus out of pocket expenses and taxes), increasing the borrowing limit up to Rs. 1,300 Crores, and authorizing the board to create/modify charges on assets to secure borrowings.
  • Auditor review: The statutory auditors expressed an unmodified conclusion on both the consolidated and standalone unaudited financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. The consolidated review noted two subsidiaries reviewed by other auditors (total revenues Rs. 121.22 Crores, net loss after tax Rs. 2.16 Crores) and two subsidiaries plus an associate not reviewed by their auditors (total revenues Rs. 35.36 Crores, net loss after tax Rs. 1.21 Crores; associate share of net loss Rs. 0.09 Crores), which management stated were not material to the Group.
  • Subsidiaries and associate: HFL Consumer Products Private Limited, Aero Care Personal Products LLP, HFL Healthcare and Wellness Private Limited (formerly Reckitt Benckiser Scholl India Private Limited), HFL Multiproducts Private Limited, KNS Shoetech Private Limited (all wholly owned subsidiaries), and Asar Green Kabadi Private Limited (associate w.e.f. August 21, 2025).
  • Segment reporting: The Group is predominantly engaged in a single business segment, “Contract Manufacturing,” and no separate segment information is furnished under Ind AS 108.

Broker Narrative

The first report was a bullish Buy built on structural tailwinds—US tariff shifts, footwear import substitution, new segments, and contract manufacturing—with near-term risks dismissed as execution/capex issues. By the last report, the tone had become a cautious Hold, with the narrative dominated by adverse seasonality, elevated agri-input costs, high-cost inventory, margin compression, and intense competitive spending. The persistent theme was FMCG demand softness: initially a risk, later acknowledged as a recovery still weighted to 2H26. The optimism shifted from aggressive expansion-driven growth to gradual volume recovery and cost-relief hopes.

Fears that came true

  • The extended subdued FMCG demand that was flagged as a risk materialized, as the last report still described demand recovery as only ‘green shoots’ and weighted to 2H26, and the first Buy’s +10.4% actual return fell well short of its +23.5% prediction.
  • Execution risk in new segments materialized: the last report cited adverse seasonality specifically hurting ice creams, juices, and aerated drinks, the very categories the first report had flagged as high-growth expansion segments, contributing to margin pressure.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected margin/RoE upside from shared manufacturing and private-label models did not materialize, with the last report citing gross and operating margin contractions from high-cost inventory and pricing lagging input-cost inflation.
  • The expansion into high-growth segments such as ice creams and beverages did not deliver the anticipated boost, as adverse seasonality dragged on those categories in the last report.
