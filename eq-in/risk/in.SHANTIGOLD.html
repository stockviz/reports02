Shanti Gold International — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin dilution from inventory accounting. The Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 9.97% included a benefit of roughly 2% to 2.5% from an unrealized gain locked in finished goods inventory, arising from a change in inventory valuation method from FIFO to WAC. Management stated that excluding this, the sustainable EBITDA margin is around 7.5% to 8%. On a year-over-year basis, EBITDA margin declined 758 bps (from 17.56% in Q1 FY26) and PAT margin declined 469 bps (from 11.73% to 7.05%), even as absolute EBITDA and PAT grew.

Working capital intensity and negative operating cash flows. Management acknowledged that cash flows from operations have been negative over the past four to five years. The stated reason is the business model: the company keeps finished jewellery stock readily available rather than operating order-to-order, so funds are tied up in inventory. Management noted cash flow could turn positive only by shifting away from this model. Working capital needs are expected to rise further with the Marol facility, the planned Dubai office, and the upcoming Jaipur facility.

Gold price volatility and hedging posture. Management described gold as having moved in a narrow band of roughly 142–150 in the current year, with May and June subdued, before rallying after the IIJS exhibition. The company had stepped away from Gold Metal Loans during a period of high volatility and has since re-started GML as prices stabilized. Management flagged the possibility of short-term jitters in gold even while remaining bullish long-term.

Capacity ramp-up and utilization. The existing Mumbai facility is running at approximately 75% utilization. The Marol facility commenced operations only in June and is expected to scale up gradually. The Jaipur facility is still under construction, targeted for mid-November or December, with capex of around INR47 crores.

Dependence on domestic market and limited export base. Exports comprised only about 4% of total revenue in Q1 FY27. The Dubai office is still pending RBI approval, and management linked international expansion partly to stabilization of geopolitical conditions (“once this war and all gets over”).

Other expenses growth. Other expenses rose 132% year-over-year. Management attributed this to indirect expenses required to support the sales growth trajectory, stating there were no hedging losses or unusual items under that head.

Documented structural risks. The supplied material also lists shortage of skilled labour due to generational craftsmanship dependency, inadequate formal training and limited youth engagement; short-lived fashion trends and rapidly evolving global preferences; external dependency for raw materials and exposure to global supply chain risks and foreign market volatility; global economic slowdown reducing export demand in key markets such as the U.S., UAE, and Hong Kong; heightened and fluctuating gold prices intensifying working capital pressures; high dependence on the domestic market; the working capital intensive nature of the business; moderate capacity utilisation; and gold price volatility.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 revenue and volume growth. Revenue from operations was INR716.38 crores versus INR292.78 crores in Q1 FY26, a 144.69% YoY increase, with volume growth of 61.6% YoY. Sequentially, revenue rose 8.72% from INR658.93 crores in Q4 FY26. Growth was attributed to healthy volume expansion, continued customer focus, rollout of new designs, and outreach to new customers and markets.

New Marol manufacturing facility. Operations commenced at the Marol, Andheri facility during the quarter, adding approximately 4,000 kg per annum of capacity. Management described it as a growth enabler that improves production efficiency and positions the company to serve large-scale organized retailers who are moving toward just-in-time inventory practices.

Upcoming Jaipur facility. Capex of around INR47 crores has been allotted, with operations targeted for mid-November or December. The Jaipur site spans 3 acres, with the 50,000 sq. ft. first phase described as well-calibrated for larger expansion if demand exceeds expectations. The facility will produce 22 and 18 karat jewellery, with capability to manufacture 14, 12, or 9 karat if customers demand it.

Product mix enhancement. Management stated approximately 75% of revenue comes from cast-studded/designer jewellery and 25% from plain gold. Designer jewellery, Turkish jewellery, and new product lines are gaining traction and contributing to improved realizations. The company exhibited at a jewellery fair and reported strong demand for studded jewellery and its new line.

Rights issue to fund growth. The Board approved a rights issue of 46,43,471 fully paid-up equity shares of face value INR10 each at INR215 per share, aggregating up to INR99.83 crores. The proceeds are to be invested in gold and working capital. Following allotment approved on August 24, 2026, paid-up equity share capital increased from INR72,09,60,000 to INR76,73,94,710.

Geographic and customer expansion. The company recently entered North India and added new clients there. Management emphasized both deepening wallet share with existing customers and adding new customers across geographies. The Dubai office is expected to increase international footprint once RBI approval is received.

Industry structure. Management cited India’s jewellery market being supported by strong wedding and occasion-led demand, rising disposable incomes, and increasing preference for organized and branded jewellery. Organized retailers are focusing on inventory optimization, wider product offerings, and faster design cycles, creating structural opportunity for scalable manufacturers. The supplied material also cites the structural shift towards outsourced manufacturing by organized jewellers, export expansion into the US, UK, Singapore, and Malaysia supported by the proposed Dubai subsidiary, operating leverage from scale, surging wedding and occasion jewellery demand, expanding middle-class populations, growing adoption of branded and design-led jewellery, and supportive macroeconomic fundamentals in India.

3. Key Risks

  • Gold price volatility affecting demand, inventory valuation, and working capital.
  • Negative operating cash flows persisting under the current stock-and-sell business model.
  • Margin sustainability — the 9.97% Q1 EBITDA margin is not expected to repeat; guidance is 7.5% to 8%.
  • Execution risk on capacity ramp-up at Marol and Jaipur, including achieving utilization levels sufficient for operating leverage.
  • Regulatory and approval risk — the Dubai office awaits RBI approval.
  • Geopolitical and global economic risk affecting export demand in key markets.
  • Competition and the company’s ability to successfully implement its strategy, per the documented risk factors.
  • Dependence on domestic market with exports at only 4% of revenue.
  • Skilled labour availability due to generational craftsmanship dependency and limited formal training.
  • Raw material supply chain and foreign market volatility from external dependency.
  • Debt-equity management — current debt-to-equity is 0.50, with management intending to keep it below 1x while adding both debt and equity.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Revenue growth guidance vs. Q1 performance. Management guided for 50% to 60% value growth and 30% to 40% volume growth for the current year, with a stated revenue target of INR3,500 crores. In Q1 FY27, actual revenue growth was 144.69% YoY and volume growth was 61.6% YoY — both above the full-year guidance ranges. Management characterized the Q1 volume growth of around 60% as above the guided 30% to 40%, and when asked whether 60% could be achieved for the full year, responded that it was “wishful thinking” but that guidance remains 30% to 40% volume and 50% to 60% value, with possible revision if demand strengthens in Q3.

EBITDA margin guidance vs. Q1 performance. Management guided for 7.5% to 8% EBITDA margin for the year, noting that Q1’s 9.97% included a 2% to 2.5% unrealized inventory gain from the FIFO-to-WAC change. Q1 actual EBITDA margin was 9.97%, above the guided range, but management explicitly attributed the excess to the one-time inventory benefit. On a longer horizon, management indicated EBITDA margin could move toward 10% over three to four years as new facilities scale up and fixed expenses are absorbed.

PAT margin. Management referenced a PAT margin of around 4% in response to a question on margin trajectory, while Q1 FY27 actual PAT margin was 7.05%. The 4% figure appears to relate to a forward-looking operating margin context rather than the reported quarterly PAT margin.

Capacity and utilization. Management stated the existing facility is at 75% utilization. The Marol facility commenced in June and is expected to scale gradually. Jaipur is targeted for mid-November or December. No specific utilization guidance was provided for the new facilities beyond gradual ramp-up.

Working capital and funding. Management guided that working capital needs will increase and that the company will use a mix of debt and equity, keeping debt-to-equity below 1x. Current debt-to-equity is 0.50. The rights issue proceeds are to be invested in gold and working capital. Management stated it will “stick with the borrowings what we’ve already taken” following the rights issue.

Export contribution. Exports were 4% of Q1 FY27 revenue. Management did not provide a specific export revenue target but described “huge potential” once the Dubai office is operational and geopolitical conditions stabilize.

Customer additions. Management did not quantify new customer additions for Q1 but stated North India entry is recent and a better calculation will be available by year-end. Management emphasized both existing customer wallet share expansion and new customer acquisition.

Three-to-five-year outlook. Management indicated the 30% to 40% volume and 50% to 60% value growth trajectory should continue for the next three to four years, citing the company’s nascent stage, small market share relative to peers, new facilities, new product lines, and new geographies. No specific numerical multi-year targets were provided beyond the current-year guidance.
