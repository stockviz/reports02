India Shelter Finance Corporation (INDIASHLTR) — Q1FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Disbursement recognition change. During Q1FY27, the company consciously transitioned to recognizing disbursements on cheque realization rather than cheque handover. This one-time accounting change shifted a portion of disbursement recognition across reporting periods and temporarily depressed reported Q1FY27 disbursements, which stood at Rs. 641 crores. Disbursement at clearance for the quarter was Rs. 1,046 crores, described as at par with Q4FY26 and about 37% higher than Q1FY26. Management stated the change has no P&L impact because interest benefit was already being provided to customers on realization. The timing gap between cheque handover and realization varies by product and state (for example, title registration timelines, BT-in acceptance by the new institution), ranging roughly from 10–12 days to 15–25 days.

Asset quality deterioration. Gross Stage 3 rose to 1.5% (from 1.2% in Q1FY26 and Q4FY26), with an increase in early delinquency buckets during the quarter. Management attributed this partly to seasonal factors and stress among smaller-ticket, self-employed customers. Collection efficiency was around 97% for the quarter, with April at approximately 96.2%–96.4%, improving into June. Management noted the sub-7 lakh ticket cohort, about 15%–20% of customers, has seen Stage 3 move from roughly 1.4%–1.45% historically to above 2% today.

Growth moderation. Reported AUM growth of 24% YoY was affected by the disbursement accounting change. Disbursement growth on a like-for-like basis was around 18%, which management acknowledged was lower than peers growing 30%–40%. No branches were added during the quarter.

Funding cost tightness. Management noted tightness in macro funding conditions, with bankers asking for 20–25 basis points higher cost of funds and discussions stretching longer. The expected overall impact on cost of funds is limited to about 10 basis points, with possible impact in H2.

Analyst concerns. Several analysts questioned the divergence from industry trends, the steady rise in 30+ and Stage 2+3 buckets since listing, whether the stress was internal rather than sectoral, and whether the 50 bps credit cost guidance was sustainable given elevated Stage 2+3 and sub-1% ECL provision on the balance sheet.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Macro environment. The Indian economy remains among the fastest-growing major economies, supported by domestic demand, government capex, and improving private investment. Reduced impact of the West Asia crisis, a narrowing monsoon deficit, and RBI’s neutral stance were cited as encouraging.

Structural housing finance opportunity. India’s mortgage penetration remains at approximately 11%–12% of GDP, well below developed and several emerging economies, indicating a long runway for growth.

Technology and AI. AI is being embedded across business, credit, collections, operations, compliance, and marketing. Initiatives include AI-enabled work assistance, AI-assisted collection voice calls, and vernacular voice and chatbots. Management cited benefits in serving customers across North to South India, faster lead response, reduced turnaround times, and operational efficiency. Technology partners were not disclosed.

Distribution expansion. The company operates 307 branches across 15 states and plans to add around 40–45 branches in FY27, with most additions expected in Q2, Q3, and beyond.

Funding position. Borrowing profile is diversified across more than 30 counterparties. NHB funding share is 15%, up 230 bps YoY. Average borrowing tenure exceeds 8 years, co-terminus with behavioural loan tenure. Liquidity was reported at over Rs. 800 crores with undrawn sanctions over Rs. 1,500 crores in the call, while the press release cited liquidity of Rs. 2,423 crores as of June 2026. ALM is positive across all buckets. In June 2026, Rs. 172 crores was drawn from National Housing Bank at 7.3%.

July momentum. July disbursements were around Rs. 400 crores under cheque realization methodology, described as better than the March number. Management indicated Q2 disbursements of around Rs. 1,200 crores are reachable, with Q3 typically stronger than Q2.

Product mix and yields. Portfolio yield was 14.8%, stable QoQ; Q1 disbursement yield was 14.9%. Finance cost was stable at 8.2%, marginal cost of funds stable at 7.9%. Incremental spreads exceed portfolio spreads. The book is 15% variable rate, 35% semi-variable, and 50% fixed. Semi-variable resets begin in Q4FY27; the first tranche was disbursed around January 2024. Fixed-rate products carry about a 50 bps premium over variable. Home loans are about 57% of AUM and disbursements, with LAP at 43%; GNPA is similar across both (home loans ~1.48%, LAP ~1.52%), with LAP average LTV at about 45%.

BT-out improvement. BT-out rate for the quarter was about 4%, down 50 bps YoY, aided by data-driven customer retention.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued stress in the smaller-ticket, self-employed customer cohort, which management acknowledged has been in pain for some time.
  • Seasonal pressure on collections, with April historically weak and Q1 typically affected.
  • Potential further increase in Stage 2 and Stage 3 buckets if resolution does not materialize as expected.
  • Elevated Stage 2+3 relative to historical levels, with ECL provision on the balance sheet remaining sub-1% and PCR for Stage 3 stable at 26%; analysts questioned whether buffers may need to be built.
  • Funding cost pressure from macro tightness, with possible H2 impact, though expected to be limited to about 10 bps.
  • Execution risk on the 25%–30% AUM growth guidance given the higher ask rate for the remaining nine months under the new disbursement recognition method.
  • Dependence on in-house sourcing (99% of processing), with no current plans to diversify into DSA or connector channels.
  • GNPA resolution timelines depend on the SARFAESI process, which takes one to two quarters to show results.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 guidance reiterated: - Branch addition of around 40–45 for the year - Spreads of more than 6% in the medium term - Credit cost of around 40–50 bps - Loan growth of around 25%–30%

Observed performance and management commentary:

  • AUM growth: Q1FY27 gross AUM grew 24% YoY to Rs. 11,284 crores, impacted by the disbursement recognition change. Management expressed confidence in catching up over the remaining nine months and retaining 25%–30% AUM growth guidance. Disbursement growth on a like-for-like basis was around 18%, and management indicated it should cross the 20% mark, which it linked to achieving the AUM target.

  • Spreads: Reported spread was 6.6% in Q1FY27, stable QoQ and up from 6.4% in Q1FY26, consistent with the medium-term guidance of more than 6%.

  • Credit cost: Reported credit cost was stable at 0.5% YoY, within the 40–50 bps guidance. Management stated confidence in maintaining 50 bps for the year and for the current quarter, citing low LGD in the category and management overlay.

  • Asset quality trajectory: Management guided that Stage 3 would stabilize around similar levels at the end of Q2FY27, with recovery from Q3FY27 onwards. It indicated 30+ would be lower than Q1 in Q2, Stage 3 would remain around the same level in Q2, and Stage 2 would remain flattish. July 1+ and 30+ numbers were described as almost the same as before. Management contrasted this with the prior year, when Q1 and Q2 remained constant and Q3 saw a spurt; this time the spurt came in Q1, with Q2 expected to remain muted and Q3 expected to show results.

  • Branch additions: No branches were added in Q1FY27, with most of the 40–45 planned additions expected in Q2, Q3, and beyond. Management noted the branch opening strategy changes each year.

  • Profitability: PAT was Rs. 143 crores, up 20% YoY (23% YoY cited in the call) and 4% QoQ. Excluding the impact of direct assignment transactions accounting, PAT growth for the year was stated as 30%. RoA was 5.8% (described as well above 5%), and RoE was 17.5%, up 30 bps YoY. Net worth stood at Rs. 3,352–3,353 crores.

  • Opex: Opex was 4.0% of gross AUM, down 10 bps YoY; cost-to-income was 36%. About 160 employees were added during the quarter, some into collections and some into head office for AI and tech initiatives, on a base of 4,700–4,800 employees.

  • Dividend: A final dividend of Rs. 10 per equity share (face value Rs. 5) for FY26 was approved by shareholders at the 28th AGM held on July 16, 2026, along with adoption of audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY26 and re-appointment of Mr. Sumir Chadha as director.

Broker Narrative

Broker coverage moved from a near-term neutral stance focused on seasonal asset-quality deterioration (GS3/NS3 up, 30+ DPD at 3.5%) to a confident structural Buy built on Vision 2030, branch expansion, and digital adoption. Persistent themes were ~6% spread discipline, robust AUM/PAT growth, Tier-2/3 affordable-housing focus, and credit costs guided around 40-50bps. The main change was opex: the first report celebrated opex/AUM improving to 4.4% and guiding down 20-25bps annually, while the last report treats elevated cost/assets (~4.6% vs industry ~3%) as a key monitorable risk. Risks also broadened to include geographic concentration, LAP exposure, competition/NIM compression, and MFI stress.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of continued opex/AUM decline did not materialize: the last report shows cost/assets at ~4.6%, above the 4.4% earlier highlighted, and calls elevated operating expenses a key monitorable.
