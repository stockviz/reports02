Viyash Scientific Limited — Q1 FY27 Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

API revenue stagnation in Q1 FY27. API revenue was broadly flat quarter-on-quarter (₹3,833 Mn in Q1 FY27 versus ₹3,836 Mn in Q4 FY26, and up only 4% year-on-year). Management attributed this to raw material price volatility and customer wait-and-watch behaviour during June, when many customers expected geopolitical disruption to end and therefore postponed procurement after prices had been raised. Management characterised this as a timing issue rather than a business loss.

EU growth phasing. Europe formulation revenue grew 13% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 (₹1,847 Mn versus ₹1,640 Mn), but on a constant-currency EUR basis the quarter was described as largely flat (EUR 16.8 million versus EUR 16.8 million in Q1 FY26). Management attributed this to phasing of contracts and tender-linked or vaccination-outbreak-linked business, and noted that some markets such as Bremer and Benelux are lagging while Spain performs strongly. Seasonality means Q3 is typically the strongest quarter.

ESOP cost overhang. Pursuant to the composite scheme of amalgamation, the company granted 1.3 crore employee stock options representing 2.8% of post-amalgamation paid-up share capital, resulting in an incremental expense of ₹19 crores in Q1 FY27 (total ESOP cost of ~₹25 crores for the quarter). Management guided total FY27 ESOP cost of ~₹150 crores, with ~₹40 crores in each of Q2–Q4, falling to ₹25–30 crores in FY28. The charge was elevated because the grant was delayed and the share price was high (₹230) at the time of allotment.

Long gestation on high-potent / complex products. High-potent (oncology) API and formulation revenues are not expected to begin meaningfully until 2029, with larger formulation revenues after 2030, because development cycles are 5–7 years and depend on patent expiries. Management stated that 50%+ of the pipeline is first-to-file or first-to-launch, but that the majority of new-product revenue comes only from FY29 onward.

Minority interest drag. Minority interests in the US and Spain operations represent approximately 16–17% of profit. Management intends to acquire these minorities within 1–2 years but has not yet done so.

Historical acquisition risk in Europe. A participant referenced the prior Alivira/SeQuent European acquisition experience, where purchase prices looked cheap but structural, local-market, restructuring and insurance issues proved costly. Management acknowledged this history and stated that the Bio For Life approach is different — no manufacturing, brand-led, and intended as a launchpad — and that Indian companies operating in Europe have performed better in recent years.

Governance-related dissent at the AGM. At the 41st AGM held on August 11, 2026, the resolution adopting a new set of Articles of Association passed with 97.99% of votes in favour, but 30.79% of Public Institutions voted against (6,372,332 votes against out of 20,696,008 polled). The re-appointment of Dr. Haribabu Bodepudi also saw 2.03% of Public Institutions vote against. All resolutions were passed with the requisite majority.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue from operations was ₹9,464 Mn, up 19.5% year-on-year and 2.9% sequentially. Adjusted EBITDA was ₹2,047 Mn, up 59.2% year-on-year, with EBITDA margin at 21.6% (up ~530 bps year-on-year). PAT was ₹793 Mn, up 114.9% year-on-year. Gross margin improved to 54.1% from 51.9%.

Balance sheet deleveraging. Net debt fell to ₹861 Mn, with net debt to LTM EBITDA at 0.1x, versus 0.2x in Q4 FY26 and ~1.0x a year earlier. Finance costs declined to ₹125 Mn from ₹204 Mn in Q1 FY26. Management described the balance sheet as the strongest in the company’s history, providing capacity for brownfield expansion and selective inorganic opportunities.

Segment momentum. Formulations grew 33% year-on-year to ₹5,547 Mn. Europe grew 13%, Emerging Markets 36%, India 63%, and USA 60%. Emerging market volume growth was ~25%, driven by Turkey, Brazil and Mexico. The US business improved through a strategy of moving volume products to India, full backward integration with API, and new product launches, with 50–55% market share maintained on a key product.

Bio For Life acquisition in Italy. The company signed an SPA on July 21, 2026 for the acquisition of Bio For Life Italia S.r.l. by Alivira Animal Health Limited, Ireland (a step-down wholly owned subsidiary). Requisite approval under applicable Italian FDI / Golden Power regulations has been received from the relevant Italian authorities. The transaction is expected to close within two months from the date of intimation, subject to remaining customary closing formalities and conditions. Bio For Life provides direct market access in Italy (one of Europe’s largest companion animal markets), a companion animal portfolio of 85–90 products extendable to other core markets, a salesforce with ~85% vet clinic coverage in Italy, and strong local talent.

Companion animal strategy. Companion animal is identified as the number one strategic growth area for the next five years, combining the Bio For Life acquisition, the BI partnership in India, and own R&D and manufacturing investments. Management targets $150–200 million in companion animal revenue by 2032.

Capacity and regulatory progress. The Vizag site received US approval and capacity expansion for Albendazole commenced commercialisation from July. During the quarter: 4 facility inspections, 4 regulatory audits, 66 customer audits; 3 API regulatory approvals (USDMF-1, EDMF-1, CEP-1) and 2 FDF-US approvals; 6 API launches and 4 FDF launches; 8 API filings and 2 FDF-US filings; 15 API and 4 FDF validations completed. R&D pipeline stands at 85+ APIs, 24+ Human FDFs and 81+ Animal FDFs.

New initiatives. The Board approved incorporation of a wholly owned step-down subsidiary in Vietnam (Alivira Vietnam Company Limited) for importation, registration, marketing, sale and distribution of animal health products, APIs and phytogenic solutions, with investment of up to USD 400,000. The Board also approved a rights issue subscription in Alivira Animal Health Limited (India) for up to ₹400,02,15,648, to be adjusted against intercompany loans, intended to strengthen AAHL’s capital structure and reduce interest burden, with completion targeted by August 30, 2026. AAHL’s turnover for FY2025-26 was ₹4,494.53 Mn.

Long-term aspiration. Management reiterated a $1 billion revenue aspiration by 2032, which it described as working out to ~18% CAGR from FY27 numbers and as a comfortable, practical aspiration supported by organic and inorganic plans.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical and raw material volatility affecting API customer procurement behaviour and pricing, as seen in June.
  • Currency and phasing risk in Europe, where quarterly EUR revenue can be flat even when full-year growth is guided at 18–20% on an INR basis.
  • Execution risk on complex/high-potent products, where revenues depend on patent expiries and regulatory filings extending to 2029–2030 and beyond.
  • Acquisition integration risk, particularly given the company’s own historical experience with European acquisitions and the broader track record of Indian companies in Europe.
  • Regulatory and compliance risk across 16 manufacturing facilities, multiple geographies and 100+ countries, with management stating compliance and governance are non-negotiable.
  • Dependence on minority-owned entities in the US and Spain for a meaningful share of profit until buybacks are completed.
  • Capital allocation risk in long-gestation R&D and M&A, though management stated that ~90% of the business is mature, capex of ₹250–300 crores per year is modest relative to company size, and diversification across products and geographies mitigates concentration risk.
  • ESOP dilution and cost, with 10,30,775 equity shares allotted on August 11, 2026 at ₹101 per share, increasing paid-up capital from ₹87,77,53,626 to ₹87,98,15,176.
