PARSVNATH — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Financial reporting has stalled. On August 13, 2026, the company informed the NSE and BSE that its annual Financial Statements (Standalone & Consolidated) for the quarter and financial year ended March 31, 2026 had still not been finalized “due to unavoidable circumstances.” As a direct consequence, the financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 could not be approved or published either. The company stated it was “sorting out all the problems” and would inform the exchanges “shortly” about meetings to approve both sets of results. The company is described in its own filings as a “Company under CIRP,” and the filings are signed by a Resolution Professional.

Supreme Court action over the Parsvnath Exotica project. A disclosure dated July 14, 2026 reported an order of the Supreme Court of India dated July 13, 2026, passed while hearing writ petitions concerning possession of flats and compensation at the Parsvnath Exotica project in Sector-53, Gurugram. The order:

  • Froze the bank accounts of Parsvnath Developers Limited (Respondent No. 3), Parsvnath Hessa Developers Private Limited (Respondent No. 2, a wholly owned subsidiary), and the personal accounts of the Managing Directors/Directors/Officers of both, until further orders.
  • Issued bailable warrants of Rs. 25,000 each against Respondent Nos. 2 and 3, their Directors and officers, to secure their personal presence before the Court on July 20, 2026, with a warning that non-appearance would lead to non-bailable warrants.
  • Stayed the creation of third-party rights, delivery of possession to third parties, or any transaction pertaining to immovable assets of Respondent Nos. 2 and 3, until further orders.

The Court’s findings on the project’s history. The order records that the writ petitioners were provisionally allotted units in 2006, with Flat Buyer Agreements in early 2007, at a total sale consideration of approximately Rs. 1.78 crores per flat. Possession was due in February 2013 under Clause 10(a) of the agreements. The petitioners paid the entire consideration between 2006 and 2014, but found construction “not even near completion.” HRERA orders dated November 26, 2021 directed compensation at 9.3% per annum for every month of delay; these were not challenged and attained finality. Execution proceedings from 2022 produced Recovery Certificates of Rs. 1.81 crores, but the order records that “not a single penny could be recovered” through the Collector, Gurugram, from June 2023 to April 2024. The Court observed that the builders had prima facie displayed a “brazen disregard” for HRERA’s authority, and that State authorities appeared to be “either colluding with the builder-cum-developer or have failed to discharge their official responsibilities.”

A related legal uncertainty. The order notes that the Punjab and Haryana High Court, by judgment dated April 24, 2025, had reportedly struck down the government notification empowering HRERA to issue recovery certificates, and that this judgment is under challenge before the Supreme Court in SLP(C) No. 19880/2025. The Court nonetheless held that the original HRERA orders in favour of the writ petitioners remain intact.

The only forward-looking statements are procedural: the company’s August 13, 2026 letter states that it is “sorting out all the problems” and will “shortly inform the exchange” about holding meetings to approve the annual Financial Statements for the year ended March 31, 2026 and the results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. No timeline or outcome is committed to.

3. Key Risks

  • Asset and account restrictions: The Supreme Court’s freeze on bank accounts of the company and its wholly owned subsidiary, and on personal accounts of their directors and officers, together with the stay on transactions involving their immovable assets, restricts financial and operational flexibility until further orders.
  • Contempt and enforcement escalation: Bailable warrants were issued for the July 20, 2026 hearing, with the express warning that non-appearance would lead to non-bailable warrants.
  • Regulatory non-compliance: The unresolved March 31, 2026 annual Financial Statements have caused a cascading delay into the June 30, 2026 quarterly results, leaving the company unable to meet its SEBI (LODR) Regulation 33 obligations.
  • Insolvency context: The company signs its filings as a “Company under CIRP,” with a Resolution Professional as signatory.
  • Adverse judicial observations: The Supreme Court’s record includes findings of disregarded final orders, frustrated execution proceedings, ignored Recovery Certificates, and evasion of arrest, as well as criticism of State authorities’ conduct.
  • Legal uncertainty around recovery powers: The validity of HRERA’s recovery-certificate mechanism is itself under challenge before the Supreme Court, even as the Court affirmed that the underlying HRERA orders remain intact.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

The company’s August 13, 2026 letter states that it is “sorting out all the problems” and will “shortly inform the exchange” regarding meetings to approve the pending financial statements. This follows the earlier position, referenced in the same letter, that the March 31, 2026 statements were not finalized “due to unavoidable circumstances as informed earlier.” The observable record shows that the March 31, 2026 annual results remained unapproved as of August 13, 2026, and that this delay had already propagated to the June 30, 2026 quarterly results. The company has not indicated a date for the approval meetings.

On the project side, the Supreme Court order records that possession at Parsvnath Exotica was contractually due in February 2013, that HRERA ordered compensation on November 26, 2021, and that as of the July 13, 2026 order neither possession had been handed over nor amounts paid, with recovery efforts through the Collector yielding nothing between June 2023 and April 2024. The Court further recorded that arrest warrants issued against directors had not been complied with and that in one case a bailiff was not allowed to enter the company’s premises on several occasions.
