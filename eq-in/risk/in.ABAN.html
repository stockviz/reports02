Aban Offshore Limited — Analysis of FY2025-26 Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Insolvency proceedings and loss of board control. The NCLT Chennai Bench admitted an insolvency petition filed by Punjab National Bank (a financial creditor) under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, and by order dated 1 September 2025 initiated Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against the company. The NCLT appointed Mr. Shailesh Bhalchandran Desai as Interim Resolution Professional by order dated 10 October 2025 (order received 14 October 2025), and a moratorium was declared. The Committee of Creditors at its first meeting on 26 November 2025 appointed the IRP as Resolution Professional. The powers of the Board of Directors have been suspended since 1 September 2025, and management and operations are vested in the RP. The RP has stated that he signed the financial results in good faith, relied on certifications and representations of the erstwhile management, and makes no representation regarding accuracy, veracity or completeness of the data.

Eroded net worth and negative working capital. The company’s accumulated losses amount to ₹9,744.68 million, resulting in negative net worth of ₹9,627.95 million, and current liabilities exceeded current assets by ₹11,697.79 million. The consolidated picture is more severe, with negative net worth of ₹282,407.24 million.

Payment defaults and unredeemed preference shares. The company has defaulted on instalments and interest payments on term loans. It has not redeemed non-convertible redeemable preference shares amounting to ₹2,630 million on due dates and has paid no dividend. The Annexure discloses that preference shares due on 29 December 2014, 28 February 2015, 30 March 2015, 16 June 2015, 16 June 2016 and 3 August 2016 have not been redeemed, and that dividend payments after 31 March 2015 have been deferred. The credit rating on the preference shares is ‘CARE D (RPS)’.

Audit disclaimer and unverifiable balances. The statutory auditors issued a disclaimer of opinion on both standalone and consolidated results. They could not obtain bank balance confirmations for bank balances including deposits of ₹592.98 million and outstanding bank borrowings from 2 banks (term loans) of ₹4,148.03 million as at 31 March 2026, and found no practicable alternative procedures. The auditors noted that the standalone report for the quarter ended 30 June 2025 and quarter/six months ended 30 September 2025 was qualified, and the quarter/nine months ended 31 December 2025 also had a disclaimer of conclusion.

Accounting adjustments reflecting CIRP crystallisation. Based on claims received by the RP from financial creditors, the company did not provide for interest on bank borrowings and dividend on cumulative redeemable preference shares for October 2025 to March 2026, since liabilities are crystallised on the CIRP commencement date. It did not reinstate foreign currency bank borrowings as at 31 March 2026. Interest income on an inter-corporate loan was accrued at a lower rate reset from 1 October 2025, and revenue from support services from the wholly owned foreign subsidiary was accrued at less than the agreed rate from 1 October 2025. No CSR provision of ₹4.62 million was considered. Land of ₹123.45 million was transferred from ‘Assets held for sale’ back to ‘Property Plant and Equipment’ because no proceedings can be initiated during the moratorium.

Subsidiary and receivable concerns. Advances to wholly owned subsidiary Aban Energies Limited were fully impaired (₹146.58 million provision for impairment of advances in the standalone results) because the subsidiary has not generated revenue for the past few years. Investments of ₹94.34 million in non-cumulative redeemable preference shares are overdue, though management believes they will be recovered.

Foreign subsidiary distress. The Singapore-based wholly owned subsidiary Aban Holdings Pte Ltd and its subsidiaries received a disclaimer of opinion from their auditor, CLA Global TS Public Accounting Corporation. That auditor reported group net losses of US$128,629,000 and company net losses of US$115,073,000 for FY2026, net current liabilities of US$3,085,749,000 (group) and US$2,887,078,000 (company), and net liabilities of US$2,989,749,000 (group) and US$2,241,120,000 (company). The group and company defaulted on borrowings, breached covenants, and received recall notices from lenders. The group sub-leases offshore drilling rigs from Aban Offshore, and potential repossession of these rigs following CIRP creates material uncertainty, as the group may be unable to fulfil drilling contracts or could face liquidated damages. The auditor also could not verify bank balances of US$1,164,000, borrowings of US$1,761,279,000 (group) and US$1,641,198,000 (company), investments in subsidiaries and associates, amounts due from subsidiaries, VAT/withholding tax receivables of US$22,201,000, or CGST payables of US$1,306,000.

Confidentiality constraints. Minutes of Committee of Creditors meetings and outcomes of certain CIRP procedures are confidential and could not be shared except with CoC members and the NCLT, though stock exchanges were informed of CoC meeting convenings.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Going-concern basis under IBC. The RP manages the company on a going-concern basis as per IBC provisions, with the objective of maximising value. The company notes that under CIRP, business operations are managed under RP supervision in accordance with the IBC’s objective to ensure going concern is continued.

Resolution process progress. The RP has begun receiving claims from financial creditors, operational creditors, other creditors and employees. As per the last update on 24 December 2025, claims were submitted to the RP, and overall liabilities will be determined during CIRP, with accounting impact considered on completion. The list of creditors as on 12 November 2025 was submitted in compliance with the Code. On 21 January 2026, the Appellate Tribunal issued further procedural directions, and the matter is scheduled for further hearing.

One-time settlement proposal. The group submitted a proposal for a one-time settlement of bank borrowings to lenders in FY2024. As at 31 March 2026, lenders were still seeking approval from relevant authorities for the proposal.

Operational continuity. The company operates in a single business segment, Offshore Drilling. Standalone income from operations for the year ended 31 March 2026 was ₹1,364 million (versus ₹1,245.49 million in FY2025), and consolidated income from operations was ₹4,086.17 million (versus ₹4,756.50 million). The group continues to sub-lease offshore drilling rigs to non-related party clients.

Asset recovery expectations. Management believes overdue preference share investments of ₹94.34 million will be recovered. The company set off the Axis Bank liability and recoverable amount in its books following the RP’s direction, related to ₹180 million of preference dues previously utilised by Axis Bank without consent.

3. Key Risks

Going-concern risk. The auditors state that material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the company’s ability to continue as a going concern. The appropriateness of the going-concern assumption depends on improvement in cash flows from normal operations, timely monetisation of assets, and ongoing CIRP processes. The standalone financial statements have been prepared on a going-concern basis although material uncertainty exists, dependent on successful implementation of a resolution plan as and when approved by NCLT.

Resolution-plan dependency. The future of the company depends on successful implementation of a resolution plan. If the group and company are unable to continue in operational existence, they may be unable to discharge liabilities in the normal course, and adjustments may be required to reflect asset realisations outside the normal course at amounts that could differ significantly from recorded values, including reclassification of non-current assets and liabilities as current.

Rig repossession and contract risk. Following CIRP commencement, potential repossession of rigs sub-leased by the group creates material uncertainty, as the group may be unable to fulfil drilling contracts or could face liquidated damages linked to the holding corporation’s insolvency. The group’s rig with carrying amount of US$28,724,000 is pledged as security for group and company borrowings.

Lender recall rights. The group and company defaulted on borrowings and breached covenants, giving lenders the right to demand immediate repayment; lenders have issued recall notices. Borrowings with original repayment terms beyond 12 months were reclassified as current liabilities.

Unverifiable financial information. The auditors could not obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence on multiple matters, including bank confirmations, going concern, investment recoverability, and tax balances. The Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications records that adjusted figures for total expenditure, net profit/loss, EPS, total assets, total liabilities and net worth are “Not ascertainable” because the auditors did not quantify the impact.

Claim determination uncertainty. Overall liabilities will be determined during CIRP, and accounting impact, if any, will be considered on completion of CIRP.

Regulatory and legal proceedings. The RP has stated that no suit, prosecution or other legal proceedings shall lie against him in terms of Section 23 of the Code, and that he makes no representations regarding the data in the financial results.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Going concern. Management prepared the standalone and consolidated financial statements on a going-concern basis, citing the IBC requirement that the company be managed as a going concern during CIRP and the RP’s supervision. The auditors, however, stated they were unable to obtain sufficient audit evidence to form an opinion on whether the going-concern basis is appropriate, citing accumulated losses, eroded net worth, negative working capital, payment defaults, and CIRP.

Recoverability of overdue investments. Management is of the opinion that overdue preference share investments of ₹94.34 million will be recovered. The auditors listed this as a basis for disclaimer, noting the investments are overdue.

One-time settlement. The group submitted a one-time settlement proposal to lenders in FY2024. As at 31 March 2026, lenders were still seeking approval from relevant authorities, indicating the proposal had not been implemented.

Interest and dividend provisioning. Management did not provide for interest on bank borrowings and dividend on cumulative redeemable preference shares for October 2025 to March 2026, and did not reinstate foreign currency bank borrowings, based on claims received by the RP and crystallisation on the CIRP commencement date. The auditors included these as bases for disclaimer.

Revenue and interest accruals. Management accrued interest income on an inter-corporate loan at a lower reset rate and support-services revenue from the foreign subsidiary at less than the agreed rate, both from 1 October 2025. The auditors cited both as bases for disclaimer.

Observed performance versus prior year. Standalone total income for FY2026 was ₹1,285.74 million versus ₹3,095.46 million in FY2025. Standalone net profit for the year was ₹316.83 million versus ₹1,678.20 million in FY2025. Consolidated total income was ₹4,927.41 million versus ₹6,197.30 million (as presented) in FY2025, and consolidated net loss after non-controlling interests and share of associate was ₹5,288.35 million versus ₹8,893.50 million in FY2025. Consolidated loss before tax was ₹5,104.75 million versus ₹8,643.78 million in FY2025. Standalone cash and cash equivalents fell to ₹13.62 million at 31 March 2026 from ₹118.20 million at 31 March 2025; consolidated cash and cash equivalents fell to ₹678.54 million from ₹1,918.96 million.

Debt service and interest coverage. The Annexure discloses standalone interest service coverage ratio of 2.18 and consolidated of 0.17. Debt service coverage ratio is shown as negative for both standalone and consolidated, with the note that it is not applicable as there is no repayment of principal and interest. Debt-equity ratio is shown as negative for both.
