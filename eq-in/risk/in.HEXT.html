Headwinds and Challenges

Currency and other income drag. Other income swung to a negative INR 501 million in Q2CY26 (versus positive INR 13 million in Q2CY25), driven by exchange rate losses of INR 783 million in the quarter and INR 1,028 million for H1CY26. This weighed on profit before tax, which declined 6.8% QoQ and 5.9% YoY to INR 4,407 million, and on reported profit, which fell 6.1% QoQ and 13.0% YoY to INR 3,302 million.

Margin pressure year-on-year. Although EBIT margin improved 68 bps QoQ to 13.6% in Q2CY26, it declined 102 bps YoY. Reported profit margin fell to 8.6% from 11.6% a year earlier.

Vertical softness. Travel and Transportation revenue declined 1.0% QoQ and 14.5% YoY in USD terms; Technology, Products & Platforms declined 3.1% QoQ; Professional Services declined 3.4% YoY despite 13.3% QoQ growth.

Working capital and cash outflow. Trade receivables and unbilled revenue rose to INR 32,845 million at June 30, 2026 from INR 25,431 million at December 31, 2025. Net cash outflow was INR 3,794 million in H1CY26, and cash and cash equivalents fell to INR 16,408 million from INR 19,708 million at December 31, 2025. DSO (billed plus unbilled) stood at 78 days.

Litigation and receivables. A European customer disputed USD 9.1 million (INR 782 million) payable to the Group; mediation failed, the Group filed recovery proceedings in the US District Court for the Southern District of New York, and a Berlin court placed the customer under preliminary insolvency administration. The Group took a charge for the receivable in Q2CY25. Separately, Natsoft Corporation and Updraft LLC filed a patent infringement and breach of contract notice claiming USD 500 million; the US District Court for the Northern District of Illinois dismissed the federal patent claims, declined jurisdiction over related state law claims, and granted the plaintiffs leave to file an amended complaint.

Labour Codes impact. The four Labour Codes notified on November 21, 2025 led to restructured compensation effective April 1, 2026, with an incremental impact of INR 818 million in additional gratuity provision and INR 293 million for compensated absences at the consolidated level, presented as an exceptional item for the year ended December 31, 2025.

Leadership transition. Srikrishna Ramakarthikeyan (Keech) is stepping down as CEO and board member effective October 28, 2026, after 12 years; Vivek Jetley is CEO Designate and assumes the CEO role on that date. The company is also seeking approval for an amendment to the incentive payment agreement with Mr. Srikrishna in connection with his cessation of employment.

Industry conditions. Management described the quarter as “challenging” for the industry.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Revenue growth. Q2CY26 revenue was USD 405.4 million, up 4.4% QoQ and 6.1% YoY (6.3% YoY in constant currency); in INR terms revenue rose 6.4% QoQ and 17.9% YoY to INR 38,452 million. H1CY26 revenue was INR 74,582 million versus INR 64,686 million a year earlier.

EBIT growth. Reported EBIT rose 9.8% QoQ and 43.9% YoY in USD terms to USD 55.3 million, with margin at 13.6%.

Vertical and geographic momentum. Healthcare and Insurance grew 6.8% QoQ and 18.9% YoY; Manufacturing and Consumer grew 3.5% QoQ and 17.9% YoY; Banking grew 3.9% QoQ and 10.8% YoY; Financial Services grew 1.1% QoQ and 1.4% YoY. By geography, Asia Pacific grew 14.1% QoQ and 24.8% YoY, Europe 7.0% QoQ and 10.9% YoY, and Americas 2.9% QoQ and 3.5% YoY.

Client metrics. The company added one customer in the USD 20 million+ category (LTM basis), taking the total to 16 from 15 in the previous quarter. Top 10 customer concentration was 35.7% (LTM basis).

AI-led positioning. Management stated AI Labs are launching one new offering every month, forming the foundation for sustained growth. The company describes itself as having AI at its core and serving 30+ Fortune 500 organizations. The company is entering its next phase of accelerated growth with AI reshaping enterprise technology services globally and creating new sources of client demand, with opportunities to scale AI-led services, deepen enterprise relationships, and build Data and AI-led growth platforms.

Key wins. Deals cited include AI for Business with a German biotechnology company, a Zero License engagement with a capital markets institution, an AI-led middle-office transformation with an American financial services company, legacy modernization with a UK music copyright organization, outsourcing and transformation with a London-based public university, a Digital ITO deal with an ANZ non-bank lender, an outsourcing and transformation deal with an APAC fintech, and a CRM transformation program with a global clinical research company.

Acquisition. On May 28, 2026, Hexaware Technologies UK Limited completed the acquisition of 100% of Consulting Professionals Services Holdings Limited and its wholly owned subsidiary CPS for an upfront GBP 6 million plus contingent consideration of up to GBP 5 million, adding a specialist technology consulting firm serving a FTSE 100 client.

Cash conversion. LTM Q2CY26 operating cash flow to reported profit was 124.7%.

ERP milestone. The quarter was the first with the ERP go-live, which management described as a major milestone in its transformation journey.

AI Day. The company held Hexaware AI Day 2026 on August 21, 2026 in Chennai for analysts and investors.

Key Risks

  • Exchange rate volatility, evidenced by the swing in other income and the INR 1,028 million H1CY26 exchange loss.
  • Customer credit and insolvency risk, as illustrated by the disputed European customer receivable and the customer’s preliminary insolvency administration.
  • Litigation risk from the Natsoft/Updraft patent and contract claims, where the plaintiffs have been granted leave to file an amended complaint.
  • Client concentration, with top 10 customers at 35.7% of revenue (LTM basis).
  • Integration and contingent consideration risk from acquisitions, including earnout remeasurement and the CPS acquisition.
  • Regulatory cost risk from the new Labour Codes.
  • Leadership transition risk associated with the CEO change effective October 28, 2026.
  • The company’s own forward-looking statement disclosure cites risks including fluctuations in earnings, ability to manage growth, intense competition, wage increases, ability to attract and retain skilled professionals, time and cost overruns on fixed-price contracts, client concentration, work visa and immigration restrictions, international operations management, tariffs, reduced demand for technology, telecom disruptions, technological disruptions such as Generative AI, acquisition integration, liability for damages on service contracts, success of products and platforms in which Hexaware has made strategic investments, withdrawal of government fiscal incentives, political instability, legal restrictions on raising capital or acquiring companies, pending litigation outcomes, unauthorized use of intellectual property, and general economic conditions.
  • Unclaimed dividends and shares are subject to transfer to the IEPF if not claimed by November 23, 2026.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

CEO commentary versus results. R. Srikrishna, CEO, stated the company “delivered strong growth in what remained a challenging quarter for the industry” and that AI Labs are “innovating at remarkable speed, launching one new offering every month,” forming “the foundation for sustained growth in the AI world.” Observed performance: revenue grew 6.1% YoY in USD and 6.3% in constant currency, with EBIT up 43.9% YoY in USD terms, while reported profit declined 13.0% YoY and profit before tax declined 5.9% YoY.

CFO commentary versus results. Vikash Jain, CFO, stated it was “our first quarter with the ERP go-live which marks a major milestone in our transformation journey,” and that the company “delivered industry-leading revenue growth coupled with margin expansion and solid cash conversion.” Observed performance: revenue grew 4.4% QoQ and 6.1% YoY; EBIT margin expanded 68 bps QoQ to 13.6% but contracted 102 bps YoY; LTM Q2CY26 OCF to reported profit was 124.7%, while H1CY26 net cash generated from operating activities was INR 3,928 million versus INR 4,565 million in H1CY25, and net cash outflow was INR 3,794 million.

Leadership transition guidance. Vivek Jetley will assume the role of CEO on October 28, 2026, with a focus on accelerating growth and scaling Hexaware’s AI-led services model globally; Srikrishna Ramakarthikeyan will remain as Senior Advisor to ensure a smooth transition.

Investor conference. The company is participating in the J.P. Morgan 10th Annual India Conference in Mumbai on September 21, 2026, involving physical one-to-one and group meetings; the schedule is subject to change due to exigencies on the part of investors or the company.

Dividend. The Board declared an interim dividend of INR 8.50 per equity share on April 27, 2026, paid on May 15, 2026 (standalone note states paid in May 2026).

Broker Narrative

Early reports framed HEXT through structural risks—margin pressure, talent shortages, and in-house center adoption—against optimistic tailwinds like cloud adoption and AI. Later reports dropped all headwinds entirely and narrowed the narrative to AI-specific growth drivers such as the ‘Zero Friction Enterprise’ framework and sectoral expansion. The shift reflects growing broker confidence, though several early optimism themes never reappeared in the tailwinds list.

Fears that came true

  • Pricing and margin pressures flagged in 2024 correlate with multiple negative actual returns, including the 2025-12-16 report’s -25.6% outcome.
  • Strong adoption of in-house centers likely contributed to underperformance as clients shifted away from outsourced models, seen in the 2025-12-16 NEUTRAL outcome.

Optimism that failed

  • Accelerated adoption of cloud post-pandemic never reappeared in later tailwinds, and returns turned negative after the initial 2024 report.
  • Evolving partner ecosystem was dropped from the tailwinds list entirely by the last report, suggesting it failed to drive growth.
