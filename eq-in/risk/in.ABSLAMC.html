ABSLAMC: 32nd AGM Summary

Headwinds and Challenges

The most visible challenge in the AGM material is shareholder dissent on Resolution 6, the special resolution re-appointing Mr. Ramesh Abhishek as an Independent Director for a second five-year term from January 1, 2027. While the resolution passed with the requisite majority, 10.8670% of votes polled were against it. The dissent was concentrated among public institutions, where 66.8179% of votes polled were against the resolution, against 33.1821% in favour. Public non-institutions voted 95.8259% in favour. The promoter and promoter group voted 100% in favour. This is the only resolution where a majority of a shareholder class voted against the board’s proposal.

Resolution 3, the re-appointment of Mr. Sandeep Asthana as a director retiring by rotation, also attracted measurable dissent: 0.2312% of total votes polled were against, with public institutions recording 1.4203% against. Resolutions 4 and 5 saw smaller against-votes of 0.2266% and 0.1351% respectively, again concentrated in public institutions.

The AGM was held through video conference and other audio-visual means, with no shareholders present in person or by proxy. Only 1 promoter/promoter group shareholder and 75 public shareholders attended through video conferencing, against 2,40,269 shareholders on the record date of July 22, 2026. Remote e-voting ran from July 25 to July 28, 2026, and e-voting during the AGM was open for 15 minutes before the meeting concluded at 12:18 p.m.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

All six resolutions placed before the meeting were approved with the requisite majority. The Scrutinizer’s report records no invalid votes.

Resolution 1, adopting the audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for the year ended March 31, 2026, passed with 99.9999% of votes polled in favour and 89.2589% of outstanding shares voted. Resolution 2, declaring a dividend of ₹25.50 per equity share of ₹5 each for FY2025-26, passed with 99.9999% in favour and 89.2886% of outstanding shares voted. Promoter and promoter group participation was near-total at 99.9999% of their holding on both resolutions.

The board composition was refreshed and renewed through the AGM. Mr. Sushil Agarwal was appointed as a Non-Executive Director (Resolution 4, passed with 99.7734% in favour). Mr. Sunder Rajan Raman was re-appointed as an Independent Director for a second five-year term from January 1, 2027 (Resolution 5, passed with 99.8649% in favour). Mr. Ramesh Abhishek was re-appointed as an Independent Director for a second five-year term from January 1, 2027 (Resolution 6, passed with 89.1330% in favour). Mr. Sandeep Asthana was re-appointed as a director retiring by rotation (Resolution 3, passed with 99.7688% in favour).

The statutory auditors’ report and secretarial auditors’ report contained no qualifications or adverse comments on the financial transactions and did not highlight any matters having an adverse effect on the functioning of the company, as stated at the AGM.

Key Risks

The concentration of promoter and promoter group voting is a structural feature: the promoter and promoter group held 21,60,73,654 shares and voted 99.9999% of that holding on every resolution, with 100% in favour on all six. On Resolution 6, promoter support of 21,60,73,334 votes in favour was decisive in offsetting the 2,80,54,547 public institution votes against. This means the outcome on the independent director re-appointment turned on promoter votes rather than broad public shareholder endorsement.

Public institution participation varied by resolution. On Resolutions 1 and 2, public institutions voted 100% in favour. On Resolutions 3, 4, 5 and 6, public institutions recorded against-votes of 5,96,326; 5,84,639; 3,48,281; and 2,80,54,547 respectively. The sharp increase on Resolution 6 indicates that a substantial portion of institutional shareholders opposed the re-appointment, even though the resolution carried.

Overall voting participation was 89.2589% to 89.2886% of outstanding shares across resolutions, leaving roughly 10.7% of outstanding shares unvoted. Public non-institution participation was very low at 0.8565% of their holding on most resolutions, and 0.8562% on Resolution 6.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from a cautious Neutral in 2023, worried about SIP decay, yield/mix pressure, and expense drag, to a Buy in 2026 that acknowledged persistent cost and SIP-flow issues but was driven by diversification into alternatives/PMS, passive/ETF growth, higher other income, and improving fund performance. The core fears around SIP traction and operating leverage persisted, while the optimism shifted from core MF revenue growth to non-core and fee-income optionality.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning on worsening SIP traction materialized: by Jun’26 monthly SIP flows declined 10% QoQ to INR10.9b, SIP accounts fell to ~4.03m, and SIP market share moderated alongside higher industry cancellations.
  • The flagged margin/expense pressure materialized: total opex rose 13% YoY, employee costs climbed 26% YoY on ESOP, cost-to-income rose to 44.4%, and EBITDA declined 3% YoY, contributing to the last report’s -0.4% actual return versus +23.7% predicted.

Optimism that failed

  • The optimism on healthy gross SIP inflows failed to sustain, as monthly SIP flows declined 10% QoQ and SIP market share weakened by Jun’26.
  • The successful debt product migration optimism proved short-lived, with debt QAAUM declining 5% YoY and duration-oriented debt funds seeing temporary outflows due to interest-rate volatility.
