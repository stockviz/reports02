Headwinds and Challenges

Cane availability in Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh. Management described cane availability in these two states as a concern, with the industry suffering as farmers shift to more lucrative crops. Crushing in Tamil Nadu during the quarter was 1.47 lakh metric tons against 2.12 LMT in the prior-year quarter, and gross recovery slipped to 7.95% from 8.02%. Cane landed cost rose to Rs. 4,031 per MT from Rs. 3,844 per MT, driven mainly by the higher FRP of about Rs. 3,550. For the current year, management expects flat to about a 5% drop in crush in these geographies. A structural challenge cited was the rampant shift toward paddy in Tamil Nadu over the last 6-7 years, since paddy is fully mechanized and allows three planting cycles.

Lower realizations across sugar, ethanol and power-linked segments. Average sugar selling price was Rs. 40.02 against Rs. 40.97 year-on-year. Distillery average realization was Rs. 63.49 per litre against Rs. 67.59 per litre. Distillery production fell to 351 lakh litres from 412 LL, and sales to 380 LL from 413 LL. Co-gen generation fell to 180 lakh units from 221 LU and power exports to 89 LU from 122 LU, though the average power tariff realized rose to Rs. 4.89 per unit from Rs. 3.67 per unit.

Consumer Products Group revenue decline. CPG turnover fell to about Rs. 94 crores from Rs. 188 crores in the prior-year quarter. Management described this as an intentional recalibration of the model, with revenue expected to remain lower while the margin pool grows. The business is still loss-making, with quarterly breakeven targeted in another 4-5 quarters.

PSRIPL refinery closure and related costs. Operations of the Parry Sugars Refinery India Private Limited unit ceased as on March 31, 2026, with residual Q1 activity limited to liquidation of closing stock. Bank liabilities are fully settled, with Rs. 610 crores infused and an additional Rs. 55 crores loan extended during the quarter, totaling Rs. 665 crores; a further Rs. 65 crores remains available as required. The SEZ debonding process is expected to close by September 30, 2026, and asset liquidation is subject to SEZ approval and site denotification. Period costs will continue to be incurred.

Elevated employee costs and debt levels. Employee costs rose to about Rs. 59 crores from Rs. 51 crores, attributed to voluntary separation schemes at legacy plants rather than CPG capability building, with more such actions intended. Standalone short-term debt stood at about Rs. 980 crores as of June 30, with about Rs. 150 crores of long-term debt; short-term debt is expected to rise again once crushing begins.

Nutraceutical certification issues. The India Nutra business, about 20% of consolidated Nutra turnover, faced certification issues in Europe that have since been resolved, and no capacity additions are planned there.

Weather dependency. El Niño conditions prevailing in the country, coupled with tight inventories and steady demand, pushed sugar prices above Rs. 45-46 per kg in the current month. Management noted that rains in the back half of August and September are critical to preserve cane yields, particularly in Karnataka.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Firmer sugar prices and balanced global outlook. The global market is transitioning from a surplus-driven bearish phase toward a more balanced outlook. The ISO expects a 2.24 MMT surplus in 2025-26 versus a 3.2 MMT deficit the prior year, on better global production of 182 MMT. London white sugar recovered from lows near $404 per ton in early 2026 to about $471 per ton by July 2026. Indian gross production is estimated at 31 MMT, net production about 28 MMT, consumption stable at 28 MMT, exports about 0.8 MMT, and closing stocks about 4.2 MMT. Sugar diversion to ethanol is about 2.9-3 MMT, supporting the E20 program, with blend levels expected to remain at 20% for the foreseeable future.

Karnataka as the offsetting growth engine. Management identified Karnataka as the only place where lower TN and AP volumes can be made up, describing operations there as having industry-leading metrics. An early crushing season is anticipated based on signals from authorities.

Tamil Nadu state support for sugarcane. A recent state announcement on support for sugarcane is seen as a positive, delivered as a direct benefit transfer from government to farmer accounts with no impact on company working capital. Initial feedback from the farming community was described as optimistic, though management remains cautiously optimistic.

Nutraceuticals growth led by Valensa. Growth is largely from the US Valensa business, supported by new product launches, particularly in derm, hair and skin health, alongside strong incumbent categories. Management expects this year to be the highest ever for Nutra revenue with healthy EBITDA, and cited steady-state EBITDA margins of 12-15% once scale builds.

CPG transformation and jaggery expansion. The recalibrated model is focused on margin-accretive products, value-added mix, new products including jaggery and brown sugar, distribution expansion into general trade in the South, and brand equity. A new jaggery plant in Karnataka is to be commissioned in six months and will more than double current jaggery capacity; combined turnover from both jaggery plants is targeted at close to Rs. 100 crores, with margins substantially better than white sugar and more akin to food product gross margins.

Balance sheet and efficiency initiatives. Management cited a focus on efficient working capital, leveraging debt cost, monetizing non-performing assets, improving current ratios, and cost reduction programs across businesses, with the aim of a stronger balance sheet over the next 4-6 quarters and by March 2027 and March 2028. Non-core land parcels unrelated to operations are targeted for disposal in FY27, with no quantum disclosed.

Ethanol-sugar fungibility. With 582 KLPD total capacity, of which about 120 KLPD is grain-based, management noted flexibility to switch between ethanol and sugar based on margins, subject to committed OMC volumes where non-supply attracts a per-litre penalty.

Key Risks

  • Continued farmer shift away from sugarcane in Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh, with crush expected flat to down about 5% in these geographies.
  • Dependence on late-season rains in August and September to preserve Karnataka cane yields; failure would undermine the offsetting volume.
  • Sugar price correction risk once crushing starts, following the current move above Rs. 45-46 per kg.
  • Penalty exposure on committed ethanol volumes to OMCs if production is redirected toward sugar.
  • Recovery risk in Tamil Nadu, described as already a low recovery zone, limiting any pre-draw crushing strategy.
  • Execution risk on the CPG breakeven timeline of 4-5 quarters and on the jaggery plant commissioning in six months.
  • Continued period costs and uncertain asset sale value from the PSRIPL refinery, with dismantling contingent on statutory clearances and SEZ denotification.
  • Working capital and short-term debt expansion during the crushing season.
  • Further VSS-related employee cost outflows as legacy plant restructuring continues.
  • Nutra scale dependency for achieving the stated 12-15% EBITDA margin band.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

CPG breakeven. Management targets quarterly breakeven in another 4-5 quarters, with margin KPIs set and Q1 described as a good start. Observed Q1 performance shows revenue down to about Rs. 94 crores from Rs. 188 crores, with the loss narrowing and the absolute contribution margin pool growing. Management acknowledged revenue will remain lower, referencing prior-year revenue of about Rs. 600-650 crores and Rs. 800 crores the year before that.

Nutra margins. Management indicated steady-state EBITDA margins of 12-15% contingent on building more scale from current levels. Observed Q1 Nutra revenue was about Rs. 6.22 crores against Rs. 6 crores year-on-year, with consolidated turnover of about Rs. 61 crores against about Rs. 27 crores. Management expects this year to be the highest ever for Nutra revenue.

Balance sheet strengthening. Management stated an aim to strengthen the balance sheet by March 2027 and March 2028 and to improve the short-term debt position. Observed data show short-term debt at about Rs. 980 crores as of June 30, down from about Rs. 1,250 crores as of March 31, with management noting it will rise again when crushing starts.

Cane volumes. Management expects flat to about 5% lower crush in TN and AP for the current year, with Karnataka as the only offsetting geography, conditional on late-season rains.

PSRIPL wind-down. Management guided to SEZ debonding closure by September 30, 2026, and to realizing cash from asset sales without quantifying the value. Observed progress includes full settlement of bank liabilities, Rs. 665 crores infused, and prospecting for asset sales underway, with dismantling pending clearances.

Strategic direction. Management framed the outlook around consistent EBITDA generation from sugar and biofuels as the core business, growing the CPG business, and improving Nutra value creation, alongside a stated focus on working capital, cost and efficient operations. No plans were indicated to rework the corporate structure holding Coromandel as a subsidiary.
