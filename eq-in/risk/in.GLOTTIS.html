GLOTTIS — Q1 FY27 Business and Risk Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Volatile global logistics environment. Management described the operating environment during the quarter as mixed across global logistics and freight markets, with freight activity uneven across individual trade lanes.
  • Geopolitical disruption. The war in Iran and US involvement were cited as having serious supply-side implications. Management characterised the impact on Glottis as minor in terms of price and supply availability, largely because 84% of revenue comes from Asia, with the majority of imports moving from the Far East to India. Management noted some impact on freight trades and vessel availability, but less than expected.
  • Lower container throughput. Ocean freight container throughput was 21,841 TEUs in Q1 FY27, against 26,278 TEUs in Q1 FY26 and 31,402 TEUs in Q4 FY26. The decline was partially offset by better realisations and a richer business mix.
  • Higher operating costs and competitive pricing. Profitability was impacted by a higher operating cost base in line with increased business activity, while overall pricing remained competitive. EBITDA fell to Rs. 163 million in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 169 million in Q1 FY26, a decline of 3.7% year-on-year, even as revenue grew 39.5%.
  • Margin compression. EBITDA margin was 6.9% in Q1 FY27 versus 10.1% in Q1 FY26. PAT margin was 4.6% versus 7.1% in Q1 FY26, with PAT declining 10.6% year-on-year to Rs. 107 million.
  • Rising depreciation and amortisation. Depreciation and amortisation rose to Rs. 225.96 lakhs in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 76.21 lakhs in Q4 FY26 and Rs. 57.70 lakhs in Q1 FY26, driven by deployment of IPO-funded fixed assets and new warehouse lease agreements increasing amortisation of right-of-use assets.
  • Working capital intensity. Trade receivable days stood at 77 days at quarter end, compared with 87 days at the end of FY26. Management described this as the beginning of normalisation flagged in the previous call as newer customer accounts move into a steady billing cycle.
  • Concentration in a single region and vertical. Asia contributed around 84% of revenue, and renewable energy remained the largest industrial vertical. Management stated the objective is to reduce dependence on any one industry or geography.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong revenue growth. Revenue from operations was Rs. 2,345 million in Q1 FY27, up 39.5% year-on-year and 19.7% quarter-on-quarter. Total income including other income was Rs. 2,366 million against Rs. 1,682 million in Q1 FY26.
  • Sequential profitability improvement. EBITDA rose 54.8% quarter-on-quarter from Rs. 105 million in Q4 FY26 to Rs. 163 million, with margin improving 150 basis points sequentially.
  • Business mix diversification. Sea export contribution rose to around 20% of revenue from about 15% in Q1 FY26, with revenue growing 83.5% year-on-year. Air import revenue grew 97.1% and air export revenue grew 240.4% year-on-year; together air freight contributed around 4.9% of revenue versus 2.9% in Q1 FY26. Road transport revenue was around Rs. 107 million, up 107.8% year-on-year, contributing 4.6% of revenue. The newly added warehousing business contributed around Rs. 9 million.
  • Customer base expansion and reduced concentration. 260 new customers were added during the quarter, with repeat customers at approximately 75%. Contribution from the top five customers was around 29% of revenue. Management expects top-five concentration to reach 15%–20% over the next two to three years.
  • Broader industry mix. Consumer durables rose to around 10% of revenue from 7% in Q1 FY26, and chemicals to 7% from 2%. Management cited opportunities across apparels, auto, chemical, defence, engineering, pharma and other segments.
  • Fleet and capability build-out. 38 owned vehicles were added in the quarter, taking the total owned fleet to 80 vehicles from 42 at the end of Q4 FY26 and 17 at the end of Q3 FY26. Container deployment is scheduled to start from Q3. Management stated the CAPEX programme will be substantially completed during FY27.
  • Geographic expansion. Management is working on increasing presence across Africa, US and Europe trade corridors. North America contribution rose to around 10% from 4%–5% in the prior quarter. Planned network expansion includes Hyderabad and Kolkata, alongside the existing Ahmedabad presence. Regional offices exist in Singapore, the UAE and Vietnam.
  • New subsidiary in Malaysia. Glottis Logistics SDN. BHD. was incorporated as a wholly owned subsidiary in Malaysia with capital of 1,000 shares at RM 1.00 each paid in cash, aimed at expanding the business and strengthening the freight forwarding footprint. Management stated further investment will be made in one or more tranches as per business requirements.
  • Policy-linked demand. The presentation cites PM-KUSUM, PM Suryodaya, Make in India, Bharatmala, Gati Shakti and Sagarmala as frameworks supporting solar capacity expansion, infrastructure improvement and connectivity. The renewable energy (solar) sector’s installed capacity is expected to grow at 23.8% CAGR during FY25–30.
  • IPO proceeds deployment. Of the total IPO proceeds of Rs. 15,999.87 lakhs, Rs. 7,238.10 lakhs had been utilised by June 30, 2026, with Rs. 8,761.77 lakhs unutilised. CRISIL reported that IPO proceeds were used as planned with no deviations.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical and trade-lane risk. International trade remained affected by geopolitical developments and freight rate changes across key trade corridors, with management flagging supply-side implications from the Iran conflict.
  • Volume versus realisation dependence. Revenue growth was driven by better realisations and mix rather than volume, as TEU throughput declined year-on-year and sequentially. Continued growth may depend on sustaining realisations.
  • Cost and margin pressure. Higher operating costs and competitive pricing compressed margins, with EBITDA declining year-on-year despite strong revenue growth.
  • Customer and geographic concentration. Although top-five customer concentration fell to 29%, Asia still accounted for around 84% of revenue, leaving the business exposed to regional trade conditions.
  • Execution risk on CAPEX and integration. The company is implementing a Rs. 132 crore CAPEX programme within FY27, including trailers and containers, with container deployment starting in Q3. Depreciation and amortisation have already risen sharply from asset deployment.
  • Working capital and receivable risk. Receivable days remain elevated at 77 days, though improved from 87 days at FY26 end.
  • Subsidiary performance. Glottis Inc, the wholly owned subsidiary, reflected total assets of Rs. Nil, total revenue of Rs. Nil and a net loss after tax of Rs. (1.18) lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026.
  • Single operating segment. The company operates only in freight forwarding activities as a single operating segment, with operations carried out majorly within India.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Revenue guidance. Management stated revenue is expected to exceed FY25 numbers, with the CFO and MD both indicating growth would surpass FY25. Observed Q1 FY27 revenue of Rs. 2,345 million grew 39.5% year-on-year, consistent with a trajectory toward that guidance.
  • Margin outlook. Management stated it is targeting margins “a little more compared to the current quarter.” Observed Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 6.9%, up 150 basis points sequentially from 5.4% in Q4 FY26, but down from 10.1% in Q1 FY26.
  • CAPEX guidance. Management guided that the full CAPEX programme, totalling Rs. 132 crores from IPO proceeds, would be implemented by end of March, with containers deployed in Q3. Observed progress: 42 trailers added in Q1 FY27, 38 owned vehicles added taking the fleet to 80, and container deployment yet to begin.
  • Customer concentration guidance. Management expects top-five customer concentration to reach 15%–20% over two to three years. Observed Q1 FY27 concentration was 29%, down from prior levels.
  • Receivable normalisation. Management had flagged normalisation of receivable days in the previous call. Observed trade receivable days improved to 77 days from 87 days at FY26 end.
  • Air freight strategy. Management stated air freight will provide a major boost going forward, without committing to specific numbers. Observed air freight contribution rose to around 4.9% of revenue from 2.9% in Q1 FY26.
  • IPO utilisation. Management confirmed the IPO proceeds would be fully implemented by end of March. Observed utilisation was Rs. 7,238.10 lakhs by June 30, 2026, with CRISIL reporting no deviation from stated objects.
