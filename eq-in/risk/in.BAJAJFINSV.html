BAJAJFINSV: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

General insurance profitability pressure. Bajaj General’s Q1 FY2027 profit after tax fell to ₹478 crore from ₹660 crore a year earlier. The company attributed this to lower capital gains stemming from the external macro environment, with profit on sale of investments down by roughly ₹250 crore before tax. The combined ratio worsened to 104.7% from 103.6%, largely due to de-growth in the fire segment amid pricing pressure. The claim ratio rose to 74.3% from 71.1%, with the company flagging higher loss ratio in government health business. Underwriting loss widened to ₹130 crore from ₹116 crore.

Tactical pullback in motor. Q1 GDPI growth of 11.6% was in line with industry growth of 11.1%, but excluding crop and government health it was 10.5% versus industry at 12.1%, which the company attributed to a tactical reduction in the motor segment on account of elevated pricing pressure.

Life insurance profit and GST impact. Bajaj Life’s shareholders’ profit after tax dropped to ₹51 crore in Q1 FY2027 from ₹171 crore, impacted by lower investment income attributable to the external macro environment and GST, partly offset by yield curve benefit. The company noted the impact of non-availability of GST credit in the current quarter.

Emerging business losses. BFS continues to fund emerging businesses — Bajaj Finserv Health, Bajaj Finserv Direct, Bajaj Asset Management and Bajaj Alternate Investment Management — with losses of approximately ₹130 crore in Q1 FY2027, described as being as envisaged. Bajaj Finserv Direct’s FY2026 total income fell to $39 MM from $64 MM in FY2025, with profit after tax at $(18) MM versus $(5) MM. Bajaj AMC posted a Q1 FY2027 loss of $(4) MM.

Crop insurance contraction. Crop insurance GDPI declined at a 5-year CAGR of (9%) for Bajaj General versus (2%) for top-5 peers, and farmers covered under crop insurance fell from 19MM in FY25 to 10MM in FY26.

Macro-driven mark-to-market volatility. The company noted that under Ind AS, insurance subsidiaries hold part of the equity portfolio as FVTPL, which may cause temporary volatility in financial results due to unrealised mark-to-market movements. Consolidated profit after tax attributable to owners grew 12% year-on-year, but excluding unrealised MTM gain/loss and including realised equity gains booked under OCI, growth was 5%.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Consolidated scale and profitability. BFS recorded all-time high consolidated revenue and PAT in FY2026. Q1 FY2027 consolidated total income rose 19% to ₹42,037 crore and consolidated profit after tax rose 18% to ₹6,297 crore. Net worth (consolidated) stood at $8,768 MM, up 10.1% year-on-year.

Bajaj Finance momentum. BFL’s Q1 FY2027 net total income rose 22% to ₹15,224 crore and profit after tax rose 28% to ₹6,081 crore. AUM grew 24% to ₹546,944 crore. Gross NPA improved to 0.96% from 1.03% and net NPA to 0.39% from 0.50%. New loans booked rose 20.1% to 1.61 crore, with 51 lakh new customers added. Annualised loan losses to average AUF fell to 1.54% from 1.87%.

Bajaj Housing Finance. BHFL’s Q1 FY2027 net total income rose 16.5% and profit after tax rose 22.6%. AUM grew 24.3% to $15,833 MM, driven by a 33.2% increase in disbursements. GNPA improved to 0.29% from 0.30%. Capital adequacy stood at 21.47%.

Life insurance VNB growth. Bajaj Life’s Q1 FY2027 VNB rose 87% to ₹271 crore, with VNB margin improving to 15.9% from 11.1%. RWRP grew 17.5%, ahead of the top-10 private companies’ growth of 14.9%. Retail protection grew 60% with 12% product mix. Renewal growth was 18%. GWP rose 35% to ₹7,399 crore. The company described the outcomes as in line with its strategy of focusing on sustainable and profitable growth.

General insurance market share and solvency. Bajaj General strengthened market share from 6.7% in FY22 to 7.1% in FY26, with solvency at 254% against the regulatory norm of 150%. The company noted significant tailwinds expected from IFRS 17 implementation effective 01 April 2027, primarily driven by discounting of claims and deferment of acquisition expenses. Bajaj Life similarly flagged IFRS 17 tailwinds from deferment of acquisition expenses and release of global actuarial reserves.

AMC scale-up. Bajaj AMC was described as the fastest AMC to reach $3bn AUM, ranked 26th among all mutual funds with equity mix at 63% and non-group share at 90.9% of total AUM. Q1 FY2027 operating revenue rose 70.4%. The SIP book stood at $20 MM as of June 2026 versus $12 MM in June 2025, a 66% increase, with SIP folios up 69%.

Health tech and marketplace. Bajaj Finserv Health reported FY2026 total income of $119 MM, up 25.5%, with PAT loss narrowing to $(17) MM from $(18) MM. The platform reported ~2 MM health transactions a month, 130,000+ doctors, 15,000+ hospitals and 6,500+ lab touch points. Bajaj Markets reported 221+ MM yearly traffic in FY26, 44+ MM app installs, ~2.2 MM monthly active users and 103 unique partners. Bajaj Finserv Direct revenue returned to growth trajectory after planned digital customer journey enhancements, with some revenue structures now trail revenue based.

New business lines. The Board approved pursuing re-insurance business through a subsidiary to be incorporated, subject to IRDAI and other approvals. Bajaj Alternate Investment Management obtained regulatory approvals for PMS, a real estate fund and a private equity fund. The company also referenced a pensions fund management company with IRDAI/PFRDA approval awaited, and NRI market through GIFT city.

Financial inclusion metrics. New-to-BFL credit customers rose from 6.3MM in FY24 to 8.7MM in FY26. Lives covered under government health schemes rose from 8MM to 49MM. MFI branches grew from 100 to 440+. Women agents rose from 63,000+ to 82,800+.

Key Risks

Concentration risk mitigation. Bajaj General stated no individual partner contributes more than 5% to overall GDPI, and Bajaj Life stated no banca partner’s individual contribution to GWP exceeds 25%, with presence in 7 out of 10 top private banks. These are presented as mitigants to concentration risk.

Regulatory and approval dependencies. The re-insurance business is subject to IRDAI and other authority approvals. The pensions fund management company awaits IRDAI/PFRDA approval. IFRS 17 tailwinds are expected effective 01 April 2027.

Macro environment sensitivity. Both Bajaj General and Bajaj Life attributed profit declines to lower investment income and capital gains attributable to the external macro environment. The company noted that unrealised MTM movements on the FVTPL portfolio may cause temporary volatility in financial results, and realised profit on the FVTOCI equity portfolio is not routed through the profit and loss account.

Health retail abuse risk. Bajaj General referenced selective focus on retail health given high abuse risk, and selective focus on crop given current economics.

Forward-looking statement caveat. The company’s long-range strategy materials state that any statements that may look like forward-looking statements are just estimates and do not constitute an assurance or indication of any future performance result. The general insurance business is described as long-term, focused on size, scale, profitability and risk, with results that could vary year on year.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Bajaj Life 2.0 aspirations. The company’s stated FY2028 aspirations include market share (RWRP) growth of ~2X the industry, protection business contribution of ~10%, product mix of ~10% protection : ~25% non-par : ~30% par : ~35% ULIP, return on EV of 18–20%, and VNB growth significantly faster than RWRP. Observed Q1 FY2027 performance showed RWRP growth of 17.5% versus top-10 private companies at 14.9% and industry at 16.2%, VNB growth of 87%, VNB margin of 15.9%, and retail protection growth of 60% with 12% product mix. FY2026 actuals showed protection contribution of 8% versus the FY2024 level of 4%, and product mix of Traditional:ULIP at 57:43 versus 61:39 in FY2024.

BFL long-range strategy. The FY2030 aspirations include customer franchise of 200–220 MM, cross-sell franchise of 120–130 MM, return on equity of 20–22%, and product per customer of 6–7. FY2026 actuals showed customer franchise of 119.3 MM, cross-sell franchise of 75.5 MM, ROE of 18.1%, and product per customer of 6.07. Q1 FY2027 observed ROE (annualised) was 20.4%, up 1.4% absolute year-on-year.

Emerging business breakeven guidance. The capital allocation framework indicated platform businesses (Bajaj Finserv Direct) with less than $50MM additional burn over the next three years, operating breakeven by FY2028-29, and insurance marketplace by FY2028. New businesses (Bajaj Finserv Health) were indicated with less than $50MM additional burn over the next three years and breakeven projected at around $13 bn AUM. Observed Q1 FY2027 emerging business losses were approximately ₹130 crore, described as as envisaged.

Bajaj General long-range strategy. The company stated its business construct is to deliver superior ROE on target solvency, with ROE (annualised @200% solvency) estimated for FY2026 at 18.5%. Observed FY2026 ROE was 15.9%, down 0.1% year-on-year, and Q1 FY2027 combined ratio was 104.7% versus the 5-year average of 101.1%.

Dividend and capital returns. The company declared a dividend of ₹1.50 per equity share of face value Re. 1 for FY2026, approved at the 19th AGM held on 31 July 2026. Bajaj General paid a dividend of ₹1,925 crore to shareholders during Q1 FY2027, and its solvency after distribution was 254%. Bajaj Life’s solvency was 285%.

Board and governance changes. Rajiv Bajaj retired by rotation at the 19th AGM and expressed his intention not to seek re-appointment; the resolution to take note of his retirement was passed. KKC & Associates LLP was re-appointed as Statutory Auditors, and remuneration to Dhananjay V Joshi & Associates as Cost Auditor for FY2027 was ratified. The Board approved issue of 15,11,358 equity shares of face value Re. 1 to the Bajaj Finserv ESOP Trust at applicable grant prices.

Analyst and investor engagement. The company scheduled group meetings with institutional investors and funds in Pune on 20 August 2026, and an international investor presentation for meetings in Singapore and Hong Kong from 3 to 6 August 2026, covering Q1 FY2027 and FY2026.

Broker Narrative

The first report framed BAJAJFINSV as a lending/AMC growth play, buoyed by credit growth, deposit traction, NBFC momentum and a favorable rate pause; by the last report, the narrative had pivoted to a holding-company view dominated by insurance underwriting stress, delayed profitability in health/digital ventures, and capital needs for new businesses like AMC and reinsurance. Persistent themes included diversified growth and new-business investments, but the key risks shifted from NIM/cost ratios and Q1 seasonality to motor/health claims, the Supreme Court TP ruling, and regulatory/competition headwinds in AMC and digital lending.

Fears that came true

  • Cost pressures materialized: Bajaj Finserv Health revenue declined 5% YoY and Bajaj Finserv Direct losses continued, with capital infusion needed for the next 5-6 quarters.
  • AMC regulatory/competition watch-items materialized as additional capital was required for the AMC, alternatives franchise, and proposed reinsurance business.
  • Q1 seasonality concerns were borne out in muted actual returns after July calls (e.g., -0.2% in 2023 and +0.8% in 2025), as seasonal weakness weighed on credit growth and stock performance.

Optimism that failed

  • Strong AMC growth did not translate into profitability, as the AMC/alternatives franchise required fresh capital by 2026.
  • Treasury-gain optimism from falling bond yields and a strong equity market failed to persist; the stock delivered flat/negative actual returns across several subsequent horizons.
  • RBI-pause/moderating-inflation optimism did not produce a sustained re-rating, as actual returns were mostly flat or negative after multiple Buy calls.
