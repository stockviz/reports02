Vikram Solar (VIKRAMSOLR): Recent Developments and Outlook

Headwinds and Challenges

Policy uncertainty around ALMM 2. The cell-content mandate took effect on June 1, 2026, but enforcement remained unclear for most of the quarter before the Ministry issued an operating memorandum on July 18, 2026 deferring the requirement to December 31, 2026. Management stated this uncertainty held back buying decisions and showed up in order flow, with customers adopting a wait-and-watch approach on pricing volatility and their own infrastructure clarity (land, evacuation).

Cost inflation concentrated in COGS. Unit cost of goods rose by INR1.86 per watt peak in the quarter. Drivers included war-related inflation in aluminum and copper (about 35% of the balance of raw materials), a crude-linked spike in EVA (about 12% of the balance of raw materials), and Chinese cell price increases from the prior quarter flowing through inventory. The Gulf conflict also pushed up freight costs.

Limited pass-through. Management clarified that cost-escalation clauses in MSAs cover only the cell, not the balance of raw materials (BORM). Even on the cell side, industry oversupply made it difficult to fully recover costs through selling prices.

Intense competition and oversupply. Management described a large volume of new module capacity coming on stream industry-wide, with the country “flooded” with capacity, preventing full cost pass-through. Pricing power in the non-DCR market was described as under pressure.

Margin compression. EBITDA for Q1FY27 was INR126 crores at an 8.06% margin, down sequentially, with PAT at INR19.78 crores. Management attributed the entire gross margin movement to the COGS line, not a broad-based squeeze, noting realizations improved and every cost line below gross margin improved.

Ramp-up penalties. Employee costs stepped up deliberately to staff new lines ahead of the volumes they will carry, and the same volume currently carries a ramp-up penalty until full utilization.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record volumes and revenue growth. Q1FY27 saw the highest-ever quarterly volume of 1,006 MW (up 32% year-on-year), with revenue of approximately INR1,563 crores (up 38% year-on-year, up 8% sequentially). Production volume was 1,085 MW, with sales close to that.

Order book and mix shift. The order book closed at approximately 7.9 GW as of June 30, 2026, with composition shifting toward a diversified customer base. The 7.1 GW large-accounts portion is entirely non-DCR. The July 18 deferment is expected to unlock C&I demand (a 15 GW per year market) that had been waiting for policy clarity.

DCR ramp-up. The company sold 76 MW of DCR modules in the quarter, exceeding the full prior fiscal year, with expectations of 2 to 2.5x quarterly growth. Multiple domestic cell procurement partners are now in place. A Solar Cell Supply Agreement was executed on July 8, 2026 with Evervolt Solar Technology India Private Limited for 130 MW of Mono-PERC 10BB DCR-compliant cells, with supply from July 2026 to March 2027. A separate agreement with Avaada Electro covers 1 GW of ALMM-compliant N-Type G12R TOPCon cells (approximately INR1,250 crores), with deliveries commencing September 2026.

Distribution and mid-market expansion. Monthly distribution run rate has doubled from roughly 40 MW last year, with 119-plus distributors and over 757 dealers across 24 states and 500 districts. The mid-market sales team has doubled, with expected realization roughly INR0.50 per watt peak higher from this customer base. Distribution realization is INR1 to INR1.5 per watt better than large accounts.

Realization improvement. Per-watt peak realization rose to INR15.02, up 8% sequentially, driven by mix (DCR product carries materially higher realization, reportedly INR22-plus). DCR is primarily routed through distribution, with some going to mid-market.

Backward integration build-out. The first module rolled out from Gangaikondan on June 29, 2026, as promised. The 9 GW cell plant is on track for first cell in Q4 FY27 (commissioning in Q4, ramp spreading into Q1 FY28), taking the company to roughly 70% backward integration. The board approved increasing wafer-ingot capacity from 6 GW to 9 GW (part of the original 12 GW plan, with phasing changed). Ground-breaking for wafer-ingot is planned for next month, with commissioning targeted around FY29. The 3 GW Phase 2 cell line (upgraded technology, not TOPCon) remains in FY28.

Technology and cost advantages. Module portfolio transitioned from M10R to G12R across the platform. New lines at Vallam and Gangaikondan are substantially more automated, with manpower deployment down around 40% and comparable improvement in line cycle time. Co-location of ingot-wafer, cell, and module inside a single fence is expected to remove a large part of fixed overhead.

BESS business (VSL PowerHive). A master plan targets 15 GWh of integrated cell manufacturing and BESS assembly in two 7.5 GWh phases. Phase 1 BESS assembly plant in Chennai: equipment ordered about three months ago, delivery planned November 2026, installation expected January 2027, commercial operations targeted March 2027. Phase 1 7.5 GWh LFP cell plant: land options shortlisted in two states, incentives being negotiated, target finalization by September 2026; LFP Gen2 large-format cells; tentative commercial operation Q4 FY29. The PowerHive brand has launched for C&I and utility-scale solutions, with a first order of 20 MWh utility-scale executed.

International expansion. A dedicated international team is being built, targeting global buyers diversifying sourcing and tightening supply chain traceability.

Recognition. Vikram Solar received the Ecovadis Platinum rating for the second consecutive time, placing it in the top 1% of approximately 200,000 companies assessed worldwide.

Demand outlook. Management cited India adding roughly 12 GW of solar in the quarter with record rooftop contribution. Utility is described as a 30-35 GW AC market this year; C&I (including rooftop) at 15 GW per year (6-7 GW rooftop, rest open access ground-mounted); PM Surya Ghar plus KUSUM combined at 14-15 GW. Fresh tendering after the August 2025 threshold date totaled 35-40 GW, with procurement decisions expected in H1/H2 FY28. Data center policies in UP, Gujarat, Rajasthan, and AP could drive 25-26 GW of data centers over five years, with a 3-5x multiplier on solar demand. C&I, data centers, and green hydrogen are expected to anchor Indian demand over the next decade. Annual module demand projections cited: 45 GW in FY27E, 70 GW in FY30E, 100 GW in FY35E.

Key Risks

  • Policy risk: ALMM 2 timelines have already been deferred once, and management noted ongoing discussions about whether further extension is required. DCR versus non-DCR volumes are described as policy-driven and not predictable at this point.
  • Execution risk on the cell line: the 9 GW cell plant is targeted for Q4 FY27 commissioning with ramp into Q1 FY28; DCR orders using own-plant cells will only be taken closer to cell line shipment.
  • Cell margin capture: management acknowledged cell manufacturers retain most of the margins in the DCR chain.
  • Competitive intensity and oversupply: pricing power in non-DCR remains under pressure; peer margin comparisons are difficult until the cell line commissions.
  • Cost volatility: metals, crude-linked materials, freight, and cell prices remain variable; pass-through is limited to cell costs only.
  • Capital intensity: approximately INR5,000 crores of capex planned for FY27 (about INR500 crores already deployed), with similar levels expected in FY28; funding planned at 75:25 debt-equity, landing at 70:30 overall. Financial closure is in process, with debt disbursement starting end of the month.
  • Forward-looking statement risks cited in company materials include Indian and international economic performance, industry performance, competition, strategy implementation, growth levels, technological implementation, changes in revenue/income/cash flows, and market preferences.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

FY27 outlook. A formal FY27 outlook was shared on the May 8, 2026 call. Management stated it will revisit that outlook at H1 results, citing the shape of Q1 and the ramp ahead. On the Q1FY27 call, management repeatedly deferred annual volume, margin, and EBITDA-per-watt guidance by “one more quarter,” citing lack of clarity on DCR/non-DCR pricing and policy.

Volume capacity versus actuals. Management stated capacity of 15.5 GW could deliver 9 to 9.5 GW for the full year, contingent on market conditions, and that the company will not take orders with negative margins. Q1 actual volume was 1,006 MW sold (1,085 MW produced). On order book executability within the year, management deferred, noting changes in customer plans.

Margin trajectory. Management indicated Q2 will still see some impact of unabsorbed prices, with focus on channel/DCR mix expected to help. Management said margins should broadly trend in the current space but could get better if competitive intensity declines; no restoration to prior levels was confirmed. EBITDA margin normalization was tied to cell line commissioning, after which margins are expected to expand by capturing cell margins.

Cell plant timeline. First cell remains targeted for Q4 FY27, consistent with prior communication. The 3 GW Phase 2 cell line was always planned for FY28, not FY27. Cell plant utilization for FY28 is targeted at 40-50% for the full year, assuming a standard six-month ramp.

Gangaikondan module. First module rolled out June 29, 2026, on the date promised.

BESS timelines. Phase 1 BESS assembly commercial operations targeted March 2027; Phase 1 LFP cell plant tentative COD Q4 FY29; land and incentives finalization targeted September 2026.

Capex. Approximately INR500 crores deployed in Q1 (80% module, 20% cell). Approximately INR4,700 crores of rest-of-year capex anticipated (about INR5,000 crores total for FY27). Wafer-ingot 9 GW project cost stated at about INR5,600 crores. No long-term debt carried; working capital utilization declined; net debt to equity described as almost negligible; sanctioned capex facilities not yet drawn, with disbursement starting end of the month.

AGM. The 21st Annual General Meeting was held August 4, 2026, where all eight resolutions were passed with requisite majority, including adoption of FY26 audited financial statements, director appointments (including Mr. Sameer Nagpal as Whole-time Director and CEO, Mr. Gyanesh Chaudhary as Chairman & Managing Director), Section 186 approvals, and material related party transactions with VSL Logistics Solutions Private Limited.

Broker Narrative

The broker started with a Buy on Vikram Solar, driven by record domestic solar additions, capacity expansion, and expectations of DCR-led EBITDA margin expansion (19.5%). By the last report, the stance had softened to Accumulate after margins contracted sharply to 8.1%, input costs could not be passed through, the order book remained non-DCR-heavy, and backward integration was delayed. Persistent themes were pricing/margin pressure and DCR mix as swing factors, while order book strength and DCR volume ramp-up remained the main reasons for eventual optimism.

Fears that came true

  • Early pricing/moderation concerns materialized as intense competition prevented full pass-through of elevated aluminum, copper, EVA, and cell costs, compressing EBITDA margin to 8.1%.
  • The fear that volume growth would be partially offset by price/margin drag came true: revenue grew 37.9% YoY and module sales rose to ~1GW, yet margins contracted and most prior calls ended in DISAPPOINTMENT with negative actual returns.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected DCR-driven EBITDA margin expansion to 19.5% failed, as actual EBITDA margin fell to 8.1%.
  • The optimism on capacity/cell scale-up failed to deliver in the near term: the 9GW cell plant was delayed to Q1FY28, wafer/ingot capacity was scaled back to 9GW, and the cell plant was expected to run at only 40-50% utilisation in year one.
  • DCR-led mix improvement was slower than hoped, since 7.1GW of the 7.9GW order book remained non-DCR, limiting near-term margin visibility.
