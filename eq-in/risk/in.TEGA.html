TEGA Industries — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Equipment business weakness. In Q1 FY27, the equipment business reported revenue of INR358 million against INR643 million in the prior year — a decline of roughly 44%. Management attributed the shortfall primarily to delays in customer clearances, with profitability broadly breakeven and impacted by operating leverage from lower volumes. The company continues to focus on strengthening the project pipeline and order conversion.

One-time acquisition and integration costs. The quarter included one-time expenses of INR1.9 billion (INR1,910 million) related to the Molycop acquisition and integration, with a related tax impact of approximately INR280 million. Management stated these costs have been fully factored into the Q1 results, with INR110 crores booked in the current quarter and INR75–80 crores in the prior year, totaling approximately INR190 crores.

Macro, geopolitical and input-cost pressures. The annual report material cites geopolitical tensions and economic volatility (including the Middle East crisis and the Ukraine–Russia conflict) affecting global supply chains and raw material availability, inflation and rising interest rates increasing borrowing costs, raw material price and supply chain volatility, intensifying competition in mining consumables, technology and cybersecurity risks, talent attraction and retention challenges, and foreign currency and interest rate market risks.

Freight and logistics. Container prices have risen across geographies and container availability and vessel frequency pose logistical challenges. Tega passes freight increases through to customers with roughly a one-quarter lag; Molycop has hedged some ocean freight rates and passed through land costs linked to gas prices.

Competition in Latin America. Competition in forged media has increased, including from Chinese players. Molycop’s management emphasized its long-standing market position, local manufacturing footprint, shorter lead times, technical support and tailored service as differentiators.

Integration and reporting complexity. Only one month of Molycop (June 2026) was consolidated in Q1 FY27, and management cautioned it would be premature to draw full-year conclusions from that single month. Purchase price accounting is provisional, with goodwill of approximately INR50 billion (INR49.96 billion / USD528 million) subject to finalization within one year under IND-AS.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong consolidated and legacy performance. On a consolidated basis, the group reported revenue from operations of INR17.2 billion with adjusted EBITDA of INR2.6 billion (15% margin). Legacy Tega revenue rose 21% year-on-year to INR4.3 billion with EBITDA up 42% to INR1 billion and margins expanding to 22.1% from 19.1%. The consumables business grew revenue 36% year-on-year to INR4 billion, with EBITDA before one-time expenses up 58% to INR1 billion and margins expanding 320 basis points to 24.1%.

Healthy order book. The consumables and equipment order book stands at INR12.3 billion, representing 22% year-on-year growth, of which approximately INR9.6 million is executable within one year. This provides visibility for future revenue.

Molycop contribution and synergies. Molycop contributed INR12.9 billion in revenue for the one month of June 2026, with EBITDA before one-time expenses of INR1.6 billion (approximately 13% margin; USD17.2 million, up 3% year-on-year). Management expects approximately USD20 million of synergies over the next two to two and a half years from expense optimization, SG&A reduction, operational efficiency, procurement synergies and combined scale. Cross-selling revenue ramp-up is expected from Q3–Q4 of the current fiscal year, with teams identifying opportunities where Tega and Molycop share customers across geographies.

Favourable commodity fundamentals. Industry forecasts cited by management project global gold market growth of approximately 2.2% CAGR through FY30 and copper demand growth of around 4.8% CAGR, a blended rate of nearly 3%. UNCTAD is cited projecting global copper demand to increase more than 40% by 2040, requiring approximately USD250 billion of investment and nearly 18 new mines — driving demand for grinding media and mill liners. Copper prices were cited above USD14,000 per metric ton and gold around USD4,400 per ounce.

Mine restarts and recoveries. Freeport Indonesia is advancing phased recovery of the Grasberg Block Cave operation, with production blocks 2 and 3 at planned operating rates and a target of full planned capacity by end-CY2027. In Panama, momentum is building toward a potential restart of the Cobre Panama mine, with the government approving processing of stockpiled ore in April 2026 and First Quantum reporting 2.1 million tons of ore processed and approximately 3,200 tons of copper produced by quarter-end.

Capacity expansion. The Chile plant remains on track for soft commissioning around January 2027 and commercial production in March 2027, subject to local regulatory approvals expected within a two-month window.

Deleveraging and capital structure. Molycop’s debt reduced by approximately INR22.25 billion (USD235 million) at closing, with total net debt declining by roughly INR32.18 billion (USD340 million) to INR63.66 billion (USD672.5 million) at June 30, from INR95.8 billion (USD1.0 billion) at March 31. The acquisition was executed through a balanced structure comprising a INR1,713 crore preferential equity issuance and a INR1,500 crore debt facility. Management anticipates net debt will decline further by year-end.

Preferential issue proceeds. The Board approved a preferential issue of 4,78,435 equity shares at INR1,994 per share (including premium of INR1,984), aggregating to INR95,39,99,390 (approximately INR95.40 crores), to AP Jupiter Holdings II, Ltd., subject to regulatory and shareholder approval via postal ballot. Of the proceeds, INR75.40 crores is allocated to repayment of existing borrowings and INR20.00 crores to working capital requirements. The minimum floor price under SEBI ICDR Regulations is INR1,705.11.

Sustainability commitments. The company targets reducing absolute Scope 1 and Scope 2 GHG emissions by 20% by FY2029-30 against a FY2023-24 baseline, scaling rooftop solar capacity up to 1 MWp by 2027, and progressing toward water neutrality.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution and integration risk on the Molycop acquisition, including realization of the approximately USD20 million synergy target and cross-sell revenue ramp-up, which management expects to quantify over the coming quarters.
  • Customer clearance and project timing risk in the equipment business, which drove the 44% revenue decline in Q1 FY27.
  • Commodity price and FX exposure. Molycop’s contracts are largely tied to steel indices (around 85% of contracts), protecting gross margin per ton but creating revenue volatility; Molycop hedges FX exposure actively, with FX historically not materially impacting EBITDA.
  • Competitive intensity, particularly in Latin American forged media markets.
  • Macro and geopolitical risks including inflation, interest rates, supply chain disruption and raw material availability.
  • Provisional purchase accounting, with goodwill of approximately INR50 billion subject to finalization within one year.
  • Debt levels, with group total debt of INR112 billion including approximately INR26 billion of redeemable preference shares, and Molycop net debt of USD672 million at June 30.
  • Forward-looking statement caution: the company states forward-looking statements are not guarantees of future performance and it assumes no obligation to update them.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Consumables growth guidance. Management maintained long-term guidance of approximately 15% CAGR for the consumables business, describing it as sustainable. Observed Q1 FY27 growth was 36% year-on-year, which management explained partly reflected Q4 orders serviced in Q1, making the quarter non-comparable; no seasonality was cited.

Consolidated margin guidance. Management expects consolidated EBITDA margins to remain in the range of 15% on a full-year basis. Observed Q1 FY27 adjusted EBITDA margin was approximately 15% (INR2.64 billion on INR17.41 billion income), consistent with that range.

Consumables margin profile. Management indicated a 20%–23% annual EBITDA margin profile is sustainable, citing ability to pass through cost inflation with about a one-quarter lag and gross margins holding at approximately 62%. Observed consumables EBITDA margin was 24.1% in Q1 FY27.

Molycop margin trend. Management stated Molycop margins should be relatively flat in upcoming quarters, with internal focus on profit per ton rather than percentage of sales. Observed June 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin was approximately 13%.

Molycop volume and EBITDA growth. For the 10-month period, management guided volume growth of approximately 5% and EBITDA growth of around 4% on a comparable basis. For the 12 months ending June 2026, volume was 1.204 million tons versus 1.223 million tons in the prior year (slightly down), while EBITDA rose 11% to USD191 million from USD172 million.

Capex guidance. Molycop capex was guided at approximately USD28 million for the 10-month period, with normalized annual capex in the low USD30 million range and low-to-mid USD30 million over the next two years. Tega (excluding Molycop) capex was guided at approximately USD40 million including Chile.

Finance cost guidance. Full-year finance cost was guided at approximately INR110–120 crores. For the 10-month period, Molycop interest and principal was guided at around USD70 million.

Chile plant timeline. Soft commissioning guided around January 2027 and commercial production in March 2027, subject to local regulatory approvals expected within a two-month window.

One-time costs. Management stated the Q1 one-time expenses are complete and fully factored in, with no further recurring one-time costs anticipated.

Preferential issue proceeds utilization. Management provided a tentative timeline of within 4 months of receipt for repayment of borrowings and by March 31, 2027, for working capital requirements.

Non-core asset divestment. Management stated it is evaluating opportunities to monetize non-productive land parcels and will use proceeds to pay down debt, noting these opportunities would not have a material impact on Molycop’s EBITDA.

Broker Narrative

The first report was Not Rated and focused on TEGA’s mill-liner product specifics, warning about steel liner drawbacks while citing ore-quality deterioration and opex exposure as tailwinds. The final report upgraded to Buy after the Molycop acquisition, reframing TEGA as a global mining consumables leader with cross-sell opportunities, synergies, and margin recovery, while acknowledging margin dilution, debt, and equity dilution. The persistent theme was mining consumables demand, but the narrative shifted from cautious product-level neutrality to bullish post-acquisition scale.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that deteriorating ore quality and deeper mining would drive higher mill-liner wear/demand did not translate into positive returns: TEGA fell -10.6% by the next report and -14.0% after the Buy call.
  • The view that opex-related spending would insulate TEGA from cyclical capex weakness was not borne out, as the stock delivered negative actual returns in both periods.
