eMudhra: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Trust Services supply chain disruption. The CCA mandate to shift to a higher token security level caused a supply chain disruption, and no tokens were ready for sale in Q1 FY27. Trust Services revenue was Rs. 284.15 Mn in Q1 FY27, down from Rs. 397.15 Mn in Q4 FY26 and Rs. 291.77 Mn in Q1 FY26, and the segment represented only 15% of Q1 FY27 revenue.

Sequential moderation. Total income declined 2.1% quarter-on-quarter (Rs. 1,925 Mn in Q1 FY27 versus Rs. 1,966 Mn in Q4 FY26), and adjusted PAT grew only 4.6% QoQ.

Legal allegations. 3i Infotech Limited made allegations of financial fraud, criminal breach of trust, cheating, and criminal conspiracy relating to the disinvestment of 3i Consumer Services Limited (now eMudhra Limited) and the purported wrongful redemption of preference shares, and filed a complaint with the Assistant Commissioner of Police, Economic Offence Wing, Navi Mumbai Police Commissionerate on 03 February 2026. eMudhra denied the allegations, described them as without merit and barred by limitation, and stated it intended to contest the matter through legal recourse.

Unreviewed subsidiary results. The auditor did not review the unaudited financial information of 12 subsidiaries and a trust, reflecting total revenue of Rs. 309.08 million and a total net loss of Rs. 39.86 million for the quarter ended June 30, 2026; this information was certified by management. The Group’s share of loss after tax of Rs. (0.18) million for the quarter in respect of one associate was also not reviewed by any auditor.

Cost and margin items. Q1 FY27 margins reflect ESOP provisioning of Rs. 15.53 Mn and notional interest on acquisition liability. Finance costs rose to Rs. 11.96 Mn in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 2.01 Mn in Q1 FY26, and depreciation and amortisation increased to Rs. 119.78 Mn from Rs. 67.26 Mn.

Market and regulatory pressures cited in the presentation. Public TLS certificate validity is being compressed from 398 days toward 47 days by 2029; post-quantum migration deadlines are set at 2029 for critical infrastructure and 2030 for high-priority enterprise systems; and the machine identity and secrets management market remains fragmented, with most organisations assembling rather than buying complete solutions.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Overall growth momentum. Total income rose 27.8% YoY to Rs. 1,925 Mn in Q1 FY27; adjusted EBITDA rose 29.5% YoY to Rs. 529 Mn (27.5% margin); EBITDA rose 40.4% YoY to Rs. 504 Mn (26.2% margin); adjusted PAT rose 24.1% YoY to Rs. 339 Mn; and PAT rose 27.9% YoY to Rs. 320 Mn. International revenue was 66% of total revenue.

Enterprise Solutions scale-up. Enterprise Solutions contributed 65% of Q1 FY27 revenue and grew 50% YoY, with SecurePass and Certinext deployed across large Banking, Government and Defence environments with EAL 4+ certification, and emSigner processing over 500,000 signing workflows a day globally.

LEI validation agent appointment. GLEIF appointed eMudhra as a Validation Agent of Legal Entity Identifier India Limited (LEIL), a GLEIF-accredited LEI Issuer and wholly owned subsidiary of the Clearing Corporation of India Limited (CCIL). LEIL is the only LEI Issuer recognized by the RBI under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act. This enables eMudhra to incorporate LEI application and renewal services into its identity-verification and onboarding processes, supporting simpler, faster issuance at scale, meeting increasing demand for the LEI in India and internationally, and supporting regulatory compliance and market-led innovation.

Supportive LEI market backdrop. India recorded the world’s highest LEI growth rate in Q2 2026, became the jurisdiction with the largest number of active LEIs in January 2026, and the RBI issued a master direction in March 2026 consolidating LEI requirements for specified financial transactions.

New product and geographic expansion. PrivaTrust consent and vendor consent are live, with data discovery, classification and DSAR automation in R&D and early talks with large regulated entities. UAE Trust Services are expected to launch in Q2, with the data centre complete and QTSP services nearing licence application with TDRA. Regulatory reform in Kenya mandates use of a licensed CA for critical government services. Cryptas product cross-sales have been successful, with first clients using the full eMudhra product stack. SecurePass has advanced-stage client engagement and PoCs in Kazakhstan.

Customer wins across regions. In India, a large Government Tax Department signed up for SecurePass and a large bank is onboarding for emSigner. In Europe (DACH), the first emSigner Enterprise customer in Austria (a Government entity) and a large German data centre client for CLM and workforce ID. In Asia Pacific, a large Government insurer and Department of Social Welfare for emSigner. In North America, deeper penetration into the Higher Education Network for Private Trust Services and other products. In the Middle East, a large CLM deal with a UAE Police Authority and emSigner for a large hospital. In Africa, sale of CA, CLM, SecurePass, Cryptas and emSigner at a Kenyan regulator, and a large hospital group purchasing emSigner.

Recognition and market positioning. Frost & Sullivan recognised the Global PKI-as-a-Service market as growing above 30% annually, with eMudhra highlighted for leadership in India. emSigner was named in Gartner’s eSignature Market Guide as a Global, Full-Service, Enterprise workflow platform — the only Indian vendor in this category. eMudhra is the only Indian member admitted to the European Cloud Signature Consortium, holds ongoing WebTrust accreditation as the only Indian CA listed in major browser trust stores, ranked 1st among 42 vendors in IDC’s India Identity and Digital Trust tracker, and was recognised among the Economic Times Iconic Brands of India, 2025. Global Cyber Security and Paperless Transformation is estimated at 21.2% growth (FY2026–FY2028) by Frost & Sullivan.

Structural demand drivers. Machine identity is growing faster than human identity; deepfakes are raising the bar on verification; agentic AI adoption needs to be trusted; certificate lifecycles are shortening; and post-quantum readiness is becoming an active workstream.

3. Key Risks

  • The 3i Infotech complaint and related allegations, which eMudhra denies and intends to contest through legal recourse.
  • The CCA token security-level mandate and associated supply chain disruption affecting Trust Services token sales.
  • Dependence on regulatory mandates and timelines, including CCA requirements, RBI LEI directions, Kenya CA licensing reform, TDRA licensing in the UAE, and CA/Browser Forum certificate validity compression.
  • Execution risk on new products and geographies, including PrivaTrust (in development), UAE Trust Services launch, and CIS market PoCs.
  • Unreviewed financial information for 12 subsidiaries and a trust, and for one associate, certified by management.
  • Foreign exchange and interest rate movements, competitive pressures, technological developments, changes in the financial conditions of third parties, and legislative developments, as flagged in the company’s forward-looking statement.
  • The fragmented machine identity and secrets management market, where most organisations assemble rather than buy complete solutions.
  • Unresolved identity and governance questions around AI agents, which industry research flags could stall a meaningful share of enterprise AI initiatives in the absence of strong trust infrastructure.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements. Management notes that the LEI development reflects a wider shift toward consistent organizational identification in cross-border financial processes driven by the FATF revised Recommendation 16, with jurisdictions expected to implement the revised requirements by the end of 2030. The presentation cites post-quantum migration deadlines of 2029 for critical infrastructure and 2030 for high-priority enterprise systems (Dept. of Science & Technology, India) and a 47-day certificate migration deadline of March 2029 (CA/Browser Forum). UAE Trust Services are expected to launch in Q2. The company’s forward-looking statement notes that a number of risks, uncertainties and other unknown factors could cause actual developments and results to differ materially from expectations.

Observed performance against prior-year comparatives. Q1 FY27 total income of Rs. 1,925 Mn was up 27.8% YoY against Q1 FY26’s Rs. 1,506 Mn. Gross margin improved to 57.3% from 53.7% YoY. EBITDA margin improved to 26.2% from 23.8% YoY. PAT margin was flat at 16.6% YoY. Basic EPS rose 28.1% YoY to Rs. 3.91 and diluted EPS rose 29.2% YoY to Rs. 3.88.

Observed performance against the immediately preceding quarter. Total income declined 2.1% QoQ, while gross margin improved to 57.3% from 52.8%, EBITDA margin improved to 26.2% from 22.4%, and PAT margin improved to 16.6% from 15.0%. Adjusted PAT grew 4.6% QoQ.

Segment-level divergence. Enterprise Solutions grew 50% YoY and Services grew 5.0% YoY, while Trust Services declined approximately 3% YoY, consistent with the token supply chain disruption described for Q1.

Legal matter outcome. Following the 3i Infotech complaint, the Economic Offence Wing, Navi Mumbai Police Commissionerate made inquiry and sought clarifications and evidence. eMudhra’s lawyers intimated on August 18, 2026 that, based on the preliminary inquiry, the Economic Offence Wing filed and closed the complaint with the comment that the matter raised is not criminal in nature and could be a civil dispute that can be raised with appropriate authorities.

Governance outcomes. At the 18th AGM held June 25, 2026, all resolutions passed with requisite majority: adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY2025-26, declaration of dividend on equity shares for FY2025-26, re-appointment of Mr. Venu Madhava as director retiring by rotation, and special resolutions re-appointing Ms. Chandra Iyer and Mr. Chandrasekar Padmanabhan as Independent Directors.
