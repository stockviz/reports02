FUSION — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Sequential disbursement decline: Disbursements fell 17% QoQ to ₹1,783 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹2,140 crore in Q4 FY26, with the earnings presentation showing a 16% QoQ decline in average monthly disbursements (₹563 crore vs ₹672 crore) and QoQ declines across every top state (Uttar Pradesh -11%, Bihar -19%, Odisha -23%, Madhya Pradesh -8%, Tamil Nadu -27%, Jharkhand -17%).
  • Flat AUM year-on-year: AUM of ₹7,702 crore grew only 0.2% YoY versus ₹7,688 crore in Q1 FY26, even as it rose 4% QoQ.
  • Net interest income contraction YoY: NII declined 12% YoY to ₹236 crore from ₹268 crore, despite improving 6% QoQ.
  • Elevated cost base: The cost-to-income ratio stood at 66.9% in Q1 FY27, and OPEX of ₹206 crore was roughly flat QoQ (up 1%) and down only 2% YoY.
  • Branch network rationalization: The branch count declined to 1,512 in Q1 FY27 from 1,536 in Q4 FY26 and 1,560 in Q1 FY26, with plans to open ~50 new branches while rationalizing ~100 existing branches.
  • Borrower leverage mix still weighted to multi-lender customers: Although the share of disbursements to “Fusion + 0” customers rose to 51% in Q1 FY27 from 46% in Q4 FY26, 34% went to “Fusion + 1” and 15% to “Fusion + 2” customers.
  • Approval rate moderation: The approval rate eased to 25% in Q1 FY27 from 30% in Q4 FY26.
  • Key managerial change: Ms. Pooja Mehta resigned as Chief Human Resource Officer and Senior Managerial Personnel effective September 15, 2026, with her last working day set for October 11, 2026; Ms. Chinmayi Trivedi was appointed as the new CHRO effective September 15, 2026.
  • Promoter group reclassification: The resolution to reclassify Mr. Devesh Sachdev and related persons from ‘Promoter and Promoter Group’ to ‘Public’ was passed via postal ballot, with remote e-voting concluding on August 8, 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Profitability momentum: PBT rose 67% QoQ to ₹62 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹37 crore in Q4 FY26, versus a loss before tax of ₹92 crore in Q1 FY26. PPOP rose 18% YoY and 10% QoQ to ₹102 crore.
  • Margin expansion: NIM improved to 11.93% in Q1 FY27 from 11.44% in Q4 FY26 and 10.29% in Q1 FY26. The marginal cost of borrowing declined ~246 bps YoY to 10.1%.
  • Asset quality improvement: Gross NPA improved to 2.51% from 3.21% in Q4 FY26 and 5.43% in Q1 FY26; NNPA stood at 0.5%. Credit cost declined to ₹40 crore, the seventh consecutive quarter of reduction, down 29% QoQ and 78% YoY.
  • Strong capital and liquidity: CRAR of 36.95%, liquidity of ₹1,880 crore, D/E of 2.3x, net worth of ₹2,521 crore (up 30% YoY), and sanctions in hand of ₹2,302 crore. ~₹1,600 crore of fresh borrowing was drawn in Q1 FY27.
  • Collections strength: MFI collection efficiency was 99.76% (up ~10 bps QoQ), with net flow rate below 0.1% in Q1 FY27 and current-to-PAR flow at 0.08%.
  • Customer deleveraging: The share of Fusion customers with loans ≤₹40k rose to 34% in June 2026 from 13% in March 2025, and 85% of customers had fewer than two lenders.
  • MSME scale-up: MSME AUM reached ₹796 crore across 90 branches and 19 states, with ₹98 crore disbursed in Q1 FY27 to 19K+ active customers; ~50 MFI branches are to be used for MSME sourcing, with Tamil Nadu as a priority market and a new Individual Loan Product planned.
  • Technology and AI rollout: Enterprise-wide phased rollout of the Loan Management System, AI-enabled capabilities (6Mn+ customer interactions, 1.1 Mn+ calls monitored, 15K video identity verifications, 74% reduction in call charges), and fully digital onboarding.
  • Credit Guarantee Scheme (CGS MFI 2.0) approval, unlocking lower cost of funds and more competitive pricing.
  • Rights issue progress: Trading approvals received from BSE and NSE for 2,27,856 equity shares converted from partly paid-up to fully paid-up (Rights Issue First Call Money), with trading under ISIN INE139R01012 commencing September 04, 2026.
  • Investor engagement: An Analysts/Institutional Investors Meeting (one-to-one and group) is scheduled in-person in Mumbai on September 29, 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Credit and portfolio risk: Stage II ECL at 64.5% and Stage III ECL at 81.5% of respective gross loan buckets; Stage III loans of ₹167 crore (2.5% of gross loans) with closing ECL of ₹233 crore. A ₹19.5 crore management overlay remains in the ECL.
  • Concentration risk: Top 5 states account for ~68% of the branch network; Uttar Pradesh (27%) and Bihar (19%) together represent 46% of Q1 FY27 disbursements.
  • Borrower leverage risk: 49% of Q1 FY27 disbursements went to customers with one or more other MFI lenders, and 15% to “Fusion + 2” customers.
  • Growth versus quality trade-off: Management’s calibrated underwriting and tighter-than-industry deleveraging guardrails have coincided with lower approval rates and QoQ disbursement declines.
  • Execution risk on strategic initiatives: MSME scaling, branch rationalization (~100 closures), LMS rollout, and AI deployment are in progress and depend on execution.
  • Key personnel risk: Resignation of the CHRO and transition to a new incumbent.
  • Governance/ownership transition: Reclassification of promoter-group entities to public category.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The earnings presentation states that forward-looking statements are subject to risks and uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ materially.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Management commentary (Mr. Sanjay Garyali, MD & CEO, Q1 FY27): “As customer leverage continues to moderate and our capital position remains strong, we are well placed to scale responsibly. Going forward, we will remain focused on technology-led operating efficiency, prudent portfolio expansion and creating sustainable long-term value for all our stakeholders.” The company describes Q1 FY27 as a “transition from stabilization to sustainable growth.”
  • Observed performance against this positioning: Customer leverage moderation is evidenced by the rising share of ≤₹40k borrowers (34% in June 2026 vs 13% in March 2025) and the increase in “Fusion + 0” disbursement share to 51%. Capital strength is evidenced by CRAR of 36.95% and liquidity of ₹1,880 crore. Operating efficiency is evidenced by the cost-to-income ratio improving to 66.9% from 70.8% YoY, though it remains elevated.
  • Growth guidance versus delivery: The stated focus on “prudent portfolio expansion” and “scale responsibly” is consistent with the observed 17% QoQ disbursement decline and flat YoY AUM, alongside improving profitability and asset quality. The presentation frames FY26 as a “Year of Stabilization” and FY27 onwards as “Building a Foundation for Sustainable Growth,” with MSME as a key growth driver supported by MFI branch infrastructure and Tamil Nadu expansion.
  • Specific forward-looking items disclosed: Plan to open ~50 new branches while rationalizing ~100 existing branches; launch of an Individual Loan Product with MSME oversight; AI roadmap milestones for FY27 (AI-driven customer interactions, AI-powered underwriting, behavioural credit scoring, predictive delinquency management) and FY30 (hyper-personalised customer service, autonomous low-risk credit decisions, AI-led hiring); trading of 2,27,856 converted rights issue shares commencing September 04, 2026; and the Analysts/Institutional Investors Meeting on September 29, 2026, at which no Unpublished Price Sensitive Information will be shared.

Broker Narrative

The first report was a structural Buy: regulatory harmonization, rural penetration, digital onboarding, and strong AUM growth were expected to offset microfinance’s unsecured-lending, political, and rate risks. In between, repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes with actual returns as low as -67% forced the narrative to shrink from broad growth optimism to a narrower recovery story. The last report still carries a Buy, but its own headwinds are now explicitly the early concerns that had materialized: AUM growth below guidance and credit costs above expectations, with the bullish case resting on Q1FY27 asset-quality and margin improvements.

Fears that came true

  • The risky nature of unsecured microfinance lending materialized as credit costs stayed higher than expected and dragged performance until Q1FY27, consistent with multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • The growth and asset-quality risk flagged early materialized: AUM growth fell short of guidance and early Buy calls saw large negative actual returns.

Optimism that failed

  • The projected 26% AUM CAGR for FY23-FY25E failed to materialize, as the last report acknowledged AUM growth was below the guided range.
  • The early optimism on improving asset quality proved premature; credit costs settled higher than expected before the Q1FY27 recovery began.
