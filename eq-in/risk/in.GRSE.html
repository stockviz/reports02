GRSE: Recent Developments, Growth Prospects and Risks

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Order book has fallen below ₹15,000 crore for the first time in a decade. Management stated on the Q1 FY27 call (6 August 2026) that the current order book stands at ₹13,596 crore, comprising 11 projects and 44 marine platforms. Management characterised this as a reflection of strong execution rather than weak demand, but it also means the two largest legacy projects — P-17 Alpha (₹4,519 crore remaining) and Anti-Submarine Shallow Water Craft (₹1,815 crore remaining) — are both approaching completion.

Next-Generation Corvette (NGC) contract signing has slipped. GRSE is L1 for the project, but the contract was expected at the end of FY26 or in Q1 FY27 and has been delayed. Management stated it expects signing in the current quarter and sees no red flags, but revenue recognition from NGC will only begin in FY28, with first ship delivery after five years and project completion in eight years (i.e., around 2034–35 if ordered now).

Capacity constraints limit participation in large commercial vessels. Current capacity supports concurrent construction of 28 platforms up to roughly 10,000-tonne displacement and 200 metres in length. This is inadequate for Aframax, VLGC and large container ship segments, and riverine constraints at the existing Kolkata facilities restrict vessel size. GRSE is addressing this through partnerships with other Indian shipyards and a foreign yard with proven VLGC capability, and through planned greenfield expansion.

Propulsion engine indigenisation remains incomplete. While hull (float) is 100% indigenous and power generation (diesel alternators) is fully indigenous, propulsion engines are not indigenised. Government initiatives for marine gas turbines and marine diesel engines will take time. Overall shipbuilding indigenisation is around 80–85%, with 100% seen as 5–10 years away.

Pay commission impact on employee costs. Management expects a 15–20% increase in employee costs in the next year due to the pay commission, and has factored this into projections.

Export orders have been consciously deprioritised. After the German multipurpose vessel order, GRSE has not pursued further export orders because domestic demand aggregation by the government has created a large pipeline within India. Management indicated it would still consider attractive, profitable and visible export opportunities.

Next-Generation Destroyer (NGD) RFP is not imminent. Management does not expect the NGD RFP before FY29, as it has not yet received DAC approval and would take a further 1.5–2 years after approval.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Navratna status conferred on 19 June 2026. This provides enhanced operational and financial autonomy, enabling faster business decisions, capital expenditure on new projects, modernisation, equipment purchase, and establishment of joint ventures and subsidiaries.

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Total income was ₹1,914 crore (up 38% YoY), revenue from operations rose from ₹1,310 crore to ₹1,815 crore (up 39%), PAT rose 44% from ₹120 crore to ₹173 crore, and EPS rose 44% from ₹10.49 to ₹15.09. Management noted this was the 17th consecutive quarter of upward financial trend.

Three ships commissioned on 21 June 2026 at Kolkata — INS Dunagiri (second P-17A Alpha stealth frigate), INS Sanshodhak (last of four Survey Vessel Large) and INS Agrai (fourth of eight ASW Shallow Water Craft).

New orders received. GRSE received a Notification of Award on 30 July 2026 for four Platform Supply Vessels for ONGC at ₹1,032.07 crore, to be completed within 48 months. It received a Letter of Intent on 3 August 2026 from Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port for one 15T Bollard Pull Electric Battery Propulsion Tug at ₹37.94 crore. On 24 August 2026, it received an order from WBTDCL for two 100-Pax Electric Ferries at ₹45.02 crore, to be completed within 18 months.

Large live and expected defence pipeline. Five live tenders from the Indian Navy and Coast Guard total over ₹80,000 crore: 120 fast interceptor crafts (~₹3,500 crore), 31 follow-on water jet FAC (~₹3,500 crore), 22 interceptor boats (~₹1,200–1,300 crore), six Next Generation Offshore Patrol Vessels for the Coast Guard (~₹2,500 crore), and P-17 Bravo (DAC AON value ₹70,000 crore). Two further Navy projects — 12 mine countermeasure vessels (AON ₹36,000 crore, expected split 8:4 between two yards) and four Landing Platform Docks (AON ~₹35,000 crore) — have RFPs expected during the current calendar year. Management estimated roughly ₹1,50,000 crore of defence orders expected in the near future, with only five to six shipyards competing.

Non-defence demand aggregation. Tenders have been published for four MR tankers, six VLGCs and six Aframax vessels, with a high-value container ship tender expected. GRSE is collaborating with sister shipyards for MR tankers and VLGCs, and with a foreign yard with proven VLGC capability, and is partnering with an Indian yard with dock and berth infrastructure for container ships.

Expansion plans. Capacity is expected to rise from 28 to 32 concurrent platforms by end of the calendar year through modernisation. Two premises have been taken on long-term lease from Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port, with revitalisation already commenced at an expected capital infusion of about ₹200 crore over 18 months. A greenfield shipyard at Raichak, West Bengal, is planned at approximately ₹2,200 crore, with approvals expected during the calendar year and infrastructure build-up from 2027, taking three to five years to become fully operational. A total ship solution facility on the western seaboard (shipbuilding, green ship recycling and ship repair) is planned at approximately ₹2,000 crore on a PPP model, with environmental clearance already obtained and operations expected to commence in about three years. Post-expansion, capacity is targeted at 43 platforms including large vessels such as Aframax and VLGC types.

Autonomous and unmanned platform capability. GRSE has developed a prototype unmanned surface vessel, executed a project for NSTL Visakhapatnam, and developed an autonomous underwater platform proven in Tengangsu Lake. It is participating in Navy-steered autonomous projects and has been shortlisted for an underwater unmanned platform project.

Water metro opportunities. GRSE is executing a World Bank-funded project for 13 hybrid ferries for the Government of West Bengal, and is engaging with multiple state governments on water metro projects. Management estimated order values of ₹200–300 crore per metro, potentially ₹1,500 crore across five states, covering 30–40 platforms, with RFPs expected over the coming year.

MoUs signed at SMM Hamburg on 2 September 2026. GRSE signed an MoU with DNV covering warship design, shipbuilding, marine engineering, digitalisation and sustainability, and an MoU with Damen Technical Cooperation B.V. to explore design and construction of advanced dredgers in India. No financial implication has been ascertained at present.

Indigenisation ecosystem. Around 17,000 MSMEs, 1,000 startups and 675 innovators are involved in Indian defence manufacturing. GRSE runs the GRSE Accelerator Innovation Engineering Team initiative, which drew participation from about 101 startups last year.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution concentration risk: A large share of the shipbuilding order book (about 74%) is from four naval projects, with P-17 Alpha and ASW Shallow Water Craft nearing completion. Replacement of this revenue depends on NGC contract signing and conversion of the live tender pipeline.
  • Contract timing risk: The NGC contract has already slipped from earlier expectations; further delay would push revenue recognition beyond FY28.
  • Capacity and infrastructure risk: Greenfield and brownfield projects at Raichak and the western seaboard require approvals, and full operationalisation is expected to take three to five years, leaving a period during which GRSE cannot address large commercial vessel segments with its own facilities.
  • Import dependence: Propulsion engines remain non-indigenous, leaving exposure to foreign OEM supply and nomination-driven procurement.
  • Cost inflation risk: Pay commission-driven employee cost increases of 15–20% are anticipated.
  • Competitive risk: The defence pipeline is contested by five to six shipyards, and commercial tenders involve partnerships with other Indian and foreign yards.
  • Working capital and turnover metrics: Trade receivables turnover declined to 6.27 times in Q1 FY27 from 17.91 times in Q1 FY26, while inventory turnover rose to 2.23 times from 1.42 times.
  • Corporate action uncertainty: On a potential bonus or stock split, management stated it is awaiting instructions from the administrative ministry and DIPAM.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Q1 FY27 guidance on execution momentum. Management stated the current quarter’s performance could be replicated in coming quarters, noting that P-17 Alpha (₹4,500 crore remaining) and ASW Shallow Water Craft (₹1,816 crore remaining) are the major projects on the anvil of completion. Observed Q1 FY27 performance showed 38% YoY total income growth and 44% PAT growth, consistent with the stated execution phase.

Delivery guidance. Management guided delivery of the last P-17 Alpha ship by November of the current calendar year (about 85% physical progress at the time of the call), delivery of the balance four ASW Shallow Water Craft during the current financial year (fifth ship at ~91%, sixth at ~88%, seventh and eighth at 65–70%), delivery of two West Bengal ferries in September and October with the balance by April 2027, and the Bangladesh dredger at about 70% physical progress. NGOPV deliveries are guided to commence in Q2 FY28, with the last ship in Q2 FY29.

NGC revenue guidance. Management guided that, assuming contract signing in the current quarter, first revenue recognition would commence in FY28, with a shipbuilding revenue pattern where design contributes 5–10% of project cost in year one, physical construction takes revenue recognition to 20–25% by around half the project duration, and the steepest recognition occurs during equipment and systems integration.

Other operating cost explanation. Management attributed the significant reduction in other operating costs in Q1 FY27 to the absence of the final commissioning-phase expenditure that was incurred in the prior quarter when three ships were commissioned. Employee cost reduction was attributed to lower deployment of contract workers, which management described as normal in the shipbuilding cycle and expected to rise in coming quarters when ships are delivered.

Pay commission guidance. Management guided a 15–20% increase in employee costs, consistent with prior pay commission cycles, and stated this has been factored into future projections.

Dividend and AGM. Management has recommended a final dividend of ₹6.70 per equity share for FY2025-26, alongside confirmed interim and second interim dividends of ₹5.75 and ₹7.15 per equity share respectively. The 110th Annual General Meeting is scheduled for Friday, 25 September 2026, with the Register of Members and Share Transfer Books closed from 19 September 2026 to 25 September 2026 and the record date for voting and dividend set as 18 September 2026.

Broker Narrative

The initial narrative was unreservedly positive, extrapolating the sector’s record order inflows and DRDO AoN approvals into continued defence ordering. By the last report the stance had softened to Hold, with the same sector pipeline cited as upside but actual GRSE order inflows disappointing and the order book almost halving to ₹13,596 crore. Execution and margin quality also replaced the earlier order-boom enthusiasm, while indigenisation and naval platform opportunities remained persistent long-term themes.

Optimism that failed

  • The early belief that record defence order inflows (BEL >₹250-300bn, ₹1,420bn AoNs, DAC approvals) would sustain GRSE’s order momentum failed: the last report noted lower-than-expected inflows and an order book fall to ₹13,596 crore from ₹25,111 crore in FY23.
