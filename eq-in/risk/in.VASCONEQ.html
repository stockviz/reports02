Vascon Engineers — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

EPC execution slowdown. Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue from operations was INR152 crores against INR221 crores in Q1 FY26. Management attributed the decline to temporary cash flow constraints in two major government projects — the Supaul project in Bihar and the Sindhudurg project in Maharashtra — which held back execution and revenue recognition. These two projects had been expected to deliver a high revenue run rate over the preceding five to six months, and the impact was felt in both Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27.

Working capital cycle stretch. The net working capital cycle moved from a historical 45 days to approximately 65–70 days over the preceding six months, attributed to milestone-based payments (where funds must be deployed before bills are certified), geopolitical instability and local issues. The CFO stated this was concentrated in the two affected projects and final-bill certification on projects nearing completion, rather than a broad-based deterioration.

Profitability compression. Consolidated EBITDA fell to INR10 crores from INR34 crores in Q1 FY26; PAT fell to INR2 crores from INR22 crores. Q1 FY26 included an INR18 crores profit on sale of an investment (the Ascent Hotels debenture sale, per Note 4). Excluding that one-time gain, Q1 FY26 EBITDA was approximately INR15 crores, making the Q1 FY27 margin of approximately 6% broadly in line year-on-year.

Real Estate segment loss. The Real Estate segment reported revenue of approximately INR4 crores with an EBITDA loss of approximately INR4 crores, attributed to the timing of project revenue recognition and associated costs. Segment disclosure shows RE EBITDA margin of negative 80% for the quarter.

Stalled and slow projects. The Royal Rides project in Goa (order value INR213 crores per the order book table) has not taken off; only about INR15 crores of revenue has been recognised, and management guided to assume no revenue from it for the next three quarters. The Vashi Hospital project had not kicked off due to local/political issues that the client was sorting out. The Vedanta–Barmer project had been stuck for a long period with pending client cash flows before restarting. The Reliance order was mutually not proceeded with after the client changed its design and construction method (including shifting to Mivan), which made the quoted rates unworkable.

Rising debt. Total debt rose from INR201.90 crores in March 2025 to INR338.76 crores in June 2026; net debt stood at approximately INR152 crores as of June 30, 2026, versus INR91.41 crores in March 2026 and INR16.62 crores in March 2025. An investor questioned where capital had been deployed given a flat order book; management cited EPC working capital (CC limit utilisation), milestone-based project funding, real estate capital infusion, and the stretched working capital cycle.

Land parcel delays. The Thane land parcel acquisition remains under process and not completed. The government corridor acquisition has seen no news over the last three to six months. Assembling a continuous road-side parcel is progressing slower than hoped, with no timeline inside a year.

Adani engagement timing. Management noted that Adani Infra onboards partners at a very early stage, before conceptual design is frozen and approvals obtained. Ground construction is not expected within the next six months, and revenue that was hoped for this year may slip to Q4 or the next year.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order book visibility. As of June 30, 2026, the total EPC order book was approximately INR2,850 crores — INR2,531 crores external and INR319 crores internal — representing about 3x FY26 EPC revenue. Government projects constitute roughly 77% of the external order book, which management linked to faster execution and uninterrupted cash flows.

New order wins. During the quarter, the company secured an INR295 crores order from CPWD for construction of the RBI Colony in Guwahati. On August 12, 2026, it received an LOI worth INR126.39 crores (excluding GST) from the Executive Engineer, Public Works Department, Arvi Division, Wardha Nagpur, for a 300-bedded general hospital, awarded on item rate basis with a 24-month completion period. Management targets INR1,500–2,000 crores of new EPC order intake in FY27.

Banking capacity. Total sanctioned banking limits were approximately INR760 crores as of June 30, 2026 (INR112 crores fund-based, INR648 crores non-fund-based), with approximately INR355 crores unutilised (INR36 crores fund-based, INR319 crores non-fund-based). The presentation states the unutilised working capital limit supports INR3,000 crores of additional orders. Lender-led collateral optimisation unlocked incremental working capital without additional security.

Real estate booking momentum. Q1 FY27 booking value was INR66 crores against INR113 crores for the whole of FY26, with 30,690 sq. ft. of new sales bookings and collections of approximately INR20 crores. Orchids recorded approximately INR38 crores of booking value in Q1 FY27, taking cumulative bookings to approximately INR87 crores. Tranquil Heights in Powai was launched in the second week of June with an encouraging initial response. Prakash CHS in Santacruz West has received IOD, CC and is awaiting RERA registration, with launch expected shortly.

Near-term launch pipeline. Prakash, Tower of Future (Baner-Pashan, Pune) and the Ajanta redevelopment (Kharadi) together represent approximately 1.74 million sq. ft. of saleable area with an estimated sales value of around INR2,000 crores, of which approximately INR1,000 crores is attributable to Vascon.

Long-term real estate ambition. Management targets annual booking value of INR1,200–1,500 crores by FY31, supported by capability investments in business development, sales and marketing, and quality/customer experience. The stated acquisition strategy focuses on minimum GDV of INR250 crores for redevelopment and greenfield projects, at least two acquisitions yearly, and an annual GDV target of INR500–1,000 crores.

Fundraise. Shareholders approved a preferential issue of 2,00,00,000 convertible warrants at INR40 per warrant (INR30 premium) at the EGM held May 18, 2026. Warrants were allotted July 27, 2026 equally to Mr. Siddharth Vasudevan Moorthy and Mr. Pratik Saraogi, with 25% upfront received and the balance payable within 18 months. Management indicated deployment of roughly 60–70% toward real estate, with some for EPC working capital and general corporate purposes.

Cash flow from existing real estate projects. Management estimated pending construction cost of approximately INR300 crores against pending collections of INR116 crores and unsold inventory of INR414 crores, implying approximately INR220 crores of free cash flows, with real estate gross margins of 25–30%.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution timing risk. The Q1 FY27 shortfall was characterised as timing-related rather than structural, but the recovery depends on fund flows commencing from August 2026 and on newer FY26/Q1 FY27 orders ramping up from Q3 FY27.
  • Client-side payment and approval risk. The Bihar and Sindhudurg projects, Vedanta–Barmer, Royal Rides and Vashi Hospital all illustrate exposure to client cash flows, approvals and local issues.
  • Order book concentration in government projects. Approximately 77% of the external order book is government work, which management frames positively for cash flows but which carries milestone-based billing and certification timing risk.
  • Working capital and leverage. Net debt has risen sharply year-on-year; the working capital cycle has stretched; and management acknowledged that real estate requires continued capital infusion.
  • Real estate revenue concentration in Q4. Management stated real estate revenue will come “only in Q4, all of a sudden,” with Coimbatore, Tower of Ascend and Orchids targeted for OC within the year — Orchids described as a tight target.
  • Competitive intensity in Mumbai redevelopment. An investor noted that every Mumbai developer is pursuing redevelopment aggressively; management’s response relies on execution track record, presence in identified micro-markets, and targeting INR250–300 crores GDV projects that large developers tend to avoid.
  • Stalled order risk. Royal Rides remains a live order but with no client go-ahead; management explicitly guided to assume no revenue from it for three quarters.
  • Legal/regulatory matter. The Almet Corporation Limited share transfer agreement has been placed in abeyance due to a dispute among transferees; the matter is sub judice. The auditors included an Emphasis of Matter on this in both standalone and consolidated review reports, without modifying their conclusion.
  • Related party resolution dissent. At the AGM on August 7, 2026, the ordinary resolution approving material related party transactions with Vascon Developers LLP passed with requisite majority, but public institutions voted 99.19% against (3,315 in favour versus 4,04,193 against), and 4,26,869 votes against in total were recorded.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

FY27 revenue target maintained despite weak Q1. Management reiterated a FY27 topline target of INR1,200 crores, comprising INR1,000 crores from EPC and INR200 crores-plus from real estate. This follows Q1 FY27 revenue of INR152 crores. Management noted Q1 FY26 had also been weak and that the company still achieved INR948.53 crores for FY26. The CFO stated that whereas Q1 and Q2 typically comprise 40% of the annual topline and Q3–Q4 60%, this year Q1–Q2 would comprise 30% and Q3–Q4 70%.

EPC margin guidance. Management stated gross profit level in EPC remains at 13–15% on average, and that executing INR1,000 crores over the year would bring PBT closer to 8–10%. Observed Q1 FY27 EPC EBITDA margin was approximately 9% (INR13 crores on INR148 crores revenue) versus 8% in Q1 FY26, which management cited as evidence that project-level profitability held despite lower volume.

Execution recovery guidance. Management stated fund flows for the two affected government projects were expected to commence from August 2026, with ramp-up over one to two months, and that newer orders would contribute meaningfully from Q3 FY27. The Q1 FY27 outcome — revenue down 31% year-on-year — is the observed result against prior expectations of a high run rate from these projects.

Real estate booking trajectory. Against FY26 total booking value of INR113 crores, Q1 FY27 alone delivered INR66 crores. Management stated the company is already close to last year’s booking number with seven to eight months remaining.

Order intake guidance. The FY27 target of INR1,500–2,000 crores in new EPC orders is set against INR295 crores secured in Q1 FY27 plus the INR126.39 crores Wardha LOI received August 12, 2026.

Adani revenue timing. Management had expected Adani-related revenues this year but revised the expectation to possibly Q4 or next year, citing pending approvals.

Royal Rides. Management guided to assume no revenue from the project for the next three quarters, against approximately INR15 crores recognised to date on an order of INR213 crores.

Closing assessment. Management described Q1 FY27 as “one of the worst quarters we’ve had over the last 3 to 4 years,” while maintaining that nothing structurally is wrong and that all projects remain in hand.
