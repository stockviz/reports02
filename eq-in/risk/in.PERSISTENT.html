Persistent Systems: Takeover Offer for Nagarro, Financing Plans and Business Momentum

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Financing-related risks. The proposed financing plan for the Nagarro acquisition carries market risk, interest rate risk, currency fluctuation risk, refinancing risk, dilution risk, and execution risk. These risks accompany the company’s plans to raise long-term debt financing of up to USD 1,250 million and to raise up to USD 450 million through securities or eligible instruments (foreign currency convertible bonds, preferential issue, qualified institutional placement, or other permissible modes), with the combined financing options not exceeding USD 1,250 million.

Operational and business risks. Persistent’s disclosed risk set includes fluctuations in earnings, intense competition in IT services, high dependence on customers in the United States, wage increases in India, reliance on attracting and retaining highly skilled professionals, time and cost overruns on fixed-price or fixed-time frame contracts, client concentration, immigration restrictions, industry segment concentration, challenges in managing international operations, reduced demand for technology in key focus areas, telecommunication disruptions or system failures, integration risks for potential acquisitions, liability for damages on service contracts, concentration of major operations in one city, withdrawal of governmental fiscal incentives, political instability, regional conflicts, legal restrictions on raising capital or acquiring companies outside India, unauthorized use of intellectual property, and general economic conditions.

Deal execution and liquidity considerations. Completion of the Nagarro offer is conditional on a minimum acceptance threshold of 50 percent plus one share of all outstanding Nagarro shares. Persistent expects the transaction to close by the end of Q1 CY27, subject to regulatory approvals and other customary conditions. Persistent intends to pursue a delisting of Nagarro shares from the regulated market (Prime Standard) of the Frankfurt Stock Exchange as soon as practicable and legally feasible; following a delisting, Nagarro shares would be discontinued from trading on the regulated market, which could result in Nagarro being excluded from the SDAX and make Nagarro shares effectively illiquid.

Investor engagement disruption. The interaction with Millennium Management, UAE, scheduled for Tuesday, September 1, 2026, has been cancelled.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strategic combination with Nagarro. Persistent and Nagarro describe complementary strengths across digital engineering, AI, cloud, industry expertise and global talent. The proposed combination is positioned to create a scaled, globally diversified AI-led digital engineering and enterprise modernization business with at-scale presence in North America and Europe and meaningful Rest of the World exposure, better able to support multi-region enterprise clients requiring integrated AI, engineering, ERP / CX, data and cloud capabilities across local and global delivery models. Management believes the combination can build one of the world’s leading AI-led digital engineering companies.

Deal momentum and support. The offer of EUR 81.00 per Nagarro share in cash represents a premium of approximately 140% to the closing price on June 25, 2026 (the last trading day prior to announcement) and approximately 93% to the three-month volume-weighted average price as of that date. Nagarro’s Management Board and Supervisory Board support the transaction and intend to recommend acceptance; members of the Nagarro Management Board and further employees have declared their intention to tender shares amounting to an approximately 15% stake, and Persistent has already secured an approximately 22% stake, with Nagarro’s largest shareholder (Lantano Beteiligungen GmbH) committing its entire stake under a fully binding share purchase agreement. The Business Combination Agreement includes commitments to employee matters, operations and management, including preservation of existing terms and conditions of employment. Persistent does not intend to enter into a domination and/or profit and loss transfer agreement for two years after closing.

Financing flexibility and capital structure. The proposed financing framework is intended to fund the acquisition of Nagarro SE, optimize capital structure, maintain a prudent debt-to-equity ratio, diversify funding sources, enhance financial flexibility, and provide working capital financing to the company and its subsidiaries. Management states the framework will provide flexibility to determine the optimal mix among equity, equity-linked, and debt financing.

Business strategy and growth vectors. Growth prospects are driven by a pivot to an AI-led, platform-driven services strategy, new growth vectors at the intersection of industries, technology and geographies, enhanced focus on outcome-based models, enterprise-wide transformation through AI and Agentic AI solutions, and expansion of the AI strategy via the 3C framework (Core, Context, Coordination), alongside the signed Business Combination Agreement with Nagarro.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from macro-driven fears about IT spending pressures and discretionary digital cuts in 2023 toward operational execution concerns by 2026. Secular digital transformation themes largely validated the bullish thesis, as Persistent crossed $1B revenue, secured record deal wins including a USD 650mn large deal, and built industry-leading AI capabilities with 239 patent filings. However, new headwinds emerged around large-deal ramp-up margin pressure, wage-hike impacts, forex losses, and deteriorating working capital, while premium valuations prompted the downgrade from Buy to Hold.

Fears that came true

  • Margin pressure from capability investments materialized as Q1FY27 EBIT margin was impacted by AI-tool/purchase costs and lower utilisation at 86.5%.
  • Discretionary digital spend getting postponed materialized as license revenue declined 21% QoQ USD after a sharp jump in prior quarters.
  • Client spending cuts materialized as Healthcare & Life Sciences revenue dipped 0.2% QoQ USD with tepid provider-segment demand.
  • INR appreciation materialized as a forex loss of INR 1,052.7mn hurt PAT and drove weak OCF/PAT of 24.2%.
  • Higher global interest rates compressing valuations materialized as premium valuations were explicitly cited as the reason for the HOLD rating, alongside multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes on recent Buy calls.
  • Longer macro down-cycle materialized as the stock recorded multiple negative actual returns in 2025-2026, including -12.9% in Jan 2025 and -11.7% in Jan 2026.
  • Annual technology budgets under pressure materialized as both license revenue fell 21% QoQ USD and healthcare demand remained comparatively tepid.
  • Unsuccessful M&A risk was implicitly realized as Q1FY27 showed margin drag from capability/AI investments rather than accretive deal integration.
  • High attrition and management-change risks (flagged for Happiest Minds) correlate with margin pressure as wage hikes effective 1 Jul’26 create an ~180bps gross headwind in Q2FY27.

Optimism that failed

  • ~290bps EBIT margin expansion failed as Q1FY27 EBIT margin was instead negatively impacted by large-deal ramp-up, lower utilisation, and higher AI-tool/purchase costs.
  • Significant revenue outside pressured annual technology budgets failed to fully hold, as license revenue declined 21% QoQ USD and Healthcare revenue dipped despite the non-discretionary thesis.
  • 29% EPS CAGR outlook appears to have decelerated, given multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes on Buy calls in 2025-2026 with negative actual returns such as -12.9%, -7.9%, and -4.0%.
  • Strong EBITDA margin/RoE/DSO expectations partially failed as DSO increased 6 days QoQ to 86 days and OCF/PAT was weak at 24.2% due to delayed collections and tax refunds.
  • 18% USD revenue CAGR target appears challenged as Q1FY27 showed base-effect risks and QoQ revenue growth falling below industry average, explicitly cited as downside risk.
