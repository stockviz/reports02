Williamson Magor & Co. Limited — Summary of Recent Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Eroded net worth and going-concern uncertainty. The company has fully eroded its net worth, and its statutory auditors have flagged material uncertainty over its ability to continue as a going concern.
  • Defaults on borrowings. Williamson Magor has defaulted on debt repayments to lenders including HDFC Bank and IL&FS Financial Services, as well as on Non-Convertible Debentures (NCDs). These defaults have led to the invocation of pledged shares.
  • Loss of NBFC registration. The Reserve Bank of India cancelled the company’s Certificate of Registration as a Non-Banking Financial Company. The matter is currently under litigation before the Calcutta High Court.
  • Arbitration liability. The company faces significant arbitration liabilities, including a joint liability of INR 50,89,591 thousand awarded to Real Touch Finance Limited.
  • Distressed asset quality. A large portfolio of non-performing assets (NPAs) exists, primarily consisting of loans to group companies.
  • Disputes with lenders. There are ongoing disputes with lenders regarding non-recognition of interest expenses.
  • Audit qualifications. The statutory auditors (V. Singhi & Associates) issued a modified/qualified opinion citing non-recognition of interest expenses, going-concern uncertainties, unprovided provisions on doubtful loans, and lack of reconciliation for various trade and other receivables, payables, and loan creditor balances. A qualified conclusion was also issued on the standalone and consolidated un-audited financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Sector-level trends. Broader tailwinds include expanding financial inclusion in underserved segments, growth in digital lending platforms, rising credit demand across retail, housing, and MSME sectors, and a supportive macroeconomic and regulatory environment promoting infrastructure and MSME financing.
  • Internal recovery measures. The company aims to recuperate its financial position through recovery proceedings and actual recovery of outstanding loans, active pursuit of One Time Settlements (OTS) with lenders to reduce interest liabilities, and ongoing business strategy reviews by management in response to economic and government policy changes.
  • Macroeconomic projections. External projections cited in the report from the Economic Survey estimate India’s real GDP growth at 6.8–7.2% for FY 2026-27.

3. Key Risks

  • Negative net worth and inability to discharge current and non-current financial obligations.
  • Default on borrowings and NCDs, with pledged shares already invoked.
  • Cancellation of NBFC registration, currently under litigation.
  • Significant arbitration liabilities, including the Real Touch Finance Limited award.
  • Large NPA portfolio concentrated in group company loans.
  • Ongoing lender disputes over non-recognition of interest expenses.
  • Material uncertainty regarding going-concern status, as reflected in the auditors’ modified/qualified opinion and qualified conclusion on the June 30, 2026 quarterly results.
  • Lack of reconciliation for various trade and other receivables, payables, and loan creditor balances.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Guidance. Management is confident that, with ongoing negotiations and discussions with lenders for early settlements, lender support, promoter support, and various measures taken, the company will generate sufficient cash flows through profitable operations to improve its net working capital position and discharge current and non-current financial obligations. On this basis, the Board decided to prepare the financial statements on a going-concern basis. Management also relies on the expected continued availability of finance and potential future profitability.

Observed performance. For FY 2025-26, the company recorded a net loss of Rs. 1.25 crores, compared to Rs. 181.39 crores previously — a result driven heavily by the write-back of provisions. The company has fully eroded its net worth, remains in default to lenders including HDFC Bank and IL&FS Financial Services, and its NBFC registration remains cancelled and under litigation. The auditors’ qualified conclusion on the quarter ended June 30, 2026, citing going-concern uncertainties, unrecorded interest expenses, and defaults, indicates that the risks underlying management’s going-concern assumption persist in the most recent reported period.

Shareholders who have not registered their email addresses are being notified via post with web links to access the AGM notice and Annual Report for the financial year 2025-26.
