Cohance Lifesciences: Recent Developments and Outlook

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Weak Q1 FY27 operating performance. Revenue from operations declined 23.1% year-on-year to ₹4,223 million. The decline reflected shipment and order phasing in Pharma CDMO, softer Agrochemicals contribution, lower Formulations revenue, and negative operating leverage. Gross margins contracted to 71.5% from 73.0% in Q1 FY26, driven by higher raw material costs and lower contributions from high-margin CDMO segments. Adjusted EBITDA fell to ₹92 million (2.2% margin), and adjusted loss after tax was ₹430 million.

Segment-specific pressures. Specialty Chemicals revenue declined 34.7% year-on-year due to expected H2-dominated product phasing in AgChem. API+ declined 10.4% year-on-year on timing factors, validation campaigns shifting to later quarters, and an operational event at a customer facility. Revenue also faces some FTE contract risk this year in the US bioconjugation platform.

Subsidiary drag. Consolidation of NJ Bio weighed on results, including an EBITDA loss of ₹328 million in the quarter.

Regulatory observation. The USFDA inspected the company’s formulation plant at Shamshabad, Telangana from 27 August to 4 September 2026. The inspection concluded with a Form FDA 483 containing four observations. The company has initiated corrective and preventive actions (CAPA).

Disclosure compliance issue. NSE sought clarification by email dated 10 July 2026 regarding financial results connected to the Outcome of Board Meeting–Financial Results submitted on 12 November 2025, specifically the filing of Non-Machine Readable Form of Financial Results. The company referenced its earlier clarification of 2 July 2026 and submitted revised Financial results XBRL on 17 July 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Pharma CDMO recovery. Prospects include scheduled commercial deliveries, a secured restocking order for a commercial molecule affected by inventory destocking, and progress across a 10-molecule Phase III portfolio. Upcoming commercial deliveries are scheduled across Q2 and Q3.

Sapala momentum. Sapala showed strong revenue growth (2.5x year-on-year) with meaningful order visibility. A new amidites facility at Nacharam is complete for product qualification, and there is a defined path to full ownership. The company announced a reorganisation of Sapala Organics Private Limited to increase its ownership to 100% in stages.

API+ support. The segment is supported by a robust order book, niche APIs, cost competitiveness, and backward integration, alongside capacity expansion and validation orders.

ADC/XDC strategy. Cohance announced an additional investment of USD 13 million in NJ Bio and a controlling investment of approximately USD 5 million in Aruka Bio to strengthen its Antibody-drug Conjugate strategy. Following completion and Dr. Jain’s upfront equity grant, Cohance’s common-equity ownership in NJ Bio will increase from 56.0% to 67.3% (with Dr. Jain retaining 32.7%). Aruka will be owned 65% directly by Cohance, 25% by NJ Bio, and 10% by Dr. Jain, making Aruka a direct subsidiary. Both transactions are funded through internal accruals. The reorganisation establishes distinct priorities: deeper integration of NJ Bio’s customer-facing services with Cohance to support end-to-end CRDMO development through commercial supply, and focused development of Aruka’s proprietary pipeline through potential partnerships, co-development, and licensing. The reorganisation reinforces commitment to the high-growth ADC and broader XDC space.

Other growth drivers. ADC payload-linker-bioconjugation integration, expansion into AgChem innovator-product-led portfolios, and emerging Performance Chemicals and electronic/semiconductor-linked opportunities.

Sustainability recognition. Cohance’s sustainability rating was upgraded from Silver to Gold by EcoVadis, and its greenhouse gas reduction targets were formally validated by SBTi Services on 24 July 2026.

Financial capacity. The balance sheet remains net cash positive at ₹2,512 million, with cash and cash equivalents of ₹4,589 million and free cash flow of ₹1,063 million generated during the quarter. Standalone business remained strong with Adjusted EBITDA margins at 9.2%.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued shipment and order phasing variability, particularly in Pharma CDMO and AgChem, with H2-weighted product phasing.
  • Gross margin pressure from higher raw material costs and mix shift away from high-margin CDMO segments.
  • Negative operating leverage on a lower revenue base.
  • Subsidiary consolidation losses, exemplified by NJ Bio’s ₹328 million EBITDA loss in Q1 FY27.
  • FTE contract risk in the US bioconjugation platform.
  • Regulatory risk arising from the four USFDA Form 483 observations at the Shamshabad formulation plant, with resolution dependent on the CAPA response.
  • Execution risk on the NJ Bio and Aruka Bio transactions, which remain subject to definitive agreements, applicable approvals, and customary closing conditions.
  • Disclosure and filing compliance risk, as reflected in the NSE clarification regarding non-machine-readable financial results and the subsequent revised XBRL filing.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Guidance. Management expects sequential improvement starting from Q2 FY27, with overall growth returning during H2 FY27, supported by Pharma CDMO recovery, order-backed growth in Sapala, API+ stability, and formulation normalisation. EBITDA improvement is expected to be weighted towards the second half of the year, backed by volume recovery, better product mix, and improved platform utilization. Management expects completion of the NJ Bio and Aruka Bio transactions by the end of September 2026, subject to definitive agreements, applicable approvals, and customary closing conditions, and states that the integration is expected to strengthen business performance across the combined platform over time. On the USFDA Form 483, the company will submit its response within the stipulated timeline and remains committed to quality and regulatory compliance.

Observed performance. Q1 FY27 results were weak: revenue declined 23.1% year-on-year to ₹4,223 million, adjusted EBITDA was ₹92 million at a 2.2% margin, and adjusted loss after tax was ₹430 million. Gross margin contracted to 71.5%. Specialty Chemicals fell 34.7% and API+ fell 10.4% year-on-year. NJ Bio contributed an EBITDA loss of ₹328 million. Against this, the standalone business delivered 9.2% Adjusted EBITDA margin, the balance sheet remained net cash positive at ₹2,512 million with ₹4,589 million in cash and cash equivalents, and free cash flow of ₹1,063 million was generated in the quarter. Sapala revenue grew 2.5x year-on-year. The NJ Bio and Aruka Bio transactions were announced with expected completion by end-September 2026; the source material does not indicate completion status. The USFDA inspection concluded with four observations, and CAPA has been initiated; the source material does not indicate the outcome of the company’s response.

Broker Narrative

The broker started with a Buy in May 2025, dismissing the Q4FY25 miss as temporary and highlighting CDMO momentum, a strong pipeline, and a double-digit FY26 revenue guide. By the August 2026 Reduce report, the narrative turned sharply cautious after Q1FY27 revenue fell 23.1% YoY, EBITDA margin collapsed to 0.3%, and NJ Bio remained a persistent drag. Persistent themes were shipment deferrals, customer inventory adjustments, and margin/mix pressure; what changed was the shift from expecting a quick recovery to acknowledging delayed growth and continued integration pain.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning of muted quarters due to shipment schedules and customer inventory adjustments materialised in Q1FY27, when revenue fell 23.1% YoY on deferred pharma CDMO and API+ shipments.
  • The concern that lower-margin mix and NJ Bio integration would weigh on margins was realised, with EBITDA margin contracting to 0.3% and NJ Bio remaining the largest profitability drag.
  • The initial API+ revenue weakness persisted, as API+ revenue fell a further 10.5% YoY in Q1FY27 due to shifting of commercial orders and validation batches.
  • The risk of delayed integration and synergies from acquired assets came true, as NJ Bio’s low utilisation remained a drag and Nacharam remediation/API programme delays continued.

Optimism that failed

  • Management’s double-digit FY26 revenue growth guidance failed to materialise, with Q1FY27 revenue down 23.1% YoY.
  • The expected continuation of pharma CDMO growth did not hold, as pharma CDMO revenue fell 38.7% YoY in Q1FY27 on deferred shipments.
  • The specialty chemicals CDMO recovery flagged in Q4FY25 reversed, with agro and specialty chemicals CDMO revenue declining 34.7% YoY in Q1FY27.
  • The expectation that API commercialisation would drive growth did not prevent API+ revenue from falling 10.5% YoY in Q1FY27.
