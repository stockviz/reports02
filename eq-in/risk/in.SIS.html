SIS Limited (NSE: SIS, BSE: 540673) Analysis

SIS Limited, a leading provider of security, cash logistics, and facility management services, reported strong Q2 FY26 results (ended Sep 30, 2025), with consolidated revenue of ₹3,759 Cr (+15% YoY), EBITDA of ₹168 Cr (+16.2% YoY), and operating PAT of ₹93 Cr (+35.1% YoY). Key segments showed robust growth, bolstered by the AP Securitas acquisition. Below is an analysis of headwinds, tailwinds, growth prospects, and key risks, derived from the filings.

Tailwinds
  • Strong Revenue Momentum: Record monthly run-rate of ₹1,300 Cr (+15% YoY topline growth). All segments grew: Security India (₹1,544 Cr, +11.5% YoY), International (₹1,607 Cr, +19.3% YoY or +17.1% CC), FM (₹629 Cr, +13.7% YoY). New wins: ₹35 Cr/month (Security India), ₹15 Cr/month (FM).
  • EBITDA Expansion: Highest-ever quarterly EBITDA (+10.7% QoQ), with margins stable/improving (consolidated 4.5%; FM +90 bps YoY to 5.2%; Intl +30 bps QoQ to 3.3%).
  • Strategic Acquisitions: Completed 51% stake in AP Securitas (₹71 Cr), creating ₹6,100 Cr combined security revenue (~2x nearest rival, +17% to India Security run-rate). Synergistic investment (₹4.5 Cr) in Wiffy for cross-selling.
  • Balance Sheet Strength: Net debt down ₹194 Cr YoY to ₹663 Cr (Net Debt/EBITDA 1.03x); DSO stable at 69 days; ROCE 14.3%, RoE 14.0%.
  • Policy Tailwind: Investor call highlights New Labour Codes (2025) as “GST moment” for business services, potentially standardizing wages/costs.
Headwinds
  • Margin Pressures: Security India margins dipped to 5.3% (-20 bps YoY) due to B2C alarm monitoring spends and lower tech revenue. Consolidated OCF/EBITDA weak at 6.3% (no tax refunds vs. ₹79 Cr in Q1).
  • International Challenges: Australia wage hike (3.5% min wage), tight labor market (4.5% unemployment), DSO +5 days QoQ.
  • Working Capital Strain: Consolidated DSO +1 day QoQ; negative working capital changes (-₹142 Cr in Q2 cash flow).
  • One-offs: Reported PAT impacted by ₹12 Cr capital gains tax (internal restructuring); higher depreciation (+13% YoY).
Growth Prospects
  • Market Leadership: Post-AP Securitas, SIS is India’s #1 security player; FM remains #1. H1 FY26 PAT +40% YoY to ₹186 Cr positions for FY26 rebound.
  • Order Pipeline: New wins in high-growth sectors (e-commerce, IT, govt, steel, energy). Monthly India run-rate >₹750 Cr (first time).
  • Expansion Avenues: Tech integration (alarm monitoring, IFM mechanization); international scaling (Australia/NZ #1/#3); ventures like Wiffy for adjacencies. Labour codes could unlock formalization/consolidation in fragmented ₹2-3 Tn industry.
  • Financial Flexibility: Improving leverage supports M&A; H1 revenue +14% YoY to ₹7,307 Cr.
Key Risks
  • Labor Cost Inflation: Wage revisions (India/Australia); 288K+ employees expose to code changes (upside/downside TBD in Nov 25 call).
  • Execution/Integration: AP Securitas Tranche 2 (to 100% by 2029); capex ₹56 Cr/Q2 for customer sites.
  • Macro/External: Forex volatility (Intl 43% revenue, unrealized FX loss ₹5 Cr); cyclical sectors (manufacturing/retail); competition in fragmented markets.
  • Liquidity/Debt: Debt ₹1,687 Cr (incl. leases); OCF variability; NCDs (₹250 Cr) with 1.5x asset cover.
  • Regulatory/Operational: Compliance (SEBI LODR), tax benefits (80JJAA eligibility), DSO stretch (Intl), impairment risks (prior FY25 ₹306 Cr goodwill hit).
Summary

Bullish Outlook with Managed Risks: SIS is firing on all cylinders—record growth, deleveraging, and market consolidation via AP Securitas position it for FY26 acceleration (guided “strong rebound”). Tailwinds from scale, new wins, and potential labour code benefits outweigh headwinds like margins/labor pressures. Growth prospects are strong in a consolidating industry, but monitor integration, wages, and cash flows. Valuation Context: At ~1x EV/Sales (peer avg 1.5x), attractive for long-term compounding; watch Nov 25 call for code clarity. Recommendation: Accumulate on dips (target upside 20-30% in 12M, assuming 12-15% FY26 growth).

Data as of Q2 FY26 filings; audited FY26 results pending.

Broker Narrative

The narrative stayed constructive from first to last: both reports were Buy calls and consistently emphasized India Security growth, market-share gains, facilities-management margin recovery, and eventual margin improvement. The main shift was that the early report treated international weakness and wage hikes as short-term pressures, while the last report treated Australia’s labor shortages, wage inflation, and high-margin contract losses as ongoing multi-quarter drags. Debt and operating-cash-flow concerns from the first report were replaced by a stronger balance-sheet and return-ratio story in the last report.

Fears that came true

  • International weakness flagged in Q1 FY24 persisted, with the last report still citing Australia pressure, labor shortages, and 70bp YoY margin contraction from lost high-margin contracts, and the affected calls ended in DISAPPOINTMENT.
  • The wage-hike risk materialized: the 3.75% minimum wage revision in Australia and salary hikes pressured margins, while India Security margin also contracted 20bp YoY, and the last report’s +24.7% predicted return became -11.9%.
  • The early miss-vs-estimates risk recurred: the last report again missed consolidated adjusted PAT estimates due to higher effective tax and interest expense, correlating with negative actual returns.

Optimism that failed

  • The positive medium-term outlook and repeated Buy calls failed to deliver the projected upside: the first report’s +30.2% predicted return ended at -2.4%, and the last report’s +24.7% predicted return ended at -11.9%.
  • The expected margin recovery was incomplete by the last report, as India Security margin was still below the pre-COVID 6% target at 5.5% and had contracted 20bp YoY.
