AHLUCONT: Recent Corporate Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin compression in 1QFY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026): - EBITDA margin fell to 4.29% from 8.59% in 1QFY26; PAT margin fell to 1% from 5.01%. - Turnover grew 12.03% to INR1,125.81 crores, but PAT declined 77.65% to INR11.42 crores; EPS fell to INR1.70 from INR7.63.

Specific causes cited by management: - AIIMS Jammu final bill: Finalisation reduced the bill value by INR29 crores, a 2.6% hit on EBITDA. The matter is disputed and will go to arbitration. Management stated the project is now closed with no potential for further hit. - Labour cost escalation in NCR: Minimum wage increases of ~35–40% across skilled and unskilled categories. NCR is ~50% of the portfolio. Management noted many large-developer contracts lack labour escalation clauses, and that this is not treated as a statutory increase that clients must necessarily bear. The quantifiable company-level EBITDA impact was stated as ~1.5%, or ~1% if no escalation is received. - West Bengal and Assam SIR drives and elections: Reduced turnover on projects in those states and higher IDC costs. - Staff cost increase: Employee base expanded and staff mobilised for a larger project portfolio; project staff cost has moved from 3.5–4.5% to over 5%, with safety budgets rising from ~1% to ~3%. - Material and supply-chain disruption: War-related price increases and delivery delays (e.g., switchgear delivery extending from 4–6 weeks to 3–4 months). - Design changes: At DLF’s Dahlias project and the Gems and Jewellery Park, client-side design changes delayed work. - NGT uncertainty: Potential NGT measures by the Delhi and Haryana governments could impact Q3; management could not quantify this. - Payment delays: A Central University project in Himachal has certified bills unpaid for 5 months due to the project going over budget; Assam bills were held up by elections but began clearing ~15 days before the call. - Higher finance cost: Rose due to mobilisation advance availed for the new Central Vista project; expected to remain at similar levels (~INR15–16 crores per quarter). - Higher depreciation: Expected to be recurring due to capital expenditure over the last 2–3 years. - Labour shortage: Management described skilled labour (bar binders, carpenters, masons) as in extreme short supply, with NCR projects operating at 40–50% labour strength.

Regulatory/compliance matters: - The company refiled its 4QFY26 and FY26 results after an exchange mail dated June 24, 2026, citing an inadvertently missed Consolidated Auditors Report, a non-machine-readable filing, and XBRL cash flow discrepancies versus the PDF. Revised XBRL was filed to match the approved PDF. - Implementation of the Labour Codes (notified November 21, 2025) resulted in an INR111.83 lakh increase in the gratuity provision, recognised as employee benefit expense in the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Order book: Net order book of INR20,663.52 crores as on June 30, 2026, to be executed over 3–3.5 years. The investor presentation cites a Gross Order Book of ₹297,138 Mn and Unexecuted Order Book of ₹206,635 Mn (excl. GST) as of June 30, 2026.
  • Order inflows: INR512.81 crores in FY27 up to June 30, 2026. RML (INR500 crores) remains L1 and not yet converted to a work order. The Odisha Government University, Bhubaneswar bid fell through after the company refused to extend bid validity on a fixed-price contract.
  • Large projects ramping up:
    • Central Vista: Nirman Bhawan fully demolished, foundation casting begun, structural steel erection to start in September; Udyog Bhawan 90% demolished with excavation started. Targeted billing of ~INR700 crores in FY27 and ~INR1,000 crores in FY28; entire project completion targeted for FY29.
    • CST project: ~INR70 crores billed in Q1; targeting INR400–450 crores in FY27 (hoping to cross INR500 crores) and ~INR700 crores in FY28.
    • Gems and Jewellery Park: Ground work likely to begin in Q3; ~INR100 crores billing targeted in FY27 and ~INR450 crores in FY28; 3.5-year completion timeframe.
    • DLF Dahlias and Downtown: Targeted monthly billings of INR30–35 crores and INR25–30 crores respectively.
  • Client compensation discussions: Management has approached NCR clients on labour cost compensation, with clients described as sympathetic; some clients (Godrej, DLF, Signature Global) have introduced progress-linked incentives that may offset higher costs.
  • Escalation mechanisms: Material cost pass-throughs operate with ~30-day lag for private clients (base price mechanism) and quarterly WPI-based escalation for government contracts.
  • Bidding discipline: New bids now incorporate higher shuttering and steel prices, higher staff and safety costs; management expects this to help restore historical margins.
  • Geographic spread: Operations across 55+ projects in 16 states and 1 project overseas.
  • Balance sheet: Cash and bank balances of INR920 crores; gross debt of INR2.28 crores. Trade payable INR776 crores, debt risk INR632 crores, retention INR401 crores, inventory (including real estate) INR391 crores, mobilisation INR924 crores, unbilled revenue INR946 crores. Of mobilisation advance, 31% is interest-bearing at an average ~8%.
  • Capex: FY27 capex guidance reduced to INR220–250 crores from INR300 crores; Q1 capex was INR60 crores.
  • Working capital: Management expects the current quarter to be lower than the existing level of 119 days, with Assam receipts expected to keep it at the previous quarter’s level.
  • Dividend: The Board recommended a dividend for FY2025-26, subject to shareholder approval at the AGM. The record date is September 22, 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Labour cost permanence: Management stated increased labour prices do not come back down, making the higher cost a new base. Contracts without escalation clauses may not be contractually compensable; clients “are within their rights to say no.”
  • NGT impact: Unquantified potential impact on Q3 and execution.
  • Recurring external disruptions: Management noted events that previously occurred 1–2 months a year now occur every other month, including elections in various parts of the country.
  • Execution delays and penalties: Management stated Ahluwalia has never paid a penalty to any customer, with delays accepted as not attributable to the company; extensions of time have been granted by private and government clients.
  • Payment risk: State government payments (Assam) were delayed by elections; a central university project has certified but unpaid bills.
  • Fixed-price exposure: 10.34% of the order book is fixed-price.
  • Forward-looking statement risk (per company disclosure): Actual results may differ materially due to future changes in business, competitive environment, technology and application, and political, economic, legal and social conditions.
  • Order inflow slowdown: Management stated it is no longer aggressive on new orders due to material and labour price volatility, guiding to at least INR4,000–5,000 crores for the full year versus the earlier INR8,000 crores.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from early caution on rich valuations, growth challenges beyond FY24E, and heavy government project dependence (2023) to acknowledging execution challenges including delays, working-capital build-up, and near-term margin pressure from commodity and freight costs (2026). Tailwinds shifted from order book visibility, tender pipeline, and pristine balance sheet toward infrastructure capex growth, project diversification, overseas opportunities, and AI in EPC execution. Despite these evolving narratives, the stock experienced severe and prolonged declines, with most Buy-rated calls resulting in DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and returns as negative as -55%.

Fears that came true

  • Rich valuations at 14x FY25E EPS limited upside and the stock declined significantly over the following years, sustaining the Reduce concern across multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Growth outlook beyond FY24E proved challenging as the stock entered a prolonged drawdown from 2024 onward, with repeated DISAPPOINTMENT results on nearly every Buy call.
  • Execution challenges flagged in the last report — involving multiple authorities, utility shifting, clearances, and design changes causing delays and working-capital build-up — materialized as persistent negative returns throughout 2024–2026.

Optimism that failed

  • Robust order book of Rs 119 bn with three-year revenue visibility failed to prevent sustained declines and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes across nearly all subsequent Buy calls.
  • Solid execution with expected 20% YoY revenue growth in FY24E did not translate into positive stock returns, as the stock instead fell by over 50% in the following quarters.
  • Pristine balance sheet with debt-free status and RoE of ~15.5% provided no protection against catastrophic drawdowns exceeding 50%.
  • Overseas projects offering better margins, advances, and payment cycles did not prevent repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and continued negative returns.
  • Infrastructure capex growth and increasing project diversification failed to drive positive returns, with the stock remaining deeply negative through the last reports.
