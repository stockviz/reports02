Huhtamaki India — Q2 CY2026 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and challenges

  • Middle East crisis and supply-chain disruption. Management stated that the Middle East crisis weighed significantly on the market, caused significant supply-chain disruption and raw material cost variation. Most of the commodity impact was felt in Q2 (the crisis began at end-February, with some impact in March). Raw material procurement was described as “hard,” though not a “problem,” given the company’s scale and global footprint.
  • Raw material volatility and pass-through uncertainty. Pricing changes are dynamic, with weekly and monthly movements. Pass-through depends on contract structure — some customer contracts have monthly or quarterly index reviews, while extraordinary changes (such as those from the Middle East crisis) were passed on to most customers. Management said the pass-through process is “never a done story” while the political situation remains unsettled.
  • Export duty/tariff instability. Exports faced challenges from duty stability, which was a headwind initially but was seen as stabilising.
  • Customer inventory build-up. Some of the Q2 sales tailwind came from customers building inventory in anticipation of further price escalation. Management could not estimate the quantum and said the picture would be clearer at the Q3 call; this build-up may partly even out.
  • Elevated working capital. Inventories rose to Rs 3,956.7 million at June 30, 2026 from Rs 2,056.9 million at December 31, 2025, and trade receivables rose to Rs 6,767.2 million from Rs 5,537.2 million. Management attributed this to higher prices, higher volumes, and a conscious decision to hold higher inventory of key raw and packaging materials to avoid out-of-stock situations amid commodity volatility. In terms of days (DOI/DSO), ratios remained broadly constant, and there were no receivables realisation concerns.
  • One-time charges. H1 2026 included a non-recurring Rs 88 million depreciation adjustment relating to FY2024 and FY2025 (depreciation erroneously calculated on WDV instead of SLM), partly offset by a Rs 22 million deferred tax benefit. Q2 also included a provision for impairment of capital work-in-progress of Rs 20.1 million (per the cash flow statement).
  • Regulatory penalty (Silvassa). A demand notice dated September 10, 2026 from the Assistant Engineer, Dadra and Nagar Haveli Planning and Development Authority approved grant of an Occupancy Certificate for part of the factory premises at Survey No. 97, Umerkoi, Silvassa — subject to a penalty of Rs 17,54,823 for delay in obtaining the Occupancy Certificate. Other than the penalty amount, there is no impact on financial or other operations.
  • Competitive market. The markets remained “robust and very competitive,” growing at 4–5%.

2. Tailwinds and growth prospects

  • Broad-based growth. Q2 2026 net sales grew 23.1% YoY to Rs 7,286.0 million, driven roughly one-third each by price, volume and product mix. Growth was balanced between domestic and exports (exports are about 30% of sales volume, spanning Southeast Asia, Africa, Europe and the Americas). Volume growth was high single digit.
  • Margin expansion. Q2 EBITDA rose 55.1% YoY to Rs 764.4 million (margin 10.5% vs 8.3%), and EBIT rose 71.8% to Rs 621.6 million (margin 8.5% vs 6.1%). H1 EBITDA was Rs 1,385 million (10.5% margin vs 8.36%) and H1 EBIT before exceptional item was Rs 1,007.3 million (7.6% margin vs 6.2%). PBT before exceptional item rose 77.5% in Q2 and EPS before exceptional item rose 77.3% to Rs 5.79; H1 EPS was Rs 9.18, up 37.6%.
  • Customer preference for scale and reliability. Management said major customers increasingly value partners with wide footprint, raw material security and scale, especially during crises — benefiting larger players over smaller ones.
  • Product mix shifts. Growth in liquid and home care categories, which align with the company’s differentiation strategy; a good beverage season due to the late onset of monsoons.
  • Festive season. Management expects the upcoming festive season to support the market in the near term.
  • blueloop. Adoption remains below 30% of the market the company serves, but the same blueloop assets are running at over 70% utilisation for other products that would otherwise be outsourced. Management remains committed to promoting the technology and expects regulation and customer awareness to drive future volumes.
  • Sustainability initiatives. A solar captive generation plant for Khopoli is expected online in Q3 2026, to supply almost 50% of power for that plant; a decarbonisation roadmap for the 2030 Scope 1 and 2 commitment is prepared; multiple plants maintain Zero Liquid Discharge; Taloja implemented MVR wastewater recycling; rainwater harvesting and groundwater recharge strengthened; expanded recycled content and packaging lightweighting; three label plants and Khopoli are FSC-certified. TRIR reduced 40% year-to-date.
  • Capacity and productivity. Management said productivity improvements over the past three to four quarters have created enough capacity to cater to future volume growth for at least another couple of years, with capex focused on modernisation and organic growth.
  • Strong balance sheet. Net debt nil; cash and cash equivalents and other bank balances Rs 2,706 million; liquid mutual fund investments Rs 1,253 million; unutilised fund-based bank limits Rs 4,272 million. Gross debt/EBITDA improved on higher EBITDA with constant debt levels. The company plans to repay an intercompany borrowing early.

3. Key risks

  • Geopolitical and raw material risk. The Middle East crisis and resulting commodity inflation remain the principal external risks; the ability to keep passing costs through depends on contract terms and customer willingness.
  • Demand normalisation risk. Customer inventory build-up may unwind, and management explicitly declined to extrapolate the 23% growth, saying it would not expect 23% growth every quarter.
  • Export exposure. About 30% of sales volume is exported; export growth depends on competitiveness, product differentiation and import tariff regulations.
  • blueloop adoption risk. Adoption remains below 30%; the pace depends on customer awareness and regulatory change.
  • Working capital risk. Absolute inventory and receivable levels are elevated, though management stated days ratios are stable and there are no realisation challenges.
  • Regulatory/compliance risk. The Silvassa occupancy certificate penalty illustrates exposure to local authority compliance matters.
  • Accounting remediation. The depreciation error for FY2024 and FY2025 was identified in Q1 2026 and fully rectified in the Q1 2026 financial results; management concluded the amounts were not material to the respective years.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. Management’s own disclaimer notes actual results may differ materially due to internal and external factors.

4. Management guidance versus observed business performance

  • Profitable growth strategy. Management repeatedly reaffirmed the strategy of profitable growth, selective market participation, disciplined capital allocation and accountability, stating it is “now delivering results” consistent with prior quarters. Observed performance supports this: Q2 net sales +23.1%, EBITDA +55.1%, EBIT +71.8%, PBT before exceptional item +77.5%, EPS before exceptional item +77.3%.
  • Volume versus profitability. Management had previously sacrificed volume for profitable growth. On the call, management said the product and customer mix has “stabilised” and that it is not reverting to volume-for-its-own-sake; volume growth was high single digit with profit also improving. Management indicated it would be satisfied growing in line with the market (which it described as growing 4–5%), rather than repeating 23% growth.
  • Margin sustainability. Asked whether ~10% EBITDA margins are sustainable, management declined to guide to a specific level, citing external tailwinds and headwinds, but reiterated commitment to the profitable growth strategy and continuous productivity and efficiency improvement, noting the rate of improvement may diminish over time.
  • Capacity. Management said productivity gains mean capacity is adequate for future growth for at least another couple of years, with capex directed at modernisation; no exact capacity utilisation figure was disclosed due to competitive sensitivity.
  • Revenue run-rate. Asked whether Rs 750 crore quarterly revenue is a reasonable assumption, management declined to confirm, noting the figure includes raw material price pass-through and depends on customer category growth; it would not specify whether Rs 750 crore or Rs 650 crore.
  • Inventory normalisation. Management said it cannot estimate the customer inventory build-up and expects the Q3 call to be more reflective of inventory movement.
  • Cash deployment. Management said there are no specific disclosures on inorganic plans; cash is being invested in market instruments generating returns at or above benchmark indices, with focus on organic growth, modernisation and capital discipline. On promoter stake monetisation, management directed the question to the parent company.
  • Land monetisation. Management declined to disclose which land assets beyond Daman are being considered, noting Thane was completed a couple of years ago and future disclosures would follow if anything happens.
  • Reporting schedule. Q3 and Q1–Q3 2026 financial results are scheduled for October 27, 2026.
