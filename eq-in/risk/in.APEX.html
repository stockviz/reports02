Apex Frozen Foods Limited — Q1 FY27 Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Volume decline: Total shrimp sales fell to 2,624 MT in Q1 FY27 from 3,015 MT in Q1 FY26. Management attributed this to labour shortages, mainly in April and May, and war-led transportation disruptions across certain export markets.
  • Labour shortage: The summer heat during April caused labour shortages that affected production. Management stated that without this, Q1 volumes should have exceeded 3,000 MT. The issue was largely resolved toward the end of Q1.
  • Freight and logistics: Ocean freight costs more than doubled between Q4 of the prior year and the current period due to war-led disruptions. Container equipment shortages also affected shipments. Management said these disruptions were still continuing at the time of the call.
  • EU/UK weakness: The EU and UK together contributed 25% of shrimp sales versus 39% in Q1 FY26. Sales were impacted by war-led transport disruptions and regulatory testing/certification requirements at origin in India. EU volumes that should have shipped in Q1 spilled over into the subsequent quarter due to clearance delays.
  • Rising farm-gate prices: Farm-gate prices rose roughly 6–7% over the three to four weeks preceding the call, which management flagged as a factor that could slightly affect margins.
  • Non-tariff barriers: Even with the India–UK FTA effective from 15 July, non-tariff barriers persist, including testing of 50% of Indian shrimp consignments arriving in destination markets. Management said these barriers limit volume growth to the UK and EU.
  • Tariff refund uncertainty: No tariff refunds had been received, and management stated there was no clarity on whether or when refunds would come, given ongoing US legal and regulatory processes.
  • Capacity utilisation constraint: Utilisation was 38% in Q1 FY27 versus 39% in Q1 FY26. Management identified labour and order-book issues as the main constraints on ramping utilisation, alongside some raw material supply tightness pending the new crop.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Realisation improvement: Average shrimp realisation rose 15% year-on-year to almost INR 930 per kilo in Q1 FY27 (versus around INR 812 in Q1 FY26), supported by improving global shrimp prices, a higher share of value-added products, and favourable currency movement.
  • Profitability expansion: EBITDA rose 79% year-on-year to INR 33 crores, with margin expanding to 12.7% from 7.1%. PAT rose 138% to INR 22 crores, with PAT margin at 8.4% versus 3.5%. Net revenue was broadly flat at INR 257 crores versus INR 258 crores.
  • US market recovery: The USA accounted for 70% of shrimp sales versus 54% in Q1 FY26, with US sales up 13% year-on-year and 121% quarter-on-quarter. Management attributed this to softening tariff-related uncertainty after the 50% tariff was removed and 10% finalised, giving US buyers more certainty.
  • Value-added / RTE mix: RTE was 16% of Q1 volume versus 15% a year earlier. Management expects RTE to reach 18–20% of volumes in the current year. RTE realisation rose to USD 12.05 per kilo from USD 11.2. The margin differential between RTE and RTC is at least USD 0.50 per kilo, with some products higher.
  • New products: Proprietary RTE and RTC products developed with specific customers are already commercialised and shipping. Management expects these specific products to contribute around 3–4% of total sales, roughly 300–400 MT, growing to at least 500 MT annually.
  • New geographies: Business with Japan was initiated after more than a decade. Russia is expected to commence by end of Q2 or Q3. Australia remains at the discussion stage pending customer audits.
  • FTAs: The India–UK FTA took effect from 15 July, with increased inquiries but results yet to materialise. Management expects the EU FTA around December or early January, and views it as the larger opportunity. Full FTA effects are typically seen after a minimum of one year.
  • Order book: Management described the order book as good through the middle of Q3.
  • Balance sheet: Debt levels were described as prudently managed and comfortable, with focus on disciplined working capital management.

3. Key Risks

  • Logistics and geopolitical disruption: War-led transport disruptions and elevated freight costs remain ongoing and could affect volumes and margins.
  • Farm-gate price inflation: Rising farm-gate prices could pressure margins if not offset by realisations or volume-driven cost efficiency.
  • Regulatory and non-tariff barriers: Testing and certification requirements at origin, and destination-market testing of 50% of Indian consignments, constrain EU and UK volumes.
  • Trade policy uncertainty: ADD and CVD reviews are pending — antidumping final determination expected around September, countervailing duty announcement expected around December. A CVD reduction of 5.77% is hoped for, but outcomes could go either way; a reduction would trigger refunds, while an increase would require additional payments.
  • Tariff refund uncertainty: No clarity on whether or when US tariff refunds would be received.
  • Raw material supply: Some shortage of raw material supply exists, expected to ease as the second crop arrives.
  • Disease and weather: Farm-specific disease issues or distress harvests, and weather/climate patterns, could affect the sector.
  • Realisation ceiling: Management noted that commodity and RTC pricing has limits beyond which consumption and volumes are affected, citing the FY22 experience when high prices were followed by volume declines in FY23.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Volume guidance: Management stated it had guided around 12,000 MT for the year at the start of the year and reiterated this on the Q4 call. Q1 FY27 actual volume was 2,624 MT, below the 3,000-plus MT management said should have been achieved absent the labour issue. Management maintained the 12,000 MT expectation for the year, contingent on stable supply and reasonable trade, non-trade, and logistics conditions.
  • Margin guidance: Management expects margins to remain broadly stable, supported by stable export realisations in dollar terms, a depreciated rupee, and cost-efficiency measures, though slightly affected by higher freight costs and farm-gate prices. Volume growth is expected to support efficient costing. Management cautioned that unforeseen international trade and logistical issues could intervene.
  • Realisation guidance: Management expects Q2 realisations around similar levels to Q1’s INR 930 per kilo.
  • RTE guidance: Management expects RTE to reach 18–20% of volumes in the current year, versus 16% in Q1 FY27 and 15% in Q1 FY26.
  • Capacity utilisation guidance: Management expects utilisation to be consistently maintained above 35–40% through the year, with a next-stage target of 14,000–15,000 MT in FY28–FY29, subject to global improvements and no new tariff issues.
  • FTA timing: Management expects full FTA effects after a minimum of one year, with EU FTA implementation hoped for around December or early January, and benefits potentially visible by Q1 FY28.
  • Observed vs. guided: Q1 FY27 revenue was flat year-on-year despite the volume decline, because realisation growth offset it — consistent with management’s emphasis on realisation and mix. Profitability exceeded the prior-year level substantially, reflecting the realisation and cost-efficiency drivers management cited. The volume shortfall against the 12,000 MT annual trajectory was acknowledged and attributed to specific, identified factors rather than a change in demand.
