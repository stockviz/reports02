JSW Cement: Recent Corporate Announcements

Headwinds and Challenges

  • Operating EBITDA declined despite volume growth. In Q1 FY27, operating EBITDA fell 7% YoY to ₹298.6 crore even as revenue rose 22% YoY to ₹1,896 crore and total volumes grew 15% YoY to 3.81 million tonnes. Operating EBITDA per tonne was ₹784 in Q1 FY27; excluding the North operations, operating EBITDA rose 4% YoY to ₹336 crore, equating to ₹979 per tonne. This indicates the newly commissioned North operations diluted consolidated profitability during ramp-up.
  • North region ramp-up is still partial. The Nagaur integrated unit (3.3 MTPA clinker, 2.5 MTPA grinding) was commissioned on March 30, 2026, with sales commencing April 2026 into Rajasthan and Haryana. It achieved an average utilisation rate of 55% in Q1 FY27.
  • GGBS volume growth was modest. GGBS volumes rose 3% YoY to 1.33 million tonnes, versus 27% YoY growth in cement volumes to 2.34 million tonnes.
  • West Bengal incentive dispute. The Company’s application for Industrial Promotional Assistance under WBSSIS 2013 for the Salboni plant faced rejection of the final Registration Certificate (RC-II) by the District Industries Centre. The Calcutta High Court quashed the first rejection and directed reconsideration, but the DIC rejected it again. The Company’s writ has been put on hold pending clarity on the Revocation Act enacted in April 2025, which retrospectively annuls grants under state incentive schemes from 1993 to 2021. The Company filed a writ challenging the Act and believes it has a good case to recover an outstanding claim balance of ₹339.87 crore as at March 31, 2026. It carries an expected credit loss provision of ₹49.18 crore as at June 30, 2026, including an additional ₹11.02 crore recognised in the previous quarter/year under exceptional items.
  • Exceptional item in the year-ago quarter. The CCPS conversion resulted in a valuation difference of ₹1,466.38 crore, disclosed as an exceptional item in Q1 FY26 and FY26, which drove the large reported loss in that comparative period.
  • Debt and funding. Net debt stood at ₹3,856 crore as at June 30, 2026. The Board approved raising up to ₹500 crore through rated, listed Non-Convertible Debentures on a private placement basis, with the Finance Committee authorised to finalise terms.
  • Risk factors disclosed. The Company’s forward-looking statement notes risks including fluctuations in earnings, ability to manage growth, intense competition in the cement industry, wage increases in India, ability to attract and retain skilled professionals, ability to manage internal operations, reduced demand for cement, withdrawal of fiscal governmental incentives, political instability, unauthorised use of intellectual property, and general economic conditions affecting the industry. A separate disclosure cites changes in cement demand, fluctuations in raw material and energy prices, competition, regulatory changes, and environmental considerations as factors that could cause actual results to differ materially.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong volume and revenue growth. Q1 FY27 total volumes rose 15% YoY to 3.81 million tonnes; cement volumes rose 27% YoY to 2.34 million tonnes. Revenue from operations rose 22% YoY to ₹1,896 crore. Growth was driven by continued momentum in existing regions and the ramp-up of the North region.
  • Nagaur expansion. The Company commissioned the Nagaur integrated unit on March 30, 2026 and commenced sales in April 2026. It is progressing installation of WHRS, OLBC and AFR co-processing systems, an additional 1.0 MTPA grinding capacity, and has begun work on an additional 2.5 MTPA grinding unit at Nagaur, which will take total grinding capacity there to 6.0 MTPA.
  • Renewable energy expansion. The Company added 56 MW of wind capacity during the quarter, taking total renewable capacity (including WHRS) to 112 MW as at June 30, 2026. Management targets increasing green power capacity to 224 MW by FY27.
  • Capacity growth pipeline. The Company reports a ~2.8x increase in overall capacity from 24.1 MTPA as at FY26 to a total approved growth of 43.3 MTPA, with future potential up to 68.3 MTPA. It currently has operational grinding capacity of 24.10 MTPA across 8 units and clinker capacity of 9.74 MTPA across 4 units.
  • Market position in GGBS. JSW Cement is India’s largest manufacturer of GGBS, with an all-India market share of approximately 84% in GGBS sales.
  • Demand outlook. Management projects cement demand growth to outpace capacity additions, with a demand CAGR of 7.5% to 8.5% versus a supply CAGR of ~5.8% (FY26P to FY31P). It cites strong demand from flagship government programs including PMAY, PM GatiShakti, Bharatmala, and Sagarmala.
  • Sustainability positioning. The Company reports the lowest CO2 emission intensity in the industry at 338 kg CO2 per tonne of cementitious materials in Q1 FY27. It received the Global ESG Award 2026 (Diamond) in the Circular Economy category; Shiva Cement received the Odisha Best Employer Brand Award and Best Innovation Award (EHS); and the Vijayanagar Plant received the GOLD AWARD at the Green Enviro Environment Award & Summit.
  • Credit rating. The Company reports an upgraded long-term credit rating of IND AA- with a Stable outlook.
  • JSW One Platforms IPO. JSW Steel’s board approved participation in the proposed IPO of JSW One Platforms Limited as a Promoter Selling Shareholder, offering shares aggregating up to ₹811 crore. JSW Cement noted it will make further announcements on the matter once considered by its board or authorised committee. JSW One Platforms is listed among JSW Cement’s joint ventures in the auditor’s review report.
  • Dividend. The 20th AGM on July 31, 2026 approved a dividend of ₹0.50 per equity share of face value ₹10 (5%).

Key Risks

  • Profitability pressure from new capacity. The North operations reduced consolidated operating EBITDA per tonne in Q1 FY27, and the unit is operating at 55% utilisation, indicating further ramp-up risk.
  • Regulatory and incentive risk. The West Bengal Revocation Act and the unresolved RC-II dispute create uncertainty over recovery of the ₹339.87 crore outstanding claim balance, with a ₹49.18 crore provision carried as at June 30, 2026.
  • Tax regime transition. The Company has decided to adopt the New tax regime under Section 115BAA from FY27 onwards, following Finance Act 2026 amendments. A reduction in net deferred tax liabilities of ₹211.21 crore (consolidated) and ₹218.92 crore (standalone) was recognised in the previous quarter/year ended March 31, 2026.
  • Labour code impact. The Labour Codes notified on November 21, 2025 led to a reassessment of past service cost liability, with an excess provision of ₹6.58 crore (consolidated) and ₹6.61 crore (standalone) reversed in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 under exceptional items.
  • Governance signal from AGM voting. At the 20th AGM, the resolution for re-appointment of Mr. Seshagiri Rao MVS as a Director retiring by rotation received 93.0987% votes in favour and 6.9013% against, with public institutions voting 61.1762% in favour and 38.8238% against. All other resolutions passed with over 99.99% support.
  • Auditor scope limitations. The auditor did not review the interim financial results of 4 subsidiaries (total revenue ₹331.58 crore, net loss after tax ₹20.54 crore for Q1 FY27) and relied on other auditors for 1 joint venture and 1 associate (share of profit after tax ₹12.72 crore). One subsidiary’s results were not reviewed by its auditor and were certified by management, though deemed not material.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Capacity expansion guidance. Management targets a ~2.8x increase in overall capacity from 24.1 MTPA as at FY26 to a total approved growth of 43.3 MTPA, with future potential up to 68.3 MTPA. Observed progress: the Nagaur integrated unit was commissioned on March 30, 2026 with 3.3 MTPA clinker and 2.5 MTPA grinding capacity; sales commenced April 2026; additional grinding capacity work at Nagaur is underway to reach 6.0 MTPA total grinding capacity there.
  • Green power guidance. Management targets 224 MW of green power capacity by FY27. Observed progress: 56 MW of wind capacity was added during Q1 FY27, taking total renewable capacity (including WHRS) to 112 MW as at June 30, 2026.
  • Demand outlook. Management projects cement demand CAGR of 7.5% to 8.5% versus supply CAGR of ~5.8% (FY26P to FY31P). Observed performance: Q1 FY27 cement volumes grew 27% YoY and total volumes grew 15% YoY, with revenue up 22% YoY.
  • Profitability. Management reported operating EBITDA of ₹298.6 crore in Q1 FY27, down 7% YoY, with operating EBITDA per tonne of ₹784. Excluding North operations, operating EBITDA rose 4% YoY to ₹336 crore, or ₹979 per tonne. Total EBITDA including other income was ₹372.3 crore. PAT was ₹153.4 crore.
  • Capex. The Company incurred capex (including maintenance capex) of ₹337 crore during Q1 FY27.
  • Investor engagement. Management is participating in the 2026 Jefferies India Forum on September 18, 2026 in Gurgaon and the PL Capital Mid & Small Cap Conference 2026 on September 29, 2026 in Mumbai, with no Unpublished Price Sensitive Information proposed to be shared.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative moved from a cautious Neutral stance to an Accumulate call, with energy/fuel costs, GGBS volume growth, North-India ramp-up, and raw-material availability remaining persistent concerns. Initially the emphasis was on structural strengths such as GGBS leadership, slag-supply synergies, and regional diversification; by the last report these were tempered by Q1FY27 EBITDA pressure from Nagaur start-up costs, Middle-East-driven fuel costs, and delayed Punjab expansion. The upgrade to Accumulate was based on actual cost-saving progress, improving Nagaur utilisation, expected North breakeven, and continued capacity expansion.

Fears that came true

  • Energy dependence and fuel price volatility materialized as power & fuel costs/t rose 25% YoY after the Middle East crisis, hurting Q1FY27 EBITDA and contributing to the last report’s -2.1% DISAPPOINTMENT.
  • Raw material availability risks materialized through temporary slag availability issues at Dolvi and aggregates availability issues in the South, which dented GGBS volumes.
  • Delay in capacity commissioning/lower utilisation in the North materialized as the Punjab expansion was delayed and North operations had not yet reached EBITDA breakeven.
  • Regulatory compliance risks materialized as the Punjab expansion was delayed due to pending environmental clearance and upcoming state elections.

Optimism that failed

  • Long-term slag supply agreements and JSW Group synergies did not prevent temporary slag availability issues at Dolvi from affecting GGBS volumes.
  • Green energy, WHRS, and digital logistics cost optimization did not stop power & fuel costs/t from rising 25% YoY in Q1FY27.
  • North-India capacity expansion as a diversification/growth driver failed to deliver early profitability, with North operations still below EBITDA breakeven.
  • Growing demand for eco-friendly/low-carbon cement and GGBS did not prevent soft GGBS volume growth due to RMC closures, aggregates issues, and election/labour disruptions.
