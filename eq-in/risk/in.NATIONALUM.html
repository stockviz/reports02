NATIONALUM: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Pottangi Bauxite Mines access: Approach road work for the Pottangi Bauxite mines is pending due to local agitation. Operationalisation of Pottangi Bauxite Mines is listed among management’s immediate priorities, indicating it has not yet been fully brought into operation.
  • Aluminium price volatility: Recovery of Middle Eastern production, higher exports from China and Indonesia, and energy prices and geopolitical developments are expected to keep prices volatile in the near term.
  • Supply-demand dynamics: India shows a persistent supply-demand gap, and the global aluminium market is expected to remain in deficit.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • 5th Stream Alumina Refinery: Expansion with 1 MTPA capacity, with pre-commissioning started from June 2026. Stabilisation and ramp-up of this stream is a stated immediate priority.
  • Brownfield Aluminium Smelter expansion: 0.5 MTPA capacity at Anugola, Odisha, with pre-project activities under progress. A Technology Licensing Agreement was signed with Emirates Global Aluminium (EGA) in Dubai on 07/09/2026 to deploy EGA’s DX+ Ultra smelting technology. This expansion is expected to move the company towards an overall aluminium production capacity of about 1 million tonnes per annum.
  • Captive Power Plant for the new smelter: 4x270 MW = 1080 MW.
  • Captive coal capacity expansion and renewable power augmentation: Listed among immediate priorities.
  • Demand drivers: Growth is driven by infrastructure, renewable energy, power transmission, transportation, construction, and manufacturing.
  • Strategic focus areas: Management highlighted operational excellence, global and domestic business scenarios, strengthening raw material and energy security, expansion and the next phase of growth, strategic diversification and critical minerals, technology innovation and digital transformation, sustainability and ESG, Corporate Social Responsibility, support to MSEs, and inclusive growth.

3. Key Risks

  • Local agitation disrupting access infrastructure for Pottangi Bauxite Mines, delaying operationalisation.
  • Near-term aluminium price volatility from Middle Eastern production recovery, Chinese and Indonesian exports, energy prices, and geopolitical developments.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk on the 5th Stream Alumina Refinery, the brownfield smelter expansion, and the captive power plant.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • FY 2025-26 performance: NALCO achieved highest-ever operational and financial performance — Revenue from Operations of ₹17,843 crore, EBITDA of ₹8,613 crore, Profit Before Tax of ₹7,767 crore, and Profit After Tax of ₹5,816 crore, with CAPEX of ₹2,068 crore. A total recommended dividend payment of ₹2,112.12 crore (230% of paid-up share capital) was made.
  • Q1 FY2026-27 performance: Revenue from operations of ₹5,302 crore and Profit After Tax of ₹2,002 crore.
  • Dividend actions: For FY 2025-26, interim dividends of Rs.4/-, Rs.4.50/-, and Rs.2/- per share (totalling Rs.10.50/-) were paid, and a final dividend of Re.1.00/- per share (20% on face value of Rs.5/-) was recommended, subject to shareholder approval at the 45th AGM. The total dividend amounts to Rs.11.50/- per share. The record date for the final dividend was fixed as Monday, 24th August, 2026.
  • Stated immediate priorities: Stabilisation and ramp-up of the 5th Stream Alumina Refinery, operationalisation of Pottangi Bauxite Mines, expansion of captive coal capacity, augmentation of renewable power, and continued progress on the proposed aluminium smelter and captive power expansion.
  • Management outlook on the EGA agreement: With EGA’s DX+ Ultra technology, the Anugola smelter expansion project is expected to emerge as one of the most efficient aluminium smelters in India, support growth and expansion plans, strengthen India’s aluminium ecosystem, contribute towards India’s economic growth while supporting greater environmental efficiency and the vision of a Viksit Bharat, and help meet growing demand in strategic and emerging sectors of the Indian economy.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from a purely technical, bearish February 2020 call—resting on bearish engulfing, Nifty/metal trend-line breaks and metal-sector weakness with no tailwinds—that failed as NATIONALUM rallied about 1,031%, to a fundamentally balanced November 2026 view weighing strong aluminium EBIT (+73% YoY) and LME assumptions against alumina-sector cost pressure and surplus risk. Brokers migrated from sector-level technical pessimism to company-specific cyclical fundamentals (input costs, refinery ramp-up, captive coal, cash position), while recent 2026 Hold/Reduce/Accumulate calls turned cautious again amid negative returns of roughly -16.7% to -4.9%.

Fears that came true

  • Persistent input-cost inflation (caustic soda, calcined petroleum coke and fuel oil) materialized, driving alumina segment EBIT down 55% YoY in Q2FY27 alongside negative returns such as the Reduce/Hold calls at -16.7%.
  • A roughly 1.6 million tonne global alumina surplus in FY27 plus incremental Indonesian refinery capacity created supply pressure on realisations, consistent with recent weak actuals.
  • The roughly 15% wage revision added to employee expenses, deepening cost pressure reflected in recent negative Hold/Reduce outcomes.
  • Pottangi bauxite mine execution bottlenecks—land acquisition, tree-felling permissions and local resistance—persisted, threatening alumina cost savings as recent Accumulate/Hold calls returned roughly -10.3% to -4.9%.

Optimism that failed

  • The call for alumina realisations near INR 35,000/t in H2FY27 failed amid the FY27 global alumina surplus and Indonesian supply pressure, consistent with the 55% YoY alumina EBIT decline.
  • Expected normalisation of input costs and better coal availability failed, as elevated caustic soda/CPC/fuel-oil costs persisted and pressured alumina margins.
  • Optimism that the 1 mtpa alumina refinery would commission by late-December 2026/early-January 2027 and add ~0.2 mt in FY27 is at risk from flagged commissioning and ramp-up delays.
  • The expectation that Pottangi bauxite development would improve ore quality and cut caustic soda use failed to take hold given land-acquisition, tree-felling and local-resistance bottlenecks.
