Astec LifeSciences — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

The operating environment for B2B agrochemicals, particularly enterprise products and CDMO, remained challenging in the first half of FY 2025-26, impacted by destocking, subdued demand, and price corrections. The enterprise segment faced pricing pressure, especially in key fungicide molecules, due to global oversupply, while the CDMO segment witnessed subdued volumes due to destocking. Export markets saw gradual recovery in volumes but continued pricing compression.

The global macroeconomic environment during FY 2025-26 remained volatile, with persistent geopolitical tensions, supply chain disruptions, and evolving trade policies. Conflicts in West Asia and Eastern Europe disrupted energy markets and critical shipping routes such as the Red Sea corridor, resulting in elevated freight costs and volatility in crude-linked intermediates. Elevated channel inventories across developed markets, particularly from prior-year overstocking, resulted in delayed order replenishment and muted demand for active ingredients, particularly in fungicides and herbicides.

Global agrochemical demand trends were further influenced by destocking by global innovators and distributors, farmer affordability constraints in certain markets, weather variability impacting crop cycles, and stringent regulatory frameworks in Europe and other developed markets. The shift towards supply chain diversification (“China+1”) continued, but entails near-term cost pressures due to compliance, qualification, and supply chain realignment.

The company reported a Loss After Tax of ₹(8,087.62) Lakh in FY 2025-26 on a consolidated basis, compared to ₹(13,471.17) Lakh in FY 2024-25. EBITDA/Sales was 0.1% in FY 2025-26 versus -15.9% in FY 2024-25, and operating margin was -11.0% versus -28.8%. Return on Capital Employed was -9.3% and Return on Net Worth was -25.8%. The Board did not recommend any final dividend for FY 2025-26.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

India continued to demonstrate strong macroeconomic resilience during FY 2025-26, supported by robust domestic demand, sustained government capital expenditure, and stable financial conditions. India’s real GDP growth remained strong at approximately 7.0%–7.4%. The agriculture sector benefited from favourable monsoon conditions, healthy reservoir levels, stable agricultural output, record foodgrain production, improving rural liquidity, and government support through agricultural and rural-focused policies.

Structural tailwinds for the Indian agrochemical sector include low per hectare agrochemical consumption compared to global averages, a rising shift towards scientific and precision farming, increasing adoption of crop protection and yield-enhancing solutions, and strong export competitiveness of Indian manufacturers.

The company’s CDMO and Enterprise businesses witnessed a strong recovery during the year, supported by improved performance in both export and domestic markets, driven primarily by higher volumes and favorable pricing trends across key products. Export sales grew by 6.7% YoY, while domestic sales grew 40.6% YoY. CDMO contributed 52% of total sales in FY 2025-26, with the Enterprise segment accounting for the remaining 48%. Geographically, exports constituted 62% and domestic sales 38% of total revenue. Gross margin improved significantly to 33.4% in FY 2025-26, compared to 22.1% in FY 2024-25. The company achieved EBITDA break-even for the year.

The global agrochemicals market is projected to grow at a CAGR of around 3.4%, with market size expected to rise from USD 223.03 billion to around $301.34 billion by 2033. The global CDMO market is poised to grow at a CAGR of 10% up to 2029. Globally, around 22 active ingredients are expected to go off patent over the next 10 years, with an estimated market size of around $4.1 billion by 2026.

The company’s R&D Center in Rabale, named the “Adi Godrej Center for Chemical Research and Development,” has seen its projects increase fivefold since inception, enhancing commercialization of new products. The company is expanding into adjacent segments such as specialty chemicals and advanced intermediates for pharmaceuticals. The company is future ready with capacity expansion and a new Herbicides facility. The company managed to improve its CDMO business by 2.5x in H2 of FY 2025-26.

Godrej Agrovet Limited, the Holding Company, increased its shareholding in the company to 1,49,35,245 Equity shares (67.03%) as on 31st March 2026, from 1,26,99,054 Equity Shares (64.75%) as on 31st March 2025.

3. Key Risks

  • Global trade and regulatory uncertainty: Escalating trade tensions, evolving tariff structures, and changing regulatory regimes across key export markets may impact demand visibility, pricing, and supply chain continuity. Given Astec’s significant export exposure, policy shifts can materially affect revenues.
  • Raw material dependence and supply chain risks: A meaningful portion of raw materials continues to be sourced from overseas markets, exposing the company to risks related to availability, logistics, geopolitical developments, and price volatility.
  • Weather variability and agricultural cyclicality: Agrochemical demand is closely linked to weather patterns, pest incidence, and crop acreage. Erratic monsoons and climate volatility can lead to demand fluctuations.
  • Foreign currency volatility and interest rates: Export-led revenues expose the company to currency fluctuations. Sharp movements in exchange rates or interest costs can impact profitability.
  • Customer concentration risks in CDMO and pricing volatility in key molecules are identified exposures.
  • Financial risk ratios: Debt Equity Ratio was 1.15 in FY 2025-26 (2.36 in FY 2024-25); Debtors’ Turnover Ratio was 2.25 (3.14); Inventory Turnover Ratio was 3.01 (2.14); Current Ratio was 0.89 (0.93).

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

The management stated that FY 2025-26 was a year that required calibrated responses and disciplined execution, and that the company delivered a strong turnaround, achieving EBITDA break-even for the year. This is consistent with the observed EBITDA of ₹54 Lakh in FY 2025-26 versus ₹(6,058) Lakh in FY 2024-25.

Management indicated that recovery was driven largely by volume growth rather than pricing, and that improved volumes, better realizations, and higher capacity utilization across Enterprise and CDMO portfolios positioned the company for sustained recovery. Observed results show sales of ₹44,815 Lakh in FY 2025-26 versus ₹38,130 Lakh in FY 2024-25, with export sales growth of 6.7% YoY and domestic sales growth of 40.6% YoY.

Management stated that the company faced challenges in H1 due to unforeseen global tensions, but managed to improve its CDMO business by 2.5x in H2 of FY 2025-26. Management also stated that the company is future ready with capacity expansion and a new Herbicides facility which will yield tangible returns, and that effective utilization of the new R&D Centre will boost efforts towards adding new customers in the CDMO segment.

Management’s outlook noted that while near-term challenges persist, the medium-to-long-term outlook for the agrochemical sector remains positive, driven by growing global demand for crop protection solutions, increasing focus on food security and farm productivity, shift toward diversified and resilient supply chains, and rising demand for CDMO and contract manufacturing partnerships. The company stated it will continue to focus on building scale in CDMO, strengthening the enterprise products portfolio, and enhancing operational excellence.

The company reported that the Secretarial Audit Report for FY 2025-26 is an unqualified report, and the Cost Audit Report for FY 2024-25 was an unqualified report. ICRA re-affirmed credit ratings of [ICRA] AA- (Negative) for long-term facilities and [ICRA] A1+ for short-term facilities and the Commercial Paper Programme.

At the 32nd AGM held on 31st July 2026, all three resolutions (adoption of financial statements for FY 2025-26, re-appointment of Mr. Ashok V. Hiremath as Director, and ratification of remuneration of M/s. Tapan Gaitonde & Co. as Cost Auditor for FY 2026-27) were passed with requisite majority.
