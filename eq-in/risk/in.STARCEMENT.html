Star Cement — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Weak demand and weather disruption. Q1 FY27 (quarter ended 30 June 2026) saw muted demand. Management attributed the softness in the Northeast to the Assam elections in April (Assam accounts for roughly 60–70% of the Northeast market) and the onset of the monsoon. Q2 FY27 has been worse operationally: management stated Assam was “completely flooded” and “completely shut down in many areas” in July, with July volume degrowth of about 12%. Management expects little volume traction in Q2, with recovery only from Q3/Q4.

Cost inflation. Fuel cost rose to about INR 1.55 per unit in Q1 FY27 from INR 1.33 in Q4 FY26. The increase was driven by the diversion of Coal India FSA coal to the power plant amid a surge in power requirement, forcing the company to buy costlier spot coal (about 30% of the fuel mix, with FSA at about 45%). Packing material (PP bag) costs rose, linked to international turmoil and war conditions, and management said these are hard to predict. Shutdown expenses at one kiln also weighed on the quarter.

Subsidy reduction. EBITDA fell to about INR 203 crores in Q1 FY27 from INR 230 crores a year earlier, and PAT to INR 74 crores from INR 98 crores, mainly on reduced subsidy, higher packing costs and shutdown expenses. Per-tonne EBITDA was INR 1,497 versus INR 1,774. The GST rate reduction from 28% to 18% (effective last year) caused a hit of about INR 40 crores in Q1 alone on the subsidy side. Separately, the Assam government issued a notification capping the remaining incentive entitlement on a proportionate annual basis over the balance eligible period; management expects the annual subsidy to fall from about INR 145 crores to about INR 115 crores. Outstanding Assam incentive receivables were stated at about INR 130 crores against a total entitlement of INR 794 crores.

Regional supply and competitive context. Management noted clinker is now also coming into the Northeast from outside, which may cause clinker sale volumes to be stagnant or degrow 5–10% in FY27 versus FY26. It expects about 1 million tonnes each of capacity addition by two players over the next three years, with other peers doing only brownfield debottlenecking of about 0.5–0.6 million tonnes.

West Bengal sand mining. A crackdown on illegal sand mining in West Bengal contributed to muted cement demand there. Management does not know whether the state plans to formalise sand mining, but expects mining to reopen in some form after the rainy season.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Expected pent-up demand. Management expects demand to improve in H2 FY27 once floods subside, with pent-up demand released in Q3 and Q4 leading to higher-than-usual growth. It noted September FY27 should be clean of both floods and the regulatory change that depressed September FY26 (the GST revision from 28% to 18%).

North India expansion. The Rajasthan (Nimbol) project is progressing: plant land purchase is complete, public hearing was scheduled for end-August 2026, EC expected in the first week of October 2026, and ground work planned from mid-October to November 2026. Management indicated an 18–20 month construction period, implying commissioning around Q4 FY28 or Q1 FY29. Vendor quotations were being called, with locking expected by end-August or early September 2026. The company has received the standard approved incentive package for the Nimbol clinker plant, involving capital subsidy and SGST benefit. The North expansion comprises about 3 million tonnes of grinding and 3.3 million tonnes of clinker in Rajasthan plus 2 million tonnes in Jhajjar, Haryana, with total capex estimated at about INR 2,600–2,700 crores (about INR 2,900 crores including GST).

West Bengal optionality. Management is awaiting West Bengal’s industrial policy (expected around 15 August 2026). If benefits are favourable, a brownfield grinding expansion at the existing Siliguri plant may make sense, given available plant land and railway siding, lower capex, and SGST benefit on existing sales. This could lead to reconsidering or redirecting the planned 2 million tonne Begusarai (Bihar) grinding capex. Management said a split of 1 million tonnes each in West Bengal and Bihar likely would not make sense due to economies of scale, including the railway siding.

Cost-saving initiatives. A railway siding at Silchar is expected by October–November 2026, enabling fuller operation of Silchar capacities and logistics savings for South Assam and other Northeast markets. Wagon tippler installation at Siliguri is expected to reduce clinker and fly ash transport costs, with about INR 150 per tonne savings on Siliguri sales. EV introduction on some routes is planned over the next 5–6 months. Management also noted one-off items (donation and some incentive) will reduce, giving about INR 60–70 per tonne saving next quarter.

Green energy and non-cement business. Green share was about 3% in Q1 (including WHRS). Solar and group captive opportunities are being evaluated, with details expected in Q3/Q4. The Building Solutions division (AAC and RMC) is targeting about INR 150 crores of revenue on an ARR basis by Q4 FY27, though sluggish demand means the company is not currently at that run rate.

3. Key Risks

  • Monsoon/flood risk: Q2 FY27 volumes are exposed to severe Assam flooding; management sees limited Q2 traction and expects recovery only in H2.
  • Fuel and input cost volatility: Fuel cost guidance depends on receiving more FSA coal rakes; PP bag prices depend on international turmoil, war and crude oil prices, which management describes as beyond its control.
  • Subsidy/tax regime changes: The Assam incentive cap reduces annual subsidy; the GST reduction from 28% to 18% continues to weigh on reported profitability until the base period laps (from September onwards).
  • Project execution and approval risk: Rajasthan timelines depend on EC receipt (expected first week of October 2026) and vendor finalisation; capex estimates remain subject to final machinery specs, with a possible INR 100–200 crore variance.
  • Competitive supply: Inbound clinker into the Northeast and planned capacity additions by two players could pressure clinker volumes and pricing.
  • Regional disruptions: West Bengal sand mining crackdown has dampened demand; management expects normalisation only after the rains.
  • Capex funding: Management is not actively considering a QIP at present, expecting to manage the North capex with net debt/EBITDA of about 1.5–1.6x, but would consider fundraising at an opportune time for further organic or inorganic opportunities.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Volume growth. Management had earlier guided to 10–12% cement volume growth for FY27. On the Q1 FY27 call, it revised this down to about 8–9%, citing floods and muted H1 demand. Observed performance: Q1 FY27 cement sales were 13.02 lakh tonnes versus 12.22 lakh tonnes a year earlier (about 6.5% growth), with Northeast volumes nearly flat (8.71 lakh tonnes vs 8.67 lakh tonnes) and outside-Northeast volumes up (4.31 lakh tonnes vs 3.55 lakh tonnes). July FY27 volumes declined about 12% year-on-year due to flooding. Management hopes to catch up in September and expects double-digit growth from Q3/Q4.

Clinker sales. Management now expects FY27 clinker sales to be stagnant or degrow 5–10% versus FY26, due to clinker inflows into the Northeast from outside. Observed: Q1 FY27 clinker sales were 0.52 lakh tonnes versus 0.74 lakh tonnes a year earlier.

Per-tonne EBITDA. Management maintained that INR 1,500–1,600 per tonne is a good full-year estimate, but guided to about INR 1,400 for Q2 due to shutdown costs and lower volume absorption of fixed costs in the Northeast monsoon, with catch-up in Q3/Q4. Observed: Q1 FY27 per-tonne EBITDA was INR 1,497.

Fuel cost. Management guided Q2 FY27 fuel cost down to about INR 1.45, with further reduction hoped for in Q3/Q4. Observed: Q1 FY27 was about INR 1.55 versus INR 1.33 in Q4 FY26.

Capex. Management reiterated FY27 capex of about INR 500 crores (INR 93 crores spent in Q1) and about INR 1,500 crores in FY28, with total North capex of about INR 2,600–2,700 crores (about INR 2,900 crores including GST). The company is not actively pursuing a QIP at present.

Subsidy. Management guided FY27 incentive income to about INR 115 crores versus the earlier expectation of about INR 145 crores, reflecting the Assam government’s proportionate annual allocation notification.

Non-cement revenue. The earlier guidance of about INR 150 crores for the Building Solutions division was clarified as a revenue (not EBITDA) estimate; management now expects to reach that run rate by Q4 FY27 rather than immediately, given sluggish demand.

Profitability trajectory. Management stated that excluding the INR 40 crore GST-related subsidy hit, Q1 FY27 would have shown positive EBITDA growth in absolute and percentage terms, and that from September onwards the subsidy-related drag on reported numbers should cease, with growth reflecting thereafter.

Broker Narrative

First report was a cautious Accumulate: it acknowledged robust Q4FY23 and expansion but worried about heavy capex, rising debt, input costs, subsidy loss and CEO transition. Last report is a Buy: despite muted Q1FY27 volumes, Assam floods, fuel spike and lower incentive guidance, it highlights superior EBITDA/t, premiumisation, cost-saving projects and FY29 North India expansion. Persistent themes were input/fuel costs, incentives, and volume/capex-led growth; the change was from fearing debt/capex to treating controlled leverage (~1.1x net debt/EBITDA FY28E) and expansion as positives.

Fears that came true

  • Early input-cost concerns materialized: variable costs, staff costs and a fuel-cost surge pushed total cost/tonne up 11.6% QoQ in Q1FY27, while many subsequent calls ended in DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • The subsidy/incentive risk flagged at the start echoed later: FY27 incentive income guidance was cut to INR 1.15bn from INR 1.45bn, coinciding with negative actual returns on several later Buy calls.

Optimism that failed

  • The initial expectation of falling fuel costs and cost-savings-led margin gains did not hold, as last report showed fuel cost spiking to INR 1.55/’000 kcal and total cost/tonne rising sharply.
  • Capacity-expansion-driven volume growth disappointed: Q1FY27 volume growth was only 4% YoY, July 2026 volumes fell ~12% YoY, and FY27 guidance was lowered to ~8-9%, undermining the earlier Eastern India market-share growth thesis.
