Bank of Maharashtra — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Proposed bank strike: Seven trade unions have proposed a one-day bank strike on 11.09.2026, which may affect the functioning of the bank’s branches and offices. The bank states it is taking necessary steps for smooth functioning on the day of the strike, though operations may be affected if the strike materializes.
  • Deposit growth lagging advances: Total global deposits grew 12.93% year-on-year to ₹3,44,493 crore, while gross global advances grew 26.90% to ₹3,05,964 crore. The domestic Credit-Deposit ratio stood at 86.58%.
  • CASA share dilution: CASA share of total deposits declined to 48.51% as of June 2026 from 50.07% as of June 2025, even as CASA deposits grew 9.26% year-on-year to ₹1,66,875 crore.
  • Sequential moderation in profit before tax: Profit before tax declined 2.24% quarter-on-quarter to ₹2,277 crore in Q1FY27 from ₹2,329 crore in Q4FY26, and provisions and contingencies (other than taxes) rose 36.21% quarter-on-quarter to ₹840 crore.
  • Fraud cases and penalties: During the quarter ended 30.06.2026, the bank reported 47 fraud cases involving ₹106.22 crore, with provisions of ₹103.84 crore held against fraud cases amounting to ₹103.86 crore. The RBI levied penalties of ₹0.02 crore on the bank during the quarter.
  • Reversal of contingency buffer: COVID-19 related contingency provisions of ₹250 crore were reversed during the quarter, with ₹760 crore remaining as of 30.06.2026, down from ₹1,010 crore as of 31.03.2026 and ₹1,200 crore as of 30.06.2025.
  • Scheduling uncertainty: The dates of the Analyst/Institutional Investor Meet (8th–24th September 2026) are subject to change due to exigencies on the part of investors or the bank.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Business scale-up: Total business nearly doubled since March 2022, registering a CAGR of 17%, with global advances growing at a CAGR of 21.18% and total deposits at 13.34%.
  • Strong year-on-year growth: Total global business grew 19.10% to ₹6,50,457 crore; gross global advances grew 26.90% to ₹3,05,964 crore; net advances grew 27.22% to ₹3,01,934 crore; total global deposits grew 12.93% to ₹3,44,493 crore.
  • RAM segment momentum: RAM (Retail, Agriculture & MSME) business grew 25.11% year-on-year. Retail advances grew 24.59% to ₹89,661 crore and MSME advances grew 23.17% to ₹55,386 crore.
  • Profitability improvement: Net profit rose 26.84% year-on-year to ₹2,020 crore; operating profit rose 21.29% to ₹3,117 crore; net interest income rose 14.53% to ₹3,770 crore. Cost-to-income ratio improved to 35.04% from 37.57%. ROA improved to 1.90% and ROE to 24.65%.
  • Asset quality strength: Gross NPA improved to 1.45% from 1.74% a year earlier; net NPA improved to 0.13% from 0.18%; provision coverage ratio improved to 98.55% from 98.36%.
  • Capital position: CRAR stood at 18.64% with Tier I at 16.35% and CET-1 at 15.56%.
  • Stated focus areas: Strengthening the deposit base, expanding retail business and co-lending models, adopting digital platforms, and geographical diversification, with 59% of branches outside Maharashtra.
  • Capital raise authorization: Shareholders approved a special resolution permitting raising of equity capital up to ₹7,500 crore through preferential allotment to GoI/LIC/FIs and/or Qualified Bonds and/or Tier II Bonds to meet CRAR requirements under Basel III.
  • Co-lending arrangements: The bank has 9 co-lending partners with a quantum of ₹3,056.80 crore, weighted average RCI of 9.49%, across retail and agri sectors, with satisfactory performance.
  • Project finance pipeline: 194 projects under implementation at quarter-end with total outstanding of ₹10,304.61 crore; 33 new projects sanctioned during the quarter (₹366.98 crore); 27 projects achieved DCCC during the quarter (₹473.14 crore).

3. Key Risks

  • Strike disruption risk: The proposed 11.09.2026 strike may affect branch and office functioning if it materializes.
  • Credit concentration in project finance: 43 accounts (₹3,364.94 crore) are under resolution processes involving extension of DCCC; of these, resolution plans for 41 accounts (₹3,052.32 crore) are under implementation.
  • Unhedged foreign currency exposure: The bank holds a provision of ₹11.92 crore as of 30.06.2026 for unhedged foreign currency exposure of constituents.
  • Stressed asset resolution framework: Loans impacted by the RBI circular on resolution of stressed assets totaled ₹561.59 crore, with no amount required to be classified as NPA and no additional provision required as of 30.06.2026.
  • Review scope limitations: The limited review covered branches representing 54.25% of the advances portfolio; 2,754 branches included in the standalone results were not reviewed, and the associate’s results were not reviewed by its auditors.
  • Debt securities are unsecured: The listed non-convertible debt securities (totaling ₹8,430.7 crore sanctioned) are unsecured, with no security cover required.
  • Investor complaints: Two complaints were received and resolved during the quarter, with none unresolved at quarter-end.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Capital adequacy: The bank’s capital raise authorization of up to ₹7,500 crore is explicitly stated as being “to meet the CRAR requirement under Basel III as per extant SEBI Guidelines.” Observed CRAR stood at 18.64% with CET-1 at 15.56%, and the bank has stated that the capital raise is intended to support CRAR requirements.
  • Strike preparedness: Management states it is taking necessary steps for smooth functioning of branches and offices on the strike date, while acknowledging operations may be affected if the strike materializes. No forward guidance on financial impact is provided.
  • Investor meet: The bank has scheduled meetings with institutional investors and analysts from 8th to 24th September 2026 via virtual mode, during which only publicly available documents will be discussed and no unpublished price sensitive information is proposed to be shared. The bank notes these dates are subject to change.
  • Dividend: The board recommended a final dividend of ₹1.20 per share for FY2025-26, in addition to an interim dividend of ₹1.00 per equity share declared on 13.01.2026 and paid during the financial year. Shareholders approved the final dividend at the AGM on 30.06.2026.
  • COVID-19 provision trajectory: The bank reversed ₹250 crore of COVID-19 related contingency provisions during Q1FY27, following a reversal of ₹190 crore during the quarter and year ended March 2026. The remaining buffer stands at ₹760 crore as of 30.06.2026, compared to ₹1,010 crore as of 31.03.2026 and ₹1,200 crore as of 30.06.2025.
  • Observed versus prior-year performance: Management-reported figures show broad-based improvement across profitability, asset quality, and capital metrics on a year-on-year basis, with net profit growing 26.84%, operating profit growing 21.29%, and gross NPA declining to 1.45% from 1.74%. On a sequential basis, however, net profit grew only 0.31% and profit before tax declined 2.24%, while provisions rose 36.21%.

Broker Narrative

The first report was a cautious, bank-specific not-rated note focused on near-term NIM compression from deposit repricing, new investment-guideline effects on trading income, and ECL provisioning costs, while positive on recoveries, asset quality, and a ~1.4-1.5% RoA path. The last report is a Buy that frames MAHABANK within a broader PSB re-rating story: PSBs’ superior advances growth, granular deposits, closed asset-quality gap versus private banks, and valuation discount. Persistent themes include NIM pressure, recoveries supporting RoA, strong CASA/liability profile, and asset-quality strength; the bank-specific ECL/trading income concerns are replaced by system liquidity, repo-rate cuts, and PSB/PVB comparison.
