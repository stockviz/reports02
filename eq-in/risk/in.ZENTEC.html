ZENTEC: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Revenue timing and execution cycle. Q1 FY27 revenue of ₹141.64 crore is described as consistent with expectations but reflecting the early stage of the current order book’s execution cycle. The order book improved substantially and was largely secured starting from October 2025, with a typical execution cycle of around twelve months. Revenue is expected to be weighted towards Q2 FY27 and Q3 FY27, with further orders received till Q2 FY27 expected to be partially executed by Q4 FY27.

Margin compression. EBITDA margin fell to 40.9% in Q1 FY27 from 54.7% in Q1 FY26 and 41.4% in Q4 FY26. Operational EBITDA margin fell to 27.3% in Q1 FY27 from 40.9% in Q1 FY26 and 28.6% in Q4 FY26. Operational EBITDA declined to ₹38.72 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹64.70 crore in Q1 FY26.

Year-on-year declines. Sales fell to ₹141.64 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹158.22 crore in Q1 FY26. Total revenue fell to ₹160.79 crore from ₹180.01 crore. Profit after tax (adjusted for non-controlling interest) fell to ₹34.46 crore from ₹47.75 crore.

Fire incident and inventory loss. During the quarter ended June 30, 2026, certain inventory of Unistring Tech Solutions Private Limited, a subsidiary, was damaged in a fire incident on April 25, 2026. The Group recognised a loss of ₹3.37 crore as an Exceptional Item. The damaged inventory is adequately insured, but the insurance survey is still pending.

QIP proceeds utilisation extended. The Board approved an extension of the timeline for utilisation of the unutilised balance of QIP funds allocated towards inorganic growth, acquisitions, strategic initiatives and general corporate purposes, by 24 months from August 23, 2026 to August 22, 2028. The QIP was completed on August 23, 2024, with funds originally proposed to be utilised within 24 months. There is no change in the objects of the issue.

Air Force entry still pending. R&D for the first product offering to the Air Force has been completed, but negotiations with OEMs are at an advanced stage and partnerships are yet to be formalised.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order book visibility. The consolidated order book stood at ₹1,239.02 crore as at June 30, 2026. A further simulator order of ₹177.50 crore was received in July 2026 for the upgradation and integration of Tank and Crew Gunnery Simulators. The majority of the order book is scheduled for conversion into revenue from Q2 FY27 onward, and the company states it remains on track with delivery schedules.

New Ministry of Defence order. On August 10, 2026, the company announced receipt of an order amounting to ₹295 crore (including GST) from the Ministry of Defence, Government of India, for supply of Simulators. The order is domestic, is to be executed within a year, and is not a related party transaction.

New product launches. During the quarter, the company introduced an AI-powered anti-drone system, the Hyperstrike interceptor drone, the Zen Vrishab unmanned ground vehicle and the Integrated Smart Border Suite. On July 26, 2026, coinciding with Kargil Vijay Diwas, it unveiled a High-Altitude Man-Portable Anti-Drone System (MPADS) with detection ranges up to 5 km, omni-directional jamming up to 3 km, multi-target and swarm detection, and 24x7 all-weather operation.

Supportive procurement environment. The Government’s sustained prioritisation of counter-drone systems, layered air defence and unmanned platforms is evidenced by Defence Acquisition Council clearances over recent months. Continued emphasis on Buy Indian-IDDM reinforces the relevance of the company’s portfolio.

Air Force domain entry. The company is already a leader in the Army and Navy domains and has completed R&D for its first Air Force product offering. It is positive about formalising OEM partnerships during the year, which would mark entry into the air simulator domain.

Broadened product suite. The company describes a materially broadened product suite spanning training solutions, live ranges, live simulation, virtual simulation, combat training centres, drone and counter-drone solutions, loitering munitions, operational equipment, force multipliers, ISR systems, naval simulation, marine solutions, air simulators, DSRV and submarine navigation simulators.

Key Risks

  • Execution timing risk: revenue conversion depends on order execution cycles of around twelve months, with revenue weighted to specific quarters.
  • Margin risk: EBITDA and operational EBITDA margins have declined year-on-year.
  • Insurance recovery risk: the insurance survey for the fire incident at Unistring Tech Solutions is still pending.
  • Partnership risk: formalisation of OEM partnerships for the Air Force entry is not yet complete.
  • QIP deployment risk: unutilised QIP funds for inorganic growth, acquisitions and strategic initiatives remain undeployed, with the timeline extended to August 22, 2028.
  • Forward-looking statements in the investor presentation and press release are subject to risks and uncertainties including fluctuations in earnings, intense competition, political instability and general economic conditions affecting the industry.

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from ZENTEC as a niche tank-simulator leader with a 2.2x order book and AMC comfort to a broader defence-tech story spanning anti-drone systems, flight simulators, exports and a ₹4,000-crore FY27-28 revenue target. Persistent themes across both reports are government-procurement dependence, working-capital intensity, competition/technology risk, and confidence in the debt-free balance sheet and order backlog. The last report adds near-term execution and margin concerns (Q1FY27 revenue -10.5% YoY, EBITDA margin -1,356 bps) that were not prominent in the first report.

Fears that came true

  • Government/client concentration and tender lumpiness materialised: Q1FY27 revenues fell 10.5% YoY after lower H1FY26 order inflows and execution lags, and the 2024-11-09 Buy call ended as a DISAPPOINTMENT with -25.4%.
  • Working-capital intensity materialised: FY26 reported negative operating cash flow of ₹71 crore.
  • Revenue volatility and R&D/financial risk materialised: Q1FY27 EBITDA margins contracted 1,356 bps YoY to 27.3% on negative operating leverage and higher R&D spend.

Optimism that failed

  • The asset-light, scalable model did not deliver the hoped operating leverage: Q1FY27 margins compressed sharply due to negative operating leverage.
  • Order-book revenue visibility was not enough to avoid a Q1FY27 revenue decline, showing that backlog did not translate into smooth near-term execution.
