asof: 2026-09-16

Headwinds and Challenges

  • Transition in disbursement reporting: From Q1 FY27, the company moved disbursement reporting from a cheque-handover basis to a cheque-realisation basis. Reported disbursements for Q1 FY27 were ₹2,036 crore against ₹1,979 crore in Q1 FY26, with the company noting that disbursement on cheque handover in Q1 FY27 was ₹23,590 million, a 19% YoY growth. This change affects comparability of reported disbursement figures.
  • Rising impairment charge: Impairment on financial instruments rose to ₹3,167 lakh in Q1 FY27 from ₹2,672 lakh in Q1 FY26 and ₹1,017 lakh in Q4 FY26.
  • Higher finance costs: Finance costs increased to ₹37,639 lakh in Q1 FY27 from ₹33,194 lakh in Q1 FY26.
  • Labour Codes impact: Following notification of the four Labour Codes on November 21, 2025, the company estimated an increase in gratuity and compensated absence liabilities from past service cost of ₹1,592 lakh (net of tax ₹1,240 lakh), presented as an exceptional item for the year ended March 31, 2026. The company continues to monitor finalisation of Central/State Rules and will reassess estimates.
  • Cancellation of investor meeting: The company cancelled its participation in the scheduled investor meeting with Anand Rathi on September 22, 2026, due to other exigencies.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • AUM growth: AUM grew 18% YoY to ₹31,364 crore as of June 30, 2026, from ₹26,524 crore as of June 30, 2025.
  • Profit growth: PAT grew 19% YoY to ₹282 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹237 crore in Q1 FY26.
  • Stable asset quality: GNPA on AUM improved to 1.31% from 1.34% YoY. Standalone GNPA was 1.32% and NNPA 0.87%, with a provision coverage ratio of 34.12%.
  • Strong capitalisation: Capital adequacy ratio (CRAR) stood at 43.39%; liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) averaged 244.96% over the last 90 days. Net worth was ₹7,853 crore.
  • Branch expansion: The ‘Urban and Emerging’ branch model expanded the network to 628 branches across 22 states and union territories.
  • Sector fundamentals: Management cited favourable operating conditions for low-income housing finance, supported by resilient demand, stable affordability, strength in Tier II and Tier III markets, low mortgage penetration, rising urbanisation, and affordable housing requirements in EWS and LIG segments. Accelerating PMAY-U 2.0 implementation and easing credit conditions were cited as constructive for the sector.
  • Technology investments: The company enhanced AI and digital capabilities across origination, credit assessment, and servicing, and aligned its AI framework with the RBI’s draft Model Risk Management guidance.
  • Funding activity: The company raised ₹250 crore via privately placed Non-Convertible Debentures on June 18, 2026, with proceeds fully utilised and no deviation. It also issued commercial papers listed on BSE during the quarter.
  • ESG rating: NSE Sustainability Ratings & Analytics Limited assigned an ESG rating of ‘71’ for FY 2026, prepared independently based on public domain data for FY 2025-26.

Key Risks

  • Asset quality: GNPA stood at 1.31% on AUM (1.32% standalone), with provision coverage ratio of 34.12%.
  • Credit concentration in secured lending: Loans are secured by pari passu first charge on standard book debts/receivables, with asset cover ratio against secured debt securities at 1.10 times and exclusive security cover ratio at 1.26.
  • Regulatory changes: The Labour Codes introduced a uniform definition of wages, resulting in a one-time liability increase; further Central/State Rules and clarifications are pending, and impact estimates will be reassessed.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The company’s disclaimer notes that forward-looking statements are subject to risks including fluctuations in earnings, ability to manage growth, competition, economic growth, ability to attract and retain skilled professionals, time and cost overruns, and government policies and regulations.
  • Assignment activity: During Q1 FY27, 4,088 loan accounts amounting to ₹39,742 lakh were assigned, with 10% retention of beneficial economic interest and 100% coverage of tangible security.

Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Management stated the company is “well positioned to achieve the targets set for this year” and that “strong first-quarter momentum reinforces its confidence in achieving the medium-term guidance on disbursement, AUM, and profit.”
  • Observed Q1 FY27 performance: AUM growth of 18% YoY, PAT growth of 19% YoY, ROA at 4.0%, ROE at 14.7%, and GNPA at 1.31% on AUM.
  • Management cited the operating environment as favourable, with resilient demand, stable affordability, and strength across Tier II and Tier III markets.
  • Management stated the company remains committed to expanding access to housing finance in underserved markets, with the ‘Urban and Emerging’ branch model driving strategy, and to maintaining sound underwriting standards.
  • Management noted continued investments in technology to improve operational efficiency, credit assessment, and customer experience, with commitment to robust governance and alignment of its AI framework with RBI’s draft Model Risk Management guidance.

Broker Narrative

The broker maintained a Buy rating throughout, but the narrative shifted from unhedged confidence in >20% AUM growth and >6% spreads to a more guarded stance acknowledging competition, spread volatility, geopolitical/monsoon risks, and the cheque-realisation transition. Persistent themes were affordable-housing growth, distribution expansion, stable ~5.8% spreads, healthy asset quality, operating leverage, and strong capital/margin targets.

Optimism that failed

  • The early expectation of >20% AUM growth did not fully materialize, as the last report showed ~18% YoY AUM growth.
  • The early ‘supporting spreads >6%’ tailwind faded, with spreads stable at 5.8%, below the 6% level highlighted in the first report.
