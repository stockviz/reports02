Pyramid Technoplast — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Export disruption linked to Middle East conflict. Management attributed a sharp volume impact in Q1 FY27 to a slowdown in exports, particularly to Dubai. Freight costs rose significantly due to the war, and the company said it was unable to export. IBCs were described as the main export product, with plastic drums used by customers to ship chemicals also affected because those customers’ own sales slowed. Management characterised this as a near-term demand disruption rather than a structural issue, and said growth of 2–4% was continuing in the quarter while the expected growth had not materialised.

Capacity utilisation below potential. Installed capacity stood at 20,936 metric tons per quarter against volumes of 1,292 metric tons, translating to roughly 62% utilisation. Management noted that production over the last six quarters had been broadly flat at around 13,000, even as capacity rose from about 16,000 in Q4 to about 21,000.

Raw material price volatility. Polymer resin prices moved sharply — from around 110 at the time of the IPO, down to 80–85, then a run-up in the last two quarters. Management said selling prices for polymer drums rose to about ₹193 per kg in Q1 FY27 from ₹138 in March, and that the elevated price gap (buying at ₹150 and selling at ₹170 or more) kept EBITDA margin below 10% in value terms even though unit economics were healthy. Management expects prices to settle around ₹140 over the next 3–4 months.

Margin compression in reported terms. Gross margin compressed to 23% as raw material costs rose in step with price increases, though gross margin in rupee terms for HDPE remained stable. EBITDA margin was 10%, and PAT margin 5%, despite a 179% year-on-year rise in finance cost and a 61% rise in depreciation tied to capacity expansion.

EPR portal closure. The EPR portal had been closed for 3–5 months for upgrading, preventing the company from passing on EPR benefits. Management said the system was expected to restart in 15–20 days.

Pending approvals. The Pollution Control (PUC) licence for the Wada facility had not been fully received — about 90% of the work was done, with one phase received and the second phase pending. Partial capacity could be run in the meantime. For the Kutch facility, management said government department processes are slower in Maharashtra than in Gujarat, and expected matters to be completed in 2–3 months.

Working capital pressure from higher raw material prices. Management noted working capital loans increased because raw material bought at ₹100 was now being bought at ₹150, and even at ₹140 working capital remained elevated. Cash conversion cycle was described as 65–70 days.

Subsidy disbursement timing. The Wada subsidy of approximately ₹24.9 crore and Bharuch Unit 7 subsidy of approximately ₹10.5 crore (totalling about ₹35.4 crore) are spread over 10 years, with the Wada sanction expected by March 2027 and the Bharuch formal sanction application yet to be filed. Management said the benefit would come in the current financial year but was unsure of the quarter, so nothing was being shown in the accounts.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Kutch expansion. The company is establishing a new manufacturing facility in Kutch, Gujarat, with an investment of ₹20–25 crore, capacity of 10,000 IBC units per month, and expected commissioning by March 2027. The location is expected to provide lower freight costs, faster deliveries, and improved customer service, and to strengthen presence in Western India. Management said the site is on owned land, with roughly ₹10 crore going to land and building and the rest to machinery. Revenue potential was described as a ₹100 crore business, though the company is planning on ₹50 crore initially, with a monthly run rate of ₹8–8.5 crore and a payback of about 4 years. The company currently serves the region from Bharuch, supplying fewer than 2,000 IBCs against a surveyed market of 10,000. Management said it would share some benefit with customers initially (about 30% to the customer, 70% retained).

Wada facility ramp-up. The Wada facility is in full swing across HDPE, IBCs, and MS Drums, operating above 70% utilisation, with expectations of reaching 80% during the year. It contributed approximately ₹43 crore, or 19% of revenue from operations, in Q1 FY27. Management noted that because a subsidy has been taken, capacity cannot be added until March; without adding capacity, utilisation would reach 80%, but extra capacity will be kept once added after March.

Government subsidy benefits. Subsidy approvals for Wada (approximately ₹24.9 crore) and Bharuch Unit 7 (approximately ₹10.5 crore) are expected to reduce the effective cost of investment and improve project economics, ROCE, IRR, and payback. The benefit is in cash form via GST, spread over 10 years, at roughly ₹3.5 crore per year. Management said it would be funded from internal accruals for the Kutch plant.

Green energy initiatives. A 6 MW solar plant was commissioned in October 2025, followed by an additional 5 MW in Bharuch and 2.25 MW in Maharashtra, with 1 MW still pending, expected next quarter. Q1 FY27 savings were approximately ₹2 crore, with full 14.25 MW capacity expected to deliver around ₹15 crore of annual savings. Management clarified that the ₹15 crore figure is a full-year number and would not be fully realised this year, but should be visible over four quarters once fully operational. The company described itself as the only company in India in both solar and recycling.

Recycling plant. Commissioned in October, with annual capacity of 5,000 metric tons, addressing around 10–12% of raw material requirements. It processed around 150 metric tons in Q1 FY27, generating around ₹25 lakh of EBITDA, with FY27 EBITDA contribution estimated at around ₹2 crore.

Financial performance. Revenue grew 36% year on year to ₹222 crore, driven primarily by price increases as raw material cost rises were passed to customers. HDPE polymer drum tonnage was down 4% year on year, and IBC volumes were hit by export demand. MS drums grew strongly. Gross profit rose 19%, EBITDA grew 50% with margins at 10%, and PAT grew 32% to ₹10.5 crore. EBITDA per ton rose to approximately ₹16,380 in Q1 FY27 from ₹11,252 in Q1 FY26 and ₹15,053 in Q4 FY26.

New product line. A new machine has been added and the product is in the testing phase, with a customer having taken some material for onward testing. Management said it is a 2–3 month process pending approval.

Geographic expansion. Management said the company would expand into South India and other locations, describing significant remaining opportunity.

3. Key Risks

  • Export demand disruption tied to the Middle East conflict and elevated freight costs, which management expects to normalise but which has not yet fully resolved.
  • Raw material price volatility, with management expecting polymer prices to fall by about ₹20 over 2–3 months; if prices do not normalise as expected, the anticipated volume-led EBITDA improvement may not materialise.
  • Execution and approval risk on the Kutch facility, including pending government processes, with commissioning targeted for March 2027.
  • Pending PUC licence for Wada, with one phase still outstanding.
  • Subsidy disbursement risk, with the Bharuch formal sanction application yet to be filed and the Wada sanction expected only by March 2027; benefits are spread over 10 years.
  • EPR portal closure delaying pass-through of EPR benefits.
  • Working capital intensity from elevated raw material prices, with finance costs rising 179% year on year and expected to remain around ₹3 crore by year-end.
  • Customer concentration/transition risk in Kutch, where the company will need to find new customers locally as volumes shift from existing facilities.
  • Employee availability and wage-related risks, which management acknowledged as a possibility though not currently a factor.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 revenue growth guidance of approximately 15% and EBITDA margins upwards of 10%. In Q1 FY27, revenue grew 36% year on year to ₹222 crore, but management attributed this primarily to price increases rather than volume, with HDPE polymer drum tonnage down 4% year on year. EBITDA margin was 10%. Management reiterated the target of 11–12%, more than 10%, and expressed confidence in reaching 11% once the price gap narrows. On the question of achieving ₹85–86 crore EBITDA in FY27, management said there was “complete hope.”

Capacity utilisation guidance. Management guided that overall utilisation would reach 70–75% by year-end, with Wada moving from 70% to 80%. Earlier in the call, management said the overall 62% would touch 70% this year. On the Kutch plant, management said it would start commercial production from FY28 and initially be considered at ₹50 crore rather than the full ₹100 crore potential.

Solar savings guidance. Management guided approximately ₹15 crore of annual savings from full 14.25 MW capacity, but clarified this would not be fully realised this year and should be seen over four quarters once fully operational. Q1 FY27 realised savings were approximately ₹2 crore.

Recycling guidance. FY27 EBITDA contribution estimated at around ₹2 crore, with Q1 FY27 contributing around ₹25 lakh.

Subsidy guidance. Approximately ₹3.5 crore per year over 10 years, starting this financial year, though the quarter is uncertain and nothing is being shown in the accounts.

Capex guidance. FY27 capex planned at approximately ₹20–25 crore, primarily towards Kutch expansion, deployed in line with actuals. A further ₹20–25 crore would be needed for a second Wada phase, planned for the next financial year after March.

Finance cost guidance. Management said finance cost would reach around ₹3 crore by year-end from ₹3.5 crore in Q1 FY27, and would not fall sharply.

Debt guidance. Management said repayment has already started and debt is not increasing; the loan taken last year to start a plant was described as ₹225–230 crore including working capital, with ₹175 crore in the market and ₹100 crore of stock, described as a healthy balance sheet.

Gross margin guidance. Management indicated gross profit margin should return to the 26–27% range once prices normalise, and confirmed this expectation for the year.

Other corporate matters. The Board approved the appointment of M/s Desai Saksena & Associates as Statutory Auditors for five years, subject to shareholder approval at the 28th AGM, succeeding M/s Banka and Banka upon completion of their tenure. The 28th AGM is scheduled for Tuesday, September 22, 2026, at 11:30 A.M. IST through video conferencing, with a proposed auditor fee of ₹14 lakhs for FY 2026-27 and cost auditor remuneration not exceeding ₹75,000. A final dividend of ₹0.50 per equity share (5%) of face value ₹10 each was proposed, along with adoption of audited standalone financial statements for the year ended March 31, 2026, and re-appointment of Mr. Jaiprakash Bijaykumar Agarwal as a director retiring by rotation.
