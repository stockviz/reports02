E2E Networks — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • No profits / inadequate profits in FY2025-26. The company attributes this primarily to a substantial increase in non-cash depreciation expenses arising from continued investments in infrastructure and assets. This caused managerial remuneration paid to executive directors to exceed the limits permitted under Section 197 of the Companies Act, 2013, and the company is seeking shareholder approval to waive recovery of the excess.
  • Rising finance costs. Interest costs increased sharply, which management attributes to the loan taken to fund the first lot of B200 GPUs. Outstanding loan stood at broadly INR 450 crores as of the Q1 FY27 call, with further increase expected as additional lots arrive.
  • Input cost inflation. Management stated that hardware prices — particularly on the CPU side, where memory cost increases hit harder in percentage terms than GPUs — necessitated price increases for both GPU and CPU customers.
  • Competitive and cyclical pressure. An analyst noted that hyperscalers are scaling aggressively, customers are getting harder on pricing, and technology cycles are shortening. Management acknowledged cycles will continue, with both good and bad phases.
  • Shortening AI hardware life cycles. An analyst raised concern about compressed life cycles of AI hardware and client preferences shifting downstream, questioning how ROIC is protected on older Hopper and early Blackwell assets.
  • Customer pushback on pricing. On CPU price revisions, some customers asked to preserve existing price points; the company responded by offering longer-term contracts (6 months to over a year, sometimes with advance payment) to hold current prices.
  • Execution and disclosure caution. Management repeatedly declined to quantify forward figures — no MRR guidance, no peak debt quantification, no customer-mix disclosure, and limited detail on SovCloud funding plans, stating it is “very early days.”

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Blackwell deployment live. 1,024 NVIDIA B200 GPUs received over recent months went online and were put on revenue; management described utilization as at “maximal” levels. A further 1,024 B200 units were expected within a couple of months.
  • Large long-term sovereign AI contract. A binding term sheet worth approximately Rs. 1,000 crore (exclusive of taxes) with an India-based Sovereign AI company for NVIDIA Blackwell cloud GPUs and allied services, valid through June 2029. This transitions the Blackwell capacity deployed in May-end 2026 from pay-as-you-go to a long-term commitment, providing multi-year revenue visibility.
  • Sovereign AI positioning. Management argues open-weight models are now very close to frontier models, enabling customers to deploy, fine-tune and control models within their own boundary via E2E’s platform (TIR and Jarvis Labs), capturing value at multiple layers and avoiding dependence on rented frontier-model APIs subject to political and private-control risks.
  • Capacity expansion pipeline. Plans to deploy more Blackwell (including non-flagship), expand into Vera Rubin, and continue adding CPU capacity. Approximately 5,100 GPUs were live at Q1 FY27 exit, excluding the expected 1,024 B200.
  • New entities. SovCloud incorporated as a wholly owned subsidiary focused on large-scale GPU infrastructure build-out and enabling funding arrangements; a Delaware entity established for international sales, market presence and alliance management.
  • Fundraise and borrowing capacity. Board approved raising up to Rs. 1,500 crores via public or private offering (QIP/Rights/FPO or other mechanism) to fund capex, repay debt, support working capital, and pursue strategic acquisitions and partnerships. AGM proposals include increasing borrowing powers up to Rs. 10,000 crore and creating asset charges.
  • Partnership with Larsen & Toubro. An arm’s-length relationship where E2E buys L&T data centre capacity, L&T uses E2E cloud infrastructure, and the two jointly approach customers; E2E may also offtake compute capacity L&T builds. AGM includes approval of material related party transactions with L&T.
  • Market opportunity cited. Global data centre capex of US$6.7 trillion needed by 2030 (~70% AI-driven); ~US$1.5 trillion sovereign-AI infrastructure opportunity this decade; India’s 8.33 GW data centre pipeline versus ~1.6 GW live; IndiaAI Mission’s 38,000+ GPUs; ~US$30 bn investment underpinning India’s data centre build-out.
  • Leadership strengthening. Alok Ohrie, former President & MD of Dell Technologies India, joined as Strategic Advisor, alongside senior hires in business and technology.
  • Stock split and BSE listing. A 10:1 stock split was executed and shares listed on BSE in addition to NSE, broadening the investor base.

3. Key Risks

  • Depreciation drag on profitability. Heavy infrastructure investment has driven large non-cash depreciation, which pushed FY26 into a loss (PAT of negative INR 156 million) and triggered the managerial remuneration excess under Section 197.
  • Leverage risk. Borrowings rose from INR 114 million (Mar-25) to INR 1,032 million (Mar-26), with the loan at ~INR 450 crores as of the Q1 FY27 call and expected to increase; finance cost rose 449% YoY in Q1 FY27.
  • Hardware obsolescence and ROIC. Risk that newer GPU generations (B300, Vera Rubin) compress pricing and returns on older Hopper/early Blackwell assets. Management counters with a minimum six-year assumed GPU life cycle and claims older generations continue to run strongly.
  • Vendor concentration. Accelerated computing is deeply anchored to the NVIDIA ecosystem; an analyst raised the ASIC threat and enterprise demand diversification. Management said it is vendor-neutral and open to integrating other accelerated computing platforms.
  • Pricing and contract risk. Price increases may face customer resistance; contracts must be honored regardless of whether they become advantageous or disadvantageous.
  • Demand-supply cyclicality. Management noted phases of demand shortage and supply shortage; long-cycle equilibrium is expected but not guaranteed.
  • Execution risk on expansion. SovCloud funding arrangements, Delaware entity operations, and future capacity additions are at early stages with limited disclosed detail.
  • Regulatory/compliance matters. AGM agenda includes waiving recovery of excess managerial remuneration, revising remuneration packages for Tarun Dua, Srishti Baweja and Megha Raheja, increasing borrowing powers, creating asset charges, and approving material related party transactions with L&T.
  • Shareholder KYC compliance. Physical shareholders must update PAN, address with PIN, mobile, bank details, specimen signature and nomination per SEBI Master Circular No. SEBI/HO/MIRSD/POD-1/P/CIR/2024/37; payments for folios lacking these details will be made only through electronic mode effective April 1, 2024.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • No formal guidance provided. Management explicitly declined to guide on exit MRR, stating: “we don’t provide guidance on MRR. We always advise everyone to look at what we have done in the past rather than predict the future.” They also declined to quantify peak debt for the year and provided no specific GPU count targets for two years out, saying only that capacity building would be “aggressive” and “judicious.”
  • Observed Q1 FY27 performance (quarter ended June 2026): Revenue INR 1,568 million, up 334.3% YoY and 64.0% QoQ; EBITDA INR 1,179 million at 75.2% margin (up 1,450 bps QoQ); PBT INR 586 million; PAT INR 439 million at 28.0% margin; basic EPS INR 2.14; exit MRR INR 718 million versus INR 374 million in March 2026.
  • Revenue mix commentary: IndiaAI revenue was about 20–21% in the prior quarter; international was closer to 37%; the rest domestic. Management declined to disclose customer-segment mix, saying the company is “quite small for that right now.”
  • Growth drivers: Management attributed the quarter’s performance mainly to increased capacity and operating leverage, with only a moderate pricing impact. Utilization of the existing fleet was described as at maximum levels versus any previous quarter, with future growth expected mainly from capacity additions rather than price hikes.
  • Sustainability view: Management described the current performance as “quite sustainable,” citing each GPU generation finding its price-performance sweet spot and increased customer willingness to sign longer-term contracts (1–3 years), providing predictability.
  • Cost outlook: Management said much of the cost for existing capacity is already baked into Q1 FY27, with some increase expected in coming quarters but not a very large percentage; electricity costs will rise with usage.
  • Profitability improvement expectation: Management stated that measures to improve operational efficiency, optimize costs and enhance resource utilization are expected to improve operational efficiency and profitability, though the exact extent depends on business and market factors and cannot be quantified with certainty.
  • Contractual commitment: Services under the ~Rs. 1,000 crore sovereign AI term sheet are to be provided up to June 2029, with management stating the engagement provides greater revenue visibility over a multi-year horizon and reinforces the strategy of building a high-quality, long-term customer base.
