ADOR Welding Limited — 73rd AGM Voting Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

The 73rd Annual General Meeting, held on 23 July 2026, saw all eight resolutions passed with the requisite majority, but the voting pattern reveals notable shareholder dissent on specific governance-related items.

  • Executive Director remuneration (Resolution 7) attracted the highest level of opposition. While the special resolution passed, 3.6783% of votes polled were against it. Public institutions recorded 20.3800% of their votes against the resolution, and public non-institutions recorded 1.1939% against. The promoter and promoter group category also showed a lower participation rate on this resolution, with 90.0744% of their held shares polled compared to 98.6667% on routine resolutions.
  • Re-appointment of the Managing Director (Resolution 6) also faced meaningful resistance. The special resolution passed with 96.4119% of votes in favour, but 3.5881% were against. Public institutions voted 20.4499% against, while public non-institutions voted 0.4097% against. Promoter participation was 93.1462% of held shares, lower than on ordinary resolutions.
  • Invalid votes were recorded in the promoter and promoter group category on both Resolution 6 (516,471 votes) and Resolution 7 (803,846 votes), indicating some processing or eligibility issues on these interested-party resolutions.
  • Overall shareholder participation stood at 63.9681% of outstanding shares for the routine resolutions, but fell to 61.0004% for Resolution 6 and 59.3491% for Resolution 7, reflecting reduced engagement on the remuneration and re-appointment matters.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong governance endorsement: All eight resolutions were passed, including adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY ended 31 March 2026, declaration of dividend, re-appointment of Mr. Ravin A. Mirchandani as a rotating director, ratification of cost auditor remuneration for FY 2026-27, and approval of non-executive director commission.
  • Broad shareholder support: Routine resolutions (1, 2, 3, 5, and 8) received near-unanimous approval, with 99.9998% of votes in favour on Resolutions 1 and 2, and 99.9986% on Resolution 5.
  • Institutional confidence: Public institutions voted 100% in favour on Resolutions 1, 2, 3, 5, and 8, and 99.9301% in favour on Resolution 4.
  • Dividend declaration (Resolution 3) was approved with 99.9998% support, signalling continued return of capital to shareholders.
  • Digital engagement: 13 promoter group and 79 public shareholders attended via video conferencing, alongside e-voting participation across 36,778 shareholders on the record date of 16 July 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Governance sensitivity around promoter remuneration: The concentration of against-votes from public institutions on Resolutions 6 and 7 (approximately 20% each) indicates ongoing scrutiny of executive compensation and related-party remuneration arrangements.
  • Invalid vote incidence: The presence of invalid votes specifically in the promoter and promoter group category on the two interested-party resolutions introduces a procedural risk around vote processing for related-party matters.
  • Lower participation on special resolutions: The decline in polled shares on Resolutions 6 and 7 relative to ordinary resolutions suggests that shareholder engagement weakens on compensation and re-appointment matters, which could affect future special resolution outcomes if dissent grows.
