EPACK Durable — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure despite strong growth. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was INR 886 crores, up about 34% year-on-year, but EBITDA grew only about 0.70% to INR 55 crores, with EBITDA margin at 6.21% versus 8.24% a year earlier. Management flagged that the prior-year quarter included INR 13.31 crores of PLI income accrued with no associated cost, which flowed almost entirely into EBITDA. Stripping that out of both revenue and EBITDA, the prior-year underlying margin was closer to 6.4%, making the like-to-like year-on-year improvement about 15 basis points rather than 203 basis points. No PLI income was accrued in Q1 FY27.

Forex and input costs. Management identified the forex exchange rate as the single largest item affecting the quarter, with a forex loss of roughly INR 6–7 crores booked in other expenses. Commodity increases were largely passed through, but with a time lag — particularly during supply-chain turbulence around March and April — and contracts with larger customers reset prices quarterly. Other expenses also rose on higher wages accompanying the ~35% increase in sales and production value.

Seasonality and structural losses in Q2/Q3. Management acknowledged that Q2 and Q3 have historically been loss-making quarters because of heavy dependence on air conditioners, and that the strategy around SDA and LDA is first to neutralise that loss. The company expects it will take four to six quarters to bring the “bleeding situation” in these quarters largely under control.

Higher depreciation and finance costs. Depreciation rose as new capacities were put to use, and finance cost increased by roughly INR 3–4 crores on higher working capital requirements accompanying the ~40% scale-up. Lower other income and wider joint venture losses also weighed on profitability.

Working capital and inventory. Working capital days run around 50–60 depending on season. Management noted that BIS and QCO revisions (compressors, copper) forced the industry to carry more than the requisite inventory, and that inventory remains slightly escalated even though it is more comfortable than the prior year. Normalising inventory is a stated focus area.

Component segment decline. The component segment reported a 23.1% year-on-year decline in the quarter. Components currently cater largely to AC-related items (controllers, cross-flow fans, copper tubing) and are therefore seasonal, tracking AC growth.

Qualified audit conclusion. The limited review report on the consolidated results contains a qualified conclusion: trade receivables of the Holding Company include a disputed balance of INR 1,961 lakhs against which no provision has been recognised, as the company believes there is a high probability of recovery.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Highest-ever quarterly revenue. Q1 FY27 revenue of INR 886 crores was the company’s highest ever, with broad-based growth across the portfolio.

RAC momentum. The RAC segment grew 43.8% year-on-year, split roughly into 30% volume growth and 12–15% value growth from increased average selling prices and commodity pass-through. Volume growth was largely driven by the anchor customer. Management stated the AC industry is expected to grow around 20% this year and that EPACK expects to surpass industry growth, as it did in Q1.

SDA and LDA scaling. SDA and LDA grew 68.9% year-on-year, led by air fryers and washing machines. SDA/LDA revenue rose from approximately INR 80 crores to INR 130 crores. Gross margins in SDA/LDA are described as roughly 1.5–2 percentage points above AC at gross margin level, though not at EBITDA level given shared facilities. Top-load fully automatic washing machines are already in production for three large national and multinational brands; front-load washing machine pilot production is targeted for end-September/October, with Hisense.

Diversification and customer de-risking. The company now serves more than 72 customers across 19 product categories, with three new categories added in the quarter and four more in the pipeline for later in the year. Management expects to reach close to 75 customers and 20 product lines by year-end. Top 2 customer concentration was reduced to 37% in FY 2025-26.

Hisense partnership. Through January–June, EPACK produced close to 60,000 air conditioners for Hisense, contributing approximately INR 120 crores of revenue, of which about 35,000 units and roughly INR 65 crores came in Q1 alone. The cumulative five-year revenue expectation from the Hisense partnership is INR 8,000 crores across ACs, washing machines and other appliances, with FY26-27 as the first year; management said the first-year estimate is largely on track.

Capacity utilisation ramp. Q1 utilisation was close to 90% at Dehradun and Bhiwadi (management also cited “more than 85%” for the two older plants) and close to 50% at Sri City, up from less than 25% previously. FY26 full-year utilisation was about 55% at Bhiwadi/Dehradun and under 25% at Sri City. For FY27, management targets overall utilisation above 60% across all three plants, with Sri City improving significantly.

Channel inventory position. Management described the current AC channel inventory situation as among the most comfortable, with trade having largely liquidated inventory and total trade/brand inventory estimated at 3.5–4 million units, below what would be typical at this point. The entire growth in the last quarter and Q1 is attributed to new BEE-rated products, with old-rated product largely flushed from the channel.

Compressor supply. The government has allowed compressor imports until end of this year, and domestic capacity already installed and in the pipeline — expected operational by end-December — is viewed as broadly in line with industry demand. Management does not foresee significant procurement challenges and does not plan to set up compressor capacity in the near term, relying instead on tie-ups with large compressor suppliers.

State incentive and capex. An MoU with the Andhra Pradesh government covers committed investments of INR 1,085 crores over five years from January 2024, with about 35 acres already allotted. The state scheme refunds approximately 50% of total capex as an investment subsidy over 10 years. Of the previously announced ~INR 450 crores capex programme, INR 330–350 crores has been booked, with INR 40–45 crores in CWIP and INR 60–70 crores of additional capex expected for the balance of the year. Q1 capex was about INR 10 crores.

PLI rollback as a margin lever. PLI benefits of roughly 1.5–2% were historically shared roughly 50-50 with customers. FY26-27 is the last PLI year for EPACK, and the company has begun rolling back the PLI discounts passed to brand customers, targeting complete reversal by year-end, which management expects to support more normalised EBITDA next year.

Market context. The domestic RAC market is projected to grow at a 16% CAGR (2025–2030), and the SDA market is expected to reach USD 27.59 billion by 2031. Tailwinds cited include government initiatives such as Atmanirbhar Bharat, Make in India, the PLI Scheme for White Goods and ECMS, rising rural penetration, disposable incomes and urbanisation.

3. Key Risks

  • Customer concentration in RAC volume growth, with the anchor customer identified as the primary driver of the 30% volume growth.
  • Commodity and currency volatility, with quarterly price-reset contracts creating timing lags between cost increases and pass-through; forex movements are not fully mitigated.
  • Seasonality, with Q2 and Q3 historically loss-making and dependent on the pace of SDA/LDA and washing machine ramp-up to offset.
  • Regulatory and standards changes, including BEE star rating revisions, BIS/QCO amendments on compressors and copper, and the compressor import regime, which have driven elevated inventory and working capital needs.
  • Execution risk on new capacity, particularly Sri City utilisation and the front-load washing machine ramp, which underpin operating leverage expectations.
  • Disputed receivable of INR 1,961 lakhs with no provision recognised, which is the basis of the auditor’s qualified conclusion.
  • Working capital and finance cost pressure if inventory normalisation does not proceed as planned.
  • Joint venture losses widening, per the Q1 FY27 results commentary.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Revenue growth. Management does not provide forward revenue numbers but stated AC industry growth of ~20% for the year and expects EPACK to surpass it, with SDA/LDA growing faster than AC. Observed: Q1 FY27 revenue grew 33.8% year-on-year, RAC grew 43.8%, and SDA/LDA grew 68.9%, consistent with the stated expectation of outpacing the industry and faster non-AC growth.

Margin trajectory. Management described current EBITDA excluding PLI at about 6.5%, with PLI contributing roughly 1.5–2% historically, and said it is rolling back PLI discounts to customers with the aim of full reversal by end of the year, supporting more normalised EBITDA next year. Observed: Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 6.21% with no PLI accrued, versus 8.24% reported a year earlier including PLI; on a like-to-like basis management characterised the improvement as ~15 basis points. Management also stated near-term profitability is held back by the investment cycle and input costs rather than demand or competitiveness.

Capacity utilisation. Management targets overall utilisation above 60% across the three plants for FY27, with Sri City improving significantly from under 25%. Observed: Q1 saw ~90% utilisation at Dehradun and Bhiwadi and close to 50% at Sri City, versus FY26 full-year figures of ~55% and under 25% respectively — a partial step toward the full-year target.

Hisense ramp. Management stated the first-year estimate for the Hisense partnership is largely on track, with front-load washing machine production targeted for end-September/October. Observed: ~60,000 ACs and ~INR 120 crores of revenue delivered January–June, with ~35,000 units and ~INR 65 crores in Q1; management affirmed the front-load timeline remains on track.

Seasonality normalisation. Management said it expects four to six quarters to bring Q2/Q3 losses largely under control, declining to give a specific Q2 PAT outlook as forward-looking. Observed: no Q2 FY27 results are available in the material; the guidance remains a multi-quarter objective.

Capex. Management guided to INR 60–70 crores of additional capex for the balance of FY27, with no significant increase in depreciation expected over the remaining three quarters. Observed: Q1 capex was ~INR 10 crores, with INR 40–45 crores in CWIP, consistent with the stated pipeline.

Working capital. Management said cash flow is stable, no incremental working capital requirement is anticipated from growth, and working capital days run 50–60. Observed: finance cost rose ~INR 3–4 crores on higher working capital, and management acknowledged inventory remains slightly escalated, with normalisation an ongoing focus.

Broker Narrative

The broker maintained a Buy rating throughout, consistently citing RAC growth, SDA/LDA diversification, and the Hisense ramp-up as core positives. Over time, the narrative shifted from early structural optimism around low RAC penetration, PLI benefits, and component expansion to a more defensive tone acknowledging input-cost inflation, forex losses, PLI accounting changes, and margin compression. Competition and execution risk remained persistent concerns, but the completed calls all produced negative actual returns and were marked disappointments.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning of steep competition from new/existing players materialized, as the last report still lists intensifying competition as a key risk and all completed calls produced negative returns.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that component business expansion and PLI benefits would support growth failed, as components de-grew 23% YoY to ₹85 crore and the PLI income accounting change hit EBITDA and PAT.
  • The hope that SDA/LDA diversification would improve margins did not materialize, as EBITDA margin fell 203 bps YoY to 6.21% despite SDA/LDA revenue growing 69% YoY.
