SoftTech Engineers: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Government procurement and payment cycles. The company operates heavily in government-linked urban governance and infrastructure approvals, where extended procurement timelines and high days sales outstanding are persistent features. Days sales outstanding stood at 462 days in FY22 and remained elevated at 372 days in FY25 before improving to 260 days in FY26, indicating that collection cycles, while improving, remain long.

Competitive intensity. AI-led compliance is attracting new entrants, intensifying competition in the company’s core permitting and compliance domain.

Talent scarcity. There is a shortage of talent combining building regulation expertise with machine learning capability, which constrains execution capacity in the company’s AI-driven product lines.

Concentration risk. The business carries concentration risk in a single customer type, geography, and regulatory domain, leaving it exposed to shifts in government priorities or regional demand.

FY25 stress. FY25 was a year of pressure: standalone PBT fell to ₹6.03 Cr (6.5% margin) from ₹9.36 Cr in FY24, and consolidated PAT fell to ₹1.33 Cr (1.4% margin). The company describes this as transient, with FY26 showing recovery.

Cash flow. Free cash flow was negative in FY25 at (4.85) and remained marginally negative at (0.09) in FY26, indicating that cash generation, while improving, has not yet turned decisively positive.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record FY26 rebound. Standalone revenue reached ₹128.30 Cr in FY26 versus ₹93.36 Cr in FY25, with EBITDA of ₹34.39 Cr and PAT of ₹9.57 Cr. Consolidated revenue reached ₹132.90 Cr with EBITDA of ₹32.19 Cr and PAT of ₹5.33 Cr. PBT and PAT indices of 2.15x and 2.31x standalone, and 2.73x and 4.00x consolidated, are described as the highest in the series.

Recurring revenue shift. SaaS and pay-per-use revenue grew to ₹31.72 Cr, representing 25% of standalone revenue, with pay-per-use growing 38% year-on-year and a five-year CAGR of 22%.

Working capital improvement. DSO fell from 462 days (FY22) to 260 days (FY26), a 43% reduction. The cash conversion cycle improved to 169 days in FY26 from 362 days in FY22. Management attributes this to pay-per-use transactions enabling immediate cash realization and milestone-linked billing with tighter credit controls.

Order book and pipeline. A confirmed order book of ₹231.99 Cr and a pipeline of ₹436.23 Cr provide growth visibility.

JNPA port and maritime breakthrough. SoftTech secured a ₹92.95 crore project from Jawaharlal Nehru Port Authority, India’s largest container port, to design, build and operate an AI/ML-enabled, cyber-aware Digital Twin and Digital Twin Command & Control Centre covering approximately 34 sq km. The project is delivered in partnership with Dassault Systèmes using their 3DEXPERIENCE platform, with SoftTech leading solution design, AI/ML development, systems integration, and long-term operations. This marks entry into the port and maritime sector.

Product portfolio expansion. CivitTwin, CivitTDR, and CivitInfra were launched in FY26, with CivitSustain and CivitMetaverse at order stage. Traction includes a BMC LOI and Germany launch for CivitTwin, a Mumbai launch for CivitTDR, a Gujarat order and Germany launch for CivitPermit/CivitPlan, an AAI launch for CivitInfra, Middle East traction and a SCON Infra order for CivitBuild, a Mitsubishi order and GIFT City pipeline for CivitSustain, and PIM & AXL orders from Metawolf for CivitMetaverse.

Domestic and international expansion. Opportunities include penetration of unserved urban authorities across India, transaction volume growth from construction growth and PMAY-U 2.0, extension into green buildings, and international expansion. Management targets Germany as a flagship market, aims to close two CivitBuild deals in the Middle East, grow Civit.ai/BIM services in the USA, and explore a Southeast Asia partnership model.

Technology positioning. AI-led internal productivity adoption, IoT and Metaverse capability creation, AI/ML/GIS embedded into Civit Core products, and a strong USA pipeline for Civit.ai and BIM-led services.

3. Key Risks

  • Government procurement and payment cycles with extended timelines and high DSO.
  • Competitive intensity in AI-led compliance from new entrants.
  • Scarcity of talent combining building regulation and machine learning.
  • Concentration risk in a single customer type, geography, and regulatory domain.
  • Negative free cash flow in FY25 and FY26, though improving.
  • Dependence on conversion of order book and pipeline into revenue.
  • Execution risk on new platform launches and international expansion.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

DSO below 200 days. Management targets DSO below 200 days. Observed: DSO improved to 260 days in FY26 from 372 days in FY25, progressing toward but not yet reaching the target.

PAT toward double digits. Management targets PAT growth toward double digits. Observed: Standalone PAT was ₹9.57 Cr in FY26 (7.5% margin) versus ₹4.14 Cr in FY25 (4.4% margin), a recovery but not yet double-digit margin. Consolidated PAT was ₹5.33 Cr (4.0% margin) versus ₹1.33 Cr (1.4% margin).

Recurring revenue mix shift. Management targets raising pay-per-use share by 10%–15% every year and launching subscriptions across products. Observed: SaaS and pay-per-use revenue reached 25% of standalone revenue in FY26, with pay-per-use growing 38% year-on-year.

International expansion. Management targets Germany as a flagship market, two CivitBuild deals in the Middle East, USA growth in Civit.ai/BIM, and Southeast Asia partnership exploration. Observed: Germany launch for CivitPermit and CivitTwin, Middle East traction for CivitBuild, and a strong USA pipeline for Civit.ai and BIM services.

EBITDA margin improvement. Management targets improving EBITDA margin. Observed: Standalone EBITDA margin was 26.8% in FY26 versus 26.9% in FY25, broadly flat. Consolidated EBITDA margin was 24.2% in FY26 versus 23.4% in FY25, a modest improvement.

Order book maintenance. Management targets maintaining a strong order book. Observed: Confirmed order book of ₹231.99 Cr and pipeline of ₹436.23 Cr.

JNPA project. Management stated a commitment to driving technology-led innovation and delivering transformative solutions tailored to the construction, infrastructure, and transportation sectors, noting that JNPA is growing towards 10 million TEU capacity by 2027. Observed: The ₹92.95 crore JNPA award represents execution against this stated direction.

Working capital improvements. Management targets sustaining working capital improvements. Observed: Cash conversion cycle improved to 169 days in FY26 from 270 days in FY25, and DSO improved to 260 days from 372 days.
