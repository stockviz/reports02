Prozone Realty Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Nagpur aviation NOC dispute (Hagwood subsidiary): The subsidiary’s original aviation No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the Airport Authority of India, Nagpur (AAIN), permitting construction up to 48 metres, was cancelled in August 2017 on renewal application. Appeals and writ proceedings followed. The Appellate Committee rejected an appeal on November 22, 2024, but the Bombay High Court (Nagpur Bench) quashed that rejection on July 8, 2025 and directed AAI to conduct an Aeronautical Study and CNS Simulation Study. AAI’s review petition was dismissed on April 2, 2026. Despite the subsidiary’s compliance and payments (Rs. 23.60 lakhs and Rs. 16.99 lakhs made on August 22, 2025), AAI had not commenced the studies, prompting a contempt petition filed on July 29, 2026, which is yet to be listed for hearing. Part occupancy certificates cover 242 of 264 flats up to the 11th floor, with part OC for 22 flats pending with the Nagpur Municipal Corporation.
  • Regulatory/compliance matter involving the erstwhile Deputy Managing Director: The MCA rejected the company’s application (dated June 10, 2020) for re-appointment of its Deputy Managing Director under Section 196 read with Schedule V of the Companies Act, 2013, by order dated September 10, 2024. The director ceased to hold that position and became a Non-executive Director. The company’s response objecting to the rejection is awaiting a reply. A postal ballot notice was issued on August 7, 2026 seeking shareholder approval by special resolution for waiver of recovery of remuneration of Rs. 512.37 lakhs paid from February 27, 2020 until the order date, and refund of a salary advance of Rs. 169.63 lakhs is expected from the concerned director, both expected on or before September 10, 2026.
  • Weak continuing operations profitability: Consolidated continuing operations recorded a loss before tax of Rs. 703.66 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, versus a loss of Rs. 449.18 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2025 and a loss of Rs. 371.03 lakhs for the quarter ended March 31, 2026. The outright sales segment posted a segment loss before interest and tax of Rs. 342.36 lakhs for the quarter.
  • Rising finance costs: Standalone finance costs rose to Rs. 687.71 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, compared with Rs. 37.63 lakhs a year earlier. Consolidated finance costs were Rs. 141.42 lakhs versus Rs. 70.06 lakhs a year earlier.
  • External environment: Input cost inflation including rising WPI and basic metal prices, a tight interest rate and higher-for-longer global rate environment, weakening urban consumer sentiment, and potential regulatory and compliance challenges.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strategic disinvestment completed: On August 24, 2026, the company completed the sale and transfer of equity shareholdings in Kruti Realtors and Developers Private Limited (100%), Alliance Mall Developers Co. Pvt. Ltd. (51.95% held by the company plus 48.05% held by Kruti), and Empire Mall Private Limited (34.71% held by the company plus 65.29% held by Kruti) to Inorbit Malls (India) Private Limited. The aggregate gross consideration is INR 1,242.50 crore. Kruti, Alliance, and Empire ceased to be subsidiaries, and FVDPL ceased to be an indirect subsidiary.
  • Capital reallocation: The company approved the strategic disinvestment of its mature mall business to redirect capital toward premium commercial and retail platforms in Mumbai while retaining its fully paid-up land banks and active residential portfolios.
  • Continued development focus: The company will continue to pursue its real estate development business and evaluate opportunities across commercial and residential segments, with a focus on the Mumbai Metropolitan Region, in addition to developing retained land parcels through relevant subsidiaries to realise their future development potential.
  • Market opportunities: Supply scarcity and pricing power in the Mumbai Metropolitan Region (MMR), robust institutional demand for stabilised retail assets, growth in Grade A office leasing driven by Global Capability Centres (GCCs), and rising values in the residential real estate market.
  • Discontinued operations performance: The leasing operations classified as discontinued (Alliance Mall and Empire Mall) contributed profit before tax of Rs. 1,075.09 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, with revenue from operations of Rs. 3,552.14 lakhs.
  • Fair value gains: Significant fair value gains on remeasuring financial assets (FVOCI) arose from remeasurement of investments in subsidiaries and joint venture based on an independent valuer’s report on property fair values — Rs. 35,291.88 lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026, and Rs. 35,131.37 lakhs for the year ended March 31, 2026 (standalone).

3. Key Risks

  • Contingent exposure on the Nagpur project: No adjustments have been made to the carrying value of inventory for floors 12 to 14 of the four towers, aggregating Rs. 6,818.25 lakhs, and no provision has been made towards expected demolition cost, rehabilitation cost, or interest payable to customers on cancellation of bookings. Bank guarantees of Rs. 396.00 lakhs (potential demolition) and Rs. 330.84 lakhs (rehabilitation) have been secured by the Nagpur Municipal Corporation from the subsidiary. The outcome of the contempt petition and the pending AAI studies remains unresolved.
  • Dependence on regulatory outcomes: The Nagpur matter depends on AAI conducting the court-directed Aeronautical Study and CNS Simulation Study; continued non-compliance by AAI is the basis of the contempt petition.
  • Remuneration recovery risk: If shareholder approval for the waiver is not obtained within the statutory timeline under Section 197(10), and if the salary advance is not refunded by September 10, 2026, the recovery position remains exposed.
  • Transaction completion conditions: As of the date of approval of the financial results (August 14, 2026), compliance with certain conditions precedent to the disinvestment was still under progress. Management also stated that the hiving-off of the land assets owned by Alliance and Empire is still under process, with necessary steps being undertaken and further disclosure to follow on completion.
  • Continuing operations losses: The outright sales segment continues to report losses, and consolidated continuing operations recorded a net loss of Rs. 683.56 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026.
  • Reliance on unaudited/management-certified components: The consolidated results include seven subsidiaries not reviewed by the auditors (total net loss after tax of Rs. 700.09 lakhs and total comprehensive income of Rs. 30,425.71 lakhs for the quarter), one jointly controlled entity not reviewed by the auditors (share of net profit of Rs. 20.95 lakhs), and one subsidiary whose results were certified by management (net profit after tax of Rs. 8.94 lakhs).

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Disinvestment completion: Management anticipated that FY2027 would be defined by completion of the proposed disinvestment, application of proceeds to reduce borrowings and fund premium Mumbai acquisitions, and continued execution on retained residential and retail development projects. Observed: the sale and transfer of Kruti, Alliance, and Empire to Inorbit Malls was completed on August 24, 2026 for aggregate gross consideration of INR 1,242.50 crore. However, the hiving-off of the land assets owned by Alliance and Empire remains under process, and conditions precedent were still being complied with as of August 14, 2026.
  • Nagpur NOC revalidation: Management believes the chances of revalidation of the original NOC are high, citing independent technical reports/studies (including CNS Simulation study, DVOR calibration report, obstacle limitation studies), receipt of Part OCs, the July 8, 2025 High Court order, and legal opinion. Observed: AAI had not commenced the directed studies despite the subsidiary’s compliance and payments, and a contempt petition was filed on July 29, 2026, yet to be listed for hearing. No adjustments or provisions were made in the results.
  • Remuneration waiver and salary advance refund: The company expects shareholder approval and refund of the salary advance of Rs. 169.63 lakhs by the concerned director on or before September 10, 2026, within the permissible period under Section 197(10). Observed: the postal ballot notice was issued on August 7, 2026; approval and refund were still pending as of the results date.
  • Discontinued operations classification: Management assessed Ind AS 105 requirements and concluded that the mall operations of Alliance Mall and Empire Mall meet the criteria for classification as discontinued operations as of June 30, 2026. Observed: these operations are presented separately, with prior period figures re-presented; the leasing segment (discontinued) reported profit before tax of Rs. 1,075.09 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, compared with Rs. 1,110.37 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2025.
  • Consolidation of subsidiaries: The consolidated results include Probliss Realty Private Limited (till January 20, 2026), Prozone Dream Realtors Pvt. Ltd. and Prozone Horizons Private Limited (both w.e.f. October 30, 2025), and Prozone Arcade Private Limited (w.e.f. February 17, 2026), reflecting changes in group composition during the year.
