DIAMONDYD — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Input cost inflation is the dominant near-term headwind. Management cites the ongoing Iran-USA War as causing significant inflationary pressure across key inputs, with palm oil and packaging laminate up around 20% YoY. Higher freight and packaging costs added to the pressure.
  • The company responded with calibrated price and grammage interventions, cost optimisation, productivity improvements and operational efficiencies, which it says substantially mitigated the inflation impact and protected margins.
  • Competitive intensity is a recurring theme: pressure from large FMCG players and strong regional brands, alongside fluctuating customer demand.
  • The demand environment is described as improving, implying it had previously been soft; the Q1 FY27 result is characterised as a return to the long-term growth trajectory after a period of subdued performance (FY25 included a loss at the PAT level).

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) was the strongest quarter in company history: income from operations of Rs. 4,904.3 million (Rs. 490 crore), up 20% YoY; operating EBITDA of Rs. 190.4 million versus Rs. 180.1 million; PAT of Rs. 24.7 million versus Rs. 6.9 million, up 258%.
  • Growth is described as broad-based, driven by improving consumption trends in key markets, encouraging response to recently launched products, momentum in Namkeen and potato chips, deeper distribution expansion, and quick commerce traction.
  • Quick commerce is a named growth lever, progressing through wider platform presence, expanded assortment and improving consumer traction. Modern trade presence is also being built.
  • Technology-led execution — Sales Force Automation and other digital interventions — is cited as enhancing productivity and market responsiveness.
  • The Board approved acquisition of RLOP Food Processing Pvt. Ltd. (up to Rs. 16.50 crore, expected completion on or before 15.09.2026), securing long-term leasehold rights over land for a proposed manufacturing facility. Management expects this to accelerate a modernised, upgraded plant, strengthening the long-term growth platform and margin profile.
  • Structural tailwinds cited: formalisation of the snacks industry, evolution of consumer purchasing channels, a large compounding snacks market (14% CAGR), expanding middle class and per-capita spending, unorganised-to-organised share gains, regional flavour rediscovery, and export opportunities.
  • FY2025-26 full-year outcomes: record revenue of Rs. 1,725 crore, gross margin expansion to 28.8%, EBITDA up 68% to Rs. 81.8 crore, PAT back to Rs. 9.7 crore after an FY25 loss, positive free cash flow of Rs. 35.3 crore, and a nearly debt-free balance sheet with over Rs. 93 crore in cash and bank balances.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and geopolitical risk: edible oil supply chains and palm oil/packaging laminate prices remain exposed to geopolitical disruption, with knock-on effects on fuel and packaging costs.
  • Inflation risk: broad inflationary pressures could outpace the company’s ability to offset them through price, grammage and cost actions.
  • Competitive risk: intensifying competition from large FMCG players and strong regional brands.
  • Demand risk: fluctuating customer demand.
  • Execution risk on the growth agenda: distribution expansion, innovation, quick commerce and the new manufacturing project all depend on continued disciplined execution.
  • Forward-looking statements are subject to regulatory changes, local political or economic developments and other factors that could cause actual results to differ.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • FY27 guidance: management expressed confidence in delivering double-digit revenue growth during FY27 while maintaining healthy margin levels, through innovation, deeper market penetration, disciplined execution and operational excellence.
  • Observed Q1 FY27 performance: 20% YoY revenue growth, the highest-ever quarterly revenue, with EBITDA broadly flat YoY (Rs. 190.4 million vs Rs. 180.1 million) and PAT up 258% off a low base. This is consistent with the double-digit growth guidance, while the modest EBITDA movement illustrates the margin pressure from input inflation that management says it substantially mitigated.
  • Long-term operating model: roughly 15% revenue growth, EBITDA margin above 10%, and ROCE in the 15–20% range. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin implied by the disclosed figures is below that threshold, indicating the margin target remains an aspiration rather than a current run-rate.
  • FY26 outcome versus prior framing: the full-year FY2025-26 results (record revenue, 28.8% gross margin, 68% EBITDA growth, return to PAT profitability, positive free cash flow, near debt-free balance sheet) provide the base off which the FY27 guidance is set.

5. Other Key Investor Facts

  • The 17th Annual General Meeting is scheduled for Thursday, September 24, 2026, at 3:30 p.m. IST via VC/OAVM. Recommended dividend for FY2025-26 is Rs. 0.50 per fully paid-up equity share of Rs. 5, with a record date of September 18, 2026.
  • Officials will attend the Emkay Confluence 2026 conference on Friday, August 14, 2026 (physical interaction); no unpublished price sensitive information is proposed to be shared.
  • Board actions disclosed: reclassification of certain promoters to the public category, allotment of 19,428 equity shares under ESARP 2018, grant of 50,906 ESARs, acquisition of 100% of RLOP Food Processing Pvt. Ltd. for up to Rs. 16.50 crore, and re-appointment of Mr. Amit Kumat as MD & CEO and Mr. Apoorva Kumat as Executive Director.
  • Operations: 14 manufacturing facilities (6 owned — Indore 1&2, Assam, Kolkata, Rajkot 1&2; 8 contract — Kolkata, Jharkhand, Tumkur, Kanpur, Karnal, Patna, Hissar, Gwalior), more than 5,000 super/sub distributors, presence across 27 states and 4 union territories, and roughly 2.5 million retail outlets.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative shifted from a cautious Hold in 2023, dominated by concerns over muted revenue growth, PLI uncertainty, competitive pressure, and rich valuations, to a Buy in 2026 that highlighted robust 20% revenue growth and distribution gains. The demand outlook completely reversed: initial softness in rural and low-income consumption gave way to an improving consumption environment. Margin concerns persisted, but the cause changed from competitive spending to raw material price inflation, including palm oil and packaging laminates. The valuation concern that capped upside in the first report did not appear in the last, as the stock price presumably remained attractive.

Fears that came true

  • The valuation and upside constraint fear materialized as the stock delivered a negative -4.8% return over the period following the first report.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of sustained gross margin expansion failed, as gross margin contracted to 27.2% by 1QFY27 due to higher raw material costs.
