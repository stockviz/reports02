Headwinds and Challenges

KSR Footwear Limited was incorporated on 22 August 2023 and became the resulting company of a Scheme of Arrangement under which the distribution business of Khadim India Limited was demerged into it. The Scheme was sanctioned by the Hon’ble NCLT, Kolkata Bench, by order dated 27 March 2025, became effective from 1 May 2025, and the demerged undertaking vested in the company as a going concern from the appointed date of 1 April 2025.

The company’s first reported period as an operating entity, the quarter ended 30 June 2025, showed a loss before tax of ₹50.78 million and a loss for the period of ₹48.27 million, with total income of ₹526.03 million against total expenses of ₹576.81 million. Depreciation and amortisation of ₹28.57 million and finance costs of ₹8.87 million weighed on the result, alongside cost of materials consumed of ₹322.72 million and other expenses of ₹107.30 million.

The company’s listing process was drawn out. Applications for listing of shares were filed with NSE and BSE on 19 July 2025 and 23 July 2025 respectively; listing approval was granted on 5 September 2025 and trading approval on 25 November 2025, effective from 27 November 2025. The interim audited financial statements for the quarter ended 30 June 2025 were submitted to the exchange on 27 November 2025, and the auditor’s report on those statements is dated 14 October 2025.

The company received NSE queries dated 17 July 2026 relating to those financial statements, covering the absence of segment details, the form of the financial results submitted, and the absence of comparative figures. In its reply dated 24 July 2026, the company explained that it operates in a single segment (Footwear), that the results had been submitted in machine readable form with a higher-resolution copy subsequently provided, and that no comparative figures were included because there was no major business activity prior to the appointed date of 1 April 2025.

The company’s balance sheet as at 30 June 2025 shows total borrowings of ₹150.00 million in current financial liabilities and ₹358.17 million in non-current financial liabilities, alongside trade payables of ₹656.12 million and lease liabilities. Liquidity risk is addressed in the notes through maintenance of adequate reserves, banking facilities and monitoring of cash flows.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

The demerger created KSR Footwear as a listed entity holding the distribution business of Khadim India Limited, with the business transferred as a going concern. The company is a wholly owned subsidiary of Khadim India Limited and is engaged in the footwear business, with revenue earned primarily from sale of footwear and leather accessories.

The company reported revenue from operations of ₹520.61 million for the quarter ended 30 June 2025, with other income of ₹5.42 million. Cash generated from operations for the quarter was positive, supported by working capital movements including a ₹77.28 million release from trade receivables, loans and advances and other assets, an ₹84.86 million release from inventories, and an ₹104.64 million increase in trade payables, other liabilities and provisions.

The company’s customer base is described as diverse, limiting credit concentration risk, with no single customer exceeding 20 per cent of gross financial assets at the end of the period. The company also received government grants, recognised as income on the basis of qualifying criteria, and reported interest income.

The company held its 3rd Annual General Meeting on Monday, 24 August 2026, at which shareholders approved the adoption of the Audited Financial Statements for the financial year ended 31 March 2026 together with the reports of the Board and Auditors, and re-appointed Mr. Rittick Roy Burman as a Director. The existence of audited financial statements for FY2025-26 indicates a full financial year of operations following the demerger.

Key Risks

The company identifies market risk, credit risk and liquidity risk as the principal financial risks facing the business. On interest rate risk, the company states that as the majority of its financial assets and liabilities are either non-interest bearing or fixed interest bearing, its net exposure is negligible. On price risk, it states that short-term funds are invested primarily in debt mutual funds and do not pose significant price risk.

Credit risk is managed through a diverse customer base, centrally issued credit guidelines and business-specific credit policies, with overdue balances evaluated by age, credit circumstances and counterparty track record. The company uses a simplified approach to compute expected credit loss allowances for trade receivables based on a provision matrix drawing on historical credit loss experience. The expected loss provision moved from an opening balance of ₹22.25 million to a closing balance of ₹26.22 million as at 30 June 2025, after provisions made of ₹3.97 million.

Foreign currency risk arises because the company undertakes transactions denominated mainly in US Dollar, Euro and Pound Sterling, and financial assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currency are subject to reinstatement risk.

Liquidity risk is managed by maintaining adequate reserves, banking facilities and reserve borrowing facilities, continuously monitoring forecast and actual cash flows, and matching maturity profiles of financial assets and liabilities. The contractual maturity table as at 30 June 2025 shows total financial liabilities of ₹1,214.95 million, of which ₹856.78 million falls due in less than one year, ₹261.30 million between one and five years, and ₹96.87 million beyond five years.

The notes also identify estimation uncertainties with a risk of causing material adjustment to carrying amounts within the next financial year, principally the useful lives of property, plant and equipment and intangible assets, and actuarial valuation assumptions for gratuity and unpaid leave balances.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

The company’s communications to the exchanges are clarifications rather than forward-looking guidance. In its reply dated 24 July 2026 to NSE queries dated 17 July 2026, management stated that the company is engaged in one business segment, Footwear, and that segment-wise reporting is therefore not applicable. It also stated that the financial results as at 30 June 2025 had been submitted in machine readable form, and provided a high-resolution file for the record. On comparative figures, management stated that as there was no major business activity prior to the appointed date of 1 April 2025, no comparative figures had been included in the interim audited financial statements for the quarter ended 30 June 2025.

Observed performance for that quarter is consistent with the explanation of a business transferred at the appointed date: the company reported revenue from operations of ₹520.61 million and a loss for the period of ₹48.27 million, with the balance sheet reflecting the assets and liabilities vested under the Scheme, including property, plant and equipment of ₹340.90 million, right-of-use assets of ₹332.19 million, trade receivables of ₹472.36 million, and total financial liabilities of ₹1,214.95 million.

The company’s capital management statement describes an aim of maintaining a strong capital base to safeguard business continuity and augmenting internal generations with judicious use of borrowing facilities to meet working capital requirements and finance business growth. The company states it is not subject to any externally imposed capital requirements. The AGM held on 24 August 2026 approved audited financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2026, providing the first full-year reporting reference point following the demerger.
