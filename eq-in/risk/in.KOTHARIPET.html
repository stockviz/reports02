Kothari Petrochemicals — Recent Announcements Analysis

Headwinds and Challenges

The company’s most recent quarterly performance points to softening momentum. Revenue from operations for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 stood at ₹14,650.37 lakhs, down from ₹16,066.91 lakhs in the same quarter a year earlier (June 30, 2025) and lower than the ₹15,285.80 lakhs recorded in the preceding quarter (March 31, 2026). Profit before tax also eased to ₹2,245.53 lakhs from ₹2,409.89 lakhs sequentially, and profit for the period declined to ₹1,702.47 lakhs from ₹1,780.23 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter. Earnings per share moved down to ₹2.89 from ₹3.03 sequentially and ₹2.99 year-on-year.

Cost pressure is visible in the materials line: cost of materials consumed rose to ₹10,053.44 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter from ₹8,244.01 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter, even as revenue declined over the same period. Utilities and stores also increased year-on-year (₹1,012.40 lakhs versus ₹799.29 lakhs). The change in inventories of finished goods swung to a negative ₹820.55 lakhs from a positive ₹1,177.65 lakhs sequentially, indicating inventory drawdown rather than build-up.

On the governance side, the 37th AGM resolutions were passed with dissent recorded on every item. Resolution 2 (re-appointment of Mr. Arjun B. Kothari as a rotating director) and Resolution 3 (ratification of cost auditor remuneration) each saw 414 votes against, while Resolution 4 (commission to the Non-Executive Chairperson) attracted 1,245 votes against — the highest dissent of the four. Resolution 1 (adoption of audited financial statements) saw 14 votes against. While all resolutions carried with requisite majority, the presence of dissent across all items is notable.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

The company maintains a single, focused business segment — manufacturing Poly Iso Butylene — with no subsidiary, associate, or joint venture as of June 30, 2026, suggesting a streamlined operating structure. Other income improved year-on-year to ₹286.15 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter from ₹222.55 lakhs in June 2025, providing some support to total income.

Finance costs continued to trend lower, falling to ₹12.47 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter from ₹17.27 lakhs a year earlier and ₹15.36 lakhs sequentially, which may reflect reduced borrowing costs or deleveraging. Depreciation remained broadly stable. The full-year FY2026 (year ended March 31, 2026) revenue stood at ₹59,138.67 lakhs with profit for the year at ₹7,249.00 lakhs and basic EPS of ₹12.32, providing a baseline of profitability.

The company continued its cost auditor relationship with M/s. P. Raju Iyer, M. Pandurangan & Associates for FY2026-27, and the Board approved the unaudited results for the June 2026 quarter at its meeting on August 14, 2026, indicating ongoing compliance and reporting discipline.

Key Risks

The sequential and year-on-year decline in revenue alongside rising material costs points to margin pressure risk. The negative change in finished goods inventory in the June 2026 quarter, compared with a build-up in the prior quarter, could signal either strong offtake or constrained production — the source material does not clarify which.

Concentration risk is inherent in a single-segment, single-product business (Poly Iso Butylene) with no diversification across subsidiaries or joint ventures. The company’s exposure to raw material price volatility is evident from the sharp sequential increase in material costs.

Governance-related risk is reflected in the dissent votes across all AGM resolutions, particularly the 1,245 votes against the Chairperson’s commission (Resolution 4) and 414 votes against the rotating director re-appointment and cost auditor ratification. While these are minority positions, they indicate some shareholder unease.

The company relies on external auditors (P. Chandrasekar LLP for statutory review) and a practising company secretary firm (SJN Associates) for scrutinizer duties, with no internal audit function disclosed in the source material.
