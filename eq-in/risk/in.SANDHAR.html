Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure from cost inflation. Q1 FY27 consolidated EBITDA margin declined to 8.49% from 9.34% a year earlier, even as revenue grew 26.77% to ₹1,381.89 Cr. The company attributes this to a minimum wage impact of ₹5.84 Cr in Haryana and Uttarakhand, an energy cost impact of ₹7.50 Cr, and a PBT loss of ₹7.41 Cr from new projects in India that are yet to achieve volumes. India Business EBITDA margin fell to 8.20% from 10.00%, and India Business EBITDA grew only 6.24% against 29.57% revenue growth.

Standalone business contraction. Standalone revenue from operations declined 8.54% year-on-year to ₹665.96 Cr, and fell 13.99% quarter-on-quarter from ₹774.25 Cr in Q4 FY26. Standalone EBITDA fell 3.20% YoY and 24.25% QoQ. The company notes that prior and corresponding quarter figures are not comparable because the Castings and Sheet Metal businesses were restructured into wholly owned subsidiaries during FY26.

Overseas revenue decline. Overseas subsidiaries’ revenue in Euro terms fell 7.50% YoY to €11.71 Mn, though in INR terms it rose 4.75% to ₹128.77 Cr. Overseas EBT remained negative at €(0.37) Mn, an improvement from €(1.19) Mn. Accumulated reserves/losses for overseas entities swung to €(0.35) Mn from €0.96 Mn.

New projects in initial stages. Four India projects are listed as being in initial stages with expected turnaround timelines: SCL Acquired Business (Hosur, ADC-HPDC & LPDC) targeted Q4 FY27; Sanaswadi (Pune, Cabins & Fabrication) targeted Q3 FY27; Chennai Oragadam (Sheet Metal Parts) targeted FY28; and EV Powertrain Business (Haryana) targeted FY28. These projects carried ₹341 Cr of investment and generated ₹130 Cr of revenue with EBITDA of ₹0.88 Cr and a PBT loss of ₹(7.41) Cr in Q1 FY27.

Macro and geopolitical risks. The company operates amid geopolitical uncertainties, particularly ongoing tensions in West Asia, which may impact crude oil prices, commodity costs, freight rates, and global trade flows. Additional risks cited include volatility in fuel prices, foreign exchange fluctuations, supply chain disruptions, shipping route constraints, and broader macroeconomic uncertainties affecting demand patterns, input costs, and operational efficiencies.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Highest-ever quarterly revenue. Consolidated revenue reached ₹1,381.89 Cr in Q1 FY27, up 26.77% YoY, described as the highest-ever quarterly revenue achieved. India Business revenue grew 29.57% YoY to ₹1,253.12 Cr.

Profit growth ahead of revenue at consolidated level. Consolidated PBT rose 40.84% YoY to ₹51.14 Cr and PAT rose 33.14% YoY to ₹37.28 Cr. Consolidated PBT margin improved to 3.70% from 3.33%. Standalone PBT rose 21.21% YoY to ₹41.89 Cr with standalone PBT margin improving to 6.29% from 4.75%, and standalone PAT rose 20.46% to ₹30.61 Cr.

India operations excluding new projects. Revenue grew 29.34% YoY to ₹1,122.71 Cr, EBITDA grew 8.55% to ₹101.89 Cr, and PBT grew 19.65% to ₹62.63 Cr. However, EBITDA margin in this subset declined to 9.08% from 10.81%.

Overseas profitability improvement. Overseas EBITDA rose 153.85% in Euro terms to €1.32 Mn and 186.05% in INR terms to ₹14.56 Cr. Overseas EBT loss narrowed 68.91% in Euro terms and 64.58% in INR terms. Equity held by STL in overseas entities rose 28.00% in Euro terms and 49.27% in INR terms.

Capacity expansion and new capabilities. The ADC new plant at Avigna Industrial Park (HPDC & LPDC) is described as one of the largest casting business facilities in Sandhar, with Phase-I expected to start by end of August 2026 and target completion March 2027. The Chennai (Oragadam) sheet metal fabricated parts Phase-I has started, with Phase-II expected by March 2027. HPDC castings have started into higher tonnage of 1250T machine capacity. A new special kind of innovative wheel rim was introduced, and the company is focused on automation and robotization of manufacturing.

Industry and policy tailwinds. Growth prospects are supported by favourable demographics, increasing vehicle penetration, infrastructure development, technological advancements, and the transition toward sustainable mobility solutions like electric vehicles. Additional drivers include strong domestic demand fundamentals, policy support, growing export competitiveness, continuous investments in R&D and technology, and integration of new business lines such as the aluminium die-casting business. Management notes the Indian automotive industry is expected to maintain a positive growth trajectory.

FY26 full-year performance. Consolidated revenue from operations for FY26 was ₹4,852.09 Cr with EBITDA of ₹512.99 Cr (10.57% margin), PBT before exceptional items and share of loss in joint ventures of ₹250.86 Cr, and net profit of ₹198.67 Cr (4.09% of revenue). Standalone FY26 revenue was ₹3,044.44 Cr with EBITDA of ₹345.01 Cr (11.33% margin) and net profit of ₹179.12 Cr (5.88%).

Key Risks

  • Margin compression from minimum wage increases, energy costs, and under-absorbed new projects.
  • Continued losses at new projects until they achieve volume, with turnaround timelines extending to FY28 for some.
  • Overseas operations still loss-making at the EBT level, with accumulated losses in overseas entities.
  • Standalone revenue contraction and the non-comparability of prior periods following the restructuring of Castings and Sheet Metal into wholly owned subsidiaries.
  • Geopolitical tensions in West Asia affecting crude oil, commodities, freight, and trade flows.
  • Fuel price volatility, foreign exchange fluctuations, supply chain disruptions, and shipping route constraints.
  • Macroeconomic uncertainty affecting demand, input costs, and operational efficiency.
  • Execution risk on capacity expansion projects, including the ADC Phase-I start and target completion dates.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements. Management states the Indian automotive industry is expected to maintain a positive growth trajectory supported by favourable demographics, increasing vehicle penetration, and infrastructure development. The company remains committed to expanding capabilities, enhancing operational excellence, pursuing organic and inorganic growth opportunities, and achieving carbon neutrality by 2050. Project timelines are provided: ADC Phase-I expected to start by end of August 2026 with target completion March 2027; Chennai sheet metal Phase-II expected by March 2027; and new project turnaround expectations of Q3 FY27 (Sanaswadi), Q4 FY27 (SCL Hosur), and FY28 (Chennai sheet metal, EV Powertrain).

Observed performance against these timelines. The Chennai (Oragadam) sheet metal Phase-I has already started, consistent with the stated project update. The ADC Phase-I start was guided for end of August 2026, and the Q1 FY27 results (quarter ended June 30, 2026) predate that target, so no confirmation of achievement is available in the supplied material. The new projects remained loss-making in Q1 FY27 with a PBT loss of ₹7.41 Cr, consistent with their description as being in initial stages and yet to achieve volumes.

Dividend and governance events. The Board recommended a final dividend of ₹4.00 per equity share (40%) of face value ₹10 for FY2025-26. The record date for determining eligibility is Friday, September 11, 2026, with payment on or before October 22, 2026, subject to member approval at the 34th AGM. The 34th AGM is scheduled for Tuesday, September 22, 2026 at 11:30 A.M. through video conferencing, with remote e-voting from September 19 to September 21, 2026. The Board appointed Smt. Gazal Kalra as an Additional Director and Non-Executive Independent Director effective August 11, 2026, for a five-year term subject to member approval, and re-appointed M/s Satija & Co. as Cost Auditors for FY2026-27. The statutory auditors issued an unmodified limited review report on the unaudited standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026.

Broker Narrative

Initially the broker saw only temporary 2W volume softness and two FX-hit JVs as headwinds, and projected strong FY24 growth, double-digit margin expansion, and overseas margin improvement. By the last report, the narrative had widened to include volatile raw material prices, OEM concentration, and prolonged losses at acquired/overseas/EV units, while optimism shifted to GST 2.0-driven demand, market share gains, and operating leverage from new capex. The persistent undercurrent was overseas unit profitability, first flagged as JV FX losses and later as European/EV gestation losses.

Fears that came true

  • The early fear of overseas JV losses from currency devaluation materialized as European operations continued to post EBT losses in FY26.

Optimism that failed

  • Softening commodity prices did not persist; raw material prices became volatile and energy costs rose, hurting margins.
  • The expectation that overseas subsidiary margins would rise to 12–13% failed; overseas only reached EBITDA break-even in Q4FY26.
