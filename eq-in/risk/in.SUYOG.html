Suyog Telematics — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Customer concentration and dependence on operator capex. Revenue is heavily skewed toward a few operators: Airtel 48.1%, Vodafone Idea 27.0%, Jio 22.6% and BSNL 2.3% in Q1 FY27. Management acknowledged that growth depends on customers’ rollout plans, stating that if Vodafone Idea or BSNL do not execute, the company would feel the pressure too. Airtel and Jio were described as not doing any major rollout during the quarter.

Vodafone Idea funding is phased and not fully tied up. Vodafone Idea has secured INR 6,400 crore of its planned INR 45,000 crore programme and is in advanced stages with SBI and a consortium for a further INR 25,000–30,000 crore, to be released in phases. Management said Vodafone Idea would raise nominals only for around 7,000–10,000 sites out of the 12,000 planned up to September–October, with further rollout contingent on the next funding tranche.

BSNL rollout delays and unresolved equipment issues. BSNL’s rollout has been delayed for 1–1.5 years due to issues with Tejas equipment. Suyog holds BSNL orders it is not executing pending resolution, and 186 BSNL sites had billing pending as of the quarter, with no additions during Q1. Management explicitly declined to guide on BSNL numbers until billing confirmation and rollout dates are provided, noting past experience of declarations not translating into ground rollout and billing issues.

Battery cost inflation and import dependence. Lithium battery prices rose from about INR 33,500 to INR 48,000 for the 100H grade within roughly 2.5 months — an increase of over 40–50% — driven by removal of Chinese subsidies and rupee depreciation. All lithium cells are imported from China, raising capex costs.

Seasonality and weather. Q2 faces monsoon impact on site conversion. Management noted that Q3 and Q4 are seasonally stronger, with roughly 35–40% of activity in H1 and about 60% in H2.

Margin optics from accounting change and IndAS. The voluntary change to gross presentation of electricity and diesel reimbursements from April 1, 2026 lowered reported EBITDA margin from around 70% to 59.3% on a higher revenue base, with no impact on absolute EBITDA, PBT, PAT, Net Worth or EPS. Separately, the CFO noted that under IndAS, long-term rental agreements produce a larger notional loss in the first five years, muting EBITDA margin.

Internal control and balance confirmation issues. The company flagged the need to further strengthen internal control design commensurate with the growing size of the business. Balances in Loan and Advance, Other Advance, Other Current Assets, Trade Receivables, Trade Payables, and advances to suppliers and vendors remain subject to confirmation, reconciliation and subsequent adjustment.

Stagnation acknowledged. Management conceded that the last two years were stagnant, with no meaningful growth in Q1 FY27 because orders only began flowing from mid-June.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Vodafone Idea’s large multi-year rollout. Vodafone Idea has declared a rollout of about 45,000 sites over 18–24 months, targeting 3,000–3,500 sites per month, backed by a INR 45,000 crore capex plan. Suyog received around 636 site orders on June 17, 2026, converted 95 towers and 150 tenancies by June 30, 2026 within 13–15 days, and is working on 700+ additional sites. Management targets 3,000 tenancies from Vodafone Idea in FY27 and another 5,000 in FY28, taking Vodafone Idea to about 30–32% of revenue.

BSNL’s proposed capex. BSNL has announced a INR 77,000 crore capex plan covering 2 lakh additional 4G sites over five years, with 98% nationwide coverage targeted by FY 2028-29. Suyog holds a PAN-India MSA with BSNL for 15 years with a 10-year lock-in, and sees potential to cover for non-performing IPs.

Zinc bromide battery commercialisation. Suyog is tying up with GBB Batteries (Kerala plant) and expects first deliveries for 10–15 sites by mid-September 2026, describing itself as the first IP1 company to deploy zinc bromide batteries on sites. Pricing is around INR 33,000 for 100H versus INR 48,000 for lithium, with fire resistance, fast charging and lower opex cited as benefits. This is positioned as a capex and opex saving lever.

Structural demand from 4G/5G densification. Management cited roughly 3 lakh towers planned over five years across operators, with no dearth of work for IP1 companies. Small cell tenancies stood at 4,000+ as of June 30, 2026, and the company operates across 26 states and UTs with 6,103 towers and 7,468 tenancies.

Tenancy ratio improvement potential. The current ratio is about 1.2x, with a target of 1.8x by end-FY28 or early FY29. Management noted payback improves from 3.5–4 years at single tenancy to about 24 months at two tenancies and 18 months at three, implying margin accretion from sharing.

Regulatory support. The Telecommunications Act, 2023 (effective June 26, 2024) and Telecom Right of Way Rules, 2024 (effective January 1, 2025) are cited as streamlining approvals and reducing deployment costs.

Delhi circle acquisition. Suyog acquired 95% of Lotus Tele Infra on March 31, 2025 for INR 13.5 crore, adding 120 telecom sites in Delhi & NCR with Bharti Airtel and Reliance Jio as major tenants, and projecting a 50% increase in tenancies within six months.

Satellite limited to a complementary role. The presentation argues satellite operators are capped at about 2 million connections with high pricing and latency, and will not materially disrupt terrestrial telecom infrastructure demand.

3. Key Risks

  • Customer funding risk: Vodafone Idea’s rollout pace depends on securing the remaining INR 25,000–30,000 crore; BSNL’s depends on government budget and resolution of Tejas equipment issues.
  • Execution and billing risk: BSNL’s history of delayed rollout and billing issues; 186 sites awaiting billing integration.
  • Input cost and forex risk: Lithium prices and rupee depreciation against the dollar directly affect capex; zinc bromide is the intended mitigation but is not yet deployed at scale.
  • Accounting and comparability risk: The gross presentation change restates comparatives and lowers reported EBITDA margin, which can obscure like-for-like comparison; IndAS notional losses further mute reported margins in early lease years.
  • Internal control and receivable confirmation risk: Balances across receivables, payables and advances are subject to confirmation and reconciliation, and internal controls are flagged for strengthening.
  • Concentration risk: Nearly half of revenue comes from Airtel, which is not currently doing major rollout; growth is therefore dependent on Vodafone Idea and BSNL.
  • Seasonality risk: Monsoon affects Q2 conversion, pushing execution into H2.
  • Fundraise uncertainty: Management has not decided whether or when to raise funds; additional orders beyond current visibility may require external funding.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Guidance on Vodafone Idea tenancies. Management targets 3,000 tenancies from Vodafone Idea in FY27 and 5,000 in FY28, describing the FY27 figure as conservative and based on the INR 6,400 crore tranche already secured. Observed performance: 150 tenancies converted in Q1 FY27 (95 towers), with 700+ additional sites in hand. Management stated it is “very confident” of achieving 3,000, citing execution capacity, funds and Vodafone Idea’s daily loading.

Guidance on total tenancies. Management indicated FY27 would end with 10,000+ tenancies (from 7,468 at Q1 exit). This implies roughly 2,500–3,000 net additions, consistent with the Vodafone Idea target.

Guidance on revenue and profitability. The MD stated an ambition of INR 100 crore revenue per quarter (INR 400 crore annualised), with bottom line growing from about 22% to 24%, and said this could be visible from Q4. Observed Q1 FY27: revenue INR 710 million (INR 71 crore), PBT INR 195 million, net profit INR 145 million, net margin 20.4%, EPS INR 12.37. Management guided to maintaining EBITDA margin around 59% (with electricity) and PAT margin around 20% for coming quarters.

Guidance on revenue per tower. Management guided to maintaining revenue per tower at around INR 31,000 excluding electricity, and around INR 50,000 including electricity. Observed: revenue per tower was maintained above INR 31,000 in Q1 FY27 despite a stagnant quarter, consistent with the prior four quarters.

Guidance on tenancy ratio. Target of 1.8x by end-FY28 or early FY29, versus 1.2x currently. Observed: Q1 rollout achieved a 1.8x ratio on new Vodafone Idea sites (95 towers, 150 tenancies), which management cited as evidence the target is achievable.

Guidance on zinc bromide batteries. First batch for 10–15 sites expected by mid-September 2026, with commercial deliveries expected from September 2026. This remains forward-looking; no deployment had occurred as of the call.

Guidance on BSNL. Management declined to provide numbers, stating it will share figures once BSNL confirms rollout dates and billing commencement. Observed: no BSNL additions in Q1, 186 sites with billing pending, and orders held unexecuted pending Tejas resolution.

Guidance on fundraising. No decision taken; internal accruals sufficient for the current 3,000-tenancy plan, with a decision deferred to Q2 or Q3 if larger orders materialise.

Guidance on fibre revenue. Fibre expected to remain 5–8% of total revenue in FY27, with no major jump, as Vodafone Idea sites are largely microwave-based and rural sites favour microwave over fibre.
