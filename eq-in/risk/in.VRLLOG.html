Broker Narrative

The broker narrative sustained a Buy thesis centered on branch-led volume growth and fleet expansion, but progressively redefined its risk profile from early concerns over vehicle-scrapping capex and broad cost inflation to later, more specific headwinds around fuel-procurement economics, longer lead distances, and rising lorry-hire charges. Thematic focus shifted from a post-divestiture pure-play story toward eastern and north-eastern geographic expansion and hub-to-hub efficiency, even as new cost headwinds emerged. While these changes eventually supported success from mid-2024, the anticipated efficiency gains from fleet modernization did not reduce hired-vehicle dependence, and the projected growth CAGR was delayed.

Fears that came true

  • Rising operating costs such as tolls, labor, and vehicle maintenance materialized as surging fuel-procurement costs, higher lorry-hire charges, and elevated vehicle-running expenses, driving DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes from 2023 through mid-2024.
  • The vehicle-scrapping policy’s INR6.97b capex requirement and continued fleet expansion coincided with persistent reliance on hired vehicles and elevated costs, pressuring returns during the DISAPPOINTMENT period.
  • Dependence on hired vehicles materialized as lorry-hire charges rose to 4.4% of total income due to longer lead distances, contributing to DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.

Optimism that failed

  • Fleet expansion was expected to improve efficiency and reduce hired-vehicle dependence, but newer vehicles did not achieve this, as lorry-hire charges instead climbed to 4.4% of total income.
  • The projected revenue/EBITDA/PAT CAGR of ~12%/17%/28% over FY23-25E did not materialize in the near term, as DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes dominated through mid-2024.
