UCO Bank — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

One-time DTA charge compresses net profit. Although operating profit grew 79.84% Y-o-Y to Rs. 2,810 crore, net profit rose only 8.05% Y-o-Y to Rs. 656 crore. Management attributed this to a tax provision of Rs. 1,919 crore, which included a one-time Deferred Tax Asset (DTA) charge of Rs. 1,237 crore arising from the statutory shift to the new tax regime (from ~35% to ~25%). Excluding this, regular tax provision was around Rs. 625 crore. Management stated that, hypothetically, without the one-time charge, ROA “could have been more than 1%,” against the reported 0.68%.

Cost-to-income ratio is flattered by non-recurring recoveries. The reported cost-to-income ratio of 37.49% was supported by recovery from technically written-off (TWO) accounts of about Rs. 1,018 crore, of which roughly Rs. 800 crore came from a few accounts not expected to repeat. Management expects the ratio to normalize higher, targeting below or around 50% for FY27.

Cost of deposits edged up Q-o-Q. Management attributed this to a reclassification of deposits and borrowings in the international and Indian books, though overall cost of funds declined to 4.36% (down 37 bps Y-o-Y) with support from lower borrowing costs.

Fee income from loan processing declined. Renewal charges are now being recovered on an actual basis rather than upfront with quarterly allocation; Q1 typically has fewer sanctions and renewals. Management expects this to normalize over the full year.

Competitive intensity. Management acknowledged competition from public sector banks, private banks, RRBs, and NBFCs, but declined to quantify the source, framing the expanding financial market as an opportunity as customers “graduate” from NBFCs/MFIs to banks.

Leadership continuity. The additional charge of MD & CEO assigned to Shri Rajendra Kumar Saboo was extended for three months beyond 31 August 2026, i.e., up to 30 November 2026, or until a regular incumbent is appointed, or until further orders.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Broad-based business growth. Total business reached Rs. 6,05,083 crore, up 15.53% Y-o-Y, with gross advances up 21.18% to Rs. 2,72,768 crore and deposits up 11.28% to Rs. 3,32,315 crore. CASA grew 12.34% Y-o-Y to Rs. 1,16,136 crore (CASA ratio 36.94%), with current deposits up 16.23% and savings deposits up 11.78%.

RAM-led diversification. RAM advances grew 25.27% to Rs. 1,57,745 crore, constituting 64.52% of advances. Retail grew 27.32% (home loans +19.98%, vehicle loans +65.45%), agriculture +30.01%, and MSME +18.79%. Corporate advances grew about 17%.

Profitability and margin improvement. NII grew 16.86% Y-o-Y to Rs. 2,808 crore; global NIM improved 9 bps Y-o-Y to 3.05% and domestic NIM 6 bps to 3.24%. Fee-based income grew 35.12% Y-o-Y to Rs. 505 crore, aided by sale of PSLC of around Rs. 2,000 crore earning commission of Rs. 55 crore.

Asset quality improvement. Gross NPA fell 55 bps Y-o-Y to 2.08%; net NPA fell 20 bps to 0.25%; PCR stood at 97.85% (tangible PCR 88.08%). Slippage ratio was 0.63% annualized. The SMA book (Rs. 1 crore and above) declined from Rs. 1,125 crore (0.43% of advances) in March to Rs. 1,009 crore (0.36%).

Digital and product initiatives. Project Parivartan 2.0 aims to make the call centre a profit centre; STP Home Loan and STP Ashray Loan journeys were launched and integrated with RBI’s Unified Lending Interface (ULI); CBDC and CASA back office implemented; DMS solution deployed. New products include UCO Rising Star (children), UCO Gig Scheme (gig workers), UCO Business Aarambh (startups), and UCO 3-in-1 (savings + Demat + trading with Aditya Birla Money). Digital business balance sheet rose to ~Rs. 35,000 crore as on 30 June from Rs. 25,000 crore as on 31 March 2026; ~70% of fixed deposits are now made digitally. Thirty-one STP journeys are live.

Pipeline and new channels. A credit sanctions pipeline of ~Rs. 15,000 crore is held. A GIFT City branch is expected to open “next month” with all approvals in place. Omni-Channel, Cash Management Services, MSME STP journeys, an enhanced GST smart product (up to Rs. 1 crore), and pre-qualified personal loans via call centre are in the pipeline.

Regional opportunity. As the only public sector bank headquartered in Calcutta, with ~400 branches in West Bengal under five zonal offices, management sees opportunities in infrastructure, steel, railway-related, and industrial financing as the state’s investment climate improves.

ECLGS 5.0 support. Sanctions of ~Rs. 2,150 crore and disbursements of ~Rs. 1,700 crore (less than 50% of eligible customers) under ECLGS, which management views as liquidity support for MSMEs.

Capital and funding flexibility. CRAR stood at 19.03% with Tier-I at 17.44%. The Board approved raising foreign currency funds up to USD 1.00 billion in one or more tranches via a Medium Term Note (MTN) Programme at its meeting on 24 August 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Non-repeat of TWO recoveries (~Rs. 800 crore of the Rs. 1,018 crore) would normalize operating profit and push the cost-to-income ratio higher.
  • ECL transition: Preliminary assessment for the transition date of 1 April 2027 indicates a buffer of ~60% of the estimated requirement has been created over and above mandatory provisions; the remaining ~40% is planned over the next 4–5 quarters.
  • Deposit repricing and cost dynamics: Management believes most deposits have repriced post the last repo cut and expects cost of funds to remain stable, but the Q-o-Q rise in cost of deposits shows sensitivity to reclassification and mix.
  • Competitive pressure on pricing: Management reported no significant pressure in retail (repo-linked) and moderate corporate pricing, with MCLR raised 0.05% to 8.80% in the current month, but the corporate bond-versus-loan trade-off remains a swing factor.
  • Sectoral stress: Management stated no stress is currently visible in any lending segment, with slippages and SMA contained.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Slippage ratio Below 1% 0.63% annualized
NIM 2.8–2.9% 3.05% global / 3.24% domestic

Management stated it has “surpassed most of the guidance.” On credit growth, it retained the 12–14% guidance despite 21% actual growth, indicating a review after Q2 FY27 and an endeavour to grow above industry as a smaller bank. On NIM, management will retain 2.8–2.9% guidance but “try to keep it above that.” On cost-to-income, it expects below or around 50% for FY27 in a normalized scenario. On ROA, no formal guidance was given, but management indicated ROA could move from 0.68% toward “slightly near to 1%” by year-end, helped by the DTA charge being behind it and contained credit costs. On ECL, the plan is to create the remaining ~40% of transition-date provisions over the next 4–5 quarters.
