Headwinds and Challenges

  • Operating margin compression. Operating EBITDA margin fell to 26.7% in Q1 FY27 from 29.0% in Q1 FY26 (down 232–233 bps) and from 28.9% in Q4 FY26 (down 220–221 bps). Operating EBITDA was roughly flat year on year (up about 1.1–1.2%) despite revenue growth of 9.9%, indicating cost growth outpacing revenue growth.
  • Employee benefit expense pressure. Employee benefit expenses rose to ₹1,999.1 million in Q1 FY27 from ₹1,800.3 million in Q1 FY26 and ₹1,840.1 million in Q4 FY26, alongside a larger sales force (13,337 total employees; 10,965 sales employees at period end).
  • Traffic stagnation year on year. Quarterly unique visitors were 192.9 million, down 0.2% YoY, with mobile visitors down 0.7% YoY and voice visitors down 4.6% YoY. Mobile’s share of traffic slipped 44 bps YoY to 86.5%.
  • Slower app download momentum. App downloads per day fell 8.1% YoY to 6,994, even though total cumulative app downloads rose 8.2% YoY to 44.1 million.
  • Sequential moderation in listings additions. Net listings addition of 1,467,180 was up 56.3% YoY but down 21.8% QoQ.
  • Deferred revenue softness. Unearned revenue of ₹5,401 million was up only 1.0% YoY and down 2.8% QoQ, a modest indicator for near-term revenue visibility relative to the revenue growth rate.
  • Leadership transition. Mr. V.S.S. Mani completes his term as Managing Director and CEO on July 31, 2026. Mr. Dinkar Ayilavarapu was appointed CEO (designate) and Key Managerial Personnel effective July 10, 2026, taking over as CEO effective August 1, 2026. Separately, Mr. Dinesh Taluja resigned as Non-Executive Director effective close of business on July 10, 2026, and was appointed Chief Financial Officer and Key Managerial Personnel effective July 11, 2026.
  • Other income volatility. Other income is described as primarily mark-to-market gains on the treasury portfolio, which can have high quarterly fluctuations depending on bond yield movements. Other income swung from ₹486.2 million in Q4 FY26 to ₹1,314.6 million in Q1 FY27 (up 170.3% QoQ), driven by a ~40–50 bps QoQ decline in yields. This makes net profit and net profit margin volatile: net profit margin rose 812–813 bps QoQ to 36.2% even as operating margin fell.
  • Prior one-time cost item. Effective November 21, 2025, the new labour code on Social Security, 2020 amended the definition of “wages” for gratuity calculations and expanded coverage to fixed-term employees. Under Ind AS 19, this was treated as a plan amendment and past service cost, resulting in a one-time exceptional item of ₹210.8 million recognised in the quarter ended December 31, 2025 and year ended March 31, 2026.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Revenue growth acceleration. Operating revenue was ₹3,274.7 million (₹327.5 Crores), up 9.9% YoY and 6.6% QoQ. Management characterised the 6.6% QoQ growth as the fastest sequential growth in a decade outside the post-COVID recovery period.
  • Listing database expansion. Total listings reached 56.1 million at period end, up 13.0% YoY and 2.7% QoQ. Net listings addition of 1,467,180 was up 56.3% YoY. Geocoded listings reached 41.7 million, up 19.7% YoY, and total images in listings reached 262.9 million, up 14.1% YoY.
  • Monetisation base growth. Active paid campaigns reached 639,200, up 3.5% YoY and 1.2% QoQ. Ratings & reviews reached 160.5 million, up 4.4% YoY.
  • AI deployment across the business. In Q1 FY27, Justdial expanded deployment of agentic AI across Voice, WhatsApp and AI-powered sales assistants. AI voice agents are used to understand user intent and qualify leads, and AI agents are deployed across sales workflows for lead nurturing, onboarding, customer support and content operations. Management stated AI is translating into tangible outcomes and is being extended to the merchant ecosystem, including helping businesses create richer content and automating customer interactions.
  • B2B expansion via JD Mart. Justdial introduced the JD Mart Super Sixer Pack, a premium suite of merchant benefits including access to a high-quality lead bank, premium 3D catalogues, ‘Trusted’ and ‘Verified’ badges, official Justdial rating certificates, personalized web banners and enhanced visibility across business listings and product searches. Management stated this reflects continued ambition to build a preferred platform for B2B commerce in India.
  • Beyond-search initiatives. The presentation lists JD Shopping, Universal Biz Listing, Travel, Bills & Recharge, Stocks, Logistics, Get Quotes, and JD Omni-Cloud (a hosted solution for digitalising businesses), alongside JD Pay, JD Ratings, catalogues and Search Plus.
  • Strong financial position. Cash and investments stood at ₹60,221 million (₹6,022.1 Crores) as on June 30, 2026, up 10.9% YoY and 2.9% QoQ. The business model is described as prepaid with an efficient and profitable structure.
  • Traffic recovery sequentially. Unique visitors rose 5.8% QoQ, with mobile up 6.8% QoQ and voice up 8.6% QoQ.
  • Sales capacity expansion. Sales employees increased 7.8% YoY and 4.7% QoQ to 10,965; total employees rose 2.1% YoY to 13,337. The sales team comprises 5,796 tele-sales employees, 1,539 feet-on-street (marketing), and 3,630 feet-on-street (cold calling & others) as on Q1 FY27.

Key Risks

  • Dependence on other income for profit growth. Profit before taxes rose 3.9% YoY to ₹2,065.9 million and net profit rose 4.1% YoY to ₹1,662.2 million, but operating PBT rose only 4.9–5.0% YoY to ₹751–752 million. The QoQ jump in net profit (66.2%) was driven substantially by the 170.3% QoQ rise in other income from mark-to-market treasury gains, which the company notes can fluctuate highly with bond yields.
  • Margin trajectory. Two consecutive comparisons (YoY and QoQ) show operating EBITDA margin declining by more than 200 bps, with operating EBITDA declining 1.5–1.6% QoQ.
  • Traffic dependence on mobile. 86.5% of traffic originates on mobile platforms; mobile unique visitors declined YoY, and mobile’s traffic share declined 44 bps YoY.
  • Execution risk in AI and B2B initiatives. The AI deployments and JD Mart Super Sixer Pack are described as recently introduced or expanded in Q1 FY27; the presentation does not provide separate financial contribution data for these initiatives.
  • Leadership transition risk. Both the CEO and CFO roles are changing around the July–August 2026 period.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. The presentation states that forecasts are connected with known and unknown risks, uncertainties and other factors that may cause actual results to deviate significantly from forecasts, and cautions readers not to place undue reliance on forward-looking statements.
  • Regulatory/accounting change risk. The new labour code on Social Security, 2020 already resulted in a one-time ₹210.8 million past service cost, illustrating exposure to regulatory changes affecting employee benefit obligations.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Sequential revenue growth. Management described Q1 FY27 revenue growth of 6.6% QoQ as “our fastest sequential growth in a decade outside the post-COVID recovery period,” and stated FY27 started “on a strong note.” Observed performance: operating revenue of ₹3,274.7 million, up 6.6% QoQ and 9.9% YoY, consistent with that characterisation.
  • AI as a productivity driver. Management stated AI “has emerged as a genuine force multiplier for both user experience and business productivity,” that AI voice agents are producing “stronger buyer-seller matches,” and that AI agents in sales workflows free sales teams to focus on high-intent opportunities. Observed performance: revenue grew 9.9% YoY while operating EBITDA grew only about 1.1–1.2% YoY and operating EBITDA margin contracted 232–233 bps YoY, so the margin benefit of these productivity claims is not yet visible in the reported operating margin.
  • AI-enabled scale and complexity management. Management stated AI is helping manage scale and complexity in cataloguing, listing verification and customer support, enabling richer business information and faster issue resolution. Observed performance: total listings rose 13.0% YoY to 56.1 million, images in listings rose 14.1% YoY to 262.9 million, and geocoded listings rose 19.7% YoY to 41.7 million, consistent with expanding content operations.
  • Extending AI to merchants. Management stated the goal is to make AI accessible to millions of MSMEs. Observed performance: active paid campaigns rose 3.5% YoY to 639,200, a slower growth rate than listings growth, and the presentation does not quantify AI-enabled merchant adoption.
  • B2B ambition. Management stated the JD Mart Super Sixer Pack “reflects Justdial’s continued ambition to build the preferred platform for B2B commerce in India” and that Justdial remains committed to equipping businesses with technology, trust and predictability. Observed performance: the pack was introduced in Q1 FY27 and no separate revenue or campaign metrics for JD Mart are disclosed in the supplied material.
  • Traffic. No explicit traffic guidance is provided. Observed performance: unique visitors were 192.9 million, down 0.2% YoY but up 5.8% QoQ.
  • Deferred revenue. No explicit guidance is provided. Observed performance: unearned revenue of ₹5,401 million, up 1.0% YoY and down 2.8% QoQ.
  • Tax rate. The press release states ETR stood at a normalised level of 19.5% for the quarter. Observed performance: total tax expense of ₹403.7 million on profit before tax of ₹2,065.9 million.
  • AGM resolutions. All resolutions set out in the Notice of the 32nd Annual General Meeting held on August 31, 2026, including adoption of audited financial statements, appointment of directors (Mr. V. Subramaniam and Ms. Geeta Fulwadaya), and re-appointment of Mr. Ranjit Pandit as an Independent Director, were passed with the requisite majority.

Broker Narrative

The early narrative was a positive sector story centered on online commerce share gains, strong B2B e-commerce, and Just Dial’s B2B revenue and margin expansion. By the last report, the focus had shifted to Just Dial-specific execution issues: slowing paid-campaign/listings growth, flat web traffic, EBITDA margin decline, and leadership/cash-distribution uncertainty. The buy call remained, but the supporting logic changed from broad growth/scale-efficiency optimism to expectations of cash distribution and leadership-transition clarity.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning of a further slowdown in discretionary spending was followed by a string of negative actual returns, including -40.7% after the April 2025 Hold and -28.7% after the July 2024 Buy, both marked DISAPPOINTMENT.
  • The early risk of weaker conversion of active profiles was borne out by later reports of flat web traffic and slowing growth in paid campaigns/listings.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that Just Dial’s B2B revenue strength and scale efficiencies would drive margin expansion failed to materialize, as the last report cited EBITDA margin decline from headcount expansion and higher marketing investments.
  • The broader optimism about online commerce and B2B digital adoption sustaining growth was contradicted by later slowing paid-campaign/listings growth and flat web traffic.
