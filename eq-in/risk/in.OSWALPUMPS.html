Oswal Pumps — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Competitive tender pricing. The tender pricing environment across the industry turned more competitive during the quarter. Industry-wide competitive bidding under the Magel Tyala scheme led to a 9% reduction in realisations, which management identified as the single largest factor behind the margin decline. With PM KUSUM 2.0 delayed, industry focus concentrated on Maharashtra state projects, attracting bidders from across the country and driving prices down aggressively. Management described the prices bid as “not sustainable” and noted that the whole industry is unlikely to tolerate them going forward.

Delay in PM KUSUM 2.0. The anticipated rollout of PM KUSUM 2.0 had not materialised as of the August 10, 2026 call. Management had earlier expected an August launch and stated it was proceeding with the expectation of a Q2 FY27 launch, while the IR advisor cautioned that no one in the industry can guarantee the timing. This delay removed the industry’s largest volume pool and pushed all participants toward the remaining state tenders.

Elevated input costs. Raw material prices increased due to the ongoing geopolitical situation. Management quantified the raw material cost impact at approximately 3%–3.5% of the margin decline. Copper price volatility was flagged by an analyst as a risk to the 15%–17% EBITDA margin guidance.

Margin compression. Gross margin declined 548 bps sequentially. Operating EBITDA margin moderated 747 bps QoQ, driven by the fall in gross margin, an increase in employee benefit expenses from annual increments and senior-level hiring, and negative operating leverage. On a YoY basis, operating EBITDA margin contracted 1,169 bps (from 27.4% to 15.7%), and PAT margin contracted 721 bps (from 18.4% to 11.2%).

Revenue decline. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was ₹4,736 million, down 7.9% YoY and 7.1% QoQ. Total pumps supplied fell to 43,077 in Q1 FY27 from 56,000 in Q1 FY26, though up from 40,400 in Q4 FY26.

Working capital deterioration. The cash conversion cycle rose to 244 days as of June 30, 2026, from 172 days as of March 31, 2026. Receivable days increased to 229 days from 155 days, attributable to delayed payments from state nodal agencies. Management noted ₹305 crores of total receivables as of June 30, 2026 was not yet due, and that receivables are entirely from government and government-backed counterparties.

Investor concern on execution versus guidance. An individual investor noted the share price had halved from IPO levels and that guidance has not always matched execution. Management attributed the gap to external factors — the PM KUSUM 2.0 delay, dependence on Magel Tyala EPC timing, aggressive bidding, and geopolitical raw material inflation — while maintaining that capacity, supply, installation, and IPO fund deployment remain on track.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Diversification beyond PM KUSUM. Given the PM KUSUM 2.0 delay, the company is sharpening focus on rooftop solar, utility, and commercial & industrial solar EPC. The order book across these segments stands at approximately 72 MW, backed by a wider pipeline of 359 MW. The investor presentation shows open and upcoming tenders of 1,474 MW, 953 MW of tenders participated, and a 359 MW pipeline.

PM Surya Ghar vertical. The company created PM Surya Ghar as a dedicated vertical with a dedicated business head to drive market penetration, bid participation, and delivery. Management set an internal target of approximately 200,000 solar installations under the scheme for FY27, translating to an estimated revenue of ₹800 crores to ₹1,000 crores. The company is participating as a manufacturer of solar modules, BOS components, and mounting structures, with installation and supply handled through its own sales channels.

New order wins. On August 13, 2026, the company announced a Letter of Award from North Bihar Power Distribution Company Limited for 20 MW of grid-connected rooftop solar across 18,089 consumer sites of 1.1 kW each in the Darbhanga Circle, under the CAPEX Plus RESCO mode of the ULA model of PM Surya Ghar. The installation order value is approximately ₹78 crores including GST, with estimated long-term revenue potential of approximately ₹67 crores from energy supply over a 10-year RESCO tenure, giving a cumulative revenue opportunity of approximately ₹145 crores. Execution is within 9 months from the signed PPA, with 10 years of mandatory O&M. The award builds on earlier order wins in Bihar.

Jal Jeevan Mission evaluation. The company is evaluating entry into the Jal Jeevan Mission, where it has identified an addressable pipeline of approximately 42,000 pumps.

Wires and cables. The company is introducing in-house manufactured wires and cables across its distribution channels in FY27, using existing spare in-house capacity without significant capex. Management expects revenue of approximately ₹70 crores to ₹100 crores from this segment, while acknowledging profitability will be lower than the PM KUSUM solar pumping scheme.

Inverter backward integration. Inverter production is expected in-house within 6 months, with a prototype already made and field trials expected to start within 2 months.

Order book and pipeline. The pump order book stands at 22,025 pumps as of the call, with a near-term pipeline of approximately 12,500 pumps across direct PM KUSUM, Magel Tyala, indirect PM KUSUM, and export orders. The investor presentation shows 1,19,402 cumulative orders executed as of July 31, 2026, and 22,025 systems to be supplied.

Magel Tyala T6 tender. A new T6 tender has been floated, the company has submitted a bid, and financial bid opening was expected within 7 to 10 days of the call. Existing Magel Tyala orders are to be executed in Q2.

Capex progress. The pump and motor plant capacity expansion and automation is expected to be completed by Q3 FY27. The first phase of the solar module expansion comprising 1 GW is expected to be completed by end of Q2, with commercial production expected in the first or maximum second week of September. Management noted a possible 1–2 month gap due to rain but no long-term gap. IPO proceeds capex is expected to be fully infused as planned.

Structural market drivers. The investor presentation cites India’s solar market reaching approximately 45 GW in FY26 across all segments, with rooftop at approximately 20% share driven by PM Surya Ghar, and C&I and KUSUM at approximately 17% each. The PM Surya Ghar program has a ~₹75,000 crore outlay targeting 1 crore households, with ₹22,000 crore allocated in the Union Budget 2026–27. As of August 7, 2026, the scheme had ~7.80 million applications, ~15.01 GW capacity installed, ~4.23 million installations, ~₹285.16 billion subsidy released, and ~5.07 million households covered.

3. Key Risks

Government policy and scheme timing. Dependence on the timing of PM KUSUM 2.0 and other government schemes is a central risk. Management and the IR advisor both stated that the release of PM KUSUM 2.0 is beyond the company’s control and that no one in the industry can guarantee timing.

Receivable and cash conversion risk. Receivable days of 229 and a cash conversion cycle of 244 days reflect delayed state nodal agency payments. Management expects normalization over the medium term, with better receivables in Q2 versus Q1 and “very big changes” in Q3, but acknowledged that Q2 may not show a very large difference in cash conversion cycle or debtor days because billing is planned in parallel.

Raw material price volatility. Copper, magnets, iron, and other input prices remain subject to geopolitical uncertainty. Management stated that if prices rise, the entire industry would be impacted, not just Oswal.

Margin sustainability. Management acknowledged that PM Surya Ghar profit margins cannot match PM KUSUM margins, and that wires and cables profitability will be lower than the PM KUSUM solar pumping scheme. Some projects may generate revenue this year without direct profitability, with profitability spread over the next 5 years.

Competitive intensity. The number of players in the PM KUSUM scheme increased as profitability became visible, with new entrants and older players with declining volumes both bidding aggressively.

Execution and payment timing. The Bihar order is to be executed within 9 months from the signed PPA, and the RESCO revenue potential of approximately ₹67 crores accrues over a 10-year tenure, meaning near-term revenue recognition depends on PPA execution and commissioning timelines.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

FY27 revenue growth guidance of 20–25%. Management maintained this guidance on the call, describing a back-ended growth profile. Q1 FY27 revenue declined 7.9% YoY and 7.1% QoQ. Management stated Q1 was expected to witness a moderate but temporary revenue decline given the timing of project awards and execution schedules, and that Q1 FY27 performance was broadly in line with this phasing. For Q2, management expects more than 10%–15% YoY growth. Management stated clear visibility of 20%–25% growth driven by projects in hand, bids placed expected to be won, Magel Tyala orders, and diversification initiatives.

FY27 operating EBITDA margin guidance of 15%–17%. Q1 FY27 operating EBITDA margin was 15.7%, within the guided range but at the lower end. Management stated the guidance is based on present bidding in hand, aggressive bidding already seen, PM Surya Ghar bid profitability, and present-scenario visualization, calculated on a “slightly safer side.” Management indicated the 15%–17% range does not assume PM KUSUM 2.0 renewal, and that if PM KUSUM 2.0 is launched, margins could improve slightly from current levels.

FY27 PAT margin guidance of 11%–13%. Q1 FY27 PAT margin was 11.2%, within the guided range at the lower end.

Medium-term growth target of 30%–40%. Management stated it is targeting sustained growth momentum of 30%–40% in the medium term as execution across multiple fronts gathers pace. Management described FY27 as a year of “a little turbulence” due to geopolitical conditions, with FY28 and FY29 looking “very clear, very good.”

Capex guidance. Management stated the entire pump and motor capex program is scheduled for completion by Q3 FY27, and the first phase of 1 GW solar module capacity by end of Q2. On the call, management said commercial production for the 1 GW module capacity is expected in the first or maximum second week of September, with a possible 1–2 month gap due to rain but no long-term gap. IPO proceeds capex is expected to be fully infused as per plan. The investor presentation shows ₹6,059.66 million of net proceeds utilized as of June 30, 2026, out of ₹8,415.14 million, leaving ₹2,355.48 million unutilized.

Cash conversion cycle guidance. Management expects the payment cycle to normalize over the medium term, leading to a reduction in the cash conversion cycle and lower interest costs. Observed performance: the cash conversion cycle rose to 244 days as of June 30, 2026, from 172 days as of March 31, 2026. Management expects better receivables in Q2 versus Q1 and “very big changes” in Q3, while cautioning that Q2 may not show a very large difference in cash conversion cycle or debtor days.

PM Surya Ghar revenue guidance. Management guided to approximately 200,000 installations and ₹800 crores to ₹1,000 crores of revenue from PM Surya Ghar in FY27. Observed performance: Q1 FY27 PM Surya Ghar contribution was described as virtually negligible, with revenue primarily driven by the pumping business. Contribution is expected to commence from Q2 onwards. The August 13, 2026 Bihar order of approximately ₹78 crores installation value plus approximately ₹67 crores of 10-year RESCO energy revenue is an early component of this pipeline.

Order book guidance. Management stated the pump order book stands at 22,025 pumps with a near-term pipeline of approximately 12,500 pumps. The investor presentation confirms 22,025 systems to be supplied as of July 31, 2026, and a pipeline of approximately 12,500 pumps. The Magel Tyala T6 tender bid was submitted, with financial bid opening expected within 7 to 10 days of the August 10, 2026 call.
