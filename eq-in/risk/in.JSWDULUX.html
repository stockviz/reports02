JSW Dulux Limited: Recent Developments and Outlook

Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material inflation and margin pressure. Management noted that calibrated price increases were taken to partly offset raw material inflation. The company’s industry-leading working capital efficiency had a temporary adverse impact on gross margins in Q1 FY2026-27.

Reported revenue decline. On a reported standalone basis, revenue from operations declined 2.8% to ₹965.0 crore in Q1 FY2026-27 from ₹993.1 crore in the prior-year quarter. Reported EBITDA from operations declined 14.4% to ₹115.1 crore from ₹134.5 crore.

Comparability distortion from divestments. The standalone and consolidated results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 are not comparable with corresponding previous periods due to the slump sale of the Powder Coatings business division and the International Research Center division during the quarter ended 30 September 2025.

GST show cause notice. The company received a Show Cause Notice dated 10 September 2026 (received 14 September 2026) from the Assistant Commissioner, LGSTO, Large Taxpayers Unit, Bengaluru, under Section 73 of the CGST Act/Rules, 2017. The notice concerns disallowance of Input Tax Credit for April 2022 to March 2023, totalling ₹10,60,96,432 (₹6,17,03,450 tax, ₹3,82,22,637 interest, and ₹61,70,345 penalty). Management stated the matter is open for further submissions and the company is responding within the stipulated time.

Regulatory and compliance obligations. SEBI has mandated that securities of listed companies can only be transferred in dematerialised form, and KYC requirements (PAN linked with Aadhaar, nomination, bank details) must be completed or folios may be frozen. New share certificates will not be dispatched to physical holders until KYC is complete.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong volume and comparable value growth. Q1 FY2026-27 delivered 25% volume growth. On a comparable basis for the retained business, revenue from operations rose 18.8% to ₹965.0 crore from ₹812.0 crore, EBITDA from operations rose 14.7% to ₹115.1 crore from ₹100.4 crore, and PAT rose 101.6% to ₹135.5 crore from ₹67.2 crore.

Broad-based business momentum. Management reported double-digit volume and value growth across all businesses with market share gains. Decorative Paints saw strong growth across Retail and Projects, led by the core Premium portfolio and supported by adjacencies. Industrial Paints saw new order wins across energy and infrastructure, automotive OEMs, premium vehicle refinish, and coil coatings.

Product innovation. Two new propositions strengthened the decorative portfolio: upgraded Dulux Aquatech Waterproof Basecoat and Dulux Aquatech Damp Cure in waterproofing, and Sadolin Hydro PU, a water-based polyurethane wood coating with low VOC, child-safe certification, superior water resistance, and fast drying.

Share split to broaden participation. The Board approved sub-division of one equity share of face value ₹10 into ten equity shares of face value Re. 1, subject to shareholder approval via postal ballot. The rationale is to enhance affordability and accessibility for small retail investors, improve liquidity, and expand the retail shareholder base. The split does not change intrinsic value or dilute existing shareholding.

JSW Group backing. In December 2025, JSW Paints Limited, part of the US$23 billion JSW Group, became the new promoter and holding company of Akzo Nobel India Limited, bringing local expertise together with global quality and innovation. The company operates five manufacturing facilities, one RD&I center, and a pan-India distribution network with around 1,300 employees.

Other income support. Q1 FY2026-27 standalone other income of ₹813 million included dividend of ₹559 million from subsidiary ICI India Research and Technology Centre Private Limited (₹447 per equity share) and interest on income tax refund of ₹215 million following resolution of certain tax matters.

Key Risks

  • Continued raw material inflation could pressure margins despite price increases.
  • The GST show cause notice represents a contingent liability of ₹10,60,96,432; the outcome of submissions before authorities is uncertain.
  • Reported results remain distorted by the prior-year divestments, complicating year-on-year comparisons.
  • Physical shareholders who have not completed KYC may face frozen folios and delayed receipt of new share certificates.
  • The share split requires shareholder approval via postal ballot (remote e-voting from 22 August 2026 to 20 September 2026) and regulatory/statutory approvals; the record date will be determined after shareholder approval.
  • The safe harbor statement notes that many factors could cause actual results to differ from forward-looking statements, including price and currency fluctuations, raw material and personnel costs, environmental risks, legal issues, and regulatory measures.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements. Management stated that Q1 FY2026-27 started on a strong note with solid progress on strategic priorities, delivering double-digit volume and value growth across all businesses with market share gains. The company took calibrated price increases to partly offset raw material inflation. Management expects the share split to enhance liquidity and broaden equity participation, and remains focused on sustainable value creation. The expected time of completion for the split is tentatively within two months from shareholder approval and any regulatory/statutory approvals. The company is in the process of responding to the GST show cause notice within the stipulated time.

Observed performance versus guidance. The reported standalone revenue decline of 2.8% and EBITDA decline of 14.4% contrast with the comparable retained-business growth of 18.8% in revenue and 14.7% in EBITDA, reflecting the impact of divestments on reported figures. The 25% volume growth and double-digit growth across businesses align with management’s stated momentum. PAT growth of 48.8% on a reported basis and 101.6% on a comparable basis was supported by other income including subsidiary dividend and tax refund interest. The temporary adverse impact on gross margins from working capital efficiency was acknowledged by management alongside the EBITDA growth achieved through cost management and growth investments.
