Dwarikesh Sugar Industries – Recent Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Q1 FY27 loss driven by seasonal and cost factors. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, the company reported a loss before tax of ₹34.38 crore and loss after tax of ₹25.73 crore, compared with a loss before tax of ₹13.03 crore and loss after tax of ₹9.38 crore in Q1 FY26. Total income declined to ₹360.07 crore from ₹405.97 crore. The company attributes this to the absence of cane crushing and sugar production during the quarter, lower margins on the sale of high-cost sugar inventory carried forward from Sugar Season 2025-26 (when a higher State Advised Price raised production costs), and a sharp reduction in ethanol production and sales.

Early closure of the 2025-26 sugar season. The season concluded early in March 2026, so no sugarcane was procured or crushed in Q1 FY27. This also meant virtually no power generation during the quarter, with power revenue at ₹0.47 million versus ₹2.96 million a year earlier, and no surplus bagasse available for sale because the entire quantity was used for captive consumption.

Ethanol segment pressure. Industrial alcohol production fell to 28.85 lakh litres and sales to 78.17 lakh litres, against production of 214.99 lakh litres and sales of 216.49 lakh litres in the prior-year quarter. Lower production led to under-absorption of fixed manufacturing overheads and higher per-litre production cost. The industry also faces the lack of revision in prices of ethanol derived from sugarcane juice and B-Heavy molasses, coupled with preferential offtake of grain-based ethanol.

Government intervention in the sugar market. In response to revised-down production estimates and firming prices, the Government imposed stock holding limits on traders and dealers and warned of punitive action against mills failing to sell their entire monthly release quota, while considering further measures to even out supply and prevent price escalation.

Weather risk. The possibility of an El Niño pattern poses a key risk to sugarcane production in Sugar Season 2026-27, with any adverse impact expected to be more pronounced in Maharashtra and southern states. For Uttar Pradesh, the eventual cane availability depends on weather during the critical post-September period.

Seasonality. The company notes that, given the seasonal nature of the industry, the results of any quarter may not be a true or proportionate reflection of annual performance.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Firming domestic sugar prices. Ex-factory prices averaged around ₹4,000 per quintal in FY 2025-26, improved modestly in Q1 FY27, and currently rule at ₹4,200–4,300 per quintal. Supported by lower carry-over inventories, steady domestic demand and a balanced demand-supply scenario, prices are expected to remain firm near term. Average realization on domestic sugar sold in Q1 FY27 was ₹4,064 per quintal versus ₹3,962 per quintal a year earlier.

Tighter global balance. Global sugar production is projected at a record 182.0 MMT (ISO) / 186.056 MMT (USDA) in 2025-26 with an estimated 2.2 MMT surplus, but for 2026-27 both agencies expect production to moderate while consumption rises to a record 179.991 MMT. The ISO expects a shift to a 0.262 MMT deficit in 2026-27, strengthening global prices, with New York raw sugar at 14.50–15.00 cents/lb and London white above USD 465/MT.

Lower domestic production and adequate stocks. Net sugar production for SS 2025-26 is expected to conclude at around 280 lakh tons (276 lakh tons achieved so far). After exports of around 7 lakh tons, closing stock is estimated at nearly two months’ domestic consumption, considered adequate.

Ethanol blending progress. India’s blending level is now around 20%, and the Government’s long-term vision of higher blends and flex-fuel vehicles could enhance energy security, reduce crude imports, lower emissions and create sustainable opportunities for the sugar industry.

Improved cane outlook in Uttar Pradesh. The cane crop appears in good condition, though area under cultivation declined marginally. The impact of red rot on the Co 0238 variety has largely receded, with substantial replacement by newer disease-resistant varieties. Subject to normal weather, a healthy sugarcane crop is expected in the 2026-27 season.

Capacity footprint. Three plants in Uttar Pradesh (Bijnor and Bareilly districts) with combined crushing capacity of 21,500 TCD, 94 MW power and 337.5 KLPD distillery capacity; ethanol is positioned as the key growth driver.

3. Key Risks

  • El Niño or other adverse weather affecting cane production and the domestic sugar balance sheet.
  • Continued non-revision of ethanol prices from sugarcane juice and B-Heavy molasses, plus preferential offtake of grain-based ethanol.
  • Government measures such as stock holding limits and punitive action over monthly release quota compliance.
  • Public scrutiny of the ethanol blending program over vehicle compatibility, engine wear and food security concerns.
  • Dependence of actual cane availability on post-September weather conditions.
  • Seasonality, meaning quarterly results may not reflect annual performance.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance. Management expects a healthy sugarcane crop in the coming season, subject to normal weather and absence of major climatic aberrations, citing an improved varietal mix and satisfactory crop development. It expects firm sugar prices to support profitability and looks ahead to coming quarters with cautious optimism. It commits to enhancing operational efficiencies and maintaining stringent cost discipline.

Observed performance. Q1 FY27 results show a loss at both PBT and PAT levels, with EBITDA of negative ₹23.76 crore versus positive ₹4.43 crore in Q1 FY26, and EPS of negative ₹1.39 versus negative ₹0.51. The quarter had no crushing, no sugar production (versus 0.54 lakh quintals a year earlier), and sharply lower ethanol volumes. Sugar stock as on June 30, 2026 was 7.80 lakh quintals versus 9.71 lakh quintals a year earlier. Outstanding long-term loan stood at ₹83.52 crore as on June 30, 2026, at a concessional rate, relating to the distillery project at the DD unit. Finance cost fell to ₹2.98 crore from ₹5.40 crore on scheduled term loan repayments, and depreciation fell to ₹76.3 million from ₹120.6 million due to a revision in estimated useful lives of certain assets under Schedule II of the Companies Act, 2013.

Governance items. The 32nd AGM was held on August 6, 2026, with a cut-off date of July 30, 2026 and remote e-voting from August 3–5, 2026. All six resolutions passed with majority votes: adoption of audited financial statements for FY ended March 31, 2026; re-appointment of B. J. Maheshwari as rotating director; declaration of dividend for FY26; re-appointment of Gautam R. Morarka as Whole Time Director designated Executive Chairman; fixing his remuneration (special resolution, passed with 97.44% in favour and 2.56% against); and ratification of Cost Auditors’ remuneration for FY 2026-27.

Broker Narrative

The broker stayed Buy throughout, initially viewing weak sugar volumes and recovery as temporary while betting on ethanol-led growth and steady sugar prices. By the last report, the narrative had become defensive: ethanol was disrupted by government feedstock restrictions, cane supply was hurt by red rot and weak monsoon, and the positives were policy reversals and export hopes rather than visible earnings momentum. Red-rot/cane recovery worries persisted from the first to last report, but the early confidence in ethanol expansion was replaced by feedstock bans and cost pressures.

Fears that came true

  • The red-rot disease and recovery-rate risk flagged early materialised in lower cane availability and eventually nil cane crushing in 2QFY25.
  • The initial nil sugar export situation persisted, with exports only being discussed as a possible Jan-Feb 2025 event rather than an actual recovery.
  • The early risk to ethanol offtake materialised in acute form, as a government feedstock ban and lower utilisation drove ethanol volumes down 28% YoY.

Optimism that failed

  • The projected ~110mn litres of FY24 ethanol production, with 60% from cane juice/syrup and 40% from B-heavy molasses, failed as ethanol volumes fell 28% YoY.
  • The expected ethanol revenue/EBIT CAGRs and overall EBIT/PAT CAGRs failed to materialise, with distillery EBIT down 79% YoY and actual returns deeply negative across the covered period.
  • The hope that cane variety recalibration would improve recovery was undermined by red rot and weak-monsoon-reduced cane availability.
