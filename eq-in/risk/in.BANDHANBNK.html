Bandhan Bank (BANDHANBNK) Analysis: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Growth Prospects, and Key Risks

Bandhan Bank, a mid-sized private sector bank with roots in microfinance (EEB segment), is undergoing portfolio diversification amid asset quality challenges. Q2FY26 results (ended Sep 2025) show muted growth and profitability, but improving mix and balance sheet strength. Total assets ~₹1,950 bn, advances ₹1,400 bn (7% YoY), deposits ₹1,581 bn (11% YoY). Below is a structured summary based on disclosures (earnings presentation, GST order, NPA sale, etc.).

Tailwinds (Positive Catalysts)

  • Portfolio Diversification & De-risking: EEB share down to 37% (from higher legacy levels); secured book at 55% (+790 bps YoY). Non-EEB advances up 24% YoY (₹883 bn), wholesale (29% share, + strong ratings: 95% FIG/H&E A-/above). Housing/retail growing steadily.
  • Deposit Franchise Strength: 11% YoY growth; retail deposits 71% of mix (+16% YoY); CASA 28%; low-cost sticky base supports liquidity (CASH ratio 28%).
  • Capital & Liquidity Buffer: CRAR 18.6% (CET1 17.8%), well above regulatory norms; enables growth/investments.
  • Digital & Outreach Momentum: 98% retail txns digital; 92% savings accounts opened digitally; expanding WhatsApp banking, UPI, QR collections. 1,754 branches + 4,593 banking units across 35 states; non-East loan share 62%.
  • Improving Collections: EEB efficiency ~98%; customer paying profile strong (95% full payers).
  • Awards & Governance: Gallup Exceptional Workplace (2x winner); strong board additions (e.g., IT/banking experts).

Headwinds (Challenges)

  • Profitability Pressure: NII down 12% YoY (₹26 bn); NIM compressed 152 bps YoY to 5.8%; PAT ₹1.1 bn (-88% YoY); cost/income 58%; ROA/ROE at 0.2%/1.8% (multi-year lows).
  • Slow Growth: Advances +7% YoY (vs. deposits +11%); overall book flat QoQ.
  • High Operating Costs: OPEX/assets 3.8%; employee productivity up but cost/income elevated.
  • Legacy EEB Drag: Still 37% of book; SMA/NPA stress (₹59 bn stress pool); collections dipped slightly QoQ.
  • NIM/Spread Squeeze: Yield spread down; higher deposit costs.

Growth Prospects (Medium-Term Opportunities)

  • Balanced Portfolio Expansion: Target sustained non-EEB growth (23-25% YoY); wholesale (29% share) scaling with high ratings; housing disbursements stable; retail (vehicles/gold/personal) ramp-up.
  • Geographic/Geography Shift: Non-East loans 62% (+228 bps YoY); SU-RU focus for deposits/outreach.
  • Digital/Agency Scale: 100%+ YoY growth in digital deposits/UPI; merchant acquiring (Jio tie-up), agency business (tax collections), fintech (e-NACH/escrow).
  • NPA Cleanup: Planned sale of ₹3,212 Cr NPAs + ₹3,719 Cr write-offs to ARCs (Swiss challenge/auction); could unlock capital, reduce credit costs (currently 3.4%).
  • Guidance Implicit: Productivity +18% YoY; training 668K hours; universal bank pivot from microfinance.

Projected Trajectory: Advances/deposits growth 12-15% FY26E (industry ~15%); NIM stabilization at 5.5-6%; RoA recovery to 1%+ by FY27 if asset quality improves. Valuations attractive (low TEVABV ~0.8x).

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from an initial Accumulate call that treated weak MFI/asset-quality numbers as near-term seasonal shocks and expected FY24 recovery, to a final Hold that cuts FY27 RoA guidance and accepts a structurally weaker profitability outlook. Persistent themes across both reports were sluggish loan growth, deposit/funding-cost pressure, and elevated opex. What changed was the loss of optimism on NIM improvement, credit growth recovery, and supportive macro tailwinds, replaced by guidance cuts, regulatory drag on retail sourcing, and caution on technology/monsoon/funding risks.

Fears that came true

  • Early concern about weak/flat loan growth and reliance on a ~20% FY24 recovery was realised, as the last report still cites slower-than-system loan growth and most growth-oriented calls ended in DISAPPOINTMENT with negative actual returns.
  • Early concern about CASA slipping to a 14-quarter low and dependence on high-cost term deposits was realised, with the last report showing deposit growth at only 7% YoY, bulk deposits down 13% YoY, and savings rates rising.
  • Early warning that elevated opex from physical expansion would persist was realised, as the last report flags continued franchise investments, technology costs up 80% YoY, salary/payout hikes, higher PSLC expenses, and a one-time gratuity provision.

Optimism that failed

  • Management’s guided ~20% credit growth by FY24-end failed to materialise, as the last report describes slower-than-system loan growth.
  • The early confidence in NIM resilience/guidance of 7-7.5% gave way to NIM pressure, with the last report guiding only stable NIM versus an earlier expected ~30bps improvement.
  • The retail/commercial growth engine touted in the first report failed to sustain, as last report notes retail loan sourcing has been impacted by recent regulatory norms and overall loan growth lagged the system.
  • The macro tailwinds of normal rural demand and limited rate-hike risk failed, as the last report cites energy crisis, unpredictable monsoons, and elevated funding costs.
