Kajaria Ceramics — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Weak demand at the start of the quarter: Q1 FY27 volume growth was 6% year-on-year, which the Chairman attributed to a “very weak April.” Revenue growth of 20% (consolidated, to Rs. 1,328 crores) came “mainly due to price increase” rather than volumes.
  • Underutilised capacity in parts of the bathware business: The Kerovit Global (KGPL) sanitaryware plant operated at only 39% in Q1 FY27, and Kajaria Sanitaryware (KSPL) at 81%. The faucet facility ran at 90%.
  • International operations: The UK showroom operation under Kajaria International FZCO was closed down because running expenses of UK showrooms are very high and it is difficult to make profit in the short to medium term.
  • Capacity reduction: Kajaria Infinity’s glazed vitrified tile capacity was reduced from 5.70 MSM to 2.70 MSM.
  • Physical shareholding compliance burden: Shareholders in physical mode who fail to furnish PAN and KYC details cannot lodge grievances, avail RTA service requests, or receive dividends electronically; transfer or sale of physical shares will not be processed unless dematerialized. PAN must be linked with Aadhaar.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Capacity expansions:
    • Srikalahasti (Andhra Pradesh): brownfield expansion of 10 MSM glazed vitrified tiles at approximately Rs. 210 crore, approved by the Board on 30 April 2026, expected completion by March 2027.
    • Gailpur (Rajasthan): brownfield expansion of 11 MSM glazed vitrified tiles at approximately Rs. 165 crore, approved by the Board on 31 July 2026, expected completion by April 2027, financed through internal accruals, to meet growing demand for glazed vitrified tiles. The existing Gailpur capacity of 35.95 MSM was stated as running at 100% utilisation.
  • New adhesive plant: Kajaria Adhesive Private Limited’s new tile adhesive facility (9,000 MT per month) in Erode, Tamil Nadu became operational from May 2026, adding to the existing Gailpur adhesive facility of the same capacity.
  • Subsidiary performance: Kajaria Vitrified and Kajaria Infinity operated at optimum capacity in Q1 FY27; South Asian Ceramics at 95%; Kajaria Surfaces at 91%; the Nepal JV (KRTL) at 89%.
  • Buyback completed: Buyback of 21,50,000 equity shares (1.35% of paid-up equity capital as on 31 March 2026) at Rs. 1,380 per share, aggregating up to Rs. 296.70 crore, was completed.
  • Bathware stake consolidation: The Board on 30 April 2026 approved acquiring the CCPS held by Aravali Investment Holdings (WestBridge Crossover Fund) in Kajaria Bathware for Rs. 50 crore, expected to complete in the following month.
  • Renewable energy cost initiative: Board approval on 31 July 2026 for investment up to Rs. 12.15 crore in equity shares of Sunsure Solarpark Fourty Three Private Limited for solar and wind power captive consumption at the Gailpur and Malootana plants, aimed at reducing power costs; completion expected in approximately 15 months, with shareholding below 20%.
  • Regulatory facilitation: SEBI’s Special Window from February 5, 2026 to February 4, 2027 facilitates transfer and dematerialization of physical shares.
  • Investor engagement: Management (Mr. Chetan Kajaria, Vice Chairman, and Mr. Sanjeev Agarwal, CFO) scheduled to attend the Nuvama India Conference 2026 (SG Edition) in Singapore on August 11–12, 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Demand volatility: The weak April experienced in Q1 FY27 illustrates sensitivity of volumes to demand conditions; revenue growth depended on price increases.
  • Execution and timeline risk on expansions: The Srikalahasti (March 2027) and Gailpur (April 2027) expansions are subject to expected completion timelines.
  • Profitability of newer/niche facilities: The Kerovit plant, set up in March 2024 to produce high-end products, operated at 39%, indicating ramp-up risk in premium segments.
  • International operations: The UK showroom closure reflects difficulty of achieving profitability in certain overseas markets in the short to medium term.
  • Regulatory/compliance risk for shareholders: Non-compliance with PAN/KYC requirements by physical shareholders can result in inability to receive dividends electronically or transfer shares.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The company’s own disclaimer notes that actual results may differ substantially from forward-looking statements due to regulatory changes, local political or economic developments, technological risks, and other factors.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Volume growth: Management reported 6% year-on-year volume growth in Q1 FY27 despite a very weak April; consolidated revenue rose 20% to Rs. 1,328 crores, attributed mainly to price increase.
  • Margins: EBITDA margin for Q1 FY27 stood at 19.60% (consolidated), compared with 16.72% in Q1 FY26; standalone EBITDA margin was 18.42% versus 14.49%. Consolidated EBITDA grew 41% year-on-year in Q1 FY27.
  • Capacity utilisation vs. expansion rationale: The Gailpur plant’s existing capacity of 35.95 MSM was stated at 100% utilisation, and the expansion is justified by growing demand for glazed vitrified tiles. Several subsidiaries operated at or near optimum capacity (Kajaria Vitrified, Kajaria Infinity), while others had headroom (South Asian Ceramics 95%, Kajaria Surfaces 91%, KRTL 89%, KSPL 81%, KGPL 39%, faucet 90%).
  • Expansion timelines: Srikalahasti 10 MSM expansion (approved 30 April 2026) expected by March 2027; Gailpur 11 MSM expansion (approved 31 July 2026) expected by April 2027; Sunsure investment expected to complete in approximately 15 months.
  • Capital returns: The buyback of 21,50,000 shares at Rs. 1,380 per share (up to Rs. 296.70 crore) was completed, with the Chairman thanking shareholders for their support.
  • Outlook: The Chairman stated confidence that the company is well positioned to sustain quality growth and create long-term value for all stakeholders.
  • AGM outcomes: The 40th Annual General Meeting held on September 15, 2026 passed all four agenda items with the requisite majority, including adoption of financial statements, declaration of a final dividend of Rs. 6 per equity share, and re-appointment of directors Mr. Chetan Kajaria and Mr. Rishi Kajaria.

Broker Narrative

Narrative evolved from gas/deflation and volume recovery optimism (2023) to PVC/import protection and channel restocking hopes (2026), with offtake concerns persisting across cycles. Early gas cost fears gave way to resin volatility and Chinese competition threats, while management’s volume doubling guidance faced recent execution doubts despite capacity additions and Wavin integration.

Fears that came true

  • Sluggish off-take in 9MFY23 materialized as DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes with negative returns in late 2023 and early 2024.
  • Input cost & supply challenges evolved into sharp resin price volatility and elevated PVC prices causing channel destocking by 2026.
  • Softness in demand (2026 headwind) correlated with the flat +0.9% actual return despite +29.5% prediction on 2026-09-01.

Optimism that failed

  • Volume growth recovery and doubling sales volume guidance failed to sustain momentum, evidenced by flat Sep 2026 returns.
  • Export market potential for Indian tiles faltered against increased availability of low-priced Chinese PVC imports.
  • Channel restocking (2026 tailwind) failed to drive expected returns, resulting in FLAT outcome despite Buy rating.
