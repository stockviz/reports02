Headwinds and Challenges

  • Sequential earnings decline: Profit for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 fell to ₹50.51 crore from ₹391.91 crore in the quarter ended 31 March 2026, even as revenue from operations declined from ₹3,879.00 crore to ₹3,661.84 crore.
  • Rising cost base: Cost of materials consumed rose to ₹2,509.37 crore in the June 2026 quarter from ₹2,297.62 crore in the March 2026 quarter. Other expenses also increased to ₹843.04 crore from ₹746.61 crore over the same period.
  • Higher finance cost: Finance cost increased to ₹126.38 crore in the June 2026 quarter from ₹96.42 crore in the March 2026 quarter.
  • Borrowings increased: Borrowings rose to ₹5,056.15 crore as at 30 June 2026 from ₹4,601.94 crore as at 31 March 2026.
  • Weak debt service coverage: The debt service coverage ratio was 0.17 for the June 2026 quarter (not annualised), compared with 0.40 for the March 2026 quarter and 0.71 for the year ended 31 March 2026.
  • Interest rate reset history: The Rupee Term Loan interest rate was reset to 12.45% p.a. with effect from 1 March 2025, then revised to 8.70% p.a. linked to 3-month MCLR from 12 April 2025, and the latest applicable rate is 8.40% p.a. effective from 12 January 2026.
  • Pending security formality: The Equitable Mortgage of Land (excluding forest land) required under the Rupee Term Loan sanction terms was yet to be formalised as on the reporting date.
  • GST dispute: GST authorities conducted an audit for July 2017 to March 2021 and issued 10 show cause notices alleging inadmissible ITC of ₹111.10 crore plus interest and penalty, with an additional audit finding on non-payment of interest on delayed RCM payments of ₹0.09 crore, totalling ₹111.19 crore. Orders were passed for ₹111.10 crore; of this, ₹8.45 crore was accepted and reversed/paid, ₹45.90 crore was dropped, ₹56.40 crore remains in abeyance pending litigation before the Hon’ble High Court, Bilaspur, and ₹0.35 crore plus interest and penalty is under appeal. Interest of ₹0.09 crore has been paid. As the matter pertains to the pre-demerger period, NMDC Ltd is filing the appeals as per the demerger scheme.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Year-on-year improvement: Revenue from operations for the June 2026 quarter was ₹3,661.84 crore, higher than ₹3,365.22 crore in the June 2025 quarter. Profit for the period rose to ₹50.51 crore from ₹25.56 crore, and total comprehensive income was ₹55.15 crore.
  • Other income growth: Other income increased to ₹42.89 crore in the June 2026 quarter from ₹20.00 crore in the June 2025 quarter and ₹26.14 crore in the March 2026 quarter.
  • Improved interest coverage year-on-year: The interest service coverage ratio improved to 1.59 in the June 2026 quarter from 1.27 in the June 2025 quarter.
  • Lower debt-equity ratio year-on-year: The debt-equity ratio was 0.38 as at 30 June 2026 compared with 0.43 as at 30 June 2025.
  • Working capital headroom: The Company has a sanctioned working capital limit of ₹4,100 crore (fund-based ₹2,600 crore and non-fund-based ₹1,500 crore). Utilisation up to 30 June 2026 was ₹1,870.73 crore of fund-based and ₹1,005.64 crore of non-fund-based limits.
  • Strategic disinvestment process: The CCEA gave in-principle approval on 27 October 2016 for strategic disinvestment of several CPSEs including the NISP unit of NMDC Ltd. On 14 October 2020, CCEA gave in-principle approval for the demerger of NISP from NMDC and strategic disinvestment of the resulting entity by selling the entire stake of the Government of India. As per the Preliminary Information Memorandum and Request for Expression of Interest, the Government of India decided to divest its 50.79% shareholding in NMDC Steel Limited along with management control to a strategic buyer, and shall offer 10% stake in the resulting company to NMDC Limited after the strategic buyer has been identified through the bidding process.
  • Single reportable segment: The Company is engaged in manufacturing iron and steel products and generates revenue primarily from iron and steel products, which is the only reportable segment under Ind AS 108.

Key Risks

  • Debt service coverage below 1: The DSCR of 0.17 for the June 2026 quarter indicates cash generation relative to debt obligations.
  • Interest rate sensitivity: The Rupee Term Loan and working capital interest rates are linked to MCLR, with the latest applicable rate at 8.40% p.a. effective from 12 January 2026.
  • Repayment schedule: The Rupee Term Loan is repayable in 30 structured quarterly instalments starting from March 2024 by June 2031, with interest payable monthly. The outstanding Rupee term loan as on 30 June 2026 was ₹3,177.27 crore after repayment of ₹1,298.54 crore. There is no default as on the reporting date in repayment of borrowings and interest thereon.
  • Security encumbrance: The Company has hypothecated the entire fixed assets including plant and machinery, and provided a first charge on the entire cash flows of the Company. Working capital borrowings are secured by a first ranking pari passu charge on all current assets, both present and future.
  • Contingent tax litigation: The GST matter of ₹56.40 crore remains in abeyance pending litigation before the Hon’ble High Court, Bilaspur, and ₹0.35 crore plus interest and penalty is under appeal.
  • No subsidiaries/JV/associates: The Company does not have any subsidiary, joint venture, or associate as on 30 June 2026.
