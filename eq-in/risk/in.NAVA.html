Nava Limited — Q1 FY27 Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Phase II 300 MW thermal commissioning delayed. The MEL Phase II project was originally targeted for January–February 2027. Management stated on the August 14, 2026 call that complete commissioning of both units has slipped to Q2 FY27-28, around the June–July 2027 timeframe, with the first unit coming earlier and the second completing later. The delay is attributed to logistical delays in shipment of critical material and equipment caused by the ongoing conflict in West Asia, which affected supply chains, vessel movements, container availability and delivery schedules. The company describes the increase in capex as marginal and says MEL is well positioned to absorb it.
  • Zambia receivables uncertainty. The auditor’s review report draws attention to Note 4, describing uncertainty over recoverability of substantially overdue trade and other receivables of ₹16,514.61 lakhs as at June 30, 2026 (₹25,989.96 lakhs as at March 31, 2026) due from a customer of Maamba Energy Limited. Management considers these good and realisable based on a sovereign guarantee from the Government of Zambia and a favourable consent award from an Arbitration Tribunal. On the call, management noted MEL realised US$15 million from ZESCO during the quarter, reducing outstanding arrears to US$13.4 million.
  • Litigation contingency. The auditor also draws attention to Note 6 concerning a lawsuit filed by and against subsidiary Brahmani Infratech Private Limited, with the outcome presently unascertainable; claims are treated as contingent liability/contingent assets with no adjustment considered necessary.
  • Deferred tax volatility. Management linked prior deferred tax expense to the Zambian Kwacha/USD exchange rate, noting the rate was around 18 as at June 30, 2026 and that the charge may reverse if the currency stabilises.
  • Metals segment softness. Ferro alloys segment revenue fell to ₹25,710.58 lakhs in Q1 FY27 from ₹36,604.63 lakhs in Q4 FY26 and ₹29,137.01 lakhs in Q1 FY26. Management attributed lower sales in the quarter to a long-term maintenance shutdown at the Orissa unit, which resumed operations on August 1, 2026. Management characterised the metals business as volume-driven rather than margin-driven, with spot pricing improving 5–10% quarter-on-quarter and about 70% of production committed under long-term or quarterly contracts.
  • Zambia energy margin decline. Management attributed the decline in Zambian energy EBITDA to lower reversal of expected credit loss credits, guiding to a sustainable EBITDA margin of around 45–50% for that segment.
  • Geopolitical and supply chain disruption. Management flagged significant challenges from geopolitical and supply chain disruptions and said remedial actions are being taken.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record quarterly income. Consolidated total income reached an all-time quarterly high of ₹1,269 crore, with revenue from operations up 6.0% QoQ to ₹1,212 crore. Standalone total income reached a record ₹689 crore, with standalone PBT before exceptional items up 92.9% QoQ, supported by lower material and manufacturing costs and dividend income.
  • Energy performance. Energy revenue rose 18.3% QoQ, supported by healthy PLFs and availability of bilateral contracts. MEL’s 300 MW plant operated at 89.3 PLF. Energy segment revenue was ₹96,203.79 lakhs in Q1 FY27 versus ₹81,306.18 lakhs in Q4 FY26.
  • Dividend upstreaming. Nava received US$15 million in dividend income from Nava Global during the quarter. Management confirmed Nava Global’s dividend policy of a minimum 30% of PAT, and that Nava Limited is the 100% ultimate beneficial owner of Nava Global.
  • Mining momentum. Mining revenue rose 20.4% QoQ to ₹12,784.50 lakhs, driven by higher sales volumes. Exploration at the 360 sq. km manganese concession in Côte d’Ivoire has yielded promising results, with the company progressing towards an exploitation permit; detailed exploration has covered 2 sq. km of the concession. Lithium and tantalum exploration continues. A separate government-controlled mine in Ivory Coast is being pursued for a JV, which would be evaluated for a manganese alloy plant once approvals come through.
  • Solar commissioning imminent. Maamba Solar Energy Limited’s 100 MW solar project is set for commissioning by September 2026, when power flows and commercial revenues commence. Management said this establishes a new niche prompting further investments in the space.
  • Avocado project. Plantation activity across all divisions is targeted for completion by end-FY27/Q1 FY28. The packhouse is in advanced stage and set for commissioning in October 2026, ahead of the FY27 harvest.
  • Kawambwa Sugar. Sugarcane plantation is progressing under pivot irrigation; sugar and power plant equipment supplies have commenced from India; construction has gained pace. Commissioning expected by March 2028, with integrated sugar cane estate, co-generation and distillery. On the call, management indicated sugar revenues of INR55–60 million per annum and commissioning in Q4 FY28.
  • Renewables and new energy options. Following the amendment of the Memorandum of Association (passed as Resolution 9 at the AGM on August 14, 2026), management said it is seriously evaluating solar, wind, hybrid with battery storage for round-the-clock power, and is watching the small modular reactor (SMR) space. Management described itself as location-agnostic, pursuing best returns for risk across India and international geographies.
  • Phase II economics. Project cost of INR400 million, funded INR300 million debt and INR100 million equity, with envisaged return on equity of about 15%. Management indicated MEL Phase II revenue of around INR200 million and an attractive tariff, though it declined to guide on margins. Phase II currently has no tax holiday, which the company is pursuing with the government.
  • Land bank study. A third-party study is ongoing to assess options for the company’s land holdings, carried at historical cost (Nacharam land historical cost around INR40 lakhs). Management said projects underway are adequately funded and there is no need to sell assets in distress.

3. Key Risks

  • Receivable recovery risk at MEL. The auditor’s emphasis-of-matter on ₹16,514.61 lakhs of overdue receivables, mitigated in management’s view by the Zambian sovereign guarantee and arbitration award.
  • Brahmani Infratech litigation. Outcome unascertainable; claims carried as contingent liability/contingent assets.
  • Project execution and supply chain risk. The Phase II delay illustrates exposure to geopolitical conflict, shipping and equipment delivery disruptions.
  • Currency risk. Deferred tax outcomes depend on the Kwacha/USD rate at reporting dates, with potential for reversal.
  • Commodity price and volume risk in ferro alloys. Although ~70% of production is contracted, the segment remains exposed to spot pricing and volume swings; the Orissa shutdown demonstrated volume sensitivity.
  • Zambia political environment. Management stated operations are not linked to any political party and have continued through multiple changes of government; Zambian elections were held the day before the call.
  • Segment margin sustainability. Zambian energy EBITDA guided at 45–50%; consolidated EBITDA guided at 35–40% of segment EBITDA after intersegment eliminations.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

  • Phase II commissioning. Previously guided to January–February 2027; now guided to Q2 FY27-28 (June–July 2027), with phased unit commissioning. The press release states commissioning “in phases by July 2027.”
  • Solar. Guided for commissioning by end-September 2026; press release states September 2026, with commercial revenues commencing on power flow.
  • Sugar. Guided to Q4 FY28 on the call and March 2028 in the press release, with revenue of INR55–60 million per annum.
  • Avocado. Plantation completion targeted end-FY27/Q1 FY28; packhouse commissioning October 2026.
  • Dividend policy. Confirmed minimum 30% of PAT at Nava Global.
  • Zambian energy EBITDA margin. Guided to 45–50% sustainable, after a decline attributed to lower ECL credit reversals.
  • Consolidated EBITDA. Guided to 35–40% of segment EBITDA after intersegment eliminations.
  • Metals margins. Guided to stability or slight increase through the financial year, with no downward trend expected; Q1 FY27 ferro alloys segment result was ₹1,782.85 lakhs versus ₹1,454.62 lakhs in Q4 FY26 and ₹1,357.91 lakhs in Q1 FY26.
  • Phase II return on equity. Guided at about 15%, with no tax holiday currently.
  • Observed versus prior periods. Consolidated EBITDA of ₹584 crore in Q1 FY27 compared with ₹423 crore in Q4 FY26 and ₹628 crore in Q1 FY26; consolidated PAT of ₹333 crore versus ₹136 crore in Q4 FY26 and ₹399 crore in Q1 FY26. Standalone EBITDA before exceptional item was ₹319 crore versus ₹170 crore in Q4 FY26 and ₹187 crore in Q1 FY26. Standalone PBT was ₹309 crore versus ₹564 crore in Q4 FY26 (which included an exceptional item) and ₹178 crore in Q1 FY26.
