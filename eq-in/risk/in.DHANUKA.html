Dhanuka Agritech — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Monsoon disruption and delayed sowing. The agrochemical industry faced a significantly challenging first quarter of FY 2026-27. Revenue growth across the sector remained under pressure due to delayed monsoon onset in several key agricultural regions, which postponed sowing and reduced product demand. Management noted a 40% rainfall shortfall in June, narrowing to a 15% shortfall by end-July, and observed that even a 1% rainfall shortfall impacts sowing area and crop growth. Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra — key cotton and soybean markets — were worst affected, with resowing required in some pockets. The South also saw weak sentiment due to poor June and July rainfall.

Weak demand and price competition. Industry profitability remained under pressure from weaker domestic demand and price competition. Price increases attempted early in the season to offset higher raw material and logistics costs linked to geopolitical tensions in West Asia could not be sustained; pricing began declining from May, fell significantly across many molecules in June, and declined further in July. Management characterised the April price spike as an artificial shortage-driven increase that reversed thereafter.

Elevated costs. The sector continued to face elevated raw material and logistics costs during the quarter. Other expenses were flat year-on-year against a 12.56% revenue decline, which management attributed to tight cost control, noting that many expenses are planned well ahead of the season.

Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue from operations was Rs. 461.93 crores versus Rs. 528.29 crores in Q1 FY26, a degrowth of approximately 12.56%. EBITDA was Rs. 55.01 crores and PAT was Rs. 36.30 crores (against EBITDA of Rs. 83.19 crores and PAT of Rs. 55.50 crores in Q1 FY26). Volume decline was approximately 12.7%, almost identical to the value decline, indicating no meaningful price growth. Herbicides, which are Q1-heavy, took the hit; management stated Q2 was also shaping up as a difficult quarter, with July better than June but large parts of the country still rainfall-deficit.

Expiry of Udhampur incentives. The incentives available to the company’s Udhampur Unit expired in March 2026.

GST notice. A GST notice received a couple of months prior remains under consideration, with Lakshmikumaran appointed as consultant and a date yet to be fixed. Management expressed confidence the order will be in the company’s favour, citing the molecules’ categorisation as fertilizer with a 5% rate per the circular.

Dahej plant profitability. Dahej revenue was around Rs. 26 crores in Q1 FY27 versus Rs. 16 crores a year earlier, with EBITDA improving from negative Rs. 3 crores to less than Rs. 1 crore. Management indicated full-year EBITDA breakeven appears difficult and guided to a negative Rs. 4-5 crores range.

Seasonal demand dependence. Demand for the company’s products depends on monsoon, pest infestation and crop patterns, which can cause period-to-period variation in results.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

New product pipeline. The company plans to launch five new products in the upcoming months — one liquid fertiliser, three fungicides and one herbicide. In the biologicals/nutrition category, two of three products have already been introduced, with the third expected by end-August after receipt of regulatory approvals and sale permissions in most states; two further nutrition-biological products are planned for the financial year. Verdor, introduced around September last year, saw good traction in Q1 FY27, while MYCORe SUPER (launched Q1 FY25) continued to perform well across cereals, pulses, sugarcane and horticulture.

Innovation Turnover Index. The company tracks new product performance through its Innovation Turnover Index, which stood at about 13.89% last year; contribution from the last three years’ introductions was around 11.56% in Q1. Management benchmarks a minimum 20% margin for any new introduction, with 9(3) and nutrition products often commanding significantly higher margins — more than double in some cases.

Bayer acquisition and international expansion. Dhanuka acquired Iprovalicarb and its variants and Triadimenol and its variants from Bayer for global markets. Customers have been established and business started in some markets, while distribution setup is ongoing in others; the Executive Director of International Business travelled to the U.S. and Brazil to meet existing and prospective customers. The acquisition provides access to over 20 international markets. In India, Bayer product revenue was booked from the previous year; Q1 is nominal as the key product is for grapes, with major turnover expected in September. Management stated Bayer product revenue will not be significant in FY27, and that two companies — one in Brazil and one in Europe — are being incorporated, with some distributors appointed.

New subsidiary in Ireland. Dhanuka Agritech Europe Private Limited was incorporated in Ireland on August 26, 2026, to undertake agrochemical business and allied activities. The company subscribed to 100 ordinary shares of EUR 1 each for EUR 100, holding 100% of its share capital.

Nagpur manufacturing plant. The company acquired land in the Butibori industrial zone of Nagpur, Maharashtra, for a new formulation plant with a proposed capacity of 23,000 MT per annum and an estimated outlay of up to Rs. 200 crores, expected to be operational by April 2028 (commissioning targeted Q4 FY28). Management described it as a significantly automated, global-standard plant with lower labour dependence, and noted the site has room for scaling up. Certain CGST benefits are available from the Maharashtra Government. The plant is deliberately kept separate from Dahej, which is committed to chemical synthesis. CAPEX of around Rs. 100 crores-plus is expected in FY27-28.

Bio-stimulant regulation. Management expects the regulatory design to squeeze smaller and unorganised players, with state governments taking a stringent view on product and player approvals — a direction seen as favourable to organised players.

Distribution and reach. The company reaches more than 10 million farmers through approximately 6,500 distributors and over 80,000 retailers, supported by four manufacturing facilities and 41 warehouses, with partnerships with 10 leading multinational innovators from Japan, Europe and the U.S., two R&D centres and NABL-accredited laboratories.

Inorganic scouting. As a debt-free company with a strong balance sheet, Dhanuka is scouting inorganic growth opportunities including product portfolios, with Dahej capabilities and Indian market access as dimensions for evaluation.

Shareholder returns. A final dividend of 100% (Rs. 2 per equity share, face value Rs. 2) was considered at the 41st AGM held August 3, 2026, with the result to be declared in due course. The company previously completed a buyback of 5 lakh equity shares at Rs. 1,400 per share, absorbing Rs. 70 crores. The AGM passed all 7 resolutions, including adoption of financial statements, dividend declaration, director re-appointments, ratification of cost auditor remuneration, and approval of employee stock option and stock appreciation rights plans. The Board also approved a loan of up to Rs. 15 crores to the Dhanuka Employee Stock Benefits Trust for the SAR 2026 plan.

Horticulture traction. High horticulture prices (tomato, cucurbits) drove growth in specific Japanese fungicides — Nissodium and Conika — in Q1, a quarter conventionally dominated by herbicides.

3. Key Risks

  • Monsoon and weather dependence: Demand depends on monsoon, pest infestation and crop patterns; rainfall deficits delay sowing and reduce product offtake, with irrigation availability (about 60% of Indian agriculture) determining whether farmers invest in crop protection or opt for low-cost/no-spray options.
  • Pricing pressure: Attempted price hikes could not be sustained amid weak demand; prices declined from May through July.
  • Input cost inflation: Elevated raw material and logistics costs linked to geopolitical tensions in West Asia.
  • Regulatory and tax exposure: The GST notice outcome remains pending, notwithstanding management’s confidence.
  • Project execution and returns: The Nagpur plant carries a higher-than-conventional formulation CAPEX (Rs. 200 crores) justified by automation; management declined to comment on asset turns until project details are finalised, expected around late Q4.
  • Dahej profitability: EBITDA breakeven expected to be difficult in FY27, with a guided negative Rs. 4-5 crores.
  • International ramp-up uncertainty: Bayer product revenue timing and scale remain uncertain, with no threshold disclosed for distribution network expansion in export markets.
  • Incentive expiry: Udhampur Unit incentives lapsed in March 2026.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 revenue guidance cut. Management guided for lower single-digit revenue growth for FY 2026-27 and an EBITDA decline of approximately 200 bps, following a steep cut to the earlier guidance. Management attributed the cut to monsoon movement and its impact, while expressing hope for growth in Q2 and Q3 and stating it was appropriate to project a clear picture to investors. Observed Q1 performance was a 12.56% revenue decline, and management acknowledged Q2 was also tracking as a difficult quarter with no larger growth visible, despite a favourable base.

Volume vs. value. Management stated value and volume declines were almost identical (12.56% vs. ~12.7%), indicating no price growth — consistent with its explanation that price hikes could not be sustained.

Dahej guidance. Full-year Dahej revenue guidance was around Rs. 65 crores, despite Rs. 26 crores already booked in Q1, on the reasoning that Q3 and Q4 demand is lower. EBITDA breakeven was guided as difficult, with a negative Rs. 4-5 crores range expected.

Nagpur timeline and CAPEX. Guidance of operational by April 2028 (commissioning Q4 FY28), with CAPEX of around Rs. 100 crores-plus in FY27-28 against a total estimated outlay of up to Rs. 200 crores.

Bayer products. Management declined to share FY27 revenue contribution figures for Triadimenol and Iprovalicarb, stating the amount appearing in this year’s balance sheet would not be significant, with major Iprovalicarb turnover expected in September.

Inventory. Management does not foresee stocking or destocking by end of Q2, describing inventory rotation as normal and business progressing in line with market demand.

Bio-stimulant revenue. Management did not provide a specific FY27 revenue expectation for the bio-stimulant category, stating the approach is being reworked and details would follow later.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from an upbeat growth story in May 2023 (double-digit volume-led growth, new technical plant, biologicals) to a defensively framed Buy in May 2026, focusing on financial tailwinds like GST refunds, marketing expense reimbursements, royalty reimbursements, and a share buyback rather than organic growth. Persisting themes include weather/monsoon risk and pricing pressure, while new concerns about regulatory challenges and the Middle East crisis emerged.

Fears that came true

  • The agrochemical industry downturn materialized, as the stock suffered a -44.2% actual return following the August 2024 Hold call.
  • Monsoon/erratic weather risk materialized, with the last report still citing erratic weather patterns as a headwind.
  • Pricing pressure persisted, evidenced by the repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and negative actual returns from mid-2024 onwards.

Optimism that failed

  • The projected double-digit volume-led revenue growth for FY24 did not materialize, as subsequent calls saw large negative actual returns.
  • The Dahej Bifenthrin Technical plant’s targeted ₹500mn FY24 sales likely fell short, contributing to the -25% actual return in May 2024.
  • The expectation of biologicals reaching ~5% of sales within 3 years has not been met, given the persistent operational headwinds.
