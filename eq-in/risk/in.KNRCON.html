KNR Constructions Limited — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Road sector recalibration. The road sector is going through a phase of recalibration, with project awarding subdued in the initial months of FY27 following softness through FY26. During the quarter, NHAI awarded approximately 107 kilometres of projects, while execution moderated to around 638 kilometres. Management noted that tendering and awards were slower to come through in the first quarter, and that the near-term awarding environment remains measured.

Execution and revenue softness. Standalone revenue for Q1 FY27 was ₹4,366.9 million, down 10% year-on-year and 18% quarter-on-quarter. Consolidated revenue was ₹5,879.4 million, down 4% year-on-year and 15% quarter-on-quarter. Consolidated EBITDA fell 47% year-on-year to ₹964.3 million.

Working capital deterioration. Standalone net working capital days rose to 133 days as of 30 June 2026, compared with 78 days as of March 2026. Consolidated debt stood at ₹1,975 crores as of 30 June 2026 versus ₹2,438 crores as of 31 March 2026, while consolidated net debt to equity rose to 0.9 from 0.49.

Telangana irrigation receivables. Collections on the Kaleswaram Package 4 irrigation project in Telangana have been stalled since March 2023. Trade receivables, retention amounts and unbilled amounts include ₹1,37,309.28 lakhs relating to this project as at 30 June 2026 (₹1,36,331.90 lakhs as at 31 March 2026). The statutory auditor drew attention to this in an Emphasis of Matter paragraph, noting management’s confidence in recovery based on contractual terms and internal assessments. Management described the situation in Telangana as different from earlier years, when bills were submitted and paid without follow-up, and said it now plans to pursue payments persistently. Receivables from Telangana Package 4 were stated at ₹1,300 crores, with overall irrigation receivables around ₹1,450 crores.

Mining project delays. The older Chhattisgarh mining project (Banhardih) has received only stage-one forest clearance; stage-two clearance has not come, and management expects a further 8–10 months before the project can start. Land acquisition is ongoing, with the Risdi village yet to be vacated. For the Kusmunda mining project, the appointed date was expected in the first week of September, with management declining to accept an earlier date during heavy rains because haul-road widening for 100-tonne dumpers could not proceed.

Mysore–Kushalnagara land and local opposition. For Mysore–Kushalnagara Packages 4 and 5, land availability was constrained: Package 5 had only 5–6 kilometres in hand, and Package 4 had only 30–35% of land available because local public opposition over service roads blocked highway construction. Land was fully handed over about two months before the call, and execution has since speeded up.

Battery energy storage caution. Management said initial battery storage bids went “very bad,” and that the way the market is moving in India is “very wrong,” with additional risk from the rising dollar and other external factors. The company is proceeding cautiously and is not confident of winning a contract.

UPEIDA qualification gap. Management stated it is not getting qualification for UPEIDA bids because they require greenfield express highway experience, which the company does not yet have, so it is not participating there.

Water pipeline project challenges. The water pipeline project initially faced some challenges, though management said it is now doing well.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Government spending commitment. The union budget allocated close to ₹3.1 lakh crore to the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, with road sector capex outlay up around 8% after two relatively subdued years. NHAI outlined a revised FY27 plan to award 54 highway and expressway projects covering 2,444 kilometres worth a combined ₹1.8 lakh crore across 13 states, which management said gives reasonable visibility for coming quarters.

Railways. A record ₹2.93 lakh crore of capital expenditure was allocated to Indian Railways for FY27, directed at capacity augmentation, multi-tracking, freight corridors, safety, station redevelopment and connectivity. Andhra Pradesh received a record ₹10,134 crores railway allocation for FY27, and the operationalisation of the South Coast railway zone strengthens regional focus. Management sees an opportunity to gradually diversify the order book into railway civil works, track infrastructure and related works.

Urban mobility. Metro and mass rapid transit expansion continues, with new corridors and extensions planned across key cities, creating a pipeline across elevated corridors, viaducts, stations, bridges and related civil work. The company has bid for the Bhubaneswar elevated metro project (which subsequently went at unhealthy prices and was later cancelled) and has started a JV with NCC for such projects.

New order wins. During the quarter, the company, in JV with Sushee Infra & Mining Limited, received a Letter of Acceptance from South Eastern Coalfield Limited for a coal mining project at Kusmunda in Chhattisgarh, with a total value of ₹3,361 crores excluding GST, to be executed over eight years. The company also received a Letter of Acceptance from Malkajgiri Municipal Corporation, Hyderabad for an EPC project — a 6-lane bi-directional flyover crossing TKR College junction, Gayatri Nagar Junction and Mandamallamma Junction — worth ₹235 crores with a 24-month construction period. A further Letter of Acceptance was received from Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation for a 2-lane unidirectional flyover at NFCL Junction, Banjara Hills and a 2-lane unidirectional underpass and 3-lane unidirectional flyover at TV9 Junction, Banjara Hills, worth ₹157,54,60,000 excluding GST, with a 24-month construction period.

Order book. As on 30 June 2026, the total order book stood at ₹8,667 crores, excluding the recently won two HAM projects and the mining project. Including those, the total order book stands at ₹15,234 crores, split 38% roads, 11% irrigation, 6% pipeline and 45% mining. Client-wise, 71% is from third-party clients and 29% from captive HAM projects; third-party order book is split 24% state government, 46% central government and 1% other private party. The order book excluding mining is executable over 3 to 3.5 years. The company is targeting order inflow of ₹8,000 crores to ₹10,000 crores during FY27, comprising NHAI projects, irrigation projects and other state government infrastructure works.

Asset monetisation. Under the share purchase agreement with Indus Infra Trust, the company completed transfer of its 100% stake in KNR Ramagiri Infra Private Limited and KNR Palani Infra Private Limited. In KNR Ramagiri, the company had invested ₹83 crores in the SPV through equity and subordinate debt and received total consideration of ₹227 crores. For KNR Palani, it had invested ₹64 crores and received consideration of ₹295 crores, including ₹90 crores of cash surplus as EPC claim. The standalone accounts show consideration of ₹43,248.32 lakhs against an invested amount of ₹14,714.31 lakhs, with a resultant profit of ₹28,533.06 lakhs shown as an exceptional item and resultant tax of ₹4,090.23 lakhs in current tax. The consolidated accounts show a resultant profit of ₹11,317.07 lakhs as an exceptional item, and a modification loss of ₹9,510.18 lakhs recognised in other expenses due to a change in estimates of the financial asset receivable, which is an Ind AS adjustment with no cash flow impact.

Credit profile. CRISIL reaffirmed the company’s long-term bank facility rating at CRISIL AA with stable outlook and short-term rating at CRISIL A1+.

Diversification intent. Management stated the next phase of growth is becoming increasingly diversified across expressways, railways, urban mobility, mining, irrigation, logistics and emerging areas such as energy storage, while maintaining discipline on project selection and returns.

Related party transactions. The Board approved material related party transactions with KNRHC Baidyanath Banhardih Coal Mine Private Limited, a subsidiary, and with KNR-SIML (JV), a related party, subject to member approval at the ensuing annual general meeting. The KNR-SIML (JV) contract is valued at ₹3,361.11 crores for EPC and mining services, and the KNRHC Baidyanath Banhardih contract at ₹3,552.43 crores, together contributing significantly to EPC turnover over 7 to 8 years.

3. Key Risks

Receivable recovery risk. The Kaleswaram Package 4 dues of ₹1,37,309.28 lakhs are treated as good for recovery by management based on contractual terms and internal assessments; the auditor’s opinion is not modified in respect of this matter. Management described ongoing discussions with the Finance Ministry and Irrigation Department, with the government considering installment payments of around ₹80–90 crores per month against approximately ₹650 crores outstanding, potentially in five to six installments, alongside a revised estimate of more than ₹700 crores that has been cleared by a technical committee and requires Finance Ministry and Irrigation Ministry approval and a G.O., a process that may take five to six months. Management said it expects ₹400–500 crores this year and plans to pursue payments persistently, noting that without pushing, things will not happen.

Mining execution and capital risk. Mining requires significant equipment deployment — ₹500–600 crores for Kusmunda, with ₹300–400 crores for Banhardih — and management described the difficulty of assessing EBITDA because equipment, labour and diesel are the major components, unlike roads where a large share of project cost is recovered in-year. Depreciation patterns vary by equipment type: dumpers on two-year buyback arrangements (with Caterpillar offering around 75% return after two years, and Komatsu agreeing to similar terms), other equipment on five-year cycles, and surface miners (costing about ₹13 crores each, with four required) continued for eight years. Management estimates 6%-plus PAT levels are possible in mining.

Order book concentration in mining. Mining constitutes 45% of the total order book, though management noted the annual turnover contribution is much lower than the headline order size — the ₹3,300 crores order can give only ₹350–400 crores turnover, and the Kusmunda mine at ₹5,000 crores with the company’s 75% share at ₹3,500 crores is to be completed over eight years.

Equity commitment risk. The company has already invested ₹595 crores out of an ₹805 crores revised equity requirement for all existing HAM projects, with additional equity of ₹210 crores to be infused as ₹125 crores in FY27 and ₹85 crores in FY28. A further additional equity investment of around ₹510 crores may be required for the Chennai ECR and Telangana NHAI HAM projects.

Bid pipeline uncertainty. Around ₹1,500 crores of L1 positions have been announced but LOAs are yet to be issued, across a few Chennai and Hyderabad City projects. Management was not certain about the status of other submitted bids.

External and market risks. Management flagged risks from the increasing dollar price and other external factors in the battery storage segment.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

FY27 revenue. Management guided to FY27 revenue of ₹2,200–2,300 crores, more than 10–15% above the prior year’s approximately ₹2,000 crores, with execution from the ECR Chennai and Telangana Mahabubnagar projects starting in Q3 and the Kusmunda mining project starting the following month. Observed Q1 FY27 standalone revenue was ₹436.7 crores and consolidated revenue ₹587.9 crores.

FY28 revenue. Management said FY28 should be a good year, with projects fully started, and that it will try to achieve more than ₹3,000 crores.

Margins. Management guided that Q1 and Q2 margins would be similar, with Q3 and Q4 EBITDA in the range of 11–12% as HAM and mining execution starts, and FY28 average EBITDA of 12–13%. For FY27 as a whole, guidance was 8–9% EBITDA, and for FY29 around 9%, with Q3 and Q4 of that year at 10–11%. Observed Q1 FY27 standalone EBITDA margin was 15.0% and consolidated 16.4%. Management clarified that excluding a one-off — revenue of around ₹76 crores and expenditure of around ₹30 crores, giving a net ₹46 crores in EBITDA — the underlying Q1 EBITDA margin was around 5.5%.

Segment revenue guidance. Irrigation: around ₹200 crores in FY27, with the irrigation order book left at about ₹800 crores after adjusting unbilled amounts. Pipeline: ₹300–400 crores in FY27 against an order book of around ₹830 crores, with the balance in the following year. Mining: around ₹150 crores in FY27 (plus or minus ₹20–40 crores), with ₹400 crores in FY28 from Kusmunda alone; Banhardih could add around ₹500 crores per annum once started, though timing is uncertain. Observed Q1 FY27 revenue split was 3% irrigation, 70% HAM and 25% EPC; water pipeline revenue was ₹7 crores.

Capex. Management guided FY27 capex of ₹350–400 crores, with mining capex the larger component. Observed Q1 FY27 capex was ₹14 crores. For Kusmunda, management indicated ₹400–450 crores this year, with 60–80% mobilisation if the department insists, and around ₹200 crores for Banhardih this year if it proceeds. Q1 FY27 depreciation was ₹10 crores.

HAM progress. Physical progress as on 30 June 2026: Ramanattukara to Valanchery 100% completed; Valanchery to Kappirikkad 100% completed; Magadi to Somwarpet approximately 91.35%; Marripudi to Somvarappadu approximately 86.03%; Mysore to Kushalnagara Package 4 around 20%; and Package 5 around 18.28%. The two new HAM projects are expected to receive appointed dates in Q3. For Mysore–Kushalnagara, management plans to complete the entire project by April, or May at the latest, and is arranging a PCOD by December on the 30–40% of land made available, to avoid further IDC blockage.

Irrigation recovery. Management expects ₹400–500 crores from the Telangana government in FY27, against approximately ₹650 crores outstanding on Package 4 and a revised estimate of more than ₹700 crores. Of the irrigation order book of ₹1,685 crores as of June, around ₹825 crores is unbilled revenue already recognised. HAM debtors as of June were ₹178 crores.

Order book and inflows. Management is targeting FY27 order inflow of ₹8,000–10,000 crores. The order book excluding mining is executable over 3 to 3.5 years; mining projects run five and eight years respectively.

Capital returns. On dividend and buyback, management said a buyback is being considered and that a conclusion meeting is to happen, with details to follow after the Board decides; the matter was described as still under assumptions. The Board fixed the record date for dividend payment as 15 September 2026, and the 31st Annual General Meeting is scheduled for Friday, 25 September 2026 at 12:30 PM through video conferencing or other audio-visual means, with key matters including declaration of a final dividend of ₹0.25 per equity share, ratification of cost auditor remuneration, and approval of material related party transactions with subsidiary and joint venture entities.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from concerns about aggressive bidding and a depleting order book in 2023 to execution challenges involving multiple authorities, utility shifting, and working capital build-up by 2026. Themes of robust infrastructure demand (NHAI pipeline, road capex) and diversification into railways persisted throughout, while the depleting order book concern was temporarily alleviated by 4QFY23 wins but execution quality remained a chronic problem.

Fears that came true

  • Aggressive bidding by inexperienced players at 20-25% below NHAI estimated costs adversely impacted order inflows, materializing as negligible KNR order wins in 2HFY22/9MFY23 and correlated with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes of -54.4% and -52.1% in late 2023.
  • Execution challenges involving multiple authorities, utility shifting, clearances, and frequent design changes causing delays and working capital build-up materialized, correlating with sustained DISAPPOINTMENT and deeply negative returns (-42.6%, -44.9%, -37.4%) across 2024-2025.
  • The first report’s concern about timely receipt of payments persisted and evolved into ongoing working capital issues cited in the last report, consistent with the stock’s prolonged negative trajectory through 2025.

Optimism that failed

  • A lean balance sheet, full debt repayment, and strong return ratios were expected to provide comfort for project execution, but the stock suffered DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes of -55.5% and -58.4% in late 2023 and late 2024 despite these positives.
  • Diversification into the railway sector was expected to mitigate operational risk and enhance growth, but persistent DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes through 2025 indicate this did not meaningfully improve returns or sentiment.
  • Higher Union Budget FY24E allocation to the Ministry of Roads was expected to drive project awarding and execution, yet the stock’s sustained negative trajectory through 2024-2025 suggests this tailwind was insufficient to overcome deeper execution challenges.
