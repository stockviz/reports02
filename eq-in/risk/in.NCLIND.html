Headwinds and Challenges

  • Discontinued Doors Division. NCL Industries has discontinued the operations of its Doors Division. An impairment loss of Rs. 2,575.37 lakhs was recognised on the division’s assets, based on an independent valuation. The related assets and liabilities were reclassified as “Assets held for sale” and “Liabilities directly associated with assets held for sale,” and the division’s financial performance is disclosed as “Discontinued Operations.” The division recorded a loss before interest and tax of Rs. 2,699.57 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 and Rs. 3,192.59 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2026, compared with Rs. 1,259.01 lakhs in the prior year. Revenue from discontinued operations fell to Rs. 491.41 lakhs for the year from Rs. 5,718.10 lakhs a year earlier.

  • Weak Boards Division performance. The Boards Division’s segment revenue declined to Rs. 16,339.92 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2026 from Rs. 20,611.94 lakhs in the prior year, and segment profit before interest and tax fell to Rs. 1,082.31 lakhs from Rs. 1,684.33 lakhs. Segment assets also declined to Rs. 11,134.13 lakhs from Rs. 14,510.22 lakhs.

  • Ready Mix Concrete Division contraction. RMC segment revenue declined to Rs. 13,088.54 lakhs for the year from Rs. 14,801.01 lakhs, and segment profit before interest and tax fell to Rs. 349.74 lakhs from Rs. 521.00 lakhs. RMC production and sales for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 fell 26% to 55,002 CuM from 73,991 CuM in the same quarter of the prior year.

  • Cement Boards production decline. Cement Boards production for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 fell 33% to 11,639 MT from 17,432 MT in the same quarter of the prior year, even as dispatches rose 18% to 13,123 MT from 11,159 MT.

  • Energy Division volatility. The Energy Division recorded a segment loss before interest and tax of Rs. 36.60 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026, compared with a loss of Rs. 39.20 lakhs in the same quarter of the prior year, though it posted a full-year profit of Rs. 340.96 lakhs. Hydro power generation was nil in the quarter ended 30 June 2026, unchanged from the prior-year quarter.

  • Exceptional items. Exceptional items of Rs. 977.21 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2026 comprised expenditure of Rs. 563.03 lakhs towards mineral-bearing cess relating to earlier years pursuant to a judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India, and a provision of Rs. 414.18 lakhs towards diminution in the value of the Company’s investment in a joint venture.

  • Repeated XBRL discrepancy queries. The Company stated that the same discrepancy regarding PAT figures in XBRL and PDF results had been raised for a third time, referencing NSE discrepancy notices dated 26 June 2026, 21 July 2026, and 22 July 2026. The Company confirmed there was no variation in PAT figures between the XBRL filing and the PDF financial results on both standalone and consolidated bases, and requested the specific XBRL field(s) where the discrepancy was identified, with screenshots if available.

  • Declining cash position. Cash and cash equivalents fell to Rs. 316.04 lakhs (standalone) and Rs. 331.01 lakhs (consolidated) as at 31 March 2026, from Rs. 1,470.53 lakhs and Rs. 1,489.16 lakhs respectively a year earlier. Net cash outflow from financing activities was Rs. 4,959.52 lakhs (standalone) and Rs. 4,888.96 lakhs (consolidated).

  • Lower tax outflows and higher tax paid. Income taxes paid rose to Rs. 3,841.60 lakhs (standalone) from Rs. 1,005.04 lakhs. The Company has opted for the concessional tax regime under Section 115BAA of the Income-tax Act.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Cement Division growth. Cement segment revenue rose to Rs. 1,50,007.57 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2026 from Rs. 1,45,023.11 lakhs, and segment profit before interest and tax more than doubled to Rs. 13,965.44 lakhs from Rs. 5,659.98 lakhs. Cement production for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 rose 9% to 692,914 MT from 634,256 MT, and dispatches rose 8% to 680,547 MT from 628,384 MT.

  • Strong quarterly profitability. Standalone net profit for the quarter ended 31 March 2026 was Rs. 6,619.34 lakhs, compared with Rs. 1,414.19 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 December 2025 and Rs. 913.06 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2025. Standalone profit before tax from continuing operations was Rs. 4,313.89 lakhs for the quarter, up from Rs. 1,302.94 lakhs a year earlier.

  • Full-year earnings improvement. Standalone net profit for the year ended 31 March 2026 was Rs. 12,365.14 lakhs, up from Rs. 3,424.27 lakhs in the prior year. Consolidated net profit was Rs. 12,374.99 lakhs, up from Rs. 3,405.74 lakhs. Standalone profit before tax from continuing operations rose to Rs. 13,726.95 lakhs from Rs. 5,654.46 lakhs.

  • Cement Boards dispatches growth. Cement Boards dispatches for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 rose 18% to 13,123 MT from 11,159 MT in the same quarter of the prior year.

  • Capital expenditure and asset growth. Property, plant and equipment (standalone) rose to Rs. 1,08,831.30 lakhs as at 31 March 2026 from Rs. 91,844.71 lakhs, with capital work-in-progress falling to Rs. 228.61 lakhs from Rs. 14,833.89 lakhs, indicating completion of ongoing projects. Payments for property, plant and equipment were Rs. 10,981.18 lakhs during the year.

  • Reduced borrowings. Non-current borrowings (standalone) declined to Rs. 14,932.87 lakhs from Rs. 14,670.50 lakhs, and current borrowings fell to Rs. 8,723.96 lakhs from Rs. 10,216.57 lakhs. Repayment of non-current borrowings was Rs. 6,985.78 lakhs.

  • Dividend recommendation. The Board recommended a final dividend of Rs. 3.50 per equity share of face value Rs. 10 each for the financial year 2025-26, including the interim dividend of Rs. 1.50 per equity share paid earlier. Dividends paid to shareholders during the year were Rs. 1,583.15 lakhs.

  • Improved operating cash flow. Net cash inflow from operating activities rose to Rs. 14,571.47 lakhs (standalone) from Rs. 9,646.74 lakhs, and to Rs. 14,497.25 lakhs (consolidated) from Rs. 8,620.13 lakhs.

  • Inventory reduction. Inventories fell to Rs. 14,806.79 lakhs from Rs. 24,810.54 lakhs, contributing to a decrease in inventories of Rs. 8,508.00 lakhs in operating cash flow.

  • Subsidiaries. The consolidated results include Tern Distilleries Private Limited and Vishwamber Cements Limited as subsidiaries, with total assets of Rs. 3,296.20 lakhs as at 31 March 2026, total revenues (including other income) of Rs. 71.82 lakhs, and net cash outflows of Rs. 3.67 lakhs for the year.

  • Labour Codes assessment. Based on the Company’s assessment, the impact of the Labour Codes notified by the Government of India on 21 November 2025 is not material. The Company continues to monitor the finalisation of Central and State Rules and other clarifications.

  • AGM resolutions passed. The 45th Annual General Meeting held on 18 September 2026 passed all proposed ordinary resolutions with the requisite majority through remote e-voting and instapoll, including adoption of financial statements, dividend declaration, re-appointment of directors Mr. NGVSG Prasad and Mrs. Pooja Kalidindi, and ratification of cost auditor remuneration.

  • Deposit invitation. The Board of Directors approved the text of the advertisement for the invitation and acceptance of deposits at their meeting held on September 18, 2026.

Key Risks

  • Regulatory and compliance risk. The repeated XBRL discrepancy queries from NSE, raised three times despite the Company’s responses and resubmissions, indicate ongoing regulatory scrutiny of financial reporting. The Company requested specific XBRL field(s) with screenshots to verify the issue.

  • Discontinued operations risk. The Doors Division’s discontinuation resulted in an impairment loss of Rs. 2,575.37 lakhs and continued losses before interest and tax. The assets held for sale (Rs. 3,178.64 lakhs) may not realise their stated values.

  • Segment concentration risk. The Cement Division dominates the business, with segment assets of Rs. 1,15,430.62 lakhs and segment liabilities of Rs. 32,375.69 lakhs, representing the largest share of the Company’s operations. Cement segment revenue was Rs. 1,50,007.57 lakhs out of total segment revenue of Rs. 1,80,172.09 lakhs.

  • Joint venture investment risk. A provision of Rs. 414.18 lakhs was made towards diminution in the value of the Company’s investment in a joint venture.

  • Legal and tax risk. Expenditure of Rs. 563.03 lakhs towards mineral-bearing cess relating to earlier years was recognised pursuant to a judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India.

  • Cash flow risk. Despite improved operating cash flows, the Company’s cash and cash equivalents declined significantly, and net cash outflow from financing activities was substantial.

  • Working capital risk. Trade payables decreased to Rs. 6,185.59 lakhs from Rs. 15,144.41 lakhs, and other financial liabilities (current) stood at Rs. 10,543.86 lakhs. The Company’s current tax liabilities (net) were Rs. 370.50 lakhs.

  • Production volatility risk. Cement Boards production fell 33% and RMC production and sales fell 26% in the quarter ended 30 June 2026, indicating potential operational or demand volatility in these segments.

  • Dependence on monsoon and power availability. Hydro power generation was nil in the quarter ended 30 June 2026, unchanged from the prior-year quarter, indicating the Energy Division’s dependence on seasonal factors.

The XBRL screenshots show standalone net profit from continuing operations of Rs. 6,619.34 lakhs for the quarter and Rs. 12,365.14 lakhs for the year, and consolidated net profit from continuing operations of Rs. 6,608.55 lakhs for the quarter and Rs. 12,374.99 lakhs for the year, matching the PDF figures.

The Company’s actual performance shows improved profitability in the Cement Division, with segment profit before interest and tax rising to Rs. 13,965.44 lakhs for the year from Rs. 5,659.98 lakhs. However, the Boards Division, RMC Division, and Energy Division showed mixed or declining performance. The discontinued Doors Division continued to record losses. The Company’s cash position declined despite improved operating cash flows, and borrowings were reduced through repayments.
