Control Print Limited — Q1 FY2027 Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Soft standalone growth in Q1 FY27. Standalone revenue from operations was ₹1,046.3 mn in Q1 FY27 versus ₹1,004.5 mn in Q1 FY26, a 4.2% YoY increase. Management attributed the slower quarter to the seasonal pattern where Q4 is the strongest quarter, and to a sluggish trend in the pipes and extrusion business, which reduced demand for consumables because “the printing didn’t happen on the pipeline.” Management also cited volatility linked to the Iran situation and polymer price movements, estimating that a few crores worth of consumable sales were deferred, with some customers becoming more conservative amid price uncertainty.

Margin compression. Standalone EBITDA margin fell to 20.15% in Q1 FY27 from 22.06% in Q1 FY26, and PAT (excluding exceptional items) declined 28.3% YoY to ₹123.9 mn. Consolidated EBITDA margin fell to 13.24% from 16.70%, and consolidated reported PAT declined 54.2% YoY to ₹39.2 mn. Management noted that, excluding foreign exchange movements, standalone PBT was broadly stable, and that the Coding & Marking margins “have remained more or less constant.”

V-Shapes execution problems. Management stated the V-Shapes issue is not demand but execution. The machines were described as “finicky,” requiring adjustment with each product change, with pack-opening reliability below 100%, and with most of the more than 70 machines previously sold into the market not running because the system was “too fiddly.” Management said the company is “not through with the machine challenges.” Cost per pack remains a bottleneck, particularly in the price-sensitive food segment. Raw material for co-packaging has risen sharply, pressuring margins on already-committed pack prices.

Assam/UNNATI scheme in limbo. The new Assam facility (46,823 sqm, ~11.57 acres, 60-year lease, consideration ₹861.18 lakhs) was intended to be eligible for benefits under the UNNATI 2024 Scheme. Management stated the government has suspended new units under the scheme, leaving the project “a bit in limbo,” with equipment already ordered for plastic film manufacturing for V-Shapes.

Subsidiary drag. The auditor’s review report for the consolidated results notes that foreign subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries not reviewed by the auditor reflected total revenue of ₹794.03 lakhs and a total net loss after tax of ₹823.76 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. Management acknowledged that “all the losses lie” in the Packaging business.

Track & Trace regulatory uncertainty. The expansion of QR code mandates beyond the top 300 brands to the top 1,000 brands, plus antimicrobials and psychotropic substances, remains a discussion paper. Management cautioned that the industry may lobby against the regulation and that it is not yet enforced.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Core Coding & Marking remains the engine. Coding & Marking contributes about 95% of standalone operating revenue. Management expects the standalone Coding & Marking business to grow 10–12–15% for the year, describing the business as “predictable and steady” with market share retained or slightly increasing. The installed base now exceeds 23,000 printers, with 574 printers sold in Q1 FY27. Management reiterated a target of 60% gross margin and 30% EBIT margin for the standalone Coding & Marking business, excluding investments in other parts of the business.

Price increases and surcharge. Management implemented a price increase and a surcharge since the Iran war began, expecting these to restore “that couple of percent” of margin lost.

Track & Trace momentum. The plain-vanilla Track & Trace business did approximately ₹20 crores in the last financial year and is already profitable or at least breakeven. Management sees the market at approximately ₹600 crores annually, potentially expanding to ₹1,500 crores if the government mandate moves from the top 300 brands (~2,000 SKUs) to the top 1,000 brands (~25,000 SKUs), with a proposed two-phase implementation. Three pharmaceutical customers are running pilots, with outcomes expected to differ across customers. Management expects more visibility by Q3.

International businesses. Markprint is described as doing well, with digital printing solutions localised and selling in India. Codeology’s Print and Apply business has been localised and is available for sale; management noted competitors do ₹25–30 crores of Print and Apply sales each, a market Control Print is currently missing entirely. Control Print MEA FZE is described as likely to break even this year. Management expects revenue growth across Markprint and Codeology.

Packaging co-packaging traction. Management reported good traction in co-packing activities, a pipeline being generated for laminates and co-packaging, strengthened division management, and tighter operational control. The Packaging business as a whole is expected to break even in the first half of the next financial year, not in H2 FY27.

IP transfer to India. On 8 May 2026, the company entered into an IP Assignment Agreement with CP Italy S.R.L. to acquire intellectual property rights including patents for ₹3,120.11 lakhs (≈ EUR 28.60 lakhs), to be completed within 90 days. As of June 30, 2026, the full amount is recorded as Intangible Assets under Development. Management described this as “pretty much going to be the last ever infusion” for V-Shapes, with total investment in V-Shapes to date at around ₹65 crores.

Sectoral strength. Pipes, food, dairy, cable, FMCG, steel and metal, and wood were top-performing verticals. The company continues as market leader in cement, plywood, sugar, and dairy.

3. Key Risks

Execution risk in V-Shapes. The core risk is whether the machine reliability and changeover issues can be resolved so that customers achieve repeatable, low-waste operation across multiple products. Management explicitly tied the investment case to execution: “if the demand was an issue, I would agree that, yes, we need to relook at that entire investment.”

Reputational risk from poor product performance. Management stated it slowed packaging sales deliberately to avoid selling machines that do not work perfectly, citing the risk of damaging long-term reputation and the precedent of V-Shapes’ prior market experience.

Raw material price volatility. Volatility, rather than absolute price level, was described as the bigger problem for customers in extrusion and conversion, affecting their willingness to buy. Sharp increases in co-packaging raw material have negatively affected margins on committed contracts.

Regulatory dependence. The Track & Trace opportunity depends on government mandates that remain in discussion paper stage and could be lobbied against or delayed. Management also noted that even the core coding business could be impacted if government rules and regulations change.

Subsidiary losses and cash burn. The Packaging business continues to generate losses. Management stated no further investments are expected beyond the IP transfer, but severance costs from manpower reduction will still be incurred this year, meaning cost savings will not show results in the current year.

Geopolitical and macroeconomic uncertainty. The Iran situation and related volatility affected consumable demand and customer buying behavior, with some recent escalations noted.

Competitive intensity in Print and Apply. Competitors already do ₹25–30 crores each in Print and Apply sales, a market Control Print has not yet entered at scale.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Standalone growth guidance vs. Q1 actual. Management guided to 10–12–15% growth in the standalone Coding & Marking business for the year. Q1 FY27 standalone revenue grew 4.2% YoY, below that range. Management attributed the gap to seasonality, the pipes/extrusion slowdown, and deferred consumable sales, and maintained that the business should be consistent for the year.

Margin guidance vs. Q1 actual. Management reiterated a target of 60% gross margin and 30% EBIT margin for standalone Coding & Marking excluding other investments. Q1 FY27 standalone gross margin was 57.68% and EBITDA margin was 20.15%. Management stated that excluding forex, standalone PBT was broadly stable YoY and that Coding & Marking margins “have remained more or less constant.”

Packaging breakeven guidance. Management stated the Packaging business as a whole would “probably only break even in the first half of next year, the first half of next financial year,” and not in H2 FY27. This is a revision from prior expectations referenced by an analyst, who noted that a previous one-on-one had indicated V-Shapes “has to pick up” this year.

Track & Trace guidance. Management said it would disclose more in the Q3 presentation depending on outcomes, and that Q2 would provide a fair idea of where pilot projects stand. No specific revenue guidance was given for the innovative Track & Trace solutions.

Codeology/Markprint guidance. An analyst referenced prior guidance of 15–20% growth. Management stated it “did see some revenue growth across Markprint and Codeology” but did not quantify it, and noted some machine orders had delivery timelines extended at the company’s request, with customers being serviced through Packaging lots at company facilities.

Investment discipline. Management stated no further investments are expected in V-Shapes beyond the IP transfer, and that the standalone Coding & Marking business is not being affected by the other businesses. Management declined to provide specific forward sales figures, stating: “I can’t predict anything. We don’t operate on intent, feelings… When I get the payment, and the job is completed and the customer signed off, that’s when Control Print considers a sale and a success.”
