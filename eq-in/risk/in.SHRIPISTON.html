SPR Auto Technologies (SHRIPISTON) — Recent Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Geopolitical and supply-chain disruption. Management described Q1FY27 as operating under an adverse environment marked by heightened geopolitical tensions, supply-chain disruptions, rising raw material costs and macroeconomic uncertainty. The company quantified the impact on EBITDA at Rs. 300 Million for the quarter, attributed to supply-chain and commodity price movements linked to the wider geopolitical environment.

Commodity cost pass-through lag. Commodity cost adjustments with customers operate on a back-to-back arrangement but with a time lag of roughly one quarter. This created a temporary gap in margins during Q1FY27, and management expected normalization in the following quarter as commodity prices began to ease from what it called unprecedented levels.

Export weakness. Exports remained affected, particularly in Europe and the US, with the Middle East (including Egypt) and Turkey also badly hit. Management stated these markets had not yet picked up and described recovery as slow, with continued ambiguity in the supply chain. The export impact was cited as one reason standalone growth lagged headline industry growth.

Margin compression. Consolidated EBITDA margin fell to 18.9% in Q1FY27 from 22.5% in Q1FY26; standalone EBITDA margin fell to 21.0% from 23.5%. Standalone EBITDA was flat year-on-year at Rs. 2,020 Million, and standalone PBT before exceptional items declined 13.5% to Rs. 1,507 Million, with PAT down 13.8% to Rs. 1,119 Million.

Elevated finance costs from the Antolin acquisition. Finance costs rose by Rs. 252 Million on a consolidated basis and Rs. 229 Million on a standalone basis to fund the acquisition of the automotive interiors and lighting businesses. Management characterized this as temporary, expected to normalize as the related debt is repaid. The company had issued Non-Convertible Debentures worth Rs. 10,000 Million in FY26 for the acquisition.

Mix-driven realization effects. Post-GST 2.0 demand shifted toward smaller vehicles, changing the product mix and per-piece realizations. Management noted that production volumes grew 22% while standalone revenue grew about 12%, and that first-quarter aftermarket seasonality also affects the comparison.

Wage cost impact. Annual wage increases effective April 1 affected employee cost percentages in Q1FY27.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong topline growth. Consolidated total income grew 51.2% year-on-year to Rs. 14,992 Million in Q1FY27, aided by consolidation of the Auto Interior Solutions and Lighting businesses. Consolidated EBITDA grew 26.6% to Rs. 2,828 Million, PBT before exceptional items grew 6.7% to Rs. 1,952 Million, and PAT grew 9.4% to Rs. 1,476 Million.

GST 2.0 demand momentum. Management reported phenomenal growth in two-wheeler and passenger car markets post-GST 2.0 reforms and expected volume momentum to continue across these segments as well as commercial vehicle and tractor segments through the year. Industry production volumes grew 22% and domestic sales volumes 21% year-on-year in Q1FY27.

Diversification into powertrain-agnostic businesses. These businesses now contribute over 35% of consolidated total income, with nearly 60% of the overall business positioned to remain relatively insulated from EV penetration. The portfolio spans high-precision injection moulded components, EV motors and controllers, and automotive interior solutions.

Legacy business expansion. The company completed the acquisition of the piston manufacturing plant and machinery from Sunbeam Lightweighting Solutions Limited during the quarter, strengthening piston manufacturing capacity. It continues to win new programs for hybrid and flex-fuel applications, and has products tested up to E85, with capacity available for the required plating.

CAFE norms opportunity. Management stated it has invested ahead of time in coating and piston ring manufacturing technology for the new CAFE norms, with products under testing and validation with customers, and expects some realization increase given higher technology content. Management described itself as leading on this front.

Interiors business turnaround. The acquired interiors business improved from the earlier 7%–8% EBITDA margin range to early-to-mid teens, supported by fixed-cost rationalization, cost-structure actions and synergies. Management indicated further improvement is possible.

EV motors and controllers ramp-up. The business doubled turnover last year, with capacity commissioned in Coimbatore around end-December, and management expects full-year benefit this year. The company makes both motors and controllers, covering traction motors, PMSM and non-PMSM motors, synchronous ferrite motors, for two-wheelers, passenger cars, trucks and buses, with technology partners EMFi International (Singapore) and Lingbo for controllers.

Capacity expansion programs. Phase four expansion at Takahata has begun with a new plant adjacent to the existing facility on five acres; TGPEL has capacity available at its second Noida plant being filled. Takahata’s new business is expected to generate revenues by early next year.

Export recovery potential. Management views export prospects positively over a three-to-five-year horizon, citing end-market recovery once geopolitics improves, competitors vacating capacities (such as Eaton’s engine valve plant closure in the US), and continued penetration into newer segments and customers. Exports grew 2%–3% last year despite the tough environment, though only for part of the year.

Non-auto business. The company supplies medical, sports and music industry components (including complete keyboards for customers such as Yamaha), plus pistons for railways, snowmobiles, compressors, marine and defense applications. Management stated this segment grew 15%–20% over the last four years and is a good-margin business, without disclosing its share.

Sustainability credentials. The company achieved a CDP B rating for climate and water disclosures, TUV-certified sustainability assurance, certifications under ISO 14064, ISO 50001, ISO 46001, ISO 17029 and ISO 27001, an EcoVadis bronze medal (top 35 percentile globally), Dun & Bradstreet’s highest ESG rating of 2 in India, and a CII award for corporate sustainability. It also obtained TISAX certification for cybersecurity.

3. Key Risks

Commodity and supply-chain volatility. Movement in commodity prices and supply-chain disruption from geopolitical tensions remain live risks, with a one-quarter lag before recovery from customers.

Export market uncertainty. Continued weakness in Europe, the US, the Middle East, Turkey and Egypt, with no clear timeline for normalization, poses a risk to the export book.

EV transition risk. The company is addressing this through diversification, but the pace of EV penetration and powertrain mix shifts remain a structural consideration. The investor presentation argues ICE engines will continue to grow given power grid limitations, charging infrastructure gaps, high EV ownership and battery replacement costs, and a limited resale market.

Integration and acquisition risk. The Antolin interiors and lighting acquisitions, the largest to date, carry integration, synergy-realization and margin-convergence risk. Management cited people alignment and cultural integration as key success parameters, alongside operational synergies.

Leverage and finance costs. The Rs. 10,000 Million NCD issuance for the Antolin acquisition raised debt, with consolidated debt-to-equity at 0.62x and standalone at 0.59x as of March 2026. Management stated net debt was around Rs. 550 crores as of June 2026 and that it would not cross 1x net debt to equity, with current levels at 0.2x.

Customer concentration and OEM dependence. The business depends on OEM programs, with growth tied to end-market demand and customer production schedules.

Technology partner agreements. Management stated it operates within the precincts of clear agreements with technology partners when pursuing business in the US and Europe.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Margin recovery. Management stated it would recover commodity cost increases through back-to-back customer arrangements with a one-quarter lag, and expected normalization in the current quarter. Observed: Q1FY27 margins compressed (consolidated EBITDA margin 18.9% vs 22.5% year-on-year), with the Rs. 300 Million EBITDA impact quantified. Management maintained that margins would not be lost.

Interiors business margins. Management guided that the Antolin business had crossed double digits and was in early-to-mid teens, from the earlier 7%–8% range, and hoped to reach mid-teens. Observed: management reported the improvement as achieved in Q1FY27, with further actions lined up.

EV motors and controllers growth. Management stated the business doubled turnover last year and expected to continue growing fast, with full-year benefit from Coimbatore capacity this year. Observed: management reported the business exceeded all targets set at the beginning of the year and performed well in Q1FY27.

Consolidated margin convergence. Management indicated that maintaining high-teens consolidated margins is an excellent possibility, providing room for capacity investment and future M&A. Observed: consolidated EBITDA margin was 18.9% in Q1FY27, within the high-teens range, though below the 21.6% FY26 level.

Fundraise progress. Management stated the fundraise was progressing as per target. Observed: the board approved the QIP on May 11, 2026, shareholders passed the special resolution on July 27, 2026, and the Finance and Investment Committee approved the opening of the issue, floor price of Rs. 4,438.20 per equity share, and preliminary placement document on August 17, 2026, for an aggregate amount of up to Rs. 10,000 Million.

Export recovery. Management expected exports to normalize within the next quarter as commodity prices fell, while also stating recovery would take more time. Observed: exports remained affected in Europe, the US, the Middle East, Turkey and Egypt as of the Q1FY27 call, with no pickup yet.

Capacity expansion timelines. Management guided Takahata’s new business to start generating revenues by early next year, with TGPEL already in process. Observed: phase four expansion at Takahata underway and TGPEL’s second Noida plant capacity being filled.

M&A pipeline. Management stated the team is working on acquisitions and would inform at the right time, with clear internal parameters on net debt to equity. Observed: no specific acquisition was announced in the supplied material; the QIP was the disclosed fundraising action.

Broker Narrative

Both reports are Buy calls and the central thesis persisted: electrification risk to the ICE core is being offset by EV-specific and engine-agnostic diversification, while core IC growth, exports, aftermarket, and margin recovery remain bullish pillars. The narrative became more qualified over time: the first report had broad EV-foray optimism and strong core-IC growth visibility, whereas the last report stresses geopolitical/export-market headwinds, decline in underlying international markets, EV motors/controllers low-margin economics, and Antolin’s ~10% margin requiring an operational reset. It also added concrete milestones—non-ICE revenue at 35% by FY27, EV motors scaling 5x and turning cash/PAT positive, and supply gaps from global capacity vacated on EV fears.

Fears that came true

  • Electrification risk to the legacy ICE business materialized as a persistent overhang: the first Buy predicted +20.1% but actual return was -17.4%, a DISAPPOINTMENT, and the last report still lists the same risk.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected export tailwind from global OEMs seeking competitive IC solutions did not materialise; by the last report export markets faced geopolitical headwinds and declining underlying international demand, even though SPRL retained client business.
  • The expectation of sustainable margin improvements did not broadly materialise; the last report flagged EV motors/controllers as likely low-margin and Antolin’s margin at ~10% requiring a major operational reset/insourcing to reach 15%.
