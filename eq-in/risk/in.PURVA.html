Puravankara Limited — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Approval delays in Karnataka. The launch timeline for several projects slipped. Hennur Road was delayed because of changes in the Karnataka state government, ministries and officers, which slowed approval meetings. Cityspire and Winworth (Kochi) were awaiting only RERA approval at the time of the call. Management characterised these as timing issues rather than structural impediments, and stated that Westend had already received RERA and been launched, while Cityspire had received all approvals.

Land acquisition outflows. Q1 FY27 saw land payments including advances and deposits of ₹574 crore, against ₹69 crore in Q4 FY26 and ₹144 crore in Q1 FY26. This contributed to cash and bank balances falling from ₹1,695 crore at the start of the quarter to ₹1,106 crore, and to net debt rising to ₹2,836 crore from ₹2,321 crore even as gross debt fell by ₹74 crore. Land purchase cost in the consolidated P&L rose sharply year-on-year.

Leverage and cost of debt. Net debt to equity stood at 1.57x as at 30 June 2026, compared with 1.31x at 31 March 2026. Average cost of debt was 11.12%. Management described leverage and borrowing cost as active management priorities.

Legal and tax matters. The standalone and consolidated results disclose ongoing legal proceedings: deposits and advances towards joint development arrangements and land acquisitions subject to title-related proceedings; inventories related to a launched project under land acquisition proceedings by government authorities; receivables and other balances under litigation; and claims from minority shareholders of a subsidiary. A search under Section 132 of the Income Tax Act was conducted in October 2023, and assessment orders have been received disallowing certain expenses with a potential tax impact disclosed in the results. A notice under the Prohibition of Benami Property Transactions Act, 1988 has also been received regarding 43.5 acres of land parcels. No provision has been made in respect of these matters, based on management’s legal evaluation.

Macro vigilance. Management flagged geopolitical tensions, energy prices and weather-related risk as warranting vigilance, while noting India’s domestic demand buffer. The RBI retained a neutral stance, held the repo rate at 5.25%, and projected FY27 GDP growth of 6.7% and CPI inflation of 5.0%.

Share price underperformance. Over 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026, PURVA declined 28.15%, against a Sensex decline of 8.61% and a BSE Realty decline of 15.91%.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Operating momentum in Q1 FY27. Presales were ₹1,439 crore, up 28% year-on-year; sales volume rose 9% to 1.36 msft; average realisation rose 18% to ₹10,589 per sq ft; collections rose 40% to ₹1,199 crore, the highest first-quarter collections in three years; and 745 homes (0.94 msft) were handed over. Total income rose 63% year-on-year to ₹877 crore, EBITDA margin expanded to 25% from 15%, and profit after tax was ₹25 crore against a loss of ₹69 crore in Q1 FY26.

Business development. Four Bengaluru opportunities were added in Q1 FY27 spanning approximately 41.93 acres, with development potential of 4.23 msft and estimated GDV of ₹5,200 crore — joint development agreements at Sarjapur and Doddagubbi, and land acquisitions at Sanna Amanikere (North Bengaluru airport corridor) and Mandur (East Bengaluru). A further joint development agreement was signed for a 7.83-acre parcel in South-East Bengaluru with estimated GDV of ₹1,100 crore and saleable area of approximately 0.89 msft, described as the fifth Bengaluru transaction of FY27, bringing cumulative FY27 business development to around 49.76 acres, approximately ₹6,300 crore of potential GDV and about 5.12 msft of saleable area as of August 2026.

Launch pipeline. 20.48 msft of planned launches across South and West India with approximate GDV of ₹27,300 crore; 15.05 msft of new launches and 5.43 msft of new phase launches. Estimated future cashflow potential from total new launches excluding new phases is approximately ₹8,636 crore. Upcoming launches include Bellandur, Grand Hills, Mallasandra, Kanakapura, Balegere, Purva Skye, Mandur, Nellakunte and Winworth-3 in the South, and Miami, Apna Ghar Unit 3, Deonar Baug, Cuffe Parade and Deccan in Mumbai.

Mumbai redevelopment portfolio. Approximately 5.49 msft of developable area and 3.14 msft of saleable area across seven projects. Miami (Breach Candy) received RERA in June; Pali Hill received 100% vacation with RERA application targeted by end-September and launch between October and November; Apna Ghar 3 and Deonar Baug are targeted for Q4 launches. Management stated it is in advanced discussions with several societies.

Commercial assets. Aerocity received OC for 1.3 msft, with the balance 0.9 msft to be built in phases once 70–80% of Phase 1 is leased. Management indicated expected LOIs in the range of ₹60–65 per sq ft and RFPs issued for approximately 2.5 msft. A new commercial project on recently acquired land in Hebbal, approximately 1.3 msft, is targeted to start construction by end of Q4. Commercial projects are estimated to generate a surplus of ₹2,220 crore.

Capital recycling. A definitive agreement was signed with ICICI Prudential AMC for Purva Zentech at an enterprise value of approximately ₹625 crore, subject to customary adjustments and closing conditions. Approximately ₹250 crore of debt on that asset is to be repaid from proceeds, with the balance to be deployed judiciously. Separately, an arrangement was entered into to sell the shareholding in subsidiary Purva Ruby Properties Private Limited, subject to regulatory approval and other obligations.

Cashflow visibility. Total estimated surplus potential of ₹19,831 crore, comprising ₹8,976 crore from ongoing projects, ₹8,636 crore from pipeline projects and ₹2,220 crore from commercial projects. Balance collections from sold units of ₹6,708 crore cover approximately 72% of balance cost to complete. Completed inventory of 3.20 msft (2,777 units) is pending recognition.

Geographic expansion. Management is evaluating the NCR market, focused on Noida, and expects some traction in land development in coming quarters. It remains open to evaluating new commercial business lines such as data centres and warehousing if good opportunities arise.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution and approval risk: FY27 delivery depends on sequencing of launches, timely receipt of approvals and consistent execution across remaining quarters, as stated by management.
  • Concentration and mix risk: revenue recognition depends on completion, handovers and project mix, and management noted one quarter does not define an earnings trajectory.
  • Balance sheet risk: net debt to equity rose to 1.57x, cost of debt was 11.12%, and cash balances declined by ₹589 crore during the quarter primarily on account of acquisitions.
  • Legal, tax and title risk: ongoing property litigation, income tax assessment orders, and the Benami notice, with no provision made pending resolution.
  • Market risk: management noted FY27 may not see the growth of FY24–25, though it reported no slowdown in South, Mumbai or Pune demand and expects branded players to gain share.
  • Pipeline timing risk: management acknowledged that projects planned for one quarter may slip to the next, with some possibly moving further out.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Presales guidance. FY27 presales guidance of ₹11,200 crore was reiterated. Q1 FY27 presales of ₹1,439 crore represent approximately 13% of that full-year figure. Management stated the guidance is built project-by-project, tracked meticulously by both CEOs, and that launches that did not occur in Q1 moved to Q2. It also noted approximately ₹10,000 crore of sustenance projects running across the country alongside the ₹27,300 crore launch pipeline.

Debt reduction guidance. Guidance to reduce debt by ₹700 crore for the year was reiterated. Observed performance: gross debt reduced by ₹74 crore in Q1 FY27 despite four new project additions. Management described the trade-off between pursuing opportunities and reducing debt as dynamic, while holding the line on the ₹700 crore target.

Margin guidance. EBITDA margin guidance of 25–30% at portfolio level was reiterated. Observed performance: Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 25%, within the guided range and up from 15% in Q1 FY26. Management noted margins vary by product mix across plotted, JDA, outright land and redevelopment projects.

Launch timeline guidance. Management maintained that the launch pipeline is on track, with Westend RERA received and launched, Cityspire approvals received, and Miami RERA received. Hennur Road was acknowledged as delayed by state-level administrative changes, with clarity expected week-on-week. Grand Hills, Balagere, Hennur Road and Winworth 3 were described as on track or ahead of curve. Mumbai launches were guided between Q3 and Q4.

Capital recycling guidance. The ICICI Prudential transaction was expected to complete during the month of the call, subject to customary condition precedents, with approximately ₹250 crore of asset-level debt to be repaid from proceeds and the balance deployed based on optimal business needs.

Commercial leasing guidance. Aerocity Phase 2 construction is contingent on leasing 70–80% of Phase 1, which management expects could occur in the next few quarters. Expected LOI range of ₹60–65 per sq ft was indicated.

Broker Narrative

The broker’s narrative shifted from bullishness on sector-wide launch pickup and pre-sales growth to caution over delayed approvals and a sharp fall in FY25 launches/pre-sales, yet it maintained a Buy stance by emphasizing resilient sustenance sales and planned FY26 launches.

Fears that came true

  • The early concern about muted launches materialized as FY25 saleable inventory launched was only 3.6msf versus the 15msf guidance, leading to a 15% YoY pre-sales decline.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that launches would double in 2HFY24 and drive ~30% pre-sales growth in FY24 failed, as FY25 pre-sales actually fell 15% YoY.
  • The assumption that healthy absorption and falling inventory overhang would support sustained growth was contradicted by the subsequent pre-sales decline.
