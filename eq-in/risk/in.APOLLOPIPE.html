Apollo Pipes — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Sharp PVC resin volatility defined the quarter. Management described the start of FY27 as soft, with geopolitical disruption in global supply chains. PVC resin prices fell by Rs.32 per kg in April, eased slightly in May, then fell another Rs.5 per kg in June. In the first 20 days of April, prices dropped roughly 30%, which management called the worst period for both primary and secondary demand.

Demand deferral and inventory rationalisation. The steep April correction prompted channel partners to defer purchases and rationalise inventories, subduing demand across home plumbing and bath fittings. Distributors remained cautious even after prices stabilised, with management noting that restocking would only begin if stability held for another 10–15 days.

Weak reported profitability. Consolidated Q1 FY27 sales volume was 24,477 MT, down 3% YoY and 22% QoQ. Revenue rose 7% YoY to Rs.3.0 billion, but EBITDA fell 85% YoY to Rs.30 million, with a 1.0% EBITDA margin (down 649 bps YoY). The company reported a net loss of Rs.86 million against a profit of Rs.81 million in Q1 FY26. Consolidated PAT after minority interest was negative Rs.8.6 crore.

Drags from new businesses and Kisan. Consolidated EBITDA was hurt by inventory write-downs, aggressive pricing and fixed expenses for new verticals. The Varanasi plant ramp-up and the direct-to-consumer window profile business added roughly 0.5% of additional cost at Apollo standalone level. Kisan Mouldings reported Q1 FY27 EBITDA of negative Rs.5 crore (negative 7.3% margin) and a PAT loss of Rs.6.7 crore, with volumes flat around 5,500 tonnes per quarter for four quarters.

Government infrastructure demand absent. The O-PVC and HDPE government infrastructure business was described as almost zero and a major drag on overall volumes. New budget allocations and fund disbursement for the Nal Se Jal programme had not yet translated into activity four months into the financial year.

Port and logistics disruption. Heavy rain at Indian ports delayed ship de-stuffing and container availability, though management characterised this as temporary and not a structural supply problem.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Minimum Import Price support. An MIP of US$766 per MT (about Rs.82 per kg on an export basis) was imposed roughly 12 days before the call. Management expects this to put a floor under PVC prices, with the market trading near or 1–2% above Reliance prices, and sees prices as stable to slightly softer in coming weeks but unable to fall below the MIP.

Sequential and second-half recovery expected. Management projects Q2 FY27 to be better than Q1 QoQ and double-digit growth YoY, with H2 FY27 significantly better than H1 as monsoon ends and construction activity picks up. Construction is 60% of sales.

Capacity expansion. The company is on track to expand annual capacity to 288,000 tonnes in the next two years from the current 240,000 tonnes. The Varanasi plant (about Rs.300 crore revenue potential) is targeted for 30% utilisation in FY27, rising to 50–60–70% in FY28 and the balance in FY29. The Kisan Maharashtra (Tarapur) plant is being positioned to serve Maharashtra and neighbouring states.

New products and partnerships. The CPVC portfolio grew YoY in Q1 despite flat consolidated volumes, supported by the Lubrizol tie-up using TempRite technology, with co-branding and co-marketing under way. Window profiles are expected to contribute 7–8% of revenue in FY27, settling around 10% at current capacity and potentially 15% of the portfolio. Water tanks are growing double-digit; bath fittings are flattish; fittings grew single-digit.

Long-term targets. Management reiterated a FY31 goal of four large plants contributing Rs.800–1,000 crore revenue each at 10%+ EBITDA margin from the PVC pipes business, plus a Rs.1,000 crore revenue portfolio from window profiles, water tanks, solvents and bath fittings. The stated ROC math is roughly Rs.1,500 crore gross block plus Rs.300–400 crore working capital, with 10–12% EBITDA margin on Rs.5,000 crore revenue giving about 25% ROC.

New tiles and ceramics entry. The company approved an investment plan of up to Rs.300 crore to set up subsidiary(ies) for the tiles and ceramics business, covering manufacturing, contract manufacturing, trading, marketing and distribution of tiles, ceramics and allied products, to expand into complementary building material categories and position the group as a diversified building materials player.

Funding capacity. The company was described as almost net cash, with Rs.500–600 crore of past capex funded from internal cash flow and promoter/foreign fund raises. FY27 and FY28 capex is estimated at about Rs.200 crore total (roughly Rs.100 crore each year), to be funded from operating cash flow. The next leg of Rs.600–700 crore capex over five years is expected to be 70–80% internally funded.

3. Key Risks

Continued PVC price volatility. Management acknowledged that volatility disrupts the industry and that smaller players with weaker balance sheets suffer, while stronger players absorb inventory write-downs. Distributors’ caution reflects repeated failed calls on price direction.

Margin pressure from competitive intensity. The company has prioritised volume and market share, and analysts questioned whether 7–8% margins can hold given competitive intensity and the push for high double-digit volume growth.

Kisan Mouldings turnaround risk. Kisan has shown operating losses in recent quarters, with flattish volumes around 5,500 tonnes per quarter against plant capacity of 8,000–8,500 tonnes per quarter. Management attributed optical losses to inventory write-downs and said the plant needs macro support.

Working capital and inventory. Inventory stood at 80 days at FY26 end and Q1 FY27, with debtor days stable at 30. Management targets net working capital of 30 days versus 45 currently, with a 10–15 day release expected eventually. Net debt was Rs.59 crore in Q1 FY27 versus net cash of Rs.40 crore in FY26.

Execution and approval dependencies. The preferential issue of up to 31,00,000 fully convertible warrants at Rs.610 each, aggregating up to Rs.189,10,00,000 to non-promoter allottees, and the increase in authorised share capital from Rs.50,00,00,000 to Rs.60,00,00,000, require shareholder approval via remote e-voting from September 1, 2026 to September 30, 2026. The objects of the issue are based on management estimates and commercial and technical factors and may deviate +/- 10% depending on financial, market and sectoral conditions, business performance, strategy, competition and external factors outside the company’s control.

South India plant timing. Land acquisition for the South India plant is targeted for FY27, with construction from Q2 of the following year, contingent on Varanasi and Maharashtra ramp-ups. Management indicated the plan could be fast-tracked if the industry turns around.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Volume guidance. Management stated confidence in high double-digit volume growth for FY27 and coming years, with Q2 FY27 expected to show double-digit YoY growth and H2 better than H1. Observed: Q1 FY27 volume was 24,477 MT, down 3% YoY and 22% QoQ, with FY26 full-year volume of 103,752 MT versus 99,705 MT in FY25 — a modest increase rather than high double-digit growth. Management attributed the gap to the April price crash and weak macro support.

Margin guidance. Management guided to 7–8% EBITDA margin for the next 12–15 months at the business level, with 50–100 bps improvement as plants stabilise. Observed: reported consolidated EBITDA margin was 1.0% in Q1 FY27, with management stating normalised business EBITDA margin was 7% consolidated (8% Apollo standalone, 6% Kisan standalone), attributing the difference mainly to inventory write-downs. FY26 consolidated EBITDA margin was 6.0% versus 8.1% in FY25.

Revenue growth guidance. The earnings presentation targets 25%+ revenue growth CAGR over the next three years and 25%+ revenue growth in FY27 focus areas. Observed: Q1 FY27 revenue rose 7% YoY to Rs.295 crore, while FY26 revenue of Rs.1,105 crore was lower than FY25’s Rs.1,182 crore.

Capacity guidance. Management said it is on track to expand to 288,000 tonnes in two years from 240,000 tonnes, and to fund expansion from internal cash flows without leveraging the balance sheet. Observed: net debt moved from net cash of Rs.40 crore in FY26 to net debt of Rs.59 crore in Q1 FY27, and free cash flow was negative Rs.111 crore in FY26 and negative Rs.136 crore in FY25.

Kisan synergy guidance. Management estimated about 1% cost synergies at the overall company level from the Kisan merger, with many synergies already achieved through single sourcing of raw material and finance/tech management. Observed: Kisan reported negative 7.3% EBITDA margin in Q1 FY27, with management stating business-level EBITDA of 5–6% and attributing the P&L gap to inventory losses.

Preferential issue utilisation. Management expects the entire proceeds from the preferential issue to be utilised in phases according to business requirements and availability, latest by October 2028.

Broker Narrative

Initially, the broker focused on the detrimental impact of PVC price corrections on margins and profitability, while expressing optimism about volume growth, expansion, and eventual raw material stabilization. By the final report, the narrative shifted to a more balanced view, with PVC price volatility and weak government demand as ongoing risks, but with optimism rebounding around market share gains, operating leverage, and specific assets like Kisan Mouldings and the Varanasi plant. The recurring themes of PVC prices and capacity expansion persisted, but the tone evolved from cautious post-correction recovery to confident structural growth.

Fears that came true

  • The massive PVC price correction persisted, causing continued margin erosion and inventory write-downs, as evidenced by repeated DISAPPOINTMENT calls and negative returns in 2023-2024.
  • The cap on EBITDA margins materialized, as operational costs from expansion kept margins within the projected range and limited profit growth.
  • The downward earnings estimate revision proved justified, as actual earnings failed to beat expectations and the stock underperformed.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of a stable raw material environment failed, as PVC resin prices remained volatile and were listed as a key headwind in the last report.
  • The aggressive expansion plan did not immediately translate into robust earnings, instead adding fixed expenses for new business verticals, as highlighted in the last report.
  • The projected revenue CAGR did not materialize, as the last report cites weak government infrastructure demand and aggressive pricing, indicating that revenue growth was offset by price and margin pressures.
