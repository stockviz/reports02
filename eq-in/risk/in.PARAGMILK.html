Parag Milk Foods — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material inflation. Milk prices averaged ₹42 per litre in Q1 FY27, up 13% year-on-year and flat sequentially. Management described sustained inflation in milk procurement prices and elevated input costs across the dairy value chain as the principal drag on the quarter. Gross margin slipped 70 basis points quarter-on-quarter to 27.3% (from 28.0% in Q4), even though milk procurement prices were stable sequentially — measured price increases and a favourable product mix were insufficient to fully offset higher costs elsewhere. Year-on-year, gross margin was held at 27.3% versus 27.4%.

Flagship category volume decline. The flagship categories — ghee, cheese, paneer and dahi — contributed 61% of Q1 revenue but saw volumes decline 2% year-on-year, attributed to a transient slowdown in specific channels. Value growth in these categories was 10%. Management clarified that the decline was concentrated in B2B, while B2C within flagship categories grew at a high single digit; paneer specifically grew in double digits. Management declined to identify the specific channel or customer involved.

Margin pressure and the pricing lag. EBITDA margin was 7.4% versus 7.7% a year earlier, with absolute EBITDA up 6% to ₹70 crore. PAT fell 20% year-on-year to ₹22 crore, primarily due to the current tax impact; PBT was broadly flat at ₹29 crore (up 0.4%). On the earnings call, an investor challenged why operating margins have remained stagnant for several quarters despite a rising share of higher-margin new-age business and premium pricing versus Amul. Management attributed this to the offsetting effect of milk price increases and to inventory carrying a weighted-average cost, which introduces a lag between procurement price moves and their full absorption in the P&L.

Distribution gaps in newer products. An investor observed that Gowardhan Ghee is widely available but higher-margin new-age products such as Go Cheese and Avvatar protein bars and coffee are unavailable or only partially available in the same stores. Management responded that these products are channel- and outlet-specific, with vending machines a major throughput channel for some formats, and that the newly launched RTD protein coffee will take a couple of quarters to reach target distribution. Management reiterated a three-year guideline to be present in more than 1.5 million outlets, working on both length and depth of distribution.

Decline in the “Others” category. The “Others” category fell 46% in the quarter. Management explained this reflects the reclassification of Dahi into the flagship categories, with “Others” now comprising beverages, UHT milk and miscellaneous items. Other income was also very low in the quarter, primarily interest on bank deposits; management noted this line fluctuates due to annual fair valuation of biological assets (typically largest in March) and one-off items such as the prior-year gain on sale of the idle Sonipat plant.

Regulatory context. Maharashtra FDA restrictions on analogue paneer (paneer not made from milk fat) were discussed. Management views this as favourable for the company, since Parag makes paneer 100% from milk fat, and as a positive for the organised, milk-based industry.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

New age business scaling. Pride of Cows and Avvatar grew 59% year-on-year in Q1, generating ₹118 crore and lifting their contribution to 13% of overall revenue from 9% a year earlier. This is in line with the stated roadmap. Avvatar’s portfolio has expanded beyond whey protein powders into protein bars, ready-to-drink products, creatine and other functional nutrition products. The newly launched RTD Avvatar coffee with 15 grams of protein per serving received an encouraging response; the protein wafer bar and RTD protein coffee together contributed 7% of brand revenue in Q1. Avvatar’s distribution mix is 75% quick-commerce, e-commerce and own website, with 25% from traditional retail and specialised protein outlets.

Cheese capacity doubling. The Board approved a brownfield expansion of cheese manufacturing capacity from 60 MT/day to 120 MT/day, involving approximately ₹105 crore, funded from a mix of internal accruals and debt, targeted for completion by FY28 (management indicated around March 2028 on the call). The existing capacity is close to full utilisation. The expansion is intended to drive a parallel expansion in whey protein generation, positioning the company across both cheese and whey. Go Cheese holds approximately 35% market share and the company is #2 in the Indian cheese category. The press release cites IMARC Group projections of the Indian cheese market growing from approximately ₹129 billion in 2025 to around ₹620 billion by 2034.

Structural whey demand. Management characterised the global surge in whey protein demand as structural rather than temporary, citing increased protein consumption trends in the US and Europe, and described a futuristic demand-supply gap in the protein segment. Parag is backward integrated in whey raw material.

Brand building and marketing reach. Avvatar partnered with India’s Got Latent Season 2, hosted by Samay Raina, which management said generated more than 80 million unique reaches across YouTube and Netflix. Website traffic and Google searches rose almost 200% over the prior two months. Gowardhan and Go ran activations including the Bappa Majha media partnership on ABP Majha and TV9 Marathi; Pride of Cows pursued digital experiences, influencer collaborations and occasion-led engagement.

Seasonal and macro support. Management expects milk availability to benefit from the progress of the monsoon, and the festive period to support demand for value-added categories. Longer-term growth prospects cited include population growth and rising incomes, expansion of value-added and premium dairy, growth of organised players, digitalisation and technology-enabled farming, e-commerce and modern retail penetration, and government support.

Sustainability commitments. By 2030, the company plans to source 50% of overall energy needs from clean and sustainable sources, optimise water usage, decrease GHG emissions by less than 20% for the same volume of production, and reduce particulate matter by more than 10%. Existing renewable initiatives include a collaboration with Tata Solar Power, biogas energy from the Bhagyalaxmi farm’s approximately 5,000 cattle, and planned solar panels on cattle sheds; the investment value referenced in the last annual report was ₹4.6 crore.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material price volatility, especially milk, which can impact costs and margins.
  • Compliance, regulatory and reputation risk from potential non-compliance with safety protocols and laws.
  • Competition risk from high industry competition, including in B2B cheese where pricing pressure was acknowledged.
  • Contract risk from failure to meet client expectations.
  • Sustainability risk from growing investor focus on ESG.
  • People risk in talent acquisition and retention.
  • Operational and environmental risks: GHG emissions affecting climate, water scarcity impacting animal welfare and farm profitability, and improper waste management causing environmental pollution.
  • Execution risk on capacity expansion: the cheese expansion requires a simultaneous increase in milk procurement to feed the plant, which management said it is working on and does not expect to be an issue given collection strength.
  • Distribution execution risk in newer, channel-specific products, where availability lags the flagship ghee portfolio.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Growth guidance. Asked whether FY27 would break out of the roughly 10% sales growth band of recent years (₹2,900 crore → ₹3,100 crore → ₹3,400 crore → ₹3,800 crore from March 2023 onward), the COO stated growth would certainly be more than 10%, noting Q1 is normally a weaker quarter. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: revenue of ₹945 crore, up 11% year-on-year, with 3% volume growth — the highest ever Q1 revenue. The Executive Director attributed the mix shift to new-age business rising from under 3–4% in FY23 to around 13% now.

New age business trajectory. An investor referenced the new age business growing from about ₹115 crore to almost ₹365 crore in FY26 and asked whether FY27 could reach ₹620–650 crore given Q1 growth of around 57%. Management did not confirm a specific number. Observed: ₹118 crore in Q1 FY27, up 59%, at 13% of revenue versus 9% a year earlier. Management reiterated that the new age business carries premium, more value-added offerings with margins above the company’s overall average, without disclosing category-level margins.

Margin outlook. Management said it would continue calibrated price increases in response to commodity costs and promotional investments, and would not pass on the full cost push on a weighted-average portfolio basis. It noted price increases were taken toward the end of Q1 in ghee and recently revisited for the new age business. On milk prices, the COO said prices are almost on a stable platform, with a possible slight increase in coming months due to monsoon seasonality reducing milk production and festive demand rising; the Executive Director clarified that a monsoon flush is expected and that prices are stable as of the call, while cautioning that commodity prices can fluctuate. Management did not commit to a specific margin improvement.

Capacity guidance. The Board approved doubling cheese capacity from 60 MT/day to 120 MT/day by FY28, with approximately ₹105 crore investment. On the call, management said the expansion would be completed in the next 1.5 years, possibly by March 2028, with simultaneous milk procurement increases. The existing plant is close to full utilisation.

Distribution guidance. Management maintained a three-year guideline to be present in more than 1.5 million outlets, stating it is working toward that roadmap.

FY26 baseline (from the annual disclosure). Revenue from operations of ₹3,818 crore, up 11% year-on-year; EBITDA of ₹310 crore at an 8.1% margin; PAT before exceptional items of ₹141 crore. Core categories of ghee, cheese and paneer contributed approximately 60% of the business, with core category volumes up 8% and value up 16%. New age businesses grew 91% year-on-year and contributed 10% of turnover. Net debt reduced to ₹484 crore and operating cash flow was ₹149 crore. Management’s stated focus is on profitable and sustainable growth through strengthening the consumer portfolio, enriching product mix, building relevance in health- and nutrition-led offerings, and enhancing operational efficiency, with core categories of ghee, cheese and paneer to be strengthened and accelerated and new age businesses remaining a key growth engine.

Governance and leadership. Mr. Rakesh Kothari was appointed CFO & KMP effective August 7, 2026, having served as President–Finance & Accounts since August 18, 2025, with over 25 years of experience including more than two decades at Citibank. Mr. Pritam Shah was relieved of additional duties as Interim CFO effective close of business on August 6, 2026, and continues as Managing Director and KMP.
