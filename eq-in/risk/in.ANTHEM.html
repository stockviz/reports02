Anthem Biosciences — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Timing shifts in deliveries: Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) was described as a soft quarter, with results reflecting timing shifts in deliveries to key customers. Consolidated revenues from operations were Rs. 418 crores, with CRDMO contributing 81.5% (Rs. 341 crores) and Specialty Ingredients 18.5% (Rs. 78 crores). Management characterized Q1 as the softest quarter of the year.
  • Customer-driven deferments: Deliveries were deferred by customers to Q2, Q3 and Q4. Management explained that customers rebalance stock across geographies when anticipated sales in certain markets do not materialize, and that delayed regulatory approvals in some markets also push back supplies. These deferments affect quarterly performance even though confirmed orders are eventually honoured.
  • Lower capacity utilization in some units: Unit 2 custom synthesis utilization was about 50% for the quarter across expanded capacity (after adding ~130 kiloliters), and fermentation utilization was about 50% across 140 kiloliters. Unit 1 custom synthesis was roughly 78% utilized. Unit 3 (Neo Anthem) utilization was about 30–35% in Q1 FY27, up from about 15% in FY26.
  • Specialty Ingredients pressure: The segment faced raw material turmoil and supply chain pressures, with the business largely focused on the domestic market.
  • GLP-1/Semaglutide not yet commercial: Commercial supply of Semaglutide API has not started; the company is awaiting CDSCO approval despite having completed work, trials and scale-up activities. Management noted GLP-1 sales have not yet kicked in.
  • Customer M&A uncertainty: One large biotech customer was acquired by a Big Pharma about a month prior. Management noted no meaningful discussions yet with the acquirer and no material impact expected for the rest of the year, with typical post-acquisition continuity for the acquired team.
  • Lumpiness inherent to the business: Management repeatedly cautioned that CRDMO revenues depend on customer delivery schedules, producing both soft quarters and bumper quarters, and advised evaluating performance on a YoY basis rather than QoQ.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong order book visibility: Management stated 60% of the full-year requirement was already in the order book at the start of the year, and after Q1 the order book was replenished to still stand at about 60% for the whole business. This underpins confidence in full-year growth.
  • Margin improvement: EBITDA was Rs. 176 crores (including other income of Rs. 25 crores) with a 39.6% margin; PBT was Rs. 145 crores; PAT was Rs. 120 crores at a 27.1% margin. Management noted YoY improvement in both EBITDA and PAT margins, supported by cost efficiencies, yield optimization, backward integration of key intermediates, and employee productivity. Material margins increased even on the softer top line.
  • New customer additions: One new Big Pharma customer was added, expected to yield significant numbers in later quarters of the year, spanning R&D, new projects and supply-chain diversification. Another customer onboarded last quarter is sending advanced starting-material and intermediate inquiries. A large biotech customer’s acquisition by a Big Pharma provides a potential “foot in the door” with the acquirer.
  • Unit 3 ramp-up: Neo Anthem (Unit 3) utilization rose to 30–35% in Q1 FY27 from ~15% in FY26, driven mostly by new projects (including peptide synthesis and oncology manufacturing and a modern pilot plant), not just shifting production from existing units.
  • Unit 4 expansion: Construction is underway on a ~30-acre property; Phase 1 adds ~365 kiloliters of custom synthesis, ~100 kiloliters of fermentation, and a food and nutra plant. Capex outlay is ~Rs. 1,200 crores, split roughly 50-50 across FY27 and FY28, with commissioning expected by end of FY28. FY27 targeted capex is ~Rs. 700 crores. Civil work is nearly complete and equipment orders are beginning.
  • Pipeline: Over 100 early-stage programs and 10 late-phase molecules; path to commercialization for late-phase assets is about 18–24 months. Customers commercialized 4 new molecules last year. A couple of late-phase molecules are in ADC modalities; peptide molecules are mostly early-stage.
  • GLP-1 opportunity: Sampled with almost all big domestic players who await CDSCO approval; management expects approval within a quarter or two and views GLP-1 as a long-term robust opportunity. Regulatory work has begun, with no other markets targeted at present.
  • Technology differentiation: Use of flow chemistry, bio-catalysis and innovation-led engagement is cited as supporting cost of goods for customers while defending margins.
  • Tax normalization: The tax rate is expected to normalize to about 25–25.5% for the full year, helped by Neo Anthem potentially turning break-even or profitable, versus last year’s elevated rate due to Unit 3 subsidiary losses.
  • Declining ESOP cost: ESOP cost is about Rs. 9 crores for FY27 (Q1: ~Rs. 2.25 crores), down from ~Rs. 16 crores in FY26, with a further decline to ~Rs. 5 crores expected next year.
  • AI optionality: Management sees potential AI use cases in QA document review, target discovery (which could increase demand for lab work), and manufacturing optimization, while noting much of the current AI discussion remains hype.

3. Key Risks

  • Quarterly revenue lumpiness: Dependence on customer delivery schedules creates volatility; management warned against benchmarking bumper quarters and soft quarters alike.
  • Delivery deferments: Customer rebalancing, stocking adjustments and delayed market approvals can shift revenues between quarters, as occurred in Q1 FY27.
  • Regulatory dependencies: Semaglutide supply is gated on CDSCO approval; timing is not fully within the company’s control. Regulatory compliance track record is cited as a key customer selection factor.
  • Customer concentration and M&A: Acquisitions of biotech customers by Big Pharma introduce uncertainty over project continuity and future volumes; outcomes depend on clinical data, commercialization success and the acquirer’s marketing.
  • Capacity ramp-up execution: Unit 4 construction and the planned ramp-up of Units 1–3 to optimum utilization over the next two years carry execution risk; new units can depress PAT initially due to large investments.
  • Margin sustainability: Management expressed confidence in defending margins this year given the order book, but acknowledged it is hard to predict the future; maintaining industry-high margins over the longer term depends on continued innovation and technology deployment.
  • Specialty Ingredients supply chain: Raw material turmoil and domestic-market focus create pressure on that segment.
  • Commoditization pressure: Management noted the timeframe from specialty to fine chemicals to commoditization is shrinking, requiring continuous innovation and addition of new modalities to preserve moats.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • Full-year growth: Management stated growth prospects for FY27 remain intact and in line with historical double-digit performance, without giving a specific percentage guidance. This follows a Q1 where revenues were soft and CRDMO was down about 25% YoY (as noted by an analyst). Management expects recovery in Q2, Q3 and Q4, with Q1 as the softest quarter.
  • Order book: Guided visibility of 60% of the full year’s order book at the start of the year; after Q1, management stated the order book was replenished and still stands at about 60% for the whole business (the order book concept applies largely to CRDMO; Specialty Ingredients is a month-on-month B2B business).
  • Margins: Management guided to sustaining margins through the year, supported by the order book, known costs and margins, and cost/yield initiatives. Observed Q1 performance showed YoY improvement in EBITDA and PAT margins, with material margins increasing despite the softer top line. Employee cost was 18.8% of sales in Q1 (elevated due to muted revenue) but is guided to ~13% of sales for the full year; other expenses were 8.2% in Q1, guided to 9–10% for the full year.
  • Capacity utilization: Guided that utilization across Units 1–3 will rise, with all units expected to be completely full and Unit 4 commissioned by end of next year (FY28). Observed Q1: Unit 1 ~78%, Unit 2 custom synthesis ~50%, Unit 2 fermentation ~50%, Unit 3 ~30–35%. Management expects higher utilization in subsequent quarters as deferred deliveries land.
  • Unit 4 capex and timeline: Guided ~Rs. 1,200 crores Phase 1 capex, ~50-50 split across FY27 and FY28, commissioning by end of FY28; FY27 capex ~Rs. 700 crores. Observed: civil work nearly done, equipment orders commencing.
  • Tax rate: Guided normalization to ~25–25.5% for the full year, contingent on Neo Anthem turning break-even or profitable; Q1 had a low tax rate.
  • ESOP cost: Guided ~Rs. 9 crores for FY27 and ~Rs. 5 crores next year, declining from ~Rs. 16 crores in FY26; Q1 observed at ~Rs. 2.25 crores.
  • GLP-1: Guided CDSCO approval expected within a quarter or two, with supply to follow; observed status is that approval is still awaited and no commercial supply has begun.
  • Working capital: Guided no significant changes; observed slight inventory build-up for Q2/Q3 raw materials and some payable increase, with receivables lower after a large Q4, described as normal course of business.
  • Recently commercialized molecules: Guided growth versus March ’26 levels, but sizable supplies will take a couple of years as innovators penetrate markets.

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from a broad capability-driven Buy thesis centered on CRDMO scale, CRO resilience, specialty ingredients, and capacity expansion to a defense of the stock after a Q1FY27 miss, emphasizing order-book visibility, deferred-delivery recovery, and a new customer. Persistent themes were pipeline growth, commercial molecule ramp-ups, capacity, and specialty-ingredient/GLP-1 upside; what changed was greater weight on near-term execution, utilization, and supply-chain disruptions rather than theoretical risks.

Fears that came true

  • Demand volatility and customer deferment risk materialized: Q1FY27 revenue fell ~23% YoY, CRDMO revenue fell 25%, and Unit II utilization dropped to ~50% due to shipment deferments.
  • The flagged margin-pressure risk from underutilization/deferments materialized: Q1 EBITDA declined 21% YoY and operating margin fell sequentially.

Optimism that failed

  • The CRO resilience thesis was undermined in Q1: the high-FFS CRDMO business still saw a 25% YoY revenue decline.
  • The fermentation/specialty-ingredients growth optimism did not hold near-term: Specialty Ingredients revenue fell 12% YoY and semaglutide API commercialization still awaited CDSCO approval.
