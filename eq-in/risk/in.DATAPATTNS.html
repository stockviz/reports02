Data Patterns (India) Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Revenue lumpiness and customer approval delays. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was Rs. 116.03 crore, up 17% year-on-year from Rs. 99.33 crore, but management described the quarter’s revenue as “modest” due to temporary delays in customer approvals. The CMD stated that products were ready but inspection teams did not arrive because customer delivery requirements had themselves slipped, and that such aberrations will recur while the order book-to-revenue model remains tight. Management declined to quantify the revenue slipped from recognition, citing customer sensitivity.

Margin compression in the quarter. EBITDA was Rs. 31.4 crore at a 27.0% margin, versus Rs. 32.1 crore and 32.3% in Q1 FY26. Gross margin was 78.9% (versus 79.8% a year earlier). Management attributed the lower EBITDA margin to elevated employee costs from capability expansion, product-mix changes, and uneven quarterly revenue distribution against a largely fixed cost base. Other expenses rose 63.5% year-on-year, driven by repairs and maintenance during facility revamping and a provision of close to Rs. 2 crore against long-standing receivables.

Order conversion delays. The order book including orders negotiated stood at Rs. 2,654 crore as on July 30, 2026, against an order book of Rs. 928 crore as on June 30, 2026. Management acknowledged that negotiated orders have taken roughly six months longer than expected to convert into contracts, and that the underlying program has been stretched by more than 1.5 years. An additional two-month extension was taken from the company. The CMD noted that the order book normally should not take this long to convert.

Dependence on customer timelines. The CMD stated that because the business is yearly rather than quarter-to-quarter, and projects are long-term, quarter-to-quarter deviations will persist until the order book reaches at least three years of revenue projections.

Program-specific uncertainties. On the HAWK radar, hardware has been developed for MiG and Su-30 platforms but software is required; discussions are ongoing with partners and Russian OEMs, with no confirmed timeline. On the Su-30 jammer pod, qualification exercises are underway with first trials expected before December, and commercial production only after flight trials. On BrahMos seekers, products are ready but qualification and approvals are pending.

Space segment deferral. Management stated that a decision on space/satellite investment will take another three to four months, pending clarity on government funding, and noted reluctance to follow a model where start-ups develop space systems without a clear revenue model.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order inflow momentum. Orders received since the start of FY27 exceeded Rs. 226 crore as of the press release date. Subsequent to the July 30, 2026 order book announcement of Rs. 2,654 crore, the company received orders amounting to Rs. 771.08 crore, of which Rs. 745.93 crore were already considered under “orders negotiated and yet to be received” and Rs. 25.15 crore were new orders. This includes an order worth about Rs. 585.76 crore from Bharat Electronics Limited for procurement of Radar Electronics, disclosed on August 20, 2026.

Order inflow guidance. Management stated confidence in securing around Rs. 2,000 crore of fresh order inflows during FY27, over and above orders already received and negotiated. The investor presentation cites a strong order book pipeline of Rs. 20–40 billion over the next 24 months.

Policy environment. Continued government focus on higher indigenous content and defence procurement is described as creating significant opportunities across armed forces, defence PSUs, and private sector companies. Import bans and Make in India categories for military radars, radio relays, and other sensors are cited as supportive.

Transition from development to full systems. Several products have moved from development to complete system solutions, expanding the addressable market and enabling participation in larger, higher-value programs. Management aims to convert differentiated capabilities into single-vendor opportunities and to leverage reusable building blocks for faster, more cost-effective product development.

Counter-drone business. Initial orders have been received. The company is pursuing active detection (radars), passive detection (ELINT/COMINT), and countermeasure jamming. Management expects contracts to fructify in the next three to six months and believes several-hundred-crore contracts could occur during the year, though these are not included in the Rs. 2,000 crore inflow projection.

Exports. International order book stood at Rs. 39 crore. The company has supplied signal processors for airborne radars to a UK company and received first orders for an antenna redesign for a fighter aircraft system, with delivery expected in six to eight months. Management expects a multi-million-dollar export business in the coming months and substantial scaling over two to three years, including civil aviation obsolescence management and upgrades with large US OEMs.

Acquisition of ST Advanced Composites (STAC). The Board approved acquisition of 100% stake in Chennai-based STAC for Rs. 10 crore, providing in-house composite manufacturing capability for radomes and composite parts used in radar and defence electronics programs. The investor presentation cites potential benefits including participation in larger integrated radar and defence system contracts, gradual gross margin improvement, better manufacturing control, lower vendor dependence, and improved quality and delivery control. A Share Purchase Agreement was completed on August 25, 2026, with consideration of about Rs. 10 crore (Rs. 1.50 crore to promoters for equity shares and Rs. 8.50 crore as a loan to the target to settle liabilities), subject to customary conditions precedent.

Large addressable market. Management cited an addressable market of about Rs. 40,000 crore based on products already developed and repeat contracts, with additional opportunities in air defence radars, seekers, and next-generation programs. The CMD referenced potential business of several thousand crores in air defence radars over 1.5 to 2 years, and a larger addressable market of Rs. 40,000–50,000 crore including new markets.

Capex and infrastructure. Around Rs. 185 crore was invested in capex over the last five years, with plans for an additional Rs. 150 crore over the next two years (management indicated Rs. 150–200 crore minimum, possibly more), covering buildings, clean rooms, integration facilities, production lines, test equipment, and AI-related IT infrastructure.

Balance sheet strength. The company remains net debt free with cash, bank balances, and investments of Rs. 530 crore as on June 30, 2026 (Rs. 4,659 million per the presentation). FY26 ROE was 16.7% and ROCE 20.8%.

3. Key Risks

  • Customer approval and inspection timing risk, which management states will continue to cause quarter-to-quarter revenue deviations while the order book-to-revenue model remains tight.
  • Order conversion risk, evidenced by negotiated orders taking roughly six months longer than expected and a program stretched by more than 1.5 years.
  • Concentration and program dependency risk, with large contracts from single clients such as HAL and BrahMos, where execution timelines vary from a few months to a few years depending on customer delivery schedules.
  • Qualification and trial risk on development programs including the Su-30 jammer pod, HAWK radar software integration, and BrahMos seekers, where commercial orders depend on successful qualification and flight trials.
  • Margin risk from elevated employee costs expected to remain high over coming quarters, product-mix changes, and a largely fixed cost base against uneven revenue.
  • Receivables risk, reflected in the Rs. 2 crore provision against long-standing receivables in Q1 FY27.
  • Competitive risk in programs such as the HAWK radar, where the CMD noted parallel development by another party and that outcomes depend on which product qualifies and wins the contract.
  • Export market execution risk, with the export business described as being in early stages and dependent on building a marketing organization and converting interest into contracts.
  • Space segment uncertainty, with investment decisions pending government funding clarity.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Revenue growth guidance. Management reiterated full-year FY27 guidance of 20–25% revenue growth and EBITDA margins of 35–40%. The investor presentation states the same 20–25% revenue growth and 35–40% EBITDA margin expectation for FY27, and 20–25% revenue growth over the next two to three years. Observed Q1 FY27 revenue growth was 16.8% year-on-year (Rs. 116.03 crore versus Rs. 99.33 crore), below the guided range, which management attributed to temporary customer approval delays expected to normalize in coming quarters.

EBITDA margin guidance. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 27.0%, below the guided 35–40% range. Management stated the quarter’s margins were impacted by high employee costs, product-mix changes, and uneven quarterly revenue, and expressed confidence in achieving full-year targeted margins, noting that gross margin remained healthy at 78.9% and that margins should be viewed annually rather than quarterly.

Order inflow guidance. Management guided around Rs. 2,000 crore of fresh order inflows in FY27 over and above orders already received and negotiated. Observed inflows: over Rs. 226 crore received as of the July 30, 2026 press release, and a further Rs. 771.08 crore received subsequent to the July 30, 2026 order book announcement (including the Rs. 585.76 crore BEL order), of which Rs. 745.93 crore were previously classified as negotiated and Rs. 25.15 crore were new.

Order book guidance. The order book including orders negotiated was stated at Rs. 2,654 crore as on July 30, 2026, providing what management described as healthy revenue visibility over coming years. The order book as on June 30, 2026 was Rs. 928 crore, versus Rs. 730 crore as on June 30, 2025.

Order conversion guidance. Management stated that negotiated orders should convert in the next few months, with an additional two-month extension taken, and that some contracts could convert within one to two weeks. This follows the earlier delay of roughly six months beyond expectations.

Capex guidance. Management guided Rs. 150–200 crore minimum capex over the next two years, with the CMD indicating potentially more than Rs. 200 crore, alongside continued product development spending and AI-related IT investments.

Segment guidance. Management stated that the counter-drone business could see several-hundred-crore contracts during the year, not included in the Rs. 2,000 crore inflow projection, and that exports could become a multi-million-dollar business in the coming months.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from a cautious Hold focused on rich valuations and margin compression in 2023 to a conviction Buy by 2026, built around a much larger order backlog, system-solution-provider ambitions, exports, and drones. Persistent concerns about government-contract dependence, working capital, and capex remained, but were increasingly outweighed by order visibility and an expanded addressable market.

Fears that came true

  • Margin pressure flagged in Q4FY23 recurred in Q1FY27, when margins declined on execution mix, higher investments, product mix and timing of revenue recognition, and the final Buy’s actual return of +10.7% trailed its predicted +15.2%.
  • Dependence on government contracts remained a live risk, as Q1FY27 execution was hit by temporary delays in customer approvals.
  • The high working capital/cash-conversion concern persisted, with the last report still citing high working capital requirements and ₹200+ crore planned capex over the next two years.

Optimism that failed

  • The early expectation of ~25% revenue growth and ~40% EBITDA margin did not fully materialize, as FY27 guidance was trimmed to 20-25% revenue growth and 35-40% EBITDA margin, with margins already under pressure in Q1FY27.
