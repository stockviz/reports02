Rushil Decor (RUSHIL) — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost inflation. Elevated chemical and other raw material prices weighed on profitability in Q1 FY27. Resin prices rose substantially — described on the call as up close to 35–40% — while wood prices remained broadly flat. Management noted resin manufacturers are concentrated more in the north than the south, adding to sourcing costs for the company’s southern MDF operations.

Freight and shipping disruption. Freight costs were significantly higher, and shipping routes continued to face disruption from the ongoing conflict in West Asia. Container shortages meant MDF export orders in hand could not be shipped at acceptable rates; MDF exports fell to just 255 CBM in Q1 FY27 versus 7,150 CBM a year earlier. Management indicated exports remained subdued into July, with roughly 3,200 CBM shipped that month.

Planned maintenance shutdowns. A planned annual maintenance shutdown was taken in April across plants, including a boiler consolidation at the laminate plant where the Jumbo capacity was implemented. This contributed to lower production and MDF capacity utilisation of 66% in Q1 FY27, down from 83% in Q4 FY26.

Margin pressure. Consolidated EBITDA margin was 7.9% in Q1 FY27 versus 11.4% in Q4 FY26. Laminate segment margin fell to 7.0% from 10.2% a year earlier; excluding Jumbo, the older laminate business margin was around 5%, which management attributed to the Gulf-region business mix, chemical cost inflation, supply commitments, and the plant shutdown. MDF margin was 8.4%, and PVC margin 7.9%.

Competitive intensity in MDF. Management acknowledged significant new capacity — organized and unorganized — entering the domestic MDF market, with good competition expected ahead. A 15% price hike taken earlier was described as sustaining, but management stated the market would not accept further hikes, and acknowledged the possibility of inventory losses if raw material prices stay elevated amid oversupply.

Demand caution. Domestic buyers were reluctant to build stock amid fluctuating chemical prices, buying only to requirement, which kept MDF volumes flat year-on-year (0.7% growth).

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Normalised MDF operations. The Andhra Pradesh MDF facility operated normally post-shutdown, driving India MDF revenue up 32.9% YoY to INR 1,447 million, with India volume up 16.1% to 52,162 CBM and India realisation up 14.4% to INR 27,736 per CBM.

Laminate momentum. Laminate revenue rose 65.3% YoY to INR 736 million, with India revenue up 60.6% and exports up 67.9%. Total volume rose 27.9% to about 8.8 lakh sheets and blended realisation improved 29.3% to INR 836 per sheet on better product mix. New Far East customers contributed, alongside 15–20% domestic growth.

Jumbo Laminates scaling. Jumbo revenue was INR 110 million with volume of about 1.13 lakh sheets and an EBITDA margin of 20.6% — in line with management expectations. Capacity utilisation was 29%, with an aspiration of 55–60% this year (implying roughly INR 75 crore revenue) and 70–75% next year; at ~90% utilisation, potential revenue from the press was indicated at around INR 140 crore across both phases, on a capex of about INR 90 crore.

Value-added MDF focus. Management targets value-added products at 60% of MDF revenue and 50% of MDF volume in FY2027; Q1 FY27 value addition was 45% by volume and 54% by revenue.

Distribution and market expansion. The company added 15 direct distributors and 46 retailers/dealers domestically and entered two new export markets, Honduras and Greece.

Debt reduction and cash flow. Debt including working capital stood at around INR 260 crore, with scheduled annual repayment of about INR 55 crore; INR 18 crore was repaid in Q1 FY27. Management indicated the company could be debt-free by Q2 FY29, with no major capex planned beyond INR 5–10 crore of maintenance capex.

Industry growth. MDF industry growth was cited at a 20% CAGR, sufficient to absorb roughly 800 CBM per day of additional capacity.

3. Key Risks

  • Sustained elevation in chemical/resin and raw material prices, with limited ability to pass through further price increases.
  • Continued freight cost inflation and West Asia conflict-driven shipping disruption constraining MDF exports despite an order book in hand.
  • Domestic MDF oversupply and intensifying competition, with potential inventory losses if raw material prices remain high.
  • Execution risk in ramping Jumbo Laminates utilisation and cracking multi-year international contracts, which management described as slow to open and requiring certifications and documentation.
  • Volatility in realisations and margins given product-mix dependence, particularly in Jumbo where management declined to give an average realisation.
  • Export concentration exposure to the Gulf region, which affected older laminate margins in Q1 FY27.
  • Governance-related: in any financial year with no or inadequate profits, the Managing Director is to be paid minimum remuneration within specified statutory ceiling limits.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

EBITDA margin (10–12% consolidated). Guidance given in the prior quarter was reiterated as an aspiration, with management stating it may take one or two more quarters to reach that level. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated EBITDA margin was 7.9%, down from 11.4% in Q4 FY26 — so the target remains unmet.

Jumbo Laminates utilisation. Aspiration of 55–60% utilisation this year and 70–75% next year, versus observed 29% in Q1 FY27. Management expects to reach the ~INR 140 crore potential revenue “very soon down the line 2 years.”

MDF utilisation. Management stated full-year utilisation “will go up” from last year’s ~75%, versus observed 66% in Q1 FY27 (impacted by the April shutdown).

MDF export run rate. The stated strategy is to export 4,000–5,000 CBM per month; observed Q1 FY27 exports were 255 CBM, with July at about 3,200 CBM — below the stated run rate.

Value-added MDF mix. Target of 60% of revenue and 50% of volume in FY2027; observed Q1 FY27 at 54% of revenue and 45% of volume.

Debt reduction. Scheduled repayment of ~INR 55 crore annually with debt-free status indicated by Q2 FY29; observed INR 18 crore reduction in Q1 FY27.

Overall FY27 revenue guidance. Management declined to give specific revenue guidance, stating only that it remains on the guidance given earlier and will “prove our best.” On long-term potential, the CFO declined to confirm a ~INR 1,100 crore steady-state revenue scenario, noting it depends on capacity utilisation and value-added proportion, and that it was “too early to give any guidance.”

Laminate margins. Management indicated normal older-laminate margins of 9–10%, with combined laminate segment margins expected above 10% including Jumbo, subject to chemical pricing and war-related uncertainty. Observed Q1 FY27 laminate margin was 7.0% (6.5% adjusted for forex), with older laminate at ~5%.

Corporate actions. The Board approved re-appointment of Mr. Rushil K. Thakkar as Managing Director for three years from 13 August 2026, and proposed M/s. Parikh & Majmudar as statutory auditors for five years from the conclusion of the 32nd AGM, following M/s. Pankaj R Shah & Associates’ decision not to seek re-appointment. The 32nd AGM is scheduled for Monday, 28 September 2026 at 10:30 A.M. via video conferencing/OAVM, with a record date of 8 September 2026 for a proposed final dividend of INR 0.05 per equity share.

Broker Narrative

From Dec-23 to Sep-25, the broker stayed Not Rated and continued to flag raw-material costs, margin pressure and debt/repayment obligations as risks. The narrative shifted from company-specific import disruption and Vizag ramp-up concerns to industry-wide MDF oversupply, a fire incident and competitor capacity additions, while optimism moved from equity-infusion-led deleveraging toward price hikes, export-led VAP shift and BIS-related regulatory support.

Fears that came true

  • The import/oversupply risk materialised: the final report notes MDF capacity of 4.5mn CBM versus demand/imports of 2.8-2.85mn CBM and ~58% industry underutilisation, consistent with the -52.8%/-45.3% actual returns.
  • Raw-material inflation flagged in the first report persisted, with the final report citing resin costs up ~3% QoQ and higher kraft paper prices.
  • MDF realisation and margin pressure did not ease: the first report flagged H1FY24 margin pressure and the final report still expects lower FY26 margins.
  • The elevated-debt concern carried through: the first report cited ~3x net debt/EBITDA, and the final report still notes Rs 2.5-2.6bn outstanding debt with ~Rs 550mn annual repayments.

Optimism that failed

  • The targeted Vizag MDF ramp-up to 90-95% utilisation did not translate into an earnings recovery; the final report still highlights ~58% MDF underutilisation and margin pressure.
  • The expected margin recovery from higher VAP share and operating leverage did not materialise on time, as the final report guides FY26 EBITDAM of only ~11% with lower near-term margins.
  • The jumbo laminate plant’s targeted INR 1.8bn revenue and 12-15% EBITDA margin remained only ‘upcoming’ as of the 2025 report, with no evidence of contribution yet.
