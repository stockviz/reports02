ZEEL — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Advertising weakness. Advertisement revenue declined 11% year-on-year in Q1 FY27. Management attributed this largely to the Middle East conflict, which severely impacted April and May as advertisers remained cautious. The subdued macroeconomic sentiment from the previous fiscal carried into the first quarter, and continued volatility in West Asia created inflationary pressures that affected advertiser spending patterns. Advertising was described as remaining a critical priority, with the team investing time and energy to gain traction.

Operating deleverage and margin pressure. EBITDA for the quarter stood at INR 789 million, with EBITDA margins at 4.1%, impacted by operating deleverage from the Middle East crisis affecting advertising revenue. Overall operating costs rose 15%, driven by higher advertising and publicity spend across platforms due to FIFA 2026, the launch of sports channels, an expanded content offering, and investments in new strategic initiatives including Bullet.

Sports monetisation timing. The FIFA 2026 rights were secured in June 2026, leaving only about 10 days before the tournament went on air. This left hardly any time for advertising monetisation, even though subscription monetisation was strong. Content was behind a paywall from day one, which supported revenue growth but the margin performance was weaker.

Regulatory and legal overhang. The company has received various show cause notices from SEBI alleging irregularities in relation to certain transactions, in connection with ongoing investigations. During the quarter, a settlement application filed with respect to an alleged lien created over a property matter was rejected; subsequent to quarter-end, the company received a SEBI order dated 31 July 2026, against which it has filed an appeal before the Securities Appellate Tribunal, Mumbai, along with an application seeking a stay and other urgent interim reliefs. Responses to the remaining SCNs are being submitted based on legal advice, denying all allegations, with corresponding settlement applications under consideration by SEBI. An investigation under Section 206(5) of the Act into certain vendor transactions is also ongoing; the Investigation Committee concluded its investigation and reported no material irregularities, and the Board and management determined the transactions were against consideration for valid goods and services received.

Fund-raising timeline uncertainty. Shareholders approved the preferential issue of fully convertible warrants to a promoter group entity at the EGM held 31 July 2026. Management noted the usual approval validity is one year, and that it has written to the regulator seeking clarification on the 18-month versus 12-month timeline issue, has approached the Securities Appellate Tribunal and filed an appeal, and is hopeful of resolution sooner rather than later. The matter was described as sub judice.

JioStar arbitration. JioStar India Private Limited has claimed damages of USD 1,097 million, plus costs, expenses and applicable interest, in an ongoing arbitration before the London Court of International Arbitration relating to the Alliance Agreement for broadcasting rights of ICC men’s global events. ZEEL filed a Statement of Defence and a counterclaim of USD 8.06 million with interest. Subsequent to the quarter-end, the final evidentiary hearing before the Arbitral Tribunal was completed, and both parties are now required to file post-hearing briefs. Management, based on legal opinion and internal assessment, has determined the company is not in default and believes JioStar’s claims are unfounded and legally not tenable. Management indicated a possible outcome in Q3, while noting the difficulty of committing on legal matters.

Land lease matter. A subsidiary, Zee Studios Limited, has a matter relating to cancellation of a land lease at Jaipur by RIICO; following rejection of its review/dismissal of appeal and further show cause notice, the company has taken steps for appropriate relief and believes it has a strong legal position, with no impairment considered necessary.

BARC ratings publication. The quarter’s viewership share numbers are only until week 24, as BARC was not allowed to publish ratings subsequently.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Digital growth. ZEE5 continued to display profitable growth for the third consecutive quarter, with revenue up 58% year-on-year to INR 4,571 million. The digital business delivered EBITDA of INR 44 million despite increased marketing spend behind Bullet and FIFA. During the quarter, 38 shows and movies were released, including 15 original shows, across 7 languages. Management stated the underlying unit economics are intact and expects digital growth momentum to continue in Q2 as further gains accrue from FIFA. B2C additions in the quarter more than doubled the subscriber base, with a portion attributable to non-sports consumers. Language packs on ZEE5 were cited as having aided growth. International territories were identified as an under-utilised lever for subscription and digital growth.

Linear viewership strength. The company achieved an all-time high network share of around 20% during the quarter — its highest in seven years — with the quarter figure at 17.9%, a gain of 110 basis points year-on-year. Zee TV has led prime time for over 32 consecutive weeks, with fiction shows ‘Ganga Mai Ki Betiyan’, ‘Vasudha’ and ‘Tum Se Tum Tak’ occupying the top three spots in the genre. Zee Cinema led the movie genre with 27% viewership in week 22, driven by world television premieres including ‘Akhanda 2 – Thaandavam’, ‘Kishkindhapuri’ and ‘Dil Madharaasi’. Language channels maintained top-two positions across key regions. Monthly unique reach across platforms remains above 800 million.

Sports expansion. In June 2026, the company partnered with FIFA to secure digital and broadcasting rights for various FIFA events until 2034 for the Indian market, covering 39 properties (38 remaining). The FIFA World Cup 2026 reached over 400 million consumers in India across linear and digital platforms, with around 83% of overall FIFA viewership live on ZEE5. Four new sports channels, “Unite8”, were launched in June 2026. The company also secured rights to Bundesliga and Serie A, and is working on a domestic calendar. Management noted right owners are queuing up for partnerships. Management stated it will be prudent in the sports business and ensure it eventually becomes profitable on a sustained basis, though no date was given. Management also noted that in ZEEL’s earlier sports avatar, football might never have reached 40–50 million viewers, whereas now 400 million was reached, and even half of that for non-FIFA content would be a large enough number to monetise.

Subscription growth. Subscription revenue registered 16% year-on-year growth, primarily driven by higher ARPU and subscriber growth in digital, and increased pricing in linear. Management noted renewal activity during and after FIFA gives confidence the trajectory is sustainable. On linear pricing, the next NTO cycle is expected around February next year, at which point price hikes would be considered.

Other businesses. Music garnered 54 billion total video views with more than 177 million YouTube subscribers, driven by a new-age catalogue and a library of over 20,000 songs, with healthy profitability. Other sales and services increased 17% year-on-year driven by studio businesses, which released 9 movies in the quarter (4 Hindi, 5 other languages), including ‘Tumbad Chi Manjula’ and ‘Rakaasa’. Movies and music are growing steadily quarter-on-quarter. The company is building presence in micro-dramas, kids’ entertainment, live events, VFX and animation.

Balance sheet and capital. Cash and treasury investments as of June 2026 stood at INR 22.1 billion, comprising cash of INR 4.4 billion, fixed deposits and other treasury investments of INR 5.9 billion, and mutual fund investments of INR 11.8 billion. Shareholders approved the preferential issue of 24,94,85,563 warrants to a promoter group entity at Rs. 126 per warrant, with promoters to invest Rs. 3,143.5 crores, eventually elevating total promoter shareholding to 23.79%. The ‘Truly Yours’ ESOP will grant 3,74,22,835 stock options to eligible employees of ZEEL and its subsidiaries. A separate disclosure noted the company issued 2,40,59,266 fully convertible warrants to Sunbright Mauritius Investments Limited at a warrant issue price of Rs. 126 each (subscription price Rs. 31.50 and exercise price Rs. 94.50), receiving total upfront consideration of Rs. 75,78,66,879 on or before 27 August 2026, with statutory auditors certifying compliance with Regulation 169(4) of the ICDR Regulations.

Macro recovery expectation. Management stated it is witnessing a marginal recovery in the macroeconomic environment and remains optimistic of stronger green shoots in the upcoming festive season. With West Asia uncertainty cooling off, management remains cautiously optimistic, citing multiple strategic initiatives across content, continued investments, and the onset of the festive season.

3. Key Risks

  • Advertising volatility tied to macroeconomic conditions and geopolitical uncertainty in West Asia, with FMCG inflation risk cited as a factor in the broader environment.
  • Sports rights cost and profitability risk, with management acknowledging sports rights costs are high compared to regular content, and no committed timeline for sustained profitability.
  • Advertiser retention risk post-FIFA, with management acknowledging some marquee advertisers may exit because they only come in for sports.
  • Regulatory and legal risk from SEBI investigations, show cause notices, the rejected settlement application, the SEBI order dated 31 July 2026 under appeal before SAT, and the ongoing Section 206(5) investigation.
  • Arbitration risk from the JioStar claim of USD 1,097 million, with the final evidentiary hearing completed and post-hearing briefs pending.
  • Fund-raising execution risk, given the unresolved clarification on the 18-month versus 12-month timeline and the matter being sub judice.
  • Content and competitive risk in a rapidly evolving entertainment landscape, referenced in the context of the fund-raising rationale.
  • Dependence on content performance and subscriber stickiness, with management noting sustainability depends on consistently providing content of consumer preference.
  • Inventory build, with the increase partly attributable to FIFA outflows in Q1 that will flow through the P&L in Q2.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Advertising. Management characterised advertising as muted due to external headwinds and declined to give full-year EBITDA margin guidance given market uncertainty. It expressed cautious optimism for stabilisation, citing improved market share, the approaching festive season, and the West Asia situation sorting itself out. Observed: advertising revenue declined 11% year-on-year in Q1, with green shoots visible in June post the FIFA rights acquisition as advertisers engaged for premium sponsorship opportunities across platforms. Management noted advertising traction picked up in Q2 because the knockout stages fell in that quarter.

Digital. Management stated the underlying unit economics are intact and expects digital growth momentum to continue in Q2 as further gains accrue from FIFA. Observed: ZEE5 revenue grew 58% year-on-year to INR 4,571 million with EBITDA of INR 44 million, the third consecutive quarter of profitable growth.

Q2 seasonality. Management indicated a significant portion of both revenue and cost related to FIFA would fall in Q2 compared to Q1, and that subscription revenue, on an accrual basis, would also see a significant boost in Q2 compared to Q1. Observed: Q1 inventory increase was partly due to FIFA outflows that will flow through the P&L in Q2.

Sports profitability. Management committed to ensuring the sports business eventually becomes profitable on a sustained basis and to approaching it prudently, but declined to give a date, citing early days. Observed: Q1 included FIFA, a high-revenue and high-viewership property, with limited pre-tournament monetisation window on advertising; management stated Bundesliga and Serie A have enough lead time for better monetisation.

Fund-raising. Management stated the usual approval validity is one year and that the matter is sub judice, refraining from further disclosure pending resolution of the regulator and Tribunal efforts. Observed: shareholder approval was secured at the 31 July 2026 EGM for the preferential issue and ESOP, with the company having written to the regulator and filed an appeal before SAT.

Music business. Management stated it is working on additional disclosures to make the segment transparent, that there is no free lunch in internal music usage — ZEE5 and linear channels pay for ZEE Music catalogue usage in line with marketplace rates — and that while a demerger has been considered, there is no strategic reason to demerge outward and it is not being pursued unless some corporate action transpires. No strategic action disclosure was available as of the call.

Amortisation. Management indicated the current D&A run rate is in the range to work with going ahead, and does not see the line item making any significant impact on results.

Star/JioStar arbitration. Management indicated an outcome possibly in Q3, while noting it cannot commit on legal matters and that further hearings may or may not be required.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from an early reliance on the Zee-Sony merger as a primary value-unlocking catalyst and the expectation that Zee5 digital losses would peak and decline from FY24, toward a later focus on sports rights acquisitions (FIFA, Bundesliga, Serie A) and ZEE5 finally reaching positive EBITDA. Core ad revenue weakness and margin compression persisted across both periods, while the merger catalyst faded as regulatory delays dragged on.

Fears that came true

  • Ad revenue decline flagged in the first report (8% YoY in FY23, 10% in Q4FY23) persisted and worsened to 11.5% YoY in the latest report, correlating with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes of -40% to -63% across 2023 Buy calls.
  • Severe margin pressure flagged early (EBITDA margin at 7.2% in Q4FY23, core broadcast below 30%) deteriorated further to 4.1% in the latest report, confirming sustained profitability erosion.
  • Merger delays and regulatory risks (NCLT review, SEBI compliance) flagged in 2023 materialized as the deal did not close within the expected timeframe, leaving the stock with multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in 2023-2024.
  • Digital losses widening (Rs 11.05bn in FY23, Rs 3.1bn in Q4FY23) persisted longer than expected, reflecting prolonged cash burn before eventual improvement.
  • Negative free cash flow of Rs -1.65bn in FY23 and elevated content inventory of Rs 79.6bn signaled sustained working capital stress that weighed on the stock through 2023-2024.

Optimism that failed

  • The Zee-Sony merger catalyst, repeatedly cited as a primary trigger for value unlocking and ~3% revenue synergies, failed to deliver in the expected timeframe, with the stock declining over 40% in 2023 across multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Management’s expectation that digital losses would peak and decline from FY24 onwards did not materialize quickly, as Zee5 losses continued to widen before eventual improvement.
  • FMCG ad spending recovery green shoots flagged in 2023 did not translate into sustained ad revenue growth, as ad revenues kept declining through 2025-2026.
  • Viewership share rebound (16.6% in Q4FY23) and NTO 2.0 embargo lift optimism failed to offset the persistent ad revenue decline and margin compression over the medium term.
