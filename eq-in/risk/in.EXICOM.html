Exicom Tele-Systems: Recent Developments, Headwinds, and Growth Prospects

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure and consolidated losses. In Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026), consolidated revenue grew 61% year-on-year to ₹331.1 crore, but the company reported a consolidated EBITDA loss of ₹21.9 crore and a consolidated PAT loss of ₹73.6 crore. Standalone EBITDA was positive at ₹20.9 crore (8.8% margin), with standalone PAT of ₹4.9 crore. Management attributed the sequential deterioration from Q4 FY26 EBITDA breakeven to the seasonal strength of Q4, the erosion of low-cost Tritium inventory acquired at acquisition, and higher fixed costs.

Input cost and supply chain pressure. Commodity-led fluctuations and availability have limited project supply quantity and margins, particularly under fixed-price contracts for HUPS, Li-ion batteries, and Smart Racks. Costs increased on account of USD movements. Management also cited geopolitical disruption in semiconductors, plastics, and copper, which has made material expensive and sometimes unavailable, at times preventing full capacity utilization.

Dual-plant transition costs. The Gurgaon and Hyderabad plants are running in parallel during the transition, adding approximately ₹8.7 crore to fixed costs. Depreciation rose sharply — standalone depreciation up about 67% year-on-year and consolidated up about 57% — following commissioning of the Hyderabad facility and capitalization of Tritium R&D. Management described the parallel-run cost as a planned, time-bound investment expected to phase out as the Gurgaon transition completes.

Working capital build-up. Inventory is temporarily elevated because both plants are being stocked to ensure continuity during the transition and to prepare for Q3/Q4 orders. Receivables are elevated as a direct function of the sharp revenue increase, though collections quality and aging profile are described as intact. Consolidated debt stood at about ₹370 crore as of June 30, 2026.

Customs duty order. The Office of the Principal Commissioner of Customs (Import), Inland Container Depot, Tughlakabad, New Delhi issued an Order-in-Original determining a customs duty liability of ₹14,48,80,677 along with applicable interest, and penalties of ₹14,48,80,677 on the company, ₹1,44,88,067 on the Managing Director & CEO, ₹1,44,88,067 on the Whole-time Director, and ₹1,44,88,067 on the Chief Financial Officer. The dispute concerns classification of imported goods declared as ‘Static Converters for Telecom’ under CTH 85044090 with a BCD exemption, which the department alleges were meant for Electric Vehicle Chargers. The company disagrees, maintains its classification was lawful, and plans to appeal.

EV charging demand timing. Standalone EV revenue grew 15% year-on-year to ₹61 crore but dipped sequentially. Management explained that customers pulled forward DC charger orders into Q4 FY26 amid commodity, forex, and geopolitical risk, so Q1 DC growth was muted even as AC charger volumes grew. Industry-level underutilization of public charging infrastructure was also discussed, with management noting that many older or tender-deployed chargers are obsolete or non-functional, and that aggregate utilization figures are not representative of well-located, reliable networks.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Critical Power momentum. Standalone Critical Power revenue rose 73% year-on-year to ₹177 crore, driven by execution of new telecom and government orders. The company entered a supply agreement for power systems and batteries with one of India’s largest tower companies, secured a large DC power systems order from a leading Indian telco worth ₹85 crore, and holds roughly a 60% share of business in BharatNet based on the system integrators it works with. Exports to Africa, Middle East, and Southeast Asia were about ₹15 crore, or 8% of Critical Power sales, with a target to reach roughly 15% within the year. The Critical Power order book stands at roughly ₹1,000 crore.

BharatNet and BSNL opportunities. BharatNet has ₹700 crore of open orders, expected to be followed by almost ₹800 crore of service orders over the next 10 years. Exicom won Phase 2 of a BSNL-led program for telecom infrastructure in underserved border regions, with expectations of about 2,000 sites/towers allocated to Exicom at a contract value of ₹90–100 crore.

BESS growth. The company operates in commercial and industrial BESS (typically under 0.5 MWh), not utility-scale. It holds about 15 MWh of orders worth roughly ₹20 crore, plus another ₹45 crore (34 MWh) in advanced pipeline. Management targets a ₹150 crore BESS order book in FY27.

EV charging tailwinds. Q1 saw nearly 31,000 passenger car EV registrations, the highest to date, alongside launches across Maruti, Tata, Mahindra, and luxury makers, and commercial vehicle launches. Total passenger EV registrations were almost 86,000, with about 1,400 buses and 6,500 goods carriers. Policy support includes state and city mandates for EV-ready apartments, Delhi’s four-year ₹15,000 crore EV policy targeting 30,000-plus charging points, mandatory OEM dealer charging stations, and PM E-DRIVE funding for electric buses and charging infrastructure. Exicom secured 100% share of business for wallbox chargers with a leading international brand, renewed an agreement with a leading truck OEM for portable chargers, launched fast chargers with a two-wheeler company, added 15 new network operators, and won orders from 10 new countries. The AC/DC charger order book is almost ₹200 crore, including about USD 2 million of exports.

Tritium turnaround signals. Tritium bookings reached about USD 20.8 million in Q1, roughly double the prior quarterly run rate of about USD 10 million, with revenue of about USD 10.5 million. New products including TRI-FLEX, DC-FLEX, and GRID-FLEX are under trial with Fortune 100 and hyperscale customers. Subject to successful trials, management expects potential contract awards of about USD 20–30 million for TRI-FLEX and about USD 20 million for GRID-FLEX for CY27. Tritium’s U.S. manufacturing capacity can service roughly 2.5x current revenue, or about USD 100 million.

Liquid-cooled power modules. On August 19, 2026, Exicom announced the start of manufacturing for liquid-cooled AC and DC power modules at its Hyderabad Smart Manufacturing Facility, making it the first company in India to produce this class of liquid-cooled power electronics for global markets. Initial production will primarily serve North America and Europe, supporting Tritium’s DC charger portfolio including TRI-FLEX and DC-FLEX. Subsequent phases will bring the technology closer to Indian customers, and over time the liquid-cooled architecture will be designed into Exicom’s flagship Harmony DC chargers, opening a new line of high-value power electronics exports from India.

Hyderabad facility. The Hyderabad Smart Manufacturing Facility, commissioned in Q4 FY26 and fully in production in Q1 FY27, provides 3x production capability and capacity, with advanced automation, digital traceability, and specialized testing. Management expects capacity utilization of about 65% on DC charging, close to 100% on AC chargers, 90–100% on DC power system lines, and close to 100% on PCBA and battery lines. An additional AC charger line has been ordered, with AC monthly run rate expected to grow about 50% over the next three months.

3. Key Risks

  • Customs duty liability and penalties totaling ₹14,48,80,677 on the company plus interest, and penalties on the MD & CEO, Whole-time Director, and CFO, subject to appeal.
  • Commodity and forex volatility under fixed-price contracts compressing margins.
  • Supply chain disruption in semiconductors, plastics, and copper affecting material availability and capacity utilization.
  • Continued consolidated losses and the risk that Tritium’s turnaround and breakeven timeline slip.
  • Elevated working capital, inventory, and receivables during the plant transition.
  • Dependence on government programs (BharatNet, BSNL, PM E-DRIVE) and policy continuity.
  • Execution risk on large strategic Tritium contracts that remain subject to successful field trials.
  • Related party transaction limits proposed for subsidiaries — Exicom Power Solutions B.V. (up to ₹240 crore), Tritium Power Solutions Pty Ltd (up to ₹235 crore), and Tritium Power Solutions Inc. (up to ₹285 crore) — for FY 2026-27 and FY 2027-28.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Tritium breakeven. Management previously guided to 3x revenue growth and EBITDA breakeven by Q4 FY27. In the Q1 FY27 call, management reiterated confidence in Tritium EBITDA breakeven in Q4 FY27 and stated consolidated breakeven may occur over the next two quarters (Q2 or Q3 FY27). Observed performance: Tritium delivered its second consecutive quarter above USD 10 million in revenue, with bookings doubling to about USD 20.8 million, but consolidated EBITDA remained a loss of ₹21.9 crore in Q1 FY27, so the guided breakeven had not yet been achieved.

Export share. Management targets roughly doubling Critical Power export share from 8% in Q1 FY27 to about 15% within FY27, and aims for a similar export percentage in EV chargers. Observed performance: Q1 exports were about ₹15 crore, or 8% of Critical Power sales, with EV charger exports at about USD 2 million in the order book.

BESS order book. Management targets a ₹150 crore BESS order book in FY27. Observed performance: about ₹20 crore of orders in hand plus ₹45 crore in advanced pipeline as of the Q1 FY27 call.

Export business. Management targets a ₹140 crore export business in FY27 and about 40% wallet share with strategic customers in FY27. Observed performance: Q1 export sales were about ₹15 crore in Critical Power, with new order bookings described as making management hopeful of better-than-expected export sales in FY27.

Capacity utilization. Management expects AC charger utilization close to 100%, DC power system lines 90–100%, PCBA and battery lines close to 100%, and DC charging about 65%. Observed performance: the Hyderabad plant is fully in production in Q1 FY27, with an additional AC charger line ordered and AC monthly run rate expected to grow about 50% over the next three months.

Order book. Management cited a consolidated order book of over ₹1,400 crore as of July 1, 2026, comprising roughly ₹1,000 crore in Critical Power and about ₹200 crore in AC/DC chargers including exports, alongside the Tritium backlog. Observed performance: Q1 standalone revenue of ₹236.8 crore and consolidated revenue of ₹331.1 crore reflect partial execution against this book.

Customs order. Management stated it will file an appeal within the prescribed time limit and, based on present assessment, does not expect the Order to have any material financial impact, with no impact on operations or other activities. Observed performance: the appeal had not yet been filed as of the disclosure, so the outcome remains pending.
