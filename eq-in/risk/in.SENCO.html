SENCO Gold: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Gold price volatility and elevated prices. Average gold prices rose approximately 61% year-on-year and about 1% quarter-on-quarter to ₹15,280 per gram in Q1 FY27, while the quarter-end price rose 48% YoY but fell 3% QoQ to ₹14,253 per gram. Management described this intra-quarter price movement as an important factor in pricing and hedging decisions.

Custom duty increase and demand contraction. The custom duty rose from 6% to 15%. Management noted that after early-Q1 festive and wedding demand (Poila Boishakh, Akshay Tritiya), low consumer sentiment and the duty rise caused a demand contraction, which marketing initiatives, old-gold schemes and consumer outreach were used to counter. The benefit of the duty rise is expected to accrue over Q3, net of hedging-level impacts from the price fall, and heavy discounting during the quarter put pressure on Q1 margin.

Margin compression. Consolidated EBITDA margin fell to 7.0% in Q1 FY27 from 10.1% a year earlier (down 310 bps) and from 13.7% in Q4 FY26 (down 680 bps). PAT margin fell to 3.3% from 5.7% YoY. Management attributed the movement to discounting, the gold price fall partly offset by the custom duty increase, and product mix. Standalone gross margin fell to 16.4% from 19.0% YoY.

Old-gold exchange impact on gross margin. The “0% deduction” old-gold exchange campaign supported 43% of total sales quantity (procurement ~40%), enabling customers to upgrade jewellery and moderating the impact of elevated prices, but it partly impacts gross margin.

Subsidiary losses. SFL is still in a growth phase and incurred losses; the Dubai entity (SGJTL) incurred losses due to an extraordinary market situation in the Gulf (described as gulf war and Hormuz imbroglio); SGAPL reported profit. These losses affected consolidated profitability.

Higher borrowing costs. Due to gold price volatility and GML unavailability, blended borrowing cost increased marginally. Finance cost rose 58% YoY to ₹679.4 Mn on a consolidated basis.

Seasonality. Management stated Q2 is seasonally softer.

Other operating factors. Adhik Maas was cited as a factor in the quarter. Other expenses rose sharply (128% YoY standalone, 141% consolidated). The full impact of the Labour Code was considered in Q1 after detailed evaluation.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Consolidated revenue grew 67% YoY to ₹3,056 Cr (highest Q1 in recent years) and 53% QoQ. Standalone sales grew 65% YoY to ₹3,006.7 Cr. Retail sales grew 50% YoY to ₹2,651.5 Cr, broad-based across COCO, FOCO and FOFO channels. SSSG was 39% YoY. TTM sales reached ₹9,660 Cr.

Festive and wedding demand. Demand was supported by Akshaya Tritiya, Poila Boishakh, Baisakhi, Bihu and the summer wedding season, plus a larger range in lower-carat (9K/14K) and lower-weight categories and fresh design launches. Gold jewellery volumes remained broadly stable despite price rise and duty impact.

Diamond and lightweight jewellery. Diamond jewellery sales grew 43% YoY (18% in volume/carat terms), supported by sub-₹50,000 offerings, the Everlite collection, wider 9K/14K assortment and new designs. Stud ratio stood at 11%.

Showroom expansion. Net 8 showrooms were added in Q1 (COCO-4, FRN-4, 1 Sennes; after closure of 1 COCO), taking the network from 201 at 31 March 2026 to 209 at 30 June 2026. The company remains on track to open another 12–15 showrooms during the remainder of FY27, with focus on franchise-led expansion, Tier-2/Tier-3 cities, and balanced East/non-East presence.

Digital growth. Digital sales (including quick commerce) grew 164% YoY as of Q1 FY27; 3.09 Mn+ website visits; ~18,000 e-commerce orders fulfilled. MyDigiGold had 1,53,902+ registrations and MyDigiSilver 75,830+ as of June 2026.

New products and brands. Launch of Aham Ti22 titanium jewellery for men; new Everlite designs for women; Sennes (lab-grown diamonds, leather bags, fragrances) with 13 EBOs and 3 MBOs.

Inventory efficiency. Inventory reduced by ₹300 Cr during the quarter, with inventory days improving to 152 days.

Vision 2030 aspirations. Stores from 201 to 300+; revenue from ₹8,430 Cr to ₹20,000 Cr; EBITDA margin to ~8%+; PAT margin to ~4.5–5%+; stud ratio from 11.0% to 15%+; ROE 25.6% to 20%+; ROCE 22.5% to 20%+. India’s jewellery TAM is projected to grow from $90 Bn (FY25) to $130 Bn (FY30E), with organised share rising from 40% to 50%.

Industry structure. Continued shift from unorganised to organised jewellers; regulatory developments (BIS hallmarking, HUID, mandatory hallmarking for 9-carat gold from July 2025, AML applicability); rural India accounts for ~60% of gold jewellery demand.

3. Key Risks

  • Gold price volatility affecting demand, margins, inventory valuation and hedging outcomes.
  • Custom duty changes (rise from 6% to 15%) and the timing of benefit accrual.
  • Margin pressure from discounting and old-gold exchange mix.
  • Losses at subsidiaries SFL and SGJTL, and geopolitical/market uncertainty affecting the Dubai operation.
  • Elevated and volatile borrowing costs; GML availability affecting the borrowing mix.
  • Seasonality, with Q2 described as softer.
  • Execution risk on expansion plans (12–15 showrooms in FY27; 300+ stores by FY30) and on franchise-led, asset-light model.
  • Dependence on festive/wedding seasons and consumer sentiment.
  • Regulatory and compliance requirements (hallmarking, AML, PAN, GST).
  • Dividend-related tax deduction process: shareholders must submit documents (PAN, Form 121, TRC, Form 41, declarations) by 30 August 2026; failure to do so can result in higher TDS (20% where PAN is unavailable/inoperative; 20% plus surcharge and cess for non-residents where DTAA documents are not provided). The company states tax deducted is final and no claim lies against it for higher-rate deduction, and shareholders indemnify the company for misrepresentation.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Guidance: FY27 value growth of 20%+; EBITDA margin of 7.5%–7.8%; sustainable PAT margin of 4.0%–4.5%; focus on RoCE, margin stabilisation, inventory days, finance cost mix (GML) and subsidiary performance. Management also guided to open another 12–15 showrooms in the remainder of FY27.

Observed Q1 FY27 performance: Revenue growth of 67% YoY is well above the 20%+ full-year value growth guidance. EBITDA margin of 7.0% is below the guided 7.5%–7.8% range; management described it as having “stabilised to 7.0% in our guided range of 7.5%-7.8% on a QoQ basis.” PAT margin of 3.3% is below the 4.0%–4.5% sustainable target. Net 8 showrooms were added in Q1, consistent with the stated expansion trajectory.

Prior-year context: FY26 consolidated revenue was ₹8,430.0 Cr (33% YoY growth), EBITDA ₹969.0 Cr (11.5% margin), PAT ₹574.3 Cr (6.8% margin). FY25 adjusted EBITDA was ₹425.1 Cr (6.7%) and adjusted PAT ₹201.8 Cr (3.2%), reflecting the one-time custom duty reduction impact of ₹57.4 Cr in Q2/Q3 FY25.

Dividend: The Board on 26 May 2026 declared a final dividend of Re. 1/- (20%) per equity share of face value ₹5/- for FY2025-26, with record date 24 August 2026, subject to shareholder approval at the 32nd AGM. The AGM was held on 31 August 2026, where resolutions passed included adopting audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY ended 31 March 2026, confirming the interim dividend and declaring the final dividend, and re-appointing Mrs. Ranjana Sen as a director.

Investor engagement: Management will hold analyst meetings in Mumbai on Wednesday, 16 September 2026, and Thursday, 17 September 2026, subject to change.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a bullish Buy with expectations of strong growth (23% revenue CAGR) and confidence in systems like hedging, to a cautious Neutral highlighting margin contraction, gold price volatility, and subsidiary losses. The early optimism on store expansion and market share partly persisted, but concerns about working capital and competition became dominant.

Fears that came true

  • The flagged need for more working capital materialized as high blended borrowing costs due to gold price volatility and GML unavailability.
  • Stiff competition materialized as discounting and the old-gold exchange scheme compressed gross and EBITDA margins.

Optimism that failed

  • The confidence in gold-price hedging as an established system failed, as the hedging ratio was only ~50% and gross margin remained volatile.
  • Expected strong EBITDA/PAT CAGRs of ~28%/36% failed to materialize as margin contraction offset robust revenue growth.
