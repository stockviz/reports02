SBCL — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Asia weakness. Asia Others revenue declined 12.7% to ₹21.3 crore, reflecting softer shunt demand in select markets. Management described Asia as “weaker during the quarter” and an area requiring a rebuild of momentum.

Bimetals lagging. Thermostatic Bimetals grew only 7.4% to ₹63.6 crore, well behind Shunts at 18.7% to ₹68.2 crore. Management noted Bimetals had been “struggling across region” for several quarters, with growth driven primarily by India.

Volume decline at standalone level. Standalone revenue growth of 13.0% to ₹131.8 crore came despite a 2.5% decline in volumes (including wastage), meaning growth was driven by realisations and mix rather than throughput.

Commodity distortion of reported numbers. Silver prices were described as roughly double year-on-year, and copper prices also rose substantially. Management indicated that about half of the reported revenue growth could be attributed to silver alone, and that a portion of profitability comes from material movements. As Electrical Contacts scales, precious metal content affects reported revenue and gross margin, making EBITDA and cash generation the more meaningful measures.

Pune ramp is back-ended. Phase I of the Pune facility only received Consent to Operate after quarter-end (valid until June 30, 2032). Full main manufacturing operations are expected only from October. Phase I capacity can currently cater to only one particular model, so additional customer programmes cannot add revenue before year-end even if developed.

EV adoption uncertainty. Management flagged that two-wheeler EV demand forecasts are subject to change — an OEM planning 10,000 units may sell only 5,000 — and that India is still absorbing the product. Imported supply chains for battery packs remain entrenched, and OEMs are reluctant to switch suppliers without a design change or lifecycle end.

Leadership transition. Mr. Rajeev Ranjan, CFO, resigned effective close of business on October 31, 2026, citing personal reasons. Separately, M/s Arora Gupta & Co. resigned as Statutory Auditors effective August 6, 2026, with M/s Walker Chandiok & Co LLP appointed to fill the casual vacancy and proposed for a five-year term.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 consolidated performance. Revenue from operations rose 33.4% year-on-year to ₹182.2 crore, EBITDA grew 35.2% to ₹43.2 crore, and PAT increased 44.9% to ₹33.0 crore. Sequentially, revenue, EBITDA and PAT rose 12.0%, 21.8% and 26.3%. Standalone EBITDA margin expanded 225 bps to 27.5%, with gross margin up 355 bps to 51.5%.

Shift to higher-value components. Shunt strip sales fell to roughly one-third of the prior-year quarter’s level, with the residual strip being higher value-add. Management stated roughly 70–75% of shunt value-addition growth came from conversion to value-added parts rather than materials, and that such business is more sustainable because a developed precision component is harder to displace than a strip.

Geographic recovery. Europe grew 21.5%, led by a 72.2% increase in Shunts. Americas revenue rose 4.8%, with Shunt growth of 30.0% partly offset by softer Bimetals — described as early signs of recovery after a softer FY26. India combined revenue rose 26.4% to ₹60.8 crore, about 46% of standalone core-product revenue.

Key customer order book. Management said the largest customer’s expectations for the year “look pretty encouraging,” with the resurgence coming in higher value-added form than a few years ago. Growth is being seen in two-wheeler EVs and smart meters. A recent patent by a large resistor customer has improved accuracy, drawing both American and larger Chinese OEMs.

Pune / Cell Connecting Systems. The Pune facility targets Cell Connecting Systems, busbar connectors and PCBA assemblies. Management indicated a business potential of ₹300–400 crore over about three years, with roughly 10–15% in year one, ₹150–200 crore in year two, and ₹300 crore onwards thereafter. The project’s capex is estimated at ₹20–25 crore, with much of the high-capex process equipment already in place at Solan. Additional opportunity exists in integrated assemblies combining shunts for BMS.

Bimetal recovery signals. Management reported the first quantity-level uptake in 4–5 quarters, driven by Indian real estate and infrastructure, with export developments restarting after the US tariff disruption. These export opportunities are expected to contribute over roughly four quarters.

New verticals under exploration. Two areas are in talks with potential technology partners or JV setups: a specialised metallurgical material for electronic applications, and automotive fuses (including equipment-level fuses within fuse boxes). These could result in greenfield projects, technology partnerships, or small acquisitions. Management indicated more concrete information may be available by the next earnings call.

Four-wheeler opportunity. Management is working on four-wheeler CCS and assemblies, starting with the Indian market, and noted flexibility to enter with non-EB-welded designs before moving to EB welding.

Capacity headroom. Welding capacity utilisation for shunts is 65–70%, with spare ready-to-build capacity that can be added in 2–3 months. Bimetal capacity utilisation is 40–45%, with capacity deliberately built ahead given long lead times.

Customer concentration reduced. Peak exposure to a single customer was 35–40%; even with the US recovery, management expects maximum exposure to remain at 17–18%, well below 20%.

3. Key Risks

  • Demand forecast risk in EV two-wheelers, where OEM volume projections may not materialise and adoption confidence is still building.
  • Import supply-chain inertia, where OEMs may retain existing imported battery pack sources absent design changes or lifecycle ends.
  • Commodity price exposure, particularly silver and copper, which affect reported revenue, gross margin and, to a limited extent, profitability.
  • Concentration in a single Pune Phase I programme, with capacity currently able to serve only one model.
  • Execution risk in new verticals, where technology partnerships, JVs or acquisitions remain in discussion and subject to NDAs.
  • Geographic demand divergence, with Asia still soft and Americas Bimetals weak.
  • Governance and continuity risk from the simultaneous CFO resignation and statutory auditor resignation, mitigated by an orderly transition and the appointment of Walker Chandiok & Co LLP.
  • NDA constraints limiting disclosure on key customers and new technology discussions.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

FY27 revenue growth guidance. Management guided to overall revenue growth of 20–30% for FY27, contingent on customer forecasts and developments materialising, while noting it is early in the financial year. Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue growth of 33.4% is above this range, though management attributed roughly half of reported growth to silver prices and indicated the sustainable underlying growth is in the 20–30% band.

Segment mix guidance. On a standalone basis, management expects approximately 44–45% of revenue from Bimetals and 54–55% from Shunts. In Q1 FY27, Shunts contributed ₹68.2 crore and Bimetals ₹63.6 crore, a roughly 52:48 split. On a consolidated basis, management expects 30–35% of revenue from the contacts business and 15–16% from assemblies (PCBA and busbar) in the first year. Q1 busbar and cell connecting system revenue was minimal, with most contribution expected in the current and upcoming quarters.

Pune revenue guidance. Management guided to ₹300–400 crore potential over about three years, with 10–15% in year one, ₹150–200 crore in year two, and ₹300 crore onwards thereafter, subject to change. Q1 FY27 included only about one month of limited busbar revenue, with full production starting only after October.

Margin quality. Management stated the majority of EBITDA margin improvement comes from a sustainable source — the shift to value-added parts — with only a minimal portion from materials. Q1 FY27 standalone EBITDA margin of 27.5% (up 225 bps) and consolidated EBITDA margin of 23.7% (up 33 bps) are consistent with this, though consolidated gross margin declined 119 bps year-on-year to 43.4% amid higher silver-linked content.

Bimetal growth timing. Management expects Bimetal to return to growth, with Indian quantity uptake visible in the current quarter and export developments contributing over roughly four quarters. Q1 FY27 Bimetal growth of 7.4% remains modest relative to this expectation.

Customer concentration. Management guided that maximum exposure to the largest customer would remain at 17–18% even with the US recovery, down from a historical peak of 35–40%.

Capital allocation. Management reiterated priorities of margin quality, working capital efficiency, cash conversion and selective capital allocation, with capex largely done and cash generation to be deployed into new verticals.
