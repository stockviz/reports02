Headwinds and Challenges

Revenue contraction in Q1 FY27. HCC’s standalone E&C business recorded turnover of ~981.7 crore in Q1 FY27, compared with ~1,069.0 crore in Q1 FY26. On a consolidated basis, revenue stood at ~993.4 crore in Q1 FY27 versus ~1,091.3 crore in Q1 FY26. Standalone net profit was ~37.1 crore in Q1 FY27 against ~38.6 crore in the corresponding quarter of the previous year.

Sharp margin compression. Standalone EBITDA margins fell to 10.7% in Q1 FY27 from 14.9% in Q1 FY26. The company notes that its business is seasonal in character and that margins vary quarter to quarter, including due to the nature of the business and consequent to receipt of awards/claims or events which may lead to revision in cost to completion, so quarterly results may not be strictly comparable.

Auditor qualifications and unresolved accounting matters. The statutory auditor’s limited review reports on both standalone and consolidated results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 are modified in respect of: - The carrying value of the investment (including deemed investments) in wholly owned subsidiary HCC Infrastructure Company Limited (HICL), amounting to ~1,096.22 crore as at June 30, 2026 (March 31, 2026: ~1,152.77 crore). HICL’s consolidated net worth is substantially eroded; management considers the investment fully recoverable based on a valuation report from an independent valuer, but the auditor states it could not obtain sufficient and appropriate audit evidence to support the judgements and estimates applied in that valuation report. - Net deferred tax assets of ~163.55 crore as at June 30, 2026 (March 31, 2026: ~173.91 crore), recognized on carried-forward unused tax losses/unabsorbed depreciation and other taxable temporary differences. The auditor cites the company’s history of losses and states it could not obtain sufficient and appropriate audit evidence on management’s projection of future taxable profits. The auditor notes that its review report for the quarter ended June 30, 2025 and its audit report for the year ended March 31, 2026 were also qualified on these matters.

Uncertainty over recoverability of legacy receivables. Unbilled work-in-progress (contract assets), current trade receivables and non-current trade receivables as at June 30, 2026 include ~102.03 crore, ~24.21 crore and ~57.52 crore respectively (March 31, 2026: ~115.71 crore, ~24.21 crore and ~57.52 crore), representing receivables in respect of closed/substantially closed projects, mainly cost over-runs arising from client-caused delays, design deviations and scope changes. These are at various stages of negotiation/discussion with clients or under arbitration/litigation. Management is confident of recovery; the auditor’s conclusion is not modified in respect of this matter.

Prior-year exceptional charges. For the year ended March 31, 2026, exceptional items included an impairment charge of ~35.63 crore on the investment in HRL Thane Real Estate Limited after that entity executed a Memorandum of Understanding for sale of land at a value lower than earlier assessed estimates; a one-time past service cost increase of ~11.28 crore for gratuity and compensated absence following the consolidation of 29 labour regulations into four Labour Codes effective November 21, 2025; and a ~49.09 crore write-back from a liability relating to a corporate guarantee, after the guarantee was reduced from 100% of outstanding debt to ~570.88 crore (20% of principal debt) in respect of non-convertible debentures aggregating ~2,854.40 crore issued by Prolific Resolution Private Limited.

Shareholder dissent at the 100th AGM (August 18, 2026). Public Institutions voted heavily against several resolutions: 85.20% against adoption of the audited standalone and consolidated financial statements (Resolution 1); 70.48% against the re-appointment of Mr. Aditya Pratap Jain as Director (Resolution 2); and 84.17% against payment of remuneration to Mr. Ajit Gulabchand, Non-Executive Chairman (Resolution 4). All resolutions passed with the requisite majority, but the aggregate votes against Resolution 1 were 24.55% and against Resolution 4 were 24.20%.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

New order win in Jammu & Kashmir. On August 11, 2026, HCC announced a ~524.17 crore contract from NHPC Limited at the Salal Power Station in Jammu & Kashmir, covering civil and hydro-mechanical works plus associated ancillary and enabling works to make the undersluices of the concrete dam fully functional and operational. The contract duration is 27 months. The rehabilitation is expected to enhance the dam’s operational efficiency, improve sediment management and strengthen long-term safety and reliability.

Order pipeline and bids under evaluation. During Q1 FY27, HCC secured a new order worth ~125 crore and emerged as the lowest bidder (L1) for projects valued at ~2,124 crore (HCC’s share ~1,671 crore). Bids aggregating approximately ~9,997 crore remain under evaluation. Total balance value of work on hand as at June 30, 2026 was ~12,976 crore (March 31, 2026: ~12,971 crore).

Execution progress across the portfolio. During the quarter, HCC achieved breakthrough of the Head Race Tunnel Faces 2 and 3 for the 520 MW Tapovan Vishnugad Hydroelectric Project. At the 1,000 MW Vishnugad Pipalkoti Hydroelectric Project, the Machine Hall for Units 1 and 2 was handed over and HRT excavation reached 85%. Earthworks and steel liner fabrication progressed at the Bhivpuri Pumped Storage Project. In metro and transport, TBM deployment commenced at the Indore Metro project; Patna Metro saw casting yard development, guide wall works and the start of segment casting, with TBM deliveries scheduled later in the year; and piling, pier and segment casting advanced on the Agardanda Creek Bridge. In the industrial segment, Hindalco’s Aditya Aluminium Smelter expansion project received approval for the first pot shell prototype, enabling bulk fabrication. In nuclear, HCC continued executing the Fast Reactor Fuel Cycle Facility and recently completed delivery of Rajasthan Atomic Power Project Units 7 & 8.

Capital-raising and capital-structure resolutions passed. At the 100th AGM on August 18, 2026, shareholders passed a special resolution for issue of securities of the Company (99.589% in favour) and an ordinary resolution to increase the authorised share capital and alter the capital clause of the Memorandum of Association (99.743% in favour).

Balance-sheet deleveraging versus the prior year. Standalone debt-equity ratio was 0.27 as at June 30, 2026, compared with 0.75 as at June 30, 2025. Consolidated debt-equity was 0.46 versus 1.64 a year earlier. Standalone net worth rose to ~3,118.70 crore from ~1,943.40 crore a year earlier; consolidated net worth rose to ~2,137.31 crore from ~1,003.90 crore.

Track record and positioning. HCC describes an engineering heritage of nearly 100 years, having constructed 26% of India’s hydro power generation and 60% of India’s nuclear power generation capacities, over 4,036 lane km of expressways and highways, more than 402 km of complex tunnelling and 403 bridges. In Jammu & Kashmir, it has built the 690 MW Salal Hydroelectric Project (commissioned 1987), Uri-II (240 MW), Kishanganga (333 MW), Nimoo Bazgo (45 MW) and Chutak (44 MW) — a combined 1,352 MW, or approximately 38% of J&K’s total installed hydropower capacity — plus India’s first cable-stayed railway bridge at Anji Khad and nearly 32 km of tunnels, about 27% of the total tunnel length for the Udhampur-Srinagar-Baramulla Rail Link project.

Key Risks

  • Recoverability risk on legacy receivables. ~183.76 crore of contract assets and trade receivables relating to closed/substantially closed projects depend on negotiation, arbitration or litigation outcomes.
  • Carrying value risk on the HICL investment. ~1,096.22 crore is supported by a valuation relying on significant judgements and estimates regarding future business plans, litigation outcomes for favourable arbitration awards and expected share of future revenues of erstwhile subsidiaries, and the auditor could not obtain sufficient evidence to support those judgements.
  • Deferred tax asset realization risk. ~163.55 crore of net deferred tax assets depend on generating sufficient future taxable profits from unexecuted orders, future projects and expected realization of claims/arbitration awards, against a history of losses.
  • Revenue and margin volatility. Turnover declined year on year in Q1 FY27 and EBITDA margin fell to 10.7% from 14.9%; the company states results are seasonal and may not be strictly comparable quarter to quarter.
  • Dependence on claims and award outcomes. The company’s own notes link margin variability and deferred tax recoverability to receipt of awards/claims and realization of arbitration awards.
  • Execution and counterparty concentration in public-sector hydro and infrastructure work. The order book and new wins are concentrated in hydro-mechanical, dam rehabilitation, metro, bridge and nuclear projects with long gestation periods (the Salal contract runs 27 months).
  • Governance signal from institutional voting. Majority opposition from Public Institutions on the financial-statements adoption, a director re-appointment and chairman remuneration resolutions at the 100th AGM.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

The source material contains no explicit numerical guidance for revenue, margin or order inflow. The observable management positions and their supporting evidence are:

  • Receivables recovery. Management states it is confident that receivables relating to closed/substantially closed projects are good and fully recoverable, citing contractual tenability, progress of negotiations/discussions/arbitration/litigations and legal advice in certain contentious matters. Observed position: the unbilled work-in-progress component within these receivables declined to ~102.03 crore as at June 30, 2026 from ~115.71 crore as at March 31, 2026, while current trade receivables (~24.21 crore) and non-current trade receivables (~57.52 crore) were unchanged. The auditor’s conclusion is not modified on this matter.
  • Deferred tax assets. Management states it is confident of generating sufficient taxable profits from unexecuted orders on hand/future projects and expected realization of claims/arbitration awards. Observed position: net deferred tax assets declined to ~163.55 crore as at June 30, 2026 from ~173.91 crore as at March 31, 2026, and the auditor was unable to obtain sufficient evidence on the future taxable profit projections.
  • HICL investment recoverability. Management believes the recoverable amount of the investment in HICL exceeds its carrying value, based on an independent valuation report. Observed position: the carrying value declined to ~1,096.22 crore as at June 30, 2026 from ~1,152.77 crore as at March 31, 2026, and HICL’s consolidated net worth remains substantially eroded.
  • Execution on track. Management states execution across the project portfolio remained on track during the quarter, supported by the Tapovan Vishnugad HRT breakthroughs, the Vishnugad Pipalkoti Machine Hall handover and 85% HRT excavation, Indore Metro TBM deployment, Patna Metro segment casting commencement, and the Aditya Aluminium first pot shell prototype approval.
  • Order book stability. Balance value of work on hand was broadly flat at ~12,976 crore as at June 30, 2026 versus ~12,971 crore as at March 31, 2026, while the company added a ~125 crore order in the quarter, was L1 on ~2,124 crore of projects (HCC share ~1,671 crore), had ~9,997 crore of bids under evaluation, and subsequently announced the ~524.17 crore NHPC Salal contract on August 11, 2026.
  • Investor engagement. The company will hold meetings with analysts and institutional investors on September 3, 2026, including a one-on-one meeting at 3:00 p.m. and an analysts and institutional investor group meeting at 4:00 p.m. at Grand Hyatt, Santacruz East, Mumbai, with discussions based on publicly available information and not involving unpublished price sensitive information.
