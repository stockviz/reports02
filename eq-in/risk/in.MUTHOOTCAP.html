Muthoot Capital Services — Recent Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Moderate disbursement growth versus industry. In Q1 FY27, the two-wheeler industry grew 14% year-on-year, while Muthoot Capital delivered 6% growth. Management attributed the gap not to market demand but to deliberately moderate credit acceptance ratios of about 35–40%, against industry approval rates of 75–80%.
  • Deliberate co-lending wind-down. Co-lending disbursements fell sharply — from about ₹120 crores in Q1 FY26 to ₹20 crores in the first month of Q1 FY27, with zero incremental co-lending business thereafter. The co-lending portfolio declined from close to ₹1,000 crores a year earlier to ₹499 crores. This weighed on overall disbursement and AUM optics even as the standalone retail book grew.
  • Corporate loan overhang. A single corporate loan case contributed ₹15–16 crores to GNPA, keeping reported GNPA at about 4% while retail GNPA stood at 3.49%. The company took security over this exposure in Q1 FY27, parked it as an asset held for sale, and expects recovery within 6–8 months.
  • Regional asset-quality stress. North India showed GNPA of 6.5% and East India 4.5%, well above South India at 3.0% and West India at 3.9%, indicating uneven portfolio quality across geographies.
  • Elevated funding costs relative to prior periods. Although costs declined, the incremental borrowing cost in Q1 was 9%, and the company continues to work on reducing it further.
  • Fraud incidents. The company classified 8 fraud incidents on account of misappropriation and forgery totalling ₹91.80 lakhs during the quarter, for which it provided 100% provision.
  • Macroeconomic uncertainty. Management flagged evolving macroeconomic conditions, including war-related impacts on the Indian economy, as a factor being closely monitored against its growth ambitions.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Credit rating upgrade. CRISIL upgraded the company to AA− stable, which management described as external validation of improved asset quality, governance and funding profile, and which is expected to lower borrowing costs — about 50 bps reduction already visible, with another ₹0.40–0.50 expected in upcoming deals.
  • Asset quality improvement. GNPA fell to 3.94%, down about 182 bps year-on-year, aided by the ARC transaction and improved collections. The 14-month-on-book portfolio showed GNPA of only 1%, versus 3–3.5% a year earlier.
  • Portfolio diversification. Commercial vehicle and used car books grew around 40% year-on-year; construction equipment grew 75%. Used car rose to 8.5% of AUM from 2% a year earlier. Management aims to reduce the two-wheeler share to about 30% of the book over 3–4 years, with 70% from car, CV, CE and potentially tractors.
  • Public deposit franchise. Public deposits crossed the ₹100 crore milestone in July 2026, providing a stable, granular funding base at roughly 150 bps below borrowing cost.
  • Group cross-sell. About 15–20% of incremental sourcing comes from group entities, with a target of 40%. Branch activation across Muthoot FinCorp is 25–30%, targeted at 50–60% by year-end.
  • AI and technology deployment. AI bots handle the entire X-bucket collection, with 55% resolution in the month; AI is also used for welcome calling, audit and compliance, and complaint segregation.
  • Securitization activity. The company completed its third securitization of FY27 on August 19, 2026, raising ₹29.80 crores by assigning vehicle-finance receivables of ₹33.12 crores, entirely from the non-priority sector.
  • New NCD issuance. The company approved issuance of senior, secured, rated, listed, redeemable, taxable, transferable NCDs up to ₹150 crores on a private placement basis — base issue ₹75 crores with a green shoe of ₹75 crores, 24-month tenure, 9.25% coupon payable quarterly, bullet principal repayment, minimum asset coverage of 1.1 times, and default interest of 2% p.a. over the coupon.

3. Key Risks

  • Credit concentration in two-wheelers. Two-wheelers remain about 69% of the portfolio, and the segment contributes the bulk of GNPA, with retail GNPA at 3.49%.
  • Provisioning and ECL model revision. PCR stands at 50%. The company maintained an additional management overlay of ₹2,047 lakhs as of June 30, 2026, plus a proactive buffer of ₹250 lakhs for the upcoming ECL model review. An ECL model revision is due within about four weeks of the call, and LGD assumptions (currently 50% for used car and CV) may be revised.
  • ARC recovery dependence. The third ARC transaction with Prasaditya ARC involved sale of ₹203.01 crores principal outstanding (₹119.83 crores GNPA plus ₹83.18 crores written-off loans) for ₹93.20 crores, an average valuation of 45.91%, with Security Receipts of ₹81.01 crores recognized. Recovery outcomes depend on future collections; if recoveries do not materialize, mark-to-market valuation of SR investments could require P&L hits.
  • Geographic concentration risk. North and East India show materially higher GNPA than other regions.
  • Capital adequacy and leverage. CRAR stood at 22.07% and gearing at 4.88x. Management indicated comfort up to 6x, with equity raising under discussion with 2–3 investors and possible meetings in Q2.
  • Regulatory and compliance exposure. The company operates under RBI guidelines on securitization, priority sector classification, LCR maintenance (maintained at 125–130% against the 100% requirement), and related-party transaction scrutiny.

4. Management Guidance versus Observed Performance

  • AUM guidance. Management reiterated the FY27 AUM projection of ₹4,000–4,200 crores and the FY28–29 objective of ₹10,000 crores. Observed Q1 FY27 AUM was ₹3,297 crores, down 1.60% quarter-on-quarter, with the balance sheet at ₹4,079 crores (up 1% QoQ). Management stated the current-year projection remains intact.
  • ROA guidance. A pre-tax ROA of about 2.5% was guided for FY27. Observed Q1 FY27 ROA was 1.35%. Management stated it is on track, expecting improvement as business grows from Q2 onward.
  • GNPA/NNPA guidance. Management guided retail GNPA to remain sub-4% through FY27 and NNPA sub-2%, with no change in ECL policy or PCR. Observed Q1 FY27 GNPA was 3.94% (retail 3.49%) and NNPA 2.36%.
  • Funding cost guidance. Management guided a further ₹0.40–0.50 reduction in borrowing cost in upcoming deals following the rating upgrade. Observed Q1 FY27 borrowing cost was 9.6%, with bank loan cost down from 10.73% to 9.88% year-on-year and PTC cost down from 10.05% to 9.87%.
  • Used car profitability. Management expects used car to break even this financial year, with an ROA tree of around 1–1.5% and opex targeted at 4% versus the current 6–6.5%. Productivity per person rose from ₹13–14 lakhs to over ₹20 lakhs, targeted at ₹30–35 lakhs.
  • Equity raising. Management indicated discussions with 2–3 investors, with data sharing completed and meetings expected in Q2, but no confirmed timeline or valuation.
  • Corporate loan recovery. Management expects to sell the secured property within 6–8 months and recover the corporate loan exposure during the year.
  • Governance updates. The Board appointed Ms. Manimekhalai A as Non-Executive Independent Director effective July 16, 2026, re-appointed Mrs. Shirley Thomas for a second five-year term effective November 25, 2026, and appointed M/s. K. Venkatachalam Aiyer & Co. as Tax Auditor for FY2026-27. All AGM resolutions dated July 16, 2026 were approved by shareholders at the 32nd AGM held on August 31, 2026.
