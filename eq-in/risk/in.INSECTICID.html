Insecticides (India) Limited — Q1 FY27 Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Unusual start to the agricultural season: Channel activity began in March, ahead of anticipated timelines. A delayed and uneven monsoon, combined with high temperatures across several regions, impacted sowing activity and delayed demand for crop protection products.
  • Financial performance in Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026): Revenue from operations declined 12% year-on-year, from INR691 crores to INR612 crores. Gross profit decreased 4%, from INR202 crores to INR193.16 crores, although gross profit percentage rose from 29% to 31.6%. EBITDA declined around 20%, with margin falling from 12.2% to 11.1%. PAT margin fell from 8.4% to 7.2%.
  • Volume decline: Volume degrowth was about 13% in Q1, with a small value growth of about 2%.
  • Segment mix shift: B2C sales were 64% of the quarter (versus 75% last year), B2B was 34% (versus 23%), and exports 2%. Revenue from patented products fell from INR97 crores to INR70 crores, mainly due to reduced sales of Hachiman. Combination product contribution fell from INR134 crores to INR98 crores.
  • Raw material and supply chain pressure: Crude and petroleum-linked raw material costs remained an area of pressure. There was an unexpected increase in prices of metal, plastic, solvents, emulsifiers, and some technicals. Supply chain disruption from China, container scarcity, and higher freight added to the strain. Solvent prices showed week-to-week and day-to-day fluctuations of 25–30%, with some solvents doubling.
  • Pricing rollbacks: A price hike was taken in March, two in April, and one attempted in May, but the May hike had to be rolled back due to delayed market and weak network sentiment; rollbacks continued until July.
  • Elevated inventory: June inventory was slightly elevated due to the slower start of the agricultural season.
  • Regional gaps: Rice sowing was delayed in parts of South India where dams were not full; about 40% of rice remained to be sown in Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu. Some dryness persisted in MP and eastern states. Cotton and maize crops declined, though oilseeds, pulses, groundnut, and chilli improved.
  • Kaeros margin drag: Kaeros Research currently operates with tight, single-digit margins; about 100 people were hired in Q1, raising salary expenses at this early stage.
  • Sales return risk: Management acknowledged the possibility of sales returns, though it expects to reduce returns to at least half of the previous year’s level, having placed stock cautiously and diversified placements across products.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Monsoon recovery: August rains were good across the country, reservoir levels improved, and crops were healthy. Management expects the agriculture cycle to have been largely deferred rather than lost, with stronger execution over the remaining three quarters and positive impact visible from Q2.
  • Premiumization: Premium products (Maharatna and Focus Maharatna) contributed around 64% of B2C in Q1, up from 58% last year. The target is to reach 70% in three to four years. Five years ago, premium contribution was 48%; last fiscal it was over 62%.
  • New product launches: GRANUVIA and Spinoace, launched in collaboration with Corteva Agriscience, were introduced through extensive dealer, distributor, retailer, and farmer engagement. Granuvia crossed about INR5 crores in Q1; Spinoace generated about INR25 lakhs as its launch was near July end. Management expects Granuvia to touch about INR20 crores and Spinoace INR10 crores-plus in FY27, totaling INR30–35 crores gross (about INR25 crores net).
  • In-licensing growth: Revenue from in-licensing products rose from around INR36 crores in Q1 last year to around INR46 crores this year.
  • Farmer engagement: Over 3,600 farmer meetings, 600 field days, 1,400 demonstrations, and more than 15,000 farmer visits were conducted in Q1, supported by over 8,500 distributors and a 70,000-strong retail network.
  • IIL Crop Solutions: The program demonstrated complete crop solutions and economics in farmer fields, with strong acceptance and demonstrated ROI from 36 existing plots. Plans are to double the number of plots and expand across 14 states and 4 crops (rice, cotton, chili, soybean).
  • Kaeros Research: More than 40 products already commercialized; portfolio and distribution footprint expanding, with B2B and pack-to-pack opportunities. Target is to grow at a CAGR of 100% initially, moderating to 50–60%, as a second growth platform.
  • International business: Momentum continued with registrations, partnerships, and customer opportunities across Latin America, Europe, and ASEAN. Technicals sales are catching up with good global acceptance, alongside white labelling.
  • R&D and partnerships: Continued work with Corteva, Nissan, and OAT Agrio on differentiated technologies and molecules; the OAT Agrio new molecule discovery is progressing. More than 30 products are in the pipeline for launch over the next two years, including mixtures and new AIs.
  • Capacity expansion: The Chopanki expansion is complete; Dahej is over 70% commercialized with the remainder to be commercialized soon. The Sotanala project (total investment roughly INR200 crores; about INR50 crores for formulations and INR150 crores for technicals; about INR70 crores invested so far) is progressing, with the formulation facility expected to commence around April–May next year and technical production by Diwali, subject to schedule. Udhampur investments to separate herbicide and insecticide plants are to be completed within the year.
  • Capex normalization: Once current projects are completed, annual capex is expected to normalize around INR30–40 crores of maintenance capex, allowing focus on utilization, cash generation, and returns.
  • Efficiency initiatives: Renewable initiatives (solar, wind, solid fuel) are expected to lower electricity and fuel consumption, with benefits from Q3 (September–October). AI and advanced technologies are expected to provide additional efficiency gains.
  • Doubling ambition: Management aims to double the business in four to five years (previously every three to four years).
  • Dahej potential: At least INR200 crores is seen as possible to be added into the technical production plant, with formulation also supporting.

3. Key Risks

  • Monsoon and weather dependence: Delayed and uneven monsoon and high temperatures materially affected Q1; residual dryness in MP and eastern states and delayed rice sowing in South India remain concerns.
  • Raw material volatility: High and unpredictable fluctuations in solvents, plastics, HDPE, emulsifiers, and technicals make cost planning difficult; management said it is difficult to commit to the contribution from increased ingredient prices.
  • Pricing power constraints: Weak market sentiment limited the ability to pass on cost increases; extraordinary hikes could not be taken, and some hikes were rolled back.
  • Sales return risk: The possibility of sales returns exists, though management expects to halve last year’s level.
  • Competitive pressure: Competitive products entering the market can pressure molecule pricing, varying product-to-product.
  • Working capital: Elevated June inventory requires focus on inventory turns, collections, and alignment between placement and demand; the objective is to reduce the working capital cycle as the season normalizes.
  • Project execution: Sotanala timelines (formulation around April–May, technical by Diwali) are subject to project schedule.
  • Kaeros profitability: Early-stage expenses are high and margins are expected to remain tight and single-digit at present.
  • Governance-related dissent: At the 29th AGM held August 12, 2026, the special resolution for approval of the Insecticides (India) Limited Employees Stock Purchase Scheme 2026 passed with 88.55% votes in favour and 11.45% against; public institutions voted 93.26% against. The special resolution appointing Shri Sanskar Aggarwal as Whole-time Director passed with 98.97% in favour and 1.03% against, with public institutions voting 8.42% against.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Growth guidance vs. Q1 outcome: Management had aggressive growth targets for the company’s 25th year, but Q1 delivered a 12% revenue decline and 13% volume degrowth. Management stated it would review and give revised targets after Q2, noting the first half contributes more than 60% of total sales.
  • Herbicide growth expectation vs. performance: Management had expected healthy herbicide growth after prior-year returns and pricing pressure. In Q1, many products touched last year’s sales but growth was delayed; management expects the growth to register in Q2, with the majority of herbicides expected to cross last year’s achievements, though the year’s dream figures were not touched.
  • Premium mix guidance vs. delivery: The stated target is 70% premium contribution in three to four years; Q1 achieved 64%, and last fiscal achieved over 62%, showing progress toward the target.
  • New product guidance vs. early performance: Granuvia and Spinoace were launched with multiyear opportunity expectations; Q1 sales were INR5.50 crores from the two new products, with FY27 guidance of INR30–35 crores gross (about INR25 crores net).
  • Prior-year launches vs. performance: Products launched last year generated INR1.67 crores in Q1 last year versus INR2.29 crores this year.
  • Kaeros guidance vs. performance: Target of 100% CAGR initially; management stated Kaeros is on the path of growing at a CAGR of 100%, with margins currently tight and single-digit.
  • Working capital guidance: Management had previously suggested a working capital cycle of approximately 120 days could potentially be achieved during FY27; in Q1, inventory was elevated, and management said the large June inventory is already tumbling down, with substantial improvement expected in the current quarter.
  • Capex guidance: Management guided that annual capex would normalize around INR30–40 crores of maintenance capex once current projects complete, and that operating leverage and efficiency initiatives would support improvement in ROCE and ROE.
  • Dividend/buyback: An interim dividend of INR2 per equity share (face value INR10) paid during the year was confirmed as the final dividend for FY2025-26 at the AGM. On buyback, management said the company typically buys back every two to three years, not every year, and a decision would be taken after completing the current quarter.
  • AGM outcomes: All six resolutions transacted at the 29th AGM on August 12, 2026 were passed with requisite majority, including adoption of audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY2025-26, confirmation of the interim dividend as final dividend, re-appointment of Shri Anil Kumar Goyal as director, ratification of cost auditors’ remuneration for 2026-27, appointment of Shri Sanskar Aggarwal as Whole-time Director, and approval of the Employees Stock Purchase Scheme 2026.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from company-specific operational stress in Q4FY23 — high-cost inventory provisions, M2M losses, margin compression, and higher opex — to external/cyclical headwinds by mid-2024, including erratic rainfall, global inventory overhang, low prices, regulatory issues, and soda ash oversupply. Persistent themes were inventory/pricing pressure and reliance on new products, but the constructive Accumulate stance with FY24 revenue guidance softened to a not-rated/neutral view.

Fears that came true

  • The high-cost inventory/provisioning fear remained relevant: the last report still cited higher-than-normal global inventory and low prices, and the stock’s last actual return was -8.5%.
  • The passive-demand and export-fluctuation risk materialized in the final report’s unfavorable pricing environment and cautious customer buying behavior, corresponding with the negative actual return.
  • The gross-margin-compression risk persisted, as soda ash oversupply/import pressures and low prices in the final report show continued pricing pressure on margins.
