Alicon Castalloy — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost volatility. Q1 FY27 (quarter ended 30 June 2026) saw significant volatility in input costs, driven by geopolitical developments in the Middle East. Metal prices, gas prices, tooling costs and other inputs moved sharply, creating pressure across the automotive supply chain. Management stated Alicon was not an exception, and that while top-line performance was strong, the inflationary environment created substantial pressure on margins and profitability.

Margin compression. EBITDA margin for the quarter was 9.5% (EBITDA of Rs. 55 crore), against 11.4% for the prior year. The CFO noted that on an adjusted basis — removing the impact of aluminium — the margin would be 11.4%. Profitability was affected by higher aluminium and alloy costs as well as increases in employee, energy, logistics and other operating costs.

European operations in transition. The international business had a softer quarter. Certain mature programs in Europe reached end of life, and the next set of programs had not yet ramped to meaningful volumes. The decline in legacy business was not yet fully offset by new program launches. Management indicated the softer trend in Europe may continue for one or more quarters, though not significantly lower than already seen, with a reversal expected from the last quarter of the financial year and a return to normal in the next year.

Cost recovery timing. Raw material inflation is contractually pass-through but with a timing lag. Manufacturing cost increases (labour and overheads) are being discussed with customers for price revisions; approvals were secured from some customers, with discussions ongoing and further closures expected during the current quarter. Management noted OEMs do not grant all corrections immediately.

Capacity constraints. Capacity utilisation reached close to 100% in the quarter (more than 90% for sure), which is driving the need for new facilities.

Historical program delays. Management acknowledged that prior order book commitments had not materialised as expected. EV volumes did not grow as anticipated, and projects such as JLR saw volumes decline or SOP extensions. High-technology EV programs carried much longer development periods (e.g., 2–3 years for an e-Axle developed for a large UK OEM) than conventional products.

Competitive intensity. Aluminium die casting capacity in the country has increased substantially. Management stated the pricing pressure dynamic has existed for 20–30 years and that Alicon is not competing on price where it has technology differentiation, while acknowledging the need to continuously upgrade process technology, R&D and tooling know-how.

Return ratios. ROCE was 10.7% in the last financial year. Management attributed part of the drag to businesses where investments were made but returns had not yet begun (e.g., JLR, where investments were made but SOP was delayed), and stated ROCE is on an increasing trend.

One-time items. An exceptional item of Rs. 256.63 lakh was recognised in the June 2025 quarter pertaining to settlement of a past legal claim related to sales commission, described as a one-time expense with no ongoing operational impact. Separately, the company is assessing the impact of Labour Code Rules notified by the Central Government on 9 May 2026, following the earlier notification of the four Labour Codes on 21 November 2025; an impact of Rs. 500.09 lakh related to gratuity and compensated absences had been recorded under exceptional items for the quarter and period ended 31 December 2025.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong domestic market. The Indian automotive industry remained structurally strong in Q1 FY27. Passenger vehicles recorded their highest ever first quarter sales at approximately 1.57 million units, growing around 11.3%; two-wheelers grew 21%; commercial vehicles grew 19.5%.

Outperformance versus the market. Alicon registered year-on-year growth of approximately 37.7% on a consolidated basis and 43.6% on a stand-alone basis. Adjusting for material inflation, underlying volume growth was approximately 17.5% consolidated and 22% stand-alone. The commercial vehicle segment grew approximately 26%, supported by conversion of recently acquired businesses into mass production.

Technology transition positioning. Management expects the future automotive landscape to combine ICE, hybrid, electric and other technologies. The hybrid segment is assessed to potentially grow at approximately 25–30% CAGR over the next several years, while EVs could grow at approximately 20–22% CAGR over four to five years. Alicon supplies the largest hybrid vehicle manufacturing company in India as a single source supplier, and its EV portfolio includes electric motor housings, e-axle housings, battery housings and inverter housings.

Order book. The executable order book stands at approximately Rs. 8,450 crore as on 30 June 2026, representing executable orders over six years from 2026 to 2031, excluding programs already part of revenue prior to FY26. Within this, non-automotive visibility is about 2% (roughly Rs. 126 crore); electric vehicles around 16%; hybrid around 12%; structural components around 10%; and exports approximately 40% of new business (of which around 30% of the total pie is deemed exports).

New business wins. During the quarter, Alicon acquired businesses across automotive and non-automotive segments with potential to generate more than Rs. 450 crore of revenue over the next five years. It gained entry into two large Indian passenger vehicle and commercial vehicle OEMs, with visibility of approximately Rs. 850 crore of business over the next five years from these two customers. Management expects order bookings in the rest of the year at a similar pattern to the prior quarter.

New facility at Shikrapur. The Board approved a new plant for GDC, LPDC and machining with a capital outlay of Rs. 1,255 million (approximately Rs. 125 crore), on a 10-year lease of a ready-built industrial premises of 1,30,000 sq. ft. near existing facilities at Shikrapur, Pune, leased from M/s. Vijay Logistics Ltd (intimation dated 31 July 2026). Possession is expected 1 September; SOP targeted by March 2027. The facility is expected to generate approximately Rs. 500 crore of annual revenue over four to five years, with tonnage starting at 3,000 tons and going up to 7,000 tons. All business for this factory is already committed orders. The investment is to be phased over two to three years, funded primarily through internal accruals supplemented by borrowings.

Non-automotive expansion. A separate non-automotive sales and marketing vertical has been created. Wins cited include an HVAC application for data server cooling from a global company, starting in India with planned expansion to Europe and Mexico; defence segment business; and a first-ever tractor segment win for a cylinder head for the world’s largest tractor manufacturer, described as the first aluminium solution replacing traditional cast iron in that application.

JLR EV ramp. JLR EV dispatches and consumption have started, with supply expected at 600 sets per week, and an indication from JLR of further increase from January 2027. The contribution was marginal in Q1, with a peak expected by the third quarter of the calendar year.

Value addition and mix shift. Value addition grew approximately 17.6% in the quarter. Machining share of passenger vehicle tonnage is currently around 65–70%, and management expects this ratio to increase with planned in-house machining investments. Alicon has intentionally reduced its two-wheeler market share by 1–2% to free capacity for higher-value products.

Global customer recognition. Relationships with large global commercial vehicle OEMs continue to grow, with multiple programs acquired regularly, and inquiries are being received from global OEMs with whom Alicon has not worked before.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued input cost volatility from geopolitical developments, metal, gas and tooling prices.
  • Timing lag and partial recovery risk on pass-through of raw material and manufacturing cost inflation; OEM price corrections may be delayed or incomplete.
  • European operations transition risk: legacy program decline may not be offset by new program ramp-ups on the expected timeline.
  • Execution risk on the new Shikrapur facility and other planned investments, including SOP timing and utilisation ramp.
  • Concentration of growth expectations in the order book; management acknowledged past order book commitments had not materialised meaningfully due to EV volume shortfalls and SOP extensions.
  • Capacity constraints at near-full utilisation until new capacity comes online.
  • Competitive intensity from expanded aluminium die casting capacity in India.
  • ROCE currently low (10.7% last financial year), with returns dependent on turnaround of investments where SOP was delayed.
  • Development cost intensity: intangible development spend of roughly Rs. 18–20 crore last year, at Rs. 3–5 crore per quarter, on critical parts, special alloys and dedicated teams, with some alloys not reusable.
  • Labour Code impact assessment ongoing following notification of Rules on 9 May 2026.

4. Management Guidance versus Observed Performance

Top-line guidance. In the prior quarter, management guided to modest growth of about 10%. In the Q1 FY27 call, the CEO revised the FY27 outlook to approximately 12% to 15% growth, neutralising the material (aluminium) effect. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: reported growth of 37.7% consolidated and 43.6% stand-alone, with underlying volume growth of approximately 17.5% consolidated and 22% stand-alone. The CEO confirmed the 17.5% figure as the relevant underlying growth reference for the one-year and three-year outlook. Management stated there is no clear reason for a slowdown as of now, while noting the company is not out of complete volatility and will watch quarter by quarter.

Margin guidance. The CFO stated the company expects at least a 1% improvement in EBITDA margins for FY27, alongside the 12–15% growth. Observed Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 9.5%, against 11.4% for the prior year, with the CFO noting an adjusted margin of 11.4% for the quarter when removing the aluminium impact. Management said margin improvement will come from customer recovery and internal productivity measures, and that improvements will not necessarily happen in a straight line each quarter. On a two-to-three-year view, management said it will progressively look for improving margins, with the CEO stating there is a possibility of reaching the 14–15% mark but declining to give a specific timeline.

Order book and revenue ambition. The executable order book is approximately Rs. 8,450 crore over six years from 2026 to 2031. In response to an analyst question, management indicated the plan is to almost double revenues by 2030, with the Rs. 8,450 crore order book including recent additions as well as businesses added over the last three to four years. Management stated further investments will be announced, with more capacity needed beyond the 20% addition represented by the new plant.

Capital expenditure. Q1 FY27 capex was approximately Rs. 40 crore; the full-year plan is approximately Rs. 150 crore, including around Rs. 70 crore of the Rs. 125 crore earmarked for the new manufacturing facility. The new facility investment is to be phased over two to three years rather than deployed upfront, funded primarily through internal accruals supplemented by borrowings as required.

Working capital and leverage. FY27 priorities stated by the CFO: support 8% to 10% underlying top-line growth while maintaining quality; progressively improve margins through customer recovery and internal productivity; deploy capital in line with customer demand while maintaining discipline around leverage and cash flow; and improve working capital efficiency so a greater proportion of operating performance converts into cash.

Financial performance versus prior periods. Total income was Rs. 579.1 crore, up 37% year-on-year and 17% sequentially, crossing Rs. 500 crore in quarterly revenue for the first time. EBITDA was Rs. 55.3 crore, up 8% year-on-year and 20% sequentially. PBT (pre-exceptional) was Rs. 18.3 crore, up 20% year-on-year (the press release headline cited PBT higher by 45% year-on-year to Rs. 18.3 crore; the CEO referenced PBT of Rs. 18 crore growing 45% year-on-year). PAT was Rs. 11.4 crore, up 23% year-on-year and 44% sequentially. Stand-alone EPS was Rs. 10.82 basic; consolidated EPS was Rs. 7.02 basic.

Tonnage. Consolidated tonnage in Q1 FY27 was 9,124 tons, of which only 150–200 tons came from Europe. This was close to 1% higher than the prior quarter, while sales growth was much higher, reflecting a shift to higher value-addition and higher revenue parts. FY26 total tonnage was 34,000 tons.

Dividend and governance. The Board recommended a final dividend of Rs. 3 per equity share of Rs. 5 each (60%) for FY 2025-26; together with the interim dividend of Rs. 2 (40%), total dividend for FY 2025-26 would be Rs. 5 per share (100%), subject to shareholder declaration. Book closure is 22–28 September 2026, and the 36th AGM is convened for 28 September 2026 via video conferencing. Mr. Anantakrishnan Krishna was appointed Additional Independent Director and Chairman of the Audit Committee; Mr. Ajay S. Patil stepped down as Director and Chairman of the Audit Committee effective close of business on 13 August 2026. A postal ballot approved the appointment of Mr. Ishaan Rai as Non-Executive Non-Independent Director.
