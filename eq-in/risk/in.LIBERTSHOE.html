LIBERTSHOE: Recent Corporate Announcements Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

The company faces volatility in raw material prices across leather, synthetic materials, rubber, EVA, and chemicals. Consumer demand has moderated, and competition is intense from both branded and digital-first players. Compliance costs are rising due to BIS standards and regulatory changes, while wage inflation is pushing up employee benefit costs.

On the regulatory front, SEBI issued an administrative warning and advisory dated 21 July 2026 regarding non-compliance with Regulation 31A of the SEBI LODR Regulations, 2015. The matter concerns reclassification requests dated 23 March 2024 from two promoter shareholders—Shri Arpan Gupta (in his individual capacity and as Karta of Dinesh Kumar Gupta HUF)—seeking reclassification from “Promoter and Promoter Group” to “Public” category.

SEBI observed that while the company disclosed initial consideration of the requests on 30 March 2024, the Board’s conclusive views formed at the meeting held on 29 May 2024 were not disclosed, and no reference was made to the requests in that outcome disclosure. SEBI further observed that the Board, having formed the view that the promoters did not satisfy reclassification conditions, did not place the requests before shareholders in a general meeting as required under Regulation 31A(3)(a)(ii), and instead conveyed rejection to the promoters via email dated 7 June 2024. SEBI stated that the provision does not empower the Board to decline to place such a request before shareholders.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Domestic consumption is growing, driven by a large young population and rising disposable incomes. GST rationalization has reduced tax on footwear priced up to ₹2,500 from 12% to 5%. E-commerce and quick-commerce channels are expanding, premiumisation trends are evident, and export opportunities are arising from the India-EU Free Trade Agreement.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material price volatility (leather, synthetics, rubber, EVA, chemicals)
  • Demand moderation and intense competition from branded and digital-first players
  • Rising compliance costs from BIS standards and regulatory changes
  • Wage inflation increasing employee benefit costs
  • Regulatory risk: SEBI has warned the company and advised corrective action, stating that failure to improve compliance standards may lead to enforcement action under the SEBI Act, 1992 and rules thereunder
  • Governance risk: the reclassification requests must now be placed before shareholders along with the Board’s views, and processed per the Regulation 31A framework

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Management remains optimistic about achieving steady revenue growth, improved profit margins, and better ROCE through cost optimization, brand expansion, digital transformation, and manufacturing modernization.

Observed performance for FY 2025-26 shows revenue from operations grew 9.55% to ₹73,999.11 lakh, while Net Profit declined 20.49% to ₹1,118.95 lakh due to rising input and employee costs. The Board did not recommend a dividend for FY 2025-26, opting to reinvest profits as growth capital. Long-term banking facilities hold a CARE BBB+ rating with a stable outlook.

For Q1 ended 30 June 2026, unaudited results show revenue from operations of ₹16,985.67 lakh and net profit of ₹68.37 lakh.

5. Leadership and Governance Developments

Sh. Shammi Bansal resigned as Executive Director and Chairman of the CSR Committee effective 10 August 2026, and was appointed as President. Sh. Raman Bansal was appointed as Additional Director/Executive Director for 3 years effective 10 August 2026. Sh. Anand Das Mundhra and Sh. Piyush Dixit were re-appointed as Independent Directors for a second term of 3 years from 11 August 2026 to 10 August 2029. Sh. Anupam Bansal was appointed as Chairman of the CSR Committee effective 10 August 2026. The 40th AGM is scheduled for 29 September 2026, with book closure from 23 September to 29 September 2026.

The company stated it has submitted all relevant facts, records, and explanations to Stock Exchanges/SEBI, remains committed to corporate governance and regulatory compliance, and is examining SEBI’s communication in consultation with legal advisors. The company noted that the communication is in the nature of regulatory observations and does not impose any monetary penalty or enforcement action. SEBI has advised the company to place the communication and corrective steps before its Board and disseminate a copy on the stock exchanges as mandated under Regulation 30 of LODR Regulations.
