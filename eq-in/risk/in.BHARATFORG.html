Bharat Forge: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

The company operates against a backdrop of uneven demand across key markets. European commercial vehicle weakness, softer passenger vehicle exports, and softening domestic CV demand in the first half of FY25 amid slower infrastructure capex around elections have weighed on performance. On the export side, U.S. tariffs and North American CV destocking have acted as headwinds, while Middle East conflict has introduced supply-chain and commodity-price volatility.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

India’s position among the fastest-growing large economies, with real GDP growth of 7.7% in FY26, provides a supportive demand environment. Strong government focus on manufacturing and localisation, PLI schemes across strategic sectors, and increasing integration of India into global manufacturing supply chains support the auto-component industry, which reached US$ 85.9 billion in FY26. Rising global military expenditure and Indian defence spending create opportunities in defence, supported by an executable defence order book of INR 111,967 million. New sectors such as aerospace, semiconductors, and data centres are identified as growth areas.

On the strategic front, Kalyani Strategic Systems Ltd. (KSSL), a wholly owned subsidiary, signed a Memorandum of Understanding with FN Herstal on September 3, 2026, to evaluate establishing a dedicated production centre in India and a Strategic Alliance / Joint Venture covering small arms, integrated weapon systems, and counter-UAS solutions. The parties will jointly explore and assess the feasibility of potential cooperation projects aimed primarily at meeting Indian market requirements, supporting defence modernisation, indigenous capabilities, and the vision of Aatmanirbhar Bharat.

The Board also approved incorporation of a subsidiary (direct/indirect) in Malaysia for activities in the semiconductor and allied areas, subject to necessary approvals.

Key Risks

The company is pursuing a fund raise of up to ₹25,000 million through equity shares, debt, or other convertible securities via further public offer, rights issue, ADRs/GDRs/FCCBs, qualified institutions placement, preferential issue, or other permitted methods. The Board has delegated powers to the Investment Committee – Strategic Business to decide structure, form, issuance, price, timing, and terms. A Postal Ballot Notice seeking shareholder approval by Special Resolution was issued, with e-voting from August 13, 2026 to September 11, 2026, and the cut-off date of August 7, 2026. Proceeds are intended for capital expenditure including greenfield facilities and expansion of current manufacturing facilities, funding organic growth opportunities, repayment or prepayment of existing debt, investment in subsidiaries for capital expenditure and/or research and development, and general corporate purposes.

The proposed issuance carries execution and market risks typical of capital raising, including pricing determined by the Board based on prevailing market conditions, and the requirement for regulatory and statutory approvals. No change in control or management is intended or expected pursuant to the issuance.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Management has articulated ESG milestones including renewable energy to power 80% of operations by 2030, carbon neutrality by 2045, water positive by 2030 with a 40% reduction in water intensity, zero waste to landfill by 2030, a 60% reduction in energy intensity by 2030, and a 75% reduction in LTIFR by 2030.

Observed performance for FY26 includes consolidated revenue from operations of INR 168,117 million, Adjusted EBITDA of INR 29,267 million, PAT of INR 10,894 million, an executable defence order book of INR 111,967 million, and plans for INR 18,000 million in growth capex across businesses. The company operates 36 manufacturing facilities globally across 3 continents and 6 countries.

The Board recommended a Final Dividend of ₹6.5 per equity share of ₹2 each (325%) for FY 2025-26, subject to shareholder approval at the AGM on August 11, 2026, with a record date of July 3, 2026 and dividend payout date of August 14, 2026.

Management intends to engage in discussions with prospective investors on September 9, 2026, and participate in a meeting with certain institutional investors on a group basis in Singapore on September 8, 2026, with no unpublished price sensitive information proposed to be shared.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from broad market-level caution about a potential Nifty top and near-term consolidation in 2021 to a granular, company-specific bull case centered on defense order momentum, naval modernization, export restructuring, and capacity expansion by 2026. While confidence in the intermediate growth trajectory persisted, near-term concerns shifted from macro market risk to company execution challenges, with the latest report acknowledging slower new vertical growth and weaker operating leverage despite a Buy call.

  • Slower-than-anticipated growth in new business verticals materialized as a concrete risk, with the stock posting a negative -6.6% actual return on the most recent report.
  • Less-than-anticipated gains from operating leverage were confirmed by the negative -6.6% actual return on the latest assessment, validating the broker’s flagged concern.

Optimism that failed

  • Export recovery and management’s guidance for stronger H2FY27, underpinned by ~₹1,800 crore capex and restructuring, have not yet translated into positive price action, with recent Buy calls predicting +15.7% and +14.7% gains but resulting in -7.3% and -5.2% actual returns.
