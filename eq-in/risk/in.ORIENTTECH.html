ORIENTTECH — Recent Corporate Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Supply chain and semiconductor shortage. Management stated that the industry-wide semiconductor shortage and supply chain pressures were expected to persist throughout FY27, and that these headwinds weighed on the top line through the second half of FY26. During Q1 FY27, supply-side conditions remained stable, but pricing continued to be competitive across key segments. Management described FY27 as a year of steady sequential improvement rather than a sharp one-quarter turnaround.

Competitive pricing in infrastructure. Infrastructure margins were described as highly competitive. Management said the company is selectively picking up deals and consciously avoiding large-ticket engagements where delivery timelines carry penalty risk. In response to an investor question about a competitor winning a much larger order, management said it took a conscious call not to go aggressive on that big-ticket item because pricing was changing weekly and delivery was not happening in 14–16 weeks, and it was not prepared to accept penalty exposure.

Margin pressure and cost escalation. The investor presentation lists margin pressure from hardware-led engagements and cost escalation, working-capital intensity due to receivable cycles and project billing, rapid technology obsolescence in AI, cloud and cybersecurity, supply chain and geopolitical risks affecting hardware availability and cost, cybersecurity and data risks, government/PSU execution cycles affecting revenue timing, and talent availability and retention for specialised skills.

Macro and external risks. The source material cites significant changes in the economic environment in India and overseas, tax laws, inflation, and litigation as material headwinds. Management also referred to challenges from the U.S. side, noting that negative messages keep coming from there.

Contingent liability. A liability of Rs. 439.92 lakhs relating to invoices raised by a vendor for committed cloud infrastructure costs was not recognised, as management assessed it did not meet recognition criteria pending commercial discussions; it was disclosed as a contingent liability. Management attributed the situation to a couple of telecom customers withdrawing services at the last minute, requiring reversal of billing and a saving plan purchased from AWS that ended on 7 August. Management said it does not expect such events henceforth and will be more careful.

IPO capex deployment lag. An investor noted that capex utilisation was behind schedule, with about Rs. 45 crores deployed against roughly Rs. 80 crores budgeted. Management said everything has been utilised except DaaS, where the company has been selective in choosing customers after verifying creditworthiness, and that the remaining Rs. 35 crores is expected to be utilised over the next couple of quarters. The utilisation table shows Rs. 7,333.00 lakhs utilised and Rs. 3,459.60 lakhs unutilised as of 30 June 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order book. The order book stood at approximately Rs. 375.43 crores as of 12 August 2026, billable during FY27, comprising infrastructure deployment projects along with cloud and managed services contracts. Management said maximum billing will happen between Q3 and Q4. This compares with approximately Rs. 200 crores guided for the Q4 FY26 period on the previous call.

New contract wins. During the quarter, the company secured a Rs. 20 crores engagement with a leading public sector insurance company and a Rs. 24 crores cloud engagement with a leading general insurance company. On 24 July 2026, the company announced a purchase order from National Payments Corporation of India for supply of servers, with an estimated value of approximately Rs. 76,20,00,000 (Rs. 76.20 crores), exclusive of GST, to be executed within 18 weeks, with seven years of warranty support backed by the OEM.

Annuity and managed services transition. Annuity-based income was 23% of revenue in Q1 FY27, with the balance project-based. Management said its constant effort is to increase annuity to a maximum of 51%, but expects this to take another three years because SOC, NOC and managed services revenue will take time to materialise. The NOC and SOC centre at Turbhe, Navi Mumbai, commissioned after possession in Q3 FY26, remains central to this strategy; revenue from it is still a single-digit percentage. The company tied up with Securonix for SIEM operations for its SOC.

OHMS 2.0. The company strengthened its managed services proposition through OHMS 2.0, described as an outcome-driven model. Management explained that where OHMS 1.0 covered outsourcing, annual maintenance contracts and renewals, OHMS 2.0 provides outcome-based services — for example, broadband connectivity combined with SD-WAN-as-a-service and NOC monitoring, which management said saves customers about 60% of connectivity costs versus MPLS. Management said customer response has been positive, particularly among digital native enterprises.

DaaS. Management said customers increasingly prefer converting IT spend from capex to opex, citing banks and digital native enterprises, GCCs and MNCs adopting opex-based solutions in India, and sees good prospects for DaaS.

Cybersecurity. Cybersecurity was called out as a strategic growth area, with investment in the Turbhe SOC and the Securonix contract. The source material describes a USD 3.15 million, three-year contract with Securonix, an expanded mandate as Securonix’s exclusive Value-Added Distributor and joint go-to-market partner in India, plans to hire around 100 cybersecurity professionals, a target of 12% growth in customer footprint, and total investments reaching approximately USD 5 million.

Industry growth. The investor presentation cites CRISIL data showing the data centre industry in India growing at 30–35% CAGR and public cloud services at 22–28% CAGR through FY27. Management said it is aligned to both and expects data centre and cloud to grow exponentially. The presentation also cites growth in the DaaS leasing industry, IT hardware equipment, ITeS, and export IT services.

AI. Management said AI is helping the business by increasing productivity and is seen as a major revenue source for Orient, contrasting this with larger IT players whose man-hour-based revenue models are affected.

Acquisitions. The company acquired a 100% stake in Red Hut Innovation Technology (effective 1 November 2025) and 46% stakes in AIT Internet Services and Athena IT & Telecom (effective 1 December 2025). In Q1 FY27, Red Hut added Rs. 2.88 crores of top line with Rs. 50 lakhs profit before tax; Athena’s profitability was around Rs. 37 lakhs and AIT’s around Rs. 30 lakhs. The limited review report notes the subsidiary’s revenue of Rs. 288.81 lakhs and net profit after tax of Rs. 36.53 lakhs, and the group’s share of profit after tax of Rs. 31.06 lakhs from two associates.

International. Management said it is not looking at international business at least for this financial year, viewing India as a large and fast-growing market, though offices in Singapore and UAE exist to serve customers who took the company there. The investor presentation notes an aim to expand geographic footprint globally.

3. Key Risks

  • Supply chain and geopolitical risks affecting hardware availability and cost, with semiconductor shortage expected to persist in the near term.
  • Margin pressure from hardware-led engagements and cost escalation.
  • Working-capital intensity from receivable cycles and project billing.
  • Rapid technology obsolescence in AI, cloud and cybersecurity.
  • Cybersecurity and data risks.
  • Government/PSU execution cycles affecting revenue timing.
  • Talent availability and retention for specialised skills.
  • Competitive pricing pressure in infrastructure deals and the risk of penalty exposure on large orders with tight delivery timelines.
  • Contingent liability of Rs. 439.92 lakhs pending resolution with a vendor.
  • Macroeconomic, tax law, inflation and litigation risks in India and overseas.
  • Execution and scaling risk on the annuity transition, which management expects to take about three years to reach the 51% target.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Order book. Management had guided approximately Rs. 200 crores for the Q4 FY26 period on the previous call; the order book as of 12 August 2026 stood at approximately Rs. 375.43 crores, billable during FY27, with maximum billing expected in Q3 and Q4.

Sequential improvement. Management had indicated on Q3 and Q4 FY26 calls that supply chain pressures would persist through FY27 and that FY27 would be a year of steady sequential improvement. In Q1 FY27, revenue rose 9.70% QoQ to Rs. 201.92 crores from Rs. 184.07 crores; EBITDA rose 161% QoQ to Rs. 15.42 crores from Rs. 5.91 crores; EBITDA margin expanded 438 bps to 7.57% from 3.19%; profit for the quarter was Rs. 5.17 crores against a loss of Rs. 4.99 crores in Q4 FY26; EPS returned to positive Rs. 1.13 from negative Rs. 1.09.

Margin sustainability. Management declined to give forward-looking revenue or EBITDA guidance but said that because of operational efficiency and a margin-led approach, the current EBITDA margin is more or less sustainable, supported by a rising share of managed services, cybersecurity and cloud, which carry better margins than typical infrastructure revenue. Management said 5–6% is the minimum gross margin needed to survive, and that most deals are beyond that.

Revenue mix. Management said 80% of Q1 FY27 growth came from existing customers and 20% from new customers, a ratio it wants to maintain, with a focus on existing customers because acquiring new logos carries a different cost structure. Annuity was 23% of revenue, with a stated ambition of 51% over about three years.

Capex utilisation. Management said the remaining Rs. 35 crores of IPO proceeds is expected to be utilised over the next couple of quarters, with DaaS customer selection governed by credit assessment.

NOC/SOC contribution. Management said NOC and SOC revenue is still a single-digit percentage of total revenue, with more numbers expected in coming quarters as ARR materialises.

Leadership changes. Mr. Shailesh Mandani was appointed Chief Financial Officer effective 12 August 2026, having previously served as finance controller and led the IPO journey, finance transformation, ERP implementation and process automation. Ms. Sayli Munj was appointed Company Secretary and Compliance Officer effective 12 August 2026; Ms. Renuka Patel stepped down as Interim Company Secretary and Compliance Officer effective close of business on 11 August 2026 and returned to her role as Deputy Company Secretary. The 29th Annual General Meeting for the year ended 31 March 2026 is scheduled for 23 September 2026 by video conferencing.
