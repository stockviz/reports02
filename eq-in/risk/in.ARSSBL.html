ARSSBL — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Macro and market environment (Q1 FY27, April–June 2026) - The quarter was shaped by heightened geopolitical tensions, global macroeconomic uncertainty and elevated volatility. The West Asia crisis and sustained uncertainty around the Strait of Hormuz caused a temporary spike in crude oil prices, second-order pressure on input costs globally, and currency volatility. - Foreign outflows continued, totalling roughly ₹1.43 lakh crores across April–June, compared with ₹1.31 lakh crores in January–March 2026. - The monsoon began with a sharp 40% rainfall deficit in June due to El Niño; early July recovery narrowed the shortfall to around 20% below normal, supporting a catch-up in Kharif sowing.

Industry transition and regulatory recalibration - The broking industry is moving into a phase of more measured activity after several years of exceptional growth. While the investor base expanded and cash market turnover grew roughly 13% sequentially, trading activity in the derivatives segment moderated, indicating incremental participation is increasingly driven by long-term investing rather than trading-led activity. - SEBI measures in equity derivatives — upfront collection of option premium, tighter expiry-day margin treatment, enhanced intraday position monitoring, higher contract sizes and rationalised weekly expiries — are aimed at curbing excessive speculation. - The RBI’s revised capital market exposure framework, effective July 1, 2026, introduces a more principle-based approach to bank lending to capital market intermediaries, resulting in a significant increase in working capital requirements.

Company-specific challenge - An exceptional expense of ₹209.96 million was recognised towards restoration of securities for two clients in the depository participant business who suffered losses from fraudulent off-market transfers from their demat accounts. The amount includes a contingent liability disclosed in the previous quarter, now recognised as an expense, plus impact of fraud identified during the current quarter in continuation of the previous fraud. The incident was reported to depositories, stock exchanges and an FIR was lodged with the Economic Offence Wing; the matter is under investigation. An external consultant (including EY for forensic audit) was engaged to review processes and systems. - Sequentially, total revenue declined 3.7% (₹2,461.0 million vs ₹2,556.5 million in Q4 FY26), EBITDA declined 11.8%, and PAT after exceptional item declined 43.8%. Distribution income fell 21.9% Q-o-Q, which management attributed to the concentration of insurance business in the January–March quarter and to the March market fall of roughly 14–15% affecting trail revenue. - MTF interest income was flat Q-o-Q (₹427.7 million vs ₹431.7 million) despite book growth, because the average book size was similar in both quarters — the book had fallen to near ₹1,100 crores in March before rebuilding to ₹1,330 crores by quarter-end.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Market and investor momentum - Benchmark indices gained roughly 6–7% sequentially. India’s demat account base rose to 23 crores in June. Mutual fund industry AUM reached ₹82.2 lakh crores in June 2026 from ₹73.73 lakh crores in March 2026. New SIP additions in June touched about 66 lakh versus 53 lakh in March. - About 42 companies filed draft offer documents with SEBI during the quarter, indicating a strong capital formation pipeline.

Business model diversification - Revenue mix was 52% broking, 29% non-broking (MTF plus distribution), with the remainder from other income. Management continues to target a near 50-50 mix over the medium term to reduce earnings volatility from broking. - Broking income was ₹1,280 million, of which 51% came from equity cash — the core focus area. Broking and related services revenue rose about 7% Q-o-Q and 15.4% Y-o-Y. - MTF book stood at ₹13,318.46 million, up 54.6% Y-o-Y. Distribution AUM reached ₹94,791 million, up 25.8% Y-o-Y. Assets under custody were about ₹1.13 lakh crores, up 21.4% Y-o-Y. - MTF yield was around 14%; the book is funded only against cash market transactions, restricted to selected securities from over 2,000 eligible scrips, with a large portion of the book below ₹1 crore per position and no funding beyond individual client limits. Management stated the MTF book has operated since 2017 with no losses or delinquencies.

Financial strength and franchise - External credit rating upgraded to A1+ for short-term bank facilities (CARE, April 8, 2026) and assigned A+ for long-term bank facilities (ICRA, April 30, 2026). - Debt-equity ratio at 0.81 as of June 30, 2026 (versus 1.93 in Q1 FY26), which management views as headroom to increase borrowing at reasonable cost; management noted industry peers operate at 1.5–2x. - Employee base grew to 2,263 from 2,148 in Q1 FY26. Approximately 57% of clients have been associated for over three years. Footprint expanded to 319 cities; over 70% of total active clients hail from Tier 2/3 cities. - The Board approved raising up to ₹500 crores via rated/unrated, listed/unlisted, secured/unsecured redeemable NCDs in one or more tranches, and approved incorporation of a wholly owned subsidiary in Dubai (UAE) to serve NRI, HNI and family office clients, subject to DET licence and SEBI approval. An existing GIFT City subsidiary, Anand Rathi International Ventures (IFSC) Private Limited, is already active. - On June 22–24, 2026, the company allotted 1,110 secured, unlisted, redeemable NCDs of ₹1,00,000 face value each, aggregating ₹11.10 crores, at a 9% coupon with quarterly interest, maturing June 22, 2029.

3. Key Risks

  • Fraud/depository operations risk: The off-market transfer fraud in the DP segment, involving dormant accounts, led to a ₹209.96 million exceptional expense. Recoveries depend on the ongoing EOW investigation (which has traced the money trail and attached certain assets and bank accounts of beneficiaries) and insurance claims; any recoveries will be accounted for only when realisation becomes reasonably certain.
  • Market and earnings volatility: Broking revenue remains susceptible to short-term capital market volatility, which is the stated rationale for the non-broking diversification strategy.
  • MTF credit risk: Management acknowledged the MTF book is susceptible to risk, mitigated by cash-market-only exposure, scrip selection criteria, position-size limits and concentration controls.
  • Regulatory risk: SEBI derivative measures and the RBI’s revised capital market exposure framework (effective July 1, 2026) are reshaping industry economics and increasing working capital requirements.
  • Liquidity and spread risk: Borrowing costs rise when market liquidity is tight; management stated the impact can be passed on to MTF customers, and that spreads depend on market conditions and execution skill.
  • Related party concentration: The Board approved material related party transactions with Anand Rathi Financial Services Limited (Holding Company) and Anand Rathi Global Finance Limited (Group Company) for FY 2026-27, subject to shareholder approval via postal ballot. These resolutions were subsequently passed as Ordinary Resolutions on September 4, 2026.
  • International expansion execution risk: The Dubai subsidiary requires trade name reservation, DET initial approval and licence issuance, possible supplementary approvals depending on the DET activity code, and SEBI approval; minimum share capital requirements are pending determination of the applicable licence category.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Guidance on growth - Management stated a long-term revenue growth range of 15–25% (with a minimum of around 15–20% while broking dependence remains high) and PAT growth of 30–35%. Observed: Q1 FY27 revenue from operations grew 22.4% Y-o-Y to ₹2,461.0 million, at the upper end of the stated range; PAT before exceptional items grew 71.2% Y-o-Y to ₹390.6 million, above the guided 30–35% band. PAT after exceptional items grew only 2.3% Y-o-Y to ₹233.5 million. - When asked whether the annual guidance would be revised upward after the 22% growth, management reiterated the 15–25% range and the 30–35% PAT growth target, without revising it.

Guidance on MTF book - Management guided the MTF book to reach ₹1,750–1,800 crores by end of FY27, from ₹1,330 crores at Q1 FY27. Observed: the book stood at ₹13,318.46 million (₹1,331.8 crores), up 54.6% Y-o-Y, with management noting the book has been continuously growing in the current quarter.

Guidance on distribution AUM - Management targeted 40% growth in distribution AUM, driven by higher wallet share of existing clients and disciplined risk management. Observed: distribution AUM reached ₹94,791 million, up 25.8% Y-o-Y; distribution income grew 31.1% Y-o-Y to ₹275.2 million but declined 21.9% Q-o-Q.

Guidance on revenue mix - Management reiterated the target of a 50-50 mix between broking and non-broking income over the medium term. Observed: the mix was 52% broking and 29% non-broking (MTF plus distribution), with the remainder from other income.

Guidance on leverage - Management indicated intent to increase the debt-equity ratio toward industry levels of 1.5–2x, supported by improved ratings. Observed: debt-equity stood at 0.81 as of June 30, 2026, down from 1.93 in Q1 FY26 and up from 0.62 at FY26 year-end.

Guidance on international business - Management stated it is not currently providing an international investing platform to domestic customers, citing LRS limits of $250,000 per account per year and a philosophy of only offering products it understands well. The distribution product team has begun evaluating dollar-denominated mutual fund and other products for NRI clients. The Dubai subsidiary is intended to serve NRI/HNI/family office clients in the UAE and other overseas jurisdictions, with the exact licensed activity to be finalised based on the DET licence category.
