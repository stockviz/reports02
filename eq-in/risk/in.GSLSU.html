GSLSU: Recent Developments and Outlook

1. Headwinds and Challenges

US tariff disruption. In August 2025, the US imposed an effective 50% tariff on a broad range of Indian imports — 25% reciprocal tariffs plus a 25% additional tariff linked to India’s purchase of Russian crude oil. Natural stone (granite, marble) and engineered quartz were impacted. Management describes the result as a sharp slowdown in shipments, delayed order execution, and margin pressure across the industry as exporters and US importers absorbed the sudden increase in landed cost.

Section 201 safeguard petition. In April 2026, the US trade industry petitioned for safeguard protection on engineered quartz under Section 201. The US International Trade Commission determined that increased imports of quartz surface products were a substantial cause of serious injury to the domestic industry and recommended a four-year tariff quota with a 25% in-quota tariff and a 50% above-quota tariff in the first year. Management says it is closely monitoring how the final measures evolve; industry bodies are engaging authorities to seek relief or exemptions.

Geopolitical and logistics disruption. Closure of the Strait of Hormuz created operational challenges in shipping material from the UAE facility in March and April 2026. The company rerouted shipments through alternative ports — Sohar in Oman and Khor Fakkan and Fujairah in the UAE — with merchandise shipping resuming in May and June. Freight costs rose to more than twice normal levels and, per management, had not normalized from Q1 levels as of the call.

Raw material cost inflation. Raw material costs escalated in the Middle East due to freight factors, and management describes this as a global phenomenon rather than Middle East-specific. Resin, a petroleum-linked key input, has driven up costs in India as well. The company has passed on roughly 30–40% of the cost increase to customers.

Low capacity utilization. Overall capacity utilization in the June 2026 quarter was 27–28%. Dubai facility utilization was 20% and India 36%. The low Dubai utilization is attributed primarily to the inability to ship in the initial months of the quarter due to the Middle East geopolitical situation.

Bagru unit discontinuation. Operations at the natural stone manufacturing unit in Bagru were discontinued effective March 31, 2026, following a board-approved strategic review of lower utilization levels that were impacting overall profitability and margin. In FY 2025-26, the unit recorded a loss of approximately ₹7–8 crore on a top line of roughly ₹12–14 crore. Limited activities continue for orderly closure, including execution of pending orders, settlement of outstanding obligations, and closure formalities. Asset disposal is in progress, with a board-appointed valuer engaged; management anticipates disposal within the financial year but has not specified a number.

Income tax penalty. An order dated June 29, 2026 under Section 271D of the Income-tax Act, 1961 was received on June 30, 2026, imposing a penalty of ₹1,73,99,169 for Assessment Year 2019-20, on the ground of alleged contravention of Section 269SS (acceptance of loans/deposits otherwise than through a specified mode). The company states it believes it has adequate legal and factual grounds, does not expect a material adverse impact on financials, operations, or other activities, and is reviewing the order with a view to filing an appeal before the appropriate appellate forum.

US housing demand. Management acknowledges that US housing demand is significantly impaired, which is a driver behind the search for other markets.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Tariff relief framework. In February 2026, the US and India announced a framework for an interim trade agreement under which reciprocal tariffs on Indian-origin goods were reduced from 25% to 18%, and the additional Russia-linked tariff was withdrawn. Management describes this as significant relief that improved the competitiveness of Indian products in the US market.

Geographic diversification. Exporters including GSL are working to diversify across Europe, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia to reduce long-term dependence on a single market. A sales team was sent to some European countries in the prior quarter, with results awaited. The sales team is also working on GCC markets, focusing on the UAE and other GCC countries.

India domestic market entry. The company is building a distribution network and launching across India in Q2 FY 2027, selling under the Global Surfaces brand through a distributor model with dealers appointed across major cities. Products will be sold from both the UAE and India facilities. The end customer base spans B2C customers, residential and commercial projects, builders, and architects; GSL sells through its distribution network, which in turn serves B2C brands. A marketing budget has been set internally, and management states that if revenue margins improve, it does not expect an overall margin impact; certain international fairs that were not yielding proper results have been pulled back.

Marquartz patented technology. The company launched Marquartz Technology, described as a proprietary patented innovation, strengthening its positioning in premium engineered surfaces and supporting better value realizations. Management states that globally only two manufacturers make this product, that it has seen good uptake and positive feedback from the North American customer network, and that the product mix from the Dubai facility has benefited from this line. Products to be launched in India include patented designs that Indian consumers will be seeing for the first time, and management states no competitor is doing certain of these designs.

Dual-country manufacturing footprint. Combined annual production capacity is 1.14 million square meters across India and Dubai, with 95% of revenue derived from exports. The international presence is anchored by Global Surfaces FZE in Dubai’s Jebel Ali Free Zone, with US subsidiaries Global Surfaces INC and Superior INC providing distribution and market access across North America. Management believes the India and UAE footprint positions the company to adapt sourcing and product mix as the trade environment evolves, and notes the evolving tariff environment could create opportunities for engineered surfaces if higher US costs lead to greater customer preference for alternative materials.

UAE facility capitalization. The board approved conversion of a loan tranche into equity shares of Global Surfaces FZE equivalent to ₹110.44 crore, expected to be completed on or before September 30, 2026. The conversion is intended to strengthen the FZE balance sheet, reduce its finance cost burden, and support long-term operational stability. The board also approved a roll-over of a ₹2 crore Standby Letter of Credit corporate guarantee in favour of HDFC Bank Limited. Management states the Dubai unit was EBITDA positive in June 2026 and was EBITDA positive in the last financial year as well.

Industry demand drivers. Rising demand for aesthetic and durable surfaces, urbanization and infrastructure growth, strong growth in export markets, tariffs on Chinese quartz, and technological advancements and innovation in customized and AI-driven manufacturing are cited as tailwinds.

Management strengthening. Mr. Ashish Agarwal was promoted to Chief Financial Officer effective August 11, 2026, following Mr. Mayank Shah’s step-down from the additional charge of CFO. Mr. L. N. Bakshi, VP Sales and Marketing, was designated as Senior Management Personnel; he heads sales and marketing in India, is responsible for setting up the India distribution network, has 30 years of experience, and has worked at companies including NITCO, Tuton, and Classic Marble.

3. Key Risks

  • Tariff and trade policy risk: The final shape of the Section 201 safeguard measures on engineered quartz remains uncertain, with a recommended four-year tariff quota of 25% in-quota and 50% above-quota in the first year. Elevated tariffs on India-origin quartz exports to the US remain a material headwind.
  • Geopolitical risk: Middle East conflict disrupted shipping routes and inflated freight costs to over 2x normal levels; management’s expectation of improved utilization depends on geopolitical conditions normalizing.
  • Raw material and currency risk: Fluctuations in currency movements and raw material costs, including petroleum-linked resin, are identified risks. Only 30–40% of cost increases have been passed on to customers.
  • Capacity under-utilization risk: At 27–28% overall utilization, fixed-cost absorption remains adverse; profitability improvement is contingent on utilization rising.
  • Execution risk in new markets: The India domestic launch and European market expansion are at early stages, with results from European sales efforts still awaited and the India sales team still being expanded.
  • Regulatory/litigation risk: The ₹1,73,99,169 income tax penalty order for AY 2019-20 is subject to appeal; the company does not expect material adverse impact but the matter is unresolved.
  • Bagru closure execution risk: Disposal of the Bagru unit’s assets is ongoing with no specific number or buyer finalized; the company anticipates disposal within the financial year.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance approach. Management declined to provide specific quantitative guidance for FY 2027 on revenue growth, volume growth, peak revenue, or capacity utilization targets, citing geopolitical uncertainties and the impact on the industry. The company states it is not in a position to quantify expected increases in productivity and profits in specific measurable terms due to contingencies including US Section 201 safeguard proceedings, currency movements, raw material costs, and evolving market demand. Management does state that restructuring initiatives — exiting the Bagru business, enhanced capitalization of the UAE facility, and focus on premium product innovation and geographic diversification — are expected to positively impact operating performance and profitability. Management also states that as a management team it feels “the worst for the company is already done” and sees positive upswing from here.

Q1 FY 2026-27 observed performance. Revenue from operations was ₹65 crore, described as growth of 44%. EBITDA was ₹8 crore (₹83 million) with a margin of 12.69%, compared to an EBITDA loss of ₹19 crore in the previous quarter. PAT turned positive, improving from a loss of ₹2 crore in the previous quarter to break-even (₹0.64 million). The company achieved consolidated break-even despite lower volumes, which management attributes to disciplined cost management and operational efficiencies. Specific cost actions cited: a reduction of almost 3% in overall manufacturing expenses in Q1 FY 2027 and 1.5% savings from business promotion and admin expenses.

Revenue context. Q1 FY 2027 revenue declined 12% year on year, per an investor’s observation on the call. FY 2025-26 standalone Total Income declined to ₹1,122.29 million, while Net Profit After Tax remained broadly stable at ₹76.12 million. Consolidated revenue for last year was ₹232 crore, of which the Dubai facility contributed approximately ₹160 crore at 44% utilization, with the balance from the Indian facility.

Utilization versus guidance. Management expects utilization to improve as geopolitical conditions ease and market demand normalizes, and states the company has a strong order book. However, no specific utilization target for FY 2027 was provided. Management indicated that at 28–30% capacity utilization the company has tried to break even, and that once a decent level of capacity utilization is reached, profitability should scale up dramatically over the next few quarters, provided geopolitical situations remain favorable. Management also noted gross margin is close to 45–50%, implying operating leverage as utilization improves.

Dubai performance versus guidance. Management states the Dubai unit was EBITDA positive in June 2026 and in the last financial year. The loan-to-equity conversion is expected to be completed on or before September 30, 2026.

India domestic contribution. Management states domestic revenue has been insignificant to date and declined to specify a revenue number for the next 2–3 years, but expects substantial contribution from the India domestic market over the next few financial years. The sales team, currently 9–10 people across regions (4–5 in India, 3–4 in the Gulf, and one temporarily in the US), is expected to expand to 25–30 people in total, with close to 15 added in the India domestic market.

Peak revenue mix. Management indicated a ballpark peak revenue split of approximately 65:35 between Dubai and India, depending on product line and utilization, while declining to give a peak revenue figure.

Demand commentary. Management states there has been no major change in product demand, which remains stable and strong, with uncertainties stemming from tariffs and geopolitical situations.
