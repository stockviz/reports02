Central Bank of India — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Margin pressure from regulatory rate cuts. NIM stood at 3.06% in Q1 FY27, described as a marginal decline “post absorption of the Regulatory rate cuts,” down 10 bps YoY from 3.16%. Yield on advances fell to 7.89% from 8.58% (69 bps), and yield on funds declined 44 bps to 7.58%.
  • Operating profit dip. Operating profit for the quarter was ₹2,186 crore against ₹2,304 crore, a 5.12% YoY decline. Total income grew only 3.08% YoY to ₹10,678 crore, constrained by a 44.27% YoY fall in non-interest income to ₹987 crore.
  • Treasury income volatility. Treasury income dropped 58.43% YoY to ₹276 crore, with profit on sale of investments down 82.32% to ₹102 crore. Other receipts (recovery in written-off accounts and others) fell 66.27% YoY to ₹224 crore.
  • Liquidity buffer normalisation. LCR declined from 235.08% to 156.49% and NSFR from 146.90% to 128.25%. Management characterised this as optimal deployment of high-quality liquid assets, noting the prior 215% LCR and 64% CD ratio carried a cost, while confirming levels remain above internal risk thresholds and regulatory requirements.
  • Slippage above internal guidance. Slippage ratio at 0.29% was above the guided “less than 0.25% on Q-o-Q basis.” Agriculture KCC slippages of ₹200 crore out of total slippages of ₹986 crore were a contributor; management linked resolution to the coming debt waiver scheme.
  • Gross NPA above guided threshold. Gross NPA at 2.60% was above the “below 2.50%” guidance, though improved 53 bps YoY from 3.13%. Agriculture GNPA remained elevated at 5.82% and MSME at 5.19%.
  • Cost-to-income slightly above target. At 55.40%, marginally above the “<56%” guidance but 10 bps higher YoY; staff cost share in cost-to-income rose 29 bps to 35.88%.
  • Macro uncertainties. Geopolitical tensions persist; IMF revised global inflation upward to 4.7% for 2026. India’s June 2026 was the driest in 12 years, with Kharif sowing 23% below last year’s pace. WPI surged from 1.1% to 9.9% and PPI Output from 1.3% to 9.6%, signalling input-cost pressures not yet passed to customers.
  • Associate losses. Generali Central Life Insurance recorded a loss of ₹56.94 crore and Generali Central Insurance a loss of ₹88.92 crore in Q1 FY27, versus a loss of ₹4.02 crore and profit of ₹13.09 crore respectively in Q1 FY26. Group share of earnings in associates turned negative at ₹(4) crore.
  • Segment result volatility. Retail segment profit was ₹1,377 crore against ₹633 crore, wholesale was ₹(139) crore against ₹284 crore, and unallocated was ₹(75) crore against ₹322 crore. Management attributed this to changes in the underlying numbers rather than provisioning imbalance.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong headline business growth. Total global business grew 18.29% YoY to ₹833,320 crore; deposits rose 11.68% to ₹478,972 crore; gross global advances rose 28.58% to ₹354,348 crore. CD ratio improved to 74.10% from 64.43%.
  • RAM franchise momentum. RAM grew 21.38% to ₹241,105 crore, with retail up 23.92%, agriculture up 21.14% and MSME up 18.03%. RAM:corporate mix at 68:32 aligns with the 65:35 (±5%) guidance.
  • Corporate credit scale-up. Corporate credit grew 46.52% to ₹112,770 crore from ₹76,966 crore, attributed to a low base. Management cited opportunities in renewable energy, data centres, HAM projects and CRE. Investment-grade standard advances (₹25 crore and above) rose 48.93% to ₹115,650 crore, with AAA up 67.58% and government-guaranteed advances up 134.42%.
  • Asset quality improvement. Gross NPA improved 53 bps YoY to 2.60%; net NPA stable at 0.49%; PCR at 95.86%; credit cost improved to 0.40% from 0.68%. SMA (₹5 crore and above) declined to ₹850 crore (0.24% of global advances) from ₹1,008 crore (0.37%). Standard restructured book fell to ₹3,818 crore from ₹4,948 crore.
  • Profitability and capital strength. Net profit rose 13.26% YoY to ₹1,324 crore; NII grew 15.70% to ₹3,914 crore; ROE improved to 14.92% from 14.17%; ROA at 1.00%. CRAR improved 92 bps YoY to 18.28% with Tier I at 16.54% and CET1 at 16.24%. Management stated no capital raise is needed to support growth guidance.
  • Cost of deposits tailwind. Cost of deposits improved 33 bps YoY to 4.60%; management stated term deposits are almost fully repriced. 85% of deposits are in the below-₹3 crore retail segment.
  • GIFT City platform. The IFSC Banking Unit at GIFT City, Gandhinagar was inaugurated 29 June 2026 and canvassed ₹472.67 crore of business by the reporting date. Plans target USD 200 million deposits and USD 500 million trade book over the next few years. FCNR(B) mobilisation of USD 8.4 million is expected to reach USD 400 million by September 2026.
  • Fee income and structural initiatives. Centralised forex cell operational, centralised BG cell opened, 159 NRI desks, 35 customer acquisition centres, 9 government business centres, 300 marketing officers hired, and 1,000 specially trained credit officers joining in the first week of October 2026. Board approval obtained for wealth management, credit card, NRI and marketing verticals. Bancassurance income rose 24% YoY to ₹31 crore.
  • Priority sector outperformance. Priority sector advances at 58% of ANBC against the 40% norm; micro enterprises at 15.73% against 7.50%; agriculture at 22.41% against 18%; small and marginal farmers at 11.16% against 10%. PSLC sold ₹2,000 crore.
  • ECLGS 5.0 pipeline. 34,824 applications sanctioned for ₹4,646 crore; guarantees issued in 30,561 accounts for ₹4,353 crore; disbursement in 27,567 accounts for ₹3,693 crore.
  • Recovery prospects. Recoveries from technical written-off accounts were ₹1,300 crore, ₹1,400 crore, ₹1,700 crore and ₹2,100 crore in prior years; ₹2,200–2,500 crore expected this year. ₹515 crore received as CGTMSE guarantee, with a land parcel going to fresh auction in August.
  • Digital traction. Cent eeZ onboarded 44.50 lakh customers, now supporting foreign mobile numbers for NRI onboarding. Cent eeZ Vyapaar Saathi onboarded 4,000+ customers with over 1.50 lakh transactions worth ₹950 crore+.
  • Group performance. Consolidated net profit attributable to the group was ₹1,324 crore, with Cent Bank Home Finance net profit at ₹4.14 crore and Centbank Financial Services at ₹1.21 crore. Indo-Zambia Bank net profit rose to ₹154.62 crore from ₹96.95 crore.

3. Key Risks

  • ECL transition. Total ECL requirement is estimated at ₹4,500–5,000 crore, against ₹1,525 crore of Stage 1 and Stage 2 provisions already made. Management expects an 80 bps CRAR impact if accounted at once, with the option to stagger over five years, and targets transition from 1 April 2027. The PD calculation is expected to remain under control due to improved underwriting and monitoring.
  • Agriculture stress. Agriculture GNPA at 5.82% and net NPA at 1.70% remain the highest among segments. KCC slippages and the pending debt waiver scheme are watch items, alongside IMD’s 90%-of-LPA rainfall projection and weak Kharif sowing.
  • Concentration in corporate growth. Corporate credit growth of 46.52% is acknowledged as base-effect driven. The share of A-and-above rated advances to total standard rated advances declined 224 bps to 74.87%, and unrated advances rose 93.07% to ₹5,485 crore.
  • Yield trajectory dependence. Management expects overall yield to rise from 7.89% toward 8% by March 2027, contingent on gold loan and SHG growth (both yielding 8%+) and structural changes placing these verticals under direct ED oversight.
  • Liquidity ratio moderation. The sharp fall in LCR and NSFR, while above regulatory floors, reduces the buffer available if deposit or credit conditions shift.
  • Audit scope limitations. The limited review relies on concurrent auditor reports for 200 branches; 4,385 branches were not reviewed for consolidated purposes. Pillar 3 disclosures including leverage ratio, LCR and NSFR were not reviewed by auditors.
  • Deferred tax asset reduction. Net Deferred Tax Asset recognised was ₹1,62,436 lakh as on 30 June 2026 versus ₹2,53,138 lakh as on 30 June 2025, flagged as an Emphasis of Matter.
  • Macro input-cost pass-through. The gap between retail and wholesale inflation signals building input-cost pressures yet to be passed to customers.

Management stated the bank is aligning with its stated guidance of 11–12% deposit growth and 14–16% advances growth, with quarter-on-quarter growth of approximately 3%. On cost-to-income, management said it expects a 1.5% reduction in the coming year through income improvement (centralised forex and BG cells, exporter meets, NRI cell, GIFT City IBU, bancassurance stabilisation, marketing/sales department) and structured cost curtailment across cash retention balances, ATMs, currency chests, land bank utilisation, electricity, water and stationery. Management expressed confidence in not only achieving but exceeding the guidance given to the market.
