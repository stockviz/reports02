Globus Spirits: Recent Developments and Outlook

Headwinds and Challenges

Income tax search and assessment overhang. The Income Tax Department carried out a search and seizure operation at various premises of the company between January 30 and February 3, 2023 under section 132 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Assessment orders for ten assessment years (AY 2014-15 to AY 2023-24) were received in April 2024, disallowing certain expenses. There is no tax demand for AY 2014-15 to AY 2020-21, but for the remaining three assessment years the aggregate tax demand is ₹4,093.82 lakh, of which ₹3,043.76 lakh has been paid under protest. The company filed appeals before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) during the year ended March 31, 2025. No provision has been made in the financial results, based on external legal counsel views and an independent review supporting the company’s position. The auditor drew attention to this matter in both the standalone and consolidated review reports without modifying the conclusion.

Partial resolution, with one period still open. On August 17, 2026, the company received complete relief in appeals for AY 2014-15, AY 2015-16 and AY 2016-17, aggregating total additions of ₹6,34,53,485. The appeal for the remaining period is ongoing.

Margin and cost dynamics. Gross profit margin was 33% in Q1 FY27, compared with 37% in Q4 FY26 and 31% in Q1 FY26. Finance costs rose to ₹157 million in Q1 FY27 from ₹146 million a year earlier, and depreciation increased to ₹264 million from ₹216 million. Employee expenses rose to ₹307 million from ₹282 million.

Segment asset build-up in consumer. Consumer segment assets increased to ₹99,105.43 lakh as of June 30, 2026, from ₹83,711.56 lakh as of March 31, 2026 and ₹70,205.12 lakh as of June 30, 2025, while manufacturing segment assets declined over the same period.

State excise structure and mix effects. Reported NSV per case in Uttar Pradesh reflects the state’s excise structure and product mix. As mandated by UP Excise Policy, all country liquor warehouses in the state must offer inventory of other company brands; segment numbers exclude ₹53.16 million of trading revenue and ₹5.66 million of trading EBITDA from GSL-managed warehouses in the state.

Dilution consideration. The CFO noted that the company has been conscious of dilution in determining the size of the QIP issue.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Successful QIP of ₹200 crore. On August 6, 2026, the Fund Raising Committee approved closure of the issue, the placement document, and an issue price of ₹840 per equity share (including a premium of ₹830), reflecting a discount of ₹43.67 per share, or 4.94% of the floor price, as permitted under Regulation 176(1) of the SEBI ICDR Regulations. On August 7, 2026, the company announced allotment of 23,80,952 equity shares at ₹840 per share, aggregating ₹200 crore, with participation from domestic and international institutional investors, including new investors. Proceeds are to be used for repayment/prepayment, in part or full, of certain outstanding borrowings and for general corporate purposes. Nuvama Wealth Management acted as Book Running Lead Manager.

Consumer-led growth strategy. The company describes a balanced brand portfolio: Regular & Others as a resilient cash-generating foundation, Prestige & Above driving premiumisation and future growth, and a selectively curated luxury portfolio building long-term brand equity. P&A revenues grew 27% YoY in FY26, and P&A share of consumer revenue increased from 6% in FY24 to 15% in FY26.

Uttar Pradesh scaling. UP continues to scale strongly, with R&O volumes growing 2.4x YoY and crossing 0.2 million cases per month, net of traded volumes. Rajasthan remains steady while UP expands the growth base.

Manufacturing performance. Capacity utilisation improved to 89% in Q1 FY27, supported by strong ethanol demand, ENA export sales and better operating throughput. Segment EBITDA grew 66% YoY, with EBITDA per litre at approximately ₹6.5. Captive consumption for ENA was 43% in Q1 FY27. The company cites interchangeability between ENA and ethanol, feedstock flexibility across broken rice, maize and surplus FCI rice, six distilleries with installed capacity of 334 million litres, and LTOA units in Jharkhand, West Bengal and Haryana enjoying preferential allocation.

Ethanol demand visibility. The ethanol blending ratio is expected to remain at 20% next year, with ethanol demand expected to grow at the rate of petrol consumption growth, approximately 7.5% per annum.

Balance sheet strengthening. Debt refinancing and completion of major capex are described as positioning the business for improved free cash flow generation and sustained deleveraging. Debt/Equity stood at 0.48 in FY26 versus 0.54 in FY25. The QIP is described as further strengthening the capital base and financial flexibility.

Industry tailwind. The presentation cites industry guidance of 15-17% growth.

Key Risks

  • The ongoing income tax appeal for the remaining assessment period, with the company stating it remains confident of successfully closing the appeal.
  • Uncertainty regarding outcomes of assessment proceedings, as noted in the financial results, with no provision made based on management’s assessment and external legal counsel views.
  • Forward-looking statements in the QIP press release are subject to risks and uncertainties, with actual results potentially differing materially. Developments cited as potentially affecting operations include an industry downtrend, significant changes in the political and economic environment in India or key markets abroad, tax laws, litigation, labour relations, exchange rate fluctuations, technological changes, investment and business income, cash flow projections, interest and other costs.
  • The auditor’s review report notes reliance on another auditor for one subsidiary, Bored Beverages Private Limited, whose interim results reflected total revenues of approximately ₹4.26 lakh and total net loss after tax and total comprehensive loss of approximately ₹23.22 lakh for the quarter ended June 30, 2026.
  • The joint venture, Globus ANSA Private Limited, contributed a share of loss of ₹83.88 lakh in Q1 FY27, compared with a share of profit of ₹1.42 lakh in Q4 FY26 and a share of loss of ₹43.57 lakh in Q1 FY26.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

QIP proceeds and capital discipline. Management stated the proceeds would be used for repayment/prepayment of certain outstanding borrowings and general corporate purposes, and that capital deployment would remain disciplined, guided by return thresholds and the stated objects of the issue. The QIP was completed with allotment of 23,80,952 shares at ₹840 per share, aggregating ₹200 crore, as announced on August 7, 2026.

Operating performance commentary. The JMD & CEO stated the capital raise comes at a time when the company is delivering improved operating performance alongside a stronger balance sheet. Observed Q1 FY27 standalone results show revenue from operations of ₹7,888 million, up 13% YoY and 25% QoQ; EBITDA of ₹795 million, up 33% YoY and 10% QoQ; and PAT of ₹276 million, up 49% YoY and 27% QoQ. FY26 standalone revenue was ₹27,081 million, up 7% YoY, with EBITDA up 67% and PAT up 278%.

Segment guidance versus delivery. The presentation cites industry guidance of 15-17%. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue from operations was ₹1,15,187.94 lakh, compared with ₹95,155.19 lakh in Q1 FY26 and ₹85,108.14 lakh in Q4 FY26. Consolidated PAT was ₹2,648.06 lakh in Q1 FY27, compared with ₹1,768.98 lakh in Q1 FY26 and ₹2,111.42 lakh in Q4 FY26.

Ethanol blending guidance. Management expects the ethanol blending ratio to be the same next year at 20%, with ethanol demand growing at approximately 7.5% per annum, tied to petrol consumption growth. Observed Q1 FY27 manufacturing segment EBITDA was ₹3,687.30 lakh, up from ₹2,222.94 lakh in Q1 FY26, with capacity utilisation at 89%.

Tax appeal confidence. Management stated it remains confident of successfully closing the remaining appeal and that it believes it can succeed in the appeals filed, with no liability expected to devolve. Observed outcome to date is complete relief in appeals for AY 2014-15, AY 2015-16 and AY 2016-17, aggregating additions of ₹6,34,53,485, with the appeal for the remaining period ongoing.

Dividend and shareholder returns. The 33rd AGM held on September 15, 2026 approved a final dividend of ₹6.53 per equity share (65.30%) along with adoption of financial statements, re-appointment of directors, remuneration approvals for executive management and the cost auditor, and the Employee Stock Option Plan 2025-A.

Broker Narrative

The broker kept a Buy rating in both reports, but over the two weeks the narrative shifted from expecting quick relief from broken-rice price corrections and robust IMIL/IMFL growth to acknowledging persistent cost pressure, weak capacity utilization, and soft IMIL volumes, while leaning on management guidance for a utilization/EBITDA ramp-up and long-term CAGRs. The persistent themes were raw-material cost headwinds, capacity/upgradation drags, and IMIL/IMFL growth expectations; what changed was the timing — the first report looked for a recovery from the FCI ban lift and maize shift, while the last report pushed recovery to Q4FY26 and beyond.

Fears that came true

  • Steep inflation in inputs such as broken rice and power materialized, as the last report said steep raw material costs caused a severe earnings decline over FY22-25.
  • Capacity expansion and upgradation costs materialized as a drag, with manufacturing capacity utilization weak at ~81% and external manufacturing sales subdued due to plant maintenance/upgradation.
  • The abrupt FCI broken-rice supply stoppage was part of the raw-material cost problem, and the stock still fell -15.4% and -24.5% after the two Buy calls.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected robust IMIL volume growth from UP entry and Rajasthan price hikes failed to materialize, as overall IMIL volume growth was only 1% YoY, with de-growth in Delhi and Haryana.
  • The structural growth optimism in the branded IMFL P&A segment did not translate into profitability, as the segment still reported a modest EBITDA loss despite 50% revenue growth on a low base.
