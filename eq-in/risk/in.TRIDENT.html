Trident: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Shareholder dissent on key resolutions. At the 36th Annual General Meeting held on July 31, 2026, voting results show notable opposition from public institutions on certain items:
    • Resolution 3 (re-appointment of Mr. Rajiv Dewan as Director): 4.7890% of public institutional votes polled were against, with 73,09,518 votes against.
    • Resolution 4 (re-appointment of Ms. Usha Sangwan as Independent Director): 2.4484% of public institutional votes polled were against.
    • Resolution 5 (re-appointment of Mr. Deepak Nanda as Managing Director): 0.9658% of public institutional votes polled were against.
    • Resolution 8 (Trident ESOP 2026): 4.0118% of public institutional votes polled were against.
    • Resolution 9 (grant of stock options to employees of subsidiary/step-down subsidiary/associate companies under Trident ESOP 2026): public institutional votes in favour were 7,98,76,318 against 7,27,56,007, i.e. 52.3325% in favour and 47.6674% against — a closely divided institutional vote.
  • Low public non-institution participation. Public non-institution voting participation was very low relative to shares held — 0.4172% of their outstanding shares were polled across resolutions.
  • ESG rating remains in the “Adequate” category. CRISIL’s updated ESG score of 58 (up 4 points from 54) carries an “Adequate” rating category, indicating room for further improvement.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Improved ESG recognition. On August 18, 2026, Trident informed the exchanges that CRISIL ESG Ratings & Analytics Limited reviewed the company’s ESG performance based on publicly available information and updated the ESG score to 58, an upward revision of 4 points from 54, with an “Adequate” rating category indicating consistent ESG performance. CRISIL also assigned a Core ESG rating of “CRISIL Core ESG 58” based on the BRSR Core framework. The company noted it did not engage CRISIL for the rating; it was assigned independently based on publicly available data.
  • Fundraising flexibility approved. Resolution 7, a special resolution to approve raising of funds by way of Non-Convertible Debentures (NCDs), was passed with 99.9975% of votes polled in favour, giving the company a route to raise debt capital.
  • Employee alignment initiatives. Shareholders approved the Trident Employees Stock Option Plan 2026 (Resolution 8) and the grant of stock options to employees of subsidiary, step-down subsidiary, and associate companies under the plan (Resolution 9), which can support retention and alignment across the group.
  • Strong promoter support. Promoter and promoter group votes were 100% in favour on all resolutions, providing stable backing for board and management proposals.

3. Key Risks

  • Governance-related dissent. The relatively high against-votes from public institutions on director re-appointments (Resolutions 3 and 4) and the near-even split on Resolution 9 indicate potential governance or alignment concerns among institutional investors.
  • Dilution risk from ESOP. The approval of the Trident ESOP 2026 and the extension of options to subsidiary/associate employees could dilute existing shareholders, which may explain institutional opposition.
  • Leverage risk from NCD issuance. The approval to raise funds via NCDs introduces potential balance-sheet leverage, though the resolution passed with near-unanimous support.
  • ESG rating dependence on public data. The CRISIL ESG rating was assigned independently based on publicly available information without company engagement, meaning the score may not fully reflect internal practices and could be revised based on disclosure changes.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • ESG trajectory. The company reported an upward revision of its CRISIL ESG score from 54 to 58, reflecting improved ESG performance as assessed by CRISIL. The rating category remains “Adequate,” indicating consistent but not top-tier ESG performance.
  • Shareholder approval outcomes. All nine resolutions put to shareholders at the 36th AGM on July 31, 2026 were passed, including adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY ended March 31, 2026, director re-appointments, cost auditor remuneration ratification, NCD fundraising, and the ESOP 2026 resolutions. However, the level of institutional dissent on certain resolutions suggests that management’s proposals did not receive uniform investor endorsement.
  • Participation levels. Total votes polled represented 76.7599%–76.7671% of outstanding shares across resolutions, with promoter and promoter group participating at 100% and public institutions at 87.7193%–87.9302% of their holdings, while public non-institutions participated at only 0.4172%.
