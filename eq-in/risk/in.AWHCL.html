AWHCL: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Q1FY27 profitability compression. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, total operating revenue rose 6% year-on-year to ₹260.1 crore, but EBITDA fell 27% to ₹45.0 crore and margins contracted to 16.8% from 24.4%. PAT dropped 97% to ₹0.7 crore. Management attributed the EBITDA moderation to higher operating expenses, including vehicle hiring and transportation costs at the CIDCO plant, and to certain Q4FY26 waste disposal transportation activities being deferred into Q1FY27, which added an incremental expense of approximately ₹10 crore. PAT was further impacted by a one-time expense of ₹7 crore related to prepayment of the Antony Lara Renewable Energy term loan.

RDF sales decline. RDF sales fell about 28% year-on-year to roughly 40,000 tonnes in Q1FY27. The company stated this is not directly comparable with Q1FY26, which had benefited from a meaningful contribution from the CIDCO bio-mining project that has since been completed. Compost sales were broadly stable at approximately 6,000 tonnes.

Force majeure event at the PCMC Waste-to-Energy facility. On July 8, 2026, exceptionally heavy monsoon rainfall destabilised thousands of tonnes of municipal waste accumulated in a legacy landfill located entirely outside the facility’s boundary and outside the company’s operational control. The waste mass collapsed onto the site, causing structural damage to the administrative building, where 23 employees were present. Rescue operations involved the NDRF, the Indian Army, PCMC officials and the Fire Brigade; fourteen employees were rescued. The company confirmed the loss of nine lives. The MRF and composting plant were subsequently made fully operational and ready to receive and process waste from PCMC, with a phased restart of the remaining facility underway. The company committed support to bereaved families, including funeral expenses, financial assistance of ₹25,00,000 per deceased employee (comprising ex-gratia support and applicable insurance benefits), employment for next of kin, educational expenses for minor children where no adult family member can take up employment, and medical coverage for injured employees. Updates are to be provided at the end of every quarter through the current financial year.

Elevated leverage relative to earlier periods. Net Debt/Equity stood at 1.3x in Q1FY27, versus 0.3x–0.5x across Q1FY25 through Q4FY26. Finance cost rose to ₹21.4 crore in Q1FY27 from ₹15.9 crore in Q1FY26, and depreciation increased to ₹22.3 crore from ₹20.6 crore.

Dilution of returns. EBIT margin fell to 8.5% in Q1FY27 from 16.3% in Q1FY26; profit before tax margin fell to 0.5% from 10.1%; EPS fell to ₹0.27 from ₹6.27.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

New contract from GNIDA. On August 11, 2026, the company announced receipt of a contract from Greater Noida Industrial Development Authority for procurement/engagement of Electric Mechanical Road Sweeping Machines along with comprehensive O&M for roads under Greater Noida (East Zone). The contract is for five years, extendable by a further two years, with broad consideration or project revenue of approximately ₹243.22 crore over the five-year period. The contract is domestic, was awarded by a domestic entity, and does not fall within related party transactions; the promoter/promoter group has no interest in GNIDA.

Refinancing of the ALREPL term loan. During Q1FY27, the company refinanced the term loan of Antony Lara Renewable Energy Private Limited, its material subsidiary operating the PCMC WtE facility, reducing the interest rate by 200 bps from 10.25% to 8.25% per annum. Management expects this to lower the interest burden at the subsidiary level, enhance free cash flow generation, and improve overall returns from the WtE asset. Consolidated average cost of borrowings declined from 12.4% as on March 31, 2020 to 10.1% as on June 30, 2026.

Volume growth. Total tonnage managed grew approximately 5.4% year-on-year to about 1.40 million tonnes in Q1FY27. C&T volumes rose about 5.1% to roughly 0.55 million tonnes, and processing volumes grew about 5.5% to roughly 0.85 million tonnes. Core revenue growth was approximately 7%. Management cited continued ramp-up in existing contracts and improved throughput across project locations.

Order book and pipeline. Management stated the company has a strong order book and an expanding project pipeline, and remains focused on disciplined execution, prudent capital allocation and operational efficiency.

Waste-to-energy expansion. The company operates three integrated Waste-to-Energy plants. The PCMC WtE plant is described as Maharashtra’s first integrated WtE project, inaugurated on August 1, 2023, with a project tenure of 2019–2040, processing over 1,000 tonnes per day, generating 14 MW of clean energy, and selling power to PCMC at ₹5 per unit under the Green Energy Open Access Rules. The company has also secured two new WtE projects in Kadapa and Kurnool, Andhra Pradesh.

Industry growth. The investor presentation cites India’s waste management market growing at a 7.2% CAGR from ₹1,211 billion in 2025 to ₹1,714 billion in 2030, with MSWM expected to double in India over the next five years. Waste generation is projected to grow from 60 million tonnes per annum in FY21 to 95 million tonnes per annum in FY26, and the MSW market from ₹65 billion to ₹95 billion over the same period.

Business model features. The company describes a de-risked model with staggered revenue streams, partially hedged exposure to major costs through tipping fees with built-in escalations, limited counter-party risk, and targeting of municipalities with stable financials. Revenue is diversified across MSW C&T (60% of FY26 revenue), MSW Processing (25%), and Contracts & Others (15%). Average ongoing contract duration is 7.7 years for C&T, 23 years for waste processing, and the company has 19 ongoing C&T contracts, 6 waste processing contracts and 1 C&D contract.

Credit ratings. CARE assigns CARE BBB+; Stable for long-term bank facilities and CARE A3+ for short-term bank facilities. Crisil assigns A-/Watch Developing and A2+/Watch Developing to Antony Lara Enviro Solutions, and BBB+/Watch Developing and A2/Watch Developing to Antony Lara Renewable Energy.

Key Risks

Force majeure and natural calamity exposure. The July 8, 2026 incident at the PCMC WtE facility demonstrates exposure to events originating outside the company’s operational control, including legacy landfill instability triggered by exceptional rainfall. The incident caused loss of life, structural damage, and a phased restart of the remaining facility.

Concentration and counterparty risk. The company’s business is primarily with municipal corporations. The investor presentation notes focus on municipalities with stable financials and credit ratings as a mitigation, and describes limited counter-party risk as a de-risking feature.

Cost inflation and pass-through. The company describes partial hedging against major costs through tipping fees with built-in escalations. Q1FY27 results show higher vehicle hiring and transportation costs at the CIDCO plant, and the deferral of Q4FY26 transportation activities into Q1FY27 added approximately ₹10 crore of incremental expense.

Project completion effects. The completion of the CIDCO bio-mining project contributed to the 28% year-on-year decline in RDF sales in Q1FY27, illustrating how project completions can affect revenue lines.

Leverage and finance costs. Net Debt/Equity rose to 1.3x in Q1FY27 from 0.3x–0.5x in prior quarters, and finance cost increased year-on-year.

Regulatory and compliance matters. The 25th AGM on August 20, 2026 approved material related party transactions with Antony Lara Enviro Solutions Private Limited (material subsidiary), Mumbai Eco Solutions Private Limited (subsidiary), Antony Lara Renewable Energy Private Limited (material subsidiary), and transactions between Antony Lara Enviro Solutions and Kadapa Renew Energy Private Limited and Kurnool Renew Energy Private Limited. The company also received approval to increase borrowing limits under Section 180(1)(c) and to create security interest under Section 180(1)(a) of the Companies Act, 2013, and to grant loans under Section 185.

Governance votes. Resolution 15, to fix the term and payment of remuneration of Mr. Shiju Jacob Kallarakal as Executive Director, passed with 80.77% of votes polled in favour and 19.23% against, with public institutions voting 16.27% in favour and 83.73% against. Resolution 13, to grant loans under Section 185, passed with 96.33% in favour and 3.67% against, with public institutions voting 84.05% in favour and 15.95% against.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Refinancing. Management stated the refinancing reduced the interest rate by 200 bps from 10.25% to 8.25%, and expected it to deliver recurring interest savings, reduce the finance burden, and support stronger cash flow generation from the WtE facility going forward. The Q1FY27 results show finance cost of ₹21.4 crore versus ₹15.9 crore in Q1FY26, and the one-time prepayment expense of ₹7 crore was recorded in the quarter.

Volume and revenue growth. Management stated Q1FY27 operating revenue of ₹260 crore reflected 6% growth versus Q1FY26, driven by higher volumes across project sites and supported by contractual tariff-led escalations. Observed performance: total operating revenue ₹260.1 crore versus ₹246.0 crore, up 6%; C&T revenue up 10% to ₹166.3 crore; processing revenue up 3% to ₹74.7 crore; other operating income down 14% to ₹19.1 crore.

EBITDA. Management attributed the EBITDA moderation to higher operating expenses including vehicle hiring and transportation costs at the CIDCO plant, and to the deferral of certain Q4FY26 waste disposal transportation activities into Q1FY27, resulting in an incremental expense of approximately ₹10 crore. Observed performance: EBITDA ₹45.0 crore versus ₹62.1 crore, down 27%; margin 16.8% versus 24.4%.

PAT. Management attributed the PAT impact to the one-time ₹7 crore expense related to prepayment of the ALREPL term loan. Observed performance: PAT ₹0.7 crore versus ₹23.0 crore, down 97%; PAT for owners of the company ₹0.8 crore versus ₹17.8 crore, down 96%.

Operational performance. Management stated the company delivered healthy tonnage growth across segments, supported by improved utilisation, continued ramp-up of existing projects and stronger throughput across project locations, with both C&T and Processing businesses witnessing steady growth. Observed performance: total tonnage managed approximately 1.40 million tonnes, up 5.4%; C&T approximately 0.55 million tonnes, up 5.1%; processing approximately 0.85 million tonnes, up 5.5%.

Outlook. Management stated that with a strong order book and an expanding project pipeline, the company remains focused on disciplined execution, prudent capital allocation and operational efficiency, and remains confident in its long-term growth prospects. The company also stated it remains committed to the phased restart of the remaining PCMC facility and will provide updates at the end of every quarter throughout the current financial year.

Investor conference. Antony Waste Handling Cell Limited officials are attending an Investors Conference organized by JM Financial Services Limited on September 23, 2026, from 02:00 p.m. onwards for 1x1 or group meetings, where discussions will be based on publicly available information.
