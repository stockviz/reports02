IRCTC: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure from revenue mix and one-off costs. In Q1 FY27, EBITDA declined 2.77% year-on-year to ₹386 crores even as revenue from operations rose 18.10% to ₹1,370 crores. Management attributed the margin moderation to a changed revenue mix (higher catering contribution), an additional HR cost of roughly ₹20 crores, and higher direct costs in the ticketing segment. Catering margins fell to 9.29% from 10.42% for FY25-26, affected by a proof-of-concept experiment on six trains (impact of over ₹4 crores), a gratuity increase from ₹20 lakhs to ₹25 lakhs, and ₹10 crores of HR cost booked in the segment.

Internet ticketing margin dilution. The segment’s EBIT margin fell to 80.33% from 84.12%. Contributing factors cited included ₹10 crores of website maintenance charges, NGET infrastructure refresh investment (around ₹150 crores infused), a planned active-active disaster recovery site at Secunderabad, removal of advertising from the beta website launched on 15 July, and restrictions on agent business (non-usage time limit raised from 15 to 30 minutes).

Non-convenience fee decline. Non-convenience fee revenue fell from ₹123 crores to ₹113 crores, with marketing/ad revenue dropping from ₹14.51 crores to ₹11.03 crores. Management noted conventional spinoffs such as marketing, advertising, and agent business are “losing.”

Rail Neer capacity constraints and margin dip. Installed capacity is around 17.77 lakh bottles per day (reduced from 18.4 due to closure of one plant) against supply of about 15.5 lakh bottles per day, while demand at some stations is 25–30 lakh bottles per day. The gap is sometimes filled by unauthorized vendors, a problem acknowledged by both Railways and IRCTC. Rail Neer margin fell from about 14–15% to 10–11%, attributed to a roughly 30% increase in resin component costs linked to the West Asia crisis, raising material cost from ₹55 crores to ₹61 crores.

Legal and tax disputes. License fee enhancements for pre-paid and post-paid trains remain unrecognized due to sub-judice challenges by licensees across multiple High Courts and arbitration. Non-sharing of input tax credit data by Developer cum Operators of four Rail Neer plants has led to non-recognition of claims receivables, with litigation involving the Sankrail and Hapur plants and termination of the Parasala plant contract. A National Anti-Profiteering Authority notice alleging profiteering of ₹5,041.44 Lakhs under the CGST Act is pending before GSTAT. The statutory auditors issued an emphasis of matter on these items.

GST input tax credit disadvantage on Vande Bharat. Management noted that on Vande Bharat, 5% GST cannot be offset by input tax credit, resulting in a loss of around ₹18 crores, though the combined license fee plus GST yields around ₹105 crores.

Convenience fee stagnation concern. An investor noted internet ticketing revenue has not grown over eight quarters despite train, passenger, and UPI growth, and that the convenience fee has not increased in seven years. Management acknowledged this as “an area of concern,” noting UPI pricing of ₹10 and ₹20 versus ₹15 and ₹30 for credit card and credit line, and that convenience fee growth this year was around 4.89% quarter-on-quarter.

Leadership transition. A question was raised about the resignation of Sanjay Jain. Management described it as a completely personal decision, stated that IRCTC will continue to grow stronger, and assured there are no material discrepancies.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Navratna status. The company’s status was upgraded to ‘Navratna’ by the Department of Public Enterprises, Ministry of Finance, Government of India, providing the Board of Directors with enhanced financial and operational autonomy.

Strong top-line growth. Revenue from operations rose 18.10% year-on-year to ₹1,370 crores from ₹1,160 crores, driven primarily by Catering and Tourism. PAT stood at ₹330 crores with an EBITDA margin of 28.17%.

Catering expansion. Catering revenue grew 33.82% year-on-year to ₹732 crores. Onboard sales from prepaid trains rose from ₹301 crores to ₹413 crores; license fees from prepaid/mobile trains rose from ₹192 crores to ₹224 crores; static unit license fees rose from ₹20 crores to ₹33 crores; e-catering license fees rose from ₹9 crores to ₹22 crores; election specials grew from ₹5 crores to ₹41 crores. E-catering volumes grew from 1.25 lakh to over 1.6 lakh meals per day, with e-pantry operating on more than 50 trains and potential to reach around 100 trains.

Tourism momentum. Tourism revenue rose 13.5% year-on-year to ₹168 crores, with EBITDA margins improving to 11.31% from 8.78% on better product mix and cost realization. Management stated tourism grew from ₹745 crores to ₹890 crores and may cross ₹1,000-plus crores next year. New agreements were signed with the Union Territory of Lakshadweep Administration, Andaman, and Majuro, and Char Dham Heli Yatra was cited as an example of leveraging e-ticketing expertise.

Non-fare revenue on private trains. IRCTC operates two private trains (Lucknow–NDLS Tejas Express and Ahmedabad–Mumbai Tejas Express). Naming rights were exercised for the first time, with the Tejas Express rebranded as Sprite Tejas, alongside vinyl wrapping and onboard advertising under a non-fare revenue policy.

iPay payment aggregator opportunity. In-principle RBI approval was received last year; the final application was submitted by the 4 August 2026 deadline, including System Audit Report and Minimum Viable Product feasibility, with a technology service provider engaged. iPay currently handles around 2.7 lakh transactions. Once licensed, expansion is envisioned across IRCTC, Railways (terminal management system, FOIS, GeM, pension schemes), and the private market.

Rail Neer capacity augmentation. Ambernath is being expanded from 2 lakh to 3 lakh bottles per day and Danapur from 1 lakh to 2 lakh bottles per day, targeted for completion this financial year. Four new plants are planned at Prayagraj, Mysore, Ranchi, and Bhagalpur; land has been allotted for Prayagraj and Mysore, and confirmation for Ranchi came on 12 August 2026. Additional plants may be considered in Varanasi and Pune, with a 250-km service radius.

Vande Bharat sleeper pipeline. Around 20 Vande Bharat sleeper trains may be introduced this financial year or over the next year, with license fee calibrated at around ₹6 crores per train, potentially ₹120 crores. Catering services on 81 Vande Bharats are currently handled by IRCTC. An 8% year-on-year passenger growth was cited as supporting catering charges.

Digital scale and leadership. Internet ticketing revenue was ₹361 crores, up about 0.5%, with nearly 89% of reserved railway tickets in India booked through the platform (share rose from 88.78% to 88.92%). Daily average tickets were 14.58 lakhs; total tickets booked in the quarter were 13.27 crores (6.92 crores AC, 6.35 crores non-AC). UPI share was 51.22% versus 48.72% a year earlier. The segment delivered EBITDA in excess of 80%.

Unified portal and cross-sell. A unified portal combining tourism and internet ticketing is planned, focused on UX/UI experience, scalability, technology, financial, and marketing, aimed at customer retention, engagement, and cross-selling tourism services.

3. Key Risks

  • Sub-judice license fee enhancement disputes across High Courts and arbitration, with amounts unrecognized.
  • Non-recognition of ITC claims receivables due to DCOs not sharing data; litigation on Sankrail and Hapur plants and termination of the Parasala contract.
  • Pending NAA anti-profiteering notice of ₹5,041.44 Lakhs before GSTAT.
  • Dependence on Ministry of Railways decisions for new train introductions.
  • Unauthorized vendors filling the Rail Neer supply gap at stations.
  • Commodity cost exposure, illustrated by resin cost inflation tied to the West Asia crisis.
  • GST input tax credit leakage on Vande Bharat services.
  • Reliance on election timing for election special revenue, which management said varies with elections.
  • Potential dilution of non-convenience fee revenue from marketing, advertising, and agent business.
  • Execution and regulatory timing risk on iPay, with RBI response expected within this financial year.
  • New Rail Neer plant timelines may spill into the next financial year.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Catering margin trajectory. Management stated catering margins have traditionally been 10% to 12% and that it will try to maintain that range. Observed Q1 FY27 margin was 9.29%, below that band, with management attributing the gap to the proof-of-concept impact (over ₹4 crores), gratuity increase, and ₹10 crores of HR cost. Management said the HR cost will not repeat, the proof of concept will wind down (two trains finishing by September, two by November, nil by Q4), and passenger and train additions should support margins.

Non-convenience fee recovery. Management said the current level “will not be like that” and that it will try to return to ₹150 crores in this segment, citing iPay, e-wallet loyalty growth of more than 200%, and modified agent arrangements. Observed Q1 FY27 non-convenience fee was ₹113 crores, down from ₹123 crores.

Internet ticketing margin. Management indicated the normal range is 80% to 85%. Observed Q1 FY27 EBIT margin was 80.33%, down from 84.12%, with management citing NGET refresh, disaster recovery investment, and website maintenance.

Rail Neer capacity timelines. Management said Ambernath and Danapur augmentation “should be finalized this year” if everything goes well, while new plants may spill to the next financial year with focus on Prayagraj, Mysore, and Ranchi first. Observed status: land allotted for Prayagraj and Mysore, Ranchi confirmation received 12 August 2026, and capacity currently constrained at 17.77 lakh bottles per day against 15.5 lakh supplied.

Vande Bharat sleeper revenue. Management calibrated potential license fee at around ₹6 crores per train, or ₹120 crores for around 20 trains, over this financial year or the next one year. This remains a forward expectation contingent on Ministry decisions.

iPay licensing. Management said RBI response “should take this financial year.” Observed status: final application, SAR, and MVP submitted by the 4 August 2026 deadline, with a TSP engaged.

Convenience fee growth. Management stated convenience fee has grown at around 5% to 7%, and around 4.89% quarter-on-quarter this year. An investor observed no internet ticketing revenue growth over eight quarters; management acknowledged the concern and said it will look into enhancing convenience-fee-based revenue.

Dividend and AGM. Management recommended a final dividend of ₹0.50 per equity share of face value ₹2 for FY ended 31 March 2026, with a record date of 22 September 2026, and stated that if declared at the AGM, it will be paid within 30 days of the AGM’s conclusion. The 27th AGM is scheduled for 29 September 2026 at 03:00 P.M. IST via video conferencing. Dividends will be paid only through RBI-approved electronic modes, with no physical warrants, cheques, or demand drafts, and are taxable under the Income-tax Act, 2025, with TDS based on residential status and submitted exemption documents.

Q1 FY27 financial results approval. The Board approved unaudited standalone and consolidated results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, along with the Limited Review Report by N. K. Bhargava & Co., on 12 August 2026, prepared under Ind-AS 34. Standalone revenue from operations was ₹136,952.93 Lakhs, total income ₹144,081.19 Lakhs, PAT from continuing operations ₹32,986.11 Lakhs, and basic and diluted EPS ₹4.12. Segment revenues were Catering ₹73,226.08 Lakhs, Rail Neer ₹11,390.95 Lakhs, Internet Ticketing ₹36,099.25 Lakhs, and Tourism ₹16,807.27 Lakhs. Consolidated revenue from operations was ₹136,952.93 Lakhs with total comprehensive income of ₹32,822.49 Lakhs.

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from a cautious Accumulate in 2023—focused on catering expansion and Rail Neer capacity while flagging margin and ticketing weakness—to a Buy in 2026 that still acknowledged margin hits but treated them as one-offs and highlighted new growth levers. Persistent themes were petroleum-linked input cost pressure on Rail Neer/catering margins, catering as the main growth engine, and capacity expansion. Changed emphases were the addition of e-catering scaling, UPI/payment-aggregator progress, and tourism targets, with the later report framing cost pressures as temporary rather than structural.

Fears that came true

  • Rail Neer margin pressure from petroleum-linked production costs materialized: the final report showed Rail Neer EBIT margin down to 9.8% on higher resin costs, and the stock saw large negative actual returns such as -49.2% and -51.4% in between.
  • Ticketing weakness materialized: the early warning of declining ticketing profitability/volumes was confirmed by the final report’s internet ticketing margin decline to 80.2%, non-convenience fee revenue down 5.5% YoY, and maintenance costs, alongside repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Overall margin pressure from adverse mix and input costs materialized: the final report’s EBITDA margin fell to 28.2% from 34.3% in Q1FY26, matching the first report’s OPM downward-risk warning and the negative actual returns seen on subsequent calls.
  • Employee cost pressure materialized: the early flag of 18% QoQ employee expense growth reappeared as a INR200mn gratuity/post-retirement hit and INR100mn HR cost allocation in the final report, while the stock’s actual returns were mostly negative across the call history.
  • Lower-margin business mix materialized: the first report’s adverse-mix concern appeared again as the final report blamed higher share of lower-margin prepaid catering for the catering EBIT margin fall to ~9.3%.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that ticketing would remain stable after the 2S impact normalised failed: the final report showed ticketing margin erosion and a 5.5% YoY fall in non-convenience fee revenue due to ad removal, agent restrictions, and Rail One diversion.
