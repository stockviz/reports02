GUJTHEM: Recent Corporate Developments — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Flat base business despite capacity constraints. Management acknowledged that revenues and margins were “pretty flat” from FY23 to FY26 because the company was sold out on capacity. Dr. Sachin Patel stated the company had been “silent for the past 3 years,” in capex and infrastructure-building mode, and “admittedly, we have been late by about a year in terms of the projects that have been implemented.”

API block delay. In a November 2023 call, management had guided that additional revenue from the API block would begin in FY25. As of the Q1 FY27 call (August 10, 2026), quarterly revenue was still around the historic ~INR 40 crore run rate. Management attributed the roughly two-year delay to insufficient fermentation capacity, which “has just begun now.”

Withdrawn merger with Themis Medicare. The merger announced around November 2024 was withdrawn about six months later. Management explained the initial rationale was to create a fully integrated pharmaceutical company with API fermentation and domestic brands, but an external strategic review shifted the focus toward a fermentation-based CDMO model instead of “other businesses, which were predominantly domestic in nature.”

Dispute with Optimus Drugs. A dispute arose after Optimus decided not to buy the company’s product. Management declined to detail the reasons due to confidentiality but stated the dispute is “behind us, and we are restarting business.” Management noted there were “more than enough buyers” for the product in the country and that capacity sold out even during the 6–9 months of non-supply.

Promoter encumbrance. An investor noted promoter pledge of around 48–48.5% as of June, with about 1.5–2% released in July. Management said it expects the pledge to “go down significantly” within a year to 15 months.

Integration and leverage concerns. An investor raised concerns about management bandwidth and leverage from two back-to-back major acquisitions relative to the company’s size. Management responded that both target businesses have been historically steady, that MBJ has a strong management continuing in place, and that the intent is not to turn around businesses but to bring synergies.

Regulatory timelines. The Sanofi portfolio acquisition requires regulatory approvals from over 50 countries, with a long stop date of June next year. The MBJ deal was expected to close by end-August 2026, subject to regulatory approvals.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue from operations was INR 43.8 crores versus INR 35.9 crores in Q1 FY26, up 22.1% year-on-year, driven by higher sales volumes. EBITDA grew 49.4% to INR 20.8 crores with a 47.5% margin, up 867 basis points. PAT was INR 11.1 crores versus INR 9.1 crores, up 22.1%. EPS for the quarter was INR 1.02.

Completed capex and new capacity. The new R&D and API units are up and running, and development of the new fermentation facility is complete. Fermentation capacity has practically doubled (capacities of up to 990 KL per the investor presentation), with some expansion also allocated to R&D and cGMP pilot facilities. The manufacturing site was to start “full steam” by end-August 2026. Management expects output from the expanded capacity to be visible partly in the current quarter and more fully in the second half of the year. The API block is producing Rifapentine, Rifaximin, and other molecules.

MicroBiopharm Japan (MBJ) acquisition. Deal value of JPY 21.5 billion (~INR 1,300 crore) for 100% stake via a wholly owned SPV in Japan, expected to close in Q2 FY2027. MBJ brings precision fermentation, a proprietary P450 enzyme library, plasmid DNA, ADC conjugation, and strain/process optimization technologies; therapy areas include immunosuppressants, oncology, and anti-infectives; 60% of revenue is CDMO; 60% of revenue is Japan and 40% outside Japan; FY26E revenue of JPY 9.5 billion (~INR 575 crore). Management said the acquisition provides a “jump start of 7 to 8 years” in capabilities that would otherwise take that long to build in-house, and expects some synergy projects to start within the first year.

Sanofi portfolio acquisition. Deal value ~EUR 158 million for 13 established brands in anti-TB and anti-infectives (including Levofloxacin, Rifampicin, Rifapentine), with ~EUR 62 million portfolio revenue (FY2025 actuals), reach across 55+ countries (Europe, MEA), ~70% retail channel contribution. Structured as an asset-light acquisition (brands, MAs, dossiers, inventory) with no manufacturing capex or employee transfer. A 3-year transition services agreement covers country-by-country transfer of marketing authorizations and manufacturing transfer; management hopes tech transfers complete in the first year. Expected to be EPS accretive with margin upside from API integration, cost optimization, and dormant MA reactivation.

Forward integration. The company is moving from intermediates to APIs and finished formulations, with the aim of capturing a larger share of the pharmaceutical value chain. Management noted the market shift from Rifampicin toward Rifapentine is favorable because more Rifa-S is required per kilo of Rifapentine, and the company is ready with both APIs.

Hybrid renewable power project. ~18 MW wind + solar hybrid for captive consumption, expected to reduce power costs and grid dependence. Phase 1 was expected to go live in September, with Phase 2 two months later. Management expects this to improve EBITDA margins.

Funding. The company allotted 58,500 NCDs aggregating INR 585 crores on a private placement basis (Series 1: up to 29,500 NCDs aggregating INR 2,950,000,000; Series 2: 29,000 NCDs aggregating INR 2,900,000,000), secured, redeemable, unlisted, rated, nominal value INR 1,00,000 each, with proceeds used to invest in the wholly owned Japanese subsidiary to finance the MBJ acquisition and for general corporate purposes. The company also allotted 2,11,86,440 equity shares at INR 354 per share to QIBs, aggregating INR 749,99,99,760, raising paid-up equity capital from INR 10,89,65,265 to INR 13,01,51,705. Management stated it is raising equity up to INR 1,000 crores and keeping debt options ready to achieve an optimal mix.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution and integration risk from two large overseas acquisitions relative to company size, including management bandwidth concerns raised by investors.
  • Leverage risk: NCD issuance of INR 585 crores and additional debt flexibility of INR 450 crores plus an INR 1,000 crore guarantee from the group company; an investor noted that at ~10% cost of capital, ~INR 200 crores of annual interest burden could absorb a large chunk of profits if the new business generates INR 1,000–1,200 crores of revenue.
  • Regulatory approval risk: Sanofi portfolio requires approvals from over 50 countries; MBJ close subject to regulatory approvals.
  • Customer concentration: management’s stated endeavor is to diversify by selling intermediates to anyone manufacturing these APIs in and outside the country.
  • Product mix transition risk: global shift from Rifampicin toward Rifapentine; management views this as favorable but noted the transition is “not as rapidly as we would like.”
  • ADC commercialization uncertainty: MBJ’s ADC technology is patented but has no CDMO project bagged yet; “only time will say how much interest it gains.”
  • Margin dilution risk from acquisitions; management hopes to be “close to where we are right now” once integration and stabilization are complete.
  • Promoter pledge at ~48–48.5% as of June, with management expecting significant reduction over 12–15 months.
  • Technical filing lapse: EPS was omitted from the XBRL filing submitted May 25, 2026; the company clarified this was a technical omission with no impact on financial results and submitted a corrected filing.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

API block revenue timing. In November 2023, management guided additional revenue from the API block in FY25. Observed: as of Q1 FY27 (August 2026), quarterly revenue remained around the historic ~INR 40 crore run rate. Management attributed the delay to insufficient fermentation capacity, now resolved.

Capex completion. Management stated most capex is now complete, with only INR 10–15 crores left plus maintenance capex; total FY27 capex including everything expected around INR 20-odd crores. Gross block moved from ~INR 62 crores in FY23 to ~INR 435 crores in FY26 including CWIP, an incremental addition of ~INR 370 crores over three years, comprising the new fermentation facility, API facility, new R&D infrastructure, and the hybrid power project. Of this, ~INR 200 crores relates to the fermentation and API facilities, with asset turns of ~1.4x–1.5x.

Growth guidance. Management declined to specify a top-line number but said on a base case it wants to grow on a “high-teen basis.” Management expects quarter-on-quarter growth from the expanded capacity, with output visible partly in the current quarter and more fully in H2.

MBJ closing. Expected close Q2 FY2027 (end-August 2026), subject to regulatory approvals; funding stated as “pretty much” secured.

Sanofi integration. Transition services agreement of 3 years maximum; management hopes tech transfers complete in the first year and manufacturing moves under its control earlier than 3 years.

Margin outlook. Management expects the hybrid power project to improve EBITDA margins and expects acquisitions to be close to current margins once stabilized, with additional upside from API integration into the Sanofi supply chain.

Concall frequency. Management committed to holding calls on a quarterly or at least semi-annual basis going forward, rather than one-off.
