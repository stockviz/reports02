India Pesticides Limited — Recent Developments Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Weak domestic demand and erratic monsoon: The quarter ended 30 June 2026 (Q1 FY27) was marked by subdued demand conditions in the domestic agrochemical market, particularly in paddy and other crop protection segments. Deficient and irregular rainfall led to delayed or reduced sowing, which weighed on demand for the company’s key herbicide, Pretilachlor. Domestic revenue fell to Rs. 167 crores in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 195 crores in Q1 FY26.

Revenue and margin decline: Consolidated revenue from operations declined 9.2% year-on-year to Rs. 256 crores in Q1 FY27 (from Rs. 282 crores). EBITDA fell to Rs. 39 crores with a 15.4% margin, compared with Rs. 52 crores and 18.4% in Q1 FY26. PAT declined to Rs. 23 crores (8.9% margin) from Rs. 35 crores (12.3%). Volume declined approximately 13% year-on-year, partly offset by a roughly 4% price increase.

Cost pressures: Margin moderation was attributed to lower sales volumes, higher employee costs, and increased fuel expenses. Other expenses included a one-time write-off of export receivables of Rs. 2.5 crores and higher job work charges (Rs. 14 crores versus Rs. 8 crores in Q1 FY26) incurred in anticipation of higher demand that did not materialise in June.

Elevated inventory and channel inventory: Inventory levels at quarter-end were higher than the same quarter last year because production was planned for anticipated higher sales. Inventory days rose from about 170 days to around 200 days, with management expecting a return to roughly 170 days by Q3. Elevated channel inventory also weighed on profitability.

Competitive pressure from Chinese imports: Management noted the market has become highly competitive, with Chinese products available at relatively lower prices, requiring ongoing process optimisation and engineering cost reductions to compete.

Tax dispute: An Income Tax Department search was conducted in December 2024, followed by assessment proceedings resulting in additions to undisclosed income aggregating Rs. 10.12 crores and an additional tax demand including interest of Rs. 7.10 crores, against which the company has filed an appeal.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

EU Technical Equivalence approval: During Q1 FY27, IPL received Technical Equivalence (TEQ) approval from the European Union for one of its fungicide products. Management expects this to add Rs. 30–40 crores of revenue from the molecule in the EU, in addition to the approximately Rs. 100 crores of annual revenue already generated from it, with sales likely commencing from November as customers complete source-approval processes.

Further international registrations: The company subsequently obtained Technical Equivalence approval for an insecticide product in the United Kingdom and registration for a herbicide product in Argentina, both on 15 September 2026. These approvals are expected to support export growth and contribute to foreign exchange earnings.

Stable exports: Export revenue rose marginally to Rs. 89 crores in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 87 crores in Q1 FY26, contributing approximately 35% of total revenue, supported by stable international demand. IPL serves customers across more than 35 countries.

New product and capacity initiatives: IPL is developing a fungicide currently imported from China, with an estimated Indian import market of 4,000–5,000 tons annually; the company targets at least 2,000 tons of capacity, priced at roughly Rs. 500–600 per kg, produced at the Sandila facility using existing infrastructure with Rs. 2–3 crores of balancing equipment. A multipurpose herbicide plant is being built at the Hamirpur subsidiary, with two of ten blocks currently operational and two additional blocks targeted for operation in FY2027.

Hamirpur expansion pathway: Management expects Rs. 50–60 crores of revenue from the Hamirpur facility in the current year, scaling to approximately Rs. 1,000 crores over three to four years as 8–10 blocks are commissioned, with capex of Rs. 70–100 crores per year at Hamirpur and Rs. 25–30 crores at Sandila.

Backward integration: An intermediate plant developed with in-house indigenous R&D technology supports supply chain stability and reduces dependence on imported intermediates.

Formulation and distribution reach: The formulation business operates with 10,200 metric tons of capacity, a sales network across 18 states, 24 depots, and over 370 sales professionals.

Governance and shareholder actions: All six resolutions at the 41st Annual General Meeting held on 31 August 2026, including adoption of financial statements, declaration of dividend, re-appointment and remuneration of directors, and ratification of cost auditors, were passed with overwhelming majority.

3. Key Risks

  • Monsoon and weather variability: Erratic rainfall patterns — both excess and deficit — disrupted sowing and crop conditions, directly affecting demand for key products such as Pretilachlor.
  • Product concentration: Management acknowledged historical dependence on individual star molecules, with one molecule underperforming in a given year affecting overall performance; the company is adding molecules to reduce this dependence.
  • Competitive intensity: Continued availability of lower-priced Chinese products pressures pricing and margins.
  • Regulatory and approval timelines: Monetisation of the EU TEQ approval depends on customers completing source-addition approvals, which may take one to two months.
  • Working capital: Higher inventory levels and receivable days of around 120 days affect the working capital cycle, though receivables were described as stable and coming in on time.
  • Tax litigation: The income tax assessment additions and demand are under appeal.
  • Geopolitical and external environment: Management linked margin recovery partly to improvement in the overall geopolitical situation.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Margin guidance: Management stated the 15.4% EBITDA margin achieved in Q1 FY27 is sustainable, with an aspiration to reach around 18% as conditions improve, and at least 15–16% on a sustainable basis. This compares with the 18.4% margin delivered in Q1 FY26 and 18.0% for FY26, indicating current performance is below the prior-year level but within the stated sustainable range.

Revenue guidance: Management expects Q3 and Q4 to be better and guided to lower single-digit growth for FY27, following the 9.2% year-on-year revenue decline in Q1 FY27. Management also indicated the current quarter could remain subdued.

Hamirpur revenue guidance: Management guided Rs. 50–60 crores of revenue from Hamirpur in FY27, revising down from an investor’s suggestion of Rs. 70–80 crores, with new products starting manufacture from month-end. The longer-term target of approximately Rs. 1,000 crores over three to four years remains unchanged.

Capex funding: Management reiterated that expansion will be funded through internal accruals without term loans, supported by a healthy cash balance of Rs. 59 crores as at 30 June 2026.

Working capital guidance: Management expects inventory days to decline from around 200 days to approximately 170 days by Q3, with receivable days stable at around 120 days.

Raw material outlook: Management noted raw material prices have declined and finished goods prices have moderated, expressing confidence in reasonable margins in the coming quarter.
