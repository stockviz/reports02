SMC Global Securities — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Derivatives segment pressure. The derivatives segment continued to reflect the impact of regulatory measures implemented over the past year. Management described the industry as shifting toward more balanced and diversified business models through wealth management, distribution, advisory and other fee-based businesses. On the call, Ajay Garg noted a segmental shift from derivative to cash market revenue, with cash market brokerage rising from 45% to 55% of the mix.

NBFC AUM contraction and product exits. The NBFC AUM stood at ₹1,025 crores as of June 2026, versus ₹1,118 crores for the quarter ended March 31, 2026. Himanshu Gupta attributed the decline to deliberate product actions: LAP was discontinued (a decline of about ₹50 crores during the quarter) and unsecured business loans were tightened as a precaution (a decline of about ₹40 crores). He described Q1 as “a little slow in the lending” and noted a rub-off effect from discontinued products that would persist for a year or two.

Insurance broking EBIT moderation. Insurance broking revenue grew 44.4% YoY to ₹167.3 crores, but segment EBIT was only ₹1.6 crores. The company attributed the moderated EBIT growth versus revenue to investments in distribution capacity and technology infrastructure to support the upcoming reinsurance opportunity following its composite broker upgrade. Sakshi Mehta cited rising corporate and life insurance manpower costs.

Financing segment performance. Q1 FY27 financing revenue was ₹46.5 crores with segment EBIT of ₹26.1 crores. Management described the moderated financial performance as reflecting a calibrated approach to growth amid an evolving operating environment.

External market factors. FPI selling of around ₹1.5 lakh crores during the quarter and intermittent global macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainties, including the US-Iran issue, were cited as factors in the operating environment, though crude prices declined during the quarter.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Broking, distribution and trading. Q1 FY27 revenue rose 15.1% YoY to ₹316.3 crores, with segment EBIT up 17.6% YoY to ₹74.3 crores. The broking client base including StoxKart grew to 13.8 lakh accounts, and broking DP AUA expanded to ₹1,64,962 crores. The MTF and T+5 book increased from ₹760 crores to more than ₹900 crores. Ajay Garg noted Nifty and Sensex grew almost 6–7% in the quarter, DII buying exceeded ₹2 lakh crores, and stated Q2 “might be much better” and the coming year “should be much better as compared to the last year.”

Distribution and wealth management. Cumulative mutual fund AUM reached ₹4,787 crores, up from ₹4,294 crores as on March 2026 — approximately 11.5% growth — supported by 92,129 active SIPs. Anurag Bansal cited enhanced product training, investor awareness initiatives and adoption of the Easy Invest digital distribution platform, with growth driven by both market appreciation and net mobilization. Wealth management focus has increased on HNIs and family offices, with tie-ups with third-party manufacturers as a priority.

Insurance broking. Gross premium for the quarter was ₹759 crores, with 2.7 lakh policies issued, supported by 492 employees, 16,747 POS agents and 385 Motor Insurance Service Providers. Non-life insurance contributed approximately 90% of total premium and was the major factor in YoY growth. The composite broker upgrade opens an upcoming reinsurance opportunity.

StoxKart. Pranay Aggarwal stated the last five quarters were strong, with revenue quadrupling YoY; Q1 FY26 contributed ₹20 crores in revenue, and around 26,000 subscription clients were added in the quarter under the Smart Trader Plan.

Financing. The secured book constitutes 75% of AUM, higher than the prior quarter and up 5% YoY. Weighted cost of funds declined by around 25 basis points, including on existing borrowings at annual reset. Management expects NIM to grow gradually, supported by a shift to higher-yielding retail products such as Micro LAP and Gold Loan.

Technology and AI. Shruti Aggarwal described a base of AI enablement built over six months, including an AI-based chatbot using a proprietary low-language model and AI agent, an AI-based Algo platform, and AI-generated insights in the mobile app (in testing). Subhash Chand Aggarwal cited investments in technology, AI automation and infrastructure, competitive pricing versus main brokers, and optimism about Gift City prospects, where the company describes itself as a major player. A tie-up with smallcase was also mentioned.

Structural drivers. Sustained SIP inflows, increasing financialization of household savings, growing retail participation, improving insurance penetration, and improving systemic liquidity with easing interest rate expectations were cited as supportive.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory risk: The derivatives segment continues to reflect regulatory measures from the past year; the industry operates in an evolving regulatory environment focused on governance, risk management and technology infrastructure.
  • Credit and asset quality risk in NBFC: Lenders remain focused on portfolio quality and disciplined underwriting; the company prioritizes portfolio quality amid an evolving operating environment. The discontinued LAP and tightened unsecured business loan books illustrate product-level credit caution.
  • Execution risk on new initiatives: The reinsurance opportunity depends on investments in distribution capacity and technology following the composite broker upgrade; operating leverage in insurance is expected only in future quarters.
  • Market and macro risk: Global macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainties, FPI flows and crude price movements affect market activity.
  • Interest rate risk: The interest rate environment was described as volatile; funding cost and NIM trends depend on rate movements and product mix.
  • Dependence on market activity: Broking revenue growth was supported by stable market activity and client engagement; a segmental shift from derivatives to cash market changes revenue composition.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

NBFC AUM. Prior guidance suggested approximately 25% growth in NBFC AUM. Actual AUM declined from ₹1,118 crores (March 2026) to ₹1,025 crores (June 2026). Management attributed the variance to discontinued LAP and tightened unsecured business loans. For the full year, Himanshu Gupta still expects to close at about ₹1,250–1,300 crores. The stated medium-term aim is 20–25% CAGR, but management said the rub-off effect from discontinued products means investors should wait a year or two before seeing that kind of CAGR.

Consolidated financials. Q1 FY27 consolidated operational income was ₹515.1 crores, up 21.2% YoY. EBITDA was ₹107 crores, up 6.9% YoY and 19.3% QoQ, with a 20.9% margin. PAT was ₹36.7 crores versus ₹30 crores in Q1 FY26, up 22.3% YoY. Standalone operational income was ₹273.4 crores, up 11.1% YoY, with EBITDA of ₹71.5 crores at a 26.2% margin (100 bps YoY expansion) and standalone PAT of ₹25.1 crores, up 9.6% YoY.

Long-term growth targets. Subhash Chand Aggarwal stated the company is growing at a CAGR of 20% year on year and expects to grow better than this. He indicated that from current revenue of more than ₹2,000 crores, 20% growth would imply revenue of around ₹6,000–8,000 crores in five years. On PAT, he noted last year’s figure was around ₹103 crores and said “we are expecting ₹170 crores,” while adding that figures are not advisable to disclose, and reaffirmed 20% CAGR growth.

Insurance operating leverage. Management expects operating leverage to improve in future quarters as results from manpower and technology investments flow through.

NIM. Management expects NIM to grow gradually over the coming years, supported by a higher-yielding retail product mix and a 25 bps reduction in borrowing cost achieved in the quarter.

Cross-selling. Anurag Bansal said mutual funds have been integrated on the broking mobile app, with other products in the pipeline, and expects integration to unlock cross-selling potential within the network.

  • The statutory auditor’s review report on the consolidated results noted that eight subsidiaries were reviewed by other auditors (total revenues of ₹24,790.89 lakhs, net profit after tax of ₹1,638.82 lakhs for the quarter), and one subsidiary was not reviewed by its auditor (total revenues of ₹205.08 lakhs, net profit after tax of ₹92.09 lakhs), with management certifying those results as not material to the Group.
  • The auditor’s report on book values and covenants confirmed that, as at June 30, 2026, book values of assets were in agreement with the books of account and the company was in compliance with the covenants in the Debenture Trust Deeds.
