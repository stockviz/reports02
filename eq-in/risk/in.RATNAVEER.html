Ratnaveer Precision Engineering: Recent Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

  • Sequential margin compression. Although EBITDA rose 31.7% year-on-year to ₹35.98 crore in Q1 FY27, EBITDA margin fell to 11.43% from 14.32% in Q4 FY26, a decline of 289 basis points quarter-on-quarter. PAT margin similarly slipped to 5.80% from 6.84% sequentially, even as it improved 15 basis points year-on-year.
  • Export contribution remains small. Of Q1 FY27 revenue, domestic revenue was ₹304.68 crore against export revenue of ₹9.96 crore, leaving the business heavily dependent on the domestic market.
  • Execution dependence on the CCL project. The Copper Clad Laminate project is approximately 60% complete, with commercial production targeted for November 2026. Key machinery shipments were still expected to arrive shortly as of the Q1 FY27 announcement, leaving commissioning milestones ahead.
  • Rights issue mechanics. The rights issue involved the extinguishment of lapsed rights entitlements and deactivation of ISIN INE05CZ20011, indicating that not all entitlements were subscribed.
  • Governance and capital-structure actions pending shareholder approval. The forthcoming AGM agenda includes raising borrowing limits from ₹900 crore to ₹1,500 crore (or the aggregate of paid-up capital and free reserves, whichever is higher) and creating mortgages or charges on company properties, both of which require shareholder consent.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong top-line momentum. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was ₹314.64 crore, up 18.9% year-on-year and 26.4% quarter-on-quarter. Total income rose 20.2% year-on-year to ₹318.43 crore.
  • Faster earnings growth than revenue. EBITDA grew 31.7% year-on-year, and PAT grew 22.0% year-on-year to ₹18.24 crore, with PBT up 21.9% to ₹21.88 crore. Management attributed this to operating leverage and execution discipline.
  • Copper Clad Laminate import-substitution opportunity. Management described a large domestic supply-demand gap in CCL, positioning the project as an import-substitution play with meaningful contributions to growth and profitability in the years ahead. The project has received in-principle approval under the Gujarat Electronics Policy for the proposed ₹472.34 crore investment.
  • Recognition and credit profile. The company received the Star Performer – Large Enterprise Award at the 42nd EEPC India Export Awards. Infomerics upgraded the long-term rating to IVR A- (Stable) and the short-term rating to IVR A2+, with rated bank facilities enhanced to ₹388.47 crore.
  • Funding visibility. NSE and BSE granted in-principle approvals for a proposed rights issue of up to ₹330 crore to support expansion and strategic growth. The rights issue was subsequently allotted at ₹264 per share for 1,24,99,669 equity shares, raising post-rights paid-up equity share capital to ₹83,92,63,500 across 8,39,26,350 shares.
  • Diversified end markets. The product portfolio spans stainless steel washers, solar mounting hooks, finishing sheets, SS tubes and pipes, and copper clad laminates, serving automotive, solar, wind energy, power plants, water treatment, food processing, oil and gas, and pharmaceuticals.

Key Risks

  • Margin sustainability. The sequential drop in EBITDA and PAT margins despite revenue growth indicates that profitability can be sensitive to cost or mix shifts.
  • Project timing and commissioning. The CCL project’s November 2026 commercial production target depends on machinery deliveries and completion of the remaining project work; slippage would delay the anticipated growth contribution.
  • Capital raise and dilution. The rights issue increased the equity base, and the EPS figures reflect this: basic and diluted EPS was ₹10.36 in Q1 FY27 against ₹2.94 in Q4 FY26 and ₹2.97 in Q1 FY26.
  • Leverage capacity expansion. The proposed increase in borrowing limits to ₹1,500 crore and the creation of mortgages or charges expand the company’s debt capacity, which carries attendant financial risk.
  • Forward-looking statement uncertainty. The company’s own cautionary statement notes that projections and estimates involve known and unknown risks and should not be taken as forecasts or promises, and that Q1 FY27 financials are unaudited and subject to limited review.
  • Concentrated domestic exposure. With exports at only ₹9.96 crore in Q1 FY27, the business is exposed to domestic demand and policy conditions.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Growth pace. Management stated that Q1 FY27 revenue growth of 18.9% year-on-year was “well ahead of the pace we set through FY26.” The reported figures support that revenue accelerated both year-on-year and quarter-on-quarter.
  • Operating leverage. Management said the combination of double-digit revenue growth and faster earnings growth “reflects the operating leverage inherent in our business.” Observed performance is consistent with this at the year-on-year level, with EBITDA up 31.7% and PAT up 22.0% against revenue growth of 18.9%. Sequentially, however, EBITDA grew only 0.9% and margins contracted, so the leverage effect was not uniform across periods.
  • CCL project timeline. Management stated the project is “roughly 60% complete and on track for commercial production in November 2026” and that it “positions us to capture a large and underserved domestic market through import substitution.” The operational highlights confirm approximately 60% completion and the November 2026 target, with key machinery shipments expected shortly. The revenue and profitability contribution remains prospective.
  • Rights issue proceeds. Management described the rights issue as supporting “future expansion and strategic growth initiatives.” The subsequent allotment of 1,24,99,669 shares at ₹264 each, raising paid-up equity capital to ₹83,92,63,500, confirms the capital raise was completed, though the deployment of proceeds and their returns are not yet evidenced in the reported results.
  • Investor engagement. A one-on-one virtual meeting with Fintrekk Capital was held on June 23, 2026, following intimation on June 18, 2026, with discussion limited to publicly available information and no unpublished price sensitive information shared.
  • AGM and governance items. The 24th Annual General Meeting is scheduled for September 26, 2026, via video conferencing, with a cut-off date of September 19, 2026. Agenda items include re-appointment of M/s. Pankaj R Shah & Associates as statutory auditors for a second five-year term, ratification of cost auditor remuneration for M/s. Ashish Bhavsar & Associates, the borrowing-limit increase, creation of mortgages or charges, and appointment of Mrs. Seema Sanghavi as Whole-time Director for five years from August 20, 2026.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a broad multi-thesis story (CCL, integrated stainless steel platform, operational efficiency, domestic penetration) to a narrower emphasis on CCL as the key growth driver, with execution and capacity expansion taking center stage. The initial concern about equity dilution was reframed as a positive balance-sheet strengthening via the rights issue, while the persistent headwinds of industrial capex slowdown and high working capital requirement remained unchanged.

Fears that came true

  • The slowdown in industrial capital expenditure likely materialized, as the latest actual return of 0.0% fell far short of the +20.4% prediction.
  • High working capital requirement proved to be an ongoing drag, contributing to the disappointment in the latest period.

Optimism that failed

  • The CCL expansion’s promise to unlock a new growth leg failed to generate returns, with the stock flat versus the predicted +20.4%.
  • The strong execution track record and resilient core business did not translate into the expected positive performance, as the shares delivered zero return in the latest period.
