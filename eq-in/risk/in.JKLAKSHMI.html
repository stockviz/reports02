JK Lakshmi Cement — Q1 FY27 Conference Call and AGM Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Geopolitical disruption to fuel supply chain. The Middle East conflict has disrupted global supply chains, particularly affecting imported fuel — coal and pet coke. Management noted that players in Northern India were more dependent on imported coal and pet coke, and were therefore impacted more on cost. Related inputs such as explosives and chemicals also saw price increases.

Cost pass-through has been partial. While price increases occurred, they did not keep pace with cost increases. Non-trade prices rose in some markets; trade prices were largely flat, improving only modestly in some markets. Management stated that pass-through has not happened to the extent costs have risen.

Fuel cost escalation. Fuel cost per Kcal rose from 1.54 in the preceding quarter to 1.65 in Q1 FY27 — an increase of about INR 0.11. Management indicated Q2 fuel cost would be higher still, potentially touching 1.8-plus or even 1.85. Pet coke prices shot up beyond 140–145, and imported coal was around USD 130–135 per ton.

Packaging cost inflation. Granule prices, which had softened to around 134, rose to 145. Management estimated an impact of about INR 3.5 to INR 4 per bag, over and above Q1.

Raw material cost pressure. Higher costs arose from kiln shutdowns in April–June requiring fly ash procurement from far-off (L2) sources, gypsum availability variations across chemical, mineral and bold gypsum, diesel price impact, and raw material requirements of the growing non-cement (SBS) business. Increased trade sales (59% of the mix) necessitated more fly ash/cementitious material procurement, including from L2 sources.

Freight cost. Freight cost per ton inched up by INR 30, attributed to diesel price increases for a month, though management stated the lead-distance reduction surpassed the freight inflation given to transporters.

Other expenses. The sharp jump in other expenses was attributed to packing cost increases and normative volume-related increases.

Seasonal and cyclical pressure. July–September is cyclically weaker for demand, and annual maintenance shutdowns are taken during this period, impacting margins. Management flagged a “double impact” in Q2 from leverage and higher cost.

Clinker sales decline. Clinker sales volume was 1.63 lakh tons, a fall quarter-on-quarter.

Northeast land litigation. A PIL was filed in the Guwahati High Court by nearby villages claiming land acquired from the local council/Assam government belongs to them. Mahabal Cement (a subsidiary) was the original recipient; JK Lakshmi was added as a notice recipient because Mahabal is its subsidiary. Management noted the case predates this instance.

Proxy advisory opposition at the AGM. At the 86th AGM (held 30 July 2026), proxy advisors recommended negative voting on some resolutions despite the company’s position that they were in full compliance with law and SEBI LODR. Some investors voted against based on those recommendations. Even the re-election of professional director Mr. Shukla was recommended to be negatively voted by some international proxy advisors. Management noted proxy advisors issue reports two days before voting, give 24–48 hours to comment, and generally attach the company’s response via addendum without changing their recommendation. International proxy advisors do not provide the same opportunity.

SES report concerns. SES’s report (23 July 2026) raised: approval not sought for continuation beyond 70 years of age; remuneration skewed in favour of proposed NRC member and Promoter EDs; absence of an absolute cap on variable pay; and the combined position of Chairperson and Managing Director. The company rebutted each point in its 24 July 2026 representation, citing shareholder approvals from 2021 and 2025 AGMs, compliance with Section 196 and Section 197 limits, the discretionary nature of separating Chairperson and MD posts under SEBI LODR, and Smt. Vinita Singhania’s abstention from NRC discussions on her own remuneration.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Demand growth. Q1 FY27 demand was reasonably all right despite external issues. Industry grew about 8%. Supply-side additions were around 11 million tons, with overall addition estimated at about 52 million tons. Total installed cement capacity in India after the 11 million ton addition is about 725 million tons. Industry capacity utilization averaged about 73–74%.

Realization improvement. Realization rose about 9% sequentially, driven by non-trade price increases (in Gujarat, Mumbai, East and North), some trade price improvement (West and East), geo-mix optimization, and a 20-kilometre reduction in lead distance (from 388 to 368). The lead reduction translated to about INR 60–70 per ton. Sales in the four major markets (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Chhattisgarh, Haryana and part of Western UP) rose to close to 90%, about 10 percentage points higher than the prior quarter.

Internal mitigation levers. The company is working on using more indigenous coal to replace imported pet coke, renewable energy, AFR, logistics, and top-line elements (volume, premium products, geo mix).

Renewable energy. Renewable energy was 49% of consumption in the last quarter (solar, wind and WHRS). Installed capacity: solar 129 MW, WHRS 45 MW, wind 4 MW, thermal 74 MW. Plans are underway to further improve the renewable percentage over 6–8 months.

Solar SPV. A captive-power-route SPV for 42 MW of solar power, with about INR 20 crores investment, contracted at a fixed tariff of INR 4.10 (landed cost about INR 5.85), substituting grid power at INR 7.50 — a saving of INR 1.65 per unit and payback of less than two years. Implementation expected in eight to nine months, with benefits from the fag end of Q4 or for sure from Q1 of the next financial year.

BESS. A 28 MW battery energy storage system is planned, sized based on demand-supply balancing and Rajasthan’s solar-to-contract-demand ratio of twice, to avoid wasting generated energy.

Capacity expansion. Installed and operational capacity expected to reach 18 million tons by the end of the financial year. The 30 million ton by FY30 target is maintained and on track. Capex guidance: INR 1,500 crores in FY27, INR 2,000 crores next year, then INR 1,500 crores — about INR 5,000 crores total, including about INR 3,000 crores for Durg and INR 1,500 crores for Northeast, but excluding land acquisition for Kutch and Nagore. INR 300 crores was spent in Q1; INR 400 crores aggregate on Durg so far. Major equipment ordering is done for Durg (second clinker 9 plus grinding), Patratu, Prayagraj and Madhubani.

Northeast expansion. Two mines were won through auction; mining plan submission and environmental clearances are in progress. Initial 1.5 million ton plan is underway, with plant land and clearances before identifying grinding station locations. Management said the project is back on track and more details may come next quarter.

Non-cement business. Q1 non-cement revenue was INR 185 crores (RMC INR 93 crores, AAC 60–70, POP 23), with 5% EBITDA margin. Full-year non-cement revenue estimated at INR 800 crores plus, versus INR 613 crores last year.

Railway siding and conveyor. The railway project is contingent on collaboration with Steel Authority of India and other stakeholders (including a PWD road), but operations continue fully. The overhead conveyor belt approval from SAIL has been received; the proposed agreement is now with SAIL for contracting.

Debt discipline. Management would not like to cross net debt to EBITDA of 2.5x to at best 2.75x, potentially touching that level toward FY29–30, provided the roadmap for tapering is clear.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued uncertainty in the cost landscape, particularly fuel, with Q2 fuel cost expected higher than Q1.
  • Inability to fully pass through cost increases, especially as it coincides with demand cyclicity — management called the extent of pass-through “a big question mark.”
  • Pet coke and imported coal prices remaining elevated (pet coke above 140–145; imported coal USD 130–135 per ton).
  • Packaging cost increases of INR 3.5–4 per bag over and above Q1.
  • Monsoon-quarter lead distance expected to rise, reversing part of the Q1 improvement.
  • Litigation over Northeast land (Guwahati High Court PIL) involving Mahabal Cement and JK Lakshmi.
  • Proxy advisory opposition and negative voting by some investors on AGM resolutions, which management said only delays appointments.
  • Capex execution and land acquisition for Kutch and Nagore going slow; land acquisition costs excluded from capex guidance.
  • Railway siding project dependent on collaboration with SAIL and other stakeholders.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Fuel cost. Management guided Q2 fuel cost higher than Q1’s 1.65, potentially 1.8-plus or 1.85. Observed: Q1 fuel cost rose to 1.65 from 1.54 in the preceding quarter.

Capex. Guidance of INR 1,500 crores for FY27, INR 2,000 crores next year, INR 1,500 crores thereafter. Observed: INR 300 crores spent in Q1; INR 400 crores aggregate on Durg so far.

Capacity. Guidance of 18 million tons installed and operational by end of the financial year; 30 million tons by FY30 maintained. Observed: overall utilization 76%; clinker utilization 95%.

Non-cement revenue. Guidance of INR 800 crores plus for the full year. Observed: INR 185 crores in Q1, with 5% EBITDA margin; prior year INR 613 crores.

Solar SPV benefit. Guidance of implementation in eight to nine months, with benefits from the fag end of Q4 or for sure Q1 of next financial year. Observed: project not yet implemented.

Lead distance. Management stated lead distance will rise in the monsoon/lean months despite team targets to contain it. Observed: lead fell from 388 to 368 in Q1.

Geo mix. Management described geo mix as a systematic, multi-year approach, improving in demand months and widening dispersion in lean months, while maintaining share in core markets. Observed: sales in the four major markets rose to close to 90%, about 10 percentage points higher than the prior quarter.

Pricing. Management stated prices had not gone down in July–August and July was better than previous years’ July, with demand improvement potentially supporting price increases. Observed: realization up about 9% sequentially in Q1.

AGM resolutions. Management stated all resolutions were passed with the requisite majority, including the special resolution, despite negative proxy advisory recommendations. Observed: the scrutinizer’s consolidated report dated 30 July 2026 concluded all resolutions were passed with requisite majority.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from near-term caution on cost inflation and capex-driven debt (2023) toward a medium-term constructive view on volume growth, margin recovery via cost optimization, and capacity scaling, while persistent concerns around cost pressures, debt, and valuation upside carried forward into the latest Hold call (2026). The optimistic case for margin expansion and deleveraging was repeatedly deferred, and the sustained string of DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes forced downgrades from Accumulate/Buy to Hold.

Fears that came true

  • Cost inflation and margin compression flagged in 2023 materialized, with EBITDA/ton declining 23.2% YoY to ₹719/ton and variable-cost inflation of ₹150+/ton in Q2FY27.
  • Debt build-up from the UCWL capex persisted, with net debt/EBITDA remaining elevated at ~2.5x (2.75x upper limit under aggressive expansion).
  • Weak near-term cement pricing and operating deleverage flagged in 2023 continued to pressure profitability into 2026, with Q2FY27 seasonal demand weakness and cost spikes.
  • Fuel cost escalation materialized, with power & fuel costs rising from ₹1.484/tn (2023) to ₹1.65/kcal in Q1FY27 and an expected ₹1.80–1.85/kcal in Q2FY27 amid West Asia crises.

Optimism that failed

  • The targeted EBITDA/tn of ₹1,000 by FY25 via geo-mix, premiumization, and cost optimization failed to materialize, with actual EBITDA/ton at ₹719 in Q1FY27.
  • Deleveraging from ₹11.8 bn in FY24E to ₹6.3 bn in FY25E did not occur, as net debt/EBITDA remained elevated at ~2.5x.
  • The 19% FY24E volume growth target and strong CAGR projections did not translate into sustained share-price outperformance, with the stock delivering multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes despite healthy 8.2% YoY volume growth in Q1FY27.
  • Cost optimization benefits from TSR increases, WHRS, and solar projects were offset by rising fly ash, gypsum, fuel, and packaging costs, preventing the expected margin recovery.
