Gufic Biosciences — Recent Corporate Announcements

Headwinds and Challenges

Margin recovery from capitalisation unwind. Management explained that the operating margin of around 18% seen between June 2023 and September 2024 was flattered because the total expense of the Indore facility, along with depreciation and interest, was being capitalised until roughly October 2024 (Q3 FY25). The subsequent drop in margin reflected those costs flowing through the P&L. The 18.09% EBITDA margin reported for Q1 FY27 is described as the start of margin improvement rather than a return to the earlier, capitalisation-supported level.

Indore capacity utilisation still well below full. Utilisation has moved from roughly 18% to 22%, then 20% to 25%, and is now close to 30%–35%, with a year-end target of 40%–45%. Margin expansion from the facility is tied to securing EU certification, which is still awaited.

EU certification for Indore pending. The company already holds EU certification for Navsari. For Indore, compliance submissions and final data have been filed over the preceding two to three months, and management expects feedback from the authority within a month or two.

Botulinum toxin capacity constraint. The current toxin facility is described as WHO Phase 1 with limited capacity. This restricts registration of Stunnox and Zarbot to Southeast Asian and African markets initially; broader global markets are deferred, potentially until after next year, pending further cash flow.

GLP-1 ramp dependent on a partner. Semaglutide operations are purely CMO for Hetero, with no front-end participation in India or abroad. Hetero received permissions in May 2026, so Q1 revenue traction was residual. A 10–15 day plant shutdown occurred in Q1 to install a new machine, and further short shutdowns are expected around media/plant visits. Meaningful traction is expected in Q3.

Product mix drag. Management noted that some product mix is being squeezed out and will be “low yielding,” which is cited as a reason the 15%–20% growth guidance is not considered conservative.

Employee cost step-up. A substantial employee addition was noted in the annual report. Management characterised this largely as a one-time transition of contract staff onto payroll, particularly at Indore and Navsari, for consistency in quality management systems and output. This is not expected to repeat at the same scale this year.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Indore scale-up and new capabilities. The Indore plant is running to plan, with qualification and validation complete and product tech transfers progressing. The depot and microsphere suite is nearing completion, enabling in-house manufacture of long-acting depot presentations during the year. A lipid-based antifungal is being set up via both a conventional and an alternate approach, with both targeted to be operational during the year. Indore is fully operational; additions are new product lines within existing capex rather than major new infrastructure.

Critical care launch. A monobactam and beta-lactamase inhibitor combination was launched immediately on expiry of the innovator patent and introduced across corporate, tertiary and secondary care networks, with encouraging early acceptance in large institutions. The division’s focus is depth and coverage within existing hospital groups rather than portfolio expansion.

Sparsh rebuild. Sparsh completed a full quarter on the rebuilt channel, with outstanding days within standard trade terms, hospital onboarding resumed at scale, and coverage balanced between nursing homes and corporate chains. Focus has shifted to own manufactured brands, aiding margin and supply reliability. The dual chamber bag is gaining acceptance in major institutions. The division entered Northeast and Jammu & Kashmir using the new channel architecture, and carries its widest-ever launch pipeline.

Women’s health and Zenova. Ferticare retains leadership in recurring implantation failure; the Puregraf group secured entry into major corporate IVF chains, and investigator-led studies with senior Indian clinicians have begun. Zenova continues its planned shift from injectables toward prescription-led chronic therapies, with the antioxidant range now a meaningful growth player and two first-mover launches ahead — one in osteoarthritis and one addressing the metabolic ovarian segment.

Botulinum toxin and aesthetics. Gufic remains the number 2 brand in India in toxin type A manufactured from its own strain. The in-licensed filler and biostimulator portfolio is progressing through supply and regulatory steps for launch this financial year with no significant capital expenditure. The franchise is expanding beyond core neurology into urology, ophthalmology, pain management and neurosurgery. The Revanesse Prollenium (Canada) filler tie-up addresses an Indian filler market estimated at around INR 200 crores, larger than the current toxin market; registration is expected by Q2 or mid-Q3, with launch around December or January. Management aims to strengthen the number 2 position and approach number 1 in toxin and fillers in India over three to five years.

Nutraceutical and Ayurveda. The lead joint care range outperformed its relevant product market this quarter. The asset blocker continues building gastrointestinal therapy as a second pillar alongside pain, and the division has prepared its first entry into allopathic pain management, launching during the year.

International model change. The shift from distributor-led B2B toward IP-led front-end presence is producing fee income alongside supply revenue. During the quarter, licensing progressed in Europe, a contract manufacturing and licensing arrangement was executed with a North American counterparty, and first contract manufacturing orders were received in Australia. Approvals were secured across 8 countries, including 5 presentations in a single therapy area cleared on the same day in one country. A team has been recruited across Mexico, Africa, the Philippines and Mumbai. Management indicated that where margins are currently around 40%–50%, an own field force could add 15%–20%, while IP and trademarks remain with the company.

CHAI partnership. A tie-up with the CHAI Foundation covers liposomal amphotericin B, providing access to more than 100 markets. Bioequivalence studies were initiated last year and are expected ready by August or September, after which filings for WHO PQ and other countries will follow. The molecule is made at Navsari, with introduction at Indore underway.

Philippines subsidiary. The Board approved incorporation of Gufic Philippines Inc. (or a similarly approved name) within 12 months, subject to statutory and regulatory formalities, with investment of up to USD 250,000 for approximately 99.99% equity. The subsidiary will undertake marketing, sale, distribution and allied activities and hold and manage IP rights in the Philippines.

Leadership additions. Ms. Ami Shah was promoted to Head-Legal while continuing as Company Secretary and Compliance Officer, and Mr. Pinak Padhya was promoted to President – Sales & Marketing, both effective September 02, 2026, reflecting enhanced leadership responsibilities and continued growth and expansion driven by experienced senior management.

Key Risks

  • Dependence on Hetero’s regulatory approvals and commercial ramp for semaglutide CMO revenue, with no front-end participation by Gufic.
  • Timing risk on Indore EU certification, which gates margin expansion and access to additional export markets.
  • Capacity limitation in botulinum toxin restricting geographic registration and launch scope.
  • Execution risk on multiple simultaneous launches and registrations across aesthetics, women’s health, critical care, nutraceuticals and international markets.
  • Product mix risk, with management noting some mix being squeezed out and low-yielding business.
  • Dependence on partner-triggered regulatory milestones, including a partner’s US submission from the Gufic site and an expected inspection visit.
  • Foreign subsidiary operations subject to local accounting conversion and regulatory approvals, as noted in the limited review report covering Gufic Prime Private Limited, Gufic UK Limited, Yeira Life FZE and Gufic Ireland Limited.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Revenue growth. Management guides to 15%–20% year-on-year growth as a minimum commitment, with efforts to grow beyond that. Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue from operations was INR 260.8 crores against INR 226.9 crores in Q1 FY26, approximately 16.5% higher, and against INR 252.1 crores in Q4 FY26.

Margins. Management indicated the 18% level marks the start of margin improvement, supported by capacity utilisation, business progression, export margin expansion and domestic business. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 18.09%, versus 14.6% in Q1 FY26 and 17.74% in Q4 FY26. PBT margin was 11.56% versus 7.1% and 10.96% respectively; PAT margin was 8.61% versus 5.3%.

Profit. Q1 FY27 PBT was INR 30.1 crores (Q1 FY26: INR 16.3 crores; Q4 FY26: INR 27.6 crores). Q1 FY27 PAT was INR 22.46 crores (Q1 FY26: INR 12.1 crores; Q4 FY26: INR 20.6 crores). EBITDA was INR 47.2 crores versus INR 33.2 crores and INR 44.7 crores.

Indore utilisation. Guided to 40%–45% by year-end, from close to 30%–35% currently, with the trajectory described as on course.

Capacity ceiling without further capex. Management indicated Navsari is largely capped at around INR 800 crores, potentially INR 900–950 crores including toxin and penem blocks, with toxin limited by market creation rather than capacity. Indore is estimated at INR 800 crores to a maximum of around INR 1,200 crores depending on product mix. Combined, this implies roughly INR 1,600 crores to INR 2,000 crores maximum revenue with the current product basket and legacy. A separate figure of INR 1,600–1,800 crores was cited for the no-capex case. FY27 expected revenue was stated at around INR 1,100 crores, with 15% annual increments implying approximately INR 1,250 crores, then INR 1,450 crores, and up to INR 1,600 crores with existing facilities.

Operating leverage. Management expects leverage to begin kicking in by mid-FY28, and hopes for it earlier. The targeted Indore mix is roughly 20% liquid, 50%–60% lyophilisation, and 20%–30% complex injectables, with the third line (suspension and liquid vial) targeted at close to 80% utilisation within three years.

GLP-1. No standalone revenue forecast; contributions are embedded within the 15% year-on-year growth guidance. CMO operations are expected to gain steam in Q2, with Q3 seen as the period of actual traction, and an objective of around 30% domestic CMO with 70% capacity use for international markets by year-end, where Hetero and Gufic as a single channel have filed in more than 22 countries.

Aesthetics. Filler revenue expected post-December, with launch around December or January, subject to registration by Q2 or mid-Q3.

Employee costs. The prior-year step-up was characterised as a one-time phenomenon not expected to repeat at the same scale this year.

Dividend. A final dividend of 10% (Re. 0.10 per equity share) was declared and all five resolutions (four ordinary and one special) proposed at the 42nd Annual General Meeting held on September 4, 2026 were passed with the requisite majority.
