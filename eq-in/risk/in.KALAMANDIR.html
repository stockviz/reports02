Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

The company continues to expand its physical footprint. On August 15, 2026, it launched its 86th store under the ‘Mandir’ format at Vijayawada, Andhra Pradesh. This follows the position described in the investor material of 83 stores across 24 cities with a combined store area of approximately 8,14,499 sq. ft as of June 30, 2026, operating five brand formats: Kalamandir, Mandir, Vara Mahalakshmi Silks, KLM Fashion Mall, and Valli Silks.

The growth narrative rests on several demand-side levers: a rising female population, with total females aged over 25 years expected to reach 45.5 crore in 2031 from 37.6 crore in 2021; the wedding and festive market’s high cultural significance for sarees; the universal appeal of sarees, with 80+ draping ways and purchases in over 90% of South Indian households; a shift from unorganised to organised retail, with organised retailing’s share of apparel rising from 14% in FY 2007 to 32% in FY 2020; and the introduction of fusion and fancy sarees.

The company also continues its engagement with the investment community, having scheduled a one-to-one virtual interaction with investors/analysts on August 4, 2026.

Broker Narrative

HDFC Securities stayed resolutely Buy across all three reports and raised its predicted return from +36% to +100% even as each call ended in a large negative actual return. The persistent themes were Tamil Nadu/VML expansion, margin expansion, and competitive-intensity risk, but the last report became more defensive: it acknowledged weak TN sales density, store-opening delays, KLM format weakness, and poor core-market recovery while still betting on store maturation. The initial e-tailer competition worry broadened into concrete AP/Telangana competitive intensity, and the optimistic unorganised-to-organised saree migration backdrop was replaced by a narrower TN ramp-up story.

Fears that came true

  • The early fear of heightened competitive intensity from e-tailers materialized as increased competitive intensity in core AP/Telangana markets, and actual returns on the subsequent Buy calls were -68.8%, -50.5%, and -33.3%.

Optimism that failed

  • The Tamil Nadu foray promise has not paid off as hoped: VML’s Tamil Nadu sales density has not caught up to the format average and FY25 performance was below expectations.
  • The disciplined cluster-based expansion thesis has not yet produced superior unit economics, as VML saw store-opening delays and Tamil Nadu sales density lagged the format average.
  • The expected top-line recovery and margin expansion stalled: recovery in core markets remains weak and the new MSME payment clause may limit gross margin expansion.
