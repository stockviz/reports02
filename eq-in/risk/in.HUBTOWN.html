Hubtown: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Auditor qualifications on interest provisioning. The statutory auditors issued a qualified conclusion on both the standalone and consolidated results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. The company did not provide for interest amounting to ₹528.48 lakhs on certain inter-corporate deposits in the quarter. As a result, finance costs for the quarter are understated by that amount, with a consequential increase in reported profit. This follows a similar observation in the auditors’ report on the financial statements for the year ended March 31, 2026, where interest of ₹1,751.85 lakhs on certain inter-corporate deposits and advances was not provided. Management states it has held meetings with lenders seeking interest rate reductions or waivers, settled with one major private lender during the quarter, and is hopeful of amicable settlements with the remaining lenders.

Weak sequential revenue performance. Standalone revenue from operations fell to ₹2,122 lakhs in Q1 FY27 from ₹7,031 lakhs in Q4 FY26 and ₹14,207 lakhs in Q1 FY26. Consolidated revenue from operations was ₹15,562 lakhs, down from ₹16,000 lakhs sequentially and ₹18,741 lakhs year-on-year. The company notes that real estate profits do not accrue evenly and quarterly results may not be representative of full-period performance.

Elevated finance costs. Standalone finance costs of ₹2,821 lakhs in Q1 FY27 were substantially higher than ₹449 lakhs in Q1 FY26, though lower than ₹5,906 lakhs in Q4 FY26. Consolidated finance costs were ₹4,178 lakhs, down from ₹8,483 lakhs sequentially but up from ₹1,557 lakhs year-on-year.

Contingent liabilities and guarantees. Corporate guarantees issued to financial institutions and others on behalf of subsidiaries amount to ₹76,040.01 lakhs. Management views these as necessary for business interests and believes there will be no sustainable claims on the company. The company also states that contingent liabilities identified will not result in financial liability.

Exposure to loss-making entities. The company has investments in controlled entities and associates, some of which have incurred losses and have negative net worth. Management considers the underlying projects to be at various stages of development and expects adequate profitability on substantial completion or current market values in excess of carrying values, hence no provision is considered necessary.

Dependence on external confirmations. Loans, advances, other receivables, debtors, and creditors are subject to confirmations and are considered payable or realizable.

General business risks. The company identifies risks including changes in market conditions, pricing and competition, ability to manage growth and costs, success in attracting and retaining customers and professionals, government regulations, tax changes, economic and monetary conditions, litigation, currency fluctuations, reliability of external data sources, supply chain issues, input cost variations, trade priorities, and adverse political, economic, or climatic developments.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Fundraise via FCCBs. On August 3, 2026, the Board approved raising up to US$150 million through foreign currency convertible bonds or other equity-linked securities on a private placement basis, in one or more tranches, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals. An FCCB Committee was constituted to finalize terms. The proposed FCCBs are optionally convertible into equity shares. Intended uses include funding expansion and operational requirements of real estate activities, land and development rights acquisition, construction and development costs, statutory approvals, working capital, repayment or pre-payment of borrowings, and inorganic growth through acquisitions. Shareholder approval is sought at the 38th Annual General Meeting scheduled for September 18, 2026.

Merger of Saicharan Consultancy Private Limited. Shareholders and unsecured creditors approved the scheme of amalgamation of SCPL with Hubtown on June 23, 2026, pursuant to NCLT orders. SCPL held 21.17% equity in Rare Townships Private Limited, which is developing the ‘Rising City’ project in Ghatkopar (East), Mumbai. The merger consolidates Hubtown’s ownership in RTPL and is expected to increase revenue share in the project by 21.17%. Of six residential buildings, five are completed with occupancy certificates received; the last is expected to be completed by March 2027. Shareholders of SCPL will receive 648 Hubtown equity shares of ₹10 each for every 1 SCPL share of ₹10.

Merger of 25 West Realty Private Limited. Shareholders approved the amalgamation of 25 West with Hubtown on June 23, 2026. 25 West is developing the super-luxury residential project ‘25 West’ at Bandra (West), Mumbai. The project is expected to generate total revenue of approximately ₹6,000 crores. Shareholders of 25 West will receive 42 Hubtown equity shares of ₹10 each for every 1 share of ₹10 held. The merger is intended to align a high-potential asset with Hubtown’s broader platform.

Operational scale and pipeline. The company reports 35 years of legacy, 47 completed developments totaling 12.76 msf, 6.69 msf of slum rehabilitation delivered, and 2.51 msf of PPP area delivered. It cites a strong execution pipeline of 7.13 msf ongoing development (34.17 msf ongoing post-merger) and a future growth platform with 346.94 acres of strategic land reserves. The commercial real estate market is expected to more than double from USD 53.5 billion in 2026 to USD 116.3 billion by 2031, growing at a 16.8% CAGR.

Consolidation of promoter group residential assets. The company is pursuing mergers involving projects including Hubtown Rising City, 25 West, 25 South, and 25 Downtown, aimed at consolidating business operations and ownership interests within the group.

Key Risks

  • Auditor-qualified financial results due to non-provision of interest on inter-corporate deposits, affecting reported finance costs and profits.
  • Outstanding corporate guarantees of ₹76,040.01 lakhs on behalf of subsidiaries.
  • Investments and advances to entities with losses and negative net worth, where recovery depends on project completion and market values.
  • Receivables and payables subject to confirmation.
  • Dependence on NCLT and regulatory approvals for mergers to become effective.
  • FCCB issuance subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals, with terms including conversion price, tenure, and coupon yet to be decided.
  • Currency fluctuations and external market conditions affecting real estate demand and input costs.
  • Litigation and regulatory changes.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance provided. Management targets a deleveraged balance sheet with net debt zero by FY31 and states it remains committed to robust growth across projects for FY27. The company reported total collections of ₹3,201 million and pre-sales of ₹5,350 million in Q1 FY27. The 25 West project is expected to generate total revenue of approximately ₹6,000 crores. The last building at Rising City is expected to be completed by March 2027. The FCCB raise is intended to fund expansion, acquisitions, and repayment or pre-payment of borrowings.

Observed performance. Q1 FY27 standalone revenue from operations was ₹2,122 lakhs, with net profit of ₹1,771 lakhs and basic EPS of ₹1.25. Consolidated revenue from operations was ₹15,562 lakhs, with net profit after tax and share of associates and joint ventures of ₹2,658 lakhs and basic EPS of ₹1.75. Total debt including merging entities stood at ₹51,813 million as of June 2026. Finance costs remain elevated relative to the year-ago quarter. The company has not provided interest on certain inter-corporate deposits in the quarter, which affects comparability of reported finance costs and profits.
