TATAPOWER — Q1 FY27 Corporate Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Mundra Plant transition and regulatory dependence. The Mundra plant operated under Ministry of Power directions under Section 11 of the Electricity Act, 2003 from 16 April 2023. Operations were temporarily suspended from 3 July 2025 for overhauling. A supplementary PPA (SPPA) with GUVNL was executed on 23 March 2026 with effect from 1 April 2025, superseding the earlier PPA for GUVNL’s contracted capacity. While approvals from remaining procurers are in progress, MoP directions issued 22 March 2026 permitted operations from 1 April 2026 to 30 June 2026 under SPPA terms, extended up to 30 September 2026, during which management expects completion of the SPPA with other procurers. Mundra continues to run under Section 11, reporting 94% availability.

Renewable generation curtailment. RE generation was partly offset by curtailment in Rajasthan and Gujarat. Solar PLF was 24.3% versus 24.4% YoY; wind PLF fell to 24.9% from 27.1%, with delayed monsoon onset impacting wind PLFs. Operating income for RE Gencos was affected by lower generation due to load curtailment and transmission constraints.

Solar EPC weakness. Solar EPC and Rooftop segment operating income fell 22% YoY to ₹1,761 crore, with large projects and group captive revenue down 71% to ₹411 crore due to no new third-party projects. Segment EBITDA fell 48% to ₹140 crore and PAT fell 48% to ₹97 crore.

Standalone performance decline. Standalone EBITDA fell 15% to ₹1,249 crore and PAT fell 47% to ₹277 crore, with other income down 63% to ₹191 crore. PBT declined 44% to ₹374 crore.

Odisha AT&C losses. Odisha DISCOMs AT&C losses stand at 26.7%. Individually, TPCODL actual AT&C losses rose to 31% from 28%, TPSODL to 32% from 31%, and TPWODL to 26% from 22%, against vesting order targets of 16%, 22% and 15% respectively.

Coal price increases. Cell and module prices were rangebound while coal prices increased.

Higher costs and leverage. Interest cost rose 10% YoY to ₹1,407 crore. Net debt increased Q-o-Q due to higher capex and working capital requirements, with net debt to equity at 1.25 versus 1.18 in Q4 FY26 and 1.08 in Q1 FY26. Total gross debt rose to ₹74,069 crore from ₹71,122 crore in Q4 FY26.

Associate losses and lower corporate income. Share of associates and JVs rose 86% to ₹241 crore on higher coal company contribution, partly offset by a loss in one associate company. Consolidated EBITDA growth excluded Mundra and was partly offset by EPC third-party business and lower corporate income.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record capex and capacity pipeline. Q1 FY27 recorded highest ever quarterly capex of ₹5,375 crore. Total capacity (operational plus under construction) stands at ~26.6 GW, with ~17.7 GW clean and green including 9.8 GW under construction. The company targets >30 GW total capacity and >20 GW clean and green by FY2030E, versus ~16.7 GW total and ~7 GW clean and green achieved in FY2026.

Renewables additions. 226 MW utility-scale in-house renewable projects were commissioned, taking installed RE capacity to 6.7 GW. The company is on track to deliver 2.5 GW of RE capacity addition in FY2027, with land acquired or visibility for all under-construction RE projects, transmission connectivity secured, and 87% of pipeline RE projects tied in long-term PPAs.

Solar manufacturing outperformance. TP Solar produced a record 1,001 MW of modules in the quarter with industry-leading yield of >96.3% for modules and 87% for cells. Revenue rose 53% YoY to ₹2,462 crore; EBITDA rose 113% to ₹626 crore and PAT rose 287% to ₹371 crore, driven by input cost efficiency and a diversified sales mix with 63% of modules and over 50% of cells sold externally. Manufacturing profitability increased due to third-party cell/module sales.

Rooftop solar growth. Rooftop business achieved billing of 371 MWp, up 37% YoY (270 MWp in Q1 FY26), with cumulative installations of 5.2 GWp and an order book of ₹639 crore. The MySine battery solution was launched, bundling solar and storage, with revenue per customer per KW increasing 1.52x versus traditional rooftop solar. Aspirations include 30 lakh rooftop solar households served by 2030 and ~₹30,000 crore cumulative revenue by 2030.

Transmission growth. TBCB projects contributed with operating income of ₹547 crore versus ₹368 crore, EBITDA of ₹71 crore versus ₹20 crore, and PAT of ₹24 crore versus ₹9 crore. A Letter of Intent was received for the Ryapte transmission project of 491 CKM. Transmission lines under construction stand at 2,332 Ckm, with 5,562 Ckm operational. India transmission capex of ₹9.2 trillion is anticipated between FY25-32E.

Regulated business strength. T&D cluster EBITDA rose to ₹1,541 crore from ₹1,348 crore and PAT to ₹492 crore from ₹443 crore, driven by higher capitalization at Mumbai Transmission, TBCB contribution, higher RoE and non-tariff income at distribution. Mumbai Transmission EBITDA rose to ₹295 crore from ₹242 crore.

Hydro and PSP pipeline. MoU signed for the 404 MW Nyera Amari I & II HPP in Bhutan. Under the Druk Green Power MoU, 2.8 GW of projects are under execution and 10.9 GW proposed, totaling 5,533 MW. Khorlochhu HPP (600 MW) has a 30-year PPA with TPTCL and UPPCL as offtaker at a levelized tariff of ₹6.75/kWh, expected commissioning FY2029. Dorjilung HPP (1,125 MW) has World Bank financing agreements totaling $515 million, with commissioning expected FY2032. Bhivpuri PSP (1,000 MW) received a SECI Letter of Award for 324 MW with a 40-year pumped storage purchase agreement and annual fixed charge of ₹1.08 cr/MW/Yr, the highest among recent bids.

EV charging expansion. More than 7,200 public and bus charging points installed as of Q1 FY27, with 5,970 public EV charging points across 717 cities and towns, 2,46,065 home chargers and 1,258 e-bus charge points.

Industry demand. Power demand rose 8.5% YoY in Q1 FY27. Clean and green accounted for 86% of capacity additions in Q1 FY27. All India AT&C losses declined to 15% in FY25.

Credit ratings. Consolidated ratings stand at AA+/Stable (CRISIL, India Ratings, ICRA, CARE), BBB/Stable (S&P Global) and Ba1/Positive (Moody’s).

3. Key Risks

Kleros arbitration award. On 26 September 2023, the SIAC published a liability award holding the company in breach of certain clauses of non-disclosure agreements with Kleros Capital Partners Limited and of its contractual duty of good faith and confidence. On 1 July 2025 and 27 August 2025, the SIAC published a quantum award and final award directing payment of damages for loss of opportunity of USD 490,320,000 with simple interest of 5.33% from 30 November 2020, and costs of SGD 11,341,963.46 with simple interest of 5.33% from 1 July 2025. Based on legal counsel advice citing justifiable grounds with high probability of a favorable outcome, the company filed an appeal on 23 October 2025 with the Singapore International Commercial Court to set aside the awards and does not foresee any affirmative payment obligation. No provision has been recorded. Hearings are completed and the order is reserved. The auditor drew attention to this matter without modifying the conclusion.

Mundra regulatory uncertainty. Continued operation depends on Section 11 directions, currently extended only to 30 September 2026, pending completion of the SPPA with remaining procurers.

Curtailment and transmission constraints. RE generation remains exposed to curtailment in Rajasthan and Gujarat and transmission constraints.

Leverage and interest costs. Rising debt and interest costs, with net debt to equity at 1.25 and total debt to total assets at 0.45.

Odisha loss reduction gap. Actual AT&C losses remain well above vesting order targets across Odisha DISCOMs.

Coal price volatility. Coal price increases affect fuel costs.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Capacity targets. FY2026 achievement was ~16.7 GW total capacity and ~7 GW clean and green. FY2030E aspirations are >30 GW total and >20 GW clean and green. Q1 FY27 shows ~26.6 GW total capacity (operational plus under construction) and ~17.7 GW clean and green including 9.8 GW under construction.

RE addition guidance. Management states it is on track to deliver 2.5 GW of RE capacity addition in FY2027. Q1 FY27 observed 226 MW of utility-scale in-house renewable commissioning, taking installed RE capacity to 6.7 GW.

Transmission targets. FY2026 achievement was 7,403 Ckm (operational and under construction); FY2030E aspiration is >10,000 Ckm. Q1 FY27 shows 5,562 Ckm operational and 2,332 Ckm under construction.

Rooftop aspirations. Management targets 30 lakh rooftop solar households served by 2030 and ~₹30,000 crore cumulative revenue by 2030. Q1 FY27 observed 371 MWp installations (up 37% YoY), cumulative 5.2 GWp, and an order book of ₹639 crore.

Mundra SPPA completion. Management expects completion of the SPPA with other procurers during the period of extended Section 11 directions up to 30 September 2026. Observed: GUVNL SPPA executed 23 March 2026; approvals from remaining procurers in progress.

Project commissioning timelines. Khorlochhu HPP expected FY2029; Dorjilung HPP expected FY2032; Bhivpuri PSP and Shirwata PSP under development. TBCB projects: Bikaner Transmission FY2027, Jalpura Khurja partially commissioned with full commissioning in H1 FY2027, Jejuri Hinjewadi, Gopalpur and Paradeep FY2028, Ryapte FY2029.

ESG targets. Net Zero by 2045, Water Neutral by 2030, Zero Waste to Landfill by 2030, No Net Loss to Biodiversity by 2030. Observed progress: clean and green portfolio at 47% in 2026 against 70% target for 2030; Scope 1 and 2 emissions at 29 million tonnes CO2e in 2026 against 43.1 million tonnes CO2e baseline in 2024 and zero target by 2045; gender diversity at 11% in 2026 against 22% target by 2030; DJSI Emerging Markets score at 77 in 2026 against 80/100 target by 2027.

Financial performance. Consolidated EBITDA grew 8% YoY to ₹4,249 crore and PAT grew 11% YoY to ₹1,401 crore, with EBITDA growth driven by growth across all three clusters excluding Mundra. Standalone EBITDA fell 15% to ₹1,249 crore and PAT fell 47% to ₹277 crore.

Broker Narrative

The first report was a no-risk, purely optimistic narrative built on US$1bn aspirations, AI/generative AI, top-200 clients, ERP, annuity/large deals, and margin/PAT CAGR targets. By the last report, the narrative had shifted to a Tata Power-specific operational story, with detailed headwinds around solar EPC losses, RE curtailment, Mundra SPPAs, coal costs, and Tata Projects, and tailwinds centered on solar manufacturing, rooftop solar, RE capacity, distribution, transmission, and pumped storage/hydro/nuclear. Very few of the first report’s broad IT-services-style growth themes persist; the emphasis moved from generic growth levers to concrete energy-transition execution.
