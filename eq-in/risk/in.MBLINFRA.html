MBL Infrastructure Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Legacy insolvency and operational disruption. The company’s operations were not normal from 30 March 2017 until 4 September 2024 due to the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. The Resolution Plan dated 22 November 2017, submitted by Mr. A. K. Lakhotia with 78.50% CoC voting share, was approved under IBC and attained finality through orders of the NCLT Kolkata, NCLAT, and the Supreme Court. The banks declared 4 September 2024 as the implementation date. Claims not filed, not admitted, or not forming part of the approved Resolution Plan stand extinguished, and payments of admitted claims remain subject to reconciliation.

Subsidiary in insolvency. Suratgarh Bikaner Toll Road Company Private Limited (SBTRCPL), a wholly owned subsidiary in which the company holds a non-current investment of Rs. 18,505 lakhs, was admitted into CIRP by NCLT Kolkata order dated 1 December 2025. The powers of its board are suspended and management vests with a Resolution Professional. No finance cost provision has been made since CIRP initiation (provision was made at 9.60% p.a. only up to 30 November 2025). The company, being an MSME, has submitted a Resolution Plan for SBTRCPL which is being evaluated by the Committee of Creditors. If the Resolution Plan is not approved, SBTRCPL may not remain a going concern.

Terminated concessions and litigation. The concession agreement of MBL Highway Development Company Limited (MHDCL) was terminated by the Authority on 18 November 2016; legal proceedings, including foreign enforcement actions, remain pending, though MHDCL has received legal advice that the foreign claims are not enforceable and has counterclaims exceeding the amounts claimed. MSP Infrastructures Limited’s concession agreement for the Raisen-Rahatgarh road was terminated by MPRDC; an earlier arbitral award in its favour was set aside by the Commercial Court, Bhopal on 24 November 2022, and fresh arbitration is pending before the Indian Council of Arbitration.

Claims and provisioning. The company carries claims for cost overruns from client delays, project suspensions, design deviations, and scope changes at various stages of negotiation, arbitration, or litigation. Realisation may be lower than recognised claims if settled out of court. Ind-AS adjustments on financial assets and liabilities remain significant in other expenses (Rs. 154 lakhs and Rs. 1,200 lakhs respectively in the June 2026 quarter on a standalone basis).

Seasonality. Infrastructure construction/project activity is seasonal, and margins vary with cost accrual and income recognition across quarters.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Resolution Plan finality. The approved Resolution Plan is binding on all creditors, including Central and State Governments and local authorities, under Section 31(1) of the IBC. The implementation date of 4 September 2024 provides a settled framework with lenders.

Sector spending pipeline. The Union Budget 2026-27 allocated Rs. 3.10 lakh crore to MoRTH, including Rs. 1.87 lakh crore to NHAI. Vision 2047 and national highway expansion plans include upgrading 25,000 km of two-lane highways to four lanes (Rs. 10,00,000 crore) and 16,000 km of four-lane highways to six lanes (Rs. 6,00,000 crore), alongside Bharatmala Pariyojana Phase-I, railway infrastructure projects, SAIL modernisation, industrial and infrastructure development in West Bengal, and the 2030 Commonwealth Games in Ahmedabad.

Arbitration recovery. The Commercial Court, Dehradun, Uttarakhand dismissed petitions filed by the Public Works Department, Government of Uttarakhand under Section 34 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 against the arbitration award dated 14 December 2024 in favour of MBL Infrastructure Ltd for the road project in Udham Singh Nagar (Package No. C-2). The award value including interest stood at Rs. 82.77 crores as on 27 July 2026, with further interest continuing at 12% p.a. until payment.

Claim book. The company has total claims of Rs. 3,120.68 crores and arbitration awards of Rs. 229.95 crores.

Continued core activity. The company continues its infrastructure construction/project activities, and management is pursuing recovery of claims through arbitration and litigation.

3. Key Risks

  • Recoverability of subsidiary investments. Non-current investments in MBL Projects Ltd (Rs. 3,984 lakhs), MHDCL (Rs. 5,110 lakhs), and SBTRCPL (Rs. 18,505 lakhs) are carried at values supported by management estimates; the net worth of these subsidiaries does not represent the true market value of underlying investments/assets. Recoverability depends on arbitration, litigation, claims, and resolution outcomes.
  • SBTRCPL going-concern risk. If the SBTRCPL Resolution Plan is rejected, its going-concern assumption may be materially affected.
  • Claim realisation uncertainty. Recognised claims are subject to the outcome of proceedings and future settlements; out-of-court settlements could yield less than recognised.
  • Corporate guarantee treatment. Amounts arising from invocation of existing corporate guarantees/contingent liabilities (other than current sub-judice matters) are payable only after all Financial Creditor dues under the Resolution Plan, without interest and penalties, subject to reconciliation and legal remedies.
  • General forward-looking risks. Regulatory changes, local political or economic developments, technological risks, and other factors could cause actual results to differ materially from statements.
  • Auditor emphasis. The statutory auditors drew attention to the Resolution Plan notes, subsidiary investment recoverability, terminated projects, and cost-overrun claims, without modifying their opinion.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance. Management expresses confidence that realisable amounts of investments in MBL Projects Ltd, MHDCL, and SBTRCPL are higher than carrying values, based on contractual tenability, legal advice, arbitration/litigation progress, future business plans, and other factors. Management also states that provisions made for MHDCL claims may be reversed upon resolution, and that SBTRCPL’s financial statements will be recast once its Resolution Plan is approved. The company notes it has no obligation to update forward-looking statements.

Observed performance (quarter ended 30 June 2026). On a standalone basis, total income was Rs. 4,497 lakhs (against Rs. 5,345 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2025 and Rs. 9,218 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026), with revenue from operations of Rs. 2,659 lakhs and other income of Rs. 1,838 lakhs. Standalone profit for the period was Rs. 376 lakhs, with EPS of Rs. 0.25. On a consolidated basis, total income was Rs. 5,719 lakhs, and the group reported a net loss of Rs. 519 lakhs, with EPS of negative Rs. 0.34. Consolidated finance costs were Rs. 455 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter, down from Rs. 1,197 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter, reflecting the cessation of SBTRCPL finance cost provisioning after CIRP initiation. Deferred tax expense was Rs. 1,594 lakhs standalone and Rs. 1,600 lakhs consolidated in the quarter.

Governance. All six resolutions presented at the 31st Annual General Meeting held on 12 September 2026 were approved by members with the requisite majority, including adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements, re-appointment of Mr. Anjanee Kumar Lakhotia and Mr. Surender Aggarwal, approval of remuneration and related party transactions, and ratification of the cost auditors.
