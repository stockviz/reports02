DCM Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Engineering Division lockout (ongoing since 2019). Industrial unrest at the Engineering Business Undertaking at Village Asron, District Shaheed Bhagat Singh Nagar (Punjab) led management to recommend a lockout, which the Board approved on October 21, 2019, effective October 22, 2019. The lockout was challenged by workmen before the Labour Authorities and the matter remains sub-judice. The company has not provided for wages of workmen on the rolls for the lockout period from October 22, 2019 to June 30, 2026, aggregating to Rs. 3,080 lakhs, of which Rs. 114 lakhs relates to the quarter ended June 30, 2026. The statutory auditors drew attention to this in their review reports (Emphasis of Matter) without modifying their conclusion. The company states it is evaluating and pursuing various options for the Engineering business, while continuing to upkeep the factory and rationalize the workmen force.

Hisar land project and terminated JDA. DCM signed a Joint Development Agreement on August 11, 2022 with GCD Prime for development of 68.35 acres at Village Bir Hisar, Sector-23, Hisar, Haryana, for an affordable residential plotted colony under Deen Dayal Jan Awas Yojana-2016. A license (No. 179 of 2022) was received on November 10, 2022 for 67.275 acres. The Director General, Town and Country Planning, Haryana suspended the license in April 2023, noting an enquiry initiated by the Deputy Commissioner regarding the company’s Hisar land. Citing inordinate delay and continuing breaches by the Developer in obtaining revocation of the suspension, the company issued a Notice of forfeiture and termination of the JDA on November 1, 2025, stating the amount paid by the Developer would stand forfeited after 15 days and the JDA terminated. The Developer’s Section 9 petition was dismissed by the Delhi High Court on July 28, 2026, but the Division Bench stayed that judgment on August 12, 2026 and directed status quo on the project land; the appeal remains pending. Arbitration has commenced before a Sole Arbitrator appointed with consent of parties.

Weak standalone performance. Standalone segment revenue for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was Rs. 24 lakhs (Others), against Rs. 4 lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 and Rs. 7 lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. Grey Iron Casting recorded nil revenue in the June 2026 quarter (Rs. 4 lakhs in March 2026 quarter; Rs. 7 lakhs in June 2025 quarter). Standalone loss before tax was Rs. 163 lakhs for the June 2026 quarter, versus Rs. 165 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter and Rs. 193 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter. For FY 2025-26, standalone loss before tax was Rs. 580 lakhs. The standalone Grey Iron Casting segment result was a loss of Rs. 91 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter.

Negative working capital and going-concern emphasis. Because the Rs. 5,000 lakh advance from the Developer continues to be shown under current liabilities pending the appeal and arbitration, standalone current liabilities exceed current assets by Rs. 4,264 lakhs as at June 30, 2026; on a consolidated basis the excess is Rs. 508 lakhs. The auditors included a “Material Uncertainty on Going Concern” section in both standalone and consolidated review reports, noting management’s representation that liquidity infusion from real estate asset management, restructuring of the Engineering business, and other interim measures will allow operations to continue, and that the results are prepared on a going concern basis.

Deferred tax asset not recognised. Due to continuing uncertainty over sufficient taxable profit to recover accumulated losses and unused tax credits, the standalone deferred tax asset has not been considered; the same applies to the Holding Company in the consolidated results.

MCD conversion demand (joint venture). The Municipal Corporation of Delhi, by letter dated September 4, 2025, raised a demand of Rs. 24,134 lakhs for conversion of Plaza 1, 2 and 3 of the joint venture project at Bara Hindu Rao, Kishan Ganj, Delhi from industrial to commercial use. The joint venture company filed objections (letters dated September 9 and 23, 2025) and a writ petition dated November 3, 2025 before the Delhi High Court. Based on legal advice, the demand is considered not sustainable and no provision has been made.

Regulatory filing error. In response to an exchange email dated July 21, 2026, the company acknowledged inadvertent errors in the consolidated XBRL (PAT and EPS figures not matching) and in the standalone XBRL (reserves excluding revaluation reserve not mentioned) for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026, and confirmed rectified results were uploaded on the NEAPS portal on July 15, 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

IT services segment. Consolidated revenue from operations was Rs. 2,021 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, up from Rs. 1,887 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter and Rs. 1,761 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter; FY 2025-26 revenue was Rs. 7,178 lakhs. The IT Services segment result was a profit of Rs. 257 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter (Rs. 247 lakhs in March 2026; Rs. 217 lakhs in June 2025; Rs. 757 lakhs for FY 2025-26). The segment’s assets rose to Rs. 5,799 lakhs as at June 30, 2026 from Rs. 5,633 lakhs at March 31, 2026. The IT business (DCM Infotech Limited) has expanded into Agentic AI-based automation solutions, secured customer engagements in healthcare and financial services, and continues to pursue opportunities in automation, AI-led business transformation, IT operations and cybersecurity. DCM Infotech Limited is described as a material unlisted subsidiary generating significant turnover and profit.

Consolidated profitability. Consolidated profit before tax was Rs. 258 lakhs for the June 2026 quarter (including share of profit of equity-accounted investee of Rs. 116 lakhs), compared with a loss before tax of Rs. 60 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter and profit before tax of Rs. 388 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter. Consolidated profit for the period was Rs. 158 lakhs, against a loss of Rs. 151 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter and profit of Rs. 324 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter. Basic and diluted EPS was Rs. 0.85 for the June 2026 quarter (Rs. (0.81) in March 2026; Rs. 1.74 in June 2025; Rs. 1.55 for FY 2025-26).

Real estate monetisation and restructuring options. The company plans to infuse liquidity by focusing on and managing its real estate assets/operations, including sale or disposal of land pieces not currently in use for business operations, and by restructuring its Engineering Business Undertaking. It continues to evaluate appropriate steps for the Hisar project land, including pursuing revocation of the suspension of License No. 179 of 2022 and exploring other lawful options. The company states it has a strong arguable case on merits for termination of the JDA and forfeiture of the Rs. 5,000 lakh advance.

Group simplification. DCM Realty and Infrastructure Limited and DCM Infinity Realtors Limited, wholly owned subsidiaries, have applied for voluntary strike off in Form STK-2 with the Registrar of Companies, New Delhi, as they have had no business operations for the past few years. Completion is expected upon completion of the statutory process under the Companies Act, 2013 and receipt of requisite approval.

Board and governance actions (August 13, 2026). The Board approved re-appointment of Mr. Vinay Sharma as Managing Director for a further three years effective August 4, 2027, subject to shareholder approval; he is not debarred from holding the office of Director by any SEBI or other authority order. The Board also approved appointment of M/s A. Gandhi & Associates, Chartered Accountants, as Internal Auditor for FY 2026-27, effective April 1, 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Labour litigation risk: the lockout dispute remains before labour authorities; no provision has been made for Rs. 3,080 lakhs of lockout-period wages, and an adverse outcome could crystallise this liability.
  • JDA/arbitration risk: the Division Bench interim order of August 12, 2026 stays the July 28, 2026 Single Judge judgment and requires status quo on the project land; the appeal and arbitration remain pending. The Rs. 5,000 lakh advance is carried as a current liability without adjustment, and the company awaits the copy of the interim order.
  • Going-concern risk: current liabilities exceed current assets (standalone: Rs. 4,264 lakhs; consolidated: Rs. 508 lakhs as at June 30, 2026), and the auditors flagged material uncertainty on going concern in both review reports.
  • License suspension risk: revocation of the suspension of License No. 179 of 2022 remains pending, with an enquiry initiated by the Deputy Commissioner into the Hisar land.
  • MCD demand risk: the Rs. 24,134 lakhs conversion demand on the joint venture project is contested via writ petition, with no provision made based on legal advice.
  • Engineering business risk: no production and continuing losses at the Engineering Division, with options still under evaluation and no finalised plan disclosed.
  • Tax asset risk: deferred tax assets remain unrecognised due to uncertainty over future taxable profits.
  • Disclosure/process risk: the XBRL errors for the March 31, 2026 results required rectification, and the company assured greater care in future.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Liquidity and going concern: Management represents that liquidity infusion from real estate asset focus/management (including sale or disposal of unused land), restructuring of the Engineering Business Undertaking, and other interim measures will enable continued operations for the foreseeable future, supporting preparation of the standalone and consolidated results on a going concern basis. Observed position: standalone current liabilities exceed current assets by Rs. 4,264 lakhs and consolidated by Rs. 508 lakhs as at June 30, 2026; the auditors nonetheless flagged material uncertainty on going concern without modifying their conclusion.
  • JDA termination and forfeiture: Management states, based on legal advice, that the company has a strong arguable case on merits for termination of the JDA and forfeiture of the Rs. 5,000 lakh advance. Observed position: the Single Judge dismissed the Developer’s Section 9 petition on July 28, 2026, but the Division Bench stayed that judgment on August 12, 2026 and directed status quo on the project land; the appeal and arbitration are pending, and no adjustment has been made to the advance, which remains under current liabilities.
  • Engineering business options: Management states it is evaluating and pursuing various options and will seek requisite approvals and make intimations when anything is finalised, while continuing to upkeep the factory and rationalize the workmen force. Observed position: the lockout has continued since October 22, 2019, with no provision for Rs. 3,080 lakhs of lockout-period wages and no finalised restructuring disclosed.
  • Subsidiary strike-off: Management expects the striking off of DCM Realty and Infrastructure Limited and DCM Infinity Realtors Limited to be completed upon completion of the statutory process under the Companies Act, 2013 and receipt of requisite approval of the STK-2 applications filed with the Registrar of Companies. Observed position: the applications have been filed; completion remains subject to the statutory process and approval.
  • IT segment momentum: The IT Services segment has delivered sequentially higher revenue and segment profit through the June 2026 quarter, with expansion into Agentic AI-based automation and engagements in healthcare and financial services, consistent with the stated pursuit of growth in automation, AI-led transformation, IT operations and cybersecurity.
