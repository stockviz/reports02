Coffee Day Enterprises Limited — Summary of Recent Disclosures

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Liquidity crisis and debt servicing defaults. The company’s disclosure to the exchanges dated 7 July 2026 states that the delay in debt servicing is due to a liquidity crisis. For the quarter ended 30 June 2026, total loans or revolving facilities from banks or financial institutions outstanding were INR 89.04 crore, of which INR 72.88 crore was in default, including INR 16.16 crore of interest default. Total financial indebtedness of the listed entity, including short-term and long-term debt, was INR 89.04 crore.

Non-compliance with debt covenants and lender recalls. The standalone and consolidated limited review reports for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 (both dated 6 August 2026) note instances of non-compliance with certain debt covenants, including interest and principal repayment defaults. Balance confirmations on loans from two lenders amounting to INR 76.98 crore have not been obtained. Certain lenders have exercised their right to recall loans in previous years.

Recoverability of dues from group companies and MACEL. The standalone review report cites a lack of sufficient evidence on recoverability of dues from group companies amounting to INR 1,444.4 crore. The consolidated review report notes that amounts due from Mysore Amalgamated Coffee Estates Limited (MACEL) to the group as a whole total INR 3,357.13 crore, with no provision made against receivables from MACEL. In Coffee Day Global Limited’s consolidated review, dues from MACEL are stated at INR 979.42 crore, with no recovery during the quarter.

SEBI penalty and arbitration. A final adjudication order dated 24 January 2023 imposed a total monetary penalty of INR 26 crore on the company under Sections 15HA and 15HB of the SEBI Act, 1992, relating to advances to MACEL by subsidiaries. The company appealed to the Securities Appellate Tribunal, which granted a stay on the penalty. The company has initiated arbitration proceedings against MACEL, and its subsidiaries have filed a claim statement.

Capital advance recoverability. Note 14 of the consolidated statement refers to doubts over recoverability of capital advance dues aggregating INR 275 crore in one subsidiary, relating to land at Mumbai acquired by CIDCO for the Navi Mumbai International Airport. CIDCO proposed alternative land, but the party has filed a legal case seeking monetary compensation instead.

Unrecognised financial guarantee. One subsidiary has not recognised a financial guarantee given to erstwhile subsidiary SICAL amounting to INR 41.67 crore, which has been invoked by lenders. The impact cannot be assessed pending a proposed restructuring plan with the lender.

Restructuring pending approvals. One subsidiary is undergoing a corporate restructuring process with its lenders, realigning debt and interest obligations under a proposed plan that remains pending approvals. The impact of exchange fluctuation differences for external commercial borrowings from one lender has not been recognised. Conversion of the foreign currency loan into rupee loans and the applicable interest rate of 8.50% are subject to Reserve Bank of India approval.

Going concern uncertainty. The standalone review report states that the matters described may have consequential implications on the company’s ability to continue as a going concern, and the auditor is unable to comment on whether the going concern basis is appropriate. The consolidated review report notes that auditors of one subsidiary and two step-down subsidiaries expressed material uncertainty over going concern, and the same was expressed in the reports of two subsidiaries and three step-subsidiaries.

Reporting gaps. The consolidated review notes that limited reviewed financial statements or management compiled accounts for three joint ventures for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 were not received and were not considered; two joint ventures are inactive following resignation of their directors, and the management has considered last compiled accounts up to 31 March 2021.

XBRL discrepancy. A clarification dated 22 September 2025 states that the company inadvertently missed entering the share of profits from associates in XBRL, resulting in a consolidated PAT figure mismatch between the PDF and XBRL; revised XBRL was filed.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Positive group net worth. The consolidated financial results were prepared using the going concern assumption in view of the positive net-worth of the group.

Debt realignment in principle. Coffee Day Global Limited has realigned its debt and interest obligations in accordance with a proposed plan, pursuant to in-principle approval accorded by all lenders, pending final execution.

Arbitration and recovery process. The company has initiated arbitration proceedings against MACEL as suggested by CrestLaw in consultation with NSE, and subsidiaries have filed a claim statement. The 18th Annual General Meeting agenda includes noting the updated report issued by CrestLaw Partners regarding the recovery process for amounts due from MACEL and its affiliates.

Stay on SEBI penalty. The appeal to the Securities Appellate Tribunal against the 24 January 2023 SEBI order resulted in a stay on the imposition of penalty.

Restructuring and settlement discussions. A proposed restructuring plan with a lender regarding a settlement is referenced in relation to the SICAL financial guarantee.

3. Key Risks

  • Inability to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence on recoverability of dues from group companies (INR 1,444.4 crore standalone) and from MACEL (INR 3,357.13 crore consolidated; INR 979.42 crore at Coffee Day Global), with no provision made against MACEL receivables.
  • Non-compliance with debt covenants, interest and principal repayment defaults, and lender recalls.
  • Unconfirmed loan balances from two lenders amounting to INR 76.98 crore.
  • Going concern uncertainty at the standalone level and material uncertainty expressed by auditors of certain subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries.
  • Pending RBI approval for conversion of foreign currency loans and the 8.50% interest rate, and pending final execution of the restructuring plan.
  • Unrecognised financial guarantee to SICAL (INR 41.67 crore) invoked by lenders.
  • Doubts over recoverability of INR 275 crore capital advance in one subsidiary, subject to litigation.
  • SEBI penalty of INR 26 crore (stayed on appeal) and related proceedings.
  • Incomplete financial information for three joint ventures, with last compiled accounts up to 31 March 2021 for two inactive joint ventures.
  • Disclaimer of conclusion by the auditors on both standalone and consolidated results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

The disclosures contain limited forward-looking management guidance. The 7 July 2026 exchange disclosure attributes the delay in debt servicing to a liquidity crisis. The consolidated review report records that the group prepared its results on a going concern basis in view of positive group net-worth, and that Coffee Day Global Limited has realigned debt and interest obligations under a proposed plan with in-principle approval from all lenders, pending final execution and RBI approval. The standalone review report records that management states dues from group companies of INR 1,444.4 crore are recoverable, and the consolidated review report records that management considers MACEL dues recoverable, with arbitration proceedings initiated and a claim statement filed; no recovery occurred during the quarter at Coffee Day Global. The 18th AGM agenda includes noting the updated CrestLaw Partners report on the MACEL recovery process.

Observed performance against these positions includes: continued defaults for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 (INR 72.88 crore of INR 89.04 crore outstanding in default, including INR 16.16 crore interest default); absence of recovery from MACEL during the quarter; no provision made against MACEL receivables; pending final execution of the restructuring plan and pending RBI approvals; and the auditors’ inability to obtain sufficient evidence on recoverability, loan balances, and the appropriateness of the going concern assumption.
