Emkay Global Financial Services — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Market and macroeconomic volatility. Q1 FY27 saw heightened volatility from the US–Iran conflict, with crude oil swinging above US$100 per barrel in April, falling to around US$60 in June, and recovering to nearly US$90. The Rupee weakened, with USD/INR moving from 92.64 to beyond 96. The company’s own strategy note (March 2026) flagged that continued hostilities in the Gulf implied crude staying higher for longer, with Brent ruling at USD100/bbl, and warned of ~10% further downside risk for the Nifty in the absence of a detente, a possible Nifty crash to ~21,000 if Iran remained at USD100 for 3–4 months, and risks of INR95/USD, a 10-year bond yield at 7%, and a spike in corporate bond spreads.

FPI selling. Q1 FY27 witnessed the highest level of FPI selling in the last five quarters, driven by the strength of the global AI trade and concerns around India’s vulnerability to higher energy prices. Most selling was concentrated in April and May.

Derivatives normalisation. Derivatives volumes returned to their long-term run rates after the one-time spike seen in Q4 FY26.

Sequential decline in revenue and profit. Consolidated total revenue fell 37% QoQ to ₹965 million (from ₹1,532 million in Q4 FY26), and profit before tax fell 9% QoQ to ₹87 million. Income from operations declined 38% QoQ.

Rising finance costs. Finance costs rose 79% YoY to ₹43 million in Q1 FY27, and rose from ₹70 million in FY25 to ₹130 million in FY26.

Sharp FY26 profitability decline. Historical consolidated statements show profit for the year falling from ₹568 million in FY25 to ₹152 million in FY26, with total expenses rising from ₹3,005 million to ₹3,751 million over the same period.

Sector and geopolitical uncertainty. The outlook for the rest of FY27 depends largely on geopolitical and macroeconomic developments; the evolving Middle East situation remains an important variable given India’s energy import dependence. The progress of the monsoon will also be closely watched. Defensive sectors such as healthcare and FMCG lagged due to elevated valuations.

Client caution on overseas allocations. While interest in overseas investments increased, clients remain mindful of valuation risks.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong domestic equity market performance. In Q1 FY27, the Nifty 50, Nifty Mid-cap 150 and Nifty Small-cap 250 delivered returns of 7.4%, 17.4% and 24.1% respectively. Corporate earnings remained resilient, with Q4 FY26 earnings growth of 7% for the Nifty, 40.7% for mid-caps and 8.5% for small-caps. The mid-June US–Iran truce improved sentiment as crude moderated.

Domestic participation and DII support. Domestic inflows of approximately ₹2.2 lakh crore occurred during the quarter even as FPIs recorded net outflows of around ₹1.75 lakh crore. Mutual funds remained the largest contributors within the DII universe, backed by sustained SIP and lump sum investments.

RBI FCNR(B) scheme. Expected inflows of US$50–70 billion should help stabilize the Rupee and support credit growth over the next three to four quarters. Early Q1 FY27 earnings were encouraging, with bank credit growth above 15% and better-than-expected FMCG volume growth and margins; the IT sector showed signs of stabilizing.

Asset Management momentum. PMS and AIF AUM increased 61% YoY to ₹23,700 million (from ₹14,689 million). All nine investment strategies outperformed their benchmarks. Net inflows across PMS, AIF and Advisory were approximately ₹5,000 million, of which nearly ₹4,000 million came from an advisory allocation by a large institutional client. Emkay Capital Builder PMS & AIF AUM reached ₹6,059 million and Emkay SMID Cap PMS & AIF AUM reached ₹1,873 million as on 30 June 2026. Distribution mix diversified, with MFDs, National Distributors and Banks together accounting for 51%; Bank and MFD contributions rose from 2% and 3% in March 2024 to 6% and 12% as of June 2026.

Wealth Management growth. Wealth AUM reached ₹2,05,444 million as on 30 June 2026, growing 33% QoQ and around 20% YoY. The business added four new locations (22 branches), strengthened its relationship management team, and completed migration to a new technology platform with zero downtime. Advisory revenue expanded 10% YoY to ₹56 million and transactional revenue expanded 25% YoY to ₹141 million. East regional hubs anchored in Kolkata, Patna and Guwahati.

Primary market recovery. Primary market activity picked up meaningfully after a subdued H2 FY26, with improved IPO sentiment and easing of rigid size preferences, allowing well-positioned microcap companies to attract interest.

Strategic priorities. Deepening presence in Tier-2 and Tier-3 markets, strengthening relationships with MFDs, National Distributors and banking partners, and pursuing white-label partnerships. Technology investment continued across digital transformation, trading infrastructure, enterprise data, cloud readiness and cybersecurity, including algorithmic and API-led execution and a governance-first approach to AI.

Institutional Equities activity. Nine roadshows, 26 corporate/expert client calls and meetings, two group events, 17 coverages initiated, 351 research reports released, and 228 stocks under coverage.

3. Key Risks

  • Prolonged Middle East disruption affecting crude oil prices, inflation expectations and investor sentiment, given India’s energy import dependence.
  • A scenario of crude at USD100/bbl for 3–4 months, with the Nifty potentially crashing to ~21,000, demand destruction and margin squeeze from input costs; no sector immune, with Technology, Pharma, Metals and Power relatively protected and Consumer and Autos most exposed.
  • Risk of INR95/USD, 10-year bond yield at 7%, and a spike in corporate bond spreads in an extended-war scenario.
  • Second-order global growth/inflation shock from an elongated conflict, with SMID earnings at greater risk.
  • Monsoon performance as a swing factor for inflation and domestic consumption.
  • Valuation risk in overseas allocations and elevated valuations in defensive sectors.
  • Default interest of 2% per annum over the applicable coupon payable on any delay or default in payment of interest and/or principal on the NCDs.
  • The NCDs are unsecured, with no charge created over assets.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements:

  • The company stated that if geopolitical tensions ease and macroeconomic conditions remain stable, FY27 has the potential to be a stronger year for capital market activity than FY26.
  • Management expects to deepen Tier-2 and Tier-3 presence and strengthen MFD, National Distributor and banking relationships, with white-label partnerships seen as a meaningful opportunity; these initiatives “should support healthy AUM growth and sustained inflows over the remainder of the year.”
  • The RBI FCNR(B) scheme’s expected inflows of US$50–70 billion “should help stabilize the Rupee and support credit growth over the next three to four quarters.”
  • Management stated it remains focused on disciplined execution, strengthening its franchise across businesses, and creating long-term value.
  • The NCD issuance has a tentative deemed date of allotment of September 7, 2026 and tentative maturity of September 7, 2029, subject to a call option exercisable at 24, 27, 30 and 33 months from the deemed date of allotment.

Observed performance against these themes:

  • Consolidated revenue increased 24% YoY to ₹965 million and consolidated PAT increased 90% to ₹91 million in Q1 FY27, which management described as a strong operating performance.
  • Asset Management AUM grew approximately 60% YoY to ₹23,700 million, with all nine strategies outperforming benchmarks, consistent with the stated growth momentum.
  • Wealth AUM grew 33% QoQ to ₹2,05,444 million, with the four new locations and platform migration supporting the stated expansion priorities.
  • However, on a sequential basis, total revenue declined 37% and PAT (before share of associates) declined from ₹56 million in Q4 FY26 to ₹80 million at the PAT line but profit for the period rose to ₹91 million including ₹11 million share of profit of associates; income from operations fell 38% QoQ, indicating quarter-on-quarter variability alongside the YoY improvement.
  • FY26 full-year profit for the year (₹152 million) was sharply lower than FY25 (₹568 million), even as FY26 income from operations (₹3,770 million) exceeded FY25 (₹3,362 million), reflecting the cost pressures evident in the historical statements.
