SPENCERS — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Natures Basket underperformance. Natures Basket delivered a muted quarter: sales of ₹59 crore, up 8% quarter-on-quarter versus Q4 FY26 (₹55 crore) but down 13.5% year-on-year versus Q1 FY26 (₹69 crore). Management described this as “a bit of a setback” and attributed it to internal issues — not having the right level of inventory at stores and insufficient online sales growth. A change in management at Natures Basket took place about 45 days before the August 13, 2026 call, and a complete reset plan has been drawn up focused on assortment availability, trimming the long tail of SKUs, and must-win fresh categories (fruits and vegetables, fresh meat, cheese, imported packaged goods). Management cautioned against reading too much into the 8% sequential recovery, pointing to the negative year-on-year growth as the more relevant measure.

Limited cost levers at Natures Basket. Unlike Spencer’s, where operating expenses were reduced by 5% year-on-year, management stated that a premium experiential grocery format like Natures Basket does not permit large-scale cost optimization because it requires well-maintained stores, more equipment, and higher-quality staff. One round of support-infrastructure optimization has already been done. The Natures Basket turnaround is therefore described as top-line led — driving sales per square foot and rupee gross margin.

Losses and negative working capital. The company incurred a standalone loss after tax of ₹3,402.82 lakhs and a consolidated loss after tax of ₹6,044.88 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. Current liabilities, including current borrowings, exceeded current assets by ₹73,052.64 lakhs on a standalone basis and ₹99,876.94 lakhs on a consolidated basis as at June 30, 2026. Consolidated PBT for Q1 FY27 was negative ₹60 crore (versus negative ₹62 crore in Q1 FY26 and negative ₹66 crore in Q4 FY26).

Lower non-operating income. Consolidated other income fell to ₹1.8 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹11.4 crore in Q1 FY26 — a reduction of roughly ₹10 crore — which management noted masks the true magnitude of the operating EBITDA improvement. At Spencer’s, other income fell to ₹1.2 crore from ₹8.0 crore year-on-year.

Debt levels and refinancing. Consolidated total debt stood at ₹1,266 crore, comprising ₹1,019 crore at SRL and ₹237 crore at NBL. Management stated that the refinancing process had started and that some progress would be seen within the month of the call.

No store expansion planned in FY27. Management explicitly stated no new store additions at Natures Basket and only calibrated activity at Spencer’s — relocations and a couple of trial stores within existing clusters (including a small suburban Kolkata store launched two days before the call). A more calibrated expansion plan is indicated for FY28, once target productivity levels are reached.

Online marketplace route not commercially viable. Management said exploring listing Natures Basket products on platforms such as Instamart or Blinkit did not make commercial sense due to substantial margin sharing, and noted that these players are building their own gourmet offerings (for example, Blinkit’s gourmet store and Flipkart’s planned “Pykd” platform). Management characterized such listings as unsustainable quick wins that would sacrifice margins.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Consolidated growth momentum. Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue was ₹469 crore versus ₹416 crore in Q1 FY26 (+13% year-on-year) and ₹436 crore in Q4 FY26 (+8% quarter-on-quarter). This was the second consecutive quarter of year-on-year growth. Gross margin was ₹93 crore versus ₹86 crore in Q1 FY26 and ₹82 crore in Q4 FY26. Consolidated EBITDA doubled to ₹9.4 crore (2% of sales) from ₹4.7 crore (1.1% of sales) in Q1 FY26.

Spencer’s format performance. Spencer’s delivered 18% year-on-year growth and 8% quarter-on-quarter growth, described as the eighth consecutive month of year-on-year growth. Offline grew 14% and online grew 49–50%. Margins were steady at 19% on a like-for-like basis (reported 18.2% versus 19.1%, with the difference attributed to accounting treatment of membership costs). Operating expenses fell 5% year-on-year to ₹57 crore. EBITDA was ₹18 crore (4.4% of sales) versus ₹14.6 crore (4.2% of sales) in Q1 FY26.

Productivity gains without new stores. Sales per square foot reached ₹1,850 — a level management said is typically achieved in a festive quarter, not Q1. Management set an internal target of ₹2,000 sales per square foot for the festive quarter. Store EBITDA was described as 2x the level of Q1 FY26, with management reiterating a publicly stated short-term aspiration of 7.5%–8% store EBITDA, up from around 2.5% previously.

Membership program traction. Launched in July 2025, the membership program has more than 125,000 members — roughly 25% of the active monthly customer base — contributing one-third of monthly sales. Members show retention about 2x that of non-members, average monthly spend roughly 3x, and frequency approaching 5 (about 2x non-members). The program was extended from a single-tier to a three-tier structure with benefits including more online free deliveries and higher payback, plus member-special pricing on select SKUs alongside percentage cashback. Management’s stated ambition is 200,000 members shopping at least five times a month.

Online unit economics turned positive. Jiffy’s contribution per order (RGM less fulfillment cost) moved from a loss of approximately ₹18 per order in Q1 FY26, to about ₹12 in Q4 FY26, to a positive ₹18 per order in Q1 FY27. Average bill value remains above ₹780. Repeat rates were about 67%, NPS above 85, on-time delivery just under 30 minutes, and in-full above 90%. Customer acquisition is calibrated, with most new online customers coming from existing offline customers.

Growth driver mix. Management stated that roughly 70% of growth came from a higher number of bills (NOBs) and 30% from higher average bill value. Category mix was broadly stable, with staples up about 100 basis points, liquor up about 100 basis points, non-food (apparel, E&E, general merchandise) down about 100 basis points, and fresh unchanged.

Natures Basket recovery levers. The reset focuses on availability, range optimization, and must-win fresh categories, with limited capex directed at refurbishing ageing premium stores rather than new openings. The online build for Natures Basket rides on the same tech backbone as Jiffy, which management described as stable with a modern consumer interface; consumer acquisition and scale-out are planned selectively in two cities with store concentration, to follow once inventory is fixed.

Financial flexibility. The company has access to unutilised credit lines with bankers, additional capital and support from promoters or promoter groups, other investments that can be monetized if needed, and has launched cost reduction initiatives. Shareholders at the Ninth Annual General Meeting on September 11, 2026 approved an increase in borrowing limits up to an additional ₹350 crore and creation of charge/security on movable and immovable properties up to an additional ₹450 crore.

3. Key Risks

  • Going concern dependence on external support. The financial results were prepared on a going concern basis despite ongoing losses and negative working capital, with management’s confidence resting on an approved business plan, cost reduction initiatives, promoter support, available credit lines, and monetizable investments.
  • Natures Basket execution risk. The reset depends on disciplined execution around assortment, availability, and fresh categories; management acknowledged the format cannot rely on significant cost reduction, making the turnaround top-line dependent.
  • Refinancing execution. A large part of the debt was expected to be refinanced during the current year; the process had started as of the August 13, 2026 call, with management indicating some progress within that month.
  • Online growth calibration. Management intends to hold unit order economics positive and avoid heavy customer acquisition spending, which caps online growth at around 25% on a full-year cumulative basis.
  • Format-specific margin sensitivity. Natures Basket’s premium experiential model carries structural cost requirements, limiting the expense-side flexibility available in the Spencer’s format.
  • Marketplace channel economics. Third-party platform listings carry substantial margin sharing, and platform-owned gourmet offerings could erode the sustainability of such channels.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Growth guidance. Management guided to sustaining the growth momentum through FY27, with full-year cumulative online growth steadying at around 25%, Spencer’s offline growth at mid-to-high single digits, and Natures Basket showing early double-digit growth from Q3/Q4 FY27 once the reset and optimization are complete. Observed: consolidated growth of 13% year-on-year in Q1 FY27, the second consecutive growth quarter; Spencer’s at 18% year-on-year with online at 49–50%; Natures Basket at negative 13.5% year-on-year but positive 8% quarter-on-quarter.

EBITDA trajectory. Management described a pivot from efficiency-led to sales-growth-led EBITDA improvement, with growth plus continued operating cost control as the expansion lever. Observed: consolidated EBITDA doubled year-on-year to ₹9.4 crore despite roughly ₹10 crore lower other income; Spencer’s EBITDA rose to ₹18 crore from ₹14.6 crore with operating expenses down 5%; Natures Basket financial EBITDA improved to negative ₹2.6 crore from negative ₹4.4 crore in Q4 FY26 but remained below the positive ₹0.8 crore of Q1 FY26.

Store EBITDA aspiration. Management reiterated a publicly stated short-term aspiration of 7.5%–8% store EBITDA, noting the starting point was around 2.5% and that store EBITDA has since reached 2x the Q1 FY26 level, with “still some journey to do.”

Sales per square foot target. Management set an internal target of ₹2,000 sales per square foot for the festive quarter, against ₹1,850 achieved in Q1 FY27 — a level it noted is typically a festive-quarter figure.

Membership target. Management stated a preference for 200,000 members shopping at least five times a month, treating their percentage contribution to sales as a derivative rather than a target. Observed: 125,000-plus members, about 25% of the active monthly customer base, contributing one-third of monthly sales.

Online unit economics. Management said positive contribution per order is expected to be sustained, supported by calibrated customer acquisition and high repeat rates. Observed: contribution per order moved from negative ₹18 in Q1 FY26 to positive ₹18 in Q1 FY27.

Store expansion. Management guided to no new Natures Basket stores and only calibrated Spencer’s activity in FY27 — relocations and trial stores within existing clusters — with a more calibrated expansion plan indicated for FY28. Observed: one new store added at each of Spencer’s (taking the count to 90) and Natures Basket (31 stores) during the quarter, with Spencer’s total trading area at 7.73 lakh square feet and Natures Basket at 1.18 lakh square feet.

Natures Basket online. Management indicated the tech stack is ready and consumer acquisition and scale-out will follow once inventory issues are fixed, with progress to be shared in the next quarter. Observed: no consumer acquisition scale-up yet, as availability and range optimization remain prerequisites.
