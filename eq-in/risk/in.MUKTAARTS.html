MUKTAARTS — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Weak quarterly profitability. The standalone results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 show a sharp deterioration versus the comparable prior-year quarter: profit from ordinary activities before tax fell to Rs. 3.42 (with a loss of Rs. 1.87 at the profit-after-tax line) against Rs. 5.82 and Rs. 7.05 respectively in the prior-year period. Total comprehensive income for the quarter was Rs. 16.26 against Rs. 231.87 a year earlier, and basic/diluted EPS declined to Rs. 0.65 from Rs. 1.01.

Subsidiary-level financial distress. The statutory auditors’ consolidated review report notes that Mukta A2 Cinemas Private Limited incurred a loss before tax (including other comprehensive income) of Rs. 85.20 lakhs for the quarter and has accumulated losses of Rs. 10,895 lakhs as at 30 June 2026, with net worth fully eroded and a shareholder equity deficit of Rs. 10,745 lakhs. These conditions indicate a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the company’s ability to continue as a going concern, though the results were prepared on a going concern basis.

Whistling Woods International Limited (WWIL) litigation and eroded net worth. The auditors flag that the company’s investment in WWIL (including deemed investment) aggregates Rs. 832,062,611 towards arrears of rent and interest, per a letter dated 3 December 2012. MFSCDCL demanded Rs. 591,966,210 up to 31 July 2014. WWIL’s special leave petition before the Supreme Court was dismissed; a High Court review application led to an interim stay granted on 30 July 2014, requiring a deposit of Rs. 10,00,38,000 by January 2015 and Rs. 45,00,000 per annum from FY2014-15 until settlement. Through FY2016-17, Rs. 11,35,38,000 was paid by the Company, and WWIL paid Rs. 45,00,000 per annum for FY2017-18 to FY2021-22. The Supreme Court dismissed the State Government of Maharashtra and MFSCDCL’s challenge on 22 September 2014. No development occurred in the current quarter on the High Court litigation. WWIL’s net worth stands fully eroded as at 30 June 2026, and the impact on carrying value of investments, recoverability of loans and advances, and consequential impact on loss and reserves is not determinable. No adjustments have been made.

Ind AS 116 uncertainty. Pending the final outcome of the litigation, no adjustments have been made in the financial information with respect to Ind AS 116 on land rights.

Industry-level headwinds. OTT platforms have grown more selective and cost-disciplined, with digital licensing revenues declining industry-wide. Shrinking theatrical-to-streaming windows have altered viewing behaviour, with viewers delaying theatrical visits. National admissions declined even as box office revenue hit a record high. India’s screen density remains structurally low relative to global peers, and near-term revenue depends heavily on a concentrated slate of tentpole releases.

Loss of subsidiary status at Mukta A2. Post execution of the proposed investment, Mukta A2 will cease to be a subsidiary and become an associate company, with Mukta Arts’ shareholding diluted to approximately 49%–42%. The Company may not transfer any part of its shareholding in Mukta A2 to a third party without the prior written consent of the proposed investor.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Proposed investment into Mukta A2. The Board approved execution of a Shareholders Agreement and Share Subscription Agreement for a proposed investment of up to Rs. 10 crores in Mukta A2 Cinemas Private Limited, a material subsidiary. The proposed investor, Mr. Sunil Shamrao Patil, is not related to the Company. Mukta A2 will issue equity shares and Compulsorily Convertible Debentures to the investor or its affiliates. No nominee director will be appointed on the Board of the listed entity, and there is no impact on management or control of the listed entity. The transaction may fall within Related Party Transactions and will be done at arm’s length.

Industry-level tailwinds. Renewed appetite for original, content-led storytelling and growing global studio co-production and financing interest in Indian films; expanding regional-language success in Gujarati, Kannada and other markets; new monetisation avenues in animation, live entertainment and stage adaptations; emerging production efficiencies from virtual production and AI-assisted workflows; and continued multiplex screen expansion in under-penetrated states.

Prior-year consolidated momentum. Consolidated Revenue from Operations grew to Rs. 1,739.1 million from Rs. 1,667.2 million; Consolidated EBITDA rose 52% to Rs. 264.4 million from Rs. 174.2 million; EBITDA margin expanded from 10% to 15%; and the consolidated net loss narrowed 32% from Rs. 173.109 million to Rs. 118.003 million.

3. Key Risks

  • Going-concern risk at Mukta A2, given fully eroded net worth and a shareholder equity deficit of Rs. 10,745 lakhs as at 30 June 2026.
  • Undeterminable impact from WWIL litigation, including on the carrying value of investments, recoverability of loans and advances, and consequential impact on loss and reserves.
  • WWIL net worth fully eroded as at 30 June 2026, with no adjustment made to the carrying value of investment, amounts due, or deposits paid.
  • Ind AS 116 land rights adjustment pending, with no adjustment made in the financial information.
  • Concentration risk in the release slate, with near-term revenue dependent on a concentrated set of tentpole releases.
  • Structural industry risk from declining digital licensing revenues, shrinking theatrical windows, and declining national admissions.
  • Dilution and deconsolidation risk at Mukta A2, which will cease to be a subsidiary and become an associate, with shareholding diluted to approximately 49%–42%.
  • Transfer restriction on the Company’s Mukta A2 shareholding without prior written consent of the proposed investor.
  • No dividend declared for the current year, with resources preserved for future growth.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Management’s stated priorities for the coming year are to sustain MA2’s operating momentum through continued content quality and footfall growth, build further on WWI’s expanding revenue and margin trajectory, consolidate the turnaround achieved in Bahrain as regional conditions stabilise, and progress the standalone Company’s new animation, film, OTT, and live entertainment initiatives.

Observed performance against these priorities is mixed. The consolidated prior-year figures show EBITDA growth of 52% and margin expansion from 10% to 15%, alongside a 32% narrowing of net loss, which is consistent with the stated momentum narrative at the consolidated level. However, the standalone results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 show a decline in profit from ordinary activities before tax to Rs. 3.42 from Rs. 5.82, a swing to a loss of Rs. 1.87 at the profit-after-tax line from a profit of Rs. 7.05, and a fall in total comprehensive income to Rs. 16.26 from Rs. 231.87. The auditors’ consolidated review report also records that Mukta A2 incurred a loss before tax of Rs. 85.20 lakhs for the quarter and that WWIL’s interim results reflect a total net loss after tax and total comprehensive loss of Rs. 245 for the quarter, with WWIL’s net worth fully eroded. These subsidiary-level outcomes sit alongside the management priorities of sustaining MA2 momentum and building on WWI’s revenue and margin trajectory.

The proposed Rs. 10 crore investment into Mukta A2 is a forward-looking action consistent with the priority of sustaining MA2’s operating momentum, though it will result in deconsolidation and dilution of Mukta Arts’ stake to approximately 49%–42%.
