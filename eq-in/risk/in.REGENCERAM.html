Regency Ceramics Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Sustained losses and eroded net worth. The company recorded a net loss of Rs. 256.80 lakhs in Q1 FY 2027 (quarter ended June 30, 2026). The accumulated loss as on June 30, 2026 stood at Rs. 13,165.67 lakhs, resulting in erosion of net worth. For the year ended March 31, 2026, the net loss was Rs. 2,384.48 lakhs, with an accumulated loss of Rs. 12,908.88 lakhs and negative total equity.

Inoperative Yanam factory. The company’s factory at Yanam has remained under lock-out since January 31, 2012, following industrial violence on January 27, 2012 that caused deaths of personnel and destruction of buildings and equipment. A Memorandum of Settlement was signed on October 24, 2019 at Puducherry under Section 12(3) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, under which the management agreed to provide house sites at Yanam to displaced workers; land development and road laying are stated to be complete, with entries to be made in the books after actual allotment of plots to eligible workers.

Reliance on an external manufacturing arrangement. The company has entered into an exclusive manufacturing arrangement to produce glazed vitrified tiles at a facility in Andhra Pradesh, which has been ongoing for the last 21 months. Its own plant operations at Yanam are yet to recommence.

Audit qualifications. The limited review report for Q1 FY 2027 contains a qualified conclusion with multiple bases, including: - Financial statements prepared without considering impairment in the value of Property, Plant and Equipment. - No provision for salary, wages and other benefits to factory employees since the lock-out, and no provision for gratuity and leave encashment under Ind AS-19. - Inability to obtain confirmation of balances from debtors, creditors, loans and advances, and other receivables. - Long-pending trade receivables and advances aggregating to Rs. 1,736.94 lakhs as on June 30, 2026, where the limitation period has expired, with insufficient evidence on recoverability. - Long-pending trade payables aggregating to Rs. 1,138.86 lakhs, with insufficient evidence to demonstrate the liability. - No provision for interest on unsecured loans from directors and body corporates, nor on overdue amounts payable to MSME suppliers exceeding 15 days. - No provision for interest and penalties on old statutory dues. - Inability to comment on the accuracy of inventory valuation; values were considered as given by management.

Write-offs during the quarter. The company wrote off trade receivables of Rs. 6,82,71,567; wrote back trade payables of Rs. 13,36,59,310; and wrote off inventory of Rs. 2,53,11,414. The notes state these balances had remained outstanding beyond the limitation period prescribed under the Limitation Act, 1963, with no acknowledgement of debt or part payment extending the limitation period, and were considered time-barred and not legally enforceable/recoverable. The inventory write-off pertained to raw materials and stores & spares identified as obsolete, non-moving, damaged, or otherwise not usable/realizable during physical verification.

Operating cost pressures. Operations face challenges from fluctuations in raw material prices, energy costs, and logistics expenses.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Revenue growth. Revenue from operations rose to Rs. 3,826.78 lakhs in FY 2025-26 from Rs. 1,314.70 lakhs in the previous year. In Q1 FY 2027, revenue from operations was Rs. 1,832.59 lakhs, compared with Rs. 672.04 lakhs in Q1 FY 2026 and Rs. 1,126.44 lakhs in Q4 FY 2026. Total income for Q1 FY 2027 was Rs. 3,186.30 lakhs, including other income of Rs. 1,353.71 lakhs.

Manufacturing arrangement and efficiency. The exclusive manufacturing arrangement involving leased facilities at Segno Ceramics Private Limited is intended to improve efficiency and logistics.

Product portfolio expansion. Opportunities exist to expand into parking tiles, cool room tiles, and other high-value products, with a broader industry shift away from the Morbi cluster cited as a favourable backdrop.

Yanam recommencement preparation. The company has refurbished one manufacturing line at the plant and states it is ready to recommence plant operations in Yanam. Part of the fixed assets have been refurbished, while some have been scrapped and sold, as reflected in the financial statements.

Arbitration award. The Hon’ble Arbitral Tribunal pronounced an award in March 2022 in favour of the company, as its claim for loss/damage to property was not settled by the Insurance Company on a replacement/reinstatement value basis. The Insurance Company filed a set-aside petition under Section 34 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 before the Court of Principal District Judge, Puducherry. The Principal District Judge largely confirmed the Arbitral Award for Rs. 133.30 crores plus interest. The company has filed subsequent applications in the PDJ court and a caveat in the Madras High Court.

Promoter funding support. Substantial unsecured interest-free loans have been extended by promoters and directors — specifically Ms. Radhika Prasad Narala and Mr. Narala Satyendra Prasad — up to an aggregate limit of Rs. 100 crore.

3. Key Risks

  • Going concern dependence. The financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis, contingent on the business activities initiated generating revenue and cash flows, recommencement of Yanam operations, and continuation of the external manufacturing arrangement.
  • Unprovided liabilities. Employee benefit obligations (gratuity, leave encashment, salary/wages), interest on unsecured loans, MSME interest, and statutory dues interest/penalties remain unprovided, with the auditors unable to quantify the impact on profit or loss and current liabilities.
  • Recoverability and enforceability of balances. Time-barred receivables and payables, and the absence of third-party confirmations, create uncertainty over the accuracy of the financial statements.
  • Asset valuation uncertainty. No impairment assessment has been performed on Property, Plant and Equipment, and inventory valuation relies on management figures without independent verification.
  • Litigation and regulatory dependencies. The insurance arbitration outcome remains subject to further proceedings, and resumption at Yanam depends on engagement with the Government of Puducherry regarding a new industrial policy and with the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas regarding gas supplies.
  • No dividend. No dividend has been recommended for the financial year ended March 31, 2026, due to the absence of distributable profits.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance: business activities initiated, generating revenue and cash flows. Observed: revenue from operations has increased across the periods presented — from Rs. 672.04 lakhs in Q1 FY 2026 to Rs. 1,832.59 lakhs in Q1 FY 2027, and FY 2025-26 revenue of Rs. 3,826.78 lakhs versus Rs. 1,314.70 lakhs in the prior year. However, the company continued to record net losses (Rs. 256.80 lakhs in Q1 FY 2027; Rs. 2,384.48 lakhs in FY 2025-26), and accumulated losses rose to Rs. 13,165.67 lakhs as on June 30, 2026.

Guidance: Yanam plant ready to recommence. Observed: the company states one manufacturing line has been refurbished and it is ready to recommence operations, while the notes also record that part of the fixed assets were scrapped and sold. The lock-out declared on January 31, 2012 remains referenced as the operative status of the factory, and recommencement is still described as pending.

Guidance: exclusive manufacturing arrangement ongoing. Observed: the arrangement to produce glazed vitrified tiles at a facility in Andhra Pradesh is stated to have been ongoing for the last 21 months, and this is the source of current revenue while Yanam remains non-operational.

Guidance: detailed review of long-pending balances in FY 2026-27. Observed: during Q1 FY 2027, the company wrote off trade receivables of Rs. 6,82,71,567, wrote back trade payables of Rs. 13,36,59,310, and wrote off inventory of Rs. 2,53,11,414, with the notes attributing these to limitation-period expiry and physical verification findings.

Guidance: confident about prospects on the revival journey. Observed: the auditors’ qualified conclusion and the multiple bases for qualification — covering impairment, employee benefits, balance confirmations, statutory dues, and inventory valuation — indicate that the financial statements remain subject to unresolved measurement and completeness uncertainties.

Board and governance development. On August 13, 2026, the Board approved the appointment of Mr. Pavan Kumar Duvva (DIN: 01282226) as an Additional Director in the category of Non-Executive Independent Director, effective August 13, 2026, for a period of three years, subject to shareholder approval at the next General Meeting or within three months from the date of appointment, whichever is earlier, and not liable to retire by rotation. He is not related to any Director on the Board. The Board also approved the standalone unaudited financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, and the trading window was closed from July 1, 2026 until 48 hours after announcement of the financial results.
