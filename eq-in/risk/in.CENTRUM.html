Centrum Capital — Recent Corporate Announcements

Headwinds and Challenges

  • Consolidated loss: The Group reported a consolidated loss of Rs 148 crore in Q1 FY 2027. Consolidated revenue declined 10% Q-o-Q following the divestment of the Housing Finance business in Q4 FY 2026, reflecting the changed business mix.
  • Brokerage rate pressure: Institutional Equities faced a reduction in brokerage rates from domestic mutual funds following recent regulatory changes, though brokerage income still improved sequentially.
  • Legacy and market-cycle drags: Unity Small Finance Bank’s PAT comparisons are affected by recoveries related to legacy PMC Bank accounts. Modulus Alternatives’ second fund operates amid broader market cycle volatility and an ongoing West Asia crisis, though the portfolio has shown no material impact from the latter.
  • Deleveraging transition: The standalone improvement came alongside ongoing deleveraging of the balance sheet following the housing finance sale and QIP fund raise during FY 2026, implying a smaller balance sheet base.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Standalone profitability turnaround: Standalone PAT was Rs 3.65 crore in Q1 FY 2027 versus a net loss of Rs 45 crore in Q4 FY 2026 (excluding exceptional items), a 92% Q-o-Q improvement, aided by a 45% Q-o-Q fall in financing costs and 25% Q-o-Q rise in standalone income.
  • Consolidated underlying improvement: Excluding the prior quarter’s exceptional gain, the Q1 FY 2027 consolidated loss represents a 16% sequential improvement, with operating expenses down 11% Q-o-Q.
  • Unity Small Finance Bank: NII grew over 6% Q-o-Q; net advances rose 17% Y-o-Y to Rs 12,016 crore; disbursements of Rs 1,824 crore were up 46% Y-o-Y led by Digital, Commercial and Inclusive Banking. Credit costs fell 44% Q-o-Q and 9% Y-o-Y. Credit cards crossed 3 lakh (up 50% from ~2 lakh at March 31, 2026). CRAR was ~27%, LCR 156%, deposits stable at Rs 12,042 crore, and CASA strengthened to 23.1%.
  • Modulus Alternatives: India Credit Opportunities Fund III (target Rs 2,000 crore including greenshoe) has secured commitments from institutional LPs, marquee banks, family offices and UHNIs, with initial closing scheduled for Q2 FY 2027. Fund II tracks a gross IRR of 16%+, concluded 2 new investments and 1 exit in the quarter, with 17 total investments and 7 exits (6 ahead of scheduled maturity).
  • Investment Banking: Debt Structuring & Advisory secured mandates exceeding Rs 3,200 crore across power, renewables, steel, pharmaceuticals and financial services, with closures expected in Q2–Q3 FY 2027. Equity Capital Markets completed the IPOs of OnEMI Technology Solutions (Kissht) and Waterways Leisure Tourism (Cordelia Cruises), raising over Rs 1,500 crore, advised buybacks for Onward Technologies and Jagsonpal Pharmaceuticals, and advised private fund raises (~Rs 250 crore) for Prime Pharmaceuticals, Lilac Insights and mPokket, plus a strategic investment for Rezon Luxury Silverware. Infrastructure Advisory advanced a power transmission transaction to an advanced stage, expected to conclude in Q2 FY 2027 subject to regulatory approvals.
  • Retail Broking: Client base rose 21%; direct equity AUM reached Rs 5,798 crore as of June 30, 2026. Bancassurance-sourced accounts more than doubled sequentially to over 7,000. Partnerships expanded with Bandhan Bank (over 1,000 branches) and Unity Small Finance Bank (~300 branches), plus in-principle approval from an additional banking partner. Algo trading went live with ten exchange-approved white-box strategies.
  • Institutional Equities: F&O business has stabilised and is expected to contribute meaningfully to revenue and profitability in coming quarters; a robust deal pipeline spans IPOs, QIPs, preferential issues, structured debt, secondary placements and private capital raises. Engagement included 118 corporates and over 1,800 meetings with 243 institutional investors.
  • Centrum Wealth: Net sales team strength increased to approximately 130, presence expanded into Goa, and the product platform now comprises 21 specialised investment desks with over 18 domain experts on a Salesforce-based ecosystem.
  • Capital actions: Shareholders approved a special resolution for raising funds through issue of securities (Item 4) and material related party transactions for corporate guarantees and debt with subsidiaries (Items 5–7), alongside re-appointment of Sharp and Tannan as statutory auditors for five consecutive years (Item 3) and board appointments (Items 8–9).

Key Risks

  • Forward-looking uncertainty: The Performance Highlights explicitly state that forward-looking statements rely on assumptions and are subject to inherent risks and uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ.
  • Execution and closure risk: Several revenue opportunities are pipeline-based — debt structuring closures expected in Q2–Q3 FY 2027, the infrastructure advisory transaction subject to regulatory approvals, and Fund III deployment commencing only after its Q2 FY 2027 initial closing.
  • Regulatory dependence: The fund raise (Item 4) is subject to approval of regulatory and/or statutory authorities. Brokerage income remains exposed to regulatory-driven pricing changes from domestic mutual funds.
  • Related-party concentration: Items 5, 6 and 7 involve material related party transactions for guarantees and inter-se debt among the Company, CRSL, CFSL and subsidiaries; related-party votes were excluded from the poll (40,000 invalid votes recorded in each).
  • Market-cycle exposure: Modulus Alternatives operates amid broader market cycle volatility, and Unity Bank’s credit costs and asset quality remain sensitive to its lending segments.

Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Deleveraging and cost discipline: Management attributes the standalone swing to a 45% Q-o-Q fall in financing costs and balance sheet deleveraging after the housing finance sale and QIP; observed standalone income rose 25% Q-o-Q and PAT turned to Rs 3.65 crore.
  • Profitability trajectory: The Group frames the Q1 FY 2027 consolidated loss as a 16% like-for-like sequential improvement; observed operating expenses fell 11% Q-o-Q against a 10% revenue decline.
  • Unity Bank growth: Management points to strong loan book growth; observed net advances rose 17% Y-o-Y to Rs 12,016 crore, disbursements rose 46% Y-o-Y, and credit costs fell 44% Q-o-Q, with capital (CRAR ~27%) and liquidity (LCR 156%) well above requirements.
  • Fund III timeline: Initial closing is guided for Q2 FY 2027 with deployment thereafter; commitments have been secured but closing had not yet occurred as of the announcement.
  • Institutional Equities: Management expects F&O to become a meaningful contributor “over the coming quarters” and cites a robust pipeline for “the next few quarters”; observed performance was resilient with sequential brokerage income improvement despite lower mutual fund brokerage rates.
  • Governance and approvals: All ten AGM resolutions were passed with requisite majority on August 12, 2026, with the scrutinizer’s report confirming the results; the statutory and secretarial auditor reports contained no qualifications, observations, comments or other remarks.
