BIL Vyapar Limited — Analysis of Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Insolvency proceedings and suspended management. The company is under Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP), admitted by the NCLT Kolkata Bench pursuant to an order dated November 13, 2025, received on November 21, 2025, on a petition filed by Punjab National Bank. Mr. Subodh Kumar Agrawal was appointed Interim Resolution Professional; Ms. Rachna Jhunjhunwala was confirmed as Resolution Professional by order dated January 13, 2026 (received February 4, 2026). The powers of the Board of Directors were suspended from November 21, 2025 and are exercised by the IRP/RP. The company is under moratorium under Section 14 of the IBC, so no fresh proceedings can be initiated or continued against it.

Eroded net worth and liquidation-basis accounts. As at June 30, 2026, accumulated losses were INR 21,790.61 lakh, fully eroding paid-up equity capital of INR 3,138.49 lakh, and liabilities exceeded total assets by INR 18,652.11 lakh. In the absence of any business plan, the going concern assumption was not considered appropriate, and the financial results were prepared on a liquidation basis.

Contingent corporate guarantee exposure. Corporate guarantees to lenders of Edayar Zinc Limited (EZL) had an aggregate outstanding of INR 8,025 lakh (excluding interest) as at June 30, 2026, unchanged from March 31, 2026. Although EZL entered a One-Time Settlement and Mina Ventures Private Limited consented to replace the guarantee and bear EZL’s liabilities without recourse, PNB and Punjab & Sind Bank filed and had admitted claims in the company’s CIRP, and the guarantee has not been formally released. A letter of comfort/undertaking of INR 5,171.20 lakh was issued for BIL Infratech Limited; a loss allowance provision of INR 2,149.10 lakh is maintained.

Wada land litigation. The company terminated a January 21, 2019 MOU with MWBIE and sold the Wada land to Afamado Advisory Services Private Limited via a Deed of Conveyance dated March 31, 2021 (registered May 11, 2021) for INR 3,500 lakh. By order dated April 6, 2026, the Trial Court restrained alienation of the property pending disposal of MWBIE’s suit. MWBIE lodged a claim of INR 9,494.92 lakh with the RP on January 6, 2026, of which only INR 59.40 lakh has been admitted; the balance remains disputed and unadmitted.

Ahmedabad property and Sirohi land issues. An Agreement for Sale dated May 22, 2025 for the Ahmedabad property was at a loss of INR 33.51 lakh, but the sale was not recognised in FY 2025-26; the purchasers’ appeal before NCLAT is pending. Sirohi Land continues to be recorded in the books even though a search report indicates it was sold and registered in earlier years; the RP has filed a Section 66 application seeking avoidance as a fraudulent and undervalued transaction.

Unresolved receivables and asset valuations. Income Tax-related receivables of INR 1,144.61 lakh could not be identified or traced against corresponding liabilities/demands due to inadequate records. Valuation reports for Assets Held for Sale (including Land and Buildings) prepared through registered valuers were not shared with the auditor as confidential, so these assets continue at book value rather than net realisable value.

Dissolution of the sole subsidiary. Global Composite Holdings Inc., a wholly owned foreign subsidiary, was dissolved by the Secretary of State, Minnesota, on May 21, 2026. The investment was derecognised at its carrying value of INR 1.50 lakh, with the resulting loss recognised as an exceptional item in the quarter ended June 30, 2026. The company ceased to have any subsidiary, and consolidated financial statements are no longer required from that quarter.

Auditor disclaimer. The independent auditor issued a disclaimer of conclusion, being unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence across the matters above.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Resolution plans under voting. Six resolution plans have been received and are currently under voting by the Committee of Creditors, as noted in the outcome of the 19th CoC meeting held August 14, 2026.

Extended CIRP timeline. The CIRP period, previously extended by 90 days and due to conclude on August 10, 2026, was further extended by 45 days by the NCLT Kolkata Bench, with the process now due to end on September 24, 2026.

Claim reconciliation progress. Claims totalling INR 1,738.63 crore were received from financial creditors, operational creditors, workmen and employees, statutory dues and other creditors; INR 1,226.02 crore has been accepted by the Resolution Professional based on documents received and the corporate debtor’s books.

Potential recoveries and settlements. The Section 66 PUFE application on Sirohi Land, if successful, would benefit unrelated unsecured financial creditors. The BNP Paribas matter was settled: pursuant to a Bombay City Civil Court order dated December 4, 2025, INR 76.25 lakh was released to the Joint Directorate General of Foreign Trade on December 31, 2025; interest income of INR 64.78 lakh was recognised in FY 2025-26 and a no-longer-required provision of INR 105.55 lakh was written back, leaving the fixed deposit carrying value at INR 169.95 lakh as at June 30, 2026. An Axis Bank account titled “Unpaid Binani Metals Ltd - Shareholders Fund for Fractional Shares” holds INR 30.35 lakh as at June 30, 2026, not reflected in the books, which the company will account for on release of funds.

Prior promoter support. Until receipt of the NCLT order, Triton Trading Company Private Limited, a promoter company, had committed and provided continued operational support; its claim filed during CIRP was accepted based on supporting papers and the corporate debtor’s books.

3. Key Risks

  • Outcome uncertainty in CIRP: the resolution plan, if any, remains under evaluation/voting, and the ultimate outcome of claims adjudication cannot presently be determined.
  • Guarantee and letter of comfort exposure: admitted claims by EZL lenders and PNB’s application against BIL Vyapar for BIL Infratech’s balance outstanding could crystallise liabilities beyond the INR 2,149.10 lakh loss allowance provided.
  • Litigation risk: the Wada land suit (with an alienation restraint order dated April 6, 2026 and a pending appeal before the Bombay High Court), the Ahmedabad property appeal before NCLAT, and the Sirohi Land PUFE application all remain unresolved.
  • Asset carrying values: Land and Buildings are carried at book value rather than estimated net realisable values, so adjustments, impairments or write-downs may be required.
  • Receivables recoverability: the INR 1,144.61 lakh Income Tax-related receivables remain uncertain in recoverability and settlement.
  • No going concern: accounts are prepared on a liquidation basis, with assets at lower of carrying amount and estimated net realisable value and liabilities at estimated settlement amounts.
  • No provision for post-CIRP expenses: no provision for expenses incurred after November 21, 2025 has been made in the books.
  • Audit assurance gap: the auditor was unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence and disclaimed a conclusion.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance and forward-looking elements. The disclosures state that six resolution plans are under voting by the CoC and that the CIRP period ends September 24, 2026. The company states it will account for the Axis Bank funds on release by the bank, and that the benefit of a favourable Section 66 outcome would accrue to unrelated unsecured financial creditors. The 63rd Annual General Meeting is scheduled for Wednesday, September 30, 2026 at 04:00 P.M. through video conferencing/other audio-visual means, and M/s. RNM & Associates, Chartered Accountant, has been recommended for appointment as Statutory Auditors for FY 2026-27 to fill the casual vacancy caused by the resignation of M/s. TLB & Co.

Observed performance. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, total income was INR 15.67 lakh and loss after tax was INR 24.44 lakh (including an exceptional item of INR 1.50 lakh on derecognition of the dissolved subsidiary), compared with a loss after tax of INR 69.69 lakh in the quarter ended June 30, 2025 and a profit after tax of INR 140.81 lakh in the quarter ended March 31, 2026. For the year ended March 31, 2026, total income was INR 173.69 lakh and loss after tax was INR 3.34 lakh. Basic and diluted EPS were INR (0.08) for the June 2026 quarter, against INR 0.45 for the March 2026 quarter and INR (0.22) for the June 2025 quarter. Accumulated losses rose from INR 21,766.17 lakh at March 31, 2026 to INR 21,790.61 lakh at June 30, 2026, and the excess of liabilities over assets widened from INR 18,627.68 lakh to INR 18,652.11 lakh over the same period. The company has ceased to have any subsidiary, and from the quarter ended June 30, 2026 only standalone results are presented.
