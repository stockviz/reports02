Lloyds Metals and Energy — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Copper segment losses and plant shutdown. The Chemaf copper operations reported negative EBITDA of USD 2.63 million in Q1 FY27. The company attributes this to the shutdown of the Chemaf Oxide Plant (EP-1) for care, maintenance and upgradation, which reduced revenue while employee and other operating costs continued to be incurred during the transition. Higher sulphuric acid and diesel prices, linked to the Middle East war, added to the pressure. Upgradation of the Sulphide EP-2 plant is in progress, with expected commencement in April 2027.

Elevated consolidated debt. Consolidated net debt stands at around INR 19,000 crores, a significant part of which relates to the Chemaf acquisition. Management states it is working to renegotiate this on more favourable terms, with financial closure targeted in the next three to four months. Restructuring is expected to reduce debt by approximately 40–50%, partly because the current figure includes accrued interest and penalties from the past.

Fuel cost pressure at Thriveni. Thriveni’s Q1 FY27 margins were marginally impacted by higher fuel costs due to the Gulf crisis. Pass-through negotiations with clients are underway. Despite this, management maintained its full-year EBITDA margin guidance of 28–30%.

NTPC wage receivable dispute. An auditor’s note flagged an NTPC wage receivable of INR 300-odd crores that continues to grow each quarter. NTPC walked away from the settlement two years ago. The company has won an arbitration award, but the matter remains sub judice, and management stated it does not intend to make a provision, expecting resolution in the next two to three months.

Flat iron ore EBITDA per tonne despite volume growth. Iron ore sales volume rose 58% year-on-year, but EBITDA per tonne remained flat at INR 2,230. Management explained that sales price stayed around INR 6,000 per tonne and that increasing volumes are being consumed internally, with margin transferring to value-added products rather than appearing in outward iron ore sales.

Pricing uncertainty. Management declined to characterise pricing as depressed or bullish, noting it is a commodity that changes with every season, and stated that predicting sustainable margins is not possible.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record standalone performance. Q1 FY27 standalone revenue was INR 5,413 crores (up 127% YoY), EBITDA INR 2,120 crores (up 172% YoY), and PAT INR 1,527 crores (up 141% YoY). EBITDA margin reached 39.2%, the highest ever, expanding 639 bps YoY and 631 bps QoQ. Consolidated revenue was INR 7,354 crores (up 209% YoY) with EBITDA of INR 2,782 crores.

Pellet ramp-up. The second pellet plant was commissioned in May 2026 and reached 100% capacity utilisation within four months. Pellet production was 1.69 million tonnes with realisation of INR 11,783 per tonne and EBITDA of INR 5,803 per tonne. The sales mix was 75.3% domestic and 24.7% export, with exports expanded into Kenya, South Korea, Indonesia and China.

Structural cost advantages. The slurry pipeline lowered logistics and freight costs, with savings of INR 500–550 per tonne on pellet costing. Management described captive ore, captive logistics and a growing share of value-added products as the basis for structural rather than cyclical margins. Value-added products contributed 41% of standalone revenue and 40% of EBIT, versus 13% and 2% a year earlier.

Thriveni scale-up. Thriveni Q1 FY27 revenue was INR 2,672 crores (up 63% YoY) with EBITDA of INR 658 crores (up 145% YoY) and margins of 24.63%. Gadchiroli ROM handling capacity was enhanced from 10 MTPA to 55 MTPA following environmental clearances. Odisha volumes are expected to grow 39% YoY to 34–35 million tonnes in FY27, supported by the Laserda-Pacheri mine commencing in Q1 FY27 (1.5 MTPA target) and Dalpahar expected in Q2 FY27 (3 MTPA target). Two new Odisha mining contracts are expected to carry EBITDA margins above 40%.

Green fleet and logistics savings. Thriveni has around 150 electric and LNG vehicles and plans to add 200 more (50 LNG, 150 EV), with 20 charging stations installed and 30 more planned. Management expects 30–40% cost savings and an increase in EBITDA for that operation from 32% to 40%.

Tata Steel partnership. BRPL began MDO operations at Tata Steel’s Joda West manganese mines on 1 June 2026, contributing pellet EBITDA of approximately INR 48 crores in Q1 FY27. Feasibility work is underway for volume expansion and for a slurry pipeline linking Tata Steel’s Odisha mines to its steel plants under a BOT service model.

Project pipeline. BHQ beneficiation is targeted for commissioning by March 2028, with pilot plant yield of 38%+ against an original assumption of 35%, and finished product grade of 66–67% with total gangue below 3%. The first 1.2 million tonne long product steel plant is expected to be commissioned by March 2027. The third pellet plant and expanded slurry pipeline network are in progress. The Panguna (ABG) project in Papua New Guinea is at a study stage, with mining lease commencement on 7 August, 400+ people employed, a Learning & Development Centre inaugurated on 11 July with 300 cadets, and exploration drilling started.

Renewable energy for captive consumption. The Board approved investments in three SPVs for a 66 MW wind and solar portfolio — 36 MW wind (Amplus Green One), 12 MW wind (Amplus Energy One) and 17.59 MWac / 26.47 MWpdc solar (Amplus Ceres) — for minimum 26% equity stakes totalling up to INR 48.02 crores. Captive tariff is fixed for 25 years, with estimated savings of INR ~620 million in Year 1 and INR ~14.5 billion in Year 25. Separately, the company subscribed to additional equity in Hexa Energy MH3 on 4 July 2026, taking its holding to 29.72%.

DRC copper export ban not a constraint. Management stated that both Chemaf assets will produce final cathodes, so the DRC government’s announced ban on copper concentrate exports does not impact the company, though it may affect players exporting concentrate.

3. Key Risks

Commodity price volatility. Management repeatedly declined to forecast margins or pricing, describing the business as a commodity exposed to seasonal and annual price changes.

Execution and financing risk on large capex. Capex of INR 13,513 crores was incurred during FY24–FY26, with a further INR 3,005 crores in Q1 FY27 alone. Management guided to approximately INR 11,000 crores over the next two years and INR 15,000–20,000 crores in the third year, excluding the PNG Panguna project. The Chemaf JV requires around USD 300+ million to complete, with financial closure targeted within three months and multiple financing parties under discussion.

Regulatory and approval dependencies. The renewable energy acquisitions are subject to compliance with the Electricity Act, 2003 and captive power regulations in Maharashtra, with consummation expected by FY27 end. The BHQ project required forest land, which has been secured.

Litigation and receivable risk. The NTPC arbitration matter remains sub judice with no provision made, and management’s expectation of resolution is time-bound to the next two to three months.

Copper segment transition risk. The EP-1 shutdown and EP-2 upgradation mean the copper business is not yet contributing positively, with commencement of the larger asset targeted for Q1 FY28.

Related party and subsidiary funding. The Board approved conversion of USD 200 million of loans to Lloyds Global Resources FZCO into equity, and an enabling approval for further investment of up to USD 200 million in preference shares or hybrid securities. The Board also approved investment of up to INR 625 crores in Thriveni Earthmovers and Infra Private Limited via rights issue, where the company holds 72.0253%.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Thriveni margin guidance. Management guided to 28–30% full-year EBITDA margins despite Q1 FY27 margins of 24.63% being marginally impacted by higher fuel costs, with pass-through negotiations underway. Observed Q1 performance was below the guided full-year range, and management expressed confidence in reaching the range as pass-throughs conclude and volumes scale.

Iron ore production guidance. FY27 guidance is 26 MnT against FY26 actual of 21.96 MnT. Q1 FY27 production was 6.05 MnT with a monthly run rate now upwards of 2 million tonnes. Management noted an opening stock of 1.5 million tonnes of iron ore to be sold during the year, implying sales above 26 million tonnes.

Pellet production guidance. FY27 guidance is 7.75–8 MnT against FY26 actual of 3.03 MnT. Q1 FY27 production was 1.69 MnT, with 100% capacity utilisation achieved within four months of the second plant’s May 2026 commissioning.

DRI production guidance. FY27 guidance is 825 KT against FY26 actual of 484 KT. Q1 FY27 DRI sales volume was 183.92 KT, up 133% YoY.

Steel commissioning guidance. The 1.2 MnT WRM plant is guided for Q4 FY27 commissioning, with management stating in the call it expects commissioning by March 2027. Construction work has started and the project is described as at advanced stages and expected on time.

Copper production guidance. FY27 guidance is 8–10 KT against FY26 actual of 2.5 KT. Q1 FY27 total copper produced was 2,754 tonnes. The presentation shows an expected production ramp-up to 45,372 tonnes in FY28 and 85,251 tonnes in FY29, with copper production by Q1 FY28.

BHQ timeline. Management reaffirmed commissioning by March 2028 as per the original schedule, with pilot plant results confirming 38% recovery against the original 35% assumption and costs broadly within original estimates.

Capex guidance. Management guided to approximately INR 11,000 crores over the next two years and INR 15,000–20,000 crores in the third year, plus around USD 300 million for foreign operations in the current financial year. Q1 FY27 capex of INR 3,005 crores is consistent with the stated trajectory.

Chemaf debt restructuring. Management guided to financial closure within three to four months and a 40–50% reduction in the relevant debt, with part of the debt (including some EPC contractors) already settled and the remainder pending before agreed deadlines.

Dividend and governance. The 49th AGM held on 19 June 2026 passed all resolutions with requisite majority, including adoption of FY26 audited financial statements and declaration of dividend for FY26. Mr. Avijit Ghosh was appointed as an Additional Director designated as Non-Executive, Independent Director for five years from 10 August 2026, subject to member approval.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a cautious Not Rated on a debt-free iron-ore/DRI miner with near-term iron-ore mix/realisation and DRI margin pressure plus a large capex plan, to a Buy on an integrated metals producer spanning pellets, steel, MDO, and copper-cobalt. Persistent themes were captive low-cost iron ore, slurry-pipeline logistics savings, BHQ/beneficiation-driven volume growth, and long-term capacity expansion. The last report added new growth pillars (Tata Steel partnership, DRC cathodes, IPS subsidies, BHQ royalty relief) and shifted risk emphasis toward project execution, geopolitics, commodity prices, subsidy timing, and Bougainville.

Optimism that failed

  • Early expectation of Rs 700-800/t and Rs 1,100-1,200/t slurry-pipeline logistics savings did not materialise at those levels; the last report cited Phase-I savings of only Rs 500-600/t and prospective savings of Rs 800-1,000/t.
