PRUDENT: Key Developments from the 23rd Annual General Meeting

Headwinds and Challenges

The 23rd AGM, held on 31 July 2026 through video conferencing, saw all five resolutions passed with the requisite majority, but the voting patterns reveal notable dissent on certain governance-related items.

The resolution to approve an increase in remuneration of Mr. Shirish Govindbhai Patel, Whole-time Director and CEO, was passed as a Special Resolution with 92.30% of votes polled in favour and 7.70% against. Public Institutions voted 80.28% in favour and 19.72% against, while Public Non-Institutions voted 99.33% in favour and 0.67% against. This was the most contested resolution of the meeting.

The resolution to approve the appointment of Mrs. Maitry Dhruvin Shah, a Related Party, to hold an Office or Place of Profit and payment of remuneration to her, was passed as an Ordinary Resolution with 85.65% of votes polled in favour and 14.35% against. The promoter and promoter group were interested in this agenda and did not vote. Public Institutions voted 85.64% in favour and 14.35% against, while Public Non-Institutions voted 99.33% in favour and 0.67% against. This resolution attracted the highest proportion of votes against among all resolutions.

The resolution to re-appoint Mr. Shirish Govindbhai Patel as a director retiring by rotation was passed with 99.93% in favour and 0.07% against. Public Institutions voted 99.82% in favour and 0.18% against, while Public Non-Institutions voted 99.79% in favour and 0.21% against.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

The AGM approved the adoption of both Standalone and Consolidated Financial Statements for the year ended 31 March 2026, with 99.99% of votes polled in favour and a negligible 0.00% against. This reflects strong shareholder support for the financial reporting.

A Final Dividend of ₹3.50 per Equity Share of face value ₹5 each for the Financial Year ended 31 March 2026 was declared, passed with 99.99% of votes polled in favour and 0.01% against. Public Institutions voted 99.98% in favour and 0.02% against, while Public Non-Institutions voted 99.9988% in favour and 0.0012% against.

The re-appointment of Mr. Shirish Govindbhai Patel as a director retiring by rotation was approved with 99.93% of votes polled in favour.

Key Risks

The resolution to increase remuneration of Mr. Shirish Govindbhai Patel, Whole-time Director and CEO, saw 7.70% of votes polled against, with Public Institutions recording 19.72% against. This indicates a segment of institutional shareholders expressed reservations on the compensation proposal.

The resolution regarding the appointment of Mrs. Maitry Dhruvin Shah, a Related Party, saw 14.35% of votes polled against, with Public Institutions recording 14.35% against. The promoter and promoter group were interested in this agenda and did not vote, meaning the outcome was determined by public shareholders. This level of dissent on a related-party matter represents a governance-related risk.

The adoption of financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2026 and the declaration of a final dividend of ₹3.50 per equity share were approved with near-unanimous support, with 99.99% of votes polled in favour for both resolutions.

The re-appointment of Mr. Shirish Govindbhai Patel as a director retiring by rotation was approved with 99.93% of votes polled in favour, with 0.07% against.

The increase in remuneration of Mr. Shirish Govindbhai Patel was approved with 92.30% of votes polled in favour and 7.70% against, with Public Institutions recording 19.72% against.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from early concerns about TER cuts and margin compression (FY23) to later focus on regulatory stabilization of gross yields and cost pressures from wage revisions (FY26), while persistent themes included MFD network strength, SIP-driven equity growth, and non-MF diversification. The initial fear of yield compression partially materialized as gross MF yield fell from 90bps to 88.2bps, and the optimism around margin resilience shifted toward acknowledging EBITDA pressure from 19% QoQ employee cost growth.

Fears that came true

  • Regulatory TER/BER cuts materialized as gross MF yield fell from 90.0 bps to 88.2 bps, with further 1-2 bps decline expected over the medium-to-long term.
  • Employee cost pressure emerged with a 19% QoQ rise due to wage revisions and branch expansion, guided at 22-24% staff cost growth in FY27.
  • Weak equity benchmark performance materialized, though partially offset by SIP flows, the Indus acquisition, and MTM gains.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that yield cuts would be successfully passed through to intermediaries was only partially validated, as net MF yield improvement relied on GST-related regulatory changes and exit load removal rather than pure pass-through resilience.
  • Direct channel migration risk persisted as a structural headwind, though the MFD network remained dominant with ~600 new partners added monthly.
