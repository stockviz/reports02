CERA Sanitaryware — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost inflation. The quarter saw elevated input cost pressures, particularly brass, which management described as continuing to rise. Brass prices moved from about INR 665 in December to INR 747 in January, INR 758 in February, INR 800 in April, INR 860 in May, INR 880 in June, and around INR 900 in July. Gas cost was also elevated, with weighted average cost at INR 48.43 per cubic meter in Q1 FY27 versus INR 33.17 per cubic meter in Q1 FY26; gas accounted for approximately 3.3% of revenue during the quarter. Gas consumption was sourced 69% from GAIL and 31% from Sabarmati.

Margin compression. EBITDA excluding other income was INR 49.2 crore versus INR 53.1 crore in the corresponding quarter of the previous year, with EBITDA margin at 10.1% versus 13.1%. Management attributed the moderation primarily to one-time and transitional factors not expected to recur beyond Q2: - A one-time provision towards long-term settlement of workers for the prior period September 2025 to March 2026, amounting to INR 6.3 crore (1.3% margin impact), with INR 3 crore for the current period; the full-year wage cost impact is anticipated at INR 12 crore, and the total P&L impact for the year at INR 18.3 crore. - Lower absorption of fixed costs due to reduced production amid temporary gas supply uncertainty. The company operated a single kiln during part of March, April, and May, starting the second kiln only in June; production was impacted by 30%–35%, with an under-absorption impact of INR 3.7 crore (0.75% margin impact). - Foreclosure of old project orders ahead of anticipated further brass and gas price increases, which raised the project share of revenue from 39% in the previous quarter to 43% in the current quarter, with an impact of about INR 4 crore (0.8% margin impact). - Delayed pass-through of price increases: the May 2026 price hike was not reflected in project contracts, and retail dispatches at old prices continued into the backlog, with an estimated 1.5% margin impact. Management stated that from July onwards retail dispatches began at revised prices.

Morbi cluster dependence. For the last few weeks and months, the company progressively reduced dependence on the Morbi cluster by internalizing several key SKUs. In Q1, there were initial availability challenges from Morbi, with anticipation of challenges from Q2 onwards, though management stated it is comfortable on availability for Q2 and the full year.

Project pricing lag. Project contracts remained insulated from price revisions due to pre-booked orders, with transition to revised pricing expected post Q2, supporting margin recovery in subsequent quarters.

Wellness decline. Wellness revenue declined by 7% year-on-year.

Management transition. Mr. Baliga resigned for personal reasons; his responsibilities were limited to Senator and Polipluz. An existing national head has been identified under the succession plan to lead these businesses, with Mr. Baliga supporting until September 30 for a smooth transition.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Revenue growth. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was INR 486 crore versus INR 407 crore in Q1 FY25 (as stated in the transcript), a 19.5% year-on-year increase. Growth was predominantly volume-driven and broad-based across retail and project segments.

Segment performance. Sanitaryware contributed 47% of revenues and grew 14% year-on-year (volume ~10%, price ~2%, favourable mix ~2%). Faucetware contributed 40% and grew 25% (volume ~18%, price ~4%, favourable mix ~3%). Tiles contributed 11% and grew 22%. Wellness contributed 2% and declined 7%. Core categories (Sanitaryware and Faucetware) accounted for 87% of total revenues.

Pricing actions. Following revisions in March and May 2026, cumulative price increases stand at approximately 12% in Sanitaryware and 16% in Faucetware, described as well-absorbed by the market.

Demand environment. Improving consumer sentiment in retail and sustained momentum in the project segment. Retail carried forward gradual improvement seen over recent quarters.

Brand and marketing. A new integrated campaign, “Your Moment of Cera,” was launched featuring Kriti Sanon as brand ambassador, rolled out across television, digital, and other media. FY27 brand-building and marketing investment is planned at approximately INR 85 crore, spanning television, digital, social media, and on-ground activations; management noted the budget could be increased if needed.

Digital transformation. The Dealer Management System (DMS) was extended to the retailer loyalty program, providing visibility into secondary sales, inventory movement, and channel engagement.

Capacity and expansion. Capacity utilization was 61% for Sanitaryware and 96% for Faucetware. Both plants have operated since June, with operational utilization at roughly 80% of total capacity. Faucetware brownfield expansion is expected to come into effect from Q4 FY27; management sees no material availability challenges before then, noting Faucetware can operate beyond 100% utilization. The greenfield Sanitaryware expansion (previously deferred) will be reviewed; a decision, once taken, would involve approximately 18 months from start. FY27 capex is planned at approximately INR 43 crore, directed at Faucetware brownfield expansion, manufacturing efficiencies, digital initiatives, and operational infrastructure. Internalization of SKUs requires INR 2–3 crore of routine investment.

Working capital and balance sheet. Inventory days decreased from 80 to 68; receivables from 38 to 30 days; payables increased from 43 to 48 days; net working capital cycle improved from 75 to 50 days year-on-year. Cash and cash equivalents stood at INR 943 crore as of June 30, 2026.

Product mix and geography. Premium accounted for 44% of sales, mid 37%, entry-level 19%. Tier 3 and Tier 1 cities each accounted for 38%, Tier 2 for 24%.

Senator and Polipluz. These newer initiatives remain in a build-up phase; targets remain intact, with progress to be evaluated over a longer horizon. Senator stores are guided to increase from 35 to 50 by the end of the current financial year.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued rise in brass prices beyond the current range (around INR 900) could necessitate further price increases, with a lag before reflecting in the topline; management indicated that if prices reach INR 950–1,000, another price rise may be considered.
  • Elevated gas costs and potential supply uncertainty affecting production and fixed cost absorption.
  • Project segment pricing transition timing: projects have validity of six months to one year, and old-price projects must close before revised pricing reflects, with some impact expected in Q2 as well.
  • Dependence on outsourcing partners, including the Morbi cluster, for certain SKUs, though internalization efforts are underway.
  • Competitive intensity in the marketplace, referenced in the context of margin guidance visibility.
  • Execution risk around newer brands (Senator, Polipluz) and the management transition, though dedicated operating teams, established processes, and a strong second line of management are cited.
  • Gross margin sensitivity: Q1 gross margin was 46% versus typical levels of 51%, with Faucetware gross margin severely impacted; recovery to around 51% is expected from Q3 if brass and gas prices hold at current levels.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Revenue growth guidance. Management maintained FY27 revenue growth guidance of 18% to 20%. Observed Q1 FY27 revenue growth was 19.5% year-on-year, within the guided range.

EBITDA margin guidance. Management maintained FY27 EBITDA margin guidance of 13.5% to 14%, adjusted for the change in revenue presentation (turnover discounts now deducted from revenue rather than reported as an expense, reducing reported revenue by 2.5% in the current quarter and 3% in the prior-year quarter, with no impact on absolute profitability or cash flows). Observed Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 10.1%, versus 13.1% in Q1 FY26. Management stated that excluding the identified one-time impacts (totalling 4.35%), EBITDA would have been in the range of 14.5%. Management expects some impact to continue in Q2, with full-year margins meeting the 13.5% to 14% guidance.

Margin recovery timeline. Management expects project segment margins to normalize post Q2, with gross margin recovery to around 51% from Q3, assuming brass and gas prices hold at current levels. No guidance was provided for FY28 or for a return to historical average EBITDA margins of 16%, with management stating visibility exists only for the current year.

Capacity and expansion guidance. Faucetware brownfield expansion expected to be effective from Q4 FY27. Greenfield Sanitaryware expansion decision to be reviewed by end of year based on demand continuation. Senator stores guided from 35 to 50 by end of FY27.

Working capital. Improvement delivered: net working capital cycle from 75 to 50 days year-on-year.

Dividend and governance. The 28th AGM was held on July 23, 2026, through video conferencing/OAVM. All four resolutions (adoption of audited financial statements for FY2025-26, declaration of dividend for FY2025-26, re-appointment of Mrs. Deepshikha Khaitan as director, and ratification of Cost Auditors’ remuneration for FY2026-27) were passed with overwhelming majority; dissent was negligible or 0.02% on the director re-appointment resolution. The Board approved unaudited financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, on August 7, 2026, reviewed by the Audit Committee the same day, with a Limited Review Report from Singhi & Co. 

Broker Narrative

The initial narrative centered on aggressive growth via market share gains, product launches, distribution expansion, and margin improvement, but by the last report, optimism shifted toward real estate completion-cycle proxy play, institutional demand from past housing sales, and debottlenecking-led capacity expansion. Persistent input cost inflation and macroeconomic weakness remained consistent concerns throughout, while the early enthusiasm for organic demand-driven outperformance faded in favor of cycle-dependent and pricing-driven drivers. The Buy call was maintained in both reports, but the underlying thesis moved from growth-outperformance confidence to cycle-timing optimism amid rising cost pressures.

Fears that came true

  • Input cost inflation in brass and natural gas flagged in the first report materialized, as explicitly confirmed by the last report’s headwinds on increasing raw material costs, correlating with a prolonged string of DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes from 2023 through 2026.
  • Macroeconomic slowdown and weak real estate activity flagged early materialized, evidenced by overwhelmingly negative actual returns and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes across more than 20 broker reports over three years.
  • Intensifying competition and potential return of the unorganized sector flagged in the first report came true, as the stock failed to deliver sustained outperformance despite repeated bullish narratives, resulting in DISAPPOINTMENT across nearly all Buy and Accumulate calls.

Optimism that failed

  • Aggressive market share gains at 3x the industry rate failed to translate into positive stock returns, as actual returns were deeply negative in nearly all reports citing this growth driver, producing DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Robust FCF generation and a strong balance sheet expected to drive rerating failed to materialize, as the stock rarely rerated meaningfully with only one SUCCESS out of 40 reports and most ratings yielding negative actual returns.
  • Margin expansion of ~50bps to 16.7% by FY25E via cost efficiency and product mix did not sufficiently offset input cost pressures, correlating with persistent DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes despite the optimistic margin narrative.
