Headwinds and Challenges

  • Middle East geopolitical disruption: The Q1 FY27 quarter (ended June 30, 2026) began against continuing uncertainty across the Middle East, with travel routes, destination preferences, and booking behaviour still adjusting to the disruption.
  • FEMA enforcement matter: A show-cause notice dated September 19, 2023 was issued by the Special Director to the company, its Joint Managing Directors, and others, alleging the company permitted foreign travel agents to book tickets with airlines and accept payments in Indian Rupees from parties other than those to whom services were rendered, in violation of Section 3(c) read with Section 42(1) of FEMA to the extent of INR 493.70 Mn. The company identified total amounts of contravention, including transactions with other customers, at INR 712.25 Mn. Section 13 of FEMA provides for a maximum penalty of three times the amount involved. The RBI declined post-facto approval requests communicated on April 7, 2025 and again on September 2, 2025. Adjudication proceedings were held on November 3, 2025 and April 2, 2026, and the matter remains sub-judice. The final outcome and related financial impact cannot be ascertained at this stage, and no adjustments have been made to the Q1 FY27 results pending resolution.
  • Foreign exchange volatility: The consolidated results show a net foreign exchange loss of INR 55.02 Mn in Q1 FY27, compared with a net loss of INR 38.25 Mn in Q1 FY25 and INR 88.31 Mn for the year ended March 31, 2026.
  • Subsidiary restructuring and closures: One subsidiary included in the consolidated results has been prepared on a non-going concern basis as it ceased operations. Winding up of one step-down subsidiary (Tek Travels Arabia for Travel and Tourism) is under process, and subsequent to the quarter end, the Board of Tek Travels FZCO approved liquidation of another step-down subsidiary (TBO Tek Spolka z ograniczonq odpowiedzialnosciq). During the year ended March 31, 2026, one step-down subsidiary incurred INR 41.04 Mn in severance pay as part of restructuring.
  • New Labour Codes: The Code on Wages, 2019, Code on Social Security, 2020, Industrial Relations Code, 2020, and Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 became effective November 21, 2025. The company recognised an additional provision for gratuity and compensated absences of INR 4.51 Mn (standalone, net of cross-charge) and the Group recognised INR 12.17 Mn, presented as exceptional items. The company continues to monitor finalisation of Central and State Rules and will record necessary accounting impacts as required.
  • Investor conference schedule risk: The schedule of investor conferences is subject to changes due to exigencies on the part of the Investors/Company.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong Q1 FY27 growth: Revenue from operations rose 81% YoY to ₹926 Cr (from ₹511 Cr). GTV rose 37% YoY to ₹11,154 Cr (from ₹8,119 Cr). Gross Profit rose 66% YoY to ₹552 Cr. Adjusted EBITDA rose 77% YoY to ₹150 Cr. PAT rose 32% YoY to ₹83 Cr.
  • Operating leverage: Adj. EBITDA grew faster than Gross Profit, which management described as early operating leverage driving margin expansion. Management stated the platform’s scalability and operating infrastructure will allow support of progressively greater volumes without a commensurate increase in costs.
  • Broad-based geographic growth: The quarter saw broad-based growth across India, APAC, Europe, North America, and LATAM. For Hotels + Ancillary, GTV in APAC grew 56% YoY, Europe 37%, and LATAM 20%.
  • Segment performance: Hotels + Ancillary GTV reached ₹7,578 Cr, up 50% YoY. Airlines GTV reached ₹3,577 Cr, up 17% YoY. India Airlines GTV grew 14.7% YoY.
  • Monthly Transacting Buyers: Reached 33,736, up 14% YoY.
  • Cash generation: Cash and cash equivalents (including bank balances, bank deposits, and liquid investments) were ₹1,984 Cr as on June 30, 2026, up ₹392 Cr from March 31, 2026, driven in part by working capital release. Management noted cash generation significantly exceeded Adjusted EBITDA due to release of timing-related working capital items.
  • Classic Vacations acquisition: On October 1, 2025, TBO LLC (step-down wholly owned US subsidiary) completed acquisition of 100% of Classic Vacations LLC, a B2B2C luxury travel company with a network of high-value travel advisors in USA, for USD 125 Mn consideration (closing consideration USD 110.58 Mn plus closing adjustments USD 14.42 Mn). Funded through internal accruals, inter-corporate loan of INR 3,154.77 Mn (USD 35.89 Mn) from the Holding Company to Tek Travels DMCC, and credit facilities (USD 70 Mn) backed by corporate guarantees. Q1 FY27 results include Classic Vacations and are not comparable with Q1 FY25.
  • India outbound opportunity: Management expressed confidence in the long-term opportunity presented by increasing scale and sophistication of Indian outbound travel.
  • Investor engagement: Company officials will attend the Goldman Sachs Asia Leaders Conference 2026 in Hong Kong on September 2, 2026, the Jefferies 5th India Forum in Gurgaon on September 18, 2026, and the Anand Rathi Annual Flagship Conference in Mumbai on September 22, 2026.

Key Risks

  • FEMA penalty exposure: Maximum penalty under Section 13 of FEMA is three times the amount involved in contravention. The alleged contravention is INR 493.70 Mn, with total identified contravention including other customers at INR 712.25 Mn. The final outcome and impact cannot be ascertained.
  • Regulatory and legal uncertainty: The FEMA matter remains sub-judice with no adjustments made to financial results pending final outcome.
  • Foreign exchange exposure: Net foreign exchange losses are separately disclosed due to volatility. Q1 FY27 consolidated net loss was INR 55.02 Mn.
  • Acquisition integration and purchase price allocation: The Classic Vacations purchase price allocation is provisional; final PPA will be determined post completion of detailed valuations, and final allocation could differ from provisional allocation. Goodwill of INR 4,225.95 Mn was recognised.
  • Subsidiary closures: Multiple subsidiary windings/liquidations are underway, with one subsidiary prepared on non-going concern basis.
  • New Labour Codes: Finalisation of Central and State Rules and clarifications could result in additional accounting impacts.
  • Investor conference schedule: Subject to change due to exigencies.

Mr. Bhaskar Pramanik was re-appointed as Non-Executive Independent Director for a second term from November 24, 2026 to November 23, 2027, subject to member approval.

Broker Narrative

Brokers moved from an unhedged Buy in 2024 based on structural travel-market growth and platform moats to a Hold in 2026 that balanced strong reported growth and market-share gains against macro overhangs and a contracting India H&A segment. Persistent themes included competition/OTA encroachment, travel-demand/macro risks, and expansion into high-growth regions. What changed was the emphasis: early optimism about broad industry tailwinds and aggressive margin forecasts gave way to a focus on organic profitability, AI, and acquisition integration.

Fears that came true

  • External factors curtailing travel materialized as macro uncertainties in the Middle East and a 2% YoY contraction in India H&A GTV.
  • Competition from large OTAs materialized as OTA encroachment and the offline-to-online shift were still cited as downside risks in the last report.

Optimism that failed

  • The India travel buyer market growth tailwind failed to materialize in the domestic H&A segment, whose GTV contracted 2% YoY.
