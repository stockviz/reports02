APAR Industries — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

External disruptions in Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026). Management described the quarter as “fairly challenging from an external perspective,” citing the impact of the U.S.-Iran war, logistics difficulties in export markets, and significant manpower shortages in May that coincided with holidays and local elections.

Conductor volumes declined despite a strong order book. Conductor volume fell 6.7% year-on-year to 53,279 MT (and 18.0% quarter-on-quarter). Management attributed this to a surge in metal prices, particularly aluminium, which led customers without hedged metal to withhold manufacturing clearance. The delays were concentrated in conventional conductors for the domestic market, including TBCB projects, where contracts allow some delivery leeway (18–36 months) but carry stringent penalties for delay.

Oil division volumes affected by Middle East crisis. Oil volumes fell 13.7% year-on-year to 129,085 kL. The UAE facility was restricted to local deliveries of inventory following the closure of Hamriyah port. Lower allocation by oil suppliers in April also affected volumes, though this was restored subsequently. Domestic transformer oil volumes declined 6.2% and global transformer oil volumes declined 8.0%, while auto oil volumes grew 6.6% and industrial lubricant volumes grew 12.1%.

Oil division provision. With ICE gas oil moving sharply upward in Q1 and then falling sharply at the end of the period, a provision of ₹93 crores (approximately ₹94 crores as described on the call) was made in the oil division in line with accounting standards.

Cable exports declined. Cable export revenue fell 13.7% year-on-year, with the export mix at 27.6% versus 41.3% a year earlier. U.S. conductor revenue was down 34.0% year-on-year and 26.2% quarter-on-quarter, which management linked to the lagged effect of a prior quarter when Section 232 classifications caused minimal ordering, and to DDP delivery terms under which revenue is recognised only on customer receipt.

U.S. tariff environment. All bare aluminium products are covered under Section 232 at a 50% duty, applying equally to ingots, rods and conductors. Management noted a change in Section 232 to apply the 50% duty on the value of the entire finished product rather than just the metal content. Section 301 for India appeared to be 10%, subject to final fine print. About 70% of the U.S. conductor market is served by local U.S. production, with imports constituting the remaining 30%.

Metal price volatility. Copper and aluminium prices rose during the quarter. MJP premium cannot be hedged in the market; APAR mitigates this only by locking in premiums with suppliers through mutually agreed contracts. Management noted that when prices fall, contract-based purchases become more expensive than spot, reversing the benefit seen in rising markets.

Publicity restrictions during the fundraise. On the Q1 FY27 call, management stated that because the company had issued a notice seeking shareholder approval for issuance of securities, publicity restrictions prevented it from taking questions on guidance, projections, forecasts, or the proposed funding requirements.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record quarterly performance. Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue reached ₹6,591 crores, up 29.1% year-on-year, with domestic revenue up 36.9% and exports up 12.4%. EBITDA post open-period forex was ₹814 crores, up 62.7% year-on-year, at a 12.4% margin. PAT was ₹467 crores, up 77.7%, at a 7.1% margin. Management described this as the highest quarterly sales and profit in the company’s history.

Conductor order book and new orders. The pending conductor order book stood at ₹10,190 crores, with exports at 56.8%. New orders received in the quarter were ₹5,245 crores, of which exports contributed 65.8%. This included large orders exceeding ₹2,800 crores from two major overseas electric utility companies — one in the U.S. and one in Europe — with delivery spread over the next four years. Management indicated that barring these two orders, a large part of the remaining backlog is executable within about a year.

Premium product mix. Premium products contributed 50.3% of conductor division revenue versus 43.7% a year earlier. EBITDA per MT rose to ₹53,418 from ₹43,688. The quarter saw the highest-ever reconducting installations, and APAR received approval from one of the largest U.S. utilities for 144-count OPGW.

HVDC and high-voltage opportunity. Management noted that a small portion of HVDC-related orders was received in the quarter, with much of India’s ordering still to come. Part of the large U.S. utility order involves a high-voltage transmission line. These were described as multiyear transmission and HVDC system opportunities.

Cable division growth and U.S. data centre access. Cable revenue grew 29.5% to ₹1,838 crores, with domestic revenue up 59.9%. APAR is now approved in the U.S. for cables supplied to Meta, Microsoft and Google data centres by major electrical contractors. The U.S. cable market is 66% copper and 33% aluminium; approvals allow APAR to participate in RFQs for copper cables across low-voltage and medium-voltage segments, PVC, XLPE and rubber varieties. Previously, U.S. cable exports were mostly aluminium. The cable order book stood at ₹1,925 crores versus ₹1,653 crores a year earlier, largely covering the coming quarter’s requirement. New order inflow from the U.S. has started to increase.

Wires and channel business. Wire sales grew 46% in the quarter. Channel business grew 92% year-on-year, distributor presence increased 60%, and active towns selling LDC and other cables through distribution increased 73%.

Oil division profitability. Despite lower volumes, oil division EBITDA grew 214.2% year-on-year to ₹329 crores, with EBITDA per kL at ₹25,482 versus ₹7,004 a year earlier. Management explained this as the effect of historical-cost inventory combined with higher prevailing prices, with contract purchases (about 65% of purchases versus 35% spot) benefiting in a rising price environment. Inventory was deliberately reduced during the quarter to limit exposure if prices reverse.

Capex and capacity. Capacity utilisation ranged between 80% and 90% across product categories. Management noted ongoing capex to build capacity for growth and to debottleneck current operations.

Fundraise completed. On August 13, 2026, the Share Issue Committee approved allotment of 16,88,618 equity shares at ₹14,805 per share (including a premium of ₹14,795), aggregating approximately ₹2,500 crores, to qualified institutional buyers. The issue opened on August 10, 2026 and closed on August 13, 2026. Paid-up equity share capital increased from ₹40,18,37,190 (4,01,83,719 shares) to ₹41,87,23,370 (4,18,72,337 shares). Allottees receiving more than 5% of the offered shares included ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund (10.0002% on a PAN-clubbed basis), HDFC Flexi Cap Fund (10.0001%), SBI Contra Fund (10.0001%), Nomura India Investment Fund Mother Fund (6.0000%), Aditya Birla Sun Life Mutual Fund (6.0000%), Axis Mutual Fund (6.0000%), and Abu Dhabi Investment Authority (5.4000%). The trading window, closed per letter dated August 7, 2026, was to reopen from August 16, 2026.

Other corporate actions (July 24, 2026 board meeting). The board approved allotment of 9,484 equity shares under the ESAR Plan 2024; incorporation of a wholly owned subsidiary in the United Kingdom; and further investment in Apar Industries Latam Ltda, Brazil, of an amount not exceeding BRL 3,000,000. During the quarter, the company infused USD 50,00,000 into Apar USA LLC and ₹10.75 crores into CleanMax Rudra, an associate.

3. Key Risks

  • Metal price volatility and customer hedging behaviour: Sudden increases in aluminium and copper prices led customers to postpone manufacturing clearances, delaying revenue recognition on orders in hand.
  • Unhedgeable MJP premium: Management stated the MJP premium over LME cannot be hedged in the market; mitigation is limited to locking in premiums with suppliers through contracts.
  • Inventory reversal risk in oil: Management explained that when prices fall, contract-based purchases become more expensive than spot, reversing the benefit seen in rising markets. The company reduced inventory during the quarter to limit this exposure.
  • Geopolitical and logistics disruption: The U.S.-Iran war and the resulting closure of Hamriyah port restricted the UAE facility to local deliveries of inventory.
  • U.S. regulatory and tariff exposure: Section 232 applies a 50% duty on all bare aluminium products regardless of stage; Section 301 for India appeared to be 10% subject to final fine print. About 70% of the U.S. conductor market is reserved for local production, limiting the addressable import segment to 30%.
  • Delivery and revenue recognition timing: U.S. deliveries are largely on DDP terms, so revenue is recognised only when product reaches the customer, creating lag even when manufacturing is complete.
  • Project delay penalties: Conductor contracts carry stringent penalties for delayed project execution, constraining how long customers can defer manufacturing clearance.
  • Working capital: Working capital was in the range of 45 to 50 days, described as fairly constant, with improvement in conductor division debtor and inventory days during the quarter despite commodity price increases.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Cable division margin guidance. On the Q1 FY27 call, the CFO stated that the cable division’s 10.6% EBITDA margin was “in line with the guidance that we give, about 10% to 11%.” The observed Q1 FY27 cable EBITDA margin was 10.6%, up 60 basis points year-on-year and flat quarter-on-quarter.

HVDC order flow guidance. Management had indicated in the prior quarter that material ordering from large HVDC projects would gradually flow through FY27. In Q1 FY27, management stated that a very small part pertaining to HVDC was received, that part of the large U.S. utility order involves a high-voltage transmission line, and that most of India’s HVDC ordering is still to come.

U.S. conductor order flow. Management had previously discussed a quarter in which Section 232 classifications caused minimal ordering. In Q1 FY27, U.S. conductor revenue was down 34.0% year-on-year, which management attributed to the corollary of that no-order period spilling into the current period, compounded by DDP revenue recognition lag. Management stated the lower shipments were primarily a matter of timing and that the order book reflects export deliveries to be executed.

Conductor volume decline characterised as temporary. Management stated the manufacturing-clearance delays were temporary because orders are already placed and delivery timelines are bounded by penalty provisions. Observed Q1 FY27 conductor volume was down 6.7% year-on-year, while the order book stood at ₹10,190 crores and new orders of ₹5,245 crores were received in the quarter.

Oil division margin sustainability. When asked whether the oil division’s ₹25,482 per kL EBITDA would normalise, management declined to guide, citing the publicity restrictions, but explained the mechanism: historical-cost inventory combined with higher current prices produced the elevated margin, and accounting-standard provisions of ₹93 crores were made as of June 30, 2026. Management also noted it had reduced inventory during the quarter to limit the impact of a price reversal.

Working capital guidance. Management stated working capital has been fairly constant at 45 to 50 days over the period. Observed Q1 FY27 working capital was in that range, with improvement in conductor division debtor and inventory days.

Capacity utilisation. Management stated utilisation ranges between 80% and 90% across product categories, with capex underway to debottleneck and build capacity for growth.

No forward guidance provided. Throughout the Q1 FY27 call, management repeatedly stated it could not take questions on guidance, projections, forecasts, or the proposed funding requirements due to publicity restrictions associated with the securities issuance notice.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from a power and cable infrastructure thesis in 2023 — centered on conductor margins, freight normalization, interest costs, domestic capex, renewable energy demand, and cable export leadership — to a defense-oriented story by 2026, highlighting import dependence versus the Atmanirbhar Bharat indigenization push, Positive Indigenisation Lists, DAP 2020, and DPM 2025. The export theme persisted but was reframed from cables/wires to defense exports, while domestic power capex and global renewable demand tailwinds disappeared entirely from the narrative.

Fears that came true

  • Conductor EBITDA per ton normalizing downward from the Rs 58,006 peak correlated with a steady decline in actual returns from +558.4% in mid-2023 to +13.9% by August 2026.
  • Rising interest expenses (73% YoY growth to Rs 791 million in Q4 FY23) coincided with persistent return deceleration across subsequent years, indicating ongoing margin pressure.
  • Import dependence on foreign defense equipment and components — the sole headwind in the last report — directly correlates with the DISAPPOINTMENT outcome on 2026-08-19, the only defense-themed report.
  • Freight margin benefit normalization (freight rates stabilizing lower) contributed to the DISAPPOINTMENT outcome on 2024-07-30, marking the first inflection point in the return trajectory.

Optimism that failed

  • Domestic Power & Infrastructure Capex was a primary growth driver in 2023 but disappeared entirely from broker tailwinds by the last report, suggesting it failed to remain a material growth catalyst.
  • Global Renewable & Infrastructure Demand, cited as a key international tailwind in 2023 (USA doubling RE capacity, Australia RE and metro expansions), was completely absent from the final report’s narrative.
