Solex Energy: Recent Announcements and Q1 FY27 Earnings Call

1. Headwinds and Challenges

ALMM-2 (ALCM) policy uncertainty. Until mid-May 2026, the industry broadly expected the ALMM List 2 (ALCM) cell timeline to be extended. On 25 May 2026 it was clarified there would be no extension. In the following weeks, upcoming projects were placed on hold and the market moved into a wait-and-watch mode while developers assessed project timelines and finances. This slowed fresh decision-making and led to customer-driven rescheduling of module deliveries during Q1 FY27. Management characterised these as timing shifts rather than cancellations, and stated it does not currently see any orders at risk.

Seasonal H1 weakness. Management described the business as closely linked to utility-scale solar project execution in India and inherently H2 weighted, with the first half seasonally softer and the second half carrying the bulk of module dispatches, EPC completion and revenue conversion. Q1 FY27 was said to have unfolded along that same seasonal shape. Monsoon conditions were cited as a further factor delaying execution for customers who had received commissioning extensions.

Cost absorption on a smaller revenue base. With softer volumes in a seasonally weak quarter, fixed costs and the full-quarter impact of depreciation and interest on the 4 GW capacity were absorbed over a smaller revenue base. Depreciation and amortisation rose to INR10.2 crore in Q1 FY27 from INR4.3 crore in Q1 FY26, reflecting the full-quarter impact of Lines 3 and 4 (commissioned November 2025). Finance cost rose to INR12.5 crore from INR5.4 crore, reflecting higher working capital deployment and a larger share of working capital met through fund-based limits rather than non-fund-based facilities.

Reduced EPC contribution. Some EPC government revenue booked in Q1 FY26 was absent in Q1 FY27, as Solex has stopped taking government projects or takes them only selectively.

Grid and connectivity constraints. In Gujarat, load curtailment and connectivity challenges were noted, attributed to concentrated solar generation in Western India. Management expects relief once National Green Grid lines from Khavda and other grid lines become operational, and as BESS capacity is added.

Cell project execution risk. Management acknowledged that several Indian players in cell manufacturing face execution and ramp-up problems, and that TOPCon lines are more challenging to operate than Mono PERC. Infrastructure such as water and power connections in Gujarat takes time. The company also noted that most Indian cell capacity is older Mono PERC technology, with few TOPCon players and most TOPCon capacity producing M10R rather than G12/G12R cells; G12R cells were described as currently unavailable domestically.

Export market competition. Chinese competitors were described as aggressive in overseas markets, with strategic pricing and grants (which the Chinese government is now removing) making competition difficult in certain markets. Export revenue is projected to be a low share of FY27.

Inventory build. Inventory grew from approximately INR1,795 million to INR2,905 million in FY26. Management attributed this to seasonality rather than technology obsolescence, citing the latest-generation G12R modules and the consistent H1:H2 pattern of roughly 1:3 in Solex’s trajectory.

Risk disclosure in AGM notice. The AGM notice states that loss or inadequacy of profit cannot be ruled out due to ever-changing domestic and international market conditions.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order inflow and pipeline. Following an order of INR628+ crore received in July 2026, in August 2026 the company secured a INR42.47 crore work order from a domestic private limited company in the power sector for N-type TOPCon 620 Wp glass-to-glass solar PV modules, with execution scheduled to commence October 2026. An LOI for a further INR175 crore order was received, with an MSA at the signing stage. Together these represent an executable order pipeline of INR845.84 crore targeted for execution by 31 December 2026. Order book visibility was stated at approximately INR3,400 crore, spread across confirmed purchase orders with deliveries underway, signed MSAs awaiting formal POs, and MSAs at an advanced stage of discussion. Management stated this visibility is not contingent on the ALMM-2 outcome.

Domestic work orders. Separate disclosures reference domestic work orders for solar PV modules with an aggregate value of INR74.77 crore (inclusive of applicable taxes), scheduled for execution across December 2026, and an LOI for approximately INR174.30 crore.

Dual listing. Solex completed listing on BSE Limited, with existing shares listed on the BSE main board and no fresh capital raised. The company is now listed on both NSE and BSE, supported by over 11,000 shareholders, and expects this to broaden the investor base, improve liquidity and strengthen the shareholder profile.

Capacity and technology position. The 4 GW module manufacturing capacity at Tadkeshwar, Gujarat continues to operate as planned, with the ramp-up of Lines 3 and 4 (commissioned November 2025, full utilisation by March 2026) behind it. Solex was the first Indian manufacturer to launch a G12R TOPCon module. The facility is fully automated and MES-driven with unit-level traceability, and the in-house testing laboratory certifies products against rigorous international climate conditions. R&D partnership with ISC Konstanz, Germany continues on next-generation back-contact and TOPCon+ cell roadmaps; engineering collaboration with TT Vision, Malaysia supports automation and process optimisation.

Cell manufacturing roadmap. The 2.2 GW N-type TOPCon+ cell line, the first phase of a planned 5 GW cell capacity at a single site, is on track for commissioning by end of calendar year 2027. Land is in the closing stage with connectivity approvals expected shortly; a 30 MW electricity connection application has verbal approval with written approval awaited. Debt of approximately INR700 crore is at an advanced stage of discussion with the lender, and equity of INR350 crore structured as NCDs and CCDs is progressing through due diligence. An experienced TOPCon cell manufacturer is being onboarded on a KPI-linked basis for line design, commissioning and operations. Management described cell manufacturing as the platform for the next phase of margin trajectory, with backward integration expected to support a meaningful uplift in profitability once operational and stabilised.

BESS. Evaluation continues with identified technology partners for a containerised BESS manufacturing setup, to be housed in a separate subsidiary. Solex BESS Private Limited was incorporated on 22 June 2026 as a wholly owned subsidiary. Management noted India’s BESS order pipeline of around 50 GW expected to be awarded over the next 24 months, and stated the company will proceed in a calibrated manner with a technology partner.

Vision 2030 and Gujarat MoU. Long-term plans include 10 GW module capacity, 10 GW solar cell capacity, 10 GW BESS infrastructure and 2 GW wafer and ingot capacity, aligned with Atmanirbhar Bharat and Viksit Bharat. An INR4,000 crore MoU with the Government of Gujarat was described as the first concrete step.

Export and OEM opportunities. Focus export markets are the Middle East, Africa and Europe, with efforts to establish a presence in the US. A full-fledged team represents Solex in the European market. The company has previously manufactured for a world-leading brand and is in discussion or at agreement stage with other global brands for Solex-branded modules for their Indian projects, with some closures likely for Q4. Contract manufacturing for global brands will be evaluated for the next financial year if domestic orders do not fully utilise capacity.

Repowering and market growth. Management cited technology shifts and repowering of older projects with TOPCon modules as a continuing source of module demand in India and overseas, alongside growth in the captive, C&I and residential rooftop segments.

CSR initiative. On 13 July 2026, the company approved becoming a member of Solex Foundation, a Section 8 company incorporated 11 March 2026, to advance its CSR objectives. The company has been admitted as a Guarantor Member to the extent of a guarantee not exceeding INR400. The activities do not impact core commercial business lines.

3. Key Risks

  • Policy risk: The ALMM-2/ALCM mandate and the possibility of extension created demand deferrals and wait-and-watch behaviour; management stated there is no impact on the order book under either scenario, with domestic cell supply arrangements for ALCM-compliant orders and secured imported cell supply for other orders.
  • Execution and timing risk: Deliveries were held rather than cancelled, with conversion dependent on post-monsoon execution and customer readiness.
  • Cell project risk: Land closing, written electricity connection approval, financial closure (debt and equity) and technology ramp-up remain outstanding. Management acknowledged the process has taken longer than expected.
  • Customer concentration: The single largest closed order is INR600+ crore, the second highest INR175 crore, and the third INR42 crore. Management characterised the customer profile of large IPPs as a source of strength rather than concentration risk, citing pre-dispatch inspection and repeat business.
  • Competitive risk: Chinese pricing strategies and grants in export markets; potential consolidation among standalone module manufacturers as domestic cell capacity becomes available.
  • Grid and offtake risk: DISCOM approvals and grid stability constraints in Gujarat and parts of the country.
  • Inventory risk: Finished goods build-up, which management attributes to seasonality rather than technology change.
  • Profit variability: The AGM notice states loss or inadequacy of profit cannot be ruled out due to changing market conditions.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

FY27 revenue and margin guidance. Management stated it remains aligned with FY27 revenue guidance and a PAT margin range of 5% to 6%. In Q1 FY27, PAT margin was 3.1% (PAT of INR8.3 crore on total revenue of INR265.6 crore). Management attributed the gap to seasonally softer volumes and the full-quarter impact of depreciation and interest on expanded capacity, and expressed confidence in delivering on FY27 guidance given the H2-weighted execution calendar.

Utilisation guidance. For FY27, management is working with an average utilisation assumption of around 55% across the year, described as conservative relative to FY26 average utilisation of around 70%, reflecting the larger 4 GW base, industry average utilisation of about 50%, and ALMM-related disruption. Management cited production slowdown in two months of the year as a factor.

Order book conversion. Management stated the executable pipeline of INR845.84 crore is targeted for execution by 31 December 2026, and that confirmed POs of roughly INR800–900 crore are to be executed by December 2026. Additional MSAs awaiting firm POs will be announced as they close.

Capex guidance revision. A previously referenced total capex plan of INR1,500 crore (INR1,100 crore cell line, INR200 crore module, INR100 crore working capital, funded by INR1,000 crore debt and INR500 crore QIP) was revised to INR1,050 crore, funded by INR700 crore debt and INR350 crore equity. Management explained the INR200 crore reduction reflects the decision not to add an additional 2.5 GW module line, given expected market availability of module capacity, and that vendor and team work placed the 2.2 GW cell line project cost at around INR1,050 crore. The funding route also shifted from a planned QIP/PREF equity raise to structured debt due to unfavourable market conditions, with due diligence completed and the matter at final evaluation stage with investors and the funding agency.

Dividend. The AGM notice includes a proposal to declare a final dividend of INR0.55 per equity share, with a record/cut-off date of 15 September 2026, book closure from 16 to 21 September 2026, and the 12th AGM scheduled for 22 September 2026. The AGM also includes proposals to revise remuneration for Chairman and Managing Director Dr. Chetan Shah, re-appoint directors, and approve borrowing limits and related party transactions. Management stated it is taking numerous initiatives to improve its financial position and is confident of an increase in revenue and profits in coming years.

Segment reporting. The company operates in a single segment — manufacturer of modules and EPC in the solar energy market — and reports no discontinued operations. The consolidated results include Solex Green Energy Private Limited (76% equity interest, incorporated 3 June 2024), Solex New Energy Private Limited (100%, incorporated 3 July 2025) and Solex BESS Private Limited (100%, incorporated 22 June 2026). For Q1 FY27, Solex Green Energy reported revenue of INR246.14 million and PAT of INR4.66 million; Solex New Energy and Solex BESS reported nil revenue and nil PAT.
