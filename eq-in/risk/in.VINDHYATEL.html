Vindhya Telelinks Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

EPC execution delays and receivables: Project execution in the EPC segment remained impacted by delays in the release of government funds under key infrastructure programmes, particularly the Uttar Pradesh Jal Jeevan Mission (UP-JJM). This resulted in slower revenue recognition and higher working capital deployment. Outstanding receivables under UP-JJM stood at ₹733 crore as on 30 June 2026. Although the Union Budget 2026 allocated ₹67,670 crore to the Jal Jeevan Mission, fund disbursements during the quarter were moderated due to delays in budget allocations and the rollout of revised payment mechanisms, including an online payment portal.

Revenue and profitability decline: Standalone revenue from operations fell to ₹71,577.08 lakhs in Q1 FY2026-27 from ₹90,161.00 lakhs in Q1 FY2025-26. Standalone PBT was ₹3,778.80 lakhs and PAT ₹2,809.97 lakhs, compared with PBT of ₹2,823.83 lakhs and PAT of ₹2,089.91 lakhs in the prior-year quarter. Finance cost rose to ₹4,151.38 lakhs from ₹3,098.87 lakhs, and depreciation increased to ₹718.29 lakhs from ₹514.42 lakhs. Consolidated revenue declined to ₹71,889.93 lakhs from ₹90,751.98 lakhs, while consolidated PBT was ₹10,148.68 lakhs and PAT ₹7,567.17 lakhs, against ₹8,250.48 lakhs and ₹6,169.03 lakhs respectively.

EPC segment contraction: The EPC segment reported revenue of ₹51,706.64 lakhs and EBITDA of ₹4,362.38 lakhs in Q1 FY2026-27, compared with ₹70,409.26 lakhs and ₹4,844.67 lakhs in the corresponding quarter of the previous year.

Governance-related resolution failure: At the 43rd AGM held on 3 August 2026, the Special Resolution proposed at Item No. 5 for the re-appointment of Shri Priya Shankar Dasgupta as a Non-Executive Independent Director for a second term failed to secure the required three-fourths majority under Section 149(10) of the Companies Act, 2013, and could not be deemed passed under Regulation 25(2A) of the Listing Regulations. Votes cast in favour were 5,710,134 (67.96%) against 2,692,449 (32.04%). The company issued a corrigendum clarifying that the resolution was not passed, while noting that Shri Dasgupta’s current term subsists until 20 November 2026, keeping board composition compliant. The Special Resolution at Item No. 6 for the appointment of Shri Pandanda Kariappa Madappa was validly deemed passed under Regulation 25(2A), despite also failing to obtain the requisite majority, because votes cast in favour by public shareholders exceeded those against.

Other risk factors disclosed: The company’s disclaimer cites risks and uncertainties regarding fluctuations in earnings, market growth, intense competition and pricing environment, ability to maintain and manage key customer relationships and supply chain sources, changes in regulatory environments, political instability, currency fluctuations, changes in fibre, copper, aluminium and oil prices and other input costs, and new or changed priorities of the trade.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Optical connectivity super-cycle: The global optical connectivity industry is entering a structural multi-year investment cycle, driven by hyperscale data centres, artificial intelligence, cloud computing, fibre-to-the-home (FTTH), 5G densification and accelerating digital infrastructure investments. The Cable segment continued to deliver operational improvement, with EBITDA rising to ₹4,286.09 lakhs in Q1 FY2026-27 from ₹1,592.45 lakhs in the corresponding quarter of the previous year, supported by a richer product mix and higher contribution from Optical Fibre Cables.

Order book and new segments: The company reported an order book of approximately ₹4,850 crore (excluding O&M orders) as on 30 June 2026. It secured a strategic ₹475 crore order for the design, supply, installation, operation and maintenance of a Smart LED Street Lighting Control & Monitoring System in the State of Andhra Pradesh, strengthening its diversified EPC order book and expanding beyond traditional segments.

Amalgamation with Birla Cable Limited: The Board approved a Scheme of Amalgamation between Birla Cable Limited and the company effective 1 April 2026. Management believes the proposed amalgamation, subject to regulatory approvals, will further strengthen its competitive position.

IP-1 passive optical fibre network assets: The company is evaluating strategic initiatives to enhance the utilisation and long-term value of its IP-1 passive optical fibre network assets, consistent with its long-term growth strategy.

Sector outlook: The company remains optimistic about long-term growth prospects, supported by sustained investments in digital connectivity, power distribution, renewable energy and smart infrastructure.

3. Key Risks

  • Delays in government fund releases under infrastructure programmes, particularly UP-JJM, affecting revenue recognition and working capital.
  • High outstanding receivables (₹733 crore under UP-JJM as on 30 June 2026) and dependence on recovery of dues.
  • Input cost volatility in fibre, copper, aluminium and oil.
  • Currency fluctuations, regulatory changes, political instability and competitive pricing pressure.
  • Execution risk in EPC projects and dependence on key customer relationships and supply chain sources.
  • Regulatory approval risk relating to the proposed amalgamation with Birla Cable Limited.
  • Governance risk highlighted by the failure of the Special Resolution for re-appointment of an Independent Director at the 43rd AGM.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Management stated it expects substantial recovery of outstanding UP-JJM dues during the current calendar year and is actively engaging with concerned authorities. The EPC division continues to focus on accelerating project execution, enhancing operational efficiencies, expanding margins and improving working capital productivity. Management expressed confidence in delivering sustainable, profitable growth, supported by the order book of approximately ₹4,850 crore, continued investments in advanced cable manufacturing capabilities and a disciplined approach to project execution, while maintaining focus on capital efficiency, balance sheet strength and long-term value creation.

Observed performance for Q1 FY2026-27 shows revenue decline at both standalone and consolidated levels, with EPC segment revenue and EBITDA lower year-on-year, reflecting the execution headwinds management described. The Cable segment, however, showed strong EBITDA growth year-on-year, consistent with management’s emphasis on the optical connectivity investment cycle and richer product mix. Standalone PAT grew year-on-year despite lower revenue, while finance cost and depreciation increased. The company also retrospectively restated its consolidated financial statements to include three previously unconsolidated wholly-owned subsidiaries, alongside approval of the Birla Cable amalgamation effective 1 April 2026.
