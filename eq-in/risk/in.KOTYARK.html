KOTYARK Industries: Recent Developments, Risks, and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Regulatory and legal proceedings. During the pendency of a renewal process with the Bio-Fuel Authority, Government of Rajasthan, four storage tanks containing raw material (Veg Ester) at the Swaroopganj unit were seized by authorities, and criminal proceedings were initiated against one of the Directors. The Rajasthan High Court permitted the company to continue operations to fulfill supply commitments to Oil Marketing Companies despite the ongoing proceedings. Management expressed confidence that the seized raw material will be released and utilized in production, and noted that the Rajasthan government has taken initiatives that could allow the registration renewal process to start in the short term. A separate matter involving the biodiesel industry association before the Supreme Court concerns a penalty imposed through a gazette notification by the Revenue Department; that case remains ongoing with no clear timeline.

Auditor transition and delayed results. M/s. Manubhai & Shah LLP expressed unwillingness to seek reappointment as Statutory Auditors for FY 2026-27, citing existing professional commitments and logistical constraints arising from the location of the company’s operations. The Board proposed M/s. Talati & Talati LLP as auditor, subject to member approval at the 10th AGM on August 22, 2026. Because the incoming auditor could only undertake the review after appointment, the company was unable to convene the Board Meeting for approval of Q1 FY27 results by the prescribed due date of August 14, 2026, and stated it would intimate a revised Board Meeting date within a reasonable period after the new auditor’s appointment.

Margin and pricing pressure. Standalone profitability remained under pressure due to the prevailing international price differential between fossil fuels and biofuels. Management described biodiesel as a commodity in which raw material prices move with OMC-offered rates, so margins are largely rate-linked; at current rates, management cited margins of 8–10%, compared with FY26 operating profit of around 15%. Volatility in energy and carbon markets may impact margins, and earnings are also affected by OMC tender cycles and procurement timing.

Demand-side uncertainties. Increasing adoption of EVs and alternative clean-energy solutions may impact demand dynamics. A participant noted that OMC tender quantities have not increased meaningfully year-on-year (around 5 crore liters per month previously versus a 15 crore liter tender for three months), and questioned the pace of biodiesel blending. Management attributed tender volumes to government policy and OMC requirements, expecting gradual increases. Management also addressed discussion of isobutanol blending into diesel, stating isobutanol is 70–75% more expensive than biodiesel and that very few isobutanol plants exist in the country.

Capacity utilization gap. FY26 results were achieved while operating at a relatively low capacity utilization level of around 7–8%, despite the company having expanded biodiesel production capacity at its Rajasthan facility from 500 KLPD to 1500 KLPD, taking cumulative annual biodiesel production capacity to approximately 4,80,000 KL.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Policy and market growth. The National Policy on Biofuels and a government target of 5% biodiesel blending by 2030 underpin expected growth. India’s biodiesel market is projected to reach USD 950 million by 2034 from USD 497 million in 2025 at a 7.45% CAGR, supported by increasing OMC procurement and growing demand for cleaner fuels. The India carbon credit market is estimated at ~USD 4.0 Bn in 2023 and expected to reach ~USD 49.4 Bn by 2030, with an implied CAGR of ~43% (2024–2030). Kotyark became the first Indian biodiesel company to receive Verra carbon certification and generate over 57,000 carbon credits.

Order book and tender wins. On August 5, 2026, the company reported Letters of Intent from BPCL (1,287 KL, ~₹11.93 crore, dated August 3, 2026, received by email August 4, 2026) and HPCL (375 KL, ~₹3.48 crore, dated August 4, 2026, received by email August 5, 2026), aggregating 1,662 KL with a total deliverable value of approximately ₹15.41 crore for supply from June 2026 to August 2026. On August 10, 2026, the company reported receipt of the Award of Contract for Tender ID 2026_MKTHO_190226_1 (Tender Reference No. OMC/EOI/BD/JUN26, Cycle 1) for biodiesel supply, with a total allocation of ₹173.45 crore against a total contract value of ₹1,397.72 crore, comprising allocations from IOCL, BPCL, and HPCL, and inclusive of the earlier ~₹15.41 crore LOIs. Execution is 92 days from the date of Award of Contract, with supply location Gujarat. The company noted that under the current tender cycle, OMCs initially issued LOIs directly and thereafter issued a consolidated allocation sheet. The company also reported an existing OMC order book of approximately ₹173.45 crore and approximately ₹60 crore from other parties, and an extension of the OMC tender by another two months.

Capacity expansion. The company is setting up two new biodiesel manufacturing facilities at Jhajjar, Haryana and Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh, each with 200 KLPD capacity, expected to be commissioned by December 2026, adding 400 KLPD and strengthening its presence across North India. The projects are being funded through internal accruals. Management stated the addition would increase capacity by around 75%.

Diversification and new channels. The company is pursuing exports, industrial sales, and FMCG products. Management stated biodiesel exports are currently banned but that the company is obtaining a license (expected in around four to six months), would need to convert one of its units into an EOU, and is also pursuing ISCC certification. Management indicated export revenue could materialize in the current year. Industrial sales growth was attributed to ARAI certifications, and the company sells biodiesel to Modern Insulators and semi-finished biodiesel to E-Diesel Biofuels. Management also noted a new tender allocation policy based on total quantity rather than location, which it expects to be beneficial.

Corporate milestones. The company migrated from the SME Platform to the Main Board on March 12, 2026. It issued bonus equity shares in a 10:1 ratio (10,27,91,160 equity shares of Rs. 10 each), factored retrospectively into EPS calculations. With effect from April 1, 2026, the company disposed of its share and ceased to have an interest in Asia Bio Fuels LLP and Parth Renewable Energy LLP at carrying cost. At the 10th AGM on August 22, 2026, all 12 resolutions were passed with requisite majorities, covering adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements, a final dividend of Re. 5.00 per equity share, appointment/re-appointment of directors and auditors, and special resolutions concerning borrowings, loans, guarantees, and related party transactions.

3. Key Risks

  • Legal and regulatory risk from the seizure of raw material storage tanks at Swaroopganj, criminal proceedings against a Director, and the pending Bio-Fuel Authority renewal process.
  • Risk from the industry association’s ongoing Supreme Court matter regarding a Revenue Department penalty, with uncertain timelines.
  • Commodity and margin risk: biodiesel is a commodity, raw material prices track OMC rates, and margins are exposed to international fossil fuel-versus-biofuel price differentials and energy/carbon market volatility.
  • Demand risk from EV adoption and alternative clean-energy solutions, and from the pace of OMC tender volumes and blending mandates.
  • Execution and utilization risk: capacity has been expanded substantially while utilization remains around 7–8%, and new Jhajjar and Kanpur facilities are yet to be commissioned.
  • Compliance and reporting risk highlighted by the delayed Q1 FY27 results and the statutory auditor transition.
  • Export plans depend on obtaining a license, EOU conversion, and ISCC certification, with timing outside the company’s sole control.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Revenue growth. Management stated an aspiration of 25–30% revenue CAGR and 18–22% EBITDA margins over the next three years, and separately indicated growth of around 60–80% over the next four to five years. In the earnings call, management guided to 15–20% growth (possibly up to 30%) for the year, with revenue and capacity utilization moving broadly in line. Observed: FY26 revenue was ₹314.9 crore with EBITDA of ₹48 crore and PAT of ₹19.4 crore. For Q1 FY27, consolidated revenue was ₹91.98 crore (up 11.54% YoY), PBT up 30.6% YoY to ₹7.47 crore, consolidated PAT ₹4.46 crore, standalone total income ₹84.92 crore, and standalone PAT ₹1.06 crore. Q1 FY27 revenue growth of 11.54% YoY is below the stated 25–30% CAGR aspiration and below the 15–20% annual growth indication.

Margins. Management guided to 18–22% EBITDA margins over three years and stated that margins would improve as utilization rises. In the earnings call, management cited current margins of 8–10% and noted FY26 operating profit was around 15%. Observed: FY26 EBITDA of ₹48 crore on revenue of ₹314.9 crore implies an EBITDA margin of roughly 15%, within the lower part of the long-term range but above the 8–10% cited for current rates. Q1 FY27 consolidated PBT growth of 30.6% YoY outpaced revenue growth of 11.54% YoY, consistent with some margin improvement, though standalone PAT of ₹1.06 crore against consolidated PAT of ₹4.46 crore indicates a gap between standalone and consolidated profitability.

Capacity utilization. Management targets increasing utilization from ~7–8% to ~60–70% over the medium term, and stated utilization would increase from the current quarter onwards. Observed: FY26 was completed at ~7–8% utilization; Q1 FY27 results do not disclose a utilization figure, so the guided ramp-up is not yet evidenced in the supplied material.

Order book execution. Management anticipated execution of the ₹173.45 crore OMC order book across current and upcoming quarters, and expects domestic biodiesel consumption to remain stable over the next 12 months. Observed: the August 10, 2026 award specifies execution within 92 days from the date of Award of Contract, and the underlying supply period is June 2026 to August 2026, providing a defined near-term execution window. The company also stated that supply is subject to receipt of requisite Indents/Supply Orders from the respective OMCs within the stipulated time period.

Exports. Management indicated export revenue could come in the current year, while also stating the license would take around four to six months and ISCC certification is in process. Observed: no export revenue has been reported in the supplied material, and the company noted biodiesel exports are currently banned pending the license.

Debt reduction. Management stated it is reducing debt and taking the company toward a zero-debt position, and that 100% of promoter group share sales came into Kotyark Industries rather than to promoters personally. Observed: the supplied material does not include balance sheet figures to verify the debt trajectory.
