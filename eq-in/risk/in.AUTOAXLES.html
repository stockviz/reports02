AUTOAXLES: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Market moderation and volume decline expectations. Management entered FY27 expecting the M&HCV industry to decline 15–20% versus FY26, which had been a peak year returning to FY19 levels. The revised forecast now points to a 5–10% decline, with a best case of matching last year’s volumes. Q1 FY27 saw a sequential dip of roughly 30% from Q4 FY26, though Q1 still ended as one of the highest in several years at almost 110,000 M&HCV vehicles.

Cost pressures absorbed in the quarter. Other expenses included cost headwinds from LPG availability and increases in tooling and consumables. These costs were absorbed in Q1, with negotiations ongoing to offset future impacts. Employee benefit expenses declined sequentially partly because workforce deployed in Q4 (when volumes were significant) was pulled back, and because the Q4 long-term settlement impact moderated.

Monsoon and geopolitical uncertainty. Management flagged the monsoon and geopolitical tensions as the two key watch items. A delayed monsoon is currently helping the industry through continued freight movement and replacement demand, but the monsoon’s trajectory remains uncertain. Geopolitical cost escalations could affect the rest of the year.

Product mix and segmental shifts. The shift from 8x2 to 4x2 tractor trailers and higher-horsepower vehicles affects product mix. Bus segment demand is seasonal, and the company’s bus axle launch has been delayed while it reworks the product spec to address upcoming pass-by noise regulations, requiring additional design iteration.

Regulatory transition ahead. Regulations anticipated after 2030 include AEBS norms, Type-II Endurance Braking, BS-VII, more stringent pass-by noise norms, and HPFE Phase-1B. Management sees minimum impact on current product lines from the near-term changes but is designing upcoming products to cover future noise requirements.

Aftermarket revenue risk from improved reliability. As new products enter the market with higher reliability requirements, parts that were historically replenished within one year or 18 months may extend to three or six years, which management expects to affect aftermarket demand beginning roughly five years out.

Competitive landscape. American Axle’s Indian business has been acquired by Bharat Forge. Management stated American Axles and Automotive Axles are competitors and declined to comment on competitor strategy, noting the overlap today is minimal and that both companies have operated in the same segment for years.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue from operations was Rs. 5,168 million with other income of Rs. 103 million. EBITDA was Rs. 702 million at 13.6%, versus 12.4% in Q4 FY26 and 9.7% in Q1 FY26 — described as one of the highest EBITDA percentages ever achieved. EBIT margin was close to 12%, roughly 1% better quarter-over-quarter and 2% better year-over-year. Profit was Rs. 455 million (Rs. 452 million net of OCI) at 8.7%, with EPS of Rs. 30 — the highest quarterly EPS in the last four years.

Favourable mix and one-offs. Raw material cost was about 65% for the quarter, helped by a small one-off and favourable product mix. Commodity increases (steel, casting, forging) are passed through back-to-back with customers.

Replacement cycle demand. Vehicles sold in 2017–2018 are entering their seven-to-nine-year service period, which management expects to drive a significant volume shift. Customers upgrading from two 6x2s to a 10x2 or 4x2 tractor trailer support the higher-horsepower trend.

Capacity expansion. Phase-1 and Phase-1(a) CAPEX of approximately Rs. 120 crore is underway, with nearly 40% of the investment already done and some production started. Management expects roughly 25–30% capacity improvement, positioning the company for a stronger Q4 and potential export opportunities.

Exports above normal range. Exports were about 13% of the pie for the quarter, above the traditional 8–12% range, though management expects to remain within 8–12% for the full year. Exports go to CDBS global entities, and India’s potential role in the Cummins Drivetrain Systems global supply chain is being evaluated more deliberately.

New products and adjacencies. A new 160 tandem axle is in pilot batch and moving into production with a large customer. The company is pursuing defense (currently 5–10% of revenue depending on tender awards), the bus axle segment, and aftermarket expansion. Meritor provides new product licensing, India-specific application testing, and customer application support.

Regulatory readiness. Management sees minimum impact on product lines from recent BS-VI, OBD-I, and OBD-II changes, and notes products already proven in Western markets can be adapted for India.

Key Risks

  • Demand risk: Industry volumes could decline 5–10% or more depending on monsoon and geopolitical developments; Q4 is expected to be the peak quarter.
  • Product mix risk: Segmental and seasonal shifts (buses, tractor trailers, higher horsepower) affect revenue conversion independent of market share.
  • Regulatory risk: Future noise, braking, and emission norms require design iteration and could delay product launches.
  • Competitive risk: American Axle’s presence in larger truck axles and its acquisition by Bharat Forge introduce competitive uncertainty over a two-to-three-year horizon.
  • Supply chain and logistics risk: Sea freight rates are unpredictable; exports are largely Ex-Works, with customers providing support.
  • Aftermarket risk: Improved product reliability may extend replacement cycles and reduce aftermarket volumes over time.
  • Labor code impact: The Labour Codes notified on November 21, 2025, increased gratuity and leave liabilities by Rs. 119.87 million, presented as an exceptional item in FY26. The company continues to monitor further impacts.

Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Margin guidance. Management indicated a steady-state PAT margin band of 7.5–8.5% for coming quarters. Q1 FY27 PAT margin was 8.7%, above that band, helped by mix and one-offs. Management stated it will work within the guided range and expects margins to move up the ladder toward 2030 aspirations as capacity and scale benefits come through.

Volume guidance. Initial FY27 outlook was a 15–20% industry decline; this was revised to 5–10%, with a best case of matching FY26’s 480,000 units (implying north of 450,000). The qualitative trajectory: reasonable Q2, increasing demand in Q3 (subject to monsoon), and Q4 ending at a peak.

Capacity guidance. The Rs. 120 crore Phase-1 and Phase-1(a) CAPEX is expected to deliver 25–30% capacity improvement, with 40% already invested and some production started. Management declined to split the CAPEX between growth capacity and equipment replacement, stating it is putting up enough capacity for the next two-to-three years of market growth plus peak volumes and potential exports.

Export guidance. Exports are expected to remain in the 8–12% range for the full year despite the 13% Q1 level, with upside if opportunities arise.

Market share commentary. Management attributed lower sales growth versus CV production to product mix rather than market share loss, stating market share with Ashok Leyland has been maintained and that the company targets single-source status for new products while acknowledging OEMs’ dual-sourcing strategies.

Dividend. Shareholders approved a final dividend of Rs. 32 per equity share of Rs. 10 each (320%) for FY 2025-26 at the 45th AGM held on August 12, 2026, with a record date of August 5, 2026. The resolution passed with 99.99% of valid votes in favour.

Broker Narrative

Initially the broker was cautious with a Hold, citing high base, inflation/rates, and full valuation, and then downgraded to Sell as the CV slowdown set in. Over the next reports it pivoted to Buy, arguing the weakness was temporary and that exports, aftermarket sales, new axle products, and cost control would drive a FY26 recovery. Persistent themes were MHCV volume weakness and macro headwinds; what changed was the rating—from valuation-limited Hold/Sell to Buy-on-recovery—and the margin outlook shifted from stable near-term to a longer-term 10-12% target.

Fears that came true

  • The high-base and inflation/rate-driven MHCV slowdown materialized: Q2FY25 MHCV truck production fell ~13% YoY and revenue declined ~15%, and the stock returned -17.2% after the first Hold.
  • The view that CV volume growth would only moderate proved too optimistic: the decline deepened into FY25, and the subsequent Buy calls in May and August 2024 still produced negative returns of -4.1% and -2.4%.
  • The full-valuation/limited-upside concern was correct in hindsight: the first report’s predicted +2.1% became an actual -17.2% disappointment.

Optimism that failed

  • The confidence that the company would outpace industry growth through exports, domestic share gains, and new products failed to materialize in near-term results: domestic MHCV volumes fell sharply and Q2FY25 revenue/EBITDA declined.
  • The expectation of stable EBITDA margins aided by commodity softening and cost control failed: Q2FY25 EBITDA fell 23% YoY with margin pressure from negative operating leverage and higher employee/fixed costs.
  • The forecast of healthy EBITDA/PAT growth and a longish CV upcycle did not show up in stock performance: the Buy ratings in May and August 2024 both yielded negative actual returns.
