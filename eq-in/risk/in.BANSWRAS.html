Banswara Syntex (BANSWRAS): Q1FY27 Earnings Call and 50th AGM Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Seasonal softness in Q1. Management described the first quarter as seasonally the weakest, with the sequential performance reflecting this pattern alongside temporary operational headwinds.

Labour availability in spinning/yarn. Labour shortages during the festival and marriage season constrained production in the yarn division. Yarn revenue fell to INR96 crores in Q1FY27 from INR110 crores a year earlier, with sales volume of 36 lakh kgs and capacity utilisation of 70%. Management stated these challenges have now largely eased and operations have stabilised.

Garment dispatch deferrals. The West Asia crisis led to temporary export logistics constraints, with customer pickups and shipping delayed. This deferred certain dispatches and reduced garment revenue to INR69 crores in Q1FY27 from INR75 crores a year earlier, with volume of 8 lakh pieces and capacity utilisation of 69%. Deferred dispatches are expected to be executed in the subsequent quarter.

Soft autumn/winter export demand in garments. Management noted the first quarter is the key window for autumn/winter export shipments, and that season was weak for export demand; domestic festive-season shipments typically pick up only from July onwards.

Softer demand in some geographies. Fabric demand was healthy in the United States and domestically, while Europe and the Middle East witnessed relatively softer demand.

Jacket volumes below run-rate. Q1 jacket demand was relatively soft, with about 135,000 jackets closed in the quarter against an expected run-rate of upwards of 75,000 per month thereafter.

Surat facility still non-operational. The Surat facility remains unavailable. GIDC approvals and a no-objection certificate with a recommendation for de-notification have been obtained. The company is now pursuing a no-dues certificate from customs to pay duties and bring the facility into the domestic tariff area, followed by debonding with SEZ authorities. Management expects the remaining steps within three months, and definitely within four to five months, hoping for DTA availability by November or December and operational start around April 2027. Restarting Surat would involve fresh investment in a modern plant with new machinery, with potential for an additional INR200 crores of business at an approximate investment of about INR50 crores.

Investor concerns on long-term growth and returns. A participant observed that revenue is around INR1,350 crores, below the FY23 peak of about INR1,500 crores, implying roughly 1% annual growth over a decade; that roughly INR500 crores of capex since FY21 is almost equal to market capitalisation; that debt has climbed back to almost INR500 crores; and that FY26 operating margin was about 9% to 9.5%, below the 11%–12% earned before the capex and well below the 14% FY23 peak. Management attributed the earlier setback to the dissolution of the French joint venture, which cost sales and required rebuilding, and pointed to a better product mix and a stronger company today without a joint-venture partner.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

India–U.K. FTA in effect. The India–U.K. Free Trade Agreement came into effect on 15 July 2026, eliminating import duties on Indian textiles and apparel entering the U.K. Management expects this to improve competitiveness, support export growth, and open a new phase, with benefits beginning to accrue from the coming quarters. Banswara Syntex cites long-standing U.K. relationships, a strong presence in value-added and MMF products for the U.K. market, and integrated manufacturing capabilities.

Potential India–EU FTA. Management believes a similar agreement with the European Union could further strengthen India’s and Banswara’s position as a preferred sourcing destination and create additional long-term growth opportunities. On garments, EU operationalisation is hoped for by end-December or early January.

Fabric division momentum. Fabric revenue was INR147 crores, up 25% year-on-year, with volumes up 18% to 59 lakh metres and capacity utilisation of 80%. Growth was driven by value-added fabrics including bi-stretch in poly viscose and poly-rich blends and wool blends. The order book remains strong, with fabric orders visible to about end-November. New core articles were added with Haggar and with NEXT in the U.K., alongside deepened engagement with existing customers.

Garment order book and customer additions. The garment order book is fully booked through November and into December. Management expects roughly INR100 crores per quarter for the next three quarters, taking the garment division to about INR375–380 crores plus export incentives, and close to about INR400 crores for the year. Business increased with Mango, Celio and Walmart; C&A and NEXT were added as new international brands. Mango and Celio together represent close to about INR100 crores of revenue.

Export mix shift in garments. Garments moved from roughly 80% domestic and 20% export six to eight years ago to about 60% export and 40% domestic, with management suggesting it could inch towards 70% export by year-end. Management linked exports to better earnings, targeting 10%–12% EBITDA in garments versus low single-digit EBITDA in a domestic-only scenario.

U.K. exposure and growth plans. Fabric exposure to the U.K. is about INR70–80 crores annually; garment U.K. business is about INR20–25 crores, expected to double next year to at least INR50 crores, with a good inquiry pipeline.

Fabric portfolio expansion. The fabric portfolio has moved into womenswear dress materials and specialised shirting, which management expects to benefit from FTA-driven garment capacity growth in India.

Capex and capacity expansion. About INR140 crores of investment is planned and already sanctioned for this year, directed at fabric, garment and common infrastructure. Management indicated a planned expansion of about 20%–25% in garments and about 20% in fabric for FY28, with yarn capacity maintained or marginally grown. The company expects to close this year near INR1,500 crores.

Domestic market. Management described the domestic market as buoyant with good demand.

3. Key Risks

  • Labour availability: Seasonal labour shortages disrupted spinning output and yarn capacity utilisation; recurrence could again affect production and sales.
  • Geopolitical and logistics disruption: The West Asia crisis caused shipping and pickup delays, deferring garment dispatches and revenue.
  • Geographic demand divergence: Softer demand in Europe and the Middle East partially offset strength in the U.S. and domestic markets.
  • Trade agreement execution: Benefits from the India–U.K. FTA and the proposed India–EU FTA depend on implementation and on operationalisation timelines, with EU operationalisation only hoped for by end-December or early January.
  • Surat facility timeline: Restart depends on customs no-dues and SEZ debonding approvals; management’s own expectation is three months, and definitely four to five months, with operational start around April 2027.
  • Capacity utilisation sensitivity: Yarn profitability was achieved even at 70% utilisation, but management noted higher utilisation (around 90%) would be needed for a really good top line and bottom line.
  • Long-term growth and leverage concerns: Investor commentary highlighted flat decade-long revenue, capex roughly equal to market capitalisation, debt near INR500 crores, and margins below pre-capex and FY23 peak levels.
  • Garment competitiveness: Management acknowledged a perceived lack of aggression relative to peers who scaled up in domestic and international markets over the last four to five years.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

FY27 revenue guidance. Management maintained FY27 revenue guidance of INR1,450–1,500 crores, with a stated expectation to close this year close to around INR1,500 crores. Q1FY27 total income grew 4.1% year-on-year to INR322.4 crores. Management attributed the modest top-line growth to labour availability in spinning and held-back garment dispatches, and expressed confidence in covering ground in Q2 and Q3, with the last quarter being the most important.

FY27 EBITDA margin guidance. Management projected 12% EBITDA on INR1,500 crores and maintained this guidance. Q1FY27 EBITDA was INR29.5 crores, which management described as 9% for the quarter, expecting to recover the remainder over the next three quarters to average 12%. Segment margin expectations were stated as 12%–14% EBITDA in fabric, 8%–10% in garments, and 8%–10% in yarn. In Q1, fabric was in line with target; yarn missed due to capacity utilisation; garments missed due to goods not dispatched in time because of container availability, creating a backlog.

Profitability improvement. Q1FY27 profit after tax was INR4.4 crores versus a loss of INR1.4 crores in Q1FY26. Profit before depreciation and tax was INR19.6 crores. Management attributed the year-on-year improvement to better realisations and a higher contribution from value-added products.

Yarn division. Q1FY27 revenue was INR96 crores versus INR110 crores a year earlier, with 36 lakh kgs volume and 70% utilisation. Management expects the division to normalise progressively over coming quarters and return to normal operating levels as labour availability improves. Higher internal yarn consumption to support downstream fabric and garments was described as a conscious strategic decision under the vertically integrated model, creating greater value across the chain.

Fabric division. Q1FY27 revenue was INR147 crores, up 25% year-on-year, with volumes up 18% to 59 lakh metres and 80% utilisation. Management described this as a strong start to FY27 and expects to continue growing fabric at about 20% for the next two to three years with existing investment.

Garment division. Q1FY27 revenue was INR69 crores versus INR75 crores a year earlier, with 8 lakh pieces and 69% utilisation. Management expects about INR100 crores per quarter for the next three quarters, about INR375–380 crores for the year plus export incentives, and close to about INR400 crores. July was described as on target at about INR30 crores, with the quarter expected to reach about INR100 crores. Jacket volumes are expected to close between 800,000 and 900,000 this year, averaging 70,000–75,000 per month, against a typical average of about 600,000 a year or about 50,000 a month, and Q1 closed at about 135,000 jackets.

Export share. Overall exports remain at 48%. Fabric and garments contribute around 70% of exports, with yarn contributing 30%. Within garments, about 60% is export and 40% domestic; fabric is about 50-50.

Capex and expansion guidance. About INR140 crores of investment is planned and sanctioned for this year in fabric, garment and common infrastructure. Management referenced roughly INR350 crores of investment over the last three years, with about INR150 crores in fabric and about INR130 crores in yarn, and stated that modernisation and maintenance work on the older mill is complete, so future investment should leverage into top line and bottom line. For FY28, planned expansion is about 20%–25% in garments and about 20% in fabric, with yarn maintained or marginally grown.

Surat guidance. Management expects the remaining customs and SEZ steps within three months, and definitely within four to five months, hoping for DTA availability by November or December and operational start around April 2027, with the main result in FY28. Restart would involve fresh investment in a modern plant with new machinery, offering an additional INR200 crores of business for about INR50 crores of investment.

Governance. The 50th Annual General Meeting was held on 30 July 2026 via video conference, with all eight resolutions passed, including adoption of audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY26, declaration of final dividend for FY26, re-appointment of Mr. Ravindrakumar Toshniwal as Vice Chairman and Whole-time Director, and re-appointment of Mr. Rakesh Mehra as Chairman and Whole-time Director.
