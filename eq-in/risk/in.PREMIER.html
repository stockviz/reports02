Headwinds and Challenges

Premier Limited has been under Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) since the NCLT, Mumbai Bench admitted the petition on 29 January 2021. The powers of the Board of Directors remain suspended and are vested in the Resolution Professional (RP), Ms. Kanak Jani, who manages the company’s affairs, business, and assets.

Manufacturing operations at the Chakan plant have remained suspended since 3 March 2020 due to a lack of working capital. The company has reported no revenue from operations in the quarters ended 30 June 2026, 31 March 2026, and 30 June 2025, as well as for the year ended 31 March 2026. Total income during these periods has consisted solely of other income.

The statutory auditors, Jayesh Dadia & Associates LLP, issued a qualified limited review report for the quarter ended 30 June 2026. The net worth of the company has been completely eroded, and the company incurred a further loss of Rs. 176 lakhs (excluding other comprehensive income) for the quarter ended 30 June 2026. The auditors noted that these conditions indicate a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the entity’s ability to continue as a going concern, despite the financial results being prepared on a going concern basis.

The auditors also reported that the company has not assessed impairment of carrying value of tangible assets, capital work in progress, and intangible assets in accordance with Indian Accounting Standard 36. They were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about the recoverable amount of these assets. Additionally, the auditors were not privy to the terms and conditions of the CoC-approved resolution plan, as it has not been made available to them, and therefore could not comment on whether provisions are required for impairment in the value of assets or cessation of liabilities.

The auditors noted that the amount of claims admitted by the RP may differ from the amounts reflected in the financial results as on 30 June 2026, and pending the final outcome of the CIRP, no adjustments have been made for any differential amount. The consequential impact, if any, is currently not ascertainable.

Employee dues, including retirement and termination benefits, were calculated based on available data and only until the initiation of the CIRP. Actuarial valuation for gratuity and privileged leave was conducted only until the nine months ending 31 December 2020, being the quarter immediately preceding the initiation of CIRP.

The auditors were unable to comment on whether all expenses incurred during the year were approved by the CoC, as the relevant records, such as minutes of CoC meetings approving expenses and payments, were not provided to them.

The company has not appointed a whole-time Company Secretary for a period exceeding six months since the resignation of the previous Company Secretary, as required under Section 203 of the Companies Act, 2013. The company has also not appointed an internal auditor as required under Section 138 of the Companies Act, 2013 during the period under review.

There has been a delay in transferring unclaimed matured fixed deposits and interest thereon amounting to Rs. 46.55 lakhs to the Investor Education & Protection Fund (IEPF) in compliance with Section 125 of the Companies Act, 2013, which was due for transfer as at 30 June 2026. The company stated it is in the process of transferring the said amount to the IEPF, and the entire amount is already funded and maintained in designated bank accounts.

In the consolidated results, the associate company, PAL Credit & Capital Limited, has not filed its annual returns with the Registrar of Companies for a period of more than five years and has not been operational for a substantial period of time. The management was unable to provide the accounts of the associate for the quarter and year ended 30 June 2026. The net worth of the associate company has been eroded, and there being no carrying value of the said investment as at the quarter end, the company has not recognized its share of any further losses of the associate during the quarter ended 30 June 2026.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

The Committee of Creditors (CoC), in its 17th meeting held on 22 January 2022, discussed the resolution plans submitted by seven prospective resolution applicants (PRA) and approved the resolution plan submitted by Fab Metals Pvt. Ltd. with 92.47% assent through e-voting. This was disclosed to the stock exchanges on 26 January 2022. The further approval from the Hon’ble NCLT, Mumbai Bench is in progress.

The company is being managed as a going concern during the CIRP. The CIRP period shall continue until the resolution plan accepted by the CoC is approved by the Hon’ble NCLT, Mumbai Bench. The future prospects of the company would be determined on the completion of CIRP.

The 80th Annual General Meeting (AGM) of Premier Limited (In CIRP) is scheduled for Wednesday, 30 September 2026 at 12:30 P.M. IST through Video Conferencing or Other Audio-Visual Means. The remote e-voting period begins on 26 September 2026 at 9:00 a.m. and ends on 29 September 2026 at 5:00 p.m., with the cut-off date for voting set as 23 September 2026.

Key Risks

The material uncertainty regarding the company’s ability to continue as a going concern, as noted by the auditors, represents a key risk. The complete erosion of net worth and continuing losses further compound this risk.

The non-assessment of impairment of tangible assets, capital work in progress, and intangible assets in accordance with Ind AS 36 means the carrying values of these assets may not reflect their recoverable amounts, and any adjustments could materially impact the financial statements.

The difference between claims admitted by the RP and amounts reflected in the financial results, pending final outcome of the CIRP, represents an unascertainable risk. No adjustments have been made for any differential amount.

The non-appointment of a whole-time Company Secretary and an internal auditor represents compliance risks under the Companies Act, 2013.

The delay in transferring unclaimed matured fixed deposits and interest thereon to the IEPF represents a compliance risk under Section 125 of the Companies Act, 2013.

The inability to obtain accounts of the associate company, PAL Credit & Capital Limited, and the non-recognition of further losses of the associate, represents a risk relating to the consolidated financial statements.

The suspension of manufacturing operations at the Chakan plant since 3 March 2020 due to lack of working capital continues to be a fundamental operational risk.

However, the transfer was due as at 30 June 2026 and has not yet been completed.

The financial results show no revenue from operations for the quarters ended 30 June 2026, 31 March 2026, and 30 June 2025, as well as for the year ended 31 March 2026, consistent with the continued suspension of operations.
