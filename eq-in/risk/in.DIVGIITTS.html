Divgi TorqTransfer Systems — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • EV transmission timing delays. EV revenues remained subdued in Q1 FY27 due to timing delays in platform adoption, parts co-location and customer testing. The EV business was also impacted in Q1 by higher inventory at the OEM in Q4, product mix change at the OEM, and a delay in the Sigma program launch.
  • EV commercialisation still pending. Sigma production is expected to commence only in Q2 FY27, following customer approval, and ongoing localisation and validation programmes are still needed to support the transition to commercial-scale EV transmission business.
  • Hybrid investment uncertainty. OEMs are assessing upcoming policy, regulations and customer needs for hybrids before deciding on investment for mass production, which affects the company’s hybrid transmission plans.
  • Automatic and manual transmission programmes at early stages. Automatic Transmission is still progressing in the Proof-of-Concept stage, with a commercial quote submitted to a leading Indian OEM and a feasibility exercise for localisation of a globally proven Automatic Transmission. Manual Transmission work is at proposal stage (5-speed MT for commercial truck application) and evaluation stage.
  • Climate and regulatory risk. The company identifies greenhouse gas emissions from manufacturing as a risk factor, noting that manufacturing processes require substantial energy for machinery, heating, cooling and lighting. Non-compliance with regulations can lead to fines, legal penalties and litigation impacting financial health and reputation.
  • Governance and organisational changes. Mr. Aniket Arun Kokane resigned as Company Secretary and Compliance Officer effective September 19, 2026, with Ms. Ishita Mangesh Kokare appointed as Compliance Officer effective September 20, 2026. The Investors/Analysts Meetings scheduled for September 09, 2026, from 10:00 am onwards were cancelled due to exigencies.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record quarterly performance. Q1 FY27 delivered the highest-ever quarterly performance, with total income of ~Rs. 142 crore, surpassing all historical milestones, led by strong transfer case volumes alongside healthy traction in components and exports.
  • Transfer Case growth led by Indonesia. Transfer Case remained the primary growth driver, supported by the ramp-up of the Indonesia 4x4 program. The company secured an exclusive order as sole supplier from a key OEM customer for 4x4 transfer cases for the Indonesian Pik-Up program, with production expected from CY26. Incremental Indonesia volumes are expected to continue for the upcoming quarters of FY27.
  • New AWD order (June 19, 2026). Divgi TTS secured a strategic order from an existing leading Indian OEM to supply Interactive Torque Management (ITM) Assemblies, Electronic Control Units (ECU) and Power Transfer Unit (PTU) Assemblies for the OEM’s upcoming AWD variants of mid-sized and full-sized SUVs. The program entails annual supply of approximately 6,000 units for five years, with Start of Production expected from Q1 FY28. This marks the OEM’s first FWD-based AWD variant development for its internal combustion powertrain models.
  • Exports as a key growth engine. Exports remained strong at ~Rs. 23 crore in Q1 FY27, with FY27 component exports targeted at ~Rs. 80 crore. The company received final production approval on all export parts with additional revenue potential of ~Rs. 10 to 12 crores per month. Export growth is expected to accelerate through entry into newer geographies and increased contribution from the components business.
  • US expansion (Project Mayflower). A wholly owned subsidiary, Divgi Transmission Technologies and Systems Inc, was established in South Carolina, with initial headcount on-boarded and encouraging feedback from existing customers. New RFQs were released by existing customers for potential sourcing from India and requiring manufacturing in the U.S.
  • H2 FY27 momentum. H2 FY27 is expected to sustain revenue momentum driven by new platforms and facelift programs with key OEM customers, with a ramp-up in domestic end market volumes.
  • Strategic partnerships. Partnerships with M&M, TATA, Force Motors, BorgWarner, MAGNA and Toyota Tsusho are positioned as growth catalysts, providing access to global drivetrain technology, global OEM platforms, and the Japanese OEM ecosystem.
  • Long-term revenue potential. The company outlines potential annual revenues of Rs. 2,000+ crores across 4-Wheel Drive Systems (~INR 300 crores), Manual Transmission and Synchronizer systems (~INR 300 crores), EV Transmission (~INR 250 crores), Automatic and Hybrid Transmission (~INR 1,000 crores), and Exports (~INR 200 crores).
  • ESG opportunities. Opportunities include sustainable product design and life-cycle management, cybersecurity and data management, employee health and safety, diversity, equity and inclusion, corporate governance and business ethics, regulatory compliance, a sustainable supply chain, employee human rights, and venturing into the EV segment by supplying advanced solutions such as E-gear transmissions.

3. Key Risks

  • EV adoption and programme timing risk. EV revenues are exposed to timing delays in platform adoption, parts co-location and customer testing, and to OEM inventory and product mix changes.
  • Dependence on OEM programmes. Growth is tied to the ramp-up of specific programmes (Indonesia 4x4, Sigma, new AWD order) and to OEM launch schedules, including new and facelift programs in H2 FY27.
  • Hybrid policy and regulation uncertainty. OEM investment decisions on hybrids depend on upcoming policy, regulations and customer needs.
  • Climate and compliance risk. GHG emissions from manufacturing and potential non-compliance with regulations could lead to fines, legal penalties and litigation impacting financial health and reputation.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. The company notes that its market opportunity and business prospects statements are subject to known and unknown risks, uncertainties and assumptions that are difficult to predict, including the performance of the Indian and international economies, industry performance, competition, strategy implementation, growth and expansion, technological changes, and changes in revenue, income or cash flows.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • Q1 FY27 outturn vs. prior guidance. Management described Q1 FY27 as a strong start to the year, reflecting improving business momentum, increasing scale and operating leverage. The company delivered total income of ~Rs. 142 crore, EBITDA of ~Rs. 42 crore with EBITDA margins at ~30%, and PAT of ~Rs. 25 crore. This was led by strong transfer case volumes alongside healthy traction in components and exports.
  • EV guidance vs. observed performance. EV revenues remained subdued in Q1 FY27 due to timing delays in platform adoption, parts co-location and customer testing. Management expects a healthy volume trajectory from Q2 onwards for existing programs and the launch of the new Sigma program in mass production. Approval has been received from the customer for production supplies, with SOP expected to commence from Q2 FY27.
  • Export guidance vs. observed performance. Exports remained strong at ~Rs. 23 crore in Q1 FY27. Management targets FY27 component exports at ~Rs. 80 crore and expects export growth to accelerate through entry into newer geographies and increased contribution from the components business.
  • Transfer Case guidance vs. observed performance. Transfer Case remained the primary growth driver in Q1 FY27, supported by the ramp-up of the Indonesia 4x4 program. Management expects continued momentum from the Indonesia programme, incremental Indonesia volumes in upcoming quarters of FY27, and a ramp-up in domestic end market volumes in H2 FY27 with the launch of new and facelift programs by key OEM customers.
  • US operations guidance vs. observed progress. In Q1 FY27, a wholly owned subsidiary was established in South Carolina, initial headcount was on-boarded, and new RFQs were released by existing customers. For Q2 FY27 and beyond, management plans quote submission and negotiations with existing customers, engagement with new OEMs and Tier 1s to generate RFQs, and continued assessment of the business case for local manufacturing in the US.
  • Long-term outlook. Management expects revenue growth to be supported by strong contract execution, including the Indonesian market opportunity in FY27 with recurring business potential, and H2 FY27 to sustain revenue momentum driven by new platforms and facelift programs with key OEM customers.

Broker Narrative

The narrative swung from an early Not Rated stance that was broadly optimistic on EV transmission growth, transfer-case leadership, and export expansion to a Reduce call after seven consecutive quarters of revenue decline. Persistent themes were export/component diversification and EV/defense opportunities, but the last report shifted focus to EV slowdown, competition, legacy transfer-case weakness, and margin pressure. The broker moved from endorsing growth stories to highlighting de-rating risks, while still acknowledging gross-margin expansion and export approvals.

Fears that came true

  • The early fear of EV transmission competition materialized: E-gear drive/EV transmission revenue fell 20% YoY amid an EV industry slowdown and heightened competition for a key EV customer, with the Feb/Mar 2024 Buy calls ending as DISAPPOINTMENT (-26.6%/-37.7%).
  • The early concern about high dependence on a single product/customer materialized: transfer-case revenue dropped 38.5% YoY due to weak key 4WD customers, and EV revenue was hit at a key EV customer, feeding seven straight quarters of revenue decline.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected EV transmission ramp from Tata Motors and Mahindra orders failed to sustain: E-gear drive/EV transmission revenue dropped 20% YoY in Q3FY25.
  • The expected 4X4 demand uptick from industry expansion and growing preference in India failed to materialize: transfer-case revenue fell 38.5% YoY due to sluggish 4W drive market penetration.
  • The first-mover EV transmission advantage did not protect growth: revenue in the EV segment shrank to ₹40mn amid slowdown and competition.
