MobiKwik FY26: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Deliberate revenue stagnation from the BNPL exit. MobiKwik wound down its short-tenure BNPL product, which had been generating approximately ₹2,500 Cr in quarterly disbursals. Management describes this as “the single most important decision we made all year,” taken as regulatory expectations for lending partners evolved. The consequence was flat headline revenue: ₹1,154 Cr, broadly flat year on year, with Financial Services revenue falling to ₹2,619 Mn from ₹4,028 Mn in FY25.

Take-rate compression in payments. Payments take rate nearly halved over two years, from 83 bps in FY24 to 66 bps in FY25 to 47 bps in FY26. UPI operates in a zero-MDR environment, and revenue is earned only on the non-UPI 60% of GMV.

Reported losses and no dividend. Consolidated loss for FY26 was ₹621 Mn (standalone ₹541.5 Mn), improved from ₹1,215 Mn in FY25 but still a loss. The Board did not recommend a dividend. Average net profit under Section 198 for the three preceding years was negative (₹(650.65) million), so no CSR spend was required or made.

A detected fraud. On September 12, 2025, management detected a fraud in which registered merchants and users, in collusion from limited locations in Haryana, claimed unauthorized settlements of ₹403.59 million. An FIR was lodged September 13, 2025. As at March 31, 2026, ₹276.02 million had been recovered, ₹9.26 million remained secured through merchant affidavits and court orders, and the remaining exposure of ₹118.31 million was given appropriate accounting treatment. The Secretarial Audit Report attributes the incident to a coding error in the payments backend that marked failed UPI QR transactions as successful in the merchant system. No employee or officer was involved.

An insolvency petition. M/s. Fusion CX Private Limited has filed a Section 9 IBC petition before NCLT Chandigarh alleging non-payment of invoices amounting to ₹1,61,13,321, arising from the Company’s termination of an agreement with Xplore-Tech on July 29, 2022. Notice has been issued; reply and vakalatnama were to be filed.

Rising fixed costs ahead. FY27 fixed costs are projected to increase 15–20%, driven primarily by manpower and depreciation from merchant device deployment.

Workforce metrics. Overall gender diversity moved only from 22% to 23%; first-line managers from 15% to 13%; leadership roles from 14% to 7%. Voluntary attrition was 39%.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Payments volume and cost re-engineering. Payments GMV reached ₹1,821 Bn, up 57% YoY, with all-time-high GMV in each of 13 consecutive quarters. Customer-initiated UPI transactions grew 170% YoY against industry growth of 26%, achieved at the lowest user incentive cost in the Company’s history — 0.02% of total Payments GMV. Payment gateway costs fell from 48 bps of GMV in FY25 to 29 bps in FY26, reaching 23 bps by Q4; user incentive costs fell from 5 bps to 2 bps. These absorbed the entire take-rate decline, leaving net payments margin at 16 bps, above the 12–15 bps guidance band. Payments gross profit was ₹2,867 Mn, up 90% YoY on flat revenue. Payments gross margin reached 39.1% in Q4 FY26.

Financial Services rebuilt around ZIP EMI. FY26 disbursals were ₹32,380 Mn, up 31% YoY and entirely ZIP EMI. Super-prime borrowers rose from under 10% to 32% of disbursals; near-prime reached 22%; repeat loan customers rose from 20% to 63.5%. Credit risk in maturing portfolios improved ~35% against the peak-stress cohort. Lending-related expenses fell 32% to ₹1,462 Mn. Net financial services margin rose from 0.35% in Q4 FY25 to 5.39% in Q4 FY26, with every quarter of FY26 setting a new high. Financial services gross margin expanded from 4% to 59%. Q4 FY26 gross profit was ₹451 Mn versus ₹24 Mn in Q4 FY25.

Profitability inflection. H2 FY26 delivered ₹324 Mn EBITDA and ₹84 Mn PAT — the first two consecutive profitable quarters. Full-year EBITDA swung ₹742 Mn, from ₹(794) Mn in FY25 to ₹(52) Mn. PAT loss halved from ₹1,215 Mn to ₹621 Mn. Contribution profit rose 21% to ₹4,374 Mn. Total direct expenses fell 14% to ₹7,168 Mn — the first absolute decline in three years — while fixed costs held flat at ₹4,426 Mn against ₹4,410 Mn in FY25.

AI as operating infrastructure. 80% of platform code was AI-generated; 55% of early-stage loan collections were managed by AI agents via KwikCollect; 86% of customer support interactions were self-served; 100% of reconciliation was automated. Lending operational costs fell 95% YoY in Q4 FY26; contribution margin expanded from 23% to 46%; customer satisfaction improved 25%.

Regulatory milestones. The RBI granted in-principle approval for an NBFC licence in April 2026 (the Secretarial Audit Report and Board’s Report reference the approval; the moat section dates it 27 April 2026). The Company also secured a PA/PG licence and a stock broking licence during the year, alongside existing PPI, BBPOU, AMFI and Investment Adviser registrations and a Corporate Agent (Composite) licence. On May 25, 2026, the RBI granted in-principle authorisation to operate as a Payment Aggregator–Physical. Zaakpay received the PA-O licence on April 30, 2025.

Merchant expansion. Merchant presence expanded from 366 cities to 1,118 cities in a single year. Registered users reached 189.6 Mn and merchant partners 4.9 Mn as of March 2026. The Company has committed to a 5x scale-up in offline devices over two years, targeting 10–20% of the market leader’s offline presence, and 10x Zaakpay GMV by FY28.

Balance sheet. The Company enters FY27 with ₹434 Cr in net owned unencumbered cash and no long-term debt. The BlackSoil NCDs (₹500 million aggregate) were redeemed in full on February 28, 2026.

Structural changes post-year-end. The Board on May 22, 2026 approved transfer of the LSP business to wholly owned subsidiary MobiKwik Distribution Services Private Limited by slump sale, approved by shareholders July 2, 2026, expected to be consummated within Q2 FY27. The Board also approved variation in IPO proceeds utilisation and extension of the timeline for unutilised proceeds, approved by shareholders via Postal Ballot on July 2, 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory risk across RBI, SEBI, NPCI, BBPS and Payment Aggregator frameworks, including NBFC regulatory compliance.
  • Credit risk: portfolio quality, FLDG exposure, underwriting standards, collections efficiency and delinquency monitoring.
  • Technology and cybersecurity risk: platform availability, infrastructure resilience, scalability and AI model governance; customer data protection, identity management, access controls, incident response and real-time fraud monitoring.
  • Operational risk: business continuity, vendor dependence, settlement processes and merchant fraud controls — the September 2025 fraud is a realised instance of this category.
  • Financial risk: liquidity management, treasury operations, working capital and capital adequacy planning for the NBFC.
  • Reputational risk: customer trust, service reliability, complaint management, regulatory standing and public disclosure quality.
  • Own-book credit exposure: as MobiKwik transitions to NBFC lending, credit risk moves directly onto its balance sheet, making underwriting quality, early-warning detection and collections “existential rather than incremental.”
  • Execution and capital allocation risk: the FY28 10x revenue ambition depends on four growth engines funded by reinvestment; FY26 consolidated EBITDA was negative ₹5.2 Cr by design after ₹55 Cr of reinvestment from ₹50 Cr of core EBITDA.
  • Dependence on partner banks and NBFCs under the current LSP model, where margins are shared under a framework capped at 5%.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Net payments margin. Guidance band of 12–15 bps. FY26 actual: 16 bps — above the band. Quarterly: 15 bps (Q1), 14 bps (Q2), 17 bps (Q3), 16 bps (Q4).

Long-term lending margin. Guidance of 4.0–4.5%, with management stating long-term modelling should align with this rather than Q4 outperformance. Q4 FY26 actual: 5.4%, which management attributes partly to gains from previously matured cohorts and deferred revenue.

FY27 targets. 30–35% growth in both payments and digital credit GMV; net financial services margin of 4.0–4.5%; EBITDA margin in the 5% range; fixed costs up 15–20%. FY26 actuals for context: payments GMV grew 57%, digital credit disbursals grew 31%, full-year EBITDA margin was negative.

FY28 ambition. 10x revenue; merchant segments at EBITDA breakeven; AI managing the full lending lifecycle; 10x offline merchant revenue; 10x Zaakpay GMV; 5x device expansion. Management states the engines “are already operational, already funded from the core, and already producing measurable results.”

NBFC timeline. Management expects to transfer the existing LSP business to a wholly owned subsidiary, complete NBFC capitalisation, and commence co-lending disbursals by the end of FY27, subject to receipt of the Certificate of Registration.

Profitability commitment. The Company entered FY26 with a mandate to “rebuild margins, recover lending quality, and deliver profitability in the second half.” It delivered two consecutive profitable quarters (Q3: ₹150 Mn EBITDA, ₹40 Mn PAT; Q4: ₹174 Mn EBITDA, ₹44 Mn PAT), with H2 FY26 PAT of ₹84 Mn.

Cost conversion. Management noted in Q3 FY26 that every ₹100 increase in contribution profit translated to a ₹65 improvement in EBITDA — a 65% conversion rate. Observed: contribution profit rose from ₹774 Mn in Q1 to ₹1,351 Mn in Q4, with contribution margin expanding from 27% to 46%.

Audit status. The Statutory Auditor’s report on both standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY26 was issued with an unmodified opinion. The Secretarial Audit Reports for the Company and for material subsidiary Zaakpay contain no qualification, reservation or adverse remark. No fraud was reported by the Statutory Auditor under Section 143(12); the September 2025 incident was management-detected.

Broker Narrative

The narrative began with an aggressive Buy and expectations of broad digital-finance tailwinds: 45%/61%/64% Revenue/EBITDA/PAT CAGRs, wallet market-share gains, and improved financial-services margins. By the last report, the broker still maintained Buy but acknowledged Q1FY26 revenue missed estimates, financial-services revenue fell 65.9% YoY, BNPL/ZIP remained weak, IPO-proceeds deployment lagged, and EBITDA break-even was pushed out. Persistent themes were wallet share gains, payment GMV growth, and eventual QoQ recovery; the changed emphasis was from high growth and margin expansion to a stretched break-even target and cost/gateway optimization.

Fears that came true

  • Intense payments competition materialized as payment-services revenue rose only 0.7% QoQ and the take rate fell from 0.64% to 0.56%.
  • Dependence on lending-partner relationships for credit products was borne out by financial-services revenue collapsing 65.9% YoY.
  • Regulatory pressure on unsecured lending/BNPL materialized as ZIP EMI GMV declined 21% YoY despite a QoQ recovery.
  • The risk of material BNPL moderation due to tighter unsecured-lending norms came true, with BNPL described as remaining under pressure.

Optimism that failed

  • The projected 45%/61%/64% Revenue/EBITDA/PAT CAGRs over FY24-FY27E failed to materialize, as Q1FY26 revenue was down 20.7% YoY with EBITDA and APAT losses of Rs415mn and Rs419mn.
  • The expected ~40% CAGR in digital lending disbursals did not materialize, with financial-services revenue down 65.9% YoY and ZIP EMI GMV down 21% YoY.
  • The anticipated benefit from IPO proceeds earmarked for growth failed to show through, as deployment was slower than expected and the company remained loss-making.
  • The early confidence in financial-services gross margin improvement to 45.7% proved misleading, as the later reported financial-services gross margin was only 13.3%.
