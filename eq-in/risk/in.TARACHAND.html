TARACHAND — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Specialised services margin collapse. The specialised-services EBITDA margin almost halved in Q1 FY27 to approximately 10%, against roughly 18% in prior periods. Management attributes this principally to a client-led change in the scope of a carried-forward project, which left equipment and manpower already mobilised at the client site idle and also incurred demobilisation cost with no offsetting revenue. Of roughly ₹10 crore of specialised-services revenue that spilled over from Q4 into Q1, only about ₹4 crore was executed. The company is in active discussion with the client for a settlement of the resulting losses and expects that to conclude in a subsequent quarter; it has declined to quantify the claim, citing business strategy and an NDA with the client.

Equipment rental margin and utilisation below normal. Standalone equipment rental EBITDA margin was 54% in Q1 FY27, below the usual 58%–62% band, and fleet utilisation dropped to about 79%. Management also cited transitional relocation costs for high-capacity machines moving to new project sites.

Warehousing & Transportation weakness. Segment revenue fell to ₹18.7 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹29.5 crore a year earlier, with 1.63 million tonnes of steel handled. Segment EBITDA margin dropped to 1% from 16%. Two structural factors were cited: the prior-year quarter still carried the RINL Visakhapatnam contract, which concluded on plan at the end of Q3 FY26, and the new SAIL Dankuni stockyard in West Bengal is still scaling toward its intended run-rate. Steel movement was additionally subdued amid elevated fuel costs (attributed to geopolitical reasons) and a decline in steel prices, which reduced movement by the OEMs the company works with. On the consolidated segment reporting, Transportation & Handling recorded a segment loss before tax and interest of ₹(64.29) lakh for the quarter, against a profit of ₹351.55 lakh a year earlier.

Prior-period items. The quarter included approximately ₹2.2 crore of items relating to earlier years — a ₹1.86 crore revenue write-off and a ₹30 lakh exceptional charge for a non-recoverable earnest money deposit on old warehousing and transportation works. These were described as one-time and now behind the company.

Receivable days above target. Receivable days stood at about 97 days against a target of 80. Management said the bulk remains the RINL closure recovery, which is taking longer than expected, and now expects those collections in H2 FY27.

Reported profitability. Revenue from operations grew 11% year-on-year to ₹67.6 crore (about 14% adjusting for the ₹1.86 crore write-off). EBITDA was ₹21 crore at a 30.7% margin, below both the prior-year first quarter (37.4%) and the medium-term 37%–38% band. PAT was ₹1.7 crore, or about ₹3.3 crore excluding prior-period items. Cash profit was ₹17.6 crore, down 6%, against depreciation of ₹16 crore on the fleet built over the prior two years.

Competitive and structural considerations. An investor noted that peers are scaling to 800–900 tonne fleets in wind turbine erection and that one OEM is moving toward captive in-house fleets. Management responded that the OEM’s own equipment covers only about 5%–10% of its activity and is intended to mitigate its dependence on rentals, and that the company works with clients beyond INOX. Management also stated it does not intend to become heavily dependent on the wind sector or any single client or sector, noting equipment is fungible across sectors.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Equipment segment growth and mix shift. Equipment Hiring & Projects revenue grew 55% year-on-year to ₹48.9 crore (from ₹31.5 crore), now about 72% of total revenue, comprising ₹35.6 crore of equipment rentals and ₹13.3 crore of specialised services. Within equipment rental revenue, Renewable Energy rose to 31% and Power to 26% — together 57%, against 24% for FY26. The balance was Cement 18%, Rural & Urban Infrastructure 10%, Metals & Minerals 9% and Petrochemical 6%. Management said the fleet has been pointed “where India is building today” and expects the renewable share to remain at or above 30% for the financial year.

Order book and pipeline. The executable FY27 order book as of July 2026 stood at ₹204.82 crore, of which 74% is equipment rentals and specialised services and 26% warehousing and transportation. Management noted the order book was ₹212 crore at the time of the prior earnings call, so despite roughly ₹68 crore of Q1 revenue, sufficient room remains to ramp up over the next three quarters. A pipeline of close to ₹150 crore was cited, primarily equipment rentals plus a good amount of specialised-services contracts, though how much executes in FY27 depends on client timelines. Roughly ₹50 crore of specialised services is expected within FY27 from the order book.

Capex and fleet. The company deployed ₹42.8 crore of capex in Q1, taking gross block to ₹601 crore. A 3 July 2026 exchange intimation confirmed completion of purchase of two 900MT crawler cranes and one 30m³ cement batching plant, with total Q1 capex of approximately ₹40.59 crore (exclusive of GST, unaudited), deployed predominantly to expand the renewable energy footprint. The full-year capex plan is unchanged at ₹80–100 crore, expected to be mostly complete by November 2026, directed toward higher-capacity cranes. Management said the Q1 capex had not yet been deployed in Q1 revenue and would begin contributing from Q2, with the new capex deployed across Q3 and Q4.

Balance sheet discipline. Net debt to equity improved to 0.87x from 0.92x, inside the company’s 1.0x ceiling, despite the heavy capex quarter. Cash profit held at ₹17.6 crore and operating cash flow was described as strong.

Dankuni ramp. The SAIL Dankuni contract has started delivering the required levels of commitment, with normalisation expected by Q3 once the monsoon passes. Revenue was realised across all three months of Q1, though below anticipation.

Metallix. Tarachand Metallix Limited, the wholly owned subsidiary incorporated 6 January 2026, remains on the drawing board. Management said active discussions with clients continue, a further two to three quarters are needed, and FY28 is the likely timeframe for entering it. The current thinking is that it will be more service-driven rather than manufacturing or material driven. The subsidiary reported nil revenue and a loss after tax of ₹2.13 lakh for the quarter, with total assets of ₹342.07 lakh.

3. Key Risks

  • Client-driven scope changes and project spillovers. The specialised-services margin hit came from a change in project scope outside the company’s control. Management acknowledged that because these projects are specialised and depend on client-level site execution, quarter-to-quarter spillovers are bound to happen, though it does not expect a repeat of the scope-change event. Mitigation cited: phased mobilisation so equipment and manpower are not left idle, and stricter change-of-scope terms in future contracts. Existing contracts already cap the percentage of scope that can change, beyond which terms are renegotiable.
  • Settlement uncertainty. Recovery of the losses from the affected project depends on concluding discussions with the client. Management expressed confidence based on existing contract terms but could not specify the extent or timing, other than that it relates to the current financial year.
  • Segment concentration and transition risk. Warehousing & Transportation is between anchor contracts, with the RINL Visakhapatnam contract concluded and Dankuni still scaling. Steel movement remains exposed to fuel costs, steel prices and monsoon seasonality.
  • Receivables. The RINL closure recovery is taking longer than expected, keeping receivable days at about 97 against an 80-day target, with collections now expected in H2 FY27.
  • Execution and guidance risk. An investor observed that the company has missed quarterly top-line and margin guidance for three to four quarters due to operational deferrals and execution delays. Management acknowledged the 20%–25% growth target is aggressive but expressed confidence in meeting it by year-end.
  • Customer concentration in renewables. The possibility of OEMs building captive fleets and the company’s exposure to wind-sector clients were raised; management characterised the captive fleet as a small share of OEM activity and emphasised multi-client, multi-sector deployment.
  • Leverage and capital intensity. Depreciation of ₹16 crore on the expanded fleet is a substantial non-cash charge weighing on reported profit, and the company continues to carry secured loans and short-term borrowings while funding an ₹80–100 crore capex programme.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Revenue growth. The medium-term framework of 20%–25% annual revenue growth was reaffirmed. Observed Q1 FY27 revenue growth was 11% year-on-year (about 14% adjusting for the write-off), with the equipment segment at 55%. An investor calculated that the full-year guidance implies FY27 revenue of around ₹350 crore, requiring roughly ₹280 crore over the remaining nine months — an average quarterly run rate of ₹90–95 crore, likely weighted further into Q3 and Q4 given monsoon softness in Q2 — and noted this would require an additional ₹70–80 crore of incremental orders assuming full conversion of the existing order book with no spillover. Management did not provide a quarter-wise breakup, citing project specifics and equipment deployment, but said Q2 is currently looking better than Q1 on deployment and that Q3 and Q4 are traditionally substantially higher.

EBITDA margin. The 37%–38% medium-term band was reaffirmed as the FY27 target. Observed Q1 FY27 margin was 30.7%. Management explicitly guided that H1 FY27 will run below the band, with recovery weighted to H2 as affected equipment redeploys, the disputed project settles and new capex generates revenue. Certain specialised-services revenues are expected to stabilise toward the end of Q2. On Q2 specifically, management said margins would remain soft and declined to put a number on it, but expected no drop as large as Q1’s, assuming the one-off does not repeat.

Utilisation. Management cited annual occupancy of about 84%–85% as the anticipated level for the year, supported by the order book composition (74% equipment rentals) and the Q1 capex deploying from July. Observed Q1 utilisation was 79%.

Receivable days. The target of below 80 days by year-end was maintained, against the observed 97 days, with the RINL recovery now expected in H2.

Capex. The ₹80–100 crore FY27 plan was reaffirmed, mostly to be completed by November 2026. Observed Q1 deployment was ₹42.8 crore (approximately ₹40.59 crore per the 3 July equipment-purchase intimation, exclusive of GST).

Order book. The executable FY27 order book of ₹204.82 crore as of July 2026 was reaffirmed across the press release, investor presentation and earnings call.

Broker Narrative

The broker retained a Buy rating across both reports and kept the same broad risk framework—regulatory change, marketplace scalability, and logistics demand—while shifting from a Q4FY25 growth/margin story to a more granular Q1FY26 outlook. Persisting themes were growth from value-added services (payments, telematics) and newer businesses; the last report added near-term cost/seasonality headwinds (Q1 seasonality, employee increments, Superloads investments) and reframed regulation as a tailwind via AIS GPS mandates. Despite the continued Buy, both predicted returns (+41.3%, +34.0%) ended as disappointments with -8.6% and -30.7% actual returns.
