Headwinds and Challenges

  • Elevated gold prices weighed on purchase decisions, particularly among price-sensitive consumers, during the early part of Q1FY27. Demand was somewhat subdued in June, though buying gained traction after a sharp correction in gold prices during June and relative stability in July.
  • The government’s increase in gold import duty from 6% to 15% in May 2026 introduced near-term cost and demand sensitivity.
  • The Adhik Maas period fell entirely within Q1FY27, a factor management cited while commenting on the quarter’s performance.
  • The industry as a whole saw no meaningful volume growth in FY26 amid geopolitical tensions, conflicts, macroeconomic uncertainty, and sharp volatility in gold and silver prices. Q1FY27 volume growth was only about 1%–2%, with most revenue growth driven by higher gold prices.
  • Operating cash flows have been negative for the last three years. Management attributed this to the working-capital-intensive nature of the business, where 95%–98% of assets consist of inventory, and stated that positive operating cash flow is difficult while inventory levels are high.
  • The company temporarily moderated the pace of expansion during Q1FY27 following developments in May, before returning to a more aggressive growth mode.
  • The proposed alteration of the Articles of Association to insert Article 165 (marking specified securities as non-transferable) carries regulatory compliance considerations, including statutory lock-in requirements for specified securities.
  • The AGM notice notes potential risks related to loss or inadequacy of profits in any financial year in the context of director remuneration.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Q1FY27 revenue grew 58% YoY to ₹853.63 crore, EBITDA rose 70% YoY to ₹93.99 crore (margin 11.01%, up 80 bps), and PAT rose 77% YoY to ₹64.45 crore (margin 7.55%, up 82 bps).
  • Same-store sales growth was 52%; footfall-to-conversion ratio was 81%; average ticket size was approximately ₹1,57,000.
  • Wedding-related purchases accounted for 62% of sales, festive and lightweight jewellery 18%, and other categories 20%.
  • Old gold exchange contributed approximately 25% of total sales in Q1FY27, improving affordability and reducing dependence on fresh gold procurement. The D.P. Swarna Plus Scheme, launched in April, saw strong response, with about 50 customers contributing more than ₹1,00,000 per month.
  • Silver revenue grew 150% YoY, from ₹16 crore in Q1FY26 to ₹40 crore in Q1FY27. Gold revenue rose 59% YoY to ₹781 crore.
  • The company finalized locations for new showrooms in Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh (approximately 3,750 sq. ft., first FOCO model) and Dahod, Gujarat (approximately 3,200 sq. ft., first Gujarat entry, COCO model). Fit-out is underway.
  • The company launched an e-commerce website and mobile application on the Play Store and App Store, and is expanding on online marketplaces.
  • Brand-building included sponsorship of the Madhya Pradesh League – Scindia Cup 2026 and the launch of the Rani Rupmati Collection, showcased in Kota.
  • Management cited sector tailwinds: growing discretionary income, weddings and festivals driving domestic demand, a rising young urban population, omni-channel and digital engagement, a shift toward branded and organised retail, increasing role of diamonds and studded jewellery, and Tier-II/III market expansion.
  • Expansion plans span Gujarat, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, and Rajasthan, with a focus on Tier-II and Tier-III cities and no immediate plans for metropolitan markets.
  • The company targets increasing the studded mix from 6%–7% to 12%–15% by March 2028, and has the Amoura brand for daily-wear lightweight studded jewellery.

Key Risks

  • Gold price volatility and elevated gold prices remain the principal risk, affecting demand, inventory valuation, and margins.
  • The gap between inventory carrying value (approximately ₹1,20,000 per unit) and prevailing market prices (approximately ₹1,51,000–₹1,52,000) is nearly 20%; this narrows only as fresh inventory is procured at prevailing prices.
  • Negative operating cash flows over the last three years, tied to high inventory levels and a working-capital-intensive model.
  • Competitive pressure from larger organised jewellery players expanding into Tier-II and Tier-III markets.
  • Execution risk on the store rollout roadmap, including the new FOCO model, which is a pilot with revenue-sharing arrangements.
  • Regulatory and compliance risk related to the proposed Article 165 on marking specified securities as non-transferable, and statutory lock-in requirements.
  • Risk of loss or inadequacy of profits in any financial year, noted in the AGM notice in connection with director remuneration.
  • Dependence on wedding and festive demand, which accounted for the majority of sales.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Store expansion: Management guided to reaching 51 stores by FY30, with 6–7 COCO and 1–2 FOCO openings annually. In Q1FY27, the company temporarily moderated expansion pace following May developments, then returned to aggressive mode. Two locations (Jabalpur, Dahod) are finalized, with discussions progressing for two more and a possibility of finalizing two to three additional locations by end of July. Management acknowledged that in a given year openings may be six instead of eight, to be compensated in later years.
  • Volume growth: Management targets approximately 10% volume growth for both FY27 and FY28. Observed Q1FY27 volume growth was only about 1%–2%, with the balance of revenue growth driven by higher gold prices. Management expects volume growth to improve as prices stabilize.
  • Studded ratio: Management targets increasing the studded mix from 6%–7% to 12%–15% by March 2028. No Q1FY27 studded mix figure was disclosed; diamond revenue was ₹29 crore in Q1FY27 versus ₹31 crore in Q1FY26.
  • Store economics: Management stated new showrooms typically reach three times inventory turnover after two to three years, optimum levels of 4.5–5 times after four to five years, and some reach eight to nine times after six to seven years. Break-even on capex is generally achieved in six to nine months. Larger format stores (8,000–10,000 sq. ft.) are expected to reach ₹350–₹400 crore turnover in three to four years; smaller stores (3,000–5,000 sq. ft.) ₹150–₹200 crore.
  • Operating cash flow: Management stated that as the business scales and inventory productivity improves, working capital efficiency should strengthen and operating cash flows should gradually move toward a more positive position. Observed performance shows negative operating cash flow over the last three years.
  • Director re-appointments: The Board approved re-appointment of Mr. Santosh Kataria as Chairman & Managing Director and Mr. Anil Kataria as Whole-time Director, each for five consecutive years effective October 1, 2026, subject to member approval at the 9th AGM scheduled for September 25, 2026. The AGM notice was published in Financial Express (English) and Chautha Sansar (Hindi) on September 04, 2026.
