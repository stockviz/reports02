Headwinds and Challenges

  • Subdued water infrastructure demand: FY25–26 was described as a challenging year for the water infrastructure sector, with the domestic DI pipe industry seeing subdued demand due to a slowdown in Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) fund releases, which slowed execution of water-related projects by state governments.
  • Weak Q1FY27 performance: Consolidated Total Income declined 4.3% QoQ to ₹1,465 crore, attributed primarily to subdued demand amid lower government spending on water infrastructure projects. Standalone Total Income fell 8.9% QoQ to ₹1,119 crore.
  • Year-on-year declines: Consolidated EBITDA fell 29.8% YoY to ₹139 crore (margin 9.5%, down 300 bps), and consolidated PAT fell 45.7% YoY to ₹48 crore. Standalone EBITDA fell 62.1% YoY to ₹71 crore (margin 6.3%, down 674 bps), with standalone PAT at ₹6 crore, down 93.1% YoY.
  • Lower sales volumes: The company sold 1.20 lakh tonnes of DI pipes, fittings and CI pipes in Q1FY27, against 1.48 lakh tonnes in Q4FY26. Annual sales fell from 7.94 lakh tonnes in FY23 to 5.84 lakh tonnes in FY26.
  • Full-year FY26 weakness: Consolidated Total Income declined 17.6% YoY to ₹6,133 crore, EBITDA fell 50.5% YoY to ₹574 crore (margin 9.4%, down 622 bps), and PAT fell 77.2% YoY to ₹161 crore. Standalone FY26 Total Income fell 23.6% YoY to ₹5,228 crore, with PAT down 81.6% to ₹131 crore.
  • Leadership transition: Whole-Time Director & CFO Mr. Ashutosh Agarwal resigned effective close of business on 10 August 2026; Mr. Rajesh Daga was appointed CFO effective 11 August 2026.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • JJM 2.0: Approved in March 2026, with the overall outlay enhanced to approximately ₹8.69 lakh crore up to December 2028 and the Central Government contribution raised to ₹3.59 lakh crore from ₹2.08 lakh crore. Target of 100% rural tap coverage for 19.4 crore households by December 2028.
  • Budget allocations and fund flows: ₹67,670 crore allocated for JJM 2.0 in the Union Budget for FY27–28, with ₹10,344 crore already sanctioned to states in FY27 to date. Significant increase in fund releases expected from the beginning of H2 FY27.
  • AMRUT 2.0: Indicative outlay of ₹3 lakh crore (FY22–FY26), with ~₹1.93 lakh crore of approved projects, ~₹1.67 lakh crore of DPRs sanctioned and ~₹1.38 lakh crore of projects awarded; ₹8,000 crore allocated in the FY26–27 Union Budget.
  • River interlinking: 30 river links identified under the National Perspective Plan; 26 Feasibility Reports and 13 DPRs completed. Ken–Betwa is the only project under full implementation (estimated cost ₹44,605 crore, expected completion 2030), with five priority links identified for accelerated development.
  • Valves diversification: Acquisition of 100% of T.I.S. Services S.p.A (Italy) in July 2025 for ~€11.5 million (7–8x EV/EBITDA), with ~€41 million turnover in CY25 and ~13% EBITDA margin. Management aims to double valves revenues in four years, start manufacturing in India, and targets ~16% margin, supported by a patented FR line product.
  • Industrial paints entry: Existing 4,200 KL facility with a 17,000 KL upcoming plant; target of ₹800–1,000 crore revenue over five years (including inorganic), with planned investment of ₹250–300 crore, addressing a ₹29,000 crore market growing at ~10% CAGR with 14–16% EBITDA margins.
  • Export expansion: Exports contribute ~22% of revenue across 130+ countries; focus on deepening presence in Africa and South-East Asia, with exports targeted at 25% of sales.
  • Rubber products for Indian Railways: Board approved manufacture of Modified Elastomeric Pads, LHB and Vande Bharat metal-bonded rubber components, intercar gangway mountings and UIC-type rubber vestibules, and vendor registration with Indian Railways, in continuation of the November 9, 2023 resolution for a 12 TPD rubber gasket plant at Punganur, Andhra Pradesh.
  • Balance sheet strength: Net debt reduction of ₹1,109 crore in FY26; net debt-to-book value of 0.11x; AA/A1+ credit rating; dividend policy of 90%–140% of face value since FY21–22. Promoter stake rose to 50.14% in Q4FY26 from 46.22% in Q3FY26.

Key Risks

  • Dependence on government spending and fund release cycles for water infrastructure, evidenced by the JJM fund slowdown in FY25–26 and its impact on demand and project execution.
  • Continued revenue and margin pressure if demand recovery does not materialise as anticipated.
  • Execution risk in diversification into valves, industrial paints and railway rubber products, including new manufacturing, vendor registration and integration of the Italian acquisition.
  • Exposure to export markets across 130+ countries, with geographic concentration risk mitigated by diversification but subject to conditions in target regions.
  • Commodity and input cost exposure given the integrated manufacturing model (coke, ferro-alloys, power, sponge iron).

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Demand recovery: Management expects demand to restore by the end of the current quarter of FY 2026-27, with the road to recovery outlined as ~5.3 lakh tonnes in FY26, recovery anticipated in H2 FY27, and a return to normal ~7.2 lakh tonnes in FY27–28. Observed Q1FY27 sales of 1.20 lakh tonnes were below Q4FY26’s 1.48 lakh tonnes, and FY26 actual sales of 5.84 lakh tonnes were below the ~7.5 lakh tonnes indicated for FY24 and FY25.
  • Valves: Management targets doubling valves revenues in four years with ~16% margin and ~₹800 crore revenues over the next four years. The acquisition was completed in July 2025; Q1FY27 results do not separately disclose valves segment performance.
  • Industrial paints: Target of ₹800–1,000 crore revenue over five years with ₹250–300 crore capex. The business is at an early stage, with the 17,000 KL plant described as upcoming.
  • JJM fund flow: Management expects a significant increase in fund releases from the beginning of H2 FY27; ₹10,344 crore has been sanctioned to states in FY27 to date, against ₹1,562 crore released in FY25–26.
  • Dividend: A final dividend of Re. 0.90 per share (90%) for FY26 was approved at the 71st AGM on 31 August 2026, consistent with the stated 90%–140% of face value dividend policy since FY21–22.
