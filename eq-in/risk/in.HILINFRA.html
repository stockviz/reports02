HILINFRA — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Q1 FY27 profitability pressure. The quarter ended 30 June 2026 saw total income of Rs. 304.3 crore (up 170.6% YoY) but EBITDA of only Rs. 4.8 crore (1.6% margin) and PAT of Rs. 1.1 crore (0.3% margin), against EBITDA of Rs. 12.0 crore (10.7% margin) and PAT of Rs. 7.2 crore (6.4% margin) in Q1 FY26. Management attributed this to lower traffic volumes at the Moti Naroli toll project and a few other toll projects, caused by geopolitical developments and global trade disruptions that affected export-import activity and freight movement through port-linked corridors, resulting in under-recovery of fixed project costs.

Toll-specific setbacks. Management cited a temporary bidding restriction from NHAI and the voluntary surrender of a toll project whose economics had become unfavourable due to the same geopolitical situation. The Moti Naroli contract (Rs. 154.6 crore, work order dated 21-02-2026, anticipated completion 21-05-2026) shows no balance amount payable to NHAI in the June 2026 order book table.

Regional traffic recovery still incomplete. On the Western Front, management stated that traffic is still some way from earlier trends because factories and trade were impacted; recovery there “will take some time,” while other regions have resumed to normal.

Seasonality. Management noted that Q1 and Q2 tend to hover around break-even, with rapid recovery in Q3 and Q4, and that Q2 is a slow quarter for EPC because of the monsoon.

Broader risk factors cited. The annual report material cites heightened volatility in the global economy, rising geopolitical tensions, shifts in trade policies, tighter financial conditions, elevated commodity prices, increased uncertainty, disruptions to energy transit, and potential risks related to competition, project execution, and changes in government policy. Other cited risks include government actions, local, political or economic developments, and industry risks.

IPO proceeds utilisation delay. The Monitoring Agency report for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 records a delay in utilisation of unutilised funds due to operational reasons, disclosed via board resolution dated 13 May 2026, with the funds to be utilised before 31 May 2026. The objects were originally to be completed up to FY 25-26. As of 30 June 2026, the monitoring account balance was nil, with no unutilised amount.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order book and pipeline. Consolidated order book stood at approximately Rs. 778 crore as of 30 June 2026. In the call, management stated that after recent wins the work order position is around Rs. 900 crore, of which EPC is almost Rs. 500 crore and the rest toll. The investor presentation cites an L1 pipeline of over Rs. 60 crore as on August 2026, and management described the Q1 pipeline as not final, with Q2 focused on accumulating quality tenders with shorter execution spans.

New toll wins and geographic expansion. In July 2026 the company secured a tollway collection contract of approximately Rs. 28.7 crore for the Kozhinjipatti toll plaza on the Dindigul–Samayanallur stretch of NH-44 in Tamil Nadu (work order 22-07-2026, 90-day contract). It also received a letter of acceptance for toll operations at the fee plaza at km 154.5 on the Krishnagiri–Thumbipadi section of NH-44, with a contract value of approximately Rs. 80 crore. Management described NH-44 as a key economic corridor and the Tamil Nadu entry as a first and a significant achievement, with stated interest in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, West Bengal, Assam and the North-East.

Gorakhpur Link Expressway win. A Letter of Acceptance was received from UPEIDA for collection of user fees, operation of toll plazas, and deployment of four patrol-cum-safety vehicles on the Gorakhpur Link Expressway — total contract value Rs. 220,66,33,332 for two years, with a first-year amount of Rs. 105,07,77,777 and 10% escalation per annum in the second year. Management stated this enhances medium-term revenue visibility, demonstrates ability to execute large-scale toll management mandates, and positions HIL to pursue larger, higher-value projects while expanding its toll operations footprint.

EPC and real estate. Execution commenced on the Beverly Hills project (contract value approximately Rs. 70 crore, timeline of about 15–16 months), expected to contribute around 40–45% of EPC revenue over the next 15–16 months. Management is pursuing newer departments such as MES and CPWD, and continues to target a balance between government and private works. Real estate revenue grew from Rs. 8.0 crore in FY25 to Rs. 41.6 crore in FY26.

Industry opportunity. The investor presentation cites the MoRTH allocation of Rs. 3.10 lakh crore in the Union Budget 2026-27 (up 8.0% YoY), NHAI allocation of Rs. 1.87 lakh crore for FY27, road works allocation of Rs. 1.22 lakh crore, ~5,614 km of national highways constructed in FY25 against a 5,150 km target, 9,000 km of expressway projects sanctioned with another 10,000 km planned for award during 2025-27, six new national highways in Madhya Pradesh covering around 670 km, and infrastructure projects worth Rs. 60,000+ crore announced in Madhya Pradesh. It also cites India’s toll collection projected to rise from Rs. 55,000 crore to Rs. 1.4 lakh crore within two years, and government plans for Multi Lane Free Flow toll collection by end-2026. Other cited opportunities include ropeways under Parvatmala Pariyojana, solar under PM-KUSUM, wayside amenities, EV charging, and renewable-linked EPC.

Technology focus. Management repeatedly emphasised integrating technology across verticals — data centralisation, AI-based analysis, reducing on-site manpower, and citing BIM as an example — with formal disclosures to follow once ready.

3. Key Risks

  • Traffic-volume risk in tolling. Toll profitability depends on actual collections versus fixed payments to NHAI; if collections fall short of cost, the project incurs a loss. Management noted that larger-value toll contracts do not necessarily yield better margins because traffic understanding is complicated.
  • Concentration and geographic risk. Revenue contribution by state in Q1 FY27 was Gujarat 42.2%, Andhra Pradesh 24.6%, Uttar Pradesh 17.8%, Delhi 11.3% and Chhattisgarh 2.9%. Management’s stated strategy is to hedge this through diversification.
  • Geopolitical and trade disruption. The Q1 FY27 impact was attributed to geopolitical strain affecting logistics, factories and freight on port-linked corridors; management flagged that a recurrence of international political developments could affect results.
  • Bidding and qualification constraints. The temporary NHAI bidding restriction and the voluntary project surrender illustrate regulatory/bidding risk. Management also noted PQ (pre-qualification) limits current ability to bid for larger EPC tenders independently, with JV collaboration as an option.
  • Execution and competitive risk. Cited risks include competition, project execution, and changes in government policy. Management noted that newer entrants bid on thin margins and that many such projects fail.
  • Macro and commodity risk. Elevated commodity prices, tighter financial conditions, and energy transit disruptions are cited in the annual report material.
  • New-segment execution risk. Renewables and EV infrastructure are described as new segments where the company does not want to “burn its hands” by bidding abruptly; no bids had been submitted in those areas as of the call.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 revenue guidance. Management guided FY27 turnover of approximately Rs. 850 crore, split as about Rs. 700 crore from toll and the remainder from EPC. In the annual report material, FY27 guidance is stated as total revenue of approximately over Rs. 900 crore, alongside a five-year aspiration to exceed Rs. 2,000 crore. Q1 FY27 actual total income was Rs. 304.3 crore, with segmental revenue of Rs. 273.9 crore from tollway collection, Rs. 27.9 crore from EPC Infra and Rs. 1.5 crore from real estate.

FY28 guidance. Management guided FY27-28 turnover of approximately Rs. 1,200 crore.

Order book conversion guidance. Of the EPC order book, management stated 24 projects are executable, with approximately Rs. 150 crore to be realised in FY27 and Rs. 200 crore in FY28, executable over three years. The EPC on-going project table shows total contract value of Rs. 712.3 crore and balance work in hand of Rs. 443.2 crore as of June 2026.

Margin recovery guidance. Management stated the Q1 FY27 margin impact was absorbed in Q1 and expects recovery, contingent on no new geopolitical developments, citing improving traffic trends visible on the IHMCL website. Management also stated the Q1 weakness would not significantly affect full-year results, based on the historical pattern of break-even in Q1/Q2 and rapid recovery in Q3/Q4.

Bid-win ratios. Management stated a success ratio of approximately 25–30% for both toll and EPC.

Observed versus guided. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 1.6% and PAT margin of 0.3% compare with Q1 FY26 margins of 10.7% and 6.4% respectively, reflecting the temporary factors management described. The order book position moved from approximately Rs. 778 crore as of 30 June 2026 to around Rs. 900 crore cited on the call following the Tamil Nadu wins, with the Gorakhpur Link Expressway LOA (Rs. 220.66 crore over two years) announced separately. The FY26 full-year figures cited in the annual report material — consolidated total income of Rs. 633.4 crore, EBITDA of Rs. 51.5 crore, PAT of Rs. 31.8 crore, and order book of Rs. 1,133 crore — provide the base against which FY27 guidance of Rs. 850–900+ crore is set.
