Headwinds and Challenges

  • Supply and production disruption in the first 45 days of Q1 FY27. The company reported that its Chairman passed away, causing 3–4 days of plant shutdown across India as a mark of respect. Separately, the Bengal elections caused movement of Bengali labour, which is concentrated in Bikaner where core bhujia is manufactured. Because supplies are spread evenly through the month rather than skewed, any lost dispatch days directly affect the quarter. The company stated it could run plants only at minimal production until 18 May.
  • Input cost inflation. Edible oil prices rose due to geopolitical issues and less rainfall, and pulses such as moth dal and chana dal also started increasing. Coal prices rose over the previous 4–5 months, adding roughly 40 basis points to manufacturing cost.
  • Elevated other operating expenses. Higher sales promotion and advertising activity in the quarter contributed a 40–50 basis point hit below gross margin.
  • Export weakness. Exports declined 2.2% for the first time, attributed to US tariff disturbances and crude oil prices pushing freight costs up 2x–3x, which deferred certain shipments. Management noted the US is a very large share of the export market and that resolution may take a quarter or a couple of quarters.
  • Papad segment decline. Papad declined 6.5% in the quarter, attributed to early monsoon affecting handmade papad production, since the product cannot dry when it rains. Papad is about 6% of overall business.
  • Category growth moderation. Management noted that until two years ago the snacking category grew at double digits, whereas over the last two years it has grown at a single digit. Standalone business grew around 11% last full year.
  • PLI tapering. PLI is expected to contribute close to 150 basis points to EBITDA this year, and management stated it will take 1.5 to 2 years to return to the original margin once PLI ends.
  • Quick commerce private labels. Quick commerce platforms have launched their own private label snack brands. Management stated it is too early to make a statement, but noted that consumers tend to stay with established brands.
  • Concentration risk in Bikaner. Management acknowledged that if Bikaji gets isolated or disconnected from Bikaner, it would impact overall business, and stated it is decentralizing bhujia production outside Bikaner.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue from operations was INR 7,343 mn, up 12.5% YoY, with volume growth of 7.7%. Gross margin was 35.7%, up 70 bps YoY. EBITDA was INR 990 mn with a 13.5% margin, versus 12.2% in the prior quarter. PAT was INR 595 mn, up 1.6% YoY.
  • Second-half momentum. Management described the quarter as a story of two halves: the last 45 days grew around 20%, June was the highest delivery month, and July continued that momentum. Management stated it is maintaining a 15% plus growth target for the year.
  • Category-wise growth. Ethnic Snacks grew 11.4%, Western Snacks 21.3%, Sweets 4.4%. Family packs grew 11% and impulse packs 10.5%.
  • Geography-wise growth. Core states grew close to 11%, focus states close to 19%, and other states 5.6%. UP grew about 37%, with a full-year budget of 30% plus growth in that state.
  • Distribution expansion. Direct reach reached 370,893 outlets, adding 17,000 outlets in Q1, with overall total reach of 14.46 lakh outlets as on 30 June 2026.
  • Quick commerce. The q-com channel grew upwards of 100% in Q1, with the company stating it is growing faster than or in line with the category on those platforms.
  • Retail business. The retail business grew 71.8% YoY, with stores increasing from 15 to 28 over one year. THF plans to open close to 10 stores this year, moving from 22 to 35-plus stores this year and 50 stores in 2.5–3 years, targeting INR 6–8 crore sales per store with 25% plus store-level EBITDA, focused on Tier 2 cities and premium sweets and gifting (65–70% of sales).
  • Capacity addition. An ASRS investment and close to 1 lakh square feet of construction in Bikaner will allow an additional 1.2–1.3 lakh cartons to ease supply chain and maintain minimum stock levels at key stock points.
  • Festive season. Management reported bullish plans from modern trade and q-com for Diwali, positive demand plans from general trade for the next three months, and expects sweets to grow 12–13% and gifting upwards of 17–18% in Q2 and Q3 combined.
  • International expansion. The Board approved an investment of up to INR 1,500 lakhs in C.G. Bikaji Private Limited, a Joint Venture in Nepal, with both parties investing close to INR 15 crores each and the plant expected to start in 8–9 months. The Board also approved incorporation of a Wholly-Owned Subsidiary in Abu Dhabi, UAE. Management stated the US business should be about 3 times current size in the next 2 years, and international contribution should rise from 3–4% to close to 5.5–6% of overall business in 3–4 years.
  • Bakery. Bikaji Bakes has not started full-fledged production; trials and R&D are ongoing. Management stated no big numbers are planned this year, with production expected to start by end of year across export, own e-com/q-com, and HoReCa channels. A strategic partnership was formed with bakery expert Mr. Thayekunni Khaleel through Bikaji Bakes Private Limited, transferring a 30% equity stake while retaining a 70% majority stake, with the aim of bringing global bakery practices and innovation to India and building a scalable, differentiated bakery business.
  • New subsidiary. The company completed acquisition of 14,800 equity shares (74% of paid-up equity share capital) of Jai Barbareek Dev Snacks Private Limited for INR 1.48 lakhs, effective 2 July 2026.
  • Non-palm oil and expanded range. The company has piloted non-palm oil offerings and expanded ranges on the q-com channel, described as being future ready for Gen Z demand, though not expected to be a substantial share of business.

Key Risks

  • Commodity and input price risk. Edible oil, pulses, and coal inflation, with the company having taken two price increases in the last four months and one MRP increase in April, and stating it will not take further MRP increases until after Diwali.
  • Geopolitical and trade risk. US tariff disturbances and crude oil-driven freight cost increases affecting export shipments.
  • Weather risk. Monsoon affecting handmade papad production and less rainfall affecting edible oil and pulse prices.
  • Labour concentration risk. Dependence on Bengali labour in Bikaner for core bhujia, with the company targeting bhujia manufacture in two plants this year as mitigation.
  • Single-location concentration. Management explicitly flagged the risk of Bikaji being isolated or disconnected from Bikaner.
  • Competitive risk. Quick commerce private labels and D2C brands penetrating faster, with management noting sustainability of some brands is a real question.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. The company stated actual results may differ materially from forward-looking statements due to risks including future changes in the company, the competitive environment, ability to implement strategies and initiatives, respond to technological changes, and sociopolitical, economic and regulatory conditions in India.
  • PLI dependence. PLI contributes close to 150 basis points to EBITDA this year and will not be available next year, requiring 1.5 to 2 years to restore original margins.
  • Subsidiary performance. During FY26, the company recognized an impairment loss of INR 554.10 lakhs on an investment in a subsidiary, disclosed as an exceptional item, and stated it will continue to monitor the subsidiary’s performance and reassess carrying value.
  • Fire incident. On 17 August 2025, a fire at the manufacturing facility of Dadiji Snacks Private Limited, a contract manufacturer in Patna, Bihar, damaged machinery owned and installed by Bikaji, resulting in a loss of INR 435.14 lakhs, disclosed as an exceptional item in FY26.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Growth guidance. Management stated a target of maintaining 15% plus growth overall this year, with mid-teens to high-teens (close to 16% plus minus) expected quarter-on-quarter. Observed Q1 FY27 growth was 12.5% in value and 7.7% in volume, with the last 45 days of the quarter growing around 20%. Management attributed the gap to the first-45-day disruption and stated mid-teens growth in core markets would be visible from the current quarter onwards.
  • Margin guidance. Management guided full-year EBITDA margin of 13%–13.5% including PLI, with at least 50 basis points year-on-year improvement, and a long-term target of 15% over three years. Observed Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 13.5%, versus 12.2% in the prior quarter. Management noted the next two quarters will carry heavy advertising, and that the 15% margin target is not for this year.
  • Advertising spend guidance. Management stated a full-year target of close to 2% of sales, with heavier advertising in the next two quarters due to festivities.
  • Price increase guidance. Management stated no price increase is planned in the next 30 days, with prices signed off with large accounts, unless a catastrophe occurs.
  • Sweets and gifting guidance. Management guided sweets growth of 12–13% and gifting growth upwards of 17–18% for Q2 and Q3 combined. Observed Q1 FY27 sweets growth was 4.4%.
  • Focus state guidance. Management guided 30% plus growth in UP for the full year. Observed Q1 FY27 UP growth was about 37%.
  • Export guidance. Management stated long-term export growth of 25–30% is expected, while noting near-term disruption from freight costs may take a quarter or a couple of quarters to resolve. Observed Q1 FY27 exports declined 2.2%.
  • Retail guidance. Management guided THF growth of 50–60% year-on-year for the next 3–4 years, with the business potentially becoming 15–16% of EBITDA with 40–50% year-on-year growth. Observed retail business growth was 71.8% YoY in Q1 FY27.
  • International contribution guidance. Management guided international contribution rising from 3–4% to close to 5.5–6% of overall business in 3–4 years, and US business tripling in 2 years.
  • PLI mitigation guidance. Management stated it will target recovering 50 to 75 basis points of the PLI impact through pricing and gross margin improvement, taking 1.5 to 2 years to reach the original margin.
  • Bakery guidance. Management stated no big numbers are planned this year, with production expected to start by end of year and numbers built into next year’s plan.

Broker Narrative

Brokers maintained a Buy rating throughout, but the narrative shifted from commodity-deflation-driven margin expansion and aggressive geographic/distribution growth to defending against raw-material inflation, supply disruptions, and higher A&P costs. Persistent themes were distribution expansion, western snacks growth, PLI benefits, and a long-term 15%+ EBITDA margin goal, while the geographic concentration risk flagged in 2023 remained an explicit key risk in the last report.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning of input cost pressure in pulses/flours materialized as sustained raw-material inflation in edible oil, pulses, and packaging, coinciding with margin declines and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Geographic concentration in core markets, flagged as a risk in the first report, was still listed as a key risk in the last report and correlated with the string of negative returns after early 2025.
  • The early concern over rising operating expenses recurred, with the last report expecting higher A&P spend over the next three quarters.

Optimism that failed

  • Commodity deflation-driven margin expansion did not persist, as raw-material inflation and a 130bp YoY EBITDA margin decline in 1QFY27 reversed the earlier tailwind.
  • The early target of 20%+ revenue growth from Phase-1 geographic expansion softened to only mid-teen growth resumption and >15% FY27 guidance, while multiple Buy calls delivered negative actual returns.
