Camlin Fine Sciences — Q1 FY27 Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material and geopolitical pressure. Management attributed the quarter’s weak margins primarily to elevated raw material prices, availability issues, and higher freight costs linked to an ongoing conflict situation. The company noted a one-quarter lag in passing cost increases through to customers, and stated it could not pass on the entire increase. The Hormuz Strait and Red Sea issues forced shipping rerouting via South Africa, lengthening transit times and increasing working capital needs.

Margin compression. Gross margin fell from 45% in the prior quarter to 4% in Q1 FY27, which flowed through to EBITDA. Management estimated that roughly 3–4% of the gross margin loss was due to geopolitical factors and 1–2% due to the company’s liquidity situation, including the use of supplier and dealer financing that raised effective input costs.

Diphenol plant shutdown. The diphenol plant remained shut for economic reasons given high phenol and other raw material prices. The company is procuring intermediates such as hydroquinone from China, having secured supply for the next two quarters. The closure weighed on the Performance Chemicals segment, which posted 2.5% EBITDA.

Aroma segment losses. The Aroma business recorded a negative EBITDA of roughly INR4–4.5 crores. Management explained this as a capacity utilization issue: ethyl vanillin production ran at only about 400 tons in the quarter against a 1,500-ton capacity, so fixed costs were not absorbed. The ethyl vanillin ramp-up was deliberately cautious to validate quality, and 95% of customers approved the product.

Working capital and liquidity. The working capital cycle elongated due to longer transit and slower customer recoveries amid a global slowdown. Management noted a general consolidated working capital cycle of about 100 days and indicated it may need INR100–200 crores of additional credit lines as the year progresses.

Brazil fire and liquidations. An exceptional item of INR111.1 mn was recorded for the shortfall in settlement of an insurance claim relating to a February fire at the Brazil unit; the company settled the claim at a 20% haircut (around INR400 mn) to obtain cash. CFS Europe has gone into liquidation with minimal residual cash burn, and China is in the process of liquidation with an expected INR10–20 mn expenditure in the quarter.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record revenue. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was INR5,198.8 mn, up about 28% year-on-year and roughly INR1,000 mn higher than the prior quarter — described as the highest ever quarterly revenue.

Blends growth. The Specialty Ingredients blends business grew at a 20% CAGR, with management budgeting 25–30% growth for the year. Blends revenue exceeded INR3,000 mn in the quarter, with the segment overall above INR4,000 mn. Straights sales were around INR927 mn, with segmental growth of 41% YoY in Straights and 35% YoY in Blends.

Vanillin demand and antidumping duty. Channel stocks of vanillin in the U.S. and Europe have dried out, removing the pre-antidumping-duty overhang. Management sees a gap of about 5,000–6,000 tons of material that will need to be imported into the U.S. and Europe because local production capacities are fully occupied. U.S. and European selling prices are expected to remain at $13–14.

Segment recovery expectations. Management expects all three verticals — Specialty Ingredients, Aroma, and Performance Chemicals — to be significantly better in Q2 than Q1, with Aroma turning positive EBITDA in Q2 and Performance Chemicals also moving positive. Q3 is expected to be the most normalized quarter, with double-digit company-level EBITDA margins anticipated.

Acquisition integration and market expansion. Vitafor and Vinpai are contributing to cross-selling and portfolio expansion, with growth momentum in blends continuing across geographies and expansion across AMEA, America, and Europe.

Debt stability. Gross debt stood at INR640 crores versus INR670 crores as of March 31, with the reduction from prepayments on long-term IFC and EXIM loans for the vanillin plant. No increase in debt occurred during the quarter.

3. Key Risks

  • Prolonged geopolitical conflict keeping raw material prices, freight costs, and transit times elevated, with limited ability to pass costs through.
  • Continued diphenol plant shutdown; a decision on alternate use or restart is expected by Q3, with raw material prices for alternative phenolic compounds also elevated.
  • Capacity utilization risk in Aroma, where fixed costs are not absorbed at low production volumes; campaign switches cost about a month of production each.
  • Working capital and liquidity pressure, including reliance on supplier and dealer financing that dilutes gross margins, and potential need for INR100–200 crores of additional credit lines.
  • Customer concentration and pricing dynamics in vanillin, where the market leader’s pricing behavior influences whether Chinese material re-enters despite antidumping duties.
  • Residual cash burn from liquidation processes in Europe and China.
  • Insurance settlement shortfall already realized as an exceptional item.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Revenue guidance maintained, margin guidance reduced. Management had previously guided FY27 revenue of INR2,000–2,400 crores with EBITDA of INR250–280 crores. On the call, management reiterated confidence in the top line but revised margin expectations down to 10–12% EBITDA for the year, citing the prolonged conflict, high prices, and logistical issues. In a later exchange, management framed the guidance as INR2,200–2,300 crores top line with 10–11% EBITDA margin, implying INR220–230 crores EBITDA.

Vanillin volume guidance lowered. Management had previously guided 3,600–4,000 tons of vanillin for FY27; this was revised to around 3,000 tons. The change was attributed to a slower-than-anticipated ethyl vanillin ramp-up (to ensure quality standards) and four campaign switches during the year, each costing about a month of production. Management indicated that ideally no more than three campaigns would be run, which could support scaling to 5,000 tons.

Quarterly progression. Management guided that Q2 would be better than Q1 across all three verticals, that Q3 would see double-digit company-level EBITDA margins, and that H2 margins could be in the 13–15% range given fixed costs remaining stable. For FY28, management indicated a normalized margin range of 12–14%.

Q1 actuals versus prior commentary. Management stated that on the prior (Q4) call it had guided for Q1 margin weakness due to the geopolitical situation while remaining confident on the top line. Q1 actuals showed revenue growth of about 28% YoY but operational EBITDA of INR176 mn (3.4% margin) and PAT of INR(318.0) mn, with the exceptional item of INR111.1 mn. Management estimated that absent the disruptions, Q1 EBITDA would have been in the range of INR37–40 crores.

Segment outlook. Management expects Specialty Ingredients and Aroma to be the better-margin verticals in a normalized environment, with Aroma potentially comparable to Specialty once capacity utilization improves. Performance Chemicals is described as a residual business whose net external sales are relatively small (about INR40 crores of the INR175 crores segment revenue, with the balance being internal transfers), and whose margin should improve as diphenol-related negatives cease.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative became markedly more cautious: the first report was a Buy with +23.6% predicted upside and multiple growth catalysts, while the last report was an Accumulate with roughly 0% predicted upside. Persistent themes were the slow vanillin ramp-up, Chinese oversupply/pricing pressure, and weak demand; the changed elements were the withdrawal of early optimism around vanillin/CFS revenue visibility and the addition of CFS Europe’s closure as a fresh drag. The later report leaned on a vanillin restart and Blends strength rather than the broad new-product/CFS upside cited initially.

Fears that came true

  • The Q1FY24 miss, negative operating leverage, and forex loss flagged in the first report were followed by a -28.6% actual return on that Buy call, a DISAPPOINTMENT.
  • The slower-than-expected vanillin ramp-up and lack of revenue visibility persisted, as the last report still cited slower ramp-up and only a May’24 campaign restart.
  • Performance Chemicals pricing pressure from Chinese oversupply and weak demand matured into ‘China dumping’ and ‘pricing pressure across the portfolio’ by the last report.
  • The global slowdown/demand risk materialized as a ‘subdued demand environment’ in the last report, keeping the stock under pressure.

Optimism that failed

  • The first report’s expectation that the vanillin plant would generate Rs 300 crore of FY24 sales failed to materialize; the last report still described slower ramp-up and only a new campaign starting in May’24.
