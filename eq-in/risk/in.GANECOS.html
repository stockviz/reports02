GANECOS: Recent Developments and Outlook

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Crude and polymer volatility. The first quarter of FY27 was marked by a complex and rapidly evolving market environment. Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East drove heightened volatility in crude oil prices and downstream polymer markets, affecting demand patterns, pricing dynamics, and customer procurement decisions across several end-use industries. Management noted polymer prices changing almost daily, with crude moving between roughly $80 and $95 within short spans.

Volume decline. Consolidated sales volume fell 11.2%, offsetting a 3.8% quarter-on-quarter rise in production to 42,826 tons. Standalone sales volume declined 13.4% from Q4 FY26. This was attributed to normalization of the elevated demand seen in the previous quarter and softer demand from the textile sector, where higher fiber prices prompted downstream customers to defer purchases.

Textile demand softness. The textile sector pipeline was described as very low and empty during Q1, with demand challenges from the textile industry.

FSSAI approval timing. The 22,500 TPA rPET granules facility at Warangal has commenced production but awaits FSSAI approval for food-grade applications. Documentary audits are complete; the physical audit was expected in the then-current month. A substantial change in FSSAI officials, including the CEO, delayed file movement. Management indicated a procedural timeline of 1 to 1.5 months per line.

Other income decline. Standalone other income fell to ₹3.52 crores from ₹9.86 crores due to discontinuation of interest income following conversion of subsidiary loans into equity at the end of the last quarter, weighing on PAT despite higher standalone EBITDA.

Competitive pressure. Management stated competitive pressure arrived last year as many new plants came online while demand fell amid confusion over the industry mandate. Current industry capacity is about 4.2 lakh tons against a mandate requirement of roughly 5.5–6 lakh tons, with actual output lower than nameplate capacity.

Export volatility. Export contribution fluctuates month-on-month due to geopolitical uncertainty and volatile ocean freight, ranging from about 5% to 20%, averaging around 10%.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Stronger financial performance. Despite external volatility, Q1 FY27 delivered consolidated EBITDA of ₹59.8 crores and a bottom line of ₹29.03 crores, up 14.2% and 25.1% sequentially. EBITDA margins improved to 14.1% from 12.4%, and PAT margins improved by 138 basis points. Standalone EBITDA rose 13.7% sequentially to ₹23.8 crores. On a year-on-year basis, standalone revenue rose 18.4%, EBITDA 155.9%, and net profits 79.4%.

rPET demand and mandate. Government mandates are driving increasing adoption of rPET, with demand currently exceeding supply. Current national adoption is estimated at 20–25% against a 40% mandate. Management sees substantial room for demand growth and no demand challenges for rPET.

Capacity expansion. The Warangal rPET granules facility has commenced production, serving export markets and domestic non-food applications. Another 22,500-ton line is underway, alongside debottlenecking to take total capacity from about 65,000 tons to 100,000 tons. The new 22,500-ton capacity is expected by December–January, with 10,000–12,000 tons from debottlenecking available upon completion without requiring FSSAI approval. Management expects operating leverage to improve substantially.

Textile demand revival. Fiber demand is reviving well, and the textile industry is described as running decently well after a low pipeline. Management expects better volumes in the current quarter and stated the trend has already started.

Market growth. India’s PET market is growing with a 10% CAGR in bottle production, and stringent EPR rules require 40% recycled content in PET bottles in FY27. The rPET industry is projected to grow to about 10 lakh tons by 2030 (at a 50% mandate), with the company targeting around 25% market share.

Value-added mix. Management targets value-added products contributing ~65% of revenue versus 40% currently, with a wider basket of functional fibers in RPSF and filament segments.

New recycling avenues. The company is working on polyolefin recycling and evaluating other materials, with a focus on rPET for now and other recycling avenues after a 3–4 year timeframe. Textile waste (post-industrial) is already being consumed at 20–25% on average, with cost savings, and could reach 50–55% for some products.

Customer position. Management stated it holds the highest market share in rPET supplies with major global brand owners and is facing higher demand than its capacity can serve, which motivated converting a greenfield project to brownfield.

3. Key Risks

  • Price volatility: Sharp swings in crude, polymer, feedstock, and finished goods prices make short-term guidance impractical; inventory gains or losses occur only on sharp upticks or downturns.
  • Demand deferral: Higher fiber prices can prompt downstream customers to defer purchases, as seen in Q1.
  • Regulatory timing: FSSAI approval delays affect the timing of domestic food-grade commercialization; the new 22,500-ton line’s full ramp could shift between the March quarter and April depending on approval.
  • Competitive intensity: New capacity additions and prior demand confusion created a difficult prior year; supply and demand are currently described as nearly matched.
  • Export and freight uncertainty: Geopolitical uncertainty and volatile sea freight affect export volumes and market mix.
  • Raw material availability: Textile waste usage is product-dependent and cannot exceed certain percentages in present technology; trade-offs between rPET and recycled textile feedstock are expected over the next 2–3 years.
  • Working capital: Legacy business working capital cycle is 75–90 days; subsidiary business is 45–50 days. The business requires 2–2.5 months of inventory at any time.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

FY27 EBITDA guidance intact. Management reaffirmed guidance of ₹225–250 crores EBITDA for FY27, with ₹70–80 crores from the legacy business and the remainder from subsidiaries. Q1 consolidated EBITDA of ₹59.8 crores represents progress toward this range.

Standalone EBITDA per kg. Management guided ₹70–80 crores full-year standalone EBITDA, equating to roughly ₹7–8 per kg, even as Q1 standalone EBITDA per kg reached about ₹9. Management declined short-term guidance due to raw material and finished goods price volatility but expressed confidence in achieving the full-year guidance.

Subsidiary EBITDA margin. Management guided a combined subsidiary EBITDA of ₹16–20 per kg long term, rather than sustaining the Q1 level of ₹22–24 per kg, which benefited from both filament yarn ramp-up and inventory gains.

Volume growth. Management guided 20%+ revenue growth in volume terms, not realization, given volatile sales prices. Q1 saw a volume decline, but management expects sequential improvement and better volumes in the current quarter, with textile demand already reviving.

Capacity utilization. Management targets about 85% utilization of the current 64,500-ton capacity by year-end, up from 72%, implying roughly 55,000 tons of production. The fourth rPET line is not yet fully utilized.

Revenue guidance. Consolidated turnover guidance for the current financial year is ₹1,700–1,800 crores, with FY28 consolidated guidance of ₹2,300–2,500 crores. Management stated it is on track.

Capex. About ₹150 crores is planned for the next 22,500-ton line, with most capex already done (approximately 60%) and the remainder over the next 2–3 months. Next year’s expansion plans are being finalized.
