HGINFRA: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Sharp standalone earnings decline in Q1 FY27. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, standalone revenue from operations was Rs. 9,072.36 million, compared with Rs. 17,092.43 million in the same quarter a year earlier. Standalone profit before exceptional items and tax fell to Rs. 64.75 million from Rs. 1,673.77 million, and standalone profit after tax fell to Rs. 282.72 million from Rs. 1,254.69 million. Standalone operating margin declined to 8.49% in Q1 FY27 from 13.79% in Q1 FY26, and net profit margin declined to 3.12% from 7.34%.

Consolidated loss in Q1 FY27. Consolidated revenue from operations was Rs. 11,005.85 million, down from Rs. 14,822.02 million a year earlier. Consolidated profit after tax was a loss of Rs. 445.18 million, compared with a profit of Rs. 992.64 million in Q1 FY26. Consolidated basic and diluted EPS was negative Rs. 6.83 for the quarter, against positive Rs. 15.23 a year earlier.

Exceptional items from SPV divestments. The company is executing the divestment of five wholly owned subsidiaries approved by the Board on August 13, 2025. In the quarter ended June 30, 2026, the company transferred its remaining 51% in H.G. Khammam Devarapalle PKG-1 on June 9, 2026 (gain of Rs. 19.73 million consolidated), 100% of H.G. Raipur Visakhapatnam OD-6 on April 23, 2026 (loss of Rs. 268.15 million consolidated), and 49% of H.G. Raipur Visakhapatnam OD-5 on June 5, 2026 (loss of Rs. 484.95 million consolidated). A further fair-value loss of Rs. 459.14 million on loss of control of OD-5 and an impairment charge of Rs. 275.33 million relating to H.G. Raipur Visakhapatnam AP-1 were also recorded. These items produced a net consolidated exceptional charge of Rs. 1,467.81 million for the quarter, versus a net exceptional gain of Rs. 510.35 million in the quarter ended March 31, 2026.

CBI/ACB proceedings. The Central Bureau of Investigation, Anti-Corruption Bureau, Patna took four company employees into custody on January 21, 2026 under a FIR invoking Sections 7, 7A, 8 and 9 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988. Searches were conducted at some company offices and at the residence of the Chairman and Managing Director. Cash of Rs. 0.77 million was confiscated from one employee and Rs. 0.93 million from the CMD’s residence (personal funds). Bail was extended to all four employees, and no charge sheet has been filed against the company or its employees. The auditors drew attention to the uncertainty in their review reports, noting the company’s position that there is no impact on the financial results at this stage.

Elevated leverage and coverage pressure. Standalone debt-equity rose to 0.56 times at June 30, 2026 from 0.46 times a year earlier. Standalone debt service coverage ratio fell to 0.37 times from 1.62 times, and interest service coverage ratio fell to 1.18 times from 6.75 times. Consolidated debt-equity was 1.43 times and consolidated interest service coverage was 2.42 times, down from 5.53 times a year earlier.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Diversified segment presence. The company operates across five business segments: Roads & Highways, Railway & Metro, Solar, BESS, and Transmission & Distribution. Consolidated segment reporting identifies two reportable segments — EPC (roads, railways, solar plants) and Renewable (generation and sale of power). Renewable segment revenue was Rs. 602.10 million in Q1 FY27, up from Rs. 110.90 million in Q1 FY26, with segment results of Rs. 350.53 million versus Rs. 59.28 million.

Order book and scale. The order book stood at Rs. 14,502 crore as of June 2026, composed of 69% EPC and 31% HAM projects. The company reports a PAN India presence across 14 states, 36+ active projects, 2,868 modern fleet equipment, and 4,800+ employees.

Historical growth. The company cites historical compound annual growth rates for FY21–26 of 17.5% in revenue, 11.9% in EBITDA, and 13.0% in PAT.

Consolidated EBITDA growth despite revenue decline. Consolidated EBITDA for Q1 FY27 was Rs. 3,038.02 million, up 17.0% year on year, even as consolidated revenue fell 25.7%.

Solar and renewable expansion. The subsidiary list shows extensive incorporation of solar project entities and BESS entities. Two subsidiaries were incorporated during the quarter ended June 30, 2026. The consolidated results include 56 subsidiaries reviewed solely by M S K A & Associates LLP and 19 reviewed solely by Shridhar & Associates, reflecting the scale of the renewable project pipeline.

Balance sheet capacity actions. At the 24th AGM on August 19, 2026, shareholders approved special resolutions to increase the company’s borrowing limits under Section 180(1)(c) and to increase limits for creation of charges and securities on properties/assets under Section 180(1)(a). These resolutions passed with 95.74% and 92.74% of votes in favour respectively, though they drew the largest negative votes of the meeting (4.26% and 7.26% against).

3. Key Risks

Governance and regulatory risk. The CBI/ACB matter remains sub-judice. The company appointed an external firm, on the Audit Committee’s recommendation, to review aspects of the matter. The auditors’ conclusions are not modified in respect of this matter, and the company states there has been no impact on operations or financial position.

Execution and divestment risk. The SPV divestment SPA closing conditions include approval from lenders, customers, and regulatory authorities, and the parties agreed modifications during the current and previous quarters, including achievement of PCOD/COD. The remaining investment in H.G. Raipur Visakhapatnam OD-5 was sold subsequently on July 23, 2026, and the remaining investment in H.G. Khammam Devarapalle PKG-1 was classified as held for sale.

Financial risk. The sharp fall in debt service and interest service coverage ratios, alongside rising debt-equity, indicates reduced headroom relative to prior periods. Consolidated net worth declined to Rs. 32,193.14 million at June 30, 2026 from Rs. 32,650.07 million at March 31, 2026.

Market and operational risk. The company’s own risk disclosure cites exposure to economic conditions, competition, strategy implementation, growth execution, technology changes, revenue and cash flow variability, and market preferences.

Forward-looking statement risk. The company states that matters discussed in its presentation may contain forward-looking statements regarding market opportunity and business prospects, and that it assumes no obligation to update any forward-looking information.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance posture. The company’s stated forward-looking framework references market opportunity and business prospects but explicitly assumes no obligation to update forward-looking information. No specific quantitative guidance figures are provided in the supplied material.

Observed performance versus prior-year levels. The Q1 FY27 results show standalone revenue down 46.9% year on year, standalone EBITDA down 67.3%, and standalone PAT down 77.5%. Consolidated revenue fell 25.7% while consolidated EBITDA rose 17.0%, and consolidated PAT swung to a loss of Rs. 445.18 million. The divergence between standalone and consolidated performance reflects the renewable segment contribution and the exceptional items from SPV divestments.

Order book versus execution. The order book of Rs. 14,502 crore as of June 2026 and the 36+ active projects provide visibility, but Q1 FY27 standalone revenue of Rs. 9,072.36 million represents a sharp sequential decline from Rs. 13,539.20 million in Q4 FY26 and a sharp year-on-year decline, indicating that order book conversion into revenue has slowed in the reported quarter.

Shareholder approval versus dissent. All eight AGM resolutions passed with requisite majority on August 19, 2026. The borrowing-limit and charge-creation resolutions attracted the highest dissent, with 4.26% and 7.26% of votes polled against, and public non-institution shareholders voted 16.98% and 3.57% against those resolutions respectively. The director re-appointment resolutions attracted dissent of 0.09% each overall, with public non-institution shareholders voting 17.71% and 17.83% against the re-appointments of Mr. Vijendra Singh Choudhary as director and Whole Time Director respectively, and 16.28% against Mr. Harendra Singh’s re-appointment as Managing Director.

Dividend. The AGM approved a final dividend of Rs. 2 per equity share of Rs. 10 each for FY26, passed with 99.9999% of votes in favour.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from specific operational and financial risks in 2023—pending appointed dates, equity outlays, debt burden, and working capital lock-up—to more generalized execution challenges involving multiple authorities, design changes, and cost pressures by 2026, while tailwinds shifted from asset monetisation and margin expansion to infrastructure capex, project diversification, and AI. However, the persistent theme of execution delays and working capital build-ups remained unchanged across all reports, correlating with overwhelmingly negative actual returns and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes throughout the period.

Fears that came true

  • The working capital lock-up and debt burden of Rs 5.8bn unbilled revenue and Rs 19bn consolidated debt materialized into recurring working-capital build-ups explicitly cited as a headwind in the 2026 report, aligning with persistent DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Pending appointed dates affecting ~35% of the order book corresponded with chronic execution delays involving multiple authorities, utility shifting, and design changes repeatedly flagged in later reports.
  • The substantial equity capital outlay of Rs 8.76bn across FY24E–FY26E likely strained the balance sheet, contributing to sustained debt burden and negative actual returns across nearly all reports.
  • The 2026 report confirmed materialization of higher commodity, freight, and insurance costs from West Asia-related disruptions, creating near-term FY27 margin pressure that brokers had not originally flagged.

Optimism that failed

  • Robust financial and margin performance was expected to continue, but near-term FY27 margin pressure from cost inflation cited in the last report indicates margin expansion stalled, and actual returns were deeply negative in nearly all reports.
  • Successful asset monetisation via the SPA for 4 HAM projects at 1.55x PBV was expected to release cash for debt reduction, yet consolidated debt remained high and working capital issues persisted, contributing to DISAPPOINTMENT.
  • Strong order book visibility of Rs 126bn and 22–23% YoY growth guidance in FY24E did not translate into positive stock returns, as actual outcomes were DISAPPOINTMENT in 12 of 15 reports.
  • Strategic non-road diversification targeting 30–35% order book contribution did not meaningfully offset persistent road-sector execution challenges, as headwinds continued to dominate across all subsequent reports.
  • The strong order pipeline of Rs 80bn in bids awaiting opening failed to convert into realized value, as the stock experienced deeply negative returns throughout the coverage period.
