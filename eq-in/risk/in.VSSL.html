AI Summary

asof: 2026-09-17

Headwinds and Challenges

  • Raw material price and foreign exchange volatility are identified as pressures on the business.
  • Trade route disruptions arising from the Middle East conflict are affecting sourcing and logistics.
  • Rupee depreciation is making imports costlier.
  • Demand for products is described as softening in the near to medium term.
  • FY26 revenue from operations declined 0.57% year-on-year to ₹1,754.43 crore, which the company attributes mainly to a decline in prices.
  • The company’s revenue mix is currently almost entirely automotive (~100%), leaving it exposed to a single end-market until diversification progresses.
  • Meaningful contribution from non-automotive sectors is expected only over 5–7 years.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Domestic demand is supported by GDP growth, and government preferences for green steel plus the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism are seen as opening European markets.
  • Debottlenecking capacity expansions are underway: the Kocks Block was commissioned in April 2025, and a new reheating furnace was commissioned in March 2026, expanding finished rolling capacity to 2.7 lakh MTPA.
  • A solar power plant has been commissioned, contributing to lower energy costs and supporting sustainability objectives.
  • The Greenfield special steel facility (5 lakh MTPA) is under development, with commissioning targeted for FY30.
  • A forging and machining plant in collaboration with Aichi Steel Corporation is planned. The foundation stone for the forging unit at Village Mangarh, Kohara, Chandigarh Road, Ludhiana was laid on 27 July 2026. Total committed investment is ₹1,116 crore across two phases; the first forging line is targeted for commissioning by March 2028, with a second line to follow based on customer demand. The project is expected to generate direct employment for more than 300 people.
  • The forging facility will produce Ring Gears and other precision-forged automotive components for OEMs in India and international markets including Europe, ASEAN, Africa, and Mexico, using Aichi Steel’s rolling mill forging technology.
  • Closed-loop scrap recycling initiatives are cited as a tailwind.
  • The company targets a diversified portfolio of ~70% auto / 25–30% non-auto revenue by FY35, with entry across eight target sectors including railways, oil & gas, defence, bearing, aerospace, tool & die steel, windmill/ship shafts, and nuclear.
  • Import substitution opportunity: ~1 million tonnes of specialty steel imports against ~6 million tonnes of domestic demand, and ~₹1,000 crore of annual tool & die steel imports.
  • Forging is positioned as a value-add engine, with blended EBITDA/tonne re-rating from ₹8–11k to ₹9–12k targeted within two years, scaling to 4–5 lines by FY35.

Key Risks

  • Forward-looking statements are subject to known and unknown risks, uncertainties, and assumptions, including the performance of the Indian and international economies, industry performance, competition, ability to implement strategy, future growth and expansion, technological changes, and changes in revenue, income, or cash flows.
  • The forging and Greenfield projects carry execution and commissioning timing risk (first forging line targeted March 2028; Greenfield targeted FY30).
  • Peak debt/equity is guided at ≤0.75x, with ~₹2,600 crore committed capex for FY26–30 across steel and forging, balanced between debt and equity.
  • The company operates in a single operating segment (“Manufacturing of Steel products”), concentrating risk.
  • Government grant recognition under Ind AS 20 depends on satisfaction of recognition conditions; incentives beyond those already recognized will be considered only post satisfaction of those conditions.

Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Management stated FY27 commenced on a strong note with healthy growth in production, revenues, and profitability, driven by sustained demand across key customer segments, improved operating efficiencies, and better realizations following price hikes received from OEMs.
  • Q1 FY27 observed performance: revenue from operations ₹486.01 crore (up 12.06% YoY), EBITDA ₹68.29 crore (up 73.63% YoY), PAT ₹41.19 crore (up 107.01% YoY), volumes 59,103 tonnes vs 55,574 tonnes in Q1 FY26. EBITDA per tonne was ₹10,764 excluding non-operational income (income on unutilized funds invested by ASC and interest on advance deposit with PSPCL).
  • FY26 observed performance: revenue ₹1,754.43 crore (down 0.57%), EBITDA ₹208.82 crore (up 17.87%), PAT ₹122.02 crore (up 31.08%), volumes 225,620 tonnes vs 215,843 tonnes (rolled products only) in FY25. EBITDA per tonne was ₹9,255; excluding non-operational income, ₹8,598. The Board recommended a dividend of ₹3.50 per share.
  • Management guides EBITDA per tonne to average between ₹8,000 and ₹11,000. FY26 EBITDA/tonne of ₹9,255 (or ₹8,598 excluding non-operational income) and Q1 FY27 EBITDA/tonne of ₹10,764 sit within this range.
  • Management aims to reach sales of 320,000 to 350,000 tonnes in three years, against FY26 volumes of 225,620 tonnes.
  • Management targets about 20,000 tonnes from the non-automotive steel business.
  • Management plans to commission a new Greenfield steel plant by FY30.
  • The company has guided peak debt/equity of ≤0.75x and committed capex of ~₹2,600 crore for FY26–30.
  • The balance sheet was debt-free as of 31 March 2026 with a net debt-to-equity ratio of 0.00x, following a ₹385 crore equity infusion by Aichi Steel Corporation. Equity rose to ₹1,277.32 crore as on 31 March 2026 from ₹797.91 crore a year earlier.
  • Aichi Steel Corporation raised its equity stake to 24.90% in June 2025.
  • The Board approved a ₹475 crore investment in December 2025 for a new steel forging and machining facility in Ludhiana, and in April 2025 approved ₹2,000 crore for a greenfield steel plant.
  • Management expects the Kocks Block and new reheating furnace to strengthen manufacturing capabilities, improve product quality and operational efficiency, and expand finished rolling capacity to 2.7 lakh MTPA, with the solar plant lowering energy costs.
  • Management states the strategic investments of the past year are translating into tangible operational benefits and that the company remains focused on executing its long-term growth strategy, including the 5 lakh MTPA Greenfield special steel facility and the forging and machining plant with Aichi Steel.
