Blue Jet Healthcare — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Contrast media revenue recognition and logistics. In Q1 FY27, contrast media revenues declined sequentially. Management attributed this to revenue being recognised only when product reaches the customer location under certain commercial terms, with transit delays caused by non-availability of containers and longer transit times amid the current geopolitical situation. Goods in transit at quarter-end were higher by about INR30 crores compared to the opening cut-off, and this is the sales expected to be recognised in the next quarter. Q4 FY26 was also described as an exceptional quarter for contrast media due to strong customer off-take, making the sequential comparison less favourable.

Raw material inflation. Raw material prices increased significantly across the board after the geopolitical situation, specifically from March. This partially impacted Q1 FY27 performance because material consumed was largely from purchases made in previous quarters. Management stated that raw material pricing trends will be uncertain and difficult to forecast in the current scenario. No price increase clause was triggered in Q1 FY27; being long-term contracts, the company is watching for raw material prices to stabilise and will evaluate pass-through on a case-by-case basis. Rupee devaluation was noted as offsetting some price increases.

Gross margin pressure. Reported gross margin of 53% was down 3% versus the previous quarter, partly from raw material price increases and partly from product mix.

Customer concentration and lumpiness in PI/API. The PI/API segment’s sharp jump this quarter followed a prior period of customer destocking. Management was asked whether the segment would remain volatile or lumpy; the response pointed to both secular end-market growth and a strong order book, with visibility for the next three to four quarters. The company declined to comment on specific customer or geographic developments (including potential Japan supplies) and on competitor developments such as another custom synthesis player signing long-term iodine-based contrast media contracts.

Peptide competition. Management acknowledged that many CDMO companies are investing in peptides, creating a competitive environment over the next three to four years. Blue Jet’s stated approach is to focus first on peptide fragments rather than end peptides, avoiding the front-end generic peptide opportunity where price erosion is significant.

Asset turn normalisation. Current asset turns were described as elevated because of a depreciated asset block; once new capitalisation occurs, asset turns are expected to be at par with industry standards.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Improving business momentum. Q1 FY27 was described as an encouraging start, with revenue from operations of INR293 crores (up 25% over the previous quarter) and EBITDA of INR98 crores. Operating EBITDA margin was 33.5% versus 30.4% in the previous quarter, attributed to operating leverage on higher sales. PAT margin was 26.7% versus 27.4% previously, with the prior quarter boosted by higher other income including forex gains.

PI vertical normalisation. Growth was driven by the PI and API segment, with supply of an intermediate restarted after customer destocking in prior quarters. Management believes this performance can be sustained for FY27 given order visibility in that molecule. The plant is running consistently, and the order book is described as strong with visibility for the next three to four quarters. Bulk of PI segment performance is expected from the existing commercial portfolio, with some contribution from new opportunities in trial/validation quantities.

Contrast media stability and new launches. Contrast media and high-intensity sweeteners were described as providing stability and diversification. The overall contrast media segment is doing well with increasing usage across geographies and stable off-take backed by long-term arrangements. During FY27, the company expects to launch three candidates within the contrast media segment. Commercial batches of iodinated contrast media intermediate are expected to start shortly, with positive trends by end of Q2 or beginning of Q3.

Mahad backward integration (Unit 3). The project is establishing a globally scaled manufacturing facility for strategically important contrast media intermediates, positioning Blue Jet among the most vertically integrated players in the global contrast media value chain. Commissioning is expected later this calendar year — ahead of the earlier H2 guidance. About INR210 crores has been invested, with a further INR40 crores committed in the next couple of months. Beyond internal requirements, there is an opportunity to supply select upstream products to third-party customers. The facility is a flexible line that can make derivatives beyond APD CPT. Once commercial, more than INR200 crores is expected to be added to the asset block.

Vizag expansion. Approximately 100 acres have been secured for long-term expansion. Phase 1 includes expanding contrast media manufacturing footprint, a multipurpose finishing block for flexible commercial opportunities including potential lateral entries, capacity for a new high-intensity sweetener under pilot validation, and a multipurpose intermediate cluster. Phase 1 represents an investment of approximately INR1,000 crores over three years. Consent to establish has been received, and engineering and pre-construction activities are progressing. A significant part of Phase 1 capacity is planned against identified customer opportunities. The facility is designed for multiple chemistry platforms including high-potency manufacturing, and is planned to include capacity for peptides and peptide intermediates. Commercialisation is expected from end FY29 to FY30.

Hyderabad R&D centre. Expected to become operational during the current month (August 2026), with critical scientific talent already onboarded. It is designed to strengthen CDMO capabilities across peptides, GLP-1 intermediates, biocatalysts, continuous manufacturing and flow synthesis, and complex synthetic pathways. Management noted significant RFPs coming on new platforms that the company previously did not have.

Development pipeline. From approximately 20 high-conviction NCE opportunities tracked, visibility exists for four programs in the chronic therapy space progressing through customer development. Two of these are expected to fructify in FY27 or FY28, described as interesting and significant opportunities with innovators. Therapeutic areas include oncology and CNS, with another category not disclosable due to CDAs. Two lateral entries into commercial, mature, blockbuster products were referenced, with more definitive comment expected next quarter.

QIP completion. The successful completion of the QIP was described as an important milestone that strengthens the balance sheet and provides financial flexibility to accelerate long-term strategy.

Sustainability credentials. Approximately 70% of energy consumption is sourced through renewable energy including wind and solar. The company received the EcoVadis Silver Medal and previously announced the CII National Award for Excellence in Energy Management. Management described ESG maturity as a must-have and part of the pre-qualification process, helping speed up pre-qualification and simplifying audits.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical and logistics disruption: container non-availability and longer transit times affected revenue recognition; raw material pricing trends are uncertain and difficult to forecast.
  • Raw material cost pass-through timing: contracts carry a lag of a few quarters for pass-through of raw material cost variance; no price increase clause was triggered in Q1 FY27.
  • Revenue recognition timing: goods-in-transit cut-off effects can shift revenue between quarters, and the September quarter closing goods-in-transit cannot be predicted.
  • Customer concentration and program dependency: PI/API performance is tied to order visibility in specific molecules; new programs depend on innovator development and commercialisation timelines, which are difficult to predict even for innovator companies.
  • Competitive intensity in peptides: multiple Indian CDMO companies are investing in the peptides space over the next few years.
  • Confidentiality constraints: CDAs limit disclosure on customer registrations, molecules and programs, constraining visibility for external assessment.
  • Capex execution and ramp-up: Vizag commercialisation is expected from end FY29 to FY30, with ramp-up potentially extending to FY31 or FY32; asset turns are expected to normalise to industry standards after capitalisation.
  • Regulatory and approval dependencies: re-appointments of the Executive Chairman and Managing Director and the statutory auditor re-appointment are subject to shareholder approval at the ensuing AGM; the special resolutions also seek approval for remuneration exceeding Regulation 17(6)(e) limits if applicable.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Mahad commissioning timing. Management had earlier guided H2 for commissioning of the Unit 3 backward integration project; on the Q1 FY27 call, management stated the project is slightly ahead of schedule, with commissioning expected later this calendar year and commercial contribution expected during H2 FY27.

PI/API destocking. Management had previously indicated the PI intermediate business was impacted by customer destocking over the last few quarters. In Q1 FY27, supply restarted and the segment drove growth; management believes this performance can be sustained for FY27 given order visibility.

Contrast media revenue recognition. Management had previously indicated that revenues from certain customers are recognised only when product reaches the customer location. In Q1 FY27, this was reflected in higher goods in transit (about INR30 crores higher than opening) and lower recognised sales; the corresponding sales are expected to be recognised in the next quarter.

Iodinated contrast media intermediate. Management had indicated commercial batches were pending; on the Q1 FY27 call, management stated commercial batches are expected to start very shortly, with positive trends by end of Q2 or beginning of Q3.

Capex guidance. For FY27, management indicated expected capital expenditure of approximately INR250 crores, within a three-year expected capex of INR1,000-odd crores. When asked whether the FY27 capex guidance of INR400 crores was maintained, the CFO stated that at the company level it will be a similar amount.

New high-intensity sweetener. Management confirmed pilot activities are happening at the existing facility, with real scale-up to happen from Vizag. On whether some revenue would start from the existing facility, management indicated that should happen.

Lateral entries. Management had previously referenced two molecules with lateral entry and dedicated blocks at Vizag. On the Q1 FY27 call, management stated it would be better to make a definitive comment in the next quarter, while confirming the products are commercial, mature-phase and blockbusters in the chronic space.

Peptides and GLP-1 RFP quantification. When asked to quantify how many of the 20 high-conviction RFPs relate to peptides and GLP-1, management stated it intends to give this clarity in the coming quarters.

Asset turns. Management noted current asset turns of about 4 to 5x are elevated due to a depreciated asset block, and once capitalisation occurs, asset turns will be at par with industry standards; a specific number was not provided, with ramp-up potentially looking at FY31 or FY32.

Dividend and AGM. The Board recommended a final dividend of INR1.20 per equity share for FY2025-26 at its meeting on May 25, 2026, with a record date of September 14, 2026. The 58th AGM is scheduled for September 21, 2026 at 11:00 A.M. IST through VC/OAVM.

Broker Narrative

The first report centered on BlueJet’s contrast-media leadership, new Guerbet contracts, and a sharp bempedoic acid intermediate ramp, while flagging Chinese sweetener competition and customer concentration. By the last report, the narrative had shifted to a post-destocking Pharma Intermediates rebound, GLP-1/peptides, and the Vizag greenfield project, with contrast media still central but now framed around delayed validations, future launches, and margin-expanding backward integration. Persistent themes included long-standing customer relationships, backward integration/captive APD, and value-chain advancement; sweetener diversification and the oncology/Parkinson’s pipeline dropped out of the bull case.

Fears that came true

  • Rising competitive intensity in contrast media intermediates materialized as FY26 growth was driven mainly by existing products/customers while new validations, commercial launches, and Japanese customer engagements remained in qualification stage, aligning with the DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes on the 2025 Buy calls.
  • Dependence on market development for new NCE molecules proved slow: Gadopiclenol/gadolinium NCE opportunities were still framed as future revenue potential rather than delivered growth by the last report.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected sharp rise in PI/API revenue from bempedoic acid intermediate supplies to Esperion failed to materialize on schedule because customer destocking and inventory normalization delayed order flows through FY26.
  • The Guerbet iodinated ABA HCL and Gadopiclenol NCE contracts did not translate into visible near-term growth, as the last report showed contrast media new validations/launches still in qualification and only expected in FY27.
  • The valuation discount cited as a margin of safety did not protect investors, as the 2025 Motilal Oswal Buy calls produced actual returns of -29.4%, -43.0%, and -29.3%.
