iValue Infosolutions — Q1 FY27 Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Sequential decline in gross sales. Gross sales fell 14.5% QoQ to ₹641.2 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹749.7 crore in Q4 FY26, even as they rose 5.7% YoY.
  • Sharp QoQ drop in profitability. Gross profit declined 44.7% QoQ (₹94.0 crore to ₹52.0 crore), operating EBITDA fell 65.6% QoQ (₹58.9 crore to ₹20.2 crore), and PAT fell 63.1% QoQ (₹42.3 crore to ₹15.7 crore). Gross margin contracted 442 bps QoQ to 8.1% on a gross basis and 554 bps to 28.9% on a net basis.
  • Net revenue decline. Revenue from operations (net basis) was ₹179.7 crore, down 21.1% YoY and 34.1% QoQ.
  • Segment mix pressure. Information Lifecycle Management declined 60.4% YoY and fell from 33.2% to 12.5% of gross sales. Cybersecurity, the largest segment at 43.9% of gross sales, grew a modest 8.1% YoY.
  • Other income weakness. Operating other income fell 51.0% YoY to ₹2.8 crore.
  • Working capital intensity. Net working capital days stood at 52 days, with trade receivables of ₹925.7 crore against trade payables of ₹747.2 crore.
  • Leadership transition. Mr. Shrikanth Manohar Shitole resigned as Chief Executive Officer effective from the close of business on July 31, 2026.
  • Shareholder exit. Sundara (Mauritius) Limited sold 5,698,416 shares (10.43% of share capital) in the open market on June 30, 2026, ending with nil holding. The company stated the transaction does not result in any change in control.
  • Competitive and demand risks. The safe harbour disclosure cites the highly competitive market for its services, the risk of customers reducing spending, currency fluctuations, and market conditions in India and elsewhere.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • YoY growth in headline metrics. Gross sales up 5.7% YoY to ₹641.2 crore; gross profit up 26.5% YoY to ₹52.0 crore; operating EBITDA up 27.7% YoY to ₹20.2 crore; PAT up 51.7% YoY to ₹15.7 crore.
  • Margin expansion. Gross margin improved 133 bps YoY to 8.1% (gross basis) and 1,091 bps to 28.9% (net basis); operating EBITDA margin rose 54 bps YoY to 3.2% on gross sales and 431 bps to 11.3% on a net basis; PAT margin rose 74 bps YoY to 2.5% on gross sales and 420 bps to 8.7% on a net basis.
  • Annuity business momentum. Annuity revenue grew 13.7% YoY to ₹297.2 crore and rose to 46.4% of gross sales from 43.1% a year earlier.
  • Data Centre Infrastructure scale-up. The segment grew 182.9% YoY and rose to 22.1% of gross sales from 8.2%.
  • ALM, Cloud and Others growth. The segment grew 46.6% YoY and increased to 21.5% of gross sales from 15.5%.
  • Demand themes. Management cited strengthening demand for cybersecurity, cloud, data management, AI-led infrastructure, and digital resilience solutions as enterprises accelerate technology transformation.
  • AI strategy. iValue is building an enterprise AI ecosystem spanning AI infrastructure, AI data platforms, AI operations, and AI security, including AI-powered SOC architectures, LLM security and AI governance use cases, AI-ready data architectures, GPU infrastructure, sovereign and air-gapped AI for government and regulated sectors, and an AI Centre of Excellence for validation and interoperability.
  • Large-deal execution. Case studies describe a large-scale technology refresh for a mission-critical public platform consolidating 11+ OEMs, a multi-year engagement with India’s largest public sector bank, and a digital infrastructure transformation for a large public sector bank delivered with 10+ global technology vendors.
  • Ecosystem scale. 115 OEM partners, 2,914 partners signed up, 474 enterprise customers using 2+ OEM products, 80%+ partner repeat rate, and presence across 10 countries.
  • Historical track record. Gross sales grew from ₹526 crore in FY21 to ₹2,914 crore in FY26, with FY26 gross margin of 9.1%, EBITDA margin of 5.1%, and PAT margin of 3.5% — described as the highest among pure-play distributors and VADs.
  • Balance sheet strength. Cash position adjusted for debt of ₹121 crore and adjusted ROCE of 17.9%.

3. Key Risks

  • Demand concentration and cyclicality. The safe harbour notes the level of market demand for services and the risk that customers may reduce spending.
  • Competitive intensity. The market for the types of services offered is described as highly competitive.
  • Execution and expansion risk. Risks include the ability to create, acquire and build new businesses and grow existing ones.
  • Talent risk. The ability to attract and retain qualified personnel is cited as a risk.
  • Currency and macro risk. Currency fluctuations and market conditions in India and elsewhere are flagged.
  • Working capital risk. Elevated trade receivables (₹925.7 crore) relative to payables (₹747.2 crore) and 52 net working capital days expose the business to collection and liquidity risk; bad debts written off were ₹25.89 million in FY26 and allowance for expected credit loss on trade receivables was ₹32.64 million in FY26.
  • Segment concentration. Cybersecurity at 43.9% of gross sales creates dependence on a single segment.
  • Governance and disclosure risk. Forward-looking statements are subject to known and unknown risks, uncertainties and assumptions difficult to predict, and the company assumes no obligation to update them.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Management commentary (Q1 FY27). Chairman and Managing Director Sunil Pillai described a “stable start to FY27,” citing resilience of the business model, growing relevance of the Technology Advisor proposition, and strengthening demand for cybersecurity, cloud, data management, AI-led infrastructure, and digital resilience. He noted continued deepening of engagement with customers, OEM partners, system integrators, and service providers, expansion of solution capabilities, and strengthening of the leadership team and organizational capabilities.
  • CFO commentary (Q1 FY27). Swaroop Muvvala stated gross profit of ₹52 crore (up 26.5% YoY, 8.1% margin), operating EBITDA before exceptional item of ₹20.2 crore (up 27.7% YoY), and PAT of ₹15.7 crore (up 51.7% YoY), attributing profitability improvement to gross profit growth and operational frugality. He stated focus on scaling higher-value solutions and services, increasing annuity-led revenue contribution, improving profitability, enhancing shareholder value, and delivering consistent financial performance.
  • Observed performance against commentary. The stated focus on annuity is reflected in annuity revenue growing 13.7% YoY to 46.4% of gross sales. The stated focus on higher-value solutions is reflected in Data Centre Infrastructure growing 182.9% YoY and ALM, Cloud and Others growing 46.6% YoY. Profitability improvement is reflected in gross profit, operating EBITDA, and PAT growing faster than gross sales on a YoY basis. However, the sequential decline in gross sales, gross profit, operating EBITDA, and PAT, along with the 21.1% YoY decline in net revenue from operations, contrasts with the characterization of a “stable start” and the stated goal of “consistent financial performance.”

  • Investor meeting.   • AGM outcomes. The 18th Annual General Meeting held on August 19, 2026 approved all six items of business with the requisite majority, including adoption of financial statements and re-appointment of directors Krishna Raj Sharma, Nagendra Venkaswamy, Sumith Ramrao Kamath, Kalpana Rangamani, and Kabir Kishin Thakur; results were scrutinized by Padmavathi & Vijayesh Associates LLP.
  • Board actions. The Board approved unaudited standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, reappointed M/s. S G S K & Company as Internal Auditors for FY 2026-27, and approved allotment of 1,23,280 equity shares of face value Rs. 2/- each under the iValue Employee Stock Option Plan 2024, increasing paid-up share capital to Rs. 10,95,07,320.
