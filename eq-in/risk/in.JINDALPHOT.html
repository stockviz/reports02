Headwinds and Challenges

  • Voluntary delisting process: The company held a postal ballot for approval of voluntary delisting of its equity shares from BSE Limited and NSE. Voting ran from July 20, 2026 (9:00 a.m. IST) to August 18, 2026 (5:00 p.m. IST). The special resolution was passed with the requisite majority under the Companies Act, 2013 and Regulation 11(4) of the SEBI (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2021.
  • MCCL compensation dispute: The Nominated Authority initially passed a claim of Rs. 22,279 Lakhs in favour of Mandakini Coal Company Limited (MCCL), the company’s joint venture, of which Jindal Photo is entitled to one-third (Rs. 7,426 Lakhs). The Nominated Authority subsequently proposed to reduce this compensation to Rs. 15,519 Lakhs. Jindal Photo and Tata Power Company Limited filed writ petitions before the Delhi High Court against this proposed reduction, and a status quo order has been granted.
  • Pending appeal for balance compensation: MCCL filed an appeal before the Coal Bearing Tribunal, Talcher, for an additional Rs. 13,361 Lakhs against land compensation purchased directly from land owners (Jindal Photo’s one-third share being Rs. 4,453 Lakhs). This appeal remains pending.
  • IFCI claim: IFCI lodged a claim before the Nominated Authority towards its loan to MCCL. To stall those proceedings, Jindal Photo and Tata Power filed writ petitions before the Delhi High Court, in which a status quo order has been passed.
  • Non-provisioning of doubtful loans and MCCL recoverables: The statutory auditor’s limited review reports (standalone and consolidated) include an Emphasis of Matter drawing attention to Notes 3(c) and 3(d), relating to non-provision of doubtful loans and non-provision of amounts recoverable from MCCL, because petitions and claims are pending finalisation/settlement.
  • Finance costs: Finance cost of Rs. 104 Lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2026, against total income of Rs. 797 Lakhs, remains a significant expense relative to revenue.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Return to quarterly profitability: For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, standalone profit before tax was Rs. 677 Lakhs and profit for the period was Rs. 667 Lakhs, compared with a loss of Rs. 103 Lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 and a loss of Rs. 90 Lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2025.
  • Consolidated profitability: Consolidated profit for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was Rs. 1,308 Lakhs, including the group’s share of net profit of joint venture and associate of Rs. 641 Lakhs, compared with a consolidated loss of Rs. 566 Lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026.
  • Fair value gains: Net gain on fair value changes was Rs. 789 Lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2026, versus Rs. 47 Lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 and Rs. 54 Lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2025.
  • Dividend income: Dividend income of Rs. 1,046 Lakhs was recorded for the year ended March 31, 2026.
  • Potential MCCL compensation: If the compensation claims are settled favourably, Jindal Photo’s one-third share of the originally approved Rs. 22,279 Lakhs claim would be Rs. 7,426 Lakhs, with an additional potential one-third share of Rs. 4,453 Lakhs from the pending appeal.
  • Associate performance: The consolidated results include the group’s share of net profit after tax of Rs. 642 Lakhs and total comprehensive income of Rs. 644 Lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, in respect of one associate (Jindal India Powertech Limited), based on reports of other auditors.

Key Risks

  • Delisting risk: The voluntary delisting of equity shares from both BSE and NSE, approved by shareholders via postal ballot, means the shares will cease to be listed and traded on these exchanges.
  • MCCL compensation reduction risk: The proposed reduction of MCCL’s compensation from Rs. 22,279 Lakhs to Rs. 15,519 Lakhs, if upheld, would reduce Jindal Photo’s one-third share from Rs. 7,426 Lakhs to approximately Rs. 5,173 Lakhs.
  • Non-provisioning risk: The auditor’s Emphasis of Matter highlights that no provision has been made for doubtful loans and amounts recoverable from MCCL. If these claims are not settled favourably, the company may need to recognise provisions, which could materially impact financial results.
  • IFCI loan claim risk: IFCI’s claim before the Nominated Authority towards its loan to MCCL could affect the compensation ultimately receivable by MCCL and, in turn, Jindal Photo’s share.
  • Dependence on associate and joint venture performance: Consolidated results are significantly influenced by the group’s share of profit/loss from Jindal India Powertech Limited (associate) and MCCL (joint venture). The joint venture’s interim financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 were not reviewed by their auditors.
  • Limited revenue diversity: Revenue from operations is primarily composed of interest income, dividend income, and net gain on fair value changes, with no other income recorded in the quarter ended June 30, 2026.
