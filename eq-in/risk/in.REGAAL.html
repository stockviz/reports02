Regaal Resources — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Deliberate revenue contraction. Operating income fell 18.0% year-on-year to ₹2,021.5 million in Q1 FY27 (from ₹2,465.7 million in Q1 FY26) and 17.4% quarter-on-quarter. Management attributes this to a deliberate reduction in low-margin trading activity, with trading contribution falling to 3.3% of the mix from 19.5% a year earlier.

Commissioning-related disruption. The doubled crushing capacity (825 TPD to 1,650 TPD), the new Liquid Glucose (180 TPD) and Maltodextrin Powder (50 TPD) facilities, and the expanded captive power plant (7.1 MW to 15.8 MW) were commissioned on 26 May 2026, with operations beginning in early June. Integration required roughly nine planned shutdown days in April–May, costing an estimated 7,200 tonnes of crushing volume. Q1 capacity utilization was 71.4%.

Elevated working capital. The cash conversion cycle widened to 130 days (annualized) at 30 June 2026, versus 50 days at 31 March 2026, driven by inventory days of 208 (annualized) as maize was procured ahead of the expanded capacity’s revenue contribution. Payable days also rose to 139. Management notes inventory days are seasonally higher in Q1/Q2 and that this effect is more pronounced this year given the larger crushing capacity.

Rising leverage. Net debt stood at ₹7,353.2 million at 30 June 2026, up from ₹5,456.5 million at 31 March 2026, reflecting expansion funding and seasonal raw material procurement. Total borrowings rose to ₹7,894.1 million. Management expects debt to peak in H1 FY27 and decline in H2, citing a historical pattern where working capital debt falls to roughly 40% of peak levels by Q4.

Input price and market volatility. Maize prices were cited as having moved from around ₹20 to ₹24, with management noting Bihar maize prices typically firm in September before softening in October. Management repeatedly declined to guide on EBITDA per tonne, citing maize and finished-product prices as outside its control. Competitive capacity additions by players such as Gujarat Ambuja and Sanstar were raised by analysts.

Prior-year SGST matter. The financial results note that SGST reimbursement claimed from April 2019 to September 2025 on supplies to certain distributors was found not to be in accordance with a January 2020 Industries Department resolution. Of the amount involved, ₹104.64 million is recoverable/recovered from distributors under agreement clauses, and ₹66.57 million was provided as an exceptional item in FY26. No departmental demand had been received as of the reporting date; management views further material impact as unlikely.

Regulatory filing lapse. On 25 June 2026, the company clarified a discrepancy in its XBRL submission of financial results for the quarter and year ended 31 March 2026 (a missing reserve figure), completed a revised filing, and described the omission as inadvertent and unintentional with no change to other financial data.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Completed capacity transformation. The 26 May 2026 commissioning doubled crushing capacity to 1,650 TPD and added Liquid Glucose and Maltodextrin Powder lines, establishing Regaal as the largest maize wet milling facility in Eastern India. Total project outlay is approximately ₹6,640 million, with about ₹5,520 million incurred as of 30 June 2026.

Margin mix shift. Value-add grew 30.3% year-on-year to ₹805.3 million, with value-add margin expanding 1,477 bps to 39.8%. Operating EBITDA rose 26.6% to ₹309.8 million (margin 15.3%, up 540 bps), and PAT rose 47.0% to ₹133.3 million (margin 6.6%, up 291 bps). Management attributes the improvement primarily to operating leverage and the reduced trading share rather than raw material costs.

Export momentum. Export contribution more than doubled to 10.4% of revenue from 4.9% a year earlier. Management cited new market development, participation in September trade fairs, and geographic positioning near Nepal and Bangladesh borders.

Value-added product pipeline. Planned FY27 entries include Dextrose Anhydrous, Dextrose Monohydrate and Hydrol, with dextrose expected to contribute by Q4 FY27. Modified starch additions planned include cationic starch, carboxymethyl starch, pre-gel starch, gulal and spray starch, with trials underway and full ramp expected by September. Management indicated roughly 70–80% of these products would flow through existing dealer and institutional channels.

Infrastructure additions. Planned additions include 30,000 tonnes of storage across two silos, a 50,000 sq. ft. maize godown, and a 1.6 MW methane gas-based power plant. Captive cogeneration met 85.2% of Q1 FY27 power requirements.

Policy support. Debt raised for the new capex qualifies for interest subvention under Bihar’s Industrial Investment Promotion Policy, with 10% interest subvention and 100% State GST reimbursement cited. Management noted the interest subvention cap was amended from ₹20 crore to ₹40 crore around November–December FY25, but that final policy confirmation is still awaited and the higher cap is not yet reflected in disclosures.

Domestic expansion headroom. Only 0.67% of FY26 revenue came from South India, with stated targeting of Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. White-labelling relationships expanded, with new customers added and existing customers increasing product volumes.

3. Key Risks

  • Price risk: EBITDA per tonne is exposed to maize and finished-product prices, which management states are not within its control.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk: Newly commissioned LG and MDP lines remain in stabilization; MDP customer qualification is expected to take two to three months more, and dextrose is targeted only by Q4 FY27.
  • Working capital and leverage risk: Net debt of ₹7,353.2 million and a 130-day cash conversion cycle leave the balance sheet sensitive to utilization ramp timing; management expects normalization as volumes flow through.
  • Competitive risk: Analyst questions referenced specialty starch capacity additions by Gujarat Ambuja and Sanstar.
  • Policy risk: The revised ₹40 crore interest subvention cap under the Bihar policy is not yet finalized; the company awaits government confirmation.
  • Seasonality and procurement risk: Bihar is described as a single-crop region for the company’s historical procurement pattern, with 80% of inventory procured in the April–July Rabi window and 10–15% planned from Maharashtra, MP or Karnataka.
  • Prior SGST matter: While no demand has been received, the noted non-compliance finding and the ₹66.57 million provision represent a contingent exposure.
  • Commodity/weather and competing demand: Volatile maize prices, weather variability affecting supply, and competing demand from animal feed and ethanol industries were identified as material risks.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Crushing volume. Management guided FY27 crushing above 400,000 tonnes (versus roughly 265,000 tonnes in FY26), describing this as a minimum. Q1 actual was 69,689 MT, up 7.6% year-on-year. Management indicated Q2 could reach 100,000–110,000 tonnes if stabilization proceeds without issues. FY26 actual was 64,770 MT in Q1, implying the FY27 target requires substantial H2 weighting.

Value-added mix. Management guided value-added products rising to 20–22% of turnover in FY27 from 3% in FY26. In a later exchange, management referenced a 35% capacity-level figure and 30–35% of turnover, while declining to give a firm full-year number. Q1 FY27 value-added contribution was not stated as a percentage of turnover in the disclosed figures, though value-add margin reached 39.8%.

Utilization. Management expects utilization to improve progressively from Q2 FY27 and to approach maximum within the year. Q1 actual was 71.4%, with management attributing the level to commissioning and integration shutdowns.

Interest cost. Management guided FY27 net interest cost of approximately ₹39–40 crore, described as broadly flat versus FY26. Q1 FY27 finance cost was ₹79.1 million, down 9.3% year-on-year but up 24.5% quarter-on-quarter.

EBITDA per tonne. Management declined to provide specific guidance, citing price variables outside its control, while stating that on a like-for-like price basis, operating leverage and value-add should drive improvement. Q1 FY27 EBITDA per tonne was cited by an analyst at approximately ₹4,450 versus FY26 at approximately ₹4,742.

Debt trajectory. Management expects debt to peak in H1 FY27 and decline in H2, with working capital debt historically falling to about 40% of peak by Q4. Net debt rose from ₹5,456.5 million at 31 March 2026 to ₹7,353.2 million at 30 June 2026.

Capex. Management stated no new expansion decisions are imminent, with focus on stabilizing the current expansion, reaching 90–95% utilization, marketing the value-added basket, and reducing debt before considering further plans. Analysts referenced management meetings with ministers in Karnataka regarding potential southern capacity; management declined to discuss a next expansion.

Dividend. The Board recommended a final dividend of ₹0.25 per equity share (5% of face value of ₹5) for FY26, with a record date of 16 September 2026 and the 14th AGM scheduled for 23 September 2026. Payment is to occur within 30 days of shareholder approval.
