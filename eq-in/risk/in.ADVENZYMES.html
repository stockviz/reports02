ADVENZYMES: Q1 FY27 Earnings Call and Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Macro and geopolitical environment. Management opened the call by noting that the global economy has been experiencing significant disturbance for some time, driven by geographical tensions, trade uncertainty, and continued disruption across global supply chains. These have directly affected the pricing of energy and certain raw materials, creating a volatile operating environment. On the call, management referenced a tariff issue and an Iran war as factors creating difficulties, alongside challenges in hiring people in the U.S. market over the last two years.

Softer Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue for the quarter stood at INR 1,898 million, up 2% YoY but down 7% sequentially. EBITDA fell 10% YoY and 19% QoQ to INR 510 million, with margin at 27% versus 30% in Q1 FY26 and 31% in Q4 FY26. PAT declined 5% YoY and 15% QoQ to INR 386 million, a 20% margin versus 22% in both comparative periods. Management attributed the margin moderation to three factors: lower top-line realization, elevated power and fuel costs driven by global energy disruption, and a temporary shift in the sales mix.

Sales reversal. An additional sales reversal of INR 100 million over and above the reversal generally accounted for every quarter impacted the quarter. Management explained this relates to goods in transit not yet delivered to the customer, so revenue recognition (which occurs on transfer of control) could not be applied. This revenue has since been recorded, and management stated the revenue pipeline remains intact. Adding the INR 10 crore back to the INR 190 crore base implies roughly INR 200 crore and about 8% growth for the quarter.

Human Nutrition decline. The segment fell 7% YoY and 11% QoQ to INR 1,139 million, primarily on lower sales in the Pharma API business. India sales in the healthcare segment were INR 580 million versus INR 699 million in Q4 FY26 and INR 659 million in Q1 FY26; international sales were INR 559 million versus INR 582 million and INR 552 million respectively. Management described the first quarter as dull, partly because price increases take time to regroup and return to growth.

U.S. business. Management characterized the U.S. situation as not conducive, citing geopolitical issues and a business model transition toward branding products, which is taking time to consolidate operations and customers. FY25 saw roughly 10% growth, while FY26 was down. Management guided to no more than 8–10% growth in the U.S. at this juncture, with about 8% expected by year-end. On currency, management noted the USD declined about 4% QoQ and about 10% YoY in rupee terms.

Subsidiary performance. JC Biotech revenue was INR 195 million with EBITDA of INR 26 million and PAT of INR 10 million, versus INR 211 million, INR 33 million, and INR 14 million in Q1 FY26. evoxx revenue was INR 74 million with negative EBITDA of INR 13 million and negative PAT of about INR 18 million, versus INR 70 million revenue, INR 14 million EBITDA, and INR 7 million PAT in Q1 FY26. SciTech revenue was INR 201 million with EBITDA of INR 31 million and PAT of INR 13 million, versus INR 145 million revenue, INR 4 million EBITDA, and negative PAT of INR 8 million in Q1 FY26.

Non-Food Bio-Processing. The Non-Food business declined 30% YoY and 8% QoQ to INR 43 million.

Animal Nutrition. Revenue declined 3% YoY to INR 252 million, though it grew 1% QoQ.

Rest of the World. Revenue declined 15% YoY and 29% QoQ.

Serratiopeptidase ban news. Asked about a ban reported in the news during the quarter, management stated there would not be any meaningful impact, perhaps 1% here and there, referencing the FDC combination.

Novel food / EFSA approval timing. Management said the application remains under consideration and is a long process, noting some EFSA food filings have been pending since 2014. The expectation is to wait perhaps three to six months, though ultimately it depends on the authorities and questions may recur.

Algae DHA. JC Biotech’s DHA molecule is not being pursued currently. Management indicated it may be revisited once the new R&D facility comes up, and noted JC Biotech faces capacity shortages and is increasing capacity.

Bioenergy. No revenues currently; products remain under development. Management indicated a possible relook after the R&D facility, perhaps next year.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Bio-Processing strength. The segment grew 30% YoY to INR 306 million, contributing 16% of revenue, driven by the Food business which grew 51% YoY to INR 263 million. Sequentially the segment declined 5%.

Specialized Manufacturing momentum. Revenue grew 41% YoY and 11% QoQ to INR 200 million, contributing 11% of revenue.

Europe and Asia growth. Europe grew 44% YoY (and 39% QoQ), with management attributing the growth mainly to the food area. Asia (ex-India) grew 37% YoY. International sales overall rose 9% YoY to INR 961 million and constituted 51% of revenue versus 47% in Q1 FY26.

B2C and branding. Management noted improvement in B2C revenue in the USA. The B2C segment contributed about USD 1.14 million versus USD 1.12 million in the prior-year period. The branding strategy is intended to make business stickier once the company’s name appears on customer labels. The company already operates a successful B2C business in the USA and is replicating the model in India, having launched the nutraceutical brand Wellfa.

Biocatalysis. Revenue was INR 45 million versus INR 44 million in Q4 FY26. Management called it still a high-growth segment and expects good growth in the second half. Products are at advanced plant-level trials with API manufacturers.

New products and pipeline. Management stated 15–20 molecules are always under the pipeline, with a few coming every quarter. Expansion areas cited include biocatalysis, animal feed, ruminant, food beyond baking, and detergents. Protein hydrolysis sales have started in the current quarter.

Patents. Management indicated a U.S. patent was granted on Alternansucrase for sugar management, another on protein was believed granted (to be confirmed), and a third is under process. The sugar management patent is viewed as good for marketing, with a large market potentially tied to GLP-1 studies; the protein patent relates to protein hydrolysis.

Capital expenditure. The AGM referenced a CapEx figure of INR 120–130 crore, confirmed at INR 123 crore, of which about INR 20 crore will stretch into Q2 FY27. About INR 20 crore is normal capital expenditure, INR 50 crore goes to R&D (already under work in progress, expected functional in the next quarter or so), and the balance is growth-related. Fermentation utilization is close to 70–75%, and management may start capacity expansion CapEx in the next quarter.

Buyback and stake consolidation. The Board approved a buyback of INR 697 million at a ceiling price of INR 500 per share via the open market route, with an indicative maximum of 1,394,000 equity shares (1.24% of existing paid-up capital). The buyback was to start August 14. The company also approved acquisition of the remaining 4.28% stake in JC Biotech for INR 79.79 million (886,544 equity shares at INR 90 per share), making it a wholly owned subsidiary, and approved infusion of up to INR 20 million into Advanced Nutrazyme Private Limited.

Dividend. A final dividend of INR 1.35 per equity share for FY26 was approved by shareholders at the AGM held July 31, 2026, and paid on August 5, 2026.

AGM outcomes. All eight resolutions set out in the 37th AGM notice were passed with requisite majority, including adoption of FY26 audited financials, declaration of dividend, re-appointment of Ms. Rasika Rathi, re-appointment of MSKA & Associates LLP as statutory auditors, re-appointment of Mr. Mukund Madhusudan Kabra as Whole-time Director, appointment of Mr. Pradip Bhailal Shah as Independent Director, approval of remuneration of Ms. Manasi Kabra in the USA subsidiary, and approval of material related party transactions with JC Biotech.

FY26 full-year context. Revenue grew 17% to INR 7,458 million, EBITDA grew 18% to INR 2,291 million, and PAT grew 30% to INR 1,736 million. Segment growth for FY26: Human HC +15% to INR 4,674 million, Animal HC +25% to INR 1,176 million, Bio Processing +16% to INR 1,018 million, Specialized Manufacturing +23% to INR 662 million.

3. Key Risks

Customer concentration. Top 10 customers accounted for 23% of revenue this quarter, versus 27% in Q1 FY26 and 26% in Q4 FY26. Top 10 products contributed 45%, versus 45% last year and 49% in the prior quarter.

Dependence on serratiopeptidase. An analyst noted the company’s disproportionate dependence on this product, with overall growth appearing better when it does well and tapering when it drops off significantly. Management declined to share product-level numbers, stating it had requested earlier to stop sharing them.

U.S. client retention. An analyst raised the concern that if the company is unable to ship, end clients may look for alternate sources. Management acknowledged that every business has challenges and that the company must work hard to sustain and achieve sustainable growth while making good money.

Pricing competition. Asked about competitive intensity in the largest product, management said pricing is better this quarter because some prices were increased, and realization is better than Q4 and Q1 of last year, but declined to quantify.

Margin trajectory. An analyst noted margins were in the 40s over the last 10–11 years and have dropped to the 30s. Management said R&D helps increase margins but stated it would like to continue with a 30–32% EBITDA margin expectation.

Working capital. Inventory as of June 30, 2026 was about INR 190 crore, receivables about INR 131 crore, payables about INR 41 crore, with roughly INR 280 crore invested in working capital. The working capital cycle is generally about 125–138 days.

Quarterly variability. Management repeatedly cautioned against quarter-on-quarter comparisons, noting that 70–80% of customers are constant while 20–30% changes, and that quick sales sometimes arrive and sometimes do not. Management advised evaluating the business on an annual horizon.

Regulatory and approval timing. The novel food / EFSA approval timeline is uncertain, with management noting some filings pending since 2014 and dependence on authority responses.

Labour codes. The Government of India notified four Labour Codes on November 21, 2025. The group assessed the incremental impact and presented it under exceptional items. For the quarter ended December 31, 2025, the impact consisted of gratuity of INR 45.67 million and compensated absences of INR 1.51 million; for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, an additional gratuity impact of INR 0.63 million was recognized. For the year ended March 31, 2026, the gratuity impact was INR 46.30 million and compensated absences INR 1.51 million. On a standalone basis, the incremental impact consisted of gratuity of INR 38.58 million.

Litigation reversal. On August 22, 2025, the United States Court of Appeals directed the District of Arizona to pass an order in favour of Advanced Supplementary Technologies Corporation in a lawsuit filed by a competitor. Based on the direction and legal opinion received in the quarter ended December 31, 2025, a provision of INR 159.64 million was reversed. For the quarter ended March 31, 2026, an additional impact of INR 1.75 million was recognized on account of foreign exchange fluctuation. The cumulative impact for the year ended March 31, 2026 was INR 161.39 million.

ESOP dilution. The ESOP Scheme 2022 was approved for 2,500,000 equity shares. The company granted 576,000 options on August 12, 2023 and 512,500 options on March 27, 2025. During the quarter ended June 30, 2026, 49,350 equity shares were allotted to employees. Management clarified that of the 25 lakh scheme, about 11 lakh shares have been granted so far, vesting over five years.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Annual growth guidance. Management stated confidence in delivering sustainable revenue growth consistent with its annual guidance. Asked whether the year could close at 12% plus over the balance three quarters against a projected 13–15% growth over three to five years, management said it believes it should be able to grow in double digits, noting the INR 10 crore sales reversal would come in the next quarter. Adding the reversal to the INR 190 crore base implies about INR 200 crore and roughly 8% growth for the quarter. Management also stated it remains on track.

EBITDA margin guidance. Management guided that EBITDA margin should return to the normal level of about 30%, and separately stated it would like to continue with a 30–32% EBITDA margin expectation. Observed Q1 FY27 margin was 27%, versus 30% in Q1 FY26 and 31% in Q4 FY26.

U.S. growth guidance. Management guided to no more than 8–10% growth in the U.S. at this juncture, with about 8% expected by the end of the year. Observed Americas revenue declined 3% YoY and 5% QoQ.

Cost trajectory. Asked whether inflated energy and import costs would come under control from Q2 onwards, management said costs should improve because of operational efficiency.

Biocatalysis growth timing. Management expects good growth from biocatalysis in the second half of the year. Observed Q1 FY27 revenue was INR 45 million versus INR 44 million in Q4 FY26.

Capacity expansion timing. Management stated fermentation utilization is close to 70–75% and that a call may be taken in the next quarter to start CapEx for increasing capacity.

R&D facility timing. The R&D capital expenditure of INR 50 crore is already under work in progress and may become functional in the next quarter or so.

Novel food approval timing. Management indicated a wait of perhaps three to six months, while noting dependence on authorities and the possibility of further questions.

U.S. business model transition. Management described the shift toward branding as shaping up gradually and not a one-day task, with better growth expected in coming quarters and about 8% by year-end.

Quarterly versus annual evaluation. Management repeatedly advised shareholders to evaluate the business on an annual horizon rather than through a single quarter, and to avoid quarter-on-quarter comparisons given the 20–30% variable portion of the customer base.

Broker Narrative

The broker maintained an Accumulate rating throughout, but the narrative shifted from expecting a US-led recovery and ~15% FY24 growth to acknowledging subdued 1QFY25 growth and missed estimates. Early themes of US/Human Nutrition weakness, RoW decline, and growth-delivery risk persisted, while earlier optimism on core-product momentum and input-cost relief faded. The final report still cited possible re-rating catalysts, but the stock delivered a -26% actual return.

Fears that came true

  • The flagged weakness in US/Human Nutrition persisted, with USA B2C revenue declining ~25% YoY in 1QFY25.
  • The flagged Rest of World decline continued, as Asia ex-India and Rest of World revenues fell 7% and 65% YoY respectively in the last report.
  • The flagged lower-than-expected growth delivery materialized, with overall revenue growing only ~5% YoY and EBITDA coming in 10% below estimates versus a 12-15% guidance.

Optimism that failed

  • The anticipated US recovery in 2HFY24 failed to materialize, as USA B2C revenue was still down ~25% YoY in 1QFY25.
  • The earlier core-product strength did not sustain, with API revenue down ~16% YoY and Sera, the largest product, expected to grow only mid-single digits.
  • The optimism on regional growth faded, as domestic Human Nutrition, Asia ex-India, and RoW declined 9%, 7%, and 65% YoY respectively in the last report.
  • The input-cost moderation thesis was challenged by increased freight costs and a partial freight-cost burden shifting to the company.
