NINtec Systems Limited — FY 2025-26: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Dividend withheld to fund growth. The Board did not recommend any dividend for FY 2025-26, stating that profits are being retained to strengthen the financial position and fund ongoing operations, growth plans and future opportunities. No unpaid or unclaimed dividend required transfer to the IEPF.

Board and committee churn during the year. Two Non-Executive Independent Directors, Mr. Vishal Shah and Mr. Hursh Jani, ceased to hold office with effect from the close of business on 12 February 2026, confirming no material reasons beyond those in their resignation letters. Mr. Mehul Makkampara and Mr. Rahul Guhathakurta were appointed as Additional Directors (Non-Executive, Independent) with effect from 1 February 2026, and their appointments for five-year terms were approved by shareholders via special resolution through postal ballot, results announced 21 March 2026. The Audit Committee and Nomination and Remuneration Committee chairmanships changed: Mr. Rahul Guhathakurta was appointed Chairperson of both committees with effect from 13 February 2026, and Mr. Mehul Makkampara joined the Stakeholders’ Relationship Committee from the same date.

Senior management departures after year-end. Mr. Parag Shah (VP & Client Success Manager) and Mr. Rushikesh Rajendra (CHRO & Sr. VP – Strategic Initiatives) resigned with effect from 8 May 2026, and Mr. Chainsingh Rathod (VP – Talent Acquisition & Operations) resigned with effect from 31 July 2026.

Statutory auditor transition. M/s. Samir M. Shah & Associates completed their second consecutive five-year term as Statutory Auditors on the conclusion of the 11th AGM. The Board, at its meeting on 10 August 2026, approved the appointment of M/s. J. T. Shah & Co. (FRN 109616W) for five consecutive years from the conclusion of the 11th AGM to the 16th AGM, subject to shareholder approval, at a proposed fee of INR 2.10 lakhs per annum (excluding taxes and out-of-pocket expenses) for FY 2026-27.

CSR committee constituted only after year-end. The amount required to be spent on CSR exceeded the Section 135(9) threshold; the CSR Committee was constituted by the Board on 26 June 2026, after the close of FY 2025-26. The Company’s CSR obligation for the year was INR 37.34 lakhs against which INR 37.38 lakhs was spent.

Historical compliance blemish. A BSE fine of Rs. 5,000 was levied in FY 2022-23 for a one-day delay in conducting the Board meeting to approve half-yearly results for the half year ended 30 September 2022. No non-compliance, penalty or strictures were reported in the last three years otherwise.

No new capital actions. No buyback, sweat equity, bonus shares, ESOPs, rights issue, GDRs/ADRs/warrants or convertible instruments during the year; paid-up capital unchanged at ₹18,57,60,000 (1,85,76,000 equity shares of ₹10 each).

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

AI-first repositioning. The Company describes itself as an AI-first software engineering company with one of Asia-Pacific’s deepest Claude engineering practices — over 300 Claude-trained engineers — and Anthropic’s Claude embedded across every phase of the development lifecycle. Its six-phase AI Engineering Method (AI Discovery, Neural Architecture, Intelligent Development, Autonomous Testing, AI-Powered Deployment, Continuous Intelligence) is stated to deliver 90%+ test coverage from sprint one and automated rollback within 90 seconds of deployment issues.

Scale and client base. The Company serves 30+ Fortune 500 clients across nine industry verticals, with a presence spanning 15 countries and customer reach across 30+ countries. Named relationships include Michelin, Toshiba, Veolia, Scania and Wolters Kluwer. Headquartered in Ahmedabad with international operations including NINtec Systems B.V. in the Netherlands.

Certifications and governance upgrades. ISO 9001:2015 and ISO 27001:2022 certifications from Alcumus ISOQAR, UKAS. The roles of Chairman and Managing Director were separated during the year, strengthening board oversight. The Board comprises a majority of Non-Executive Directors, including three Independent Directors (50% of the Board).

Frontier technology investments. The Company is extending into cognitive AI and, through its Quantum Horizon initiative, into post-quantum cryptography, quantum machine learning and quantum cloud computing on AWS Braket, Azure Quantum and IBM Qiskit.

Subsidiary performance. Nintec Systems B.V. (wholly owned, acquired 23 November 2023) reported turnover of ₹6,847.21 lakhs, profit before taxation of ₹553.02 lakhs and profit after taxation of ₹421.27 lakhs for April 2025 to March 2026.

Management’s outlook. The Chairman and Managing Director both cite substantial opportunity as global enterprises re-architect around AI and seek partners to engineer that transformation responsibly and at scale, entering FY 2026-27 with a strong balance sheet, differentiated methodology and world-class client base.

3. Key Risks

Risk management framework. The Company follows a process for timely identification, categorization and prioritization of operational, financial and strategic business risks, with teams authorized to manage risks and update senior management; the Board and Audit Committee review risk assessment regularly. The Risk Management Policy is on the Company’s website. The Company states it does not fall under the criteria for formation of a Risk Management Committee, and hence has not constituted one.

Cybersecurity and responsible AI. The Board pays close attention to risk, cybersecurity and the responsible, ethical adoption of AI across everything the Company builds.

Foreign exchange exposure. Foreign exchange earnings were ₹9,587.78 lakhs and outgo ₹207.76 lakhs in FY 2025-26 (prior year: ₹7,995.92 lakhs earned, ₹133.85 lakhs outgo). The Company does not deal in commodities; detailed discussion of foreign exchange risk and hedging is referenced to the Management Discussion and Analysis Report.

Internal financial controls. The Board is of the opinion that the Company’s internal financial controls were adequate and effective during FY 2025-26. The CEO/CFO certification states there were no significant changes in internal controls over financial reporting, no significant changes in accounting policies, and no instances of significant fraud of which they became aware.

Litigation and regulatory orders. No litigations outstanding as on 31 March 2026. No significant or material orders passed by regulators, courts or tribunals impacting going concern status or future operations. No fraud reported by Statutory Auditors under Section 143(12).

Forward-looking caution. The Board’s Report states that statements describing objectives, expectations or forecasts are forward-looking and actual results may differ materially.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Consolidated FY 2025-26 performance. Revenue from operations ₹17,016.78 lakhs (FY25: ₹13,980.42 lakhs, up 21.72%); PBT ₹4,281.12 lakhs (FY25: ₹3,499.10 lakhs); PAT ₹3,201.16 lakhs (FY25: ₹2,632.20 lakhs, up 21.62%); EPS ₹17.23 (FY25: ₹14.17). The Chairman and Managing Director cite consolidated revenue of ₹170 crore, net profit of ₹32 crore, EPS of ₹17.23, operating margin of 26% and return on equity of 42%, placing NINtec among the top decile of India’s listed IT companies.

Standalone FY 2025-26 performance. Revenue from operations ₹10,169.56 lakhs (FY25: ₹8,584.70 lakhs, up 18.46%); PBT ₹3,728.10 lakhs (FY25: ₹3,109.16 lakhs); PAT ₹2,779.89 lakhs (FY25: ₹2,331.34 lakhs, up 19.24%); basic and diluted EPS ₹14.96 (FY25: ₹12.55). Cash and cash equivalents as at 31 March 2026 were ₹3,394 lakhs on a standalone basis. Retained earnings closing balance: ₹6,634.69 lakhs standalone and ₹7,501.09 lakhs consolidated.

Multi-year trajectory. Over five years, revenue and profit compounded at approximately 80% and 89% respectively. EPS grew from ₹1.90 in FY 2020-21 to ₹14.97 in FY 2025-26, supported by two bonus issues since listing.

Audit outcomes. The Statutory Auditors issued an unmodified opinion on the financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2026, with no qualification, reservation, adverse remark or disclaimer. The Secretarial Audit Report for FY 2025-26 likewise contains no qualification, reservation, adverse remark or disclaimer. The Secretarial Auditor confirmed compliance with the Companies Act, SCRA, Depositories Act, FEMA and applicable SEBI regulations, and noted the two special resolutions passed by postal ballot on 20 March 2026 for the appointment of the two new Independent Directors.

Remuneration versus performance. Mr. Niraj Gemawat’s remuneration was ₹12.00 lakhs (₹1.00 lakh per month), a 33.33% increase; Mr. Mohit Soni’s remuneration rose 11.98% and Ms. Disha Shah’s 18.36%. The ratio of each Director’s remuneration to the median employee remuneration was 1.79 for Mr. Gemawat, 2.46 for Mr. Soni and 1.59 for Ms. Shah. The median employee remuneration increased approximately 4-5%; average salary increase for non-managerial personnel was 7-8% versus 21% for managerial personnel. The Company had 441 permanent employees on its rolls.

Re-appointment guidance. The Board approved Mr. Niraj Gemawat’s re-appointment as Managing Director for five years from 29 June 2026 to 28 June 2031, not liable to retire by rotation, subject to shareholder approval at the 11th AGM. Mrs. Rachana Gemawat retires by rotation and offers herself for re-appointment. The 11th AGM is scheduled for Friday, 18 September 2026 at 3:00 p.m. IST through video conferencing, with remote e-voting from 15 September 2026 (9:00 a.m.) to 17 September 2026 (5:00 p.m.) and a cut-off date of 11 September 2026.
