Dreamfolks Services — Q1 FY27 Update

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Structural reset in domestic airport lounges. FY26 was described as a year of significant disruption and structural change in the domestic airport lounge ecosystem, with a material impact on financial performance. Banking and enterprise partners are shifting away from a single standardized benefit toward personalized combinations of experiences based on customer segments, spending behavior, and usage patterns.

Geopolitical conflict hitting international traffic. Management attributed the sharp quarter-on-quarter revenue decline primarily to the war affecting the Middle East, which has suppressed international traffic. The global lounge business numbers “dropped drastically” compared to the prior quarter, and the Middle East region is effectively on hold — the Dubai-based ETT acquisition cannot be pushed forward in the region while the conflict persists. Management stated that without the war, transaction numbers and top line would have been much better.

Revenue and profitability pressure. Q1 FY27 revenue was INR 39 crores, against INR 52.6 crores in the preceding quarter and INR 348.9 crores in Q1 FY26. Gross profit was negative INR 0.9 crores, adjusted EBITDA negative INR 16.4 crores (versus negative INR 14.4 crores in the prior quarter and positive INR 30.5 crores in Q1 FY26), and PAT negative INR 13.8 crores. The profitability impact was attributed to lower revenue against an existing operating cost base, plus upfront minimum guarantee payments supporting global lounge expansion.

Slow ramp of new services. Newly introduced services launched with a large bank in mid-May are expected to take at least a year to gain traction, as awareness builds mainly through word of mouth rather than sustained bank communication.

No promoter buying or strategic investor action. Management indicated no current plans for promoters to increase shareholding and no active pursuit of strategic investors, with focus entirely on rebuilding the business.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Diversification is showing in the numbers. Non-airport lounge services contributed approximately 33% of the top line in the quarter, which management called a significant milestone demonstrating that diversification is reflected in business composition rather than remaining strategic intent.

Global network and new client wins. The network reached 1,100+ global airport lounges, with more than 70 new outlets added during the quarter. Three large APAC clients were signed: a large card network in Singapore (program expected live by end of August or early September), one of the largest banks in Indonesia (already live), and another Singapore bank (integration underway).

Railway lounges via Ten11 Hospitality. The Ten11 acquisition strengthened operational control and enables direct participation in premium railway travel. Railway lounges have 100% coverage across the country. Management cited a INR 500 crores opportunity over five years, contingent on the government’s envisaged railway investment over the next three to four years. Capex per lounge ranges from INR 1.5 crores (2,000 sq ft) to INR 5–6 crores (up to 14,000 sq ft), plus security deposits and initial advances to the railways.

Golf and lifestyle categories. Access now covers 80+ golf outlets in India and 860+ international golf courses.

DF Club Membership. Initial response is encouraging, with memberships sold showing steady quarter-on-quarter growth. Three tiers (white, orange, black) are offered, with black — priced at INR 50,000 — selling most; the blended average is around INR 30,000. Social media promotion has only recently begun, and the company will publish numbers once the contribution becomes significant.

Structural market trends. India’s credit card transaction value is expected to grow from approximately INR 21 trillion in FY25 to INR 54 trillion in FY30 (around 21% CAGR), with volumes rising to nearly 13 billion. Global travel and tourism contributed approximately USD 11.6 trillion to the global economy in 2025; APAC remains a key aviation growth engine, and Southeast Asia’s tourism market is expected to grow from approximately USD 35.5 billion in 2025 to USD 67.4 billion by 2031.

New partnership. Dreamfolks partnered with Elixir to be the official provider of premium spa benefits for Elixir’s upcoming wellness-focused credit card, announced at the Global Fintech Fest on September 09, 2026 — adding a wellness-led use case to the lifestyle benefits portfolio.

Balance sheet strength. Cash and cash equivalents rose to INR 193.3 crores from INR 149 crores in the previous quarter, aided by INR 40-plus crores of collections from customers. Net worth stood at INR 300.4 crores as of June 30, 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical and airspace disruption in the Middle East continuing to suppress international traffic and delay Middle East expansion.
  • Client concentration and reliance on marquee banking partners, with the pace of new-service adoption dependent on bank-led communication and program design.
  • Execution risk in B2C and international expansion, including the early-stage DF Club and integration of acquisitions such as ETT and Ten11.
  • Competition in global and Middle East markets, where management noted some competitors are dominant.
  • Cost structure mismatch — lower revenue against a fixed operating cost base, with minimum guarantee payments creating near-term margin drag.
  • Macro uncertainties such as volatile fuel prices and elevated energy costs affecting travel sentiment.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Breakeven timing. Management reiterated its prior commitment to EBITDA breakeven by H2 FY28, stating it would stick to what was committed last time and announce earlier if it happens sooner. In the call, management also said it expects to “come to the breakeven” by next year. Observed performance remains negative: adjusted EBITDA of negative INR 16.4 crores in Q1 FY27, following negative INR 14.4 crores in the prior quarter.

Minimum guarantee recovery. Management stated the upfront minimum guarantee payments supporting global lounge expansion are expected to be recovered through increased transaction volumes and scale-up over coming quarters, and that no such payments will recur going forward, so positive margins should follow. Observed Q1 FY27 gross profit was negative INR 0.9 crores due to these payments.

Global lounge scale-up. Management expected global lounge, golf, and other services to grow over a couple of quarters. Observed Q1 FY27 global lounge revenue fell sharply quarter-on-quarter due to the war, with management noting the drop was drastic versus the last quarter.

New service ramp. Management guided that services launched with a large bank in mid-May will take at least a year to pick up and show numbers. No revenue contribution from these services was quantified for Q1 FY27.

Railway margin economics. Management indicated ETT margins are presently in a similar range to historical India lounge margins, with improvement expected eventually for Global, but not better in the near term given advances and pricing benefits extended to lounge operators.

Cost trajectory. Management stated cost of services is a derivative of revenue and that the cost line and revenue line will remain in line, with gross margins holding at existing levels. Q1 FY27 showed cost of services approximately matching revenue.

Payroll costs. ESOP impact is minimal — approximately INR 14 lakhs on a full-year basis. Management guided that payroll cost will continue at roughly the current quarter’s level, noting the March quarter was unusually low due to a variable pay reversal.

Receivables. Management reported significant collection efforts, with INR 40-plus crores collected from customers and cash rising to INR 190-plus crores. Management stated it sees no risk in receivables given the counterparties are banks, though the June quarter debtors balance was not disclosed as only P&L numbers were published.

Governance. The Board approved the reappointment of Mr. Sunil Kulkarni as Independent Director for five years from November 21, 2026 through November 20, 2031, and the appointment of Mr. Lloyd Mathias as Additional Director and Independent Director for three years from August 14, 2026 through August 13, 2029, both subject to shareholder approval.
