Exide Industries Limited (EXIDEIND) Analysis

Current Context (H1 FY26): Modest revenue growth of 1.3% YoY to ₹8,688 Cr (standalone), with EBITDA margin at 10.9% (impacted by input costs and GST destocking). Debt-free balance sheet, strong cash flow (+₹500 Cr from inventory optimization), and lithium-ion gigafactory nearing commissioning. GST cuts (batteries: 28%→18%; solar: 12%→5%) passed fully to consumers, creating short-term headwinds but long-term tailwinds.

Headwinds (Near-Term Pressures)

  • GST 2.0 Destocking: Announcement (Aug 15, 2025) led to channel de-stocking, production cuts, and Q2 degrowth (-2.1%). Aspirational segments (solar -5% Q2, home UPS) hit hardest; extended monsoon compounded issues.
  • Input Cost Inflation: High lead prices (20-30% above historical norms), forex (₹90/USD), and partial pass-through delayed due to GST. Pressures EBITDA (Q2: 9.4% vs. historical 13.5-14.5%).
  • Weak Segments: Exports (tariffs/geopolitics), telecom, e-rickshaw (last-mile) declined; ~12% of revenue impacted.
  • EPR Compliance: New accruals for battery waste management (Q2 “other expenses” elevated); ongoing cost.

Tailwinds (Supportive Factors)

  • GST Benefits: Full pass-through aligns with govt. goals; expected to boost auto OEM (SIAM Oct data: PV +11%, 2W retail +52%), solar (PM Surya Ghar), and consumer demand.
  • Operational Resilience: Inventory down to 93 days (from 110); +₹500 Cr cash in H1. Tech upgrades (Punched Grid for 100% 2W batteries, automation) yielding cost savings/quality gains.
  • Strong Core: 88% business grew 7% YoY; aftermarket double-digit (4W/2W ~10-12%). Debt-free (standalone), ROCE 16.3%, ICRA AAA rating.
  • Solar Momentum: Q1 +35%; GST 5% + subsidies to drive Q3 rebound.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative stayed bullish from 2023 to 2026, consistently emphasizing strong auto replacement/OEM demand, industrial/solar growth, and lithium-ion expansion as the key long-term driver. Input-cost pressure and export weakness were recurring concerns, though the last report notes exports eventually recovered after several weak quarters. The biggest evolution was the lithium-ion project timeline slipping from FY24 Phase 1 production to FY27 revenue, with the last report adding new caveats about operating leverage and uncertain lithium margins while highlighting the Bengaluru gigafactory and Hyundai/Kia MoU.

Fears that came true

  • Raw-material/logistics cost pressure materialized: the last report still cites rupee depreciation and commodity inflation forcing price increases, and in the intervening weak period 2024-07-30 (Add, predicted +13.2%, actual -11.6%) and 2024-09-01 (Buy, predicted +14.3%, actual -4.7%) ended as DISAPPOINTMENT with negative actual returns.
  • Automotive export weakness materialized: the last report says exports returned to over 20% growth only after several weak quarters, matching the early warning on anti-dumping duties in GCC countries and modest Western demand, and that weak stretch overlapped with negative actual returns in mid-2024.

Optimism that failed

  • The lithium-ion project did not begin Phase 1 production in FY24; the last report says the Bengaluru gigafactory was only nearing commercialization with revenue expected in FY27, and the margin profile of the venture is now uncertain rather than the earlier 15% ROIC expectation.
  • The expectation that margins would improve as inflationary pressures stabilized failed to materialize; the last report still flags commodity inflation, currency depreciation, and lower-than-anticipated gains from operating leverage as risks.
