Marico — Recent Corporate Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Comparability disruption from restructuring and acquisitions. The consolidated results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 include the impact of the acquisition of 75% of Skinetiq Joint Stock Company (a Vietnam-incorporated entity owning a digital-first business) with effect from April 2, 2026, and the results are stated to be not comparable with other periods reported in the statement. The Group also has an obligation to acquire the remaining equity stake from non-controlling interests, contingent on achievement of certain business milestones at a future date, accounted for at present value with a corresponding debit to other equity.
  • Intra-group restructuring activity. The business undertaking of Just Herbs vested in the Company effective October 1, 2025 following its liquidation, and the business undertaking of Beardo (Zed Lifestyle Private Limited) vested in the Company effective June 29, 2026. Both are common control transactions under Appendix C of Ind AS 103 and had no impact on consolidated financial results, but comparative periods have been restated.
  • Accounting policy reclassification. Certain customer-related advertisement and promotional expenses previously included within Advertisement and Sales Promotion expenses are now netted off from Revenue from Operations, applied retrospectively to the quarter ended March 31, 2026, the quarter ended June 30, 2025, and the year ended March 31, 2026. This does not affect profit before tax, profit after tax, EPS or shareholders’ equity, but it changes reported revenue and expense lines.
  • Entities under voluntary liquidation. Apcos Naturals Private Limited and Zed Lifestyle Private Limited are wholly owned subsidiaries under voluntary liquidation.
  • Scope limitations in review. The statutory auditor did not review the interim financial results of 3 subsidiaries (total revenues before consolidation adjustments of Rs. 918 crores and net profit after tax of Rs. 162 crores for the quarter ended June 30, 2026) and 16 subsidiaries were not reviewed at all (total revenues of Rs. 464 crores and net profit after tax of Rs. 30 crores), with management stating these are not material to the Group.
  • Balancing-figure basis for Q4 comparatives. The figures for the three months ended March 31, 2026 are balancing figures between audited full-year figures and published year-to-date figures up to the third quarter, which had only been reviewed and not audited.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Double-digit consolidated growth. The Managing Director & CEO briefed members at the 38th AGM on double-digit consolidated revenue and profit after tax growth during FY 2025-26.
  • Strategic and operational programmes. Management highlighted Project SETU, the outlook on foods and premium personal care businesses, the International business, innovation and digital transformation, and emphasis on AI, data and smart analytics.
  • Vision 2030. The eighth Integrated Annual Report was themed “Advancing towards Vision 2030,” with the Company advancing towards its aspiration of becoming one of India’s leading diversified digital-first consumer goods companies.
  • Portfolio expansion. The consolidated entity list now includes Zea Maize Private Limited (subsidiary with effect from January 29, 2026), Cosmix Wellness Private Limited (subsidiary with effect from February 5, 2026), and Skinetiq Joint Stock Company (subsidiary of MSEA with effect from April 2, 2026). The brand portfolio includes Parachute, Saffola, Hair & Care, Parachute Advansed, Nihar Naturals, Mediker, Pure Sense, Coco Soul, Revive, Set Wet, Livon, Beardo, Just Herbs, True Elements, Plix, Cosmix and 4700BC.
  • International footprint. The overseas consumer products portfolio contributes about 24% of Group revenue, with operations across India, Bangladesh, UAE, Egypt, South Africa, Malaysia, Sri Lanka, Vietnam and USA.
  • Sustainability progress. The flagship Jalashay Programme has created a cumulative 547 crore litres of water conservation potential across Maharashtra, Gujarat, Pondicherry and Tamil Nadu till FY26. Marico achieved a 24% reduction in water consumption intensity in FY26 compared to the FY22 baseline, and aims to achieve water neutrality certification across 100% of its manufacturing units located in water-stressed zones.
  • ESG recognition. Marico was assigned an ESG rating of 79 for FY 2026 by NSE Sustainability Ratings & Analytics Limited, placing it in the ‘Leader’ rating category. The company did not engage NSE Sustainability for this assessment, as it was independently determined.
  • Investor engagement. Management will attend the 2026 Jefferies India Forum in Gurgaon on Thursday, September 17, 2026, and the J.P. Morgan India Conference in Mumbai on Tuesday, September 22, 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Contingent acquisition obligation. The Group’s obligation to acquire the remaining equity stake in Skinetiq from non-controlling interests is contingent on achievement of certain business milestones at a future date, introducing uncertainty around future cash outflow and equity impact.
  • Reliance on unaudited/unreviewed components. A portion of the consolidated results derives from subsidiaries whose interim financial results were reviewed by other auditors or not reviewed at all, and the auditor’s conclusion on those amounts is based solely on the reports of other auditors or on management’s representation of immateriality.
  • Restructuring execution. The integration of Just Herbs and Beardo businesses into the Company, and the liquidation of Apcos Naturals and Zed Lifestyle, involve execution and transition considerations, though these are common control transactions with no impact on consolidated financial results.
  • Comparability risk for investors. The combination of the Skinetiq acquisition, the retrospective accounting policy reclassification, and the restatements for common control business combinations means reported figures across periods are not directly comparable, which could complicate trend analysis.
  • No adverse audit findings. The Reports from the Statutory Auditors and Secretarial Auditor did not contain any qualifications, observations or remarks having any adverse effect on the functioning of the Company, and the limited review reports state that nothing came to the auditors’ attention causing them to believe the statements contain any material misstatement.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Guidance/outlook provided. At the 38th AGM on August 6, 2026, the Managing Director & CEO briefed members on strategy and outlook, demand trends in the FMCG sector, Project SETU, outlook on foods and premium personal care businesses, the International business, innovation and digital transformation, emphasis on AI, data and smart analytics, sustainability and ESG progress, and other corporate developments during FY 2025-26 including declaration of dividend. The Company also aims to achieve water neutrality certification across 100% of its manufacturing units in water-stressed zones as part of its ESG 2030 roadmap and Vision 2030 aspiration.
  • Observed performance. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, consolidated revenue from operations was Rs. 3,957 crores (against Rs. 3,301 crores in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 and Rs. 3,221 crores in the quarter ended June 30, 2025), with net profit for the period of Rs. 652 crores and basic EPS of Rs. 4.86. Standalone revenue from operations was Rs. 2,794 crores, with net profit of Rs. 460 crores and basic EPS of Rs. 3.55. For FY 2025-26, Marico recorded a turnover of ₹136.1 billion (USD 1.5 billion).
  • Dividend. A final dividend of ₹4 per equity share of ₹1 each for the financial year ended March 31, 2026 was declared and passed as an ordinary resolution at the 38th AGM.
  • AGM outcomes. All resolutions set out in the Notice were passed with requisite majority: adoption of audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY ended March 31, 2026; declaration of final dividend; re-appointment of Mr. Rishabh Mariwala as director retiring by rotation; and ratification of remuneration payable to M/s. Ashwin Solanki & Associates, Cost Accountants, for the financial year ending March 31, 2027.
  • AGM participation. 76 members participated (27 promoter and promoter group, 49 public). Remote e-voting commenced at 9:00 a.m. IST on Monday, August 3, 2026 and concluded at 5:00 p.m. IST on Wednesday, August 5, 2026, with the cut-off date of Thursday, July 30, 2026.

Broker Narrative

The early narrative framed digital adoption, ecommerce salience, and niche consumer demand as growth tailwinds against inflation, venture funding dry-ups, and digital customer acquisition costs as headwinds. This evolved into a sharper focus on acute input-cost inflation (copra, crude derivatives, edible oils) compressing margins, while digital/B2C and international growth persisted and broadened into a 2030 vision of 50% Foods/premium/B2C/digital share. The niche consumer demand theme faded entirely, replaced by mass-market franchises and explicit margin guidance.

Fears that came true

  • Higher inflation impacting unit economics materialized as copra prices rose ~15% from the bottom and crude-linked derivatives and edible oil prices surged 60-70%, compressing QoQ margins and driving recent negative actual returns (-5.2% in Aug 2026) after earlier DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in 2024 where bearish inflation calls were wrong-footed by the rally.

Optimism that failed

  • Robust demand in niche consumer areas failed to materialize, as the narrative dropped the niche theme entirely and concentrated growth commentary on mass-market brands (Parachute, VAHO) and mainstream B2C/digital portfolios instead.
