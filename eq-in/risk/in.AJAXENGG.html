AJAX Engineering — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Government infrastructure spending below budgeted allocations. Management states that actual government spending on infrastructure projects has remained well below budgeted allocations over the last year or so, resulting in slower-than-expected project execution across the country. This broadly persisted through Q1 FY27 despite a seasonal uptick in volumes in Q4 FY26.
  • Delayed payments from certain state governments. Contractors continue to face payment delays from some state governments, causing cash-flow constraints that dampen sentiment and reduce their appetite to place new orders or invest in fresh equipment.
  • Weak quarterly demand and volumes. Industry registrations in Q1 FY27 fell 27%, while AJAX registrations fell 21%. SLCM revenue grew only marginally year-on-year, and lower volumes versus Q1 FY26 led to adverse operating leverage.
  • Margin pressure. EBITDA margin declined 70 basis points year-on-year to 12.5% in Q1 FY27 (EBITDA of ₹59 crores versus ₹61 crores in Q1 FY26). Management described Q2 FY27 as “fairly challenging” from a margin perspective and said 12.5% looks challenging for that quarter, with 12% also looking challenging.
  • Input cost inflation. Towards the latter part of Q1 FY27, direct material costs began inching upwards due to increases in fuel and steel prices (including E250 and E350 grades). Freight outward costs rose on higher diesel prices and export-related freight. Other expenses also included costs related to the SAP ECC 6 to SAP HANA migration (approximately ₹16 million for the year).
  • Seasonality. Q2 is expected to remain seasonally soft; nearly 60% of revenue is typically generated in H2, so the business is best assessed on an annualised, multi-year basis.
  • State-level weakness. Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh — traditionally large, high-volume states — saw significant volume declines (MP volumes down close to 50%; Maharashtra volumes down sharply versus prior years). Traditional applications such as irrigation, railways, bridges and power remained muted, and government impetus in rural roads was not aggressive.
  • Pricing environment. Following a ~2% price increase in Q4 FY26, the company is evaluating another calibrated price hike, with timing and quantum dependent on market conditions. Management noted it does not play the price game and has resisted discounting even where this makes business tougher.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Market share gains despite premium pricing. SLCM and retail market share expanded to 75.1% in Q1 FY27, up from about 69% in Q1 FY26 and 73.5% for full-year FY26, achieved despite a price premium versus peers. Share improvements were cited across Maharashtra, Karnataka (~85%), MP (~80%), Gujarat (~82%), Rajasthan (66% to 86%), Odisha (61% to 88%) and Chhattisgarh (73% to 91%).
  • Long-term infrastructure drivers. Government investments in highways, railways, power, irrigation and rural infrastructure, along with schemes such as Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana and Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana, are cited as supporting demand for construction equipment and the concrete industry.
  • Non-SLCM portfolio. Non-SLCM revenue grew 6.4% year-on-year to ~₹48 crores, led by healthy growth in concrete pumps. Management expects momentum to continue, supported by B2B channel initiatives and multiple customer engagements (including large players such as Ultratech, JSW, ACC and J. Kumar) that are expected to translate into meaningful wins over the next two to three quarters. Management indicated it would be happy with 10–15% growth in non-SLCM over the previous year.
  • New products and portfolio gaps. The UDAAN series is scaling up (121 units in Q1 FY27, following 202 units last year, with 35–40 units in the last month). The new ARGO 4000 is planned for launch in Q2 FY27 to fill an important portfolio gap.
  • Spares and services. Revenue grew 6.2% year-on-year to ₹39 crores in Q1 FY27. Management sees this as a significant opportunity, supported by a growing installed base, with near-term focus on significantly increasing the share of AMCs.
  • Exports. Exports contributed 9% of revenue in Q1 FY27. The company exported its first slip-form paver during the quarter and sees encouraging traction in Africa and Southeast Asia. Algeria, Morocco and Nigeria constitute about 38% of shipments, with repeat buying in SLCM and batching plants in Nigeria. Management anticipates export business growing at a 30% CAGR, if not more, over the next three years, while remaining prudent on country and currency risk.
  • Application mix shift. Urban infrastructure and buildings — largely private-sector led — have risen to roughly 35–40% of applications, offsetting muted traditional government-led segments; roads constitute about 40–43%.
  • Financial strength and inorganic optionality. Cash balance exceeded ₹1,100 crores as of June 2026, providing flexibility to pursue growth opportunities, including inorganic ones, which management said it is aggressively pursuing within defined guardrails.
  • State-level green shoots. Gujarat is described as leading growth (volumes up 40%, share up from 71% to 82%), with anticipated growth in Odisha, Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka; a state election in UP could provide a fillip. Improved rainfall expectations (below normal or near normal versus earlier deficient forecasts) could support rural cash flows and government spending.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution and spending risk. Continued shortfall in government capex versus budgeted allocations and slow project execution would keep demand subdued.
  • Counterparty/payment risk. Delayed payments from specific state governments constraining contractor cash flows and order appetite.
  • Margin and input-cost risk. Steel and fuel price increases, geopolitical developments and currency movements affecting commodity costs; management noted these pressures “are not going to go away anytime soon.” The company is working with suppliers on the timing and extent of cost increases and has accelerated internal cost-optimisation measures originally planned for later in the year.
  • Pricing risk. Ability to implement further calibrated price increases in a weak demand environment is uncertain.
  • Seasonality and demand visibility. Q2 is expected to be soft, and management said it is too early to make a call on full-year volumes; a clearer view was expected by August–September.
  • Export-related risks. Country risk and currency risk in export markets, which management said it will manage prudently.
  • Competitive dynamics. Some key players lost share during the quarter (the second-ranked player lost about 4% share), while gainers such as Conmat and Venus were noted — indicating a competitive market even as AJAX gained share.
  • Weather. Rainfall in certain markets towards the end of June shifted some batching plant and boom pump activity, with recoveries expected in August–September.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Market share: Management highlighted SLCM/retail share of 75.1% in Q1 FY27 versus ~69% in Q1 FY26 and 73.5% for FY26 — observed performance shows share expansion despite a price premium and a declining industry (registrations down 27%, AJAX down 21%).
  • H2-weighted seasonality: Management reiterated that nearly 60% of revenue is typically generated in H2 and that Q2 would remain seasonally soft, expecting sentiment and demand momentum to improve in H2. Observed Q1 FY27 performance — revenue growth of 1.7% to ₹475 crores, marginal SLCM growth, EBITDA margin down 70 bps to 12.5% — is consistent with a soft first half.
  • Margins: Management said 12.5% looks challenging for Q2 FY27 and that 12% also looks challenging, while reiterating a longer-term 13–15% corridor and pointing to ~15% margin achieved in Q4 FY26 as evidence of operating leverage and premiumisation when demand recovers. Observed Q1 FY27 margin was 12.5%.
  • Non-SLCM: Management guided to 10–15% growth for the year on a lower base, with H2 potentially better than H1. Observed Q1 FY27 non-SLCM growth was 6.4% year-on-year, led by pumps.
  • Exports: Management guided to ~30% CAGR over the next three years. Observed Q1 FY27 export share was 9% of revenue, with the first slip-form paver exported during the quarter and improved shipments.
  • UDAAN: Management said the series is scaling up reasonably well. Observed volumes: 202 units last year, 121 units in Q1 FY27, and 35–40 units in the last month.
  • New product launch: ARGO 4000 planned for launch in Q2 FY27 — a forward-looking commitment not yet observable in Q1 results.
  • Cost optimisation: Management said measures originally planned for later in the year were initiated early and are being accelerated/preponed to mitigate input cost pressures; observed Q1 FY27 other expenses rose ~17% on a flat revenue line, attributed primarily to higher freight outward costs and SAP HANA migration costs (~₹16 million for the year).
  • Full-year volumes: Management declined to guide full-year volumes, stating it is too early and that H2 would be the deciding factor.
  • Capital allocation: Management said it is aggressively pursuing inorganic opportunities with the cash balance exceeding ₹1,100 crores as of June 2026, and would revert when there is clarity; no transaction was announced in the supplied material.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from cautious optimism in August 2025, where they highlighted cost pressures and demand uncertainties but expected festival-driven pickups and new product tailwinds, to a more somber tone in February 2026, acknowledging sectoral headwinds like infrastructure weakness and project delays, before finally recognizing early signs of recovery. Persistent themes included the CEV-V transition, dependence on infrastructure spending, and the growing importance of non-SLCM equipment. The optimism on festival-driven demand and low-base effects failed to materialize initially, but the later recovery vindicated their long-term Buy stance.

Fears that came true

  • The cost increase and pricing pressure from the CEV-V transition materialized, as the industry faced subdued demand and an inability to raise prices.
  • Heavy discounts on CEV-IV models by competitors persisted, depressing realizations until they were discontinued.
  • Liquidity risk at buyers’ end materialized, with contractors delaying projects due to cash flow issues from state governments.
  • The dependence on infrastructure growth proved valid, as weakness in the infrastructure sector led to project delays and poor demand.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected demand pick-up around Janmashtami and Ganesh Chaturthi did not occur, as the stock declined further in the November 2025 report.
  • The low base effect for new concrete equipment products did not prevent sharp negative returns in the early recommendation period.
