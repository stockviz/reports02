Capacit’e Infraprojects — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Workmen shortage. Execution in the first half of Q1 FY27 was impacted by a shortage of workmen. Management noted labour had dipped to 50% in May and there was a shortfall of nearly 30% in June, with the workforce at roughly 7,000. This was described as a factor beyond the company’s control. The situation has since normalized, with the labour requirement stated to be fully in place as of the August 10, 2026 call.

Mumbai water supply restriction. The Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation suspended all water supply connections to construction sites across Mumbai effective June 17, 2026, partially impacting execution in the region.

Commodity price volatility and provisioning. Volatility in non-ferrous metals (aluminium, copper) has not yet been reflected in government-published inflation indexes. Steel prices moderated, but aluminium rose 35–40% and the price variation has not caught up. CPWD has published a new DSR, but management expects it will take a quarter or two for the full escalation to be covered. As a prudent measure, the company took an additional provision of ₹10 crores in Q1 FY27, following a similar ₹10 crores provision in Q4 FY26 — a cumulative ₹20 crores. This provision relates to materials to be purchased over the next 3–4 quarters on government contracts where escalation is not currently covering cost increases (e.g., NBCC escalation at 6%, aluminium windows, electromechanical works).

Project-specific execution delays. - IIT Bombay (₹550 crores contract): Was to start in Q4 FY26 but started only in Q2 FY27 due to lack of tree-cutting permissions with the client. Management stated Q1 and Q2 FY27 should have had revenues in excess of ₹65–70 crores from this project alone. The first building has been handed over, design approved, and execution started. - CIDCO: Of 7 locations, 2 have been handed over; work continues at 4 locations. Handover of the balance land is expected starting Q2 and ending Q3 FY27. Completion for the remainder has been extended to March 2028. - Great Value: Order booked in Q4 FY26 but approvals not yet received; virtually no revenue recognized. - Raymond Wadala: Expected to start from Q2 end.

Revenue growth deceleration. Revenue growth slowed from 22% in FY25 to 12% in FY26 to 7% in Q1 FY27, despite a record order book of ~₹13,500 crores (about 5x annual sales). Management attributed this to sites where work had not yet begun — stating execution was effectively happening on only ₹8,000–9,000 crores of the order book — rather than a structural bottleneck.

Delhi NCR GRAP construction bans. A recurring annual event from November to January. Management has factored an average of 20 days of impact for the fiscal, noting that a 40-day ban would affect everyone.

Contract assets. Contract assets plus debtors stood at approximately 78% of the top line as of March 31, versus 56–65 days for competition (as characterized by an investor). Management noted this had peaked at 120% of the top line post-COVID when ₹325 crores was held up, and has since improved by ~45%. CIDCO has ~₹300 crores of unbilled revenue and MHADA ~₹200 crores. Management stated nothing is above 1–3 years and these relate to milestone payments on ongoing projects.

Gross debt increase. Gross debt rose to ₹522 crores, partly because approximately ₹150 crores of payments shifted by 10 days (subsequently collected in the current month). Management stated this should not be viewed quarterly.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Normalized workforce. Labour is fully in place as of the August 10, 2026 call, with management expecting a satisfactory uptick in Q2 FY27.

Order book and inflows. Order book stood at ₹13,535 crores as on June 30, 2026 (₹13,532 crores per the press release), with public sector at 55% and private at 45%. FY27 order inflow target is ₹4,500–5,000 crores; YTD order booking is ₹1,071 crores. Q1 inflows were entirely private; Q2 is expected to see equivalent public inflows. Management expects to cross 50% of the higher end of the band in Q2 itself.

Bid pipeline. Identified quality bid pipeline of ₹22,000 crores in public sector and ₹5,000 crores in private sector over Q2 and Q3.

L1 positions. The company is L1 for the CIDCO Maha Awas DRS Housing project and the Chennai Metro commercial complex, with conversion expected within the month. As a policy, the company does not declare L1 positions on stock exchanges.

Project revenue ramp-ups expected: - NBCC: Revenue build-up began in Q1; certified bill at ₹300 crores, with billing targeted at ~₹60 crores per month for the remainder of the year (up from ~₹15 crores per month last year). Profit booking has started after crossing the 20% threshold. Expected ~₹150 crores in the current quarter. - MHADA BDD Worli (TCC): Total order value at TCC level ~₹17,000 crores including escalation; 50% handed over (34 rehab buildings), translating to ₹7,500–8,000 crores at TCC level, of which 35% belongs to Capacit’e. Target of ~₹20 crores per month in Q2, ~₹60 crores for the quarter, and ₹75 crores-plus in Q3 and Q4. Four more buildings expected to open in Q3; each building is ~₹140 crores at subcontractor level, with each slab casting giving ~₹3.1 crores of revenue. - CIDCO: Targeting ~₹600 crores for the full year; balance backlog of ~₹2,000 crores plus escalation (~₹2,500 crores including escalation), with ~₹1,000 crores plus escalation to be certified over the next 18 months. - Signature Global: Targeting ~₹22 crores monthly revenue, with more than 1,000 workers on site; improvement seen in July and expected between July and August. - Downtown project: Expected to contribute ~₹55 crores in the current quarter, rising in Q3 and Q4.

Non-core asset monetization. ₹6.5 crores realized in Q1 FY27 from disposal of non-core assets, reflected in other income. FY27 target is ₹50 crores.

Promoter pledge reduction. Promoter share pledge reduced from 85.5 lakh shares as on March 31, 2026 to 50 lakh shares currently, with a target of full release by the end of the current financial year.

Capital expenditure and technology. FY27 capex target of ₹193 crores (₹56 crores plant and machinery, ₹121 crores aluminium extrusion and related formwork, ₹5.43 crores IT). SAP implementation targeted to go live in Q3 FY27. Capex is linked to executing 15-plus super-high-rise buildings and bidding for composite/steel-structure buildings requiring heavier cranes.

Working capital and debt trajectory. Management targets net debt-free status in 8 quarters. Net working capital reduced by 43 days last year; a further 25–30 days reduction is expected this year. Cash flow from operations improved from ~₹50 crores to ~₹250 crores last year, with momentum expected to continue. Term loans may increase by ₹45–50 crores net due to equipment purchases, with term loan repayment of ₹102 crores for the year.

CSR foundation. Capacit’e Foundation was incorporated on July 3, 2026 as a Section 8 not-for-profit company limited by guarantee for CSR activities under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013, with Capacit’e Infraprojects Limited as holding company having control over management and affairs.

AGM outcomes. At the 14th AGM held July 24, 2026, all six resolutions passed with requisite majority, including adoption of FY26 financial statements, re-appointment of Rahul Katyal as director liable to retire by rotation, re-appointment of Subir Malhotra as Whole Time Director, ratification of cost auditor remuneration, alteration of the Object Clause of the Memorandum of Association (special resolution), and renewal of commission payable to Independent Directors.

3. Key Risks

Commodity escalation recovery uncertainty. Reversal of the ₹20 crores provision depends on inflation indexes (copper, aluminium, non-ferrous) catching up with actual price increases, not on food, retail, or wholesale prices. Management expressed confidence based on 7–8 years of history but attached a disclaimer. If escalation does not catch up, the provision may not reverse; if geopolitical tensions ease and prices fall, a complete reversal is possible.

Client-side approvals and right-of-way. Tree-cutting permissions, encroachment issues, and land handovers with government clients have caused delays (IIT Bombay, CIDCO, Great Value). Management acknowledged similar issues could arise with other clients.

Monsoon seasonality. Q2 typically sees monsoon impact.

Delhi NCR construction bans. Factored at 20 days; a longer ban (e.g., 40 days) would affect the industry.

Working capital and contract assets. Contract assets plus debtors remain elevated relative to peers; improvement is targeted but depends on certification and collection cycles.

Provisioning recurrence. Management does not expect further provisioning in Q2 but will confirm Q3 at the next earnings call.

Debt levels. Gross debt increased in Q1 due to timing of collections; term loans may rise by ₹45–50 crores net for equipment.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Revenue growth guidance vs. actual. Management guides 20% year-on-year revenue growth for FY27 and expects to achieve the full-year guidance over the remaining three quarters. Observed: Q1 FY27 revenue grew 7% year-on-year (₹629 crores vs ₹589 crores). Management attributed the shortfall to workmen shortage and BMC water supply restrictions, and expects Q3 and Q4 quarterly run rate above ₹850 crores. FY26 revenue growth was 12% (₹2,623 crores vs ₹2,350 crores), and FY25 growth was 22%.

EBITDA margin guidance vs. actual. Management guides 15.5–16% EBITDA margin (excluding other income) and Vision 2028 targets 16–17%. Observed: Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 15.7% (vs 17.2% in Q1 FY26), impacted by the ₹10 crores provision. FY26 EBITDA margin was 16.3%.

Order inflow guidance vs. actual. FY27 target is ₹4,500–5,000 crores. Observed: YTD order booking ₹1,071 crores. Management expects to cross 50% of the higher end of the band in Q2.

Non-core asset monetization guidance vs. actual. FY27 target is ₹50 crores. Observed: ₹6.5 crores realized in Q1 FY27.

Net debt-free target. Management targets net debt-free in 8 quarters and states this is well on track. Observed: Gross debt ₹522 crores as of June 30, 2026; net debt-to-equity 0.16x; gross debt-to-equity 0.27x. Gross debt increased in Q1 due to ~₹150 crores of payments shifting by 10 days, subsequently collected.

Working capital reduction guidance vs. actual. Management targets 25–30 days reduction in FY27 (after 43 days reduction in FY26) and leadership positioning by March 2028. Observed: Contract assets plus debtors at ~78% of top line as of March 31, 2026, improved from 120% post-COVID.

Project-specific guidance vs. observed: - IIT Bombay: Guided Q1 and Q2 FY27 revenues in excess of ₹65–70 crores; observed no revenue in Q1 due to delayed start. Execution started, first building handed over. - NBCC: Guided revenue jump from ₹20 crores to ₹60 crores per month; observed certified bill at ₹300 crores, profit booking started, ~₹150 crores expected in current quarter. - CIDCO: Guided ~₹600 crores for full year; observed 2 of 7 locations handed over, balance handover expected Q2–Q3, completion extended to March 2028. - MHADA: Guided ~₹60 crores in Q2 and ₹75 crores-plus in Q3 and Q4; observed target of ~₹20 crores per month in Q2. - Signature Global: Guided ~₹22 crores monthly; observed serious dip in Q1 due to labour shortfall, improvement in July and expected between July and August.

Capex guidance vs. actual. FY27 target ₹193 crores (revised from earlier ₹165 crores guidance for aluminium extrusion). Observed: Q1 FY27 capex ₹52.2 crores.

Provisioning guidance. Management does not expect further provisioning in Q2 FY27 and will confirm Q3 at the next earnings call. Observed: ₹10 crores provision in Q1 FY27, following ₹10 crores in Q4 FY26.
