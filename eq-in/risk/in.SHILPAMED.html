Shilpa Medicare: Recent Developments, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Regulatory and approval uncertainty. Management identified the regulatory pathway as the most likely source of slowdown, noting that “once in a while, the way regulatory authorities look at us could be a bit different,” and that unexpected regulatory challenges could slow the growth trajectory. The Unicycive Therapeutics program (OLC opportunity, where Shilpa is CDMO partner for API and formulation) has received a CRL from USFDA; the innovator is working with the agency and plans to refile in Q3, with Shilpa working closely with its partner on next steps.

Raw material cost pressure. The slight dip in gross margin (71% in Q1FY27 versus around 75% in the prior-year quarter, which had benefited from higher licensing income) was attributed partly to elevated raw material prices linked to the global political situation. Management noted only partial pass-through to customers is possible, making the margin impact difficult to quantify.

Quarter-on-quarter variability in reported segments. Europe revenue declined from around INR 77 crores to INR 57 crores, which management attributed to the tendered nature of the business and quarter-to-quarter supply scheduling rather than any weakness in end-market performance. Similarly, domestic formulation revenue dipped sequentially in Q1FY27; management explained Nor-UDCA stocking and manufacturing schedules vary by quarter and that product-level numbers are not disclosed.

Litigation and insolvency matters. Shilpa Biologicals Pvt. Ltd. (a material subsidiary) and Sartorius Stedim India Pvt. Ltd. entered a settlement agreement executed on 23 June 2026 before the XI Addl. City Civil and Sessions Court, Commercial Court, Bengaluru, for payment of Rs. 9,00,00,000, against Sartorius’s alleged claim of Rs. 20,13,47,308 plus interest; the impact is limited to the settlement amount. Separately, the wholly owned subsidiary FTF Pharma Private Limited had an application admitted under Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 by the NCLT Ahmedabad Bench – Court II on 9 September 2026, over an alleged operational debt of Rs. 2,18,03,319 claimed by Immacule Lifesciences Private Limited, with Mr. Sunil Kumar Kedia appointed Interim Resolution Professional and a moratorium under Section 14 of the IBC. The company said it is evaluating the legal position with advisors.

Policy and trade uncertainty. Management commented on the U.S. tariff threat and the U.S. Biosecurity Act, stating that Shilpa does not sell me-too generics in the U.S. — its products are complex and differentiated — so no material impact is currently seen, and that the Biosecurity Act is mainly aimed at China. On evaluating U.S. manufacturing facilities, management said the industry is monitoring how the Trump administration’s terms develop before deciding next steps.

Tax-related transition. The company is switching to the new tax regime, forgoing accumulated MAT credit in Shilpa Medicare; the tax rate is expected to normalize at around 25% in coming quarters, with MAT credit usable to the extent of 25% of tax, reducing the effective rate by roughly 6–6.5%.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record financial performance and balance sheet improvement. Q1FY27 was the highest-ever quarterly revenue and EBITDA for the fourth successive quarter: revenue of INR 469 crores (+43% YoY), EBITDA of INR 139 crores (+42% YoY) at a 30% margin, operating PBT of INR 92 crores (versus INR 50 crores), reported PBT of INR 98 crores (+98% YoY), and reported PAT of INR 101 crores (+115% YoY). Net debt-to-EBITDA improved to 1.3x from 6.7x three years ago; ROCE improved to 12.5% from 8.8% in FY25, and to 18.3% adjusted for the still-early biologics and NBE businesses. The credit rating was upgraded from A+ to AA-, and the company was certified as a Great Place to Work.

API division. Revenue of INR 260 crores (+15% YoY), with non-captive third-party API sales also growing ~15%, driven by newly expanded capacities, captive demand from formulations, and specialty CDMO traction from new client acquisition in developed markets. Three NCE programs are in advanced phases and expected to commercialize in FY28; 15 new oncology product validations are targeted for completion in FY27; over 25 NCE programs are underway, with three in late stage. A large peptide manufacturing capex in India (solid-phase synthesis, start-to-end capability) is to complete commissioning by end of FY27. Specialty CDMO was confirmed as a leading growth driver, with innovative CDMO (not polymers) contributing the growth.

Formulation division. Revenue of INR 198 crores, growing over 100% YoY, with the base business ex-licensing income up approximately 112%, driven by the complex FDA portfolio in the U.S. plus EU and ROW. Nor-UDCA (first NCE, approved in India last year) is performing as expected with good early clinical outcomes and strong FY27 order visibility; European and U.S. scientific advice has been completed and global Phase II studies will start in FY27. Three commercial 505(b)(2)s approved last year are growing quarter-on-quarter. Abraxane, Enzalutamide and Abiraterone formulations are on track for FY28 launch. Rotigotine transdermal patch is approved in Europe, filed in the U.S. with a strong U.S. partner, and on track for FY28 launch. Captive API sourcing for formulations exceeds 50%.

Biologics division. Revenue of INR 52 crores (+42% YoY), driven by licensing, partnership and CDMO deal momentum. Aflibercept clinical studies are complete, with launch in India targeted in FY27 through partnerships with three leading Indian ophthalmic companies. The first European biosimilar partnership (Nivolumab with Orion Corporation) supports global commercialization; India clinical study has started, with FY28 India launch targeted. More than five biosimilars are in the pipeline. On large-molecule CDMO, Shilpa Biologics offers clone development to fill & finish, with more than six active NCE programs; one will enter human clinical studies in FY27 through a partner. Two NBE programs (mAbTree and Alveolus Bio) are on track for human studies in FY27, as is the first ADC biosimilar. Recombinant human albumin is expected to start human clinical studies in the current year with filing in India next year. Management described Biologics growth potential as significantly higher given the smaller base, with a larger portion expected from international markets.

CDMO scale-up and strategic investments. Across the Shilpa Group there are more than 20 CDMO customers and more than 25 NCE programs, with only one product currently commercial on the CDMO side; three late-stage programs are expected to enter commercialization next year. Management confirmed that where Shilpa invests in smaller biotechs alongside supply contracts, outcomes can be materially higher than a normal CDMO arrangement. The Board approved a strategic long-term equity partnership by Shilpa Biocare Private Limited in Gate2Brain aggregating up to EUR 7 million, in a fungible manner through cash infusion, equity for services, or project development.

New product approval. CDSCO’s Subject Expert Committee granted final approval for marketing authorization of OERIS (Ondansetron Extended-Release Injection, 100 mg/mL), described as the world’s first extended-release ondansetron injectable, providing sustained antiemetic action up to 120 hours after a single administration, based on a successful multicenter, randomized, double-blind Phase III trial for CINV prophylaxis and control. The product has patent protection in India until 2039, and the company plans commercial launch across India upon receipt of final marketing authorization.

Capital allocation shift. Management stated the reinvestment phase is largely behind: no significant new capex is foreseen for at least three years in Biologics or Albumin, with continued investment only in API and select new formulation molecules. Q1 capex was about INR 114 crores, funded through internal accruals.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory delays or divergent regulatory views slowing the pipeline, including the Unicycive CRL and refiling timeline.
  • Raw material price inflation with only partial pass-through, pressuring gross margins.
  • Partner-dependent commercialization timelines for NCE and CDMO programs, since these programs are run by partners, not Shilpa.
  • Concentration and timing risk in tendered businesses (Europe, complex U.S. generics), producing quarter-to-quarter revenue variability.
  • Litigation and insolvency exposure: the Sartorius settlement (Rs. 9,00,00,000) and the FTF Pharma CIRP admission (alleged operational debt of Rs. 2,18,03,319).
  • Policy risk from U.S. tariffs and the U.S. Biosecurity Act, though management currently sees no major impact.
  • Execution risk in monetizing underutilized Biologics, NBE and formulation assets; management acknowledged these divisions were ROCE-negative about 1–1.5 years ago and are now ROCE-positive at a lower level.
  • Tax normalization to around 25% after the Q1 benefit from deferred tax liability reversal.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements:

  • FY27 is expected to be stronger, with momentum building as regulatory and manufacturing investments mature and scale; the API business is likely to see steady growth in FY27.
  • Three NCE programs in advanced phases to commercialize in FY28; 15 oncology validations in FY27; peptide block commissioning by end of FY27.
  • Nor-UDCA global Phase II in FY27; Abraxane, Enzalutamide, Abiraterone and Rotigotine U.S. launches in FY28; Aflibercept India launch in FY27; Nivolumab India launch in FY28; recombinant human albumin human studies this year and India filing next year; first ADC biosimilar and two NBE programs into human studies in FY27.
  • Tax rate to normalize at around 25% in coming quarters.
  • EBITDA margins expected to remain in a similar range (~30%), with management preferring to “be conservative and overdeliver”; no specific numeric growth guidance provided.
  • ROCE expected to improve on better business mix and operating leverage; growth trajectory described as robust with faster profitability growth.

Observed performance against prior statements:

  • The claim that reinvestment is behind and harvesting ahead is supported by four successive record quarters, 43% revenue growth, 30% EBITDA margin, net debt-to-EBITDA of 1.3x, ROCE of 12.5% (18.3% adjusted), and the AA- upgrade.
  • The stated intent to maintain ~30% EBITDA margins has held: margins were maintained around 30% despite the gross margin dip to 71%.
  • The expectation of margin normalization flagged “2, 3 quarters back” has not materialized, with margins consistently sustained around 30%.
  • Nor-UDCA was described as performing as expected with strong FY27 order visibility, though the sequential dip in domestic revenue prompted investor questions; management attributed this to stocking and manufacturing schedules rather than demand.
  • The strategy of partnering biosimilars is progressing: Aflibercept has three India partners and Nivolumab has an Orion Corporation European partnership, while management noted eight molecules still require partnering.
  • The stated preference for sustainable, complex products over short-lived generic opportunities is reflected in the product selection for FY28 launches (Rotigotine as first-wave generic; Abraxane as a complex, tender-heavy product with few generic players).
  • Corporate actions have proceeded as communicated: the 39th AGM was held on 11 September 2026 with all five resolutions passed, including adoption of financial statements, a final dividend of Rs. 0.60 per equity share, appointment of Mr. Sharath Reddy Kalakota as Director, ratification of cost auditor remuneration (M/s. V.J. Talati & Co.), and approval of commission to Mr. Madhav Vishnukant Bhutada (up to 1% of SBPL net profits w.e.f. 1 April 2026).

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a product-specific bull case centered on RTU injectables, API expansion, and differentiated launches, with concerns about regulatory compliance and execution risk, to a broader strategic optimism around biosimilars, consumer business, NCEs, and acquisitions. The compliance risk theme persisted, morphing into ongoing challenges at INDR and the SASV online pharmacy transition, while pricing pressures on generic APIs and domestic weakness remained. The overall thesis strengthened as strong US performance and new launches validated the growth drivers, shifting the risk emphasis from initial execution fears to near-term earnings unpredictability.
