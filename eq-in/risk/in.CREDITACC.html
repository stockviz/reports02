CreditAccess Grameen: Recent Developments and Outlook

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Industry credit cycle overhang. The company describes navigating an “industry crisis” through FY25 and FY26, with FY25 credit cost at 7.7% and FY26 at 6.7%, and GNPA of 4.76% in FY25 moderating to 3.17% in FY26. FY26 ROA and ROE (2.7% and 10.7%) remained below the levels of FY24 (5.6% and 24.9%).

Borrower base contraction. Borrowers declined 2.4% YoY to 44.51 lakh in Q1 FY27, with Karnataka borrowers falling from 1,127 thousand to 1,021 thousand and Tamil Nadu from 822 thousand to 766 thousand. GL AUM in Karnataka declined from INR 7,018 Cr to INR 5,887 Cr YoY.

Disbursement seasonality. Disbursements fell 26.5% QoQ to INR 6,107 Cr in Q1 FY27, attributed to typical Q1 seasonality.

Write-offs. Q1 FY27 included INR 364 Cr of write-offs, with a write-off impact on credit cost of INR 111 Cr. AUM growth of 16.4% YoY was achieved despite a 6.3% TTM write-off.

Geopolitical provisioning. The revised ECL model incorporates additional provisions of INR 41 Cr in light of the ongoing West-Asia crisis.

Mortgage segment stress. RF mortgage PAR 90+ rose from 0.6% in Q1 FY26 to 1.5% in Q1 FY27, with PAR 0+ increasing from 1.8% to 2.2%.

Elevated attrition. Employee attrition stood at 20.6% in Q1 FY27, though improved from 25.8% in Q1 FY26.

Promoter stake sale. On August 6, 2026, promoter CreditAccess India B.V. sold 36,57,500 equity shares (2.28% of paid-up capital as of June 30, 2026) via block deal, described as a liquidity measure for CAI’s long-term investors and aimed at enhancing free float. CAI holds 66.21% and has reaffirmed commitment to hold up to the regulatory requirement.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

AUM momentum. AUM grew 16.4% YoY and 2.5% QoQ to INR 30,319 Cr in Q1 FY27. The ten-year AUM CAGR is 28.6%.

Retail Finance scale-up. RF share of AUM rose 250 bps QoQ to 20.6% from 18.1%, up from 6.8% in Q1 FY26. RF AUM grew from INR 1,784 Cr to INR 6,258 Cr YoY.

Asset quality normalisation. PAR 1-90 at 74 bps is described as back to pre-crisis levels. GNPA improved to 2.18% (from 4.70% YoY and 3.17% QoQ); NNPA at 0.76%; PAR 90+ at 1.46%. Credit cost fell to 0.72% (non-annualised) in Q1 FY27 from 2.07% in Q2 FY26. X-Bucket CE for Jun-26 was 99.68%.

Profitability rebound. PAT rose 719.7% YoY to INR 493 Cr; PPOP rose 33.6% YoY to INR 873 Cr. NIM expanded to 14.4% (from 12.8% YoY), interest spread to 12.6%, ROA to 5.9% and ROE to 24.4% (TTM: 4.0% and 16.0%).

Funding and capital strength. CRAR at 24.9% (Tier 1: 24.2%); D/E at 3.0; total equity INR 8,347 Cr. Liquidity assets of INR 3,536 Cr (10.4% of total assets); undrawn sanctions of INR 2,993 Cr and pipeline of INR 9,440 Cr. Cost of borrowing at 9.2%. Private NCD issuance of INR 425 Cr diversified the liability base. The Board approved a public NCD issue of up to INR 2,000 Cr and private placement of NCDs up to INR 1,000 Cr on July 24, 2026.

Digital and customer engagement. Grameen Mahi onboarded 4.0 lakh customers in Q1 FY27, reaching 15.3 lakh (34.5% of borrowers). Digital collections rose to 24.2% from 16.3% YoY. New borrowers added: 2.5 lakh, 35% NTC.

Graduation and household expansion. AUM from unique GL borrowers rose to 45.7% (Jun-26) from 26.6% (Aug-24). The company frames its evolution from microfinance to lifecycle finance, targeting a ~INR 70 lakh Cr rural/semi-urban micro-retail credit market.

Network expansion. Branches grew 7.7% YoY to 2,276, with 42 new branches; 457 districts covered, with 86.8% of districts representing under 0.5% of AUM.

Project ‘Shakti’. Strategic vision targets ROE of 18.0%–20.0%, leadership in inclusive finance, secured lending growth via adjacencies, digital-enabled services for women, and technology/AI as growth enablers.

ESG positioning. Sustainalytics ESG risk score 20.7 (Medium Risk); S&P Global 53/100; CDP ‘C’; M-CRIL Gold client protection certification; NSE ESG Index 67/100. CSR: 18.36 lakh+ beneficiaries, INR 76 Cr invested, 60+ aspirational districts (FY2021–FY2026).

3. Key Risks

  • Credit cost volatility: FY25 and FY26 credit costs of 7.7% and 6.7% demonstrate susceptibility to sector-wide stress; the revised ECL model now incorporates stress scenarios including regulatory changes, weather patterns, and economic outlook.
  • Geopolitical exposure: The West-Asia crisis prompted INR 41 Cr of additional ECL provisions.
  • Concentration: Top 5 states account for 81.8% of AUM; Karnataka alone is 29.3%. Karnataka PAR 90+ was 1.6% in Jun-26 (down from 2.8% in Mar-26), while Madhya Pradesh (1.9%) and Bihar (2.1%) remain relatively elevated.
  • Mortgage book deterioration: RF mortgage PAR 90+ has risen for five consecutive quarters to 1.5%.
  • Regulatory guardrails: The company cites adherence to MFIN guardrails and risk-based pricing as shaping growth strategy.
  • Attrition and execution: Employee attrition at 20.6% and the need for manpower stabilisation are flagged.
  • Funding concentration: Foreign borrowings at 23.9% of the liability mix; 39% of pipeline sanctions are foreign.
  • Promoter overhang: CAI’s 66.21% holding and stated intent to hold only up to the regulatory requirement implies potential future dilution.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 guidance: AUM growth 20.0%–25.0%; NIM 12.8%–13.2%; credit cost 3.0%–4.0%; cost-to-income 33.0%–35.0%; ROA 4.0%–4.8%; ROE 16.0%–20.0%.

Observed Q1 FY27 performance against guidance: - AUM growth: 16.4% YoY — below the 20–25% guided range. - NIM: 14.4% — above the 12.8–13.2% guided range. - Credit cost: 0.72% non-annualised in Q1 — the quarterly run-rate is below the 3.0–4.0% annual guidance, though the company notes PAR accretion remains within the normalised range. - Cost-to-income: 29.3% — better than the 33.0–35.0% guided range. - ROA: 5.9% (TTM 4.0%) — the TTM figure sits at the lower end of the 4.0–4.8% range. - ROE: 24.4% (TTM 16.0%) — the TTM figure sits at the lower end of the 16.0–20.0% range.

Project ‘Shakti’ ROE target: 18.0%–20.0%, versus TTM ROE of 16.0% in Q1 FY27 and FY26 ROE of 10.7%.

Historical context: The company notes return ratios during the FY25/FY26 industry crisis (ROA 2.7%/1.9%; ROE 10.7%/7.7%) were higher than during the Covid crisis (ROA 2.2%/1.1%; ROE 9.0%/4.9%), describing itself as having emerged stronger.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from an upbeat tone emphasizing strong earnings, expanding NIMs, and improving asset quality to a more cautious stance focused on asset quality volatility, elevated write-offs, and macroeconomic uncertainties. Persistent themes included robust borrower acquisition, retail finance graduation, and margin expansion, while new emphases emerged on Project Shakti execution and digital efficiency under the Grameen Mahi platform.

Fears that came true

  • External shocks materialized through global uncertainties (West Asia conflict, El Niño) and sector stress, contemporaneous with multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and negative actual returns.

Optimism that failed

  • The early expectation of improving asset quality with decreasing GNPA and healthy ECL coverage failed to hold, as later reports highlighted elevated write-offs (6.3%) and asset quality volatility trumping the earlier recovery assumptions.
