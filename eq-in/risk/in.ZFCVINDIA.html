Headwinds and Challenges

  • GST show cause notice. On August 11, 2026, the Company received a show cause notice from The Additional Commissioner GST & Central Excise, Audit-I Commissionerate, Chennai, issued pursuant to an audit under Section 65 of the CGST Act, 2017. The notice proposes a demand of INR 27,99,29,495, primarily on account of excess availment of ITC as reflected in Table 8(D) of GSTR-9, availment of ineligible ITC, excess availment of ITC on import as compared to GSTR-2B, and ineligible ITC on import on account of time limitation. The period covered is April 2020 to March 2024. The notice proposes to levy a penalty under Section 74 of the CGST Act. The Company is in the process of filing a detailed response. It has stated that there is no material impact of the same on its financial, operations or other activities.
  • New Labour Codes. The Company assessed the impact of changes arising from the four Labour Codes notified by the Government of India — the Code on Wages, 2019; the Industrial Relations Code, 2020; the Code on Social Security, 2020; and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 — notified on November 21, 2025. This resulted in a one-time estimated increase in provision for employee benefits of INR 793.51 lakhs, recognised under the “Exceptional item” in the consolidated financial results for the year ended March 31, 2026. The Company continues to monitor developments and will assess additional guidance/clarifications as notified by regulatory authorities.
  • Dilution of shareholder support on a board appointment. At the 22nd AGM held on July 24, 2026, the resolution for appointment of Ms. Claudia Christina Jehle as a Non-Executive Non-Independent Director saw 3.80% of valid votes cast against it (38,75,141 shares), with 11.4858% of Public-Institutions votes cast against. All five resolutions were passed with the requisite majority.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • ESC business nominations from three major OEMs. On July 28, 2026, the Company announced it had secured Electronic Stability Control (ESC) business nominations from three leading Indian commercial vehicle OEMs for multi-year domestic programs. The nominations cover truck platforms across both 24V and 12V architectures, with Start of Production (SOP) scheduled for Q3 2027. The product will be assembled locally and supplied from ZF CVCS India’s manufacturing facilities in India.
  • Regulatory mandate for ESC. India’s AIS regulations will make Electronic Stability Control mandatory for trucks from October 1, 2027. The Company notes that demand for proven stability-control technologies is increasing rapidly as vehicle manufacturers prepare next-generation platforms to meet the new requirements.
  • Proven product and scale. ESCsmart™ is already deployed on more than 60,000 vehicles in India and is backed by ZF’s 20+ years of ESC expertise and over 3 million systems delivered globally. The Company cites dedicated validation capabilities at its ESC test track, strong local engineering and manufacturing expertise, and end-to-end ESC engineering support including validation and pre-homologation services.
  • Global alignment. Several mature commercial vehicle markets already mandate ESC, and the India implementation is described as a significant step towards aligning with global safety standards, expected to contribute to safer roads, improved vehicle stability and more secure freight transportation.
  • Dividend continuity. The Board recommended a dividend of Rs. 4/- per share for the financial year ended March 31, 2026, which was approved by shareholders at the 22nd AGM.
  • Bonus issue. The Board on May 13, 2026 recommended a bonus issue in the ratio of 1:5 (one equity share of Rs. 5/- each for every five equity shares of Rs. 5/- each held). The bonus issue was approved by shareholders through postal ballot on June 17, 2026, and 9,48,37,920 equity shares were allotted on June 25, 2026. The record date was June 24, 2026.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from an initial Hold built on CV-cycle peaking, import/margin and export-execution worries to a Buy conviction anchored on regulatory safety mandates, domestic CV growth and technology wins. Export risk persisted but was reframed from general execution/competition concerns to U.S.-tariff-driven revenue weakness and geopolitical disruptions. Early fears of OEM bargaining power and new-segment competition faded, replaced by raw-material/operating-cost inflation; CV-cycle caution was dropped after heavy-truck demand stayed robust through FY26.

Fears that came true

  • The early export-execution risk materialized as FY26 export revenue fell 11.1% YoY, with Americas revenue down ~22% due to U.S. tariffs and weak demand, and the 2026 Buy calls saw negative actual returns (-0.6%, -1.6%).
  • The early margin-pressure/input-cost concern materialized through sharp aluminium inflation (₹233/kg to ₹274/kg in Q4FY26) and Q4FY26 EBITDA margin contraction of ~210–230 bps from cost pressures, weighing on the 2026 Buy outcomes.

Optimism that failed

  • The export-momentum tailwind failed: strong EU/US demand and Oragadam scale-up did not prevent an 11.1% YoY export decline, with the U.S. market especially weak.
  • The expected gross-margin improvement from the high-margin electronic-product mix shift did not visibly materialize, as raw-material inflation and salary revisions compressed margins.
