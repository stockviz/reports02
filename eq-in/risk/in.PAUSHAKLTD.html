Paushak Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements: Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Cost pressure and margin mix. In the quarter ended 30 June 2026, revenue from operations rose to ₹8,355 lakh from ₹5,588 lakh in the same quarter a year earlier, but total expenses rose faster, to ₹6,768 lakh from ₹4,214 lakh. Cost of materials consumed more than doubled year-on-year (₹2,899 lakh versus ₹1,086 lakh), and depreciation and amortisation more than doubled (₹842 lakh versus ₹406 lakh). Finance costs also rose sharply to ₹136 lakh from ₹4 lakh. The result is that profit before tax of ₹1,902 lakh was only about 22% higher than the ₹1,563 lakh recorded a year earlier, despite roughly 50% higher revenue.

Sequential volatility. Compared with the immediately preceding quarter (Q4 FY2025-26, ended 31 March 2026), revenue improved (₹8,355 lakh versus ₹5,514 lakh) but other income fell to ₹315 lakh from ₹821 lakh. Total comprehensive income for Q4 FY2025-26 was ₹478 lakh, depressed by an item that will be reclassified to profit or loss of negative ₹785 lakh (with a related tax effect of ₹110 lakh), against nil such item in the June 2026 quarter.

Shareholder servicing gaps. The company’s letter dated 2 July 2026 notes that no e-mail address was registered against certain shareholders’ demat accounts or folios, requiring physical follow-up communication. It also flags that shareholders holding shares in physical form must have PAN, contact details, mobile number, bank account details and specimen signature fully updated with the Registrar and Transfer Agent for dividends to be paid electronically.

Unclaimed shares in escrow. Following the October 2025 corporate actions, new equity shares against shares held in physical form were credited to a designated Escrow Demat Account rather than to shareholders directly. Claiming them requires submission of original old share certificates (face value ₹10), a client master list, PAN, address proof, cancelled cheque and KYC documents in prescribed formats. This creates a servicing and reconciliation burden for the company and a delay for affected shareholders.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong year-on-year revenue growth. Revenue from operations for the June 2026 quarter was ₹8,355 lakh against ₹5,588 lakh for the June 2025 quarter. For the full year ended 31 March 2026, revenue from operations was ₹21,860 lakh.

Profit growth. Profit after tax for the June 2026 quarter was ₹1,510 lakh against ₹1,203 lakh a year earlier. Earnings per share (basic and diluted) was ₹6.13 for the quarter, against ₹4.88 restated for the prior-year quarter, and ₹15.95 for the full year ended 31 March 2026.

Improved sequential profitability. Profit after tax rose to ₹1,510 lakh in the June 2026 quarter from ₹1,251 lakh in the March 2026 quarter, and total comprehensive income rose to ₹1,512 lakh from ₹478 lakh.

Capital structure actions. Members approved, by postal ballot on 18 September 2025, a subdivision of one equity share of face value ₹10 into two equity shares of face value ₹5, and the issue of three bonus equity shares of face value ₹5 for every one fully paid-up equity share of ₹5. These were carried out as on the record date of 3 October 2025. Paid-up equity share capital stood at ₹1,233 lakh as at 30 June 2026, against ₹308 lakh as at 30 June 2025, reflecting the bonus issue.

Dividend continuity. The board proposed, and shareholders approved at the 53rd AGM, a dividend on equity shares for FY2025-26, with a record date of 23 July 2026 and payment on or from 3 August 2026.

Single-segment focus. The company operates solely in speciality chemicals, with one reportable segment, and holds other equity excluding revaluation reserves of ₹38,363 lakh as at 31 March 2026.

3. Key Risks

Governance and related-party approval risk. Resolution 4 at the AGM concerned payment of commission to Mr. Udit Amin, a Non-Executive Director, for FY2025-26. It required a special resolution. While the promoter and promoter group were recorded as not interested in the resolution, the resolution passed with 98.31% of public non-institution votes in favour and 1.69% against — the highest dissent among the resolutions tabled. Mr. Udit Amin also stood for re-appointment by rotation under Resolution 3, which likewise drew 1.69% of public non-institution votes against.

Concentration of voting power. Promoter and promoter group holdings of 1,65,89,144 shares represented 100% participation in the poll and constituted the large majority of the 1,67,03,516 votes polled (67.74% of outstanding shares). Public institutions held only 19,924 shares and polled 3,662 votes (18.38% of their holding). Public non-institutions held 80,47,844 shares but polled only 1,10,710 votes (1.38% of their holding). This skew means outcomes are effectively determined by the promoter block, with limited public participation.

Cost and depreciation trajectory. The sharp rise in depreciation and finance costs alongside material costs indicates a heavier asset and borrowing base. If revenue growth does not keep pace, profitability could be squeezed, as seen in the June 2026 quarter where expense growth outpaced revenue growth.

Dependence on other income. Other income contributed ₹315 lakh in the June 2026 quarter and ₹1,211 lakh for FY2025-26. Its quarter-to-quarter variability (₹821 lakh in Q4 FY2025-26 versus ₹315 lakh in Q1 FY2026-27) adds volatility to total income.

Physical shareholding and KYC non-compliance. Shareholders in physical form who have not updated PAN, bank details and KYC cannot receive dividends electronically, and their bonus/subdivided shares remain in an escrow demat account until documentation is completed. This is an ongoing operational and compliance exposure.
