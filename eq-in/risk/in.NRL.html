NRL — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical and regional tensions in Eastern Europe and the Middle East, alongside ongoing geopolitical conflicts, are cited as material headwinds.
  • Supply chain disruption in the Gulf shipping corridor affected the wider Indian non-ferrous recycling industry during Q1 FY 2026-27; NRL’s multi-geography sourcing network across Europe, USA, UAE and other developed markets provided operational resilience through this period.
  • Tight domestic scrap supply and higher LME metal prices during the quarter shaped industry conditions, though they supported better realisations.
  • Volatility in global prices of metals, crude oil and energy; foreign exchange and financial risks; and an inherently volatile metal scrap market.
  • Logistical constraints: shipping times and logistical delays require 45 to 60 days of inventory, and the company operates without hedging mechanisms for US dollars, Euros, pounds, or on the LME.
  • Economic slowdown affecting key end-user industries, increasing competition, changing environmental regulations and compliance risks, rapid technological changes, cybersecurity risks, and extreme weather and climate-related events.
  • Rising stakeholder expectations regarding ESG performance.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Structural policy support: India’s transition to a circular economy, the Government’s focus on Aatmanirbhar Bharat, and initiatives such as Make in India, PM Gati Shakti, National Infrastructure Pipeline, Production Linked Incentive Schemes, plus focus on infrastructure development, manufacturing expansion, renewable energy, urbanization and logistics modernization.
  • Rising domestic demand for recycled non-ferrous metals across automotive, solar, electrical and industrial end-markets.
  • Global transition towards renewable energy, electric vehicles and sustainable manufacturing, and India’s emergence as an alternative manufacturing destination within global supply chains.
  • Capacity expansion: the new 4.5-acre Sampla (Haryana) facility for zinc ingots and blended materials is progressing as planned, with commissioning to unlock incremental installed capacity. The LFP lithium-ion battery recycling facility in Sampla is expected to become operational over the next 2 to 3 months.
  • Nupur Extrusion Private Limited continues to operate at full capacity from its Haryana facility, catering to solar plant manufacturing and OEM customers — a segment witnessing strong secular demand.
  • Aluminium value chain expansion: Group company Frank Metals Recyclers Limited (subsidiary) has commenced its foray into the aluminium extrusion business, investing in extrusion-related machinery and equipment, with further planned investments and capacity expansion.
  • Diversified multi-geography sourcing model, forward integration into value-added products and auto components, and increased capacity utilization across facilities.
  • Strengthening subsidiary contribution at group level (Frank Metals Recyclers Limited, Tycod Autotech Private Limited, Nupur Extrusion Private Limited, Eligo Business & Advisory Private Limited and Nupur Business & Consulting Private Limited), in line with the strategy of building an integrated non-ferrous recycling platform.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity price and currency exposure: fluctuations in global metal, crude oil and energy prices; foreign exchange and financial risks; no hedging for USD, EUR, GBP or LME.
  • Supply chain and logistics risk: geopolitical conflicts, regional tensions, Gulf shipping corridor disruptions, shipping times and logistical delays necessitating 45–60 days of inventory.
  • Demand-side risk: economic slowdown affecting key end-user industries.
  • Competitive and regulatory risk: increasing competition; changing environmental regulations and compliance risks; changes in government regulations and tax laws.
  • Operational and technological risk: rapid technological changes; cybersecurity risks; extreme weather and climate-related events.
  • ESG and stakeholder risk: rising stakeholder expectations regarding ESG performance.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: management’s objectives, projections, estimates and expectations are forward-looking statements, and actual results could differ materially due to economic conditions affecting demand, supply and prices, domestic and overseas market conditions, and changes in government regulations and tax laws.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Guidance / forward-looking statements:

  • Management expects the new LFP battery recycling facility in Sampla, Haryana to become operational over the next 2 to 3 months.
  • Management anticipates stable or improving blended margins ranging between 7% to 10% (and 10% to 25% gross margins).
  • Management projects that future topline numbers will be in multiples of current revenues.
  • Management states it remains focused on scaling recycling and processing capacity, deepening the product portfolio through extrusion and alloy verticals, strengthening subsidiary contribution and driving sustained margin expansion, expressing confidence in delivering profitable growth with a healthy balance sheet, diversified sourcing base and clear execution roadmap.
  • Management notes that while global economic activity is expected to remain positive and India’s medium-to-long-term economic outlook remains favourable — driven by domestic fundamentals, infrastructure investments and demographic advantages — these are forward-looking statements subject to material differences.

Observed performance (Q1 FY 2026-27, quarter ended June 30, 2026):

  • Total Revenue of ₹8,303.05 Lakh, up 56.56% YoY (from ₹5,303.29 Lakh in Q1 FY 2025-26) and up 37.31% QoQ (from ₹6,046.86 Lakh in Q4 FY 2025-26).
  • EBITDA of ₹1,285.60 Lakh, up 111.46% YoY (from ₹607.96 Lakh) and up 88.33% QoQ (from ₹682.63 Lakh); EBITDA margin of 15.48% versus 11.46% YoY and 11.29% QoQ.
  • PAT of ₹738.57 Lakh, up 82.61% YoY (from ₹404.45 Lakh) and up 120.61% QoQ (from ₹334.78 Lakh); PAT margin of 8.9% versus 7.63% YoY and 5.54% QoQ.
  • FY 2025-26 (full year): Total Revenue ₹22,661.35 Lakh, EBITDA ₹2,775.11 Lakh (12.25% margin), PAT ₹1,648.46 Lakh (7.27% margin). Consolidated total income ₹22,661.35 Lakh and consolidated net profit after tax ₹1,648.46 Lakh; standalone total income ₹14,259.67 Lakh and standalone net profit after tax ₹813.51 Lakh.
  • Growth drivers cited: higher volumes across non-ferrous scrap trading, improved realisations, steady contribution from Frank Metals and the newly commissioned Nupur Extrusion facility in Haryana; better product mix, improved capacity utilisation and disciplined cost management.
  • Balance sheet: the company is described as almost debt-free at its core, funding expansions largely through internal accruals.
  • Dividend: the Board of Directors did not recommend any dividend for FY 2025-26.

Other key facts:

  • The 8th Annual General Meeting of Nupur Recyclers Limited is scheduled for September 29, 2026.
  • The company has five subsidiaries as of March 31, 2026, including material subsidiaries Frank Metals Recyclers Limited and Tycod Autotech Private Limited.
