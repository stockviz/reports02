MANAKSTEEL: Recent Corporate Announcements — Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Sector-level pressures. The company’s disclosures point to volatility in scrap prices, persistent raw material shortages (particularly iron ore and coal), limited availability of non-coking coal, and elevated conversion costs. Inadequate logistics infrastructure, high taxation and duties in India relative to global peers, and regulatory/policy challenges around land acquisition add to the operating burden.

Competitive and technological pressure. Aggressive pricing and dumping of steel products by China, the proliferation of low-cost steel produced using lower-grade inputs, and increasing competition from alternative materials such as High Entropy Alloys and advanced ceramics all weigh on the competitive landscape. The sector also faces a limited pace of technological innovation and adoption.

Project execution and policy dependence. The Haldia expansion is contingent on external factors. The company has approached the State Government seeking special incentives for the Specialty Steel sector under the upcoming State Industrial Policy, and has stated that the timelines for implementation of the expansion project will be finalised based on the outcome of the incentive request. The company also cites the evolving market scenario and the upcoming West Bengal industrial policy as reasons for structuring the expansion in phases.

Working capital and cost dynamics. Standalone finance cost for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 was ₹1,578.78 lacs, up from ₹1,135.00 lacs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 and ₹269.43 lacs in the quarter ended 30 June 2025. Standalone cost of materials consumed (including traded goods) rose to ₹89,076.62 lacs in Q1 FY2026-27 from ₹22,167.30 lacs in Q1 FY2025-26, alongside a sharp swing in the change in inventories line.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Demand drivers. Rising steel consumption is being driven by infrastructure development and sustained growth in the automobile and railway sectors. Demand for value-added coated steel products is increasing across housing, industrial, and warehousing applications.

Operational and technological opportunities. The company identifies scope for enhanced digitisation and adoption of advanced manufacturing technologies, significant potential for automation, opportunities to reduce power costs through alternative sources, innovation in raw material usage, process improvements, and advancement in electric arc furnace technologies, with the potential to achieve globally competitive cost structures.

Capacity expansion at Haldia. On 10 August 2026, the Board granted in-principle approval for expansion of the manufacturing facility at Haldia, West Bengal for production of Cold-Rolled Coil and Coated Steel in two phases, at an estimated investment of approximately ₹800 crore, funded through a mix of debt and internal accruals. Phase I involves a proposed addition of 95,000 TPA of Cold Rolled Coil, 31,000 TPA of Coated (GL/GP), and 90,000 TPA of Colour Coated capacity, at an investment of approximately ₹375 crore, to be added by the financial year ended 2029-30 (approx.). Phase II involves a further proposed addition of 1,00,000 TPA CR, 1,00,000 TPA Coated, and 90,000 TPA Colour Coated, at approximately ₹425 crore plus land value, to be added by the financial year ended 2033-34 (approx.). The first phase includes the previously announced Colour Coating Line – II, presently under implementation, and a Cold Rolling Mill whose implementation is scheduled to commence shortly. The company also proposes additional GP/Alu Zinc Coils production capacity and is evaluating a 10 MW captive solar power plant to support expanded operations.

Existing capacity utilisation. Existing capacity utilisation is approximately 86% for Cold Rolled Coil (80,000 TPA), approximately 75% for Coated GL/GP (1,44,200 TPA), and approximately 100% for Colour Coated (60,000 TPA), indicating limited headroom in the colour-coated line.

Financial scale-up in FY 2025-26. Revenue crossed the ₹1,000 crore milestone, reaching ₹1,053.19 crore (an 80.29% increase); EBITDA almost tripled to ₹76.56 crore; and profit after tax rose 226.24% to ₹38.17 crore. A ₹125 crore capacity expansion was undertaken entirely through internal accruals without raising long-term debt, including commissioning of a new Alu-Zinc coating line (1,10,200 TPA) and augmentation of the colour-coating line. The debt-equity ratio improved from 0.67x to 0.44x, ROCE increased to 15.68%, and the credit rating was upgraded to ICRA A (Stable).

3. Key Risks

  • Policy and incentive risk: Expansion timelines depend on the outcome of the incentive request under the upcoming West Bengal State Industrial Policy.
  • Commodity and input risk: Scrap price volatility, iron ore and coal shortages, and limited non-coking coal availability.
  • Competitive risk: Chinese dumping and aggressive pricing, low-cost steel from lower-grade inputs, and substitution by alternative materials.
  • Execution risk: The two-phase Haldia programme spans approximately FY2029-30 and FY2033-34, with Phase II also dependent on land value and financing through debt and internal accruals.
  • Cost and leverage risk: Rising finance costs and material costs, with the expansion to be funded partly through debt.
  • Infrastructure and regulatory risk: Inadequate logistics infrastructure, high taxation and duties versus global peers, and land acquisition challenges.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Guidance. Management expects consolidated revenues of ₹1,500 crore in FY 2026-27, driven by full-year visibility of capacity expansion and commissioning of a new 90,000 TPA colour-coated line in FY 2026-27. Following commissioning of a planned ₹100 crore 6-hi Reversible Cold Rolling Mill by December 2027, the company expects revenues of approximately ₹3,000 crore in FY 2028-29.

Observed performance. For the quarter ended 30 June 2026, standalone revenue from operations was ₹30,221.55 lacs, with total income of ₹30,588.34 lacs and net profit of ₹2,237.92 lacs (EPS ₹3.41). Consolidated revenue from operations was ₹32,748.77 lacs, with total income of ₹33,115.59 lacs and net profit of ₹2,264.59 lacs (EPS ₹3.46). On a year-on-year basis, standalone revenue from operations rose from ₹20,097.78 lacs in Q1 FY2025-26 to ₹30,221.55 lacs in Q1 FY2026-27, and standalone net profit rose from ₹634.71 lacs to ₹2,237.92 lacs. Sequentially, standalone revenue from operations was ₹31,008.08 lacs in Q4 FY2025-26 versus ₹30,221.55 lacs in Q1 FY2026-27, while standalone net profit was ₹1,871.20 lacs versus ₹2,237.92 lacs.
