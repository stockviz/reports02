Kirloskar Brothers Limited — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Foundry modernization disrupted dispatches. The company completed a foundry improvement program, but the transition left some orders half-completed, preventing full shipment. Management stated that inventory rose as a result, with some orders withheld because they could not be shipped as complete sets. This affected industrial segment revenue and standalone dispatch timing. Management indicated improvements were already visible in the current month and expected this to reflect in the following quarter.

International margins moderated. International EBITDA stood at Rs.207 million with a 5.1% margin. This moderation was attributed to a lower contribution from the services business, which traditionally carries higher margins. The weakness was concentrated in SPP UK, where chemical and petrochemical service contracts were described as idling amid difficult conditions in the European and UK chemical industry, including high energy prices. Rodelta also saw a jump in losses, attributed to delayed execution while Dutch entities were being consolidated.

Order inflow growth was modest. Consolidated order intake grew 4% year-on-year to Rs.13,954 million, with standalone bookings growth of 5.4% (after correction of an initially stated 14.9%). Management attributed this to a certain large order being delayed, while stating other bookings were as per plan.

KPML margin compression. KPML’s margin fell from 12.5% to 7.3% in the quarter. Management explained this was due to the merger of the loss-making TKSL (The Kolhapur Steel Limited) into KPML, with the expectation that margins improve going forward as TKSL turns around.

Legal proceedings. The Supreme Court disposed of Special Leave Petitions relating to the Deed of Family Settlement dated September 11, 2009, directing constitution of a three-member Arbitral Tribunal (Justice Nitin Madhukar Jamdar and Justice K. R. Shriram as nominee arbitrators, seat at Pune) to decide all contentions on arbitrability as a preliminary issue. The disputes concern the DFS, non-compete obligations, board appointments, and arbitration proceedings. Management stated the financial impact cannot presently be ascertained.

Rising costs. Finance costs and other expenses increased, with other expenses attributed to digitalization and advertisement spending.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Consolidated and standalone growth. Consolidated revenue for Q1 FY27 was Rs.11,049 million, up 13% year-on-year. Standalone revenue rose 9% to Rs.6,738 million, with EBITDA up 16% to Rs.920 million and PAT up 15% to Rs.540 million. International revenue grew 19% year-on-year.

Order book visibility. Domestic pending orders stood at Rs.25,577 million (excluding small pump order book), and overseas pending orders at Rs.15,045 million. Management indicated roughly two-thirds of the domestic order board could be executed in the current year, noting that the retail/small pump business (approximately 45–50% of business) has no order book as it is delivered in the same month.

Data centers and US operations. SPP USA saw encouraging traction in data centers, fire, and HVAC projects, with US growth of over 20% quarter-on-quarter. Management described being in final steps of signing a multinational framework contract with a major US data center operator, covering pumps and modular systems globally. Hyperscale data center packages (excluding intake water systems) were described as typically USD 7.5–10 million, sometimes up to USD 12 million. Approximately 4,000 operating data centers exist in the US, with another 2,000 having received planning permission.

Nuclear and power. Management stated receipt of primary circuit pump orders close to Rs.70 crores in Q1, plus roughly Rs.40 crores of secondary circuit orders for nuclear power plants. KBL described itself as having 100% market share in metallic volute pumps for nuclear plants and as the world’s largest manufacturer of concrete volute pumps. Power order book grew 30%.

Oil and gas / petrol pumps. Approximately Rs.217 crores of bookings in this business as of Q1 end, with around Rs.74 crores dispatched during the quarter. KBL is qualified by all three to four large PSUs buying this product.

Services and IoT. Management expects the services business to recover, with contracts with power plants and water utilities expected to take effect, restoring blended margins. IoT devices are in the hundreds in India; a second version allows multiple pumps per device, reducing customer cost. Demand is seen in municipal water and irrigation projects where remote pump houses lack skilled maintenance manpower.

New order win. SPP Pumps Ltd, UK secured a significant order from Saipem Offshore Construction SPA for vertical pumps and spares worth GBP 11.7 million (approx. Rs.149.59 crore), executable over 52–60 weeks from receipt.

Board strengthening. Appointment of Dr. Madhuri Kanitkar as Independent Director for five years from September 15, 2026 to September 14, 2031, intended to strengthen the Board with expertise in strategy, management, administration, governance, and institution building.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution timing risk: Capital goods orders can be delayed or arrive in clusters; management advised evaluating performance half-yearly or annually rather than quarterly.
  • Geographic concentration risk: SPP UK services weakness tied to European chemical industry conditions; Rodelta losses from delayed Dutch entity consolidation.
  • Order inflow volatility: A single large delayed order materially affected quarterly intake growth.
  • Legal/arbitration risk: DFS-related disputes with financial impact not presently ascertainable.
  • Margin mix risk: Consolidated margins sensitive to services business contribution, which carries higher margins.
  • Subsidiary performance risk: TKSL was loss-making before merger into KPML; KPML margin declined as a result.
  • Government budget dependency: Marine and defense orders depend on government budget approvals and shipbuilder order placement timing.
  • Raw material cost risk: Price increases of approximately 10% since January were taken to cover raw material costs; management believes these are sufficient.

Broker Narrative

The first report was a macro-driven Buy, centred on FII inflows, earnings upgrades, attractive valuations, peak-rate hopes, and broad sector tailwinds. By the last report, the Over Weight view had narrowed to consumption-led festive demand, income-tax/GST reforms, and financial-sector fundamentals such as NIMs, operating leverage, and credit costs. The constructive stance and belief in domestic demand/reforms persisted, while early external macro risks such as monsoon, oil, global rates/war, and overbought markets were replaced by domestic credit-quality and SME/2-3 wheeler stress concerns.
