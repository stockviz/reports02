P N Gadgil Jewellers Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Elevated gold and silver prices. Management noted that the industry remained resilient “despite record gold prices” during April–June 2026, and that silver prices “hovering at a high level” dampened the Adhik Maas season, which is typically a silver-driven occasion.
  • Muted franchise and e-commerce growth. In Q1 FY27, franchise revenue grew only 8% and e-commerce 20%, against 56% retail growth. Management attributed the franchise softness to timing differences between when franchise partners pay and when stock is sold, and to the fact that franchise sales are booked B2B rather than B2C. E-commerce softness was attributed to a conscious decision to reduce bars and coin sales online and focus on jewellery.
  • Flat reported gross margin despite favourable mix. Analysts questioned why gross margin was flat year-over-year despite a higher retail mix. Management explained that hedging gains were similar in absolute terms on a larger revenue base, and that adjusted gross margin (net of hedging gains) improved by roughly 40–50 bps. Management also noted that new territories (UP and Central India) contributed only about 3% of the retail mix, so their higher-margin potential is not yet visible.
  • Other income decline. Other income fell to INR10.04 crores in Q1 FY27 from INR31.14 crores in Q4 FY26, which management attributed to a reduction in fixed deposits and funds being deployed toward hedging margins.
  • Inventory turnover normalisation. The inventory ratio declined to about 3.7x–4x from roughly 7x–8x two years earlier, which management said was in line with its guidance of 3.5x–4x, following the end of refinery and B2B sales.
  • Promoter shareholding overhang. An analyst flagged that promoter shareholding remains above 75% and described it as an overhang on the stock. Management said an enabling board resolution exists and that a QIP would be considered at a suitable time.
  • Related-party nature of the Silvostyle acquisition. The acquisition of Silvostyle Jewellers Limited is a related party transaction, as SJL is part of the promoter group, and Mr. Saurabh Gadgil, Mrs. Radhika Gadgil, and Mr. Aditya Gadgil are directors and shareholders of SJL. The transaction is stated to be undertaken at arm’s length.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue grew 41% year-over-year to INR2,413 crores, EBITDA grew 57% to INR192.4 crores (8% margin), and PAT grew 52% to INR105.3 crores (4.4% margin). Retail grew 56% with 46.1% SSSG. Festive sales grew 80.3% year-over-year to INR251.4 crores.
  • Store expansion pipeline. The company ended Q1 FY27 with 78 stores and plans to open around 25 stores in FY2027, reaching approximately 103 stores by year-end. Management outlined a path to about 140 stores by FY28 and 177 stores by March 2029, of which about 113 would be legacy PNG stores and about 64 LiteStyle stores. Expansion is planned to be franchise-led (FOCO) in FY27, with a mix of COCO and FOCO thereafter.
  • LiteStyle by PNG. The LiteStyle format posted a stud ratio of 32.9% in Q1 FY27, with gross margin of 18–20%. Management plans to shift the merchandise mix to 80% studded and 20% gold, targeting a 50–60% studded ratio and 30–35% gross margins over the next two years.
  • Studded jewellery traction. Retail stud ratio improved to 10.9% from 9.9% in the previous quarter. New stores in North and Central India are running stud ratios of 15–18%, ahead of the mature Maharashtra network.
  • Gold bars and coins conversion. 53% of gold bars and coins purchases converted into jewellery in Q1 FY27, up from 46% a year earlier.
  • New store in Gurugram. On August 15, 2026, PNG Jewellers launched a 3,500 sq. ft. company-owned, company-operated store in Sector 65, Gurugram, marking its entry into the NCR region. The store offers gold, diamond, and silver jewellery, with introductory offers running from August 15 to August 31, 2026.
  • Silvostyle acquisition. On August 20, 2026, the board approved the acquisition of 100% of Silvostyle Jewellers Limited for INR27.96 crores (INR27.68 crores via fresh issue and INR0.28 crores from existing shareholders, at INR18.64 per share). SJL acquired Silvostyle Jewellery LLP by slump sale effective May 1, 2026. The LLP reported turnover of INR72.93 crores and PAT of INR6.36 crores for the year ended March 31, 2026. The acquisition is expected to create operational synergies, broaden product offerings, and strengthen market presence, with completion targeted on or before December 31, 2026.
  • QIP completion. On August 4, 2026, the company allotted 1,14,94,252 equity shares at INR609.00 per share, raising INR7,000 million. Paid-up capital increased from INR1,357.08 million (13,57,08,333 shares) to INR1,472.02 million (14,72,02,585 shares). Allottees included BNP Paribas Financial Markets (9.29%), Morgan Stanley Asia Singapore (5.71%), Tata AIG General Insurance (15.48%), FLC Investco LLC (8.57%), Astorne Capital VCC Arven (7.14%), and Bandhan Small Cap Fund (15.66%).
  • Industry formalisation. The investor presentation cites GST (2017) and mandatory BIS hallmarking (2021) as regulatory catalysts for organised share gains, with organised penetration projected at ~55% by FY30 versus China at 72% and the USA at ~95%.
  • Debt reduction plans. Management stated that borrowings of approximately INR1,500–1,550 crores (including gold metal loans of INR300–400 crores) are planned to be reduced by INR500–600 crores by FY29, falling below INR1,000 crores, with a debt-free target in four to five years.

3. Key Risks

  • Gold price volatility. Record gold prices were cited as a factor shaping demand, with customers gravitating toward lightweight and studded products.
  • Hedging policy transition. Management noted hedging coverage had fallen to around 70% and plans to reach 80% by Q2/Q3 and 90–100% next year. The company stated it intends to be fully hedged in the next financial year.
  • Franchise economics and data visibility. Management acknowledged it does not have full access to franchise B2C data and is working to obtain it, which affects monitoring of franchise performance.
  • Cannibalisation risk. Management stated that store expansion is planned strategically with catchment-area analysis and a framework requiring stores to be a certain distance apart, though some natural cannibalisation is factored in.
  • Related-party transaction risk. The Silvostyle acquisition involves promoter-group entities and directors, though it is stated to be at arm’s length.
  • Execution risk on expansion. The bulk of FY27 store openings are planned for Q3 and Q4, with 25 stores targeted for the year and a longer-term path to 177 stores by March 2029.
  • Margin mix risk. Management noted that increasing FOCO share will dilute gross margin, though it expects EBITDA and PAT margins to improve, targeting PAT margin of 4.5–4.7% by FY29.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • FY27 store target. Management guided to opening around 25 stores in FY2027, taking the network to approximately 103 stores. As of Q1 FY27, the company had 78 stores, with no new stores opened in the quarter. Management stated the bulk of expansion is planned for Q3 and Q4.
  • EBITDA margin guidance. Management guided to approximately 7% EBITDA margin for the full year. Q1 FY27 actual EBITDA margin was 8.0%, above the full-year guidance.
  • PAT margin guidance. Management guided to a PAT margin of 4.1–4.25% for FY27, described as underlying without hedging gains. Q1 FY27 actual PAT margin was 4.4%.
  • Other expenses guidance. Management guided to other expenses of approximately INR400 crores for the full year, around 3% of total sales. Q1 FY27 other expenses were INR85.65 crores, which management said was lower than normal due to no new store openings, reduced marketing, and lower scheme redemptions.
  • Gross margin guidance. Management stated that with a studded ratio of 10–11%, gross margin should be in the range of 12.5–13.5%, and with retail jewellery at 80% and bars/coins at 20–21%, gross margin should be 13–14%. Q1 FY27 reported gross margin was 13.2%.
  • Hedging guidance. Management guided to reaching 80% hedging by Q2/Q3 and 90–100% next year. As of the call, hedging was around 70%.
  • LiteStyle guidance. Management guided to doubling the LiteStyle studded ratio from around 34% over the next 12 months and reaching 50% in a couple of years. Q1 FY27 LiteStyle stud ratio was 32.9%.
  • Debt reduction guidance. Management guided to reducing borrowings by INR500–600 crores by FY29 and becoming debt-free in four to five years. Current borrowings were stated at approximately INR1,200 crores excluding GML, plus INR300–400 crores of GML.
  • Inventory turnover guidance. Management had previously guided to stock turns stabilising at 3.5–4x. Q1 FY27 inventory turnover was 3.7x (annualized), in line with guidance.
  • QIP utilisation. The QIP raised INR7,000 million on August 4, 2026. Management had previously stated that expansion would be funded partly from profits and that the QIP would address the promoter shareholding overhang.

Broker Narrative

The first report was highly optimistic, framing weak reported revenue as a bullion-sales/accounting issue while celebrating festive demand, store expansion, and margin gains. By the July report, the narrative shifted to a sharp gold-price-driven demand slowdown, fewer festivals, and reported growth capped by the refinery-transfer accounting change, though the Buy call and structural growth/margin story were retained. Persistent themes included store expansion, studded-mix improvement, debt reduction, and full hedging; the key change was from festive momentum to expecting a 2QFY26 recovery.

Fears that came true

  • The concern that lower bullion-related sales would depress reported revenue materialized, as the refinery-transfer accounting change and high base were guided to cap 1HFY26 reported growth at ~8-10%.

Optimism that failed

  • The strong festive demand that drove 18% Navratri and 53% Diwali growth did not persist, with 1QFY26 demand turning soft after gold prices breached INR100,000.
  • The retail/e-commerce/franchisee revenue surge was not sustained, as the July report guided only ~8-10% reported growth for 1HFY26.
  • The 25.7% same-store sales growth momentum was not maintained, with the quarter marked by soft demand and a high old-exchange gold mix.
