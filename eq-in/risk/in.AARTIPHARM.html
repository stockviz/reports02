AARTIPHARM: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Pricing pressure in select API products, alongside customer inventory adjustments, is weighing on the API business.
  • Input cost volatility is a recurring theme: inflation in certain raw material categories, particularly solvents, methanol, ammonia, and propylene. Geopolitical tension has kept raw material prices at elevated levels.
  • Shipping and logistics disruption from conflict in West Asia is affecting shipping routes.
  • Regulatory and compliance burden: evolving global regulations are raising compliance costs.
  • Stamp duty demand: An order dated August 29, 2026 (received September 01, 2026) from the Office of the Superintendent of Stamps, Gujarat, Gandhinagar, alleges short payment or non-payment of stamp duty under the Gujarat Stamp Act, 1958 in connection with the Scheme of Arrangement (Demerger) between Aarti Industries Limited and Aarti Pharmalabs Limited. The total demand, including stamp duty and penalty, is Rs. 59,50,00,000 (Rupees Fifty-Nine Crore Fifty Lakh). The company states it believes it has a strong case on merits with adequate factual and legal grounds and will take appropriate action to challenge the order.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • CDMO/CMO momentum: Strong growth in the CDMO and CMO business, with CDMO sales having grown to ₹276 Crores. The company is expanding its on-ground sales presence in the USA and EU to enhance its CDMO global footprint.
  • Xanthine derivatives leadership: The new manufacturing block at Tarapur Unit 5 (Plot No. L-28/29/99) was inaugurated on June 30, 2026, with a capacity of 3,600 TPA for Xanthine derivatives, taking total capacity to 9,600 TPA and placing the company among the top 3 Xanthine derivatives manufacturers globally. Trial production has been initiated, with ramp-up to optimum utilization expected over the next few quarters. Expanded Xanthine capacity is expected to lift global market share from 15–20% to 20–25%.
  • China-plus-one tailwind: Global supply chain shifts away from China and increasing global outsourcing of drug development and manufacturing support the CDMO/API opportunity.
  • Government support: Initiatives such as the PLI scheme are cited as supportive.
  • New modalities: Expansion into emerging modalities such as peptides and oligonucleotides (TIDES), with R&D investment being initiated in FY27.
  • Capacity expansion pipeline: On August 07, 2026, the Board approved development of an Intermediate Block at an estimated capex of Rs. 149 Crores, adding 405 KL of capacity within one year, financed through internal accruals and borrowings, with the rationale of serving intermediate and CDMO customers.
  • Early signs of easing: Early indications of supply chain easing are noted.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material and input cost volatility (solvents, methanol, ammonia, propylene) and elevated prices linked to geopolitical tension.
  • Supply chain and shipping route disruption, particularly from conflict in West Asia.
  • Pricing pressure and customer inventory adjustments in select API products.
  • Regulatory and compliance risk, including evolving global regulations and associated cost increases.
  • Contingent liability risk from the Gujarat stamp duty order of Rs. 59,50,00,000, which the company intends to challenge.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk on new capacity: the Tarapur Xanthine block and the approved Intermediate Block (405 KL, Rs. 149 Crores) require successful commissioning and utilization ramp-up.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Guidance provided: - Target of 15–18% revenue and EBITDA CAGR over the next 3–4 years on a standalone basis from FY 2025-26. - CDMO sales growth guidance of 40–50% for FY 2026-27E. - FY 2026-27 capex expected broadly in line with FY 2025-26 (approximately ₹400 Crores). - Immediate priorities: raising capacity utilization at Atali, completing the Tarapur Xanthine expansion (targeted for commissioning in June 2026), improving API business performance, expanding the CDMO portfolio, and advancing TIDES capabilities. - On the stamp duty order: the company believes it has a strong case on merits and will challenge the order.

Observed performance and developments: - FY 2025-26: Standalone revenue of ₹1,798 Crores; consolidated revenue of ₹1,819.4 Crores; consolidated PAT of ₹1,747 million. Capex executed during the year was approximately ₹400 Crores. Net debt-to-equity stood at 0.32x as of March 31, 2026, with an AA- credit rating retained. - Q1-FY27: Standalone revenue of INR 5,346 Mn with an EBITDA margin of 24.82%. - Tarapur Xanthine expansion: The additional block was inaugurated on June 30, 2026, consistent with the targeted June 2026 commissioning. Trial production has been initiated, with ramp-up expected over the next few quarters. - CDMO segment: Grew to ₹276 Crores, with management guiding to 40–50% growth for FY 2026-27E. - Capacity expansion: The Board on August 07, 2026 approved a 405 KL Intermediate Block at Rs. 149 Crores capex over one year, aligned with the stated priority of expanding the CDMO/intermediate footprint. - Leadership changes: Effective October 1, 2026, Shri Rashesh C. Gogri moves from Non-Executive Director to Managing Director, and Smt. Hetal Gogri Gala moves from Managing Director to Executive Director, subject to shareholder approval at the ensuing AGM. Committee reconstitutions (Audit, CSR, and Stakeholders Relationship Committees) also take effect from October 1, 2026. - Investor communication: An earnings conference call on Q1-FY27 results was held, with the audio recording uploaded on August 10, 2026, following the August 03, 2026 intimation.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative shifted from an upbeat Buy on transitory operational hurdles and a near-term margin recovery to a cautious Hold focused on margin and profitability pressures after Q4FY26. Xanthine expansion, CDMO growth, and favorable trade/pricing dynamics remained persistent bullish themes, while optimism on sequential margin recovery gave way to concerns about startup costs, heavy capex, and raw-material inflation.

Fears that came true

  • Early-flagged expansion delays in xanthine debottlenecking and Atali capacity materialized as slight commercialization delays, contributing to the first Buy’s +18.8% actual falling short of the +26.1% predicted.
  • Early API margin pressure and flat growth materialized as FY26 API segment weakness from inventory destocking and pricing pressures, a disappointment versus the Buy thesis.
  • Early-flagged quarterly CDMO volatility and ₹49 crore postponed sales materialized as sharp QoQ swings, with CDMO +220% QoQ in Q4 showing the segment’s lumpiness that undermined forecast predictability.

Optimism that failed

  • The sequential margin recovery thesis failed: Q3FY26’s 60.1% GPM/36.9% QoQ EBITDA growth did not sustain, as Q4FY26 GPM fell 863 bps to 51.4% and EBITDA margins declined 133 bps.
  • The expectation of xanthine capacity expansion to 9,000 MTPA by Q4FY26 did not fully materialize on time, as the last report flagged slight delays in commercialization.
