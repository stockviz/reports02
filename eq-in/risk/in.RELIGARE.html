Religare Enterprises — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Demerger setback from RBI. The Board approved a Scheme of Arrangement on February 14, 2026 to demerge REL’s financial services business (lending, broking, investment activities and ancillary/support services) into Religare Finvest Limited, while retaining the Care Health Insurance shareholding in REL. The company received “no objection” from NSE and “no adverse observations” from BSE on July 7, 2026. Subsequently, REL received a letter dated August 6, 2026 from the RBI conveying that its application had been examined and the request had not been acceded to; RFL received a similar communication dated August 7, 2026. Management stated the RBI letter was brief and contained no reasoning, and that the company is engaging with the regulator. On the earnings call, management acknowledged that the previously indicated timeline of Q1 FY28 for the demerger could be delayed.

Consolidated loss and accounting transition. Consolidated total income rose 26% YoY to ₹2,358.43 crore in Q1 FY27 (vs ₹1,876.25 crore in Q1 FY26), but consolidated PAT was negative at ₹(46.98) crore versus ₹8.12 crore in Q1 FY26. Management attributed this mainly to differing reporting standards: Care Health Insurance took a one-year forbearance from IRDAI on Ind AS implementation (effective April 1, 2026), so the impact of Ind AS 117 is not reflected in the consolidated accounts while Care’s standalone results are on Ind AS. Consolidated PBT was ₹(76.73) crore against a positive ₹5.73 crore in the corresponding quarter. On a standalone basis, REL reported income of ₹4.38 crore and expenses of ₹14 crore, a net loss of ₹9.62 crore.

Financial services businesses still sub-scale. RFL’s total income fell to ₹14.4 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹19.3 crore a year earlier, due to a reduced loan book and lower recoveries from the GNPA book; PAT was ₹15 crore versus ₹17.9 crore. Net interest income fell to ₹13.5 crore from ₹18.6 crore. RFL’s core SME book is only about ₹53 crore, with cash of over ₹600 crore sitting idle — a point raised by investors. Management explained this stems from a period when lending activities were under embargo and legacy issues had to be cleaned up. Religare Housing reported a total income of ₹7.4 crore, operating expenses of ₹12.5 crore and a loss of ₹5 crore for the quarter; its AUM has not grown over the past year, which management attributed to RFL being under a Corrective Action Plan and the housing subsidiary being unable to access market funds for seven to eight years.

Broking revenue softness. Broking total income grew 7% YoY to ₹99.5 crore, but revenue declined sequentially. Management described this as a temporary blip linked to business repair work, unsupportive markets, annual increments and natural attrition, while noting the client debit book and interest income grew.

Legacy legal and regulatory matters. REL continues to be barred from declaring dividends per an RBI letter issued in December 2019. Unredeemed preference shares issued to Oscar Investments Limited (due October 31, 2013, redemption value ₹4,190.28 lakh) and to RHC Finance Pvt. Limited (due August 30, 2021, redemption value ₹4,212.75 lakh) remain disputed, with matters sub-judice before the Delhi High Court and NCLT respectively. Elive Infotech Pvt. Ltd. has sought sale of the Religare trademark and payment of approximately ₹323 crore from REL for alleged unauthorized usage. A governance review commissioned by the Board (covering REL, RFL and RHDFCL) remains in progress, with the external law firm working on closure of the report. Income tax litigation continues: for AY 2017-18, a rectification order reduced the demand to ₹10,853 lakh (including ₹3,792 lakh interest), with an appeal pending before ITAT and interim stay granted; RFL has ongoing income tax litigation for AY 2013, 2016-17 and 2017-18 with total demand aggregating ₹30,341 lakh.

Insurance combined ratio and leverage. Care’s Ind AS combined ratio stood at 102.6% (improved 30 bps YoY), which management noted is higher than some peers. Ajay Shah explained this reflects the corporate wellness book written in Q1 with upfront claims payment, and reiterated the commitment to reach 100% within two years. Investment leverage of 4.3x was flagged by an investor as high for a standalone health insurer; management indicated it should reduce to around 3.7x by year-end with additional capital infusion. Solvency stood at 1.58x as of end-June 2026 against the 1.5x requirement; a planned sub-debt raise was deferred due to capital market turmoil and completed at ₹200 crore in August 2026.

Competitive intensity. Management acknowledged high competitive intensity in health insurance with newer players entering, and described broking as a scattered industry with many niche and full-service competitors.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Care Health Insurance momentum. Care grew GWP 37% YoY to ₹3,247 crore on a full premium basis, with retail business growing 45% YoY and retail new business growing more than 50%. It is India’s second-largest standalone health insurer, with 6.7% market share of the industry and 24% within the SAHI segment; retail health market share is 12.6% of the industry and 21.6% among SAHI. PBT grew 59% YoY to ₹163 crore (Ind AS), and the company has delivered a 30% CAGR over four financial years. Management described the post-GST period as a “land grab moment,” with growth driven by fresh business growing faster than renewals, a rising number of consumers particularly in Tier 2 and Tier 3 geographies, and a declining share of porting business. Gross opex ratio improved 100 bps YoY to 31.2%. The investment book rose over ₹800 crore versus March 2026, with 93% in bonds and G-Secs yielding 7.2–7.3%. Digital metrics include 99.9% digital policy issuance, over 85% of cashless claims processed within 30 minutes, 13.2 million app installations and 1.4 million active users.

Capital raising and promoter commitment. Care raised ₹150 crore through a rights issue in June 2026 (REL subscribing ₹119.68 crore) and ₹200 crore of sub-debt in August 2026. REL’s preferential allotment of up to 6,38,29,782 warrants at ₹235 per warrant (aggregating up to ₹1,500 crore) has seen ₹618 crore of proceeds received as of August 2026, with ₹881 crore due for conversion by March 2027. Promoter shareholding increased to 30.56% as of June 30, 2026 through warrant conversion. Management stated REL and co-promoter Kedaara have both funded Care as needed, with ₹600 crore earmarked for Care and over ₹376 crore already infused across the September 2025 and June 2026 rights issues.

RFL positioned for restart. RFL has tangible net worth of ₹915 crore, net owned funds of ₹837.8 crore, cash balance of over ₹600 crore, stable collection efficiency of 98%, NNPA of about 0.8% and CRAR of 238%. The RBI withdrew all Corrective Action Plan restrictions via letter dated July 23, 2025. All 18 lenders that had classified RFL as fraud have removed the classification from the RBI’s Central Fraud Registry. Srinivasan Karthik joined as MD & CEO in May 2026 from HDB Financial Services, and management expects business to commence in the next three to four months. The written-off book pool of roughly ₹350–400 crore is being worked by a dedicated team, with recoveries expected over the next two years, though management noted this is a dwindling book.

Housing finance rebuild. RHDFCL has a net worth of roughly ₹180 crore with about ₹250 crore of further committed capital. Pavan Kumar Gupta joined as MD & CEO effective July 1, 2026 (previously CEO of Muthoot Housing Finance) and Pankaj Rathi joined as CFO (previously CFO of Grihum Housing Finance). The business serves over 2,800 customers across 15 branches in 8 states, with an average ticket size of ₹12 lakh, AUM of ₹247 crore (66% home loans, 34% loan against property), capital adequacy over 121% and investment-grade ratings (ICRA BBB- Stable long-term; ICRA A3 short-term). Management cited structural tailwinds in affordable housing finance, favorable government initiatives and robust Indian retail credit growth.

Broking profitability and platform build. Broking PAT grew 65% YoY to ₹7.5 crore and PBT grew 53% YoY to ₹10 crore. Total income rose 7% YoY to ₹99.5 crore, supported by 13% growth in brokerage income, 28% growth in interest income and 78% growth in the client debit book. Assets under custody stood at ₹47,946 crore as of June 30, 2026, with average daily turnover (cash + derivatives) of ₹11,622 crore. Revenue mix was diversified: brokerage 47%, interest income 30%, e-governance 16%, with the balance from depository services and third-party product distribution. Net worth of RBL and subsidiaries was ₹384 crore. Vijay Goel leads the business, which is building out its tech platform, margin funding book and third-party product business.

Leadership architecture completed. Arjun Lamba was re-designated as Managing Director effective August 12, 2026 (earlier Executive Director/Whole-time Director), with a five-year term commencing April 1, 2026. Ajay Shah and Manish Dodeja were elevated to MD and ED respectively at Care. Management described Q1 FY27 as a quarter of deliberate, measured progress focused on building foundations.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory approval risk on demerger. The RBI has declined the demerger application, and management stated that any value-unlocking route — including a potential alternative structure — requires RBI approval. Management said it is not currently considering a demerger of Care instead of the financial services business, and that engaging with the regulator is the priority. No timeline was provided.
  • Accounting comparability risk. The one-year Ind AS forbearance at Care means consolidated results do not reflect Ind AS 117 impacts, making the consolidated picture diverge from Care’s standalone Ind AS results until next year.
  • Legacy litigation and contingent liabilities. Preference share disputes, the Daiichi garnishee matter, the trademark claim of approximately ₹323 crore, income tax demands (REL ₹10,853 lakh for AY 2017-18; RFL ₹30,341 lakh across three assessment years) and the ongoing governance review all carry potential financial impact. The company has treated tax litigations as contingent liabilities without recognizing a provision, based on legal opinion that favorable outcomes are likely.
  • Execution risk in financial services restart. RFL and RHDFCL are rebuilding from a multi-year period of restricted lending. Management’s ambition of a ₹10,000–15,000 crore book size with ₹1,500–2,000 crore of capital depends on business plans still being finalized by newly appointed CEOs.
  • Market and competitive risk. Broking performance is exposed to market vagaries, and health insurance faces rising competitive intensity. Management declined to give full-year growth guidance for Care beyond stating an intent to beat the market.
  • Capital adequacy management. Care’s solvency of 1.58x is close to the 1.5x requirement, with a stated intent to maintain 1.7x; further equity or sub-debt raises may be required. Investment leverage of 4.3x is expected to moderate with capital infusion.
  • Dividend restriction. REL remains barred from declaring dividends per the December 2019 RBI letter.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Demerger timeline. Management had previously indicated a tentative demerger deadline of Q1 FY28. Following the RBI communication, management acknowledged on the call that this could be delayed, while declining to give a new timeline and committing to “best effort.” No revised date has been provided.

Care combined ratio. On the prior earnings call, Ajay Shah had stated the objective of reaching a 100% combined ratio within two years. In Q1 FY27, the Ind AS combined ratio stood at 102.6%, improved 30 bps YoY, with management reiterating the two-year commitment and attributing the Q1 level to the corporate wellness book’s upfront claims payment pattern. The prior-year trajectory cited was 103% in Q1 landing at 101.1% for the full year.

Care growth. Management declined to give full-year growth guidance, stating only that it intends to beat the market. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: 37% GWP growth versus industry growth of 22.4% overall and 32% in retail health, with Care growing 18% higher than the industry on a 1/n basis for retail health.

Care solvency. Management stated an intent to maintain solvency at 1.7x. Observed solvency was 1.58x at end-June 2026; the planned Q1 sub-debt raise was deferred to August 2026 (₹200 crore), which management said addresses solvency as of now.

Care leverage. Management indicated investment leverage should reduce to around 3.7x by year-end with additional capital infusion. Observed leverage was 4.3x in Q1 FY27.

RFL business restart. Management stated on the call that business commencement is targeted within the next three to four months, with the platform, cash and capital in place. Observed Q1 FY27: core SME book of approximately ₹53 crore, total income of ₹14.4 crore (down YoY), PAT of ₹15 crore (down YoY), and cash of over ₹600 crore awaiting deployment. Management stated the profile should change significantly over coming quarters.

RFL recoveries. Management stated the written-off pool of ₹350–400 crore would likely be recovered over the next two years, while noting the book is dwindling and legacy NPA write-off recovery will start coming down every quarter. Observed Q1 FY27: approximately ₹20 crore collected from the written-off book, with PAT supported by strong recoveries.

Broking model and market share. Management stated the clear direction on the broking model would be established in about one to two quarters, with intent to increase market share through margin funding and third-party products. Observed Q1 FY27: 7% YoY income growth, 65% PAT growth, sequential revenue decline described as temporary, and continued investment in technology and talent ahead of aggressive client acquisition.

Financial services scale ambition. Management stated ambitions for a ₹10,000–15,000 crore book size with ₹1,500–2,000 crore of capital across RFL and RHDFCL, while noting business teams are still working on annual operating plans and that plans would be shared later. Observed current scale: RFL core SME book approximately ₹53 crore; RHDFCL AUM ₹247 crore.

Warrant conversion. Management stated ₹881 crore of warrant proceeds remain due for conversion by March 2027. Observed progress: ₹375 crore received upfront in September 2025 (25% of the ₹1,500 crore raise), ₹3,500 lakh in Q3 FY26 from four promoter group entities for 1,985,816 warrants (allotted December 5, 2025), and ₹14,705 lakh in Q1 FY27 from five warrant holders for 83,43,263 warrants (allotted June 4, 2026), totaling ₹618 crore of proceeds as of August 2026.
