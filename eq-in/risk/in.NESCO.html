NESCO: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Tower 2 approvals and timeline slippage. The company received an intimation of disapproval (IOD) for Tower 2 in March 2025. Shareholders noted that more than 12 months later, final clearances were still awaited, and that the completion timeline in the annual report had moved from 48 months to 60 months. Management confirmed the works have now started — 360-degree barricading completed on the 6-acre plot, dust suppression systems installed, and existing structures demolished, with the site at ground zero. The stated completion period is 60 months from receipt of the commencement certificate (described as the date of breaking ground/start of excavation). The estimated investment is about ₹3,500 crores.

Engineering (Indabrator) revenue decline. The division reported lower revenue of ₹35 crores, attributed mainly to softer demand across certain capital goods sectors. Management characterised the year as focused on process improvements, cost optimisation, lean logistics, waste reduction and turnaround times.

Wayside Amenities site surrenders. A few sites were surrendered due to local villager unrest, infrastructure deficiencies, regulatory and land-related issues, and handover conditions that reduced commercial feasibility. Project costs of about ₹1.25–1.5 crores were charged to the P&L in the quarter of June 2026.

Treasury income dip. Treasury income fell, mainly because about ₹640 crores was spent in March 2025 on premium and fees to BMC for the new Tower 2.

Exhibition competition and event incident. Extremely large exhibitions previously hosted at BEC have moved to venues several times larger, such as Pragati Maidan or Noida; management stated there is no major impact from venues within the city or state. Separately, the 9x9 music concert incident of 11 April 2026 was addressed: management expressed condolences, stated no Nesco employees were involved, confirmed cooperation with authorities, and noted industry associations are taking up the matter with the Government of Maharashtra on the principle that neither venue nor organiser should be held responsible in such situations.

Quarter-on-quarter topline movement. A shareholder observed that June-quarter topline reduced from ₹251 crores to ₹211 crores, while profit before tax increased. Management attributed this to cyclicality — Q4 is usually better than Q1 — and noted Q1 was up almost 15% versus the prior-year Q1.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record scale. FY26 total revenue crossed ₹1,000 crores for the first time. Total income was ₹1,031 crores (22% year-on-year), EBITDA ₹590 crores, profit after tax ₹412 crores (10% year-on-year), with the company debt-free and liquidity of ₹1,471 crores. A final dividend of ₹7 per share was recommended and subsequently declared.

Realty. Towers 3 and 4 maintained 100% occupancy, with total chargeable area of about 17.70 lakh sq ft (6.60 lakh sq ft in Tower 3 and 11.10 lakh sq ft in Tower 4), 26 clients (13 in each tower), and average rent of about ₹180 per sq ft per month, ranging from about ₹150 to ₹210 per sq ft. The division reported revenue of ₹398 crores, up 8.6%. Work commenced on Tower 2, an integrated office-and-hotel development. The company completed its transition to 100% renewable energy, and Towers 3 and 4 hold Platinum-rated Green Building certifications. FSI consumed is about 14% by Towers 3 and 4, with Tower 2 to consume a further 18%, excluding the Bombay Exhibition Center portion.

Exhibition and Events. The venue hosted over 135 exhibitions, corporate events and live experiences. Division revenue was ₹260 crores, up 30%. Modernisation plans involve replacing and redeveloping Hall 1 (currently being dismantled, to be constructed and operational within 12 months, with overall capex within ₹200 crores), followed by redeveloping Halls 2 and 3 fused into one. Existing capacity comprises five exhibition halls and three large banquet halls (two semi-permanent), totalling 55,500 square metres.

Foods. With 12 restaurants, a gaming zone, three large food courts, indoor and outdoor catering, eight in-house brands and partnerships with over 50 food brands, the division reported revenue of ₹238 crores, up 107%. Three new restaurants were added during the year.

Wayside Amenities. Construction is progressing at four sites along the Hyderabad–Visakhapatnam Expressway, with operations expected to start by the end of the financial year. The venture aims to combine hospitality, fuel and motorway services.

Sector backdrop. Management cited India’s position as one of the world’s fastest-growing major economies, healthy demand for premium Grade-A office space from GCCs, MNCs and the services sector, robust hospitality growth, momentum in exhibitions and events, and government investment in highways and expressways creating opportunities for organised wayside amenities.

ESG and CSR. The company commenced work on an ESG Roadmap 2030 with measurable goals. FY26 CSR contribution was ₹8.30 crores, focused on education and healthcare, including support to two old age homes and senior citizen living. Certifications cited include Green Building Platinum, ISO 45001, FSSAI “Eat Right Campus”, ISO 9001/45001/14001 at the Vishnoli factory, and “Great Places to Work” for two consecutive years.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution and approval risk on Tower 2, given the prior intimation of disapproval, the extended 60-month timeline, and the ₹3,500 crores estimated investment.
  • Cyclicality in demand, evidenced by softer demand in certain capital goods sectors affecting Indabrator.
  • Site development risk in Wayside Amenities, demonstrated by surrenders due to local unrest, infrastructure deficiencies, regulatory and land issues, and handover conditions.
  • Competition from larger exhibition venues, with very large exhibitions having moved to Pragati Maidan or Noida.
  • Event liability and regulatory exposure, highlighted by the 9x9 concert incident and the industry’s engagement with the Government of Maharashtra.
  • Concentration and lease-renewal considerations in Realty, though management stated most leases are in force with several years ahead and no exits are envisaged in the near future.
  • Funding risk, mitigated by a stated preference for internal accruals, with debt only as a possible last-mile top-up.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Tower 2 timeline. Prior annual report guidance indicated 48 months; the current guidance is 60 months from commencement certificate. Observed progress: works began about 7–8 months before the AGM, with the site now at ground zero. A shareholder asked whether the first full year of rental income would be FY33; management did not confirm a specific year, instead reiterating the 60-month completion period from the commencement certificate.

Tower 2 commencement. Guidance in the prior year indicated commencement in H2 FY26; observed status is that enabling works and demolition have occurred, with final clearances previously pending.

Hotel timing. Management guided that the hotel is expected to start on the same timelines as the IT Park and commercial building because they form an integrated structure, with both operations commencing at roughly the same time.

Wayside Amenities. Guidance is that operations start by the end of the financial year; observed status is that four sites are under construction and the vertical remains in its development phase.

Hall 1 modernisation. Guidance is that Hall 1 will be constructed and operational within 12 months, with overall capex within ₹200 crores; observed status is that Hall 1 is being dismantled.

Capex funding. Guidance is that funding will mostly come from internal accruals, with debt considered only if needed as a last-mile top-up. Observed liquidity: treasury of ₹1,650 crores as on 30 June 2026, ₹1,520 crores as on 31 March 2026, and ₹1,135 crores as on 31 March 2025, invested in debt funds, bonds, preference shares, CDs and mutual funds. CWIP as on 31 March 2026 was ₹763 crores (Realty about ₹740 crores, BEC about ₹16 crores, others about ₹7 crores).

Rent trajectory. A shareholder asked what the ₹179–180 per sq ft weighted average rent would be by FY30; management did not provide a figure, noting contracts are at different levels with escalation clauses kicking in annually or every three years, and that the weighted average would need to be computed.

REIT structure. Management stated there are no current thoughts of a REIT but will keep it in mind.

Bonus issue. Management stated no decision has been taken on a bonus; the Board will decide at an appropriate time in the best interest of the company, stakeholders and the proposed expansion and operating plans.

Investor communication. Management indicated the CFO and Chairman will take a call on arranging more regular updates, possibly on a six-month basis or better, in response to a request for quarterly presentations and investor calls.

AGM mode. Management stated the company has taken cognizance of shareholder preference and shall continue virtual meetings in the future, while noting one shareholder’s request for a physical or hybrid meeting would be kept in mind.

Vision. For the next 5–6 years, management described the vision as enhancing and maximising growth in the right direction, considering possible diversification, and growing top line and bottom line.

Governance outcomes. All seven resolutions were passed with requisite majority at the 67th AGM held on 27 July 2026, including adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY26, declaration of a ₹7.00 (350%) dividend per equity share of face value ₹2, re-appointment of Mrs. Sudha S. Patel, appointment of Mr. Rajesh G. Upadhyay as Director and as Whole-time Director designated Executive Director (Commercial & Operations), and ratification of cost auditors’ remuneration for FY27. The auditor’s report contained no qualifications, observations or comments with adverse effect on the functioning of the company, and the secretarial audit report and corporate governance report had no observations.
