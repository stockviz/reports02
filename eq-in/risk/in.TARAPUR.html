Tarapur Transformers Limited — Summary of Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Weak financial position and going-concern doubt. The auditor’s limited review report for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 records that the company’s net worth (share capital plus reserves plus securities premium) is negative Rs. 283.49 lakhs. The auditor states that this negative net worth creates a material uncertainty casting significant doubt on the company’s ability to continue as a going concern, and notes that management has not provided disclosures on this in the results.

Loss-making quarter. The unaudited standalone results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 show a loss from operations before exceptional items of Rs. (45.62) lakhs, a loss before tax of Rs. (45.62) lakhs, and a net loss for the period of Rs. (7) lakhs (as printed), with basic and diluted EPS of Rs. (0.29). This compares with a loss from operations of Rs. (16.32) lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 and a full-year loss of Rs. (75.41) lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2026.

Management’s own profit expectation. Note 12 to the results states that management estimates the chances of the company making profits in this financial year are very less, and accordingly the company has not recognised deferred tax assets on brought-forward losses and unabsorbed depreciation.

Non-provision of interest on borrowings. The auditor notes that the company borrowed Rs. 300 lakhs from Gaganbase Vincom in May 2024 at 18% per annum but has not provided interest from April 2026 onwards, amounting to Rs. 13.46 lakhs for the quarter ended 30 June 2026. For FY 2025-26, interest of Rs. 67.5 lakhs was not provided, taking total unprovided interest up to the first quarter of FY 2026-27 to Rs. 80.96 lakhs. Penal interest claimed by Gaganbase cannot be quantified as details have not been received, and Gaganbase has not confirmed the balance outstanding as at 30 June 2026. The auditor states the loss for the quarter is understated by Rs. 13.46 lakhs and current liabilities understated by the same amount, with reserves overstated by Rs. 13.46 lakhs (and accumulated reserves overstated by Rs. 80.96 lakhs).

Non-receipt of balance confirmations. The company sent balance confirmation letters to parties not covered under the register maintained under section 189 of the Companies Act, 2013, but in many cases did not receive written confirmation of balances as at 30 June 2026. The auditor lists parties including Lamcotec SRL (Rs. 64.2 lakhs Dr.), Panchganga Ispat Private Limited (Rs. 23 lakhs Dr.), EMD – Bihar State Electricity Board (Rs. 200 lakhs Dr.), EMD – Haryana Vidyut Prasaran Nigam Limited (Rs. 200 lakhs Dr.), Security Deposit – KSEB (Alappuzha) (Rs. 133 lakhs Dr.), and Asmi Developers (Rs. 192.86 lakhs Dr.), among others.

Non-compliance with Ind AS 116 (Leases). The company pays lease rental for its registered office at S-112, Rajiv Gandhi Complex, Ekta Nagar, Kandivali (West), Mumbai – 400067, from lessor Narendra Tiwari, with a lease tenure of 22 months and lease paid during the first quarter of FY 2026-27 of Rs. 59,064, with an interest-free deposit of Rs. 50,000. The auditor notes that because the lease term exceeds 12 months, the company needs to comply with Ind AS 116 by creating a Right of Use asset and corresponding lease liability, and to bifurcate the interest-free deposit under Ind AS 109. This accounting has not been done, and quantification is not possible as information on EIR and amortisation is not available.

No documentation for loans and advances granted. The auditor states that no loan agreements or MoUs have been provided for any loans granted, whether written off or still continuing in the books. The opinion on the standalone financial results for the year ended 31 March 2026 was also qualified on this matter.

Breach of section 186 limits. The company has advanced loans to parties of Rs. 465.43 lakhs (for which no provision is made), which is in excess of the limits prescribed under section 186 of the Companies Act, 2013.

Asset sale blocked by mortgage. Note 6 states that shareholders authorised the sale of part of the factory land and building at Plot No. 141, Pali, Wada, but the land and building is mortgaged with Upsurge Investment & Finance Limited against a loan availed by the company. As immediate sale is not possible in the present condition, the asset has not been classified as a Non-Current Asset held for Sale. The auditor adds that under the Upsurge loan agreement a repayment schedule for principal and interest was attached, but on scrutiny of the ledger only interest is being accrued and principal repayment is not being done; Upsurge has confirmed the balance outstanding as at 30 June 2026.

Insolvency application and bad-debt provision. Note 7 states the company has filed an application under section 7 of the IBC, 2016 with the NCLT Mumbai Bench for initiating Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process against Choudhary Global Limited for an outstanding amount of Rs. 8,23,35,000. Management has decided to create a provision for bad and doubtful advances of Rs. 8,23,35,000.

SEBI show cause notice. Note 8 states the company received a Show Cause Notice with reference No. SEBI/HO/QIC-1/P/OW/2025/16460/1 dated 20/06/2025 from SEBI, and the company is in the process of giving an appropriate reply.

Litigation and recovery actions. Note 9 states Bilpower Limited, which holds 3,994,000 shares of the company, is under liquidation. Note 10 states Karnataka Power Transmission Corporation Ltd has filed money suits with the District & Sessions Judge, Mysore for recovery of Rs. 2,97,07,710 from the company; the liability for this amount was provided in earlier financial years, and further orders have been received from a Bengaluru-based court for recovery of Rs. 5,66,05,645. Bilpower Limited had pledged 2,822,460 equity shares with Comfort Itech Limited and 1,600,000 equity shares with Comfort Fincap Limited, and these were invoked.

Prior-year audit qualifications. The material relating to the year ended 31 March 2026 records a qualified opinion from Grand Mark & Associates citing negative net worth (Rs. -186.21 lakhs), non-receipt of balance confirmations, non-compliance with Ind AS 116, lack of loan documentation, breach of section 186, non-compliance with section 194A of the Income Tax Act regarding TDS deduction, significant contingent liabilities of Rs. 959.71 lakhs, non-provision of interest on Gaganbase Vincom borrowings, no physical verification of property, plant and equipment for the year, and severe going-concern doubts. It also notes the company previously failed to include the CFO’s signature and proper format on the Statement of Impact of Audit Qualifications due to an unforeseen family emergency and connectivity issues, which was subsequently rectified.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Revival and restart efforts. The material relating to FY 2025-26 records that management is in the process of reviving the facility, restarting operations, hiring skilled manpower to complete physical verification of fixed assets, and making efforts to recall and recover advanced loans and arrears.

Recovery of advances and arrears. Management is making efforts to recall and recover advanced loans and arrears, and is committed to ensuring full compliance with SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015 and to complying with Ind AS 116 in subsequent periods.

Shareholder authorisations for future financing. The 38th AGM notice includes resolutions to approve loans/guarantees/securities up to INR 25 crores under Section 185 of the Act and investments, loans, and guarantees up to INR 100 crores under Section 186 of the Act, which would provide headroom for such transactions if approved.

Other income. Note 11 states other income includes interest, rent etc. Total income for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 was Rs. 1,829 lakhs (as printed), compared with Rs. 911 lakhs (as printed) in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 and Rs. 5,557 lakhs (as printed) in the quarter ended 30 June 2025.

3. Key Risks

  • Going-concern risk arising from negative net worth of Rs. 283.49 lakhs as at 30 June 2026 and management’s own view that profits this financial year are very less likely.
  • Understatement of liabilities and losses due to non-provision of interest of Rs. 13.46 lakhs for the quarter and Rs. 80.96 lakhs cumulatively, with penal interest unquantified.
  • Recoverability risk on loans and advances, including the Rs. 465.43 lakhs advanced in excess of section 186 limits with no provision, and the Rs. 8,23,35,000 provision for bad and doubtful advances against Choudhary Global Limited.
  • Asset sale risk: the Pali, Wada factory land and building cannot be sold while mortgaged to Upsurge Investment & Finance Limited, and principal repayment on that loan is not being made.
  • Regulatory and legal risk from the SEBI show cause notice dated 20/06/2025, the NCLT application under section 7 of the IBC, money suits by Karnataka Power Transmission Corporation Ltd, and court recovery orders.
  • Shareholding risk from Bilpower Limited being under liquidation and the invocation of its pledged shares by Comfort Itech Limited and Comfort Fincap Limited.
  • Accounting and compliance risk from non-compliance with Ind AS 116, lack of loan documentation, non-receipt of balance confirmations, and prior-year non-compliance with section 194A of the Income Tax Act.
  • Contingent liabilities of Rs. 959.71 lakhs recorded in the FY 2025-26 material.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance on profitability. Management estimates that the chances of the company making profits in this financial year are very less, and accordingly has not recognised deferred tax assets on brought-forward losses and unabsorbed depreciation. Observed performance is consistent with this: the quarter ended 30 June 2026 recorded a loss from operations before exceptional items of Rs. (45.62) lakhs and a net loss for the period, with EPS of Rs. (0.29), following a full-year loss of Rs. (75.41) lakhs for FY 2025-26.

Guidance on revival and operations. Management states it is in the process of reviving the facility, restarting operations, and hiring skilled manpower to complete physical verification of fixed assets. The FY 2025-26 material notes that no physical verification of property, plant and equipment was conducted for that year, and management is in the process of hiring manpower to complete it as soon as possible.

Guidance on compliance. Management states it intends to comply with Ind AS 116 in subsequent periods and is committed to ensuring full compliance with SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015. The auditor’s report for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 records that Ind AS 116 has not been complied with, and the FY 2025-26 material records prior non-compliance with section 194A of the Income Tax Act and the earlier omission of the CFO’s signature and proper format on the Statement of Impact of Audit Qualifications, which was subsequently rectified.

Guidance on recovery of advances. Management states it is making efforts to recall and recover advanced loans and arrears. The auditor notes that no loan agreements or MoUs have been provided for any loans granted, and that loans of Rs. 465.43 lakhs were advanced in excess of section 186 limits with no provision made.

Guidance on the asset sale. Shareholders authorised the sale of part of the Pali, Wada factory land and building, and management is committed to selling the asset. However, because the asset is mortgaged to Upsurge Investment & Finance Limited and immediate sale is not possible in its present condition, the asset has not been classified as held for sale, and the auditor notes principal repayment on the Upsurge loan is not being made.

Guidance on the SEBI notice. The company states it is in the process of giving an appropriate reply to the SEBI Show Cause Notice dated 20/06/2025.

AGM and governance timeline. The 38th Annual General Meeting will be held on Tuesday, 29 September 2026 at 12:00 P.M. at the registered office at S-112, 1st Floor, Rajiv Gandhi Commercial Complex, Ekta Nagar, Kandivali (West), Mumbai – 400067. The cut-off date for e-voting is Tuesday, 22 September 2026; book closure runs from Wednesday, 23 September 2026 to Tuesday, 29 September 2026; and e-voting runs from 26 September 2026 to 28 September 2026. The board meeting that approved the results commenced at 03:30 P.M. and concluded at 6:00 P.M. on 13 August 2026.
