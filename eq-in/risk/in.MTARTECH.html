MTAR Technologies: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Working capital intensity remains elevated. Net working capital stood at 82 receivable days and 145 inventory days in Q1 FY27, though both improved from Q4 FY26 levels of 140 and 208 days respectively. Inventory days of 145 reflect the long-gestation nature of nuclear and aerospace programmes.

Finance costs have risen sharply. Finance costs increased to Rs. 15.8 Cr in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 5.8 Cr in Q1 FY26, alongside a rise in borrowings (current borrowings of Rs. 221.6 Cr and non-current borrowings of Rs. 147.7 Cr as of March 2026, versus Rs. 96.2 Cr and Rs. 81.1 Cr a year earlier).

Gross margin compression. GP% declined to 45.5% in Q1 FY27 from 54.2% in Q1 FY26, even as absolute gross profit grew 93.4% year-on-year.

Subsidiary drag. The two subsidiaries (Gee Pee Aerospace and Defence, and Magnatar Aero Systems) reported total revenues of Rs. 16.79 million and a net loss after tax of Rs. 7.22 million for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. A scheme for their merger into the holding company has been filed with the NCLT.

Execution and qualification risk. Several growth vectors remain at the first-article or qualification stage — data centre infrastructure solutions, SLB Class A components, and certain MNC aerospace programmes — where volume production is contingent on successful qualification.

Dependence on a concentrated customer set. The civil nuclear business is tied to NPCIL, which controls all operational, under-construction and planned reactors in India given the absence of private participation.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record order inflow. MTAR secured orders worth Rs. 2,895.1 Cr in Q1 FY27 — the highest ever in a single quarter — surpassing the entire FY26 order inflow of Rs. 2,453.3 Cr.

Civil nuclear expansion. The company received its single largest order inflow of Rs. 504 Cr for the Kaiga 5 & 6 projects. It expects Rs. 150 Cr of orders in FY27 from reactors due for refurbishment. Separately, it secured Rs. 126.74 Cr of purchase orders from NPCIL for coolant channel assemblies for refurbishment of RAPS-4 and MAPS-2, to be executed by 26 May 2028, taking the closing nuclear order book above Rs. 775 Cr — the highest in its history. NTPC’s partnership with NPCIL for four reactors at Mahi Banswara is expected to generate significant orders. The Government of India’s target of 100 GWe civil nuclear capacity by 2047 underpins the long-term opportunity.

Clean Energy order book. Closing order book of Rs. 3,431 Cr in Clean Energy as of end-Q1 FY27, supported by rising power demand, the global energy transition, and the expanding data centre ecosystem.

Data centre infrastructure entry. MTAR received Rs. 45 Cr of orders from SLB to supply sheet metal components and assemblies for data centre infrastructure solutions, with robust orders expected post-qualification.

Aerospace & Defence ramp-up. The MNC Aerospace vertical is positioned for growth as programmes transition from first-article qualification to volume ramp-up. The company received an order for the Main Landing Gear Support Structure Test Box assembly for the AMCA programme and was declared L1 for the Fuselage Door Assembly. Significant orders are expected for actuator assemblies for the LCA Tejas Mark IA programme. First articles have been delivered to Thales and GKN, with volume production underway for storage boxes.

New customer additions. Over the past 2–3 years, MTAR added customers including SLB, Weatherford, Voith, GKN Aerospace and Thales across clean energy, oil & gas and MNC aerospace.

Capacity expansion. A greenfield facility for oil & gas (Weatherford) and Phase 2 fuel cell expansion is expected to be commissioned by Q3 FY27. Phase 3 fuel cell capacity expansion at a new facility is to be completed by March 2027.

PFBR milestone. The achievement of criticality by the Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor is strategically significant; MTAR supplied a majority of the critical assemblies for the reactor core.

3. Key Risks

  • Qualification and ramp-up risk: Multiple growth programmes (data centre solutions, SLB Class A components, MNC aerospace first articles) depend on successful qualification before volume orders materialise.
  • Working capital and cash flow risk: Elevated inventory and receivable days, combined with rising borrowings and finance costs, pressure cash generation. Net cash from investing activities was negative Rs. 352.6 Cr in FY26.
  • Customer concentration: Heavy reliance on NPCIL in nuclear and on a limited set of global OEMs in aerospace.
  • Margin risk: Gross margin declined year-on-year in Q1 FY27 despite strong revenue growth.
  • Execution risk on capacity: Greenfield and phased expansion timelines (Q3 FY27, March 2027) carry commissioning risk.
  • Subsidiary performance: The subsidiaries are currently loss-making and are pending merger with the holding company.
  • Borrowing limit increase: The AGM proposes increasing borrowing limits up to Rs. 2,000 Cr under Section 180(1)(c) and approval for mortgage/charge creation on assets under Section 180(1)(a), indicating potential future leverage.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Guidance: Management guided for FY27 revenue growth of 80%, with an EBITDA margin of 24% ±100 bps. The Managing Director stated the quarterly performance remains in line with the growth guidance provided for the current fiscal year, and described the company as being at an inflection point with each key vertical positioned for the next phase of growth.

Observed performance (Q1 FY27): - Revenue from operations: Rs. 360.7 Cr, up 130.4% YoY and 17.9% QoQ — the highest ever quarterly revenue. - EBITDA: Rs. 85.1 Cr, up 199.7% YoY and 37.6% QoQ; EBITDA margin of 23.6%, within the guided 24% ±100 bps band. - PBT: Rs. 67.4 Cr, up 355.0% YoY. - PAT: Rs. 50.2 Cr, up 364.5% YoY; PAT margin of 13.9%.

Order book vs. guidance: Q1 FY27 order inflow of Rs. 2,895.1 Cr exceeded the full FY26 inflow of Rs. 2,453.3 Cr, supporting the revenue growth trajectory. Management expects a steady inflow of orders over the coming years in the nuclear segment.

Historical context: FY26 revenue was Rs. 876.2 Cr with EBITDA margin of 19.5% and PAT margin of 10.7%. Q1 FY27 margins (23.6% EBITDA) represent a step-up toward the guided full-year level, though the 80% full-year revenue growth guidance implies a significant acceleration from the 29.6% revenue growth recorded in FY26 over FY25.

Other forward-looking statements: Management expects significant orders from the Mahi Banswara project, robust orders in data centre infrastructure post-qualification, significant orders for LCA Tejas Mark IA actuator assemblies, and volume ramp-up across MNC aerospace customers as programmes transition from first-article qualification.

Broker Narrative

Initially, the broker framed the stock as a recovery play: Q3FY24 revenue had fallen 26% YoY, fuel-cell/YUMA deliveries were disrupted, and Bloom concentration and 45V tax-credit rules were key risks, with aerospace, products and nuclear acting as growth offsets. By the last report, the narrative had shifted to strong execution and scaling — 1QFY27 revenue/EBITDA/PAT grew 2.3x/3x/4.7x YoY, the order book reached INR51.4b, and working capital improved to 59 days. Persistent themes were nuclear, aerospace/data-centre opportunities and orderbook-led growth; key risks changed from clean-energy delivery disruptions and Bloom concentration to product-mix-driven gross margin contraction and a potential US data-centre slowdown.

Fears that came true

  • The flagged disruption from the YUMA hotbox phase-in/out and the fuel-cell design change to Santa Cruz 2 continued to weigh on shipments/revenue, contributing to the 2024-08-14 DISAPPOINTMENT (-12.7% actual return).

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that nuclear revenue would pick up from FY26 did not materialize by 1QFY27 — Clean Energy – Civil Nuclear revenue declined 41% YoY to INR32m.
  • The expectation that lower Bloom concentration would support margins did not materialize — gross margin contracted 870bp YoY in 1QFY27 due to product mix.
