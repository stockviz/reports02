Nilkamal — Q1 FY27 Results, AGM Commentary and Related Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Since March 2026, global geopolitical developments and supply chain disruptions have driven a significant increase in major raw material costs, described as approximately 50%.
  • The raw material cost surge mainly hit the B2B business, where volume growth declined by 37% in Q1 FY27.
  • Within B2B, Plastic Furniture fell 13%, Material Handling fell 16%, and Bubbleguard fell 36% during the quarter.
  • Standalone revenue from operations declined to ₹79,929.81 lakhs in Q1 FY27 from ₹86,448.81 lakhs in Q1 FY26; the press release describes this as a degrowth of about 7.5% (₹799 crores versus ₹865 crores).
  • Consolidated revenue from operations declined to ₹81,973.30 lakhs from ₹88,314.44 lakhs year-on-year.
  • Other expenses rose to ₹20,545.32 lakhs standalone in Q1 FY27 from ₹23,159.15 lakhs a year earlier, while employee benefits expense increased to ₹8,119.84 lakhs from ₹7,518.40 lakhs.
  • Finance costs remained a drag at ₹987.10 lakhs standalone, though lower than ₹1,111.70 lakhs in Q1 FY26.
  • The prior year (FY26) included an exceptional item of ₹1,540.60 lakhs related to the New Labour Codes effective 21 November 2025, arising from actuarial valuation of gratuity and leave liability.
  • The Chairman’s AGM statement noted that towards the end of FY26, global events created uncertainty in supply chains and a sharp increase in raw material prices, and that these market conditions continue to remain challenging.
  • The cautionary statement flags significant changes in the political and economic environment in India, tax laws, import duties, litigation and labour relations as developments that could affect operations.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • EBITDA rose 39% to approximately ₹80 crores in Q1 FY27 from ₹57 crores in Q1 FY26.
  • Standalone PBT and PAT both increased by 145%, to approximately ₹31 crores and ₹23 crores respectively.
  • The Retail & E-commerce segment grew 13% to approximately ₹104 crores from ₹92 crores, with E-commerce up 22% and retail stores up 6%.
  • The Retail & E-commerce segment turned profitable, recording EBIT of approximately ₹1.68 crores against a negative EBIT of approximately ₹9.5 crores in the prior year — described as coming “out of red to black.”
  • B2B profitability was sustained in value terms despite the 37% volume degrowth, helped by proactive price increases and product mix optimisation, with improved gross margin.
  • Within B2B, Mattress and Foam grew 49% and Ready Furniture grew 20%.
  • The retail network comprised 100 stores under COCO and FOFO formats as of 30 June 2026.
  • Net borrowing fell sharply to approximately ₹116 crores as of 30 June 2026 from approximately ₹331 crores as of 30 June 2025.
  • Capex spend rose to approximately ₹41 crores in Q1 FY27 from approximately ₹36 crores in Q1 FY26.
  • CARE Ratings reaffirmed the long-term rating at CARE AA Stable and short-term at CARE A1+.
  • In FY26, non-plastic furniture, mattresses and foam sales exceeded plastic furniture sales for the first time, which the Chairman described as an important milestone reflecting successful product diversification.
  • FY26 revenue from operations rose to approximately ₹3,686 crores from approximately ₹3,239 crores, a growth of about 14%.
  • FY26 PAT rose to approximately ₹105 crores from approximately ₹91 crores, despite the one-time exceptional employee benefit charge of approximately ₹15.41 crores.
  • The Company had a net cash surplus of approximately ₹67 crores at the end of FY26 and expects to meet capex and working capital requirements from internal accruals.
  • Renewable energy use from wind and solar rose 15% year-on-year and contributed around 16% of total energy consumption.
  • A third school was established in Gujarat, expected to educate more than 700 students annually, with all three schools together serving approximately 1,400+ students.
  • The Board recommended a final dividend of ₹20 per equity share (200% of face value), with total payout of approximately ₹29.84 crores, approved by members at the 40th AGM.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued raw material price inflation and supply chain disruption could further pressure B2B volumes and margins.
  • B2B volume degrowth of 37% indicates demand sensitivity to pricing actions taken to offset input costs.
  • Segment concentration risk: B2B revenue of ₹69,560.74 lakhs standalone dwarfs Retail & E-commerce at ₹10,369.07 lakhs, so B2B weakness disproportionately affects overall performance.
  • The Bubbleguard business declined 36% and Material Handling declined 16%, indicating weakness across multiple industrial product lines.
  • Consolidated results depend on subsidiaries in Sri Lanka and Ajman and a joint venture; two subsidiaries with revenues of ₹128 lakhs and PAT of ₹6 lakhs were not reviewed by any auditors, and certain overseas subsidiaries required conversion adjustments from local GAAP to Ind AS.
  • The Company has ₹4,900 lakhs of secured listed non-convertible debentures outstanding, secured by pari passu charge on plant and equipment and furniture and fixtures, with a security cover maintained above 1.25 times.
  • Forward-looking statements are subject to changes in the political and economic environment in India, tax laws, import duties, litigation and labour relations.
  • The New Labour Codes effective 21 November 2025 resulted in an incremental provision of ₹1,541 lakhs towards gratuity and leave liability, and could give rise to further such charges.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Management stated in the FY26 AGM that both B2B and B2C businesses continued to grow steadily during the year, and that both achieved healthy growth. This is supported by FY26 revenue growth of about 14% to approximately ₹3,686 crores.
  • Management stated that efforts to expand beyond plastic furniture were showing good results, with non-plastic furniture, mattresses and foam sales exceeding plastic furniture sales for the first time in FY26. In Q1 FY27, Mattress and Foam grew 49% and Ready Furniture grew 20%, while Plastic Furniture declined 13%, consistent with that diversification trend.
  • Management stated that suitable pricing and operational measures were taken to reduce the impact of raw material inflation and maintain competitiveness. In Q1 FY27, B2B value degrowth was limited to 10% against volume degrowth of 37%, with improved gross margin and sustained B2B profitability in value terms — evidence that these measures had an effect.
  • Management stated the Company would continue to meet all capex and working capital requirements from internal accruals, supported by a net cash surplus of approximately ₹67 crores at end-FY26. In Q1 FY27, net borrowing fell to approximately ₹116 crores from approximately ₹331 crores a year earlier, and capex of approximately ₹41 crores was funded during the quarter.
  • Management stated confidence that Nilkamal is well placed for future growth with a strong business foundation, committed employees and clear focus on long-term value creation. Observed Q1 FY27 performance shows EBITDA up 39%, PBT and PAT up 145%, and the Retail & E-commerce segment turning EBIT-positive, while revenue declined 7.5% — a mixed picture against that confidence.
  • Management’s cautionary statement acknowledges that forward-looking statements are based on the present business environment and regulatory framework and that the Company assumes no responsibility to update them as circumstances change.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative shifted from a broad, macro-driven buy thesis spanning multiple sectors (banking, hotels, infrastructure, non-Chinese sourcing) to a company-specific buy focused on Nilkamal’s Hosur plant ramp-up, grey-sector market share gains, and investments in retail/e-commerce. Persistent themes were higher input costs and a sluggish macro/industrial environment; the early generic tailwinds were replaced by operational drivers like non-plastic furniture and brand-engagement initiatives.

Fears that came true

  • Macro weakness, inflation, and rising interest rates in the US/Europe materialized as the economic slowdown and slow industrial capex noted in the last report, coinciding with the first report’s -3.9% actual return (DISAPPOINTMENT).
  • The early warning on higher input costs and import pressures in wood panel/building materials came true through higher polyethylene/polypropylene costs that Nilkamal could not fully pass on.

Optimism that failed

  • The anticipated ‘stable demand for pipes and infrastructure capex’ did not materialize; the last report highlighted economic slowdown and slow industrial capex instead.
  • The hoped-for ‘increasing preference for non-Chinese suppliers’ did not translate into a durable tailwind, as the last report cited keener competition from both non-regulated and regulated manufacturers.
