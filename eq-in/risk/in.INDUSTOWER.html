Headwinds and Challenges

  • Supply chain disruptions arising from geopolitical developments affected operations during the quarter ended June 30, 2026, though the company described its performance as resilient despite these.
  • Declining return ratios: Return on Equity (Pre-Tax) fell to 25.4% from 40.8% year-on-year, Return on Equity (Post Tax) fell to 18.9% from 30.8%, and Return on Capital Employed declined to 19.9% from 28.1%.
  • Softening per-tower economics: Sharing Revenue per Tower per month declined 0.9% year-on-year to Rs. 66,416 and 0.3% quarter-on-quarter. Sharing Revenue per Sharing Operator per month was broadly flat year-on-year at Rs. 41,082.
  • Marginally lower sharing factor: The average and closing sharing factor stood at 1.62 in the June 2026 quarter versus 1.63 a year earlier.
  • Customer concentration risk: A large customer accounts for a significant part of revenue from operations and a significant part of outstanding trade receivables and unbilled revenue as at June 30, 2026. The company does not recognise revenue equalisation income or the straight-lining lease rental asset for this customer given its financial condition, and continues to monitor that customer’s financial condition.
  • Rising doubtful receivable allowances: Allowances for doubtful receivables (net) were Rs. 233 million in the June 2026 quarter, compared with a write-back of Rs. 883 million in the June 2025 quarter (which had aided that quarter’s results).
  • ESG rating slippage: SES ESG Research Private Limited noted the company’s rating marginally declined to 72.1 in FY 2025-26 from 72.3 in FY 2024-25, due to changes in select Environmental and Social parameters, primarily renewable energy and safety.
  • Governance-related dissent at the AGM: At the 20th AGM held on August 19, 2026, the resolution to re-appoint Mr. Soumen Ray as a Director liable to retire by rotation saw 31.07% of Public Institutions’ votes against, and the resolution to re-appoint Mr. Rajan Bharti Mittal saw 37.19% of Public Institutions’ votes against. Both resolutions passed with requisite majority.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Steady top-line and operating growth: Consolidated revenue for Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) was Rs. 8,431 Crores, up 4.6% year-on-year; EBITDA was Rs. 4,521 Crores, up 3.0% year-on-year, with an EBITDA margin of 53.6%; Profit after Tax was Rs. 1,746 Crores, up 0.5% year-on-year.
  • Strong cash generation: Operating Free Cash Flow rose 23.4% year-on-year to Rs. 1,781 Crores, and Adjusted Fund from Operations rose 5.2% to Rs. 2,987 Crores.
  • Network expansion: Total tower base grew 6.3% year-on-year to 267,611, and co-location base grew 5.1% year-on-year to 432,250. Sequentially, towers rose by 3,097 and co-locations by 4,236.
  • International expansion: The company has secured licenses across all three target markets in Africa and remains on track to commence rollouts in 2026. Subsidiaries were incorporated in Uganda (January 20, 2026), Zambia (January 15, 2026), Nigeria (January 15, 2026), and Dubai (December 2025), plus Indus Towers Global Ventures IFSC Limited (April 28, 2026).
  • Digital and energy investments: Management cited investments in digital transformation, AI-led capabilities, and energy management as strengthening operational agility, productivity, and service delivery.
  • Demand backdrop: Management stated that demand for digital infrastructure continues to rise, and believes the company’s value proposition, execution, and technology-led approach position it to capitalise on growth opportunities.
  • Improved ESG rating: Crisil ESG Ratings & Analytics Limited improved the company’s rating to 63 (Strong) in FY 2025-26 from 57 (Adequate) in FY 2024-25.
  • Lean business line growth: Lean co-locations stood at 14,024, with sharing revenue per sharing operator per month up 2.1% year-on-year to Rs. 16,707.

Key Risks

  • Customer financial condition: The large customer’s ability to generate sufficient cash flow to meet obligations payable over the next 12 months is central to the company’s revenue recognition and receivables recovery assumptions. The company’s management believes carrying amounts of receivables (including unbilled revenue) and property, plant and equipment related to this customer will be recovered in the normal course of business, but will continue to monitor the customer’s financial condition.
  • Revenue recognition constraint: Because of the customer’s financial condition, the company does not recognise revenue equalisation income and asset on account of straight-lining of lease rentals for that customer.
  • Return ratio compression: The year-on-year declines in RoE and RoCE indicate pressure on profitability relative to capital employed.
  • Pricing pressure: Declining sharing revenue per tower suggests softer per-unit monetisation.
  • ESG parameter risk: The SES rating decline was attributed to renewable energy and safety parameters, indicating exposure to these areas.
  • Shareholder dissent: The significant minority votes against two director re-appointments at the AGM indicate governance-related scrutiny from public institutional shareholders.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • International expansion: Management stated the company has secured licenses across all three target markets in Africa and remains on track to commence rollouts in 2026. Observed evidence: subsidiaries were incorporated across Uganda, Zambia, Nigeria, and Dubai between December 2025 and April 2026, consistent with preparation for rollouts, though no rollout revenue has yet been reported.
  • Resilient performance despite supply chain disruptions: Management stated that despite supply chain disruptions arising from geopolitical developments, the company delivered resilient performance supported by customer-led network expansion, disciplined cost management, and strong cash flow generation. Observed evidence: revenue grew 4.6% year-on-year, EBITDA grew 3.0%, tower base grew 6.3%, co-location base grew 5.1%, and Operating Free Cash Flow grew 23.4%, consistent with the stated resilience.
  • Profitability trajectory: While management highlighted steady performance, Profit after Tax grew only 0.5% year-on-year (to Rs. 1,746 Crores per the press release; Rs. 17,458 million per the consolidated statement), and EBIT declined 2.1% year-on-year, indicating that bottom-line growth lagged revenue growth.
  • Return ratios: No forward guidance was provided on return ratios; observed performance showed year-on-year declines in RoE (Pre-Tax to 25.4% from 40.8%) and RoCE (to 19.9% from 28.1%).
  • Dividend: The AGM on August 19, 2026 approved a dividend on equity shares for the financial year ended March 31, 2026, with 99.9997% of votes polled in favour.
  • Related party transaction: The AGM approved a Material Related Party Transaction with Bharti Airtel Limited, with 99.9992% of votes polled in favour (promoter/promoter group did not vote, as they were interested in the resolution).
  • Investor engagement: Officials of Indus Towers Limited will attend an investor conference with Eastspring Investments and T. Rowe Price on Friday, September 18, 2026, from 01:00 P.M. IST to 02:00 P.M. IST, in an in-person group meet format.
  • ESG ratings: The company has not engaged SES and Crisil for ESG ratings; both ratings were independently and voluntarily assigned based on public disclosures of the company.

Broker Narrative

The narrative swung from an aggressive Buy in July 2023, driven by expectations of Bharti/RJio-led ARPU and tenancy growth and nil VIL provisioning, to a Neutral in July 2026 as energy under-recoveries, lower ARPT, moderating tenancy additions, VIL fundraise delays and RJio tenancy exit risk dominated. The persistent themes were telecom operator health and tower tenancy/revenue quality, while the later report added Africa expansion, FCF distribution and structural energy-cost fixes as offsets.

Fears that came true

  • The early concern about VIL’s deteriorating financial health materialised in delays to Vi’s potential fundraise and a INR233m bad debt provision, weighing on tenancy and earnings.

Optimism that failed

  • The assumption of nil VIL receivable provisioning failed, as the last report disclosed a INR233m bad debt provision.
  • The early expectation of strong Indus tenancy additions led by Bharti’s 4G rollout faded, with tower/tenancy additions moderating QoQ due to manufacturing constraints.
  • The optimism on RJio subscriber/tenancy growth reversed, with ~5k RJio tenancy exits baked into 2HFY27 estimates.
  • The expected ARPU-led uplift did not persist, as 1QFY27 recurring EBITDA rose only 1% QoQ with marginally lower ARPT.
