AvenuesAI Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Net revenue softness and take-rate compression. In Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026), gross revenue from operations rose 109% year-on-year to INR 2,680 crore, but net revenue stood at INR 147 crore, marginally down 3% year-on-year. Management attributed this to Q1 typically being a seasonally weaker quarter (with Q2/Q3 stronger due to the festival season), compression in take rates, and payments being a competitive business. EBITDA growth in the quarter came largely from the expense line rather than net revenue expansion.

Front-loaded cost base. Management stated the cost base remains front-loaded as the company invests in AI infrastructure, international expansion, and new financial services capabilities, and that it is not building for a short-term margin spike.

International expansion shortfall. Reflecting on FY26, management said it could have expanded internationally more aggressively, that macroeconomic conditions had an impact, and that it could have made larger AI investments to reach market faster. Middle East operations (UAE, Saudi Arabia) were noted as potentially affected by macroeconomic conditions, though management viewed this as short-term.

Early-stage new businesses. PayCentral merchant onboarding was described as early days, with consumer adoption of agent-based transactions still in early phases. RediffPay was still in CUG (closed user group) and not yet in production during the quarter. Management stated FY27 guidance assumes no meaningful contribution from RediffPay.

Token cost dynamics. Token pricing was described as slightly inflationary, though for client-facing usage this is treated as a pass-through; SLM-based on-premise deployments were cited as reducing token cost to roughly one-tenth of typical levels.

Partly paid-up share forfeiture. Holders of 69,43,139 partly paid-up equity shares failed to pay the outstanding First and Final Call Money of Rs. 5 per share by August 31, 2026, resulting in forfeiture of those shares, including amounts already paid thereon.

Risk factors cited in the annual report material. Regulatory and compliance risks across multiple jurisdictions; take rate and margin pressure from zero-MDR volumes and pricing competition; concentration and counterparty risks; technology and cybersecurity vulnerabilities; data protection and privacy regulatory demands; enterprise platform execution risks; AI deployment risks; currency fluctuations; and international licensing and fee regulations. Other cited risks include fluctuations in foreign exchange rates, changes in global economic conditions and consumer spending, world events, changes in Indian government policies or regulations, competition, management of growth, and potential fluctuations in operating results.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Regulatory approvals. The Central Bank of the UAE granted In-Principle Approval to Avenues World FZ LLC for a Retail Payment Services – Category III Licence. The RBI authorised AvenuesAI Limited (GoWallet) to set up a payment system for issuance and operation of Prepaid Payment Instruments under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. Management described these as strengthening the regulated payments infrastructure across India and international markets.

Credit distribution via asset-light investments. The company progressed on a proposed investment of up to 2.5% in RatnaFin Capital (an RBI-registered NBFC with an SME lending franchise); the Board on August 11, 2026 approved increasing the investment limit from not more than Rs. 66 crore to not more than Rs. 70 crore, to be completed in one or more tranches on or before March 31, 2027. The company also announced plans to acquire up to a 7% secondary equity stake in Online PSB Loans (OPL). The model keeps lending risk with regulated partners while AvenuesAI provides AI payments infrastructure and merchant distribution.

AI-led initiatives. Phronetic AI’s PayCentral was described as India’s first agentic payment platform built on Google’s Agent Payments Protocol. The company is developing on-premise AI solutions using small language models (roughly $1 billion to $10 billion parameter range) for enterprises with sensitive data requirements. The proposed merger of Nueromind Technologies (a wholly owned subsidiary engaged in AI/ML research and development) into AvenuesAI is intended to integrate AI talent, IP, and technology development and accelerate commercialisation. The company also plans a Transaction Intelligence Score (TISco) as an intelligence layer across its payments and financial ecosystem.

Consumer and enterprise platforms. RediffPay (UPI-based consumer payments) was expected to move out of CUG into production in the month of the call. RediffOne is evolving into an operating platform for businesses spanning commerce, communication, compliance, and intelligence; RediffTV and RediffGurus add content and community. Management framed Rediff as three opportunities: RediffOne (enterprise), RediffPay (consumer payments/fintech), and content/RediffTV.

Payments volume growth and embedded distribution. Q1 FY27 transaction processing volume grew 74% year-on-year to INR 1,479 billion, with strength across utility payments, government payments, retail, B2B, and hospitality. The CCAvenue gateway was integrated with Shiprocket Checkout, giving B2C and SME merchants access to same-day settlement, international payments, no-cost EMI, and payment links across WhatsApp, SMS, Instagram, and email. BillAvenue, ResAvenue, and the form builder platform were cited as deepening merchant workflow embedding.

UPI MDR monetisation. Management noted that approximately 22% of transactions are on UPI and that any monetisation of UPI would add revenue. The enabling legislation had passed the Lok Sabha and awaited Rajya Sabha passage, Presidential approval, and gazette notification, with pricing and monetisation parameters to be determined by NPCI and RBI. Management stated it expects the MDR to apply to large merchants and higher-value transactions, and that on the online acquiring side the market is evenly spread, with the company holding an 8%–10% share.

International expansion. The United States was named a key strategic market for FY27 payments expansion. Management stated the company aims to operationalise Australia and the United States in FY27 and targets 12–15% of Payments Net Revenue from international operations by FY28. The company also cited an expanded regulated perimeter across online aggregation, physical aggregation, bill payments, prepaid instruments, and GIFT-IFSC services, plus authentication liberalisation and growth in B2B invoice flows and embedded finance.

Partly paid-up share conversion. 10,68,411 partly paid-up equity shares were converted into fully paid-up equity shares upon receipt of the First and Final Call Money.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory and compliance risk across jurisdictions, including international licensing and fee regulations and data protection/privacy requirements.
  • Margin and take-rate pressure from zero-MDR volumes and pricing competition; management noted large incumbents can subsidise payments, making the eventual UPI MDR benefit uncertain in magnitude.
  • Execution risk in new businesses — PayCentral, RediffPay, TISco, and on-premise SLM offerings are early-stage; management indicated AI business impact is roughly 9 to 18 months out and that meaningful platform results should begin showing in the Q3–Q4 timeframe.
  • Concentration and counterparty risk, technology and cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and enterprise platform execution risk.
  • Macro and currency risk — foreign exchange fluctuations, global economic conditions, consumer spending, world events, and changes in Indian government policies or regulations.
  • Capital allocation risk — incremental accruals are being reinvested into AI and new businesses rather than maximising near-term earnings, which management described as a conscious decision with an internal EPS guardrail (described as approximately 15%, a guardrail rather than a target).
  • Forfeiture-related risk — non-payment of call money by August 31, 2026 resulted in forfeiture of 69,43,139 partly paid-up shares, including amounts already paid.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

FY27 guidance. Management expects FY27 consolidated revenue of INR 11,000 crore to INR 13,000 crore (INR 110,000 million to INR 130,000 million), described as growth of around 35% and above, and FY27 EPS of INR 8.75 to INR 9.50 per share based on the proposed post-corporate-action face value of INR 10 per share.

Q1 FY27 observed performance. Gross revenue from operations was INR 2,680 crore (up 109% year-on-year); net revenue was INR 147 crore (down 3% year-on-year); EBITDA excluding other income was INR 100 crore (up 41%); PAT was INR 85 crore (up 45%). In the press release figures, revenue from operations was INR 26,804.0 million versus INR 12,802.1 million, EBITDA excluding other income was INR 1,000.2 million versus INR 709.9 million, profit before tax was INR 866.5 million versus INR 788.9 million, and PAT was INR 847.6 million versus INR 584.3 million.

FY26 baseline. Full-year FY26 gross revenue was INR 8,116 crore (up 103%), net revenue was INR 603 crore (up 15%), and PAT was INR 295 crore (up 25%). The annual report material cites FY26 consolidated revenue from operations of INR 81,158 million, a 55% increase in Payments Transaction Processing Value to INR 5,038 billion, and the successful conclusion of a INR 700 crore Rights Issue.

Guidance versus observed trends. The FY27 revenue guidance implies growth of around 35% and above off the FY26 gross revenue base, a marked deceleration from the 103%–109% gross revenue growth observed in FY26 and Q1 FY27. Management explicitly directed investors to focus on net revenue rather than gross revenue, noting gross revenue may fluctuate based on mix. On net revenue, Q1 FY27 declined 3% year-on-year, while management expects credit distribution and other new revenue streams to contribute to net revenue over time. Management stated FY27 guidance assumes no meaningful contribution from RediffPay.

Margin commentary. Management declined to specify a sustainable EBITDA margin target, describing approximately 15% as a guardrail rather than a target, and stated it will not pursue short-term margin spikes but will build for a structurally stronger margin profile through FY27 and FY28. Management also stated the FY27 EPS guidance is net of capital allocated for AI and platform spending, and that incremental accruals beyond a certain level will be selectively reinvested.

Corporate actions and timelines. The Board on August 11, 2026 approved: the scheme of amalgamation of Nueromind Technologies into AvenuesAI (subject to NCLT and other approvals); variation in the objects of the Rights Issue (Rs. 29,399.59 lakh allocated, Rs. 944 lakh utilised, Rs. 28,455.59 lakh remaining unutilised, to be undertaken directly by the company instead of through Nueromind, subject to special resolution); consolidation of ten Re. 1 equity shares into one Rs. 10 equity share (expected completion on or before three months from conclusion of the AGM); and convening the 16th AGM on September 29, 2026. On the Rediff IPO, management said it was slightly early to discuss directly but that due process had been followed and updates were visible on the SEBI website, and that the corporate actions have no bearing on the Rediff filing. The company is completing formalities for conversion of the 10,68,411 partly paid-up shares into fully paid-up shares and obtaining listing and trading approvals.
