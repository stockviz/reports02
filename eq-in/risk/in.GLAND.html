Gland Pharma: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Other Core Markets decline: Revenue from Canada, Australia and New Zealand fell 28% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 (to ₹534 million) and 9% quarter-on-quarter. Management attributes this to volume decline in existing products in these markets.
  • India B2B softness: India revenue declined 1% quarter-on-quarter (though up 12% year-on-year), with the decline attributed to lower sales of Enoxaparin.
  • B2B sequential dip: The B2B business (including B2C) declined 3% quarter-on-quarter in Q1 FY27, even as it grew 19% year-on-year.
  • Profitability moderation versus the preceding quarter: PBT and PAT both declined 14% quarter-on-quarter, and EBITDA margin (27%) and adjusted EBITDA margin (28%) were lower than Q4 FY26 (29% and 30% respectively), though higher year-on-year.
  • Customs demand: On July 16, 2026, the company disclosed an Order-in-Original (No. HYD-CUS-004-COM-26-27 dated July 10, 2026, received July 15, 2026) from the Principal Commissioner of Customs, Hyderabad, confirming an IGST demand of ₹3,52,93,678 along with applicable interest, plus an equal penalty of ₹3,52,93,678, relating to an IGST benefit availed under Notification No. 18/2015-Cus for FY 2020-21 and FY 2021-22. The company states it views the demand and penalty as not justified, is filing an appeal, and assesses no material impact on financials, operations or other activities.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record quarterly revenue: Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was ₹18,003 million, up 20% year-on-year and 3% quarter-on-quarter, described as yet another quarter with record revenues.
  • CDMO momentum: The CDMO business contributed 50% of revenues and grew 20% year-on-year (and 11% quarter-on-quarter) to ₹8,915 million, driven by recent product launches from the CDMO portfolio and strong customer demand.
  • B2B scale: The B2B business contributed the other 50% of revenues and grew 19% year-on-year to ₹9,088 million.
  • US market strength: US revenue rose 32% year-on-year to ₹9,810 million, and Europe rose 20% year-on-year to ₹3,954 million. Rest of the world grew 2% year-on-year but 19% quarter-on-quarter.
  • New launches and pipeline: Four molecules were launched in the USA in Q1 FY27, including Multi-Vitamin and Leucovorin calcium. Six in-house complex products have been launched, with three more in line for approval. Fifteen co-development products (seven 505(b)(2) and eight ANDAs) are in progress, with commercialization anticipated to begin in FY28.
  • RTU bags: 21 Ready-to-Use infusion bag products have been filed and 18 approved so far, with 11 more under development; the total RTU bag portfolio addresses a market opportunity of approximately $644 million in the US.
  • Large CDMO partnership: A CDMO partnership with a global pharmaceutical company carries an expected annualized revenue potential of approximately USD 90–100 million once all products are commercialized, with technology transfer planned for completion within two years and revenues expected from calendar year 2029.
  • Strategic collaborations: A long-term strategic collaboration with Neuland Laboratories for manufacturing sterile APIs for microparticle depot products, and an in-licensing agreement with a China-based development company for a niche liposomal product for the US and European markets.
  • Corporate simplification: Management has proposed making Gland Pharma USA Inc. a direct wholly-owned subsidiary (acquiring 100% of its equity from Gland Pharma International Pte. Ltd for a cash consideration of USD 501,208), removing multiple layers and streamlining the corporate structure. The transaction is a related party transaction conducted at arm’s length.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory and tax exposure: The customs order illustrates exposure to demands, interest and penalties on past incentive claims; the company is appealing and assesses no material impact.
  • Market concentration and product dependence: The Other Core Markets decline and the India B2B decline tied to lower Enoxaparin sales show sensitivity to volume trends in specific products and geographies.
  • Execution and timing risk on pipeline: Commercialization of the 15 co-development products is anticipated only in FY28, and CDMO partnership revenues are expected to commence from calendar year 2029, with technology transfer planned over two years.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The company notes that actual results may vary materially from statements of future expectations due to changes in economic, business, competitive, technological and/or regulatory factors.
  • Investor meeting schedule variability: The schedule of analyst and institutional investor meetings (September 16–25, 2026, including East Bridge Capital, IIFL Capital, Antique Broking and a Nomura-arranged group meeting) is subject to change due to exigencies on the part of the investor, analyst or company.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Growth platform and CDMO strategy: Management stated the strong start to FY27 reflects successful execution of the CDMO strategy and resilience of the B2B model, with growth driven by recent CDMO product launches and strong customer demand. Observed performance is consistent: CDMO revenue grew 20% year-on-year and total revenue grew 20% year-on-year in Q1 FY27.
  • Margin trajectory: Management highlighted strengthening the growth platform through a robust CDMO pipeline, investments in differentiated technologies and capacity expansions. Observed margins improved year-on-year (EBITDA margin 27% vs 24%; adjusted EBITDA margin 28% vs 25%) but moderated sequentially from Q4 FY26 (29% and 30%).
  • Pipeline commercialization: Management anticipates commercialization of the 15 co-development products to begin in FY28, and completion of technology transfer for the global pharmaceutical company CDMO partnership within two years with revenues from calendar year 2029. These are forward-looking timelines without corresponding observed revenue yet.
  • Corporate restructuring: Management expects to complete the acquisition of Gland US equity shares by October 31, 2026; the transaction had been proposed but not yet completed as of the disclosure.
  • Governance: The 48th Annual General Meeting held on August 25, 2026 passed five ordinary resolutions with the requisite majority, including adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements, declaration of a final dividend, and reappointment of Mr. Wenjie Zhang and Ms. Wei Huang as Directors.

Broker Narrative

The narrative stayed strongly positive, with Buy ratings dominating from the first to the last report, but the emphasis shifted from defending margin dilution and weak ex-Cenexi core sales to celebrating US growth, complex-product/CDMO wins, and a margin recovery. Early concerns about Cenexi persisted, first as a lower-margin dilutive acquisition and planned shutdown, later as a slowdown and heatwave-driven quality-release delays. Base-business worries also evolved: the initial ex-Cenexi sales decline reappeared as pricing pressure and flat RoW revenue from delayed Saudi tenders. By the last report, optimism was anchored on a broader pipeline, new CDMO contracts, GLP-1 collaborations, and upgraded revenue guidance.

Fears that came true

  • Cenexi’s dilutive/operational drag materialized: later reports flagged a Cenexi slowdown and European heatwaves delaying quality releases, mirroring the initial concerns about Cenexi’s lower margins and facility shutdown.
  • Core-market weakness persisted as pricing pressure in the base business and flat RoW revenue from delayed Saudi tender awards, correlating with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in 2024 such as -5.4%, -12.9%, and -6.1% actual returns.

Optimism that failed

  • Expected growth in RoW/GCC markets did not materialize as RoW revenue stayed flat due to delayed Saudi tender awards.
  • Cenexi’s expected CDMO and complex-product synergies were slow to materialize because of the Cenexi slowdown and quality-release delays.
