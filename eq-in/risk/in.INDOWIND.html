Indowind Energy: Recent Announcements and Financial Performance

Headwinds and Challenges

The company attributes a shortfall in income to the delayed onset of the wind season, as noted in the financial results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026. This seasonal factor weighed on revenue generation during the period.

On a sequential basis, consolidated total revenue declined from ₹526.78 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 to ₹843.58 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2026 — although the March quarter itself had been depressed. More notably, consolidated revenue fell sharply year-on-year, from ₹1,155.15 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2025 to ₹843.58 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2026. Standalone revenue followed the same pattern, dropping from ₹940.88 lakhs to ₹695.05 lakhs over the same comparison.

The statutory auditors issued a qualified review opinion on both the standalone and consolidated results, citing several accounting concerns:

  • An arbitration claim of ₹9,083.39 lakhs filed against the Suzlon Group in earlier years, with ₹1,494.36 lakhs paid to Suzlon Energy Ltd. accounted as an advance without adequate audit evidence and not expensed. A further ₹845.59 lakhs was recognised as compensation receivable from Suzlon Energy Ltd. without counterparty confirmation or measurement under Ind AS 109. The auditors state that had the advance been expensed and the receivable appropriately amortised, profit and reserves would have been lower by those amounts.
  • ₹102 lakhs recognised as recoverable from Bank of Baroda based on a legal claim pending since 2007 before the Hon’ble High Court of Bombay, without confirmation or sufficient audit evidence, which the auditors state is not in compliance with Ind AS 37 and Ind AS 109.
  • Goodwill of ₹7,454.69 lakhs transferred from Ind Eco Ventures Limited pursuant to a common-control merger, for which no impairment assessment was carried out as required under applicable Ind AS. The auditors state they are unable to determine whether an impairment loss is required.

The company also received a stock exchange query regarding the format of the Statement of Impact of Audit Qualifications (not signed by MD/CEO) and discrepancies between XBRL and PDF figures in the standalone P&L and Balance Sheet. The company responded that the statement was signed by Whole-Time Director Mr. N.K. Haribabu since it has no MD/CEO, and that corrections were being resubmitted.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

The company reported positive profitability in the quarter ended 30 June 2026. Consolidated profit after minority interest was ₹199.38 lakhs, compared with a loss of ₹745.90 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 and a profit of ₹229.45 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2025. Standalone profit after tax was ₹156.57 lakhs, versus a loss of ₹630.49 lakhs sequentially and a profit of ₹192.11 lakhs year-on-year.

Consolidated earnings per share stood at ₹0.13 basic and diluted for the June 2026 quarter, against ₹0.20 in the prior-year quarter and a negative ₹0.47 in the March 2026 quarter. Standalone EPS was ₹0.10, compared with ₹0.15 a year earlier.

The board approved the notice for the 31st Annual General Meeting, scheduled for 25 September 2026 via video conferencing, with a record date of 18 September 2026 and the register of members closed from 19 to 25 September 2026.

The board also recommended director appointments for shareholder approval: the re-appointment of Ms. Sangeetha Harilal Lakhi as an Independent Director for five consecutive years from 8 August 2027, and the appointment of Mr. N.K. Haribabu as Whole-Time Director for three consecutive years from 7 June 2027. The company confirmed neither director is debarred by any SEBI or other authority order. Ms. Lakhi is described as a Senior Partner at Rajani Associates with over 33 years of legal experience, including leadership of International Capital Markets practice since 2004 and 64 domestic capital market transactions completed in the last four years. Mr. Haribabu is a Chartered Accountant with extensive experience in finance, merchant banking, and capital markets, notably with PNB.

The consolidated results include the subsidiary Indowind Power Private Limited, whose interim financial information (not reviewed or audited) reflected total revenue of ₹158.08 lakhs and total net profit of ₹22.95 lakhs for the quarter ended 30 June 2025 as considered in the statement.

Key Risks

  • Audit qualification risk: The qualified opinion identifies departures from Ind AS requirements, including unrecognised expenses and receivables, a provision not made for the Bank of Baroda claim, and an untested goodwill impairment. These matters could affect reported profits and reserves if resolved adversely.
  • Litigation and recovery risk: The Suzlon arbitration claim and the long-pending Bank of Baroda legal claim (since 2007) carry uncertainty over recoverability and counterparty confirmation.
  • Goodwill impairment risk: The ₹7,454.69 lakhs goodwill from the Ind Eco Ventures merger has not been impairment-tested, leaving uncertainty over potential write-downs.
  • Seasonal revenue risk: The delayed onset of the wind season directly affected income, indicating sensitivity to wind patterns.
  • Disclosure and compliance risk: The exchange flagged format and XBRL/PDF discrepancies, requiring resubmission of corrected integrated financials.
  • Subsidiary review gap: Indowind Power Private Limited’s interim financial information was not reviewed or audited.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

The company’s stated explanation for the income shortfall — the delayed onset of the wind season — is the only forward-looking or causal guidance provided in the material. Observed performance shows the June 2026 quarter returned to profitability on both standalone and consolidated bases after the March 2026 quarter losses, but revenue remained below the prior-year June quarter. The company has not provided quantitative guidance on future revenue, margins, or the resolution of the audit qualifications, the Suzlon arbitration, the Bank of Baroda claim, or the goodwill impairment assessment. The board’s director re-appointment recommendations and AGM scheduling represent governance actions rather than business performance guidance.
