Ramco Systems: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Sequential revenue decline. Consolidated revenue from operations fell from Rs. 1,853.63 million in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 to Rs. 1,727.70 million in the quarter ended June 30, 2026. Standalone revenue also declined sequentially, from Rs. 974.60 million to Rs. 853.89 million over the same period.

Sharp compression in consolidated profitability. Consolidated profit before exceptional items and tax dropped from Rs. 220.11 million in the March 2026 quarter to Rs. 34.49 million in the June 2026 quarter. Consolidated profit after tax fell from Rs. 251.71 million to Rs. 5.62 million. Consolidated basic earnings per share declined from Rs. 6.70 to Rs. 0.16.

Rising cost base. Consolidated other expenses increased from Rs. 602.22 million in the March 2026 quarter to Rs. 669.06 million in the June 2026 quarter. Standalone other expenses rose from Rs. 203.17 million to Rs. 245.85 million. Consolidated depreciation and amortisation also edged up, from Rs. 230.91 million to Rs. 232.37 million.

Foreign exchange and translation drags. Standalone other expenses for the June 2026 quarter included a foreign exchange fluctuation loss of Rs. 1.10 million, compared with a gain of Rs. 8.91 million in the prior-year June quarter. On the consolidated statement, the effect of change in functional currency of foreign operations was a negative Rs. 11.44 million in the June 2026 quarter, following a negative Rs. 73.56 million in the March 2026 quarter and a negative Rs. 201.57 million for the year ended March 31, 2026.

Tax-related earnings impact. The company adopted the New Tax Regime effective from Tax Year 2026-27 and re-measured deferred tax assets and liabilities as of April 1, 2026. This resulted in a tax impact of Rs. 20.54 million recognised in the profit and loss account and a tax reversal impact of Rs. 3.00 million recognised in other comprehensive income during the June 2026 quarter. Standalone total tax expense was Rs. 20.54 million in the June 2026 quarter.

Bad debts and doubtful advances. Standalone other expenses included bad debts/provision for doubtful debts and advances of Rs. 5.30 million in the June 2026 quarter, compared with Rs. 0.06 million in the prior-year June quarter. On a consolidated basis, the corresponding figure was Rs. 17.11 million, compared with a reversal of Rs. 12.90 million in the prior-year June quarter.

Leadership transition. Mr. Ravikula Chandran R, Chief Financial Officer and Key Managerial Personnel, will retire upon superannuation with effect from the close of business hours on November 30, 2026. Mrs. Gayathri R has been appointed as CFO and Key Managerial Personnel with effect from December 1, 2026.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Year-on-year revenue growth. Consolidated revenue from operations rose to Rs. 1,727.70 million in the June 2026 quarter from Rs. 1,613.17 million in the June 2025 quarter. Standalone revenue from operations rose to Rs. 853.89 million from Rs. 877.68 million a year earlier.

Year-on-year profit improvement at the standalone level. Standalone profit before tax increased to Rs. 107.42 million in the June 2026 quarter from Rs. 83.98 million in the June 2025 quarter. Standalone profit after tax rose to Rs. 86.88 million from Rs. 48.82 million, and standalone basic earnings per share rose to Rs. 2.31 from Rs. 1.31.

Dividend income from wholly owned subsidiaries. Standalone other income included dividend income from wholly owned subsidiaries of Rs. 116.99 million in the June 2026 quarter, compared with nil in the prior-year June quarter and Rs. 15.21 million in the March 2026 quarter. Standalone other income rose to Rs. 131.98 million from Rs. 32.44 million a year earlier.

Safran Helicopter Engines MoU. Ramco Systems signed a Memorandum of Understanding with Safran Helicopter Engines, described as the world’s leading manufacturer of rotorcraft turbines, to enable seamless integration of Safran’s engine data with Ramco Aviation Software and to earn the EngineLife Connect label. The partnership is expected to reduce manual data entry, improve data accuracy and consistency, strengthen digital services for operators, and reduce the time and effort required for engine data initialization within the MIS while improving the structure, quality, and consistency of data supporting airworthiness and maintenance planning.

Employee retention and talent attraction. Shareholders approved the Employee Stock Option Scheme 2026 at the 29th Annual General Meeting held on August 20, 2026, with voting results declared on August 21, 2026. The scheme covers 15,00,000 options convertible into 15,00,000 equity shares of Rs. 10 each. Its stated purposes include rewarding employees for association and performance, motivating contributions to growth and profitability, attracting high-quality talent, ensuring long-term retention, and fostering a sense of belonging. Vesting commences one year from the grant date and extends up to a maximum of ten years from the grant date; options may be exercised within a maximum of ten years from the respective vesting date. The scheme is implemented through fresh allotment of equity shares and administered by the Nomination and Remuneration Committee.

Capital base expansion through ESOS. During the June 2026 quarter, the company allotted 122,334 equity shares under its Employee Stock Option Schemes, increasing paid-up capital to Rs. 376.19 million from Rs. 374.97 million as at March 31, 2026.

Key Risks

Dilution from ESOS 2026. The scheme provides for new issue of up to 15,00,000 equity shares. The diluted earnings per share disclosure was marked not applicable at the time of shareholder approval, as the outcome pertained to approval of the scheme by members. The exercise price is to be determined by the Nomination and Remuneration Committee at grant, linked to market price, and may carry a discount, subject to a floor of face value and a ceiling of market price.

Concentration limits within the scheme. The maximum number of options granted to any eligible employee during any one year shall not equal or exceed 1% of issued equity share capital at the time of grant, unless separately approved by shareholders. Non-Executive Directors excluding Independent Directors may be granted up to 3,00,000 options in a financial year and up to 5,00,000 options in aggregate.

Vesting and lapse conditions. Vesting is subject to continued and uninterrupted employment, with the grantee not having submitted a resignation, received notice of termination, or being in a notice period at the time of vesting. Failure to exercise vested options within the exercise period results in lapse, with lapsed options added back to the pool.

Foreign exchange exposure. The consolidated results reflect significant sensitivity to currency movements, evidenced by the effect of change in functional currency of foreign operations and foreign exchange fluctuation losses and gains recorded within other expenses.

Tax regime transition effects. The adoption of the New Tax Regime and the associated re-measurement of deferred tax assets and liabilities produced a material charge in the June 2026 quarter, with the resulting tax impact recognised in both profit and loss and other comprehensive income.

Dependence on subsidiary distributions at the standalone level. Standalone other income in the June 2026 quarter was substantially supported by dividend income from wholly owned subsidiaries of Rs. 116.99 million, which compares with nil in the prior-year June quarter.
