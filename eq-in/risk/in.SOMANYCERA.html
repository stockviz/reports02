Somany Ceramics: Recent Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Energy cost and transition burden: Rising energy costs and the substantial upfront investment required for energy-efficient technologies are identified as a continuing significant challenge. The Company also flags growing expectations for energy reporting and increasingly stringent emissions regulations as pressures on its energy-intensive operations.
  • Water scarcity and regulation: Growing water scarcity, increasingly stringent regulations, and potential reputational impacts from unsustainable water use are identified as risks. The Company notes it has submitted a fresh application to the Haryana Water Resource Authority to ensure continued compliance for groundwater extraction.
  • Product quality and technology obsolescence: Dependence on outdated technologies that have not evolved with current sustainability regulations and tile-design innovations can negatively affect product quality and safety, customer loyalty, brand image, and profitability.
  • Supply chain ESG exposure: Environmental and social issues across the value chain can potentially disrupt supply chain operations.
  • Compliance and ethical conduct exposure: Failure to comply with legal and ethical standards can lead to legal and reputational sanctions, supply chain harm, restricted market access, and loss of stakeholder trust.
  • Workforce composition and turnover: As at the end of FY 2025-26, total employees were 1,739 (4.49% female) and total workers were 2,624 (0.42% female). Permanent employee turnover rose to 12.68% in FY 2025-26 from 11.90% in FY 2024-25 and 11.78% in FY 2023-24. Permanent worker turnover declined to 3.85% from 5.70% and 5.89% in the two preceding years. Female representation on the Board was 1 of 8 directors (12.50%), and nil among Key Management Personnel.
  • Stakeholder complaints: Three shareholder complaints were filed in FY 2024-25, all resolved with none pending at year end; no complaints were filed by any stakeholder group in FY 2025-26.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Core business mix: Ceramic/vitrified wall and floor tiles contributed 83.47% of turnover and sanitaryware and bath fittings 12.02%, together accounting for over 90% of turnover.
  • Reach: Operations span 2 national plants and 23 national offices, plus a representative office in Nepal. The Company serves 29 states, 6 union territories, and 62 countries internationally, with exports at 2.74% of total turnover.
  • Water management position: Both the Kadi and Kassar facilities operate as Zero Liquid Discharge units, with 100% of wastewater treated and reused, supported by three effluent treatment plants. A government-authorised water audit commended the effectiveness of the ZLD system. Additional measures include reuse of cooling tower blowdown water, prioritising recycled water, leak detection, and targeted conservation goals.
  • Energy and emissions initiatives: Existing rooftop renewable capacity of 3.5 MWp plus 1 MWp third-party solar at the plants, with an additional 9.9 MWp secured through a special purpose vehicle for the Kassar plant. A Bi-Fuel Kit has been installed on a high-capacity generator, multiple Variable Frequency Drives installed, aged gearboxes replaced, and cooling tower systems optimised.
  • Product quality and cost efficiency: Operations are anchored in an ISO 9001:2015-certified Quality Management System. The Kassar plant received Brand Owner certification from the Haryana Pollution Control Board for responsible management of plastic packaging waste. Adoption of cost-effective raw materials at both Kassar and Kadi contributed to improved cost efficiency. A roadmap is outlined to achieve GreenPro and GRIHA certifications.
  • Process improvements: Kassar initiatives include refined raw material compositions, trials with cost-effective formulations, reduced specific energy consumption in material preparation, pressing and drying, and structured waste segregation, recycling and reuse. Kadi initiatives include LED lighting replacement, reduced excess air in material handling, upgraded drying system designs, direct dust collection and reuse, transparent roofing sheets for natural daylight, more energy-efficient grinding media, partial substitution of conventional raw materials with recycled industrial by-products, thermal system optimisation, and more durable and cost-efficient packaging.
  • Supply chain and community positioning: A Supplier Code of Conduct defines ESG expectations for vendors, supported by awareness initiatives, BRSR Core training, GST verification and reconciliation, and monthly contractor documentation reviews for PF and ESI compliance. Supplier assessments cover workplace safety, working conditions, environmental impact, and human rights. Local community development is treated as an opportunity, with CSR interventions in education, healthcare, environmental sustainability, and socio-economic development. Employee well-being is also treated as an opportunity, with benefits cited as lower absenteeism, improved productivity, reduced attrition, and greater innovation.
  • Certifications adopted: ISO 9001, BIS Certification, ISO 45001, ISO 14001, and CII GreenPro.

3. Key Risks

  • Water conservation: Ineffective water management could lead to increased operational and regulatory costs, reputational harm, and supply chain disruption.
  • Energy and emissions management: Rising energy costs and upfront investment in efficient technologies pose a significant challenge.
  • Product quality and safety: Inferior quality could reduce sales, increase return rates, damage brand reputation, create legal liabilities and product recall costs, and cause lost business opportunities.
  • ESG in the supply chain: Inadequate ESG practices in the supply chain could cause operational disruptions, reputational harm, and adverse financial effects.
  • Compliance and ethical business practices: Non-compliance or unethical conduct could lead to legal consequences, reputational damage, operational disruption, restricted market access, and financial penalties.
  • Portfolio and transaction risk: The sale of the Company’s entire equity stake in Acer Granito Private Limited, approved by the Board on 15 January 2025 with a Share Purchase Agreement dated 04 February 2025, had an original 15-month timeline that expired on 03 May 2026. Both parties have mutually agreed to extend completion by a further 9 months, to 03 February 2027. The Company ceased to exercise significant influence or control over AGPL with effect from 01 December 2024.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Sustainability commitments: The Company states it remains committed to advancing its sustainability agenda across environmental, social, and governance pillars, enhancing resource efficiency in water and energy, increasing adoption of renewable energy and circular practices, elevating ESG performance across the value chain, strengthening employee development, fostering inclusive growth, and expanding community impact. On performance against these commitments, the Company reports continued strengthening of initiatives on energy and water efficiency, improved health and safety training, robust employee welfare practices, high product quality standards, regular training on key workplace policies, and support for CSR initiatives.
  • Renewable energy: The Company reports existing rooftop renewable capacity of 3.5 MWp and 1 MWp third-party solar at its plants, with an additional 9.9 MWp secured through a special purpose vehicle for the Kassar plant.
  • Water: Both Kadi and Kassar operate as ZLD units with 100% wastewater reuse, three ETPs installed, an Annual Canal Water Agreement secured for raw water, regular NABL-accredited testing of ETP and STP outputs, a CGWA NOC for groundwater extraction, and a fresh application submitted to the Haryana Water Resource Authority.
  • Financial reporting: The Board meeting held on Wednesday, 12 August 2026 considered and approved the unaudited standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, with a Limited Review Report issued by M/s. Singhi & Co., Statutory Auditors, reviewed by the Audit Committee. The meeting commenced at 1:45 P.M. and concluded at 2:50 P.M.
  • BRSR reporting: The Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report for FY 2025-26 was submitted on 18 July 2026 pursuant to Regulation 34(2)(f) of the SEBI Listing Regulations, presented on a standalone basis covering only the operational scope of Somany Ceramics Limited, with no assurance provider and no assurance obtained.
  • Portfolio actions: The Company completed the acquisition of a 51% equity stake in Dura Build Care Private Limited (11,04,886 equity shares aggregating to ₹10,30,19,570.64), making it a subsidiary with effect from 15 July 2025. On 15 September 2025, the Company additionally acquired approximately 5.71% (3,00,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each) of Somany Max Private Limited, raising its shareholding to approximately 85.71%.
  • CSR applicability: CSR is applicable under section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013, with turnover of ₹2,64,029.86 lakhs and net worth of ₹89,073.42 lakhs. Paid-up capital is ₹820.26 lakhs.

Broker Narrative

The narrative started with optimism around a Q4FY23 beat, softening gas prices, and high volume growth targets, but later reports shifted to caution as gas volatility and supply disruptions persisted. Early themes of volume-led growth and margin expansion remained, but growth expectations were progressively lowered from 13% tile volume growth in FY24 to mid-single-digit in FY27. The focus also moved from aggressive expansion and bathware growth to price hikes, cost absorption, and organised-player market share gains amid distress in the unorganised/Morbi segment.

Fears that came true

  • Elevated gas costs persisted and worsened, with the last report citing gas volatility and Morbi gas scarcity constraining tile volumes in Q1FY27, while early Buy calls saw negative returns of -17% to -35%.
  • Low sanitaryware utilisation/bathware weakness remained a drag, as Somany Max continued to post losses through FY26, with FY27 losses still expected even at a reduced level.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of softening gas prices driving sustained margin expansion failed, as gas price volatility and elevated costs remained key headwinds in the final report.
  • The target of 13% tile volume growth in FY24 did not hold, as the last report only guided to mid-single-digit tile volume growth for FY27 and cited early-quarter volume constraints.
  • The projected 20-25% bathware growth did not translate into profitability, as Somany Max remained loss-making through FY26 and was still expected to lose money in FY27.
