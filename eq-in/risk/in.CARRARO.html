Carraro India — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Export weakness: Export revenue declined ~14% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 (INR 1,652 Mn vs INR 1,913 Mn), which management attributes to ongoing geopolitical uncertainty, supply chain issues, and uneven demand across key overseas markets. Export market conditions “remained challenging during the quarter.”
  • Margin pressure: EBITDA margin contracted to 10.4% in Q1 FY27 from 11.0% in Q1 FY26. Management cites higher energy and raw-material costs arising from geopolitical disruption, along with labour availability constraints.
  • Subdued gears business: The gears business “remained subdued” during Q1 FY27, with only a gradual improvement expected over coming quarters.
  • Monsoon uncertainty: Domestic agricultural demand strength came “despite uncertainty surrounding the monsoon outlook.”
  • Tax dispute: Carraro received an Order-in-original dated 1 September 2026 from the Office of the Commissioner of Customs confirming a differential IGST demand of Rs. 15,24,59,511/- plus applicable interest and penalties, and an additional penalty of Rs. 5,00,00,000/- under section 114AA of the Customs Act, 1962.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Domestic momentum: Domestic revenue grew ~26% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 (INR 3,795 Mn vs INR 3,016 Mn), driven by robust demand for 4WD axles in agricultural vehicles.
  • GST-driven 4WD shift: The GST reduction narrowed the price gap, bringing post-GST 4WD tractor prices close to pre-GST 2WD levels, accelerating the shift to 4WD models. Carraro is ramping up capacity to support anticipated demand.
  • Construction equipment — TBH: Ramp-up of the new tele-boom handler axle range for a major international OEM continued with healthy traction and strong visibility; export programmes progressed as planned. New domestic projects with global and Indian OEMs are on track.
  • BHL outperformance: Carraro’s driveline sales to Indian OEMs grew ~18% year-on-year, versus ~14% growth in overall BHL sales by Indian OEMs.
  • Engineering services: The INR 175 million e-transmission project with Montra Electric is progressing well, with a prototype order received for field validation. Assignments worth ~INR 33 million under discussion at quarter-end were subsequently received (finalized July 2026). Discussions with another customer are progressing positively.
  • Gears nomination: A business nomination from a major OEM for bull gears valued at ~INR 150 million per annum effective FY28; the company is also working with two new customers/projects.
  • Higher-HP transmissions: Export market gradually recovering; start of production for a Turkish customer commenced in Q1 FY27, with SOP for an Indian customer expected by FY28.
  • Capex and capacity: Construction of a new paint-shop building commenced in Q1 FY27; a side-drive sub-assembly line and backlash machine were commissioned to enhance Portal axle capacity and 4WD differential-support capacity. During FY26, INR 417 million was deployed toward telescopic-handler axle production, high-performance agricultural transmissions, and capacity expansion.
  • Dividend: Shareholders approved a final dividend of Rs. 6.75 per equity share at the 29th AGM held on 10 September 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical and global demand risk: Export performance and input costs (energy, raw materials) remain exposed to geopolitical developments and mixed global demand trends.
  • Tax/regulatory risk: The confirmed IGST demand and penalties (totalling over Rs. 20 crore including the section 114AA penalty) represent a contingent liability; the company intends to challenge the order before CESTAT within the prescribed timeline, believes it has a strong case on merits, and expects appropriate relief from the appellate forum.
  • Execution risk on new programmes: Ramp-ups (TBH axles, higher-HP transmissions, engineering services) and capacity additions must proceed as planned to support growth expectations.
  • Monsoon/agri cycle risk: Domestic agricultural demand, a key growth driver, is sensitive to monsoon outcomes.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Guidance: Management remains focused on delivering positive growth in FY27, expecting better cost absorption, improved operating efficiencies, higher utilisation levels, and continued cost discipline to support margin improvement, working towards medium-term revenue and profitability objectives.
  • Observed performance (Q1 FY27): Total income rose 12% year-on-year to INR 5,587 Mn; revenue from operations rose 10% to INR 5,447 Mn; EBITDA (incl. other income) rose 6% to INR 579 Mn; PAT rose 8% to INR 314 Mn. Growth was driven by domestic revenue (+26%), partially offset by export decline (-14%). Margins contracted year-on-year (EBITDA 10.4% vs 11.0%; PAT 5.6% vs 5.8%), consistent with management’s stated cost pressures, while absolute profitability improved.
  • Segment performance: Agricultural Equipment INR 2,559 Mn (+15%), Construction Equipment INR 2,264 Mn (+4%), Others INR 624 Mn (+17%).
  • Programme milestones delivered: Turkish customer SOP commenced in Q1 FY27 as part of the higher-HP transmission programme; the ~INR 33 million engineering services assignments under discussion at quarter-end were subsequently received in July 2026.
