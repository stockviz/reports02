Schneider Electric Infrastructure Limited — Q1 FY27 Results and Business Update

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Commodity inflation and fixed-price legacy orders. Management stated that gross margin was impacted by commodity inflation, specifically in copper, transformer wire, aluminium and steel, where pricing is not within the company’s control. The orders executed in Q1 were largely booked in the prior year, with an average turnaround time of about six months. Orders booked before December of the prior year carried firm prices with a revision period of about six months, and where contracts lacked price variation clauses, the company was unable to return to customers for revisions. This exposure was described as largely in the Power & Grid segment. Management noted that price variation clauses cover only about 20–25% of contracts, mostly large projects with execution cycles exceeding six months to a year, and that in government tenders backed by EPC contractors, price variation cannot always be enforced. A residual risk remains if customer project execution is delayed.

Currency depreciation. The rupee depreciated by about 8% since the start of the year, affecting dollar-denominated expenses, recharges and imports. Imports are in the range of 10–15% of cost of goods sold, largely naturally hedged by exports in a similar range.

Soft revenue start and negative operating leverage. Sales grew about 5% year-on-year in Q1, described as a historically soft start after financial year close. Employee costs and other expenses rose, reflecting the normal Q1 salary increment and cost inflation of approximately 8–10%, and because sales growth was moderate, this produced negative operating leverage. EBIT was ₹32 crores, lower than the prior year, and total income margin was 1.9%.

Margin gap versus competitors. In response to an analyst question, management said it had not delayed price action and had initiated price increases when cost increases were first observed, quantifying them through factory tools and communicating them for price-list-covered standard products. Management attributed the quarterly margin decline mainly to operating leverage and raw material impact.

Product portfolio gaps in transmission. The company does not make transmission line conductors or high-voltage/extra-high-voltage transformers of 400 kV and above, so the transmission segment is not its core addressable space.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Macro and policy backdrop. Management cited India 2030 as inflecting on a new infrastructure cycle, with GDP forecast at 6.5–7% for the next four years, per capita GDP growing at least 1.5x by 2030 to close to ₹4 lakhs, and per capita electricity consumption rising to 1,800–2,000 kWh by 2030 from about 1,000 kWh or less in 2014. Four levers were highlighted: electrification (non-fossil fuel moving from 300 to 500; energy storage from about 13 GWh today to 200+ GWh by 2030), data centres and AI/digitalisation (capacity growing from 1.6 GW to about 8 GW by 2030; e-commerce users to about 450 million; digital economy contributing about 20% by 2030), urbanisation (EV penetration from about 8% to 30%; Vande Bharat trains from about 160 to 800, powered by circuit breakers made at the Kolkata plant), and Make in India (goods exports of about $1 trillion by 2030). Schemes cited as enabling these levers included RDSS, green energy corridors, the new energy policy, VGF for BESS, interstate transmission waivers, the DPDP Act, the National Data Center Policy, the India AI Mission, railway capex, high-speed rail, e-drive, PLI and the India Semiconductor Mission.

Order intake and backlog. Q1 order intake was ₹915 crores, the highest ever quarterly order booking, up 0.5% year-on-year and double digits sequentially. Backlog grew close to 33% to around ₹2,100 crores plus entering Q2. More than one-fifth of the order bank comes from emerging segments — data centres and semiconductors — while solar is described as already having emerged. Management said the opportunity pipeline is healthy with a good mix including Metals & Mining.

Quarterly wins. In semiconductors, the company supplied digitally enabled transformers for a front-end fab order in Central India, described as its largest such order from a customer in this area. In data centres, it supplied medium voltage panels, transformers and automation devices and panels. In renewables, it supplied transformers in the solar segment, distinguished by volume and efficiency. It also supplied equipment for a cement plant slurry pipeline project and an SF6-free RMU pilot at an airport in South India, described as an outdoor first of its kind at an Indian airport. Digital wins included an integrated energy management system in Energy & Chemicals and smart grid and transformer monitor solutions in Utilities, the latter an attachment to equipment supplied earlier.

Capacity expansion. The capex programme as of July 31, 2026 spans the Kolkata plant for VI and VCB (₹184 crores investment; VI capacity from 80,000 to 2,50,000 nos.; breakers from 5,000 to 45,000 nos.), Vadodara MV panels (₹107.2 crores; panels from 8,000 to 14,000 nos.), Vadodara RMU (₹156.4 crores; functions from 25,600 to 32,000), and Vadodara transformers (₹15.8 crores and ₹13.6 crores; 5,500 MVA to 7,000 MVA). Management said all programmes are on track with staggered completion timelines and ramp-up plans, with several milestones in calendar year 2027, a few completing by 2028, and no hiccups foreseen. The Kolkata plant, catering largely to exports, started operationalising early in the year and is at a ramp-up stage. Over the last three years, roughly ₹500 crores of capex was undertaken across three plants, aimed at enhancing capacity and reducing imports from other Schneider factories outside India.

Exports. Export revenue is currently 10–12% of revenue. Management declined to give a medium-term percentage target, citing variability with overall growth, but said absolute value will increase as part of the Kolkata capex strategy.

RDSS and distribution opportunity. Management noted the RDSS scheme of ₹300,000 crores and said the company supplies equipment for strengthening distribution infrastructure and participates in grid modernisation, while not undertaking underground cabling. It described itself as selective, engaging only where site visibility, project cycle clarity and executional ease are favourable, and said the addressable portion of any given TAM depends on the nature of the job undertaken by the state or circle.

ESG and recognition. The company was ranked number one among the world’s most sustainable companies for the third consecutive year. Four manufacturing sites are at 100% electricity sourced from renewable power plants including on-site solar and RECs, with zero recordable incidents. Women comprise close to 20% of workmen. About 1,900 people were trained across 16 skill development centres in Gujarat, and about 220,000 beneficiaries were reached through Anganwadi and public health centre support and community electrification programmes. CRISIL awarded a strong rating of 63 out of 100 about three months ago for 2025.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity price volatility in copper, aluminium, steel and transformer wire, with limited ability to pass through increases in fixed-price contracts and government tenders where price variation clauses cannot be enforced.
  • Execution delays by customers on projects, which management said could again impact gross margin where price variation cannot be applied.
  • Currency risk on dollar-denominated imports, recharges and expenses, partially but not fully offset by natural hedging from exports.
  • Revenue linearity and operating leverage, with Q1 historically a soft start and fixed cost increases from April creating margin pressure when sales growth is moderate.
  • Segment concentration in fixed-price exposure, described as largely Power & Grid.
  • Limited addressable scope in transmission, given the absence of high-voltage transformer and conductor offerings.
  • Dependence on government schemes and policy, including RDSS, PLI and semiconductor incentives, for demand in key segments.
  • Ramp-up risk at the Kolkata export plant, which is still at an early stage and will take time to accelerate export growth.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Order intake. Q1 FY27 order intake of ₹915 crores was the highest ever quarterly booking, with 0.5% year-on-year growth and double-digit sequential growth. Management had previously attributed tepid order growth in the prior quarter to conscious decisions amid extreme commodity volatility; in response to a question on how much of the Q1 intake was fresh versus spillover, management said it does not track this and that quarter-boundary carryover is typical and not materially influential, sometimes 15–20% and sometimes less.

Revenue. Q1 sales grew about 5% year-on-year and double digits sequentially, described as a historically soft start. Management did not provide forward-looking revenue numbers but said the underlying demand environment and opportunity pipeline are healthy, pricing actions have been initiated and will take time to fructify, and the forward-looking three quarters are seen as good. In response to a question on Q2, management said historically the company does better than Q1, without giving forward-looking statements.

Margins. Gross margin declined, which management linked to commodity inflation on orders executed from the prior year, consistent with what it had flagged on the Q4 earnings call. EBIT of ₹32 crores was lower than the prior year, and total income margin was 1.9%, attributed to gross margin impact and negative operating leverage. Management said internal cost competitiveness actions have been taken and it expects to catch up in subsequent quarters, and that the Q1 expense increase is a normal seasonal pattern that evens out through the year.

Finance cost. The year-on-year increase was attributed to a non-cash accounting adjustment — a gain in the prior-year Q1 from modification of accounting fair value — with current-year cost being normalized interest and no exceptional loan taken.

Backlog. Backlog growth of close to 33% to around ₹2,100 crores plus entering Q2 was cited as supporting the outlook.

Capacity expansion. Management confirmed all expansion programmes are on track as per plan, with additional capacity becoming available in the second half of the financial year and further milestones in calendar year 2027 and 2028, followed by ramp-up.

Mix disclosure. In response to a request for revenue mix between systems, transactional products and services, management said it would try to provide clarity from the next quarter. It noted that emerging segments come with higher scope including installation and commissioning, lengthening project execution cycles, while the transactional mix is being maintained at similar revenue growth.

Exports. Management declined to give a medium-term export percentage target, stating absolute value will increase with the Kolkata capex but the mix is difficult to estimate at this stage given growth in emerging segments.

Governance and leadership. The 16th AGM is scheduled for September 10, 2026, through video conferencing, with e-voting from September 7 to 9, 2026 and a cut-off date of September 3, 2026. Resolutions include adoption of FY2025-26 audited financial statements; re-appointment of Mr. Udai Singh as Director liable to retire by rotation; his re-appointment as MD & CEO for three years from September 15, 2026 to September 14, 2029, with remuneration not exceeding 5% of net profits, including salary of ₹14,740,008 per annum, LTIP of ₹16,208,640, target STIP of ₹6,633,000 and additional benefits of ₹129,000; ratification of M/s. Shome & Banerjee as Cost Auditors for FY2026-27 at ₹5,45,000 plus taxes; appointment of Mr. Soumya Bagchi as Whole-Time Director for three years from August 14, 2026 to August 13, 2029, with fixed salary of ₹9,497,372, LTIP of 29,953 Euro (2024), 29,947 Euro (2025) and 15,000 Euro (2026), target STIP of ₹2,849,211, and a one-time joining payment of ₹222,763; and appointment of Ms. Nirupa Chander as Non-Executive Non-Independent Director with effect from August 14, 2026. Mr. Bagchi was appointed as Additional Director effective June 30, 2026, and Ms. Chander as Additional Non-Executive Director effective August 14, 2026. Mr. Udai Singh attended 7 out of 7 Board meetings in FY2025-26 and holds 500 equity shares. No unclaimed dividends or shares were transferred to the IEPF Authority during FY2025-26; cumulative transfers for FY2011-12 were ₹843,224 and 1,460,437 shares.

FY2025-26 reported performance. Order intake grew 27.4% year-on-year to ₹3,430 crores. Sales increased 9.6% to ₹2,891 crores from ₹2,637 crores. EBITDA was ₹389 crores versus ₹407 crores, and PAT was ₹213 crores versus ₹268 crores, with ₹31.8 crores of exceptional items on a year-on-year basis and higher copper, aluminium and steel prices cited as the challenging cost environment.

Broker Narrative

The broker kept a Buy rating across both reports, but the narrative shifted from an almost unqualified positive outlook in February to a more cautious tone in May. While the first report called headwinds ‘minimal’ and focused on strong growth and government-driven demand, the last report acknowledged moderating order backlog growth, margin pressure from product mix, softer private capex, and trade uncertainties. Persisting themes include capacity expansion, energy transition, data centers, renewables, and innovation.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that order growth would pick up and build a strong backlog did not materialize: order backlog growth slowed to 2.2% YoY by the last report, even though order intake grew.
