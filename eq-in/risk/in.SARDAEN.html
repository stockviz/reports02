SARDAEN — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Operational disruptions in Q1 FY27 - Planned replacement of the 30 MW captive power plant unit at Raipur, plus improvement works across steel and ferro alloys facilities, temporarily reduced production of billets, wire rod and related products. - The Vizag captive power plant underwent a 23-day scheduled maintenance shutdown, affecting ferro alloys production. - One ferro alloys unit at Siltara was shut for 53 days for refurbishment. - The 113 MW Sikkim hydropower plant was shut from 18 June to 5 July 2026 after a transmission tower collapsed due to heavy rainfall and a landslide. The plant has since resumed full operations. - Delayed monsoon conditions affected small hydropower generation in Q1; July trends were better.

Market and pricing environment - Steel prices remained range-bound with a mild negative bias during the quarter; power sale realizations were lower year-on-year. - India’s small hydropower generation declined roughly 30% year-on-year and large hydropower about 7%, due to delayed monsoon. - Imports have again exceeded exports after two quarters, partly due to free trade agreements; the company welcomes government anti-dumping investigations. - The West Asia conflict has raised input costs and caused supply chain disruptions, including imported equipment for the mineral wool project being stuck. - Elevated oil prices could add inflationary pressure and affect demand in some end-use sectors, including steel.

Project-specific challenges - Commissioning of the 50 MW captive solar project has been delayed by right-of-way issues for the transmission line; a railway line in the region is acquiring land, with permission expected in one to two months. - The mineral wool project is not yet profitable; production is at roughly 60–65% of capacity, with ramp-up constrained by imported equipment delays. - An adverse arbitration award (including interest) related to project execution resulted in a liability of Rs 22.44 crore provided by the group. - The hydropower business is seasonal.

Risk factors disclosed - Cyclical business nature and unforeseen demand/supply shifts; project execution risks including commissioning delays and approval timelines; environmental risks from pollutant discharge and tightening emission standards; financial risks around fund availability and capital expenditure; foreign exchange risks on imported inputs and exports; raw material procurement cost and timing risks; human resources risks; safety and health risks; regulatory risks; and system/cyber security risks.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Q1 FY27 performance and balance sheet - Highest-ever quarterly EBITDA and PAT: total income Rs 1,717 crore, EBITDA Rs 762 crore, PAT Rs 478 crore (9.4% YoY growth). Net profit included a one-time net benefit of Rs 110 crore from regulatory approval of the final project cost for the 113 MW Sikkim hydropower plant. - Energy contributed nearly 70% of consolidated EBITDA. - Net debt-free on both standalone and consolidated basis, with liquidity of more than Rs 2,500 crore as of 30 June 2026. - CRISIL ‘AA’ rating with positive outlook; India Ratings affirmed IND A1 for bank loan facilities of Sarda Metals Alloys Ltd. (wholly owned subsidiary) for Rs 500 million. - FY26 was a record year: total income Rs 5,928 crore, EBITDA Rs 2,025 crore (up 44%), PAT Rs 1,109 crore (up 58%); consolidated net debt fell over 85% to Rs 215 crore; record dividend of Rs 2 per equity share recommended.

Power and energy expansion - Medium and long-term power supply agreements secured for over 380 MW of the 710 MW saleable power capacity; approximately 330 MW pertains to SKS, with tariffs between Rs 5 and Rs 6 per unit. Maximum quantity for SKS is 540 MW. - Regulatory approval process on track for brownfield expansion of SKS thermal capacity from 600 MW to 1,200 MW; management expects completion by FY31. Environmental clearance process: TOR submission expected in about 3 months, public hearing and final clearance in another 6–8 months. - Approval process progressing for three small hydro projects in Chhattisgarh (74 MW aggregate). - 66 MW hydropower project in Arunachal Pradesh: most key statutory approvals in hand, critical land acquisition complete, approach road work started; detailed engineering to commence this year. - 50 MW captive solar project commissioning expected before the end of the next quarter. - Plans to double energy generation capacity and increase renewable portfolio to 400 MW by FY30; hydropower capacity targeted above 300 MW. - India’s National Electricity Plan targets approximately 900 GW of installed capacity by FY32.

Mining expansion - Plans to quadruple mining capacity; approved coal mining capacity moving toward 7.10 MTPA as four additional blocks reach production. - Shahpur West high-grade coal mine on schedule, commissioning targeted before end of FY27; coal will serve sponge iron and ferro alloy plants. - Bartunga Hill coal mine expected to open by end of next financial year. - Regulatory approvals progressing for Gare Palma IV/5 and Senduri coal blocks. - Gare Palma IV/7 (1.8 MTPA) expansion not being considered in the immediate future, as new mines have better quality coal.

Other growth initiatives - Approximately Rs 300 crore investment in a waste heat recovery power plant at Vizag to improve energy efficiency. - Over Rs 10,000 crore growth capex planned, focused on high-return energy and mining assets; all ongoing expansions funded through internal accruals. - Raipur pellet capacity to be more than doubled to 2.0 million tonnes per annum. - Mineral wool revenue expected at Rs 90–110 crore for FY27, with full capacity in the next 3–6 months. - Board seeking enabling consent to raise up to Rs 1,000 crore through debt instruments. - Sector tailwinds from Viksit Bharat 2047, Make in India, Atmanirbhar Bharat, government push toward coal gasification, and investment across manufacturing, logistics, electronics, semiconductors, data centers and industrial infrastructure.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution risk on the large expansion pipeline, including regulatory approvals; management stated no material risk is currently seen except possible minor delays from regulatory approvals.
  • Hydropower generation depends on rainfall; Q2 is the largest quarter for hydropower.
  • Power price volatility and the impact of excess daytime solar capacity on merchant realizations; peak-hour prices have risen and peak periods extended from 5 hours to 8–10 hours.
  • Steel and ferro alloys price volatility tied to global developments, including the West Asia conflict.
  • Import competition in steel, partly from free trade agreements.
  • Input cost inflation from elevated oil prices and higher coal indices (Indian coal index for June 2026 about 21% higher than June 2025 and about 11% higher than March 2026).
  • Foreign exchange movement risk on imported inputs and exports.
  • Environmental and regulatory compliance risk as emission standards tighten.
  • Cyber security and system risks.
  • Arbitration liability of Rs 22.44 crore already provided.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements - Management expects to return to a normal operating trajectory from Q2 FY27, with temporary disruptions behind. - Steel production expected to resume in August 2026 following the turbine shutdown; the new 30 MW unit is ready for trial operations, with stable commercial operations expected by the middle of August 2026. - FY27 growth drivers cited: improvement in power price realizations, improvement in IPP capacity utilization, and commissioning of the 30 MW power plant. - IPP generation expected to exceed last year’s approximately 415 crore units with a reasonable margin; annual PLF expected to be better than the previous year. - Power realizations generally expected between Rs 5 and Rs 6 per unit for the full year, with hydropower realizations rising over time with inflation and peak demand. - Steel and ferro alloys prices expected to remain stable; ferro alloys margins may be slightly higher on softening raw material prices; steel prices could rise if the West Asia situation settles. - 50 MW solar commissioning expected before the end of the next quarter (Q3 FY27). - Shahpur West coal mine commissioning targeted before end of FY27; Bartunga Hill by end of next financial year. - SKS brownfield expansion (600 MW to 1,200 MW) expected completion by FY31; turnover expected to more than double by then. - Mineral wool FY27 revenue guidance of Rs 90–110 crore; full capacity in 3–6 months. - Medium-term targets: double EBITDA, quadruple mining capacity, double energy generation capacity, expand manufacturing footprint, maintain a robust balance sheet. - Management does not provide specific PAT or EBITDA guidance, citing market volatility.

Observed performance versus guidance - Q1 FY27 delivered highest-ever quarterly EBITDA and PAT despite the disruptions management described, with energy contributing nearly 70% of consolidated EBITDA. - The 30 MW unit replacement, which management said would temporarily impact production, is now ready for trial operations, consistent with the guidance of resumed steel production in August 2026. - The Sikkim plant, shut from 18 June to 5 July 2026, has resumed full operations; management stated no residual Q2 impact is expected, and July generation was at par with the previous year despite the 13-day closure. - Small hydropower generation was affected by delayed monsoon in Q1, but July rains were better, aligning with management’s expectation of improved Q2 momentum. - The 50 MW solar project slipped from earlier expectations due to right-of-way issues; the revised timeline is before the end of the next quarter. - The mineral wool project is not yet profitable and is at 60–65% capacity utilization, with FY27 revenue guidance of Rs 90–110 crore contingent on resolving imported equipment delays. - Management declined to quantify normalized quarterly EBITDA or FY27 profitability, stating operational performance should be better but specific numbers depend on rainfall, power demand-supply and steel price volatility.
