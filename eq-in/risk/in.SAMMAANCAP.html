Sammaan Capital Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Competitive positioning: Management describes the company as an AA+ issuer competing with 14 AAA companies and about five to six other relevant AA+ peers. It notes it was previously the eighth largest player, is currently the fifteenth largest, and aims to be in the top five over the next few years.
  • Cost structure during expansion: Absolute operating expenditure is expected to continue increasing due to expansion plans. Management expects cost-to-income to remain around 50% this year, declining only from the second half of next year, with a 30%–35% range targeted by fiscal 2029.
  • Complexity management: With the product portfolio widening from five products toward eight by end of year and 15 by end of next year, management flags the need to strengthen assurance functions, risk and compliance management, and senior management capacity.
  • Hiring timelines: Senior management hiring is described as not a quick process; mid-level and ground-level hiring has an offer-to-joining lag of about 90–120 days, with the company 60–90 days into that process.
  • Legacy issues: While management states the company does not have a burdensome legacy tech stack, it acknowledges several legacy issues that need solving.
  • Litigation backdrop: A Public Interest Litigation led to an FIR registered by the Economic Offences Wing (EOW) of Delhi Police on December 15, 2025, naming Mr. Sameer Gehlaut (erstwhile promoter of Indiabulls Housing Finance Ltd.), DLF Group, Vatika Group, Americorp Group, Chordia Group, and Reliance ADAG Group as accused. Sammaan Capital Limited was not an accused and was viewed as a victim of alleged quid pro quo arrangements. On August 11, 2026, the EOW filed a status report establishing that Sammaan Capital received back all monies in respect of loans to the five groups mentioned in the PIL. On August 18, 2026, the Hon’ble Supreme Court asked the CBI to examine the allegations in the FIR. Separately, CBI is investigating transactions among companies of promoters of Yes Bank Ltd. and those of Mr. Sameer Gehlaut; the company states there is no investigation against Sammaan Capital Limited in that matter. Over seven years, the company states it has been absolved of wrongdoing by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, National Housing Bank, SEBI, RBI, and EOW Delhi Police.
  • Management transition: Mr. Subhash Sheoratan Mundra completes his second and final term as Independent Director and ceases to be a Director with effect from close of business hours on August 17, 2026. Mr. Dinabandhu Mohapatra becomes Interim Chairman from August 18, 2026. Mr. Sachin Chaudhary is retiring as Director and Executive Director at the conclusion of the ensuing AGM, though he remains with the company as COO.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Promoter support: The company operates under new promoter Abu Dhabi-based International Holding Company PJSC (IHC), which has committed to invest USD 1 billion, described as one of the largest FDI transactions in the Indian NBFC sector, and now controls the board. IHC has two board members (Mr. Alwyn Crasta, Group CFO, and H.E. Dalia Khorshid) and functional heads on key operating committees.
  • Rating upgrades and cost of funds: Domestic ratings upgraded from AA to AA+; international ratings upgraded from B+ to BB-. Management targets domestic AAA and international BB+ by H2FY27, with a FY28 target of AAA. Stock cost of funds started at 10.5%, reached 10% through the quarter, with a target of 9.3% by end of year and 9.6% by September. Incremental cost of funds is down to about 9%, targeted at 8.5% by year-end, and 7%–7.5%–8% range over two to three years. Management states the company is saving approximately INR450 crores annualized from the 90 basis points reduction on a roughly INR50,000 crores borrowing program, with potential annualized savings of INR1,000 crores (USD 120–130 million) at 160 basis points on INR60,000 crores of borrowings by year-end.
  • Liability franchise improvements: Repricing of existing borrowings negotiated with lenders; return of private and foreign banks after a multi-year gap; company became third-largest bond issuer among AA+ peers during April–August 2026; secondary yields re-rated for domestic and international bonds; completed USD 63 million buyback of dollar bonds, now trading at a premium to comparable rated peers.
  • Asset growth: AUM increased to INR56,239 crores. Q1FY27 disbursements of approximately INR3,875 crores across five products to approximately 12,000 new customers. Disbursement targets: INR30,000 crores this year (INR10,000 crores in H1, INR20,000 crores in H2) and INR40,000–50,000 crores next year. FY29–30 target of INR50,000–92,000 crores per year.
  • Product expansion: Retail anchored expansion in H2FY27 with digital personal loans, micro-LAP loans, and rural home loans. FY28 adds gold loans, 2W/3W financing, and retail e-commerce lending. FY29–30 scales into core rural individual loans. App-first availability for all retail and MSME products.
  • Distribution expansion: Hub-and-spoke network building from base of 220 branches and 23 master service centers in 20 states, covering practically 100% of lendable opportunity. Branch roadmap: 270 in FY27, ~800 in FY28, ~1,300 in FY29, ~1,605 in FY30.
  • Asset-light model: Approximately INR7,000 crores of loans originated and sold down last year. Strategy to securitize at least 30% of originations, with 60%–70% kept as backup for potential securitization. Products range from 8.5% to 20% yield.
  • Recoveries: Gross recoveries of INR424 crores; net recoveries of INR240 crores in Q1FY27. Of security receipts sold to ARCs in FY2023, 99% recovered; of FY2024 sales, 95% recovered. Over four years, 61% collected versus industry at about 40%.
  • Digital and AI: 53 AI use cases identified for FY27–28, with 66% to be implemented within FY27. Initiatives include Salesforce CRM, AWS partnership, Darwin Box, Temenos, chatbot/co-pilot, and AI-driven cross-sell.
  • Fundraising authorization: The Board approved enabling authorization to raise funds through issuance of secured and/or unsecured redeemable non-convertible debentures/bonds on a private placement basis for an aggregate amount of up to ₹25,000 Crores.
  • Scheme of Arrangement: Equity shareholders approved the Scheme of Arrangement between Sammaan Finserve Limited and Sammaan Capital Limited with requisite majority during the NCLT-convened meeting held on September 10, 2026.
  • Inorganic growth: Management states inorganic growth will be on the table from next financial year.

3. Key Risks

  • Execution risk on ambitious targets: Management acknowledges that publicly stated five-year guidance (ROE moving from 6.8% to 18.7%, ROA from 1.8% to 8.1%, NIM from 3.5% to 8%) may be viewed as ambitious, and states confidence is based on liability franchise improvements, product launches, tech, and governance.
  • Rating trajectory dependence: The cost of funds reduction and competitive positioning depend on achieving targeted rating upgrades (AAA domestic, BB+ international), which management states is supported by balance sheet strength, operating scale, complexity management, and IHC group comfort.
  • Litigation overhang: Although the company is not an accused, the FIR and CBI examination of allegations involving the erstwhile promoter and borrower groups remain ongoing. The company states it has been absolved by multiple statutory agencies over seven years.
  • Growth versus yield and risk balance: Management emphasizes the need to balance growth with yield and risk while staying mortgage-focused in the near term, and to operate within defined risk guardrails.
  • Securitization dependence: The asset-light model relies on continued ability to securitize or sell down loans; management states at least 30% of originations will be securitized on a sustainable basis.
  • Hiring and integration risk: The company is hiring across functions, including senior management, and integrating with IHC group functions; management notes complexity management and assurance function strengthening are required.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • AUM guidance: Management states AUM increased to INR56,239 crores “in line with our guidance.”
  • Disbursement guidance: Q1FY27 disbursements of approximately INR3,875 crores were described as “bang on target” for the planned INR10,000 crores in H1FY27. Management confirms in the call (August 13, 2026) that disbursements are on track to achieve INR10,000 crores in H1.
  • Profitability: Q1FY27 profit after tax of INR243 crores is described as the “first building block” of a compounding journey, following prior-quarter adjustments, provisions, and markdowns. Management states recoveries will exceed internal projections and profit projections made to investors, with “100% confidence.”
  • Cost of funds: Guidance of 9.3% stock cost of funds by end of year and 9.6% by September; incremental cost of funds down to about 9%, targeted at 8.5% by year-end. Management states the company started at 10.5% and reached 10% through the quarter.
  • Cost-to-income: Guidance of around 50% this year, declining from second half of next year, targeting 30%–35% by fiscal 2029.
  • Rating trajectory: Domestic AA+ achieved; international BB- achieved. Targets: domestic AAA and international BB+ by H2FY27; FY28 target AAA.
  • Branch network: Guidance of approximately 240 branches at end of H1FY27, increasing to 270 branches; 800-type branch network by end of next year.
  • Recoveries: Gross recoveries of INR424 crores and net recoveries of INR240 crores in Q1FY27; management states recoveries will exceed internal projections.
  • Disbursement mix: Guidance that this year about 75% of disbursements will be mortgage-backed, 25% new products; about 90% secured, 10% unsecured this year, becoming 80% secured, 20% unsecured next year. Q1FY27 observed: 97% of disbursements were secured loans and 97% of AUM is secured.
  • Product count: Guidance that disbursement boxes will increase from five to at least eight by end of year and at least 15 by end of next year.
  • AGM and share transfer: The Twenty First AGM is scheduled for September 30, 2026, with share transfer books closed from September 26 to September 30, 2026. Mrs. Shefali Shah was re-appointed as an Independent Director for a further term of three years from November 14, 2026 to November 13, 2029.
