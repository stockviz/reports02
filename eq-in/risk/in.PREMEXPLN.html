Premier Explosives Limited – Recent Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Q1 FY27 revenue decline and execution delays. Revenue for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 fell 28% year-on-year to INR102.6 crores. Management attributed this to delays in dispatches and project execution, driven by global headwinds and supply chain disruptions across certain programs. Specifically, export order deliveries were delayed by maritime problems, and imported components for countermeasures were delayed from abroad. Defense and Space revenue declined 35% year-on-year.

Margin pressure. EBIT fell 80% year-on-year to INR4.8 crores (4.7% margin), and net profit fell 80% to INR3 crores (3% margin). Management cited elevated raw material costs amid global market conditions, product-mix effects from the “bouquet of products,” and under-absorption of fixed costs. Existing contracts must be executed before prices can be raised.

Export licensing friction. Management noted that obtaining export licenses is difficult and typically takes at least 3–4 months, and that import licenses from the importing country must be received before processing can begin. The April 2026 INR350 crores international order’s export license was still in process, with dispatches expected only in the last quarter of the year.

Land and expansion constraints. For the Andhra Pradesh expansion, land pricing was an issue; the company requested the government to rework the price, and the matter remained at that status.

Industrial explosives segment weakness. Margins in bulk explosives are described as very thin, with some places negative. Coal India business was withdrawn in prior years due to low prices; Singareni Collieries is serviced at somewhat better-than-Coal India prices. Management said FY28 is difficult to predict given raw material price dependence and more than 40 players in the field.

Accident and pending insurance claim. An accident occurred at the manufacturing facility in the previous financial year, resulting in an outstanding insurance claim receivable of Rs. 609.80 lakhs pending settlement with the insurer as at June 30, 2026.

Funding requirements. The company cites increased long-term fund requirements, present and future projects, and increasing working capital borrowing, bank guarantees, and letters of credit. Special resolutions at the AGM include increasing borrowing limits and creating security up to Rs. 1,000 Crores.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong order book. As of the call, the order book stood at INR1,393 crores, with approximately 94% (INR1,309 crores) from Defense, 3% (INR42 crores) from Explosives, and 3% (INR42 crores) from O&M Services. Management described this as providing strong revenue visibility.

Apollo Micro Systems acquisition. On July 9, 2026, Apollo Micro Systems entered a definitive share purchase agreement to acquire 41.33% of Premier Explosives promoter shares for approximately INR1,550 crores in an all-cash deal, expected to close in Q3/December 2026 subject to approvals. Management expects enhanced access to larger and more complex defense programs, stronger technology and R&D capabilities, wider customer reach, operational efficiencies through scale, and a stronger position in defense indigenization and export opportunities. Apollo’s defense electronics expertise is expected to complement Premier’s energetic materials, rocket motors, ammunition, and propulsion systems. Management expects more details by the next quarter and said the total potential of the association would be known by December.

Export license receipts and recovery. In the week before the call, several export licenses were received and material moved out of factories, with more in the pipeline. Management expected to complete the backlog of previous quarters in the current quarter and targeted INR150–200 crores of exports in the quarter.

New product and raw material development. An alternate raw material proposed for land mines and loitering munitions for drones was under testing by DRDO, ARDE, and HEMRL; clearance would open the door to Adrushy and Ulka mines, described as in demand from the Indian Army. Management expected this to take another 6 months. The company is partnering with drone manufacturers for payloads rather than developing its own drone technologies.

Capex progress at Katepally. Integration of pipelines and plant and machinery erection/installation for RDX and HMX production is almost complete, with water trials expected in September, followed by production. A 2.5-tonne planetary mixer’s imported components were delayed by maritime movement; the supplier was pressed to ship by air, with the plant expected ready by end-September for dummy trials.

RDX/HMX export demand. Demand is good and prices are presently good due to the war scenario, though licensing remains difficult and the outlook beyond 2–3 years is uncertain.

Dividend. The Board recommended a final dividend of Rs. 0.50 per equity share for 2025-26, with a record date of September 23, 2026.

Key Risks

  • Continued dependence on imported chaff material payloads and components, exposing execution to international shipping and supply conditions.
  • Export licensing timelines (minimum 3–4 months) and dependence on importing-country import licenses, which can defer revenue recognition.
  • Raw material price volatility and thin-to-negative margins in industrial explosives, with limited pricing power under existing contracts.
  • Concentration of the order book in Defense (94%), making performance sensitive to defense program timing and customer acceptance processes.
  • Pending insurance claim of Rs. 609.80 lakhs from the prior-year accident.
  • Rising working capital, borrowing, bank guarantee, and letter of credit requirements, with borrowing limits and security creation up to Rs. 1,000 Crores proposed.
  • Uncertainty around the Andhra Pradesh land parcel pricing and the timing of the Apollo transaction close (Q3/December 2026, subject to approvals).

Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 revenue guidance. Management retained a target of around INR600 crores turnover for FY27, describing it as a considerable increase over the previous year. Against this, Q1 FY27 revenue was INR102.6 crores, leaving roughly INR500 crores to be executed in the balance nine months. Management expressed confidence in capacity for flares and other products made in-house, while noting chaffs depend on imports.

EBITDA margin guidance. Management reiterated a yearly target of 15%–20% EBITDA margin. Q1 FY27 EBIT margin was 4.7%, which management attributed to product mix, raw material costs, and under-absorption, and said it expects to make up in coming quarters.

October 2025 INR430 crores order. Earlier guidance was that approximately two-thirds (about INR285 crores) would be done in FY27. On the call, management revised this, stating the entire INR430 crores would be completed in FY27. Q1 execution was only about INR21 crores, with a better number expected in Q2. Management confirmed capability to complete the order, noting chaff material payload imports were completed and flares are made in-house.

July 2023 flares order. Earlier guided for completion in Q1 FY27 with an expected INR30 crores LD reversal. On the call, the LD process was still ongoing, with clarity expected by quarter-end or October–November. A backlog of about INR75 crores was to be completed in the next 4–5 months.

Export guidance. Earlier guidance referenced around INR400 crores of export licenses pending and INR200–300 crores of exports for FY27. Management said licenses had recently been received, material was moving, and it targeted INR150–200 crores of exports in the current quarter.

Order inflow guidance. Management expected INR200–300 crores of additional order inflow in FY27, with orders generally spanning the next two years and a run rate expectation of INR1,000 crores plus.

QRSAM. Management stated no tenders had been called, so no expectation could be provided.

Other expense run rate. Management indicated other expenses of around INR9–10 crores as a sustainable run rate, versus INR11 crores in Q1 FY27 and around INR9 crores in the prior-year comparative, noting prior quarters included expected credit loss provisions and forex losses.

Singareni Collieries contract. The balance of about INR25–30 crores under the executed contract was to be executed in the financial year, with total industrial explosives revenue of close to INR80 crores indicated for FY27.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from a Buy built on an expected Q4FY26 execution surge and margin recovery to a Hold after FY26 ended with muted execution and a Q4 EBITDA loss. Execution delays, raw-material constraints and government dependence remained persistent concerns, while the order-book strength theme actually strengthened with backlog rising to ₹1,569 crore and a new export order. The growth thesis was effectively pushed out to FY27, with the broker acknowledging the FY26 miss and downgrading the call.

Fears that came true

  • Dispatch delays and pending MoD inspections materialized, as FY26 revenue declined 7.0% YoY to ₹388.3 crore, well below the revised ~₹500 crore guidance.
  • Raw-material supply-chain issues materialized, causing a Q4FY26 EBITDA loss of -0.4% and a 33.4% YoY drop in FY26 EBITDA.
  • Heavy dependence on government contracts proved costly, as execution remained tied to delayed approvals/dispatch clearances and the defence-dominated order book did not prevent the FY26 disappointment.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected Q4FY26 execution surge failed to materialize, with the last report instead describing muted FY26 execution due to delayed dispatches and a full-year revenue decline.
  • The margin-expansion optimism failed near-term, as Q4FY26 swung to an EBITDA loss despite the Q3FY26 margin improvement.
