Bajaj Consumer Care — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost pressure in Light Liquid Paraffin (LLP). The Q1 FY27 investor presentation (filed July 13, 2026) notes that the Q1 FY27 average LLP price rose sharply over Q4 FY26 — up over forty percent — reflecting a crude-linked spike. Cooling had started and further corrections were expected in July.

Elevated Refined Mustard Oil (RMO) prices. The Q1 FY27 average RMO price was broadly stable sequentially but held at elevated levels, and the usual Q1 dip linked to the harvest did not materialise.

Pricing correction in the coconut portfolio. The growth portfolio registered strong sequential growth despite a pricing correction in the coconut portfolio, which is a significant component of the overall mix. Prices were corrected at the beginning of the quarter.

Subdued Canteens & Institutions. This channel was subdued, with robust growth in CSD offset by a decline in the institutional business.

West Asia crisis disruption. The international business delivered a strong Q1 FY27 on a low base despite disruptions from the West Asia crisis, registering a comeback after a reset in leadership and distribution partners.

Dilution in director-related resolutions. At the 20th AGM held August 5, 2026, the ordinary resolution to re-appoint Mr. Kushagra Nayan Bajaj as a rotating director passed with 89.8752% in favour and 10.1248% against; the special resolution on continuation of remuneration to Mr. Sumit Malhotra passed with 89.3271% in favour and 10.6729% against. Public-Institutions recorded 26.0964% against on the re-appointment resolution and 27.5092% against on the remuneration resolution.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong consolidated topline momentum. Q1 FY27 consolidated net sales value was INR 341.4 Cr, up 28.3% YoY and 4.6% QoQ. Total operating income was INR 341.5 Cr, up 24.9% YoY.

Profitability expansion. Consolidated EBITDA was INR 84.4 Cr (24.7% of sales), up 101.2% YoY; consolidated PAT was INR 70.7 Cr (20.7% of sales), up 84.8% YoY. Contribution was INR 211.0 Cr (61.8% of sales), up 39.7% YoY.

ADHO brand momentum. The domestic business delivered strong growth in the 30s in Q1 FY27 with healthy sequential momentum. The brand delivered robust underlying volume growth (adjusted for ml-age reductions) in the early teens for the quarter. LUPs (sachets and price point packs) continued sharp growth, well ahead of the brand average, and the brand gained traction across consumer metrics.

Growth portfolio build-up. Except Amla, all sub-portfolios saw double-digit sequential growth. Coconut saw good traction in price point packs and across Traditional Trade. Banjara’s financial and team integration has been completed.

Broad-based channel performance. GT performed at parity with OT, delivering high-20s YoY growth in Q1. Urban led, driven by retail and wholesale; rural growth strengthened meaningfully, narrowing the gap with urban. OT delivered strong twenties growth. Modern Trade and E-commerce both performed well across customers and sub-channels.

International business comeback. MEA, GCC, Africa and RoW staged a strong comeback with all countries performing well. Focus markets Nepal and Bangladesh both saw sustained double-digit topline growth and EBITDA improvement. The IB business is now delivering double-digit EBITDA, with confidence in scaling it profitably and sustainably. “Aarohan Phase 3 is underway.”

Commodity relief in COPRA. Q1 FY27 average COPRA price corrected meaningfully — down over a fifth sequentially — and prices have flattened over the last four weeks.

Corporate action. The Scheme of Arrangement between the Company and Vishal Personal Care Limited (VPCL) was approved by the Hon’ble NCLT on 9 April 2026, effective 1 May 2026, with an appointed date of 15 March 2025. Financial information has been restated to give effect to the Scheme.

3. Key Risks

  • Fluctuations in earnings, competitive intensity and pricing dynamics.
  • Economic conditions affecting demand and supply, and changes in input costs (as reflected in the LLP and RMO price movements).
  • Ability to maintain key customer relationships and supply chain arrangements.
  • Market and trade priorities.
  • Political or regulatory changes in India or globally, taxation policies, climatic conditions and natural calamities.
  • Commodity and currency volatility, and litigation.
  • Other risks beyond the Company’s control.

These risk factors are set out in the safe harbour statement of the Q1 FY27 investor presentation.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from cautious early optimism about a gradual hair-oil recovery and an ~18% EBITDA margin target to a far stronger thesis of accelerated ADHO volumes, 24.4% EBITDA margin, rural recovery, and successful Banjara integration, with FY27/FY28 estimates upgraded. The persistent themes were ADHO single-brand concentration, input inflation, and demand/rural weakness; the changed elements were the margin ceiling being raised into the low-to-mid-20s, new West Asia/US-Iran geopolitical risk, and the shift from ‘rural sluggish’ to ‘rural recovery’ in the final report.

Fears that came true

  • The over-reliance on ADHO remained a live risk in the final report, and the three DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in 2024 (-32.8%, -30.9%, -14.5%) align with the stock’s vulnerability to that concentration.
  • The inflationary environment flagged in the first report materialised as ‘higher-than-expected commodity/input inflation’ in the final report, coinciding with the negative actual returns seen in the 2024 DISAPPOINTMENT spells.
  • The early rural sluggishness/near-term demand weakness was reflected in the 2024 negative actual returns and was still echoed in the final report’s ‘near-term demand slowdown’ concern, before rural recovery emerged as a late tailwind.

Optimism that failed

  • The early focus on expanding the Amla portfolio did not fully materialise: the final report said non-ADHO growth was double-digit ‘except Amla oil’, leaving Amla oil as the laggard.
