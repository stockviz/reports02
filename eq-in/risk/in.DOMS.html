Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material inflation and volatility. The sharpest pressure in Q1 FY27 came from raw material costs linked to the West Asia conflict and broader global uncertainty. Management stated that average raw material prices rose about 20% in the quarter, translating to roughly a 10–11% increase in consumption cost, against calibrated price increases of only 4–5%. Gross margin fell nearly 400 basis points year-on-year, and gross profit margin declined to 38.2% in Q1 FY27 from 42.1% in Q1 FY26. Management noted that prices moderated toward the end of June as the war situation appeared to improve, but renewed West Asia tensions pushed raw material prices up again in the weeks before the call.

Margin compression. EBITDA fell 16.4% year-on-year to ₹82.6 crore, with EBITDA margin at 12.3% versus 17.6% in Q1 FY26. PAT fell 23.4% to ₹45.3 crore, with PAT margin at 6.8% versus 10.5%. Beyond raw materials, EBITDA was affected by higher employee benefit expenses from a new tranche of ESOP grants and increased headcount to support the upcoming new facility, and by elevated other expenses from a Channel Partners Meet and the milestone event marking possession of the first building in the 50+ acre project. Management quantified the ESOP impact at about 0.2% of cost and the non-recurring event expenses at about 0.4% of Q1 margins. PAT was additionally hit by higher depreciation from capacity expansion and commissioning of new facilities.

Export softness. Export growth was flattish in Q1 FY27 due to demand softness amid persistent inflation and subdued consumer sentiment in certain EU economies, plus West Asia disruptions causing longer transit times, higher freight costs and shipment deferrals. West Asia, which contributes about 2% of overall sales, had not fully reopened. West India revenue also declined year-on-year, which management attributed to merchant exporters based in Mumbai and Gujarat whose onward export sales were affected by the same disruptions.

Capacity constraints. Management repeatedly described physical space as the binding constraint on growth. Reynolds was a brand and asset acquisition without any manufacturing facility, so Reynolds volumes must come from the same planned capacity that would otherwise make DOMS-branded products. Management said it could not simply accelerate capex to ₹400 crore because land and buildings must be developed first, and noted that new facilities take 18–24 months to reach full optimal utilization.

Diaper business seasonality and lagged cost impact. The Uniclan baby hygiene business was also affected by crude-linked inputs (superabsorbent polymer and non-synthetic fabric), but long-term purchase agreements and advance ordering delayed the impact. Management indicated the higher raw material cost would likely show up in the current quarter. Q1 is seasonally slower for diapers due to heat across India, with lower sales and weaker fixed cost absorption; the season picks up from Q2.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Domestic demand and back-to-school season. Revenue grew 19.2% year-on-year to ₹670.5 crore, driven by robust domestic demand, a strong back-to-school season, new product launches and marginally higher ASPs from calibrated price increases. All core categories grew: scholastic stationery, scholastic art material and paper stationery led by back-to-school demand, new launches and recent capacity additions; office supplies continued high positive traction led by pens and a widening portfolio.

New product acceptance. New launches across mechanical pencils, erasers, paper stationery, pens, pencil boxes, school bags, and kits and combos received strong consumer acceptance.

New-age channels. High growth traction was seen in modern trade, e-commerce and quick commerce, primarily driven by momentum in the baby hygiene segment.

Capacity expansion. The 50+ acre greenfield project is on track, with close to 300,000 square feet of operational area expected to be commissioned by the end of Q2 FY27. This will increase capacity for wooden pencils, pens and erasers. The company invested close to ₹100 crore in Q1 FY27 primarily toward capital investments. An additional 11+ acres adjacent to existing facilities has been acquired, and land and building has been added in Jammu. Management targets ₹3 of sales for every rupee of capex, with FY26 gross fixed asset turnover at 2.7x, and expects 18–24 months to reach full optimal utilization.

Reynolds brand acquisition. The asset purchase agreement implementation is progressing as planned, with team integration complete and asset movement to the Umbergaon facility done. Reynolds had sales of about ₹130–140 crore in the previous financial year under Newell. Management intends to operate Reynolds as a parallel brand focused on the office segment and professional users, in the ₹10–₹100 price segment, complementing the portfolio and elevating the group’s price architecture. Manufacturing under the Reynolds brand is aligned with commencement of the first phase of the greenfield project targeted for end of Q2 FY27. Management expects Reynolds to contribute close to 10% of overall revenues by FY29. Margins for Reynolds are expected to operate in line with the company’s margin structure of about 16% EBITDA, since DOMS will control production and costs.

Backward integration. DOMS has India’s first tip manufacturing plant, a Swiss imported plant currently being installed, with more machines ordered. Management expects to manufacture 30–40% of tip requirements in-house over the current and coming calendar year.

Export recovery potential. With new pencil capacity coming up, exports are expected to grow, since 25–30% of wood pencil sales come through export. Management guided full-year exports at around 13–15% of overall sales.

Industry pricing discipline. After DOMS took its calibrated price increase, the industry followed with similar steps, with no participant doing anything very high or very low.

Key Risks

  • Continued raw material price volatility tied to the West Asia conflict and broader global uncertainty, with limited visibility on margin guidance.
  • The gap between raw material cost increases and price increases: management estimated a remaining gap of about 4–5% at current spot prices that would need to be recovered to return to the targeted 16–17% margin range.
  • Potential margin drag from Reynolds cost apportionment until desired scale is achieved, which management said may exert some temporary minor impact on margins.
  • Capacity constraints limiting the pace of growth and the ability to capitalize on Reynolds demand.
  • Export exposure to EU demand softness, West Asia disruptions, freight costs and shipment deferrals.
  • Lagged raw material cost impact in the diaper business in the current quarter.
  • Depreciation increases from capacity expansion and commissioning of new facilities affecting PAT.
  • Rising ESOP costs: about ₹10 crore for FY27 across both tranches, with management indicating the number will rise as further grants are made under the five-year ESOP policy.
  • Execution risk on the greenfield project, which management noted had already seen a slight delay before progressing toward commercialization of the first phase.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Revenue guidance. Management guided 18–20% consolidated sales growth for FY27, reinforced by positive domestic demand. Q1 FY27 revenue growth of 19.2% was described as in line with the annual guided range. Management declined to guide FY28 revenue growth, saying it was too early, but indicated the growth trajectory momentum should remain similar. Management clarified that Reynolds would not significantly add to FY27 revenue projections because capacity is the constraint and Reynolds volumes represent substitution from DOMS-branded products rather than incremental volume, though ASPs may improve somewhat.

Margin guidance. Management maintained a targeted EBITDA margin range of about 16–17%. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 12.3% was below this range. Management characterized the moderation as a temporary blip rather than structural deterioration, and said that once raw material prices stabilize, the company will take required decisions to return to the guided range. Management stated it was not looking at further price hikes in the current quarter, preferring to wait for volatility to settle. On whether Q1 was the bottom for EBITDA margin, management said it believed so and expected things to improve going forward. For FY28, management indicated that at current spot raw material prices, an additional 4–5% price increase would be needed to reach the targeted 16%-odd margin, and that FY26 gross and EBITDA margins should be viewed as the sweet spot to maintain going forward.

Capex and capacity guidance. Management guided commissioning of close to 300,000 square feet of operational area by the end of Q2 FY27, with a large part of H2 FY27 growth coming from these facilities. Management reiterated the historical target of ₹3 of sales per rupee of capex, noting FY26 achievement was close to 2.7x, and 18–24 months to full optimal utilization.

Reynolds guidance. Management guided Reynolds to contribute close to 10% of overall revenues by FY29, and to operate at margins in line with the company’s structure of about 16% EBITDA. Management noted Reynolds had done sales of about ₹130–140 crore in the previous financial year under Newell, and that full sales potential would be unlocked over time as integration progresses and manufacturing commences.

Export guidance. Management guided full-year exports at around 13–15% of overall sales, with better momentum expected in the near term from new pencil capacity and differentiated products. Exports were close to 12% of total revenues at the time of the call.

Observed performance versus guidance. Q1 FY27 revenue growth of 19.2% was within the 18–20% guided range. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 12.3% was well below the 16–17% targeted range, which management attributed to transitory raw material inflation, ESOP costs and non-recurring event expenses. PAT margin of 6.8% was below FY26’s 10.3%. Management stated that visibility on margin guidance remained limited owing to abrupt fluctuation in raw material prices, while revenue guidance was reaffirmed.

Broker Narrative

The first report framed DOMS as a Buy on product differentiation, distribution scale, FILA-backed export growth, and falling polypropylene prices. The last report kept the Buy rating and reiterated 18–20% FY27 revenue guidance, but the narrative shifted to defending margin compression, flat exports, and raw-material inflation, calling Q1FY27 a margin trough. Capacity expansion and operating leverage remained persistent themes, while early optimism on raw-material costs and exports reversed completely.

Fears that came true

  • The flagged raw-material/commodity risk materialized: raw-material inflation lifted the consumption basket cost ~10% YoY and gross margin contracted 398 bps, followed by widespread DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • The dependence on exports for growth proved fragile: export revenue stayed broadly flat on softer European demand, elevated freight/logistics disruptions and West Asia disruption, while most later calls missed targets.
  • Execution/margin risk materialized: EBITDA margin contracted 525 bps and PAT margin 355 bps due to higher headcount, ESOP, one-off channel expenses and depreciation, associated with negative actual returns from May 2025 onward.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of raw-material tailwinds, especially lower polypropylene prices, failed as input inflation instead compressed margins.
  • The FILA-backed export growth/export opportunities thesis failed to show up in actuals, with export revenue broadly flat in the last report.
