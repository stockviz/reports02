JK Tyre & Industries — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Raw material inflation: The continuing West Asia crisis drove a steep increase in raw material prices of approximately 20% versus Q4 FY26, which management states impacted gross and operating margins. Average raw material costs in Q1 were up ~20% on a sequential basis. Approximately 70% of tyre industry raw materials are petro-based, making the business highly vulnerable to oil price movement.
  • Margin compression: Consolidated EBITDA fell to Rs. 268 crore in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 424 crore in Q1 FY26, with EBITDA margins at 6.8% versus 10.9% a year earlier. Cash profits stood at Rs. 169 crore versus Rs. 309 crore in the corresponding quarter, and consolidated EPS was Rs. 1.55 per share against Rs. 6.03 per share in Q1 FY26.
  • Mexico operations (JK Tornel): Operations were impacted by ongoing geopolitical disruptions that constrained availability of key inputs, including bead wire sourced from China, alongside shipping disruptions and container unavailability. Productivity enhancement negotiations with workers resulted in industrial relations issues, described by management as a slowdown rather than a strike; these have since been resolved. The Mexico business was reported to have declined sharply, with one participant citing an approximately 82% decline.
  • Natural rubber prices in Mexico: Prices rose by almost 18%, mirroring the trend in India.
  • Pricing lag in OEM channel: Price increases with OEMs typically occur with a lag, so increases did not fully reflect in Q1 and are expected to flow through in subsequent quarters.
  • Debt increase: Consolidated net debt as on 30.06.26 stood at Rs. 4,945 crore, up Rs. 500 crore QoQ, driven by capex-led long-term loans and additional working capital requirements due to higher raw material prices and volumes. Net debt to equity rose to 0.81x from 0.73x, and net debt to EBITDA to 2.56x from 2.13x as on 31.03.26.
  • Regulatory matter: A letter was received from the Collector Stamps, Jaipur under the Rajasthan Stamp Act, 1998; the Company subsequently received communication on 22nd July 2026 waiving interest of Rs. 2 crore. Exceptional items for the quarter included a stamp duty expense of Rs. 2.75 crore pursuant to the Scheme of Amalgamation of Cavendish Industries Ltd.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Auto industry momentum: The Indian auto industry posted another consecutive quarter of record performance across all segments, with overall retail sales growth of over 15% (Source: FADA), supported by GST reforms, rising infrastructure activity, higher freight movement, new model launches, easy financing, and lower interest rates. PV grew 23%, CVs 14%, 2/3W 14%, and the farm segment 22% YoY.
  • Domestic volume growth: Domestic sales volumes grew 25% YoY across both replacement and OE markets, with OEM volumes up 42%. TBR volumes grew 18% in OE and 15% in replacement; passenger line volumes grew 10% led by OEM; farm volumes grew 31% (35% OEM, 25% replacement); 2/3W OE volumes grew 17% while replacement grew 48%.
  • Product mix and premiumisation: Increasing contribution from higher-value added products; in PCR, tyres of 16-inch and above make up 35% of total PCR sales volumes.
  • Pricing actions: A ~5% price increase was taken in Q1, with cumulative increases of about 11% taken in the replacement market. Management expects further increases in the range of 8–9%. Net sales realisation rose ~5% sequentially on a standalone basis.
  • Commodity moderation: Recent moderation in commodity and crude oil prices is expected to lower input costs and improve profitability. Natural rubber prices are softening in India, with management hopeful of a further fall by the next quarter.
  • Capacity expansion: The previously announced Rs. 4,980 crore expansion for PCR and TBR at the Chennai plant will add about 24% capacity over four years. Additions by the next financial year will be mainly TBR tyres plus passenger car capacity balancing at the Banmore plant, increasing roughly 7% of total installed capacity. Full ramp-up expected by Q3 of the current financial year.
  • Mobility and EV: The Mobility business continues high double-digit growth, supported by a PAN India ecosystem of 100+ truck wheels and 700+ pitstops. EV volumes grew double digits over the previous quarter, with a full-stack EV tyre offering. EV tyres wear 5–10% faster than ICE tyres due to higher torque.
  • Mexico outlook: JK Tornel production has returned to normal levels. The USMCA agreement has been renewed for 10 years, which management expects to be favourable for Mexico’s duty structure. Mexico’s economy is expected to grow more than 1.5% in 2026, outperforming the IMF’s 1.2% projection.
  • Exports and trade: Recently signed FTAs with several nations including the EU and UK are expected to strengthen India’s exports. Export volumes from India remained steady despite geopolitical uncertainties and were up 2% sequentially over Q4.
  • Recognition: Vikrant and Chennai Tyre Plants received the International Safety Award from the British Safety Council; CII recognised several plants for sustainability, environment, health and safety practices. The Global Tech & Innovation Centre, RPSCOE, signed an MoU with the National Institute of Engineering, Mysuru.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued exposure to petro-based raw material prices and oil price movements, with ~70% of raw materials petro-based.
  • Geopolitical disruptions affecting input availability, particularly for the Mexico operations and bead wire sourced from China.
  • Industrial relations risk at JK Tornel, Mexico, following the productivity enhancement negotiations.
  • Execution risk on the Rs. 4,980 crore capacity expansion and its ramp-up timeline.
  • Leverage risk, with net debt to EBITDA at 2.56x and net debt to equity at 0.81x as on 30.06.26, and a planned further debt increase of Rs. 500–700 crore in the financial year.
  • Dependence of margin recovery on raw material price stabilisation and the ability to pass through price increases.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Margins: Management expects FY27 operating margins in the range of about 10–11%, noting this may not match FY26 levels because of the Q1 impact. It expects progressive margin improvement from at least H2 FY27, contingent on raw material prices stabilising, with an anticipated 8–10% increase in RM cost in the next quarter due to higher-cost inventory. Observed Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 6.8%, against 10.9% in Q1 FY26.
  • Revenue growth: Management expects good double-digit growth for FY27, similar to the ~10–11% achieved in FY26, on the back of price increases and demand-led volume growth. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue was Rs. 3,956 crore, up 2% YoY from Rs. 3,891 crore.
  • Price increases: Management stated that increases taken to date plus contemplated increases should cover the entire increase in raw material prices except for a minor 1–2%, with the residual offset through cost reduction, operating leverage, product mix, and premiumisation. Observed: ~5% taken in Q1, ~11% cumulative in the replacement market.
  • Mexico: Management assured that the remaining three quarters would show better results than Q1, with production back to normal levels and top line expected to be broadly similar to earlier levels. Observed Q1 Mexico performance was significantly impacted.
  • Debt: Management expects an overall debt increase of Rs. 500–700 crore in FY27, alongside annual debt repayment, to fund expansion and working capital. Observed net debt rose Rs. 500 crore QoQ to Rs. 4,945 crore.
  • Capacity utilisation: Management reported ~95% utilisation in India and ~80% on a consolidated basis, with TBR and 2/3W nearly fully utilised, PCR at 95%, and non-truck bias including LCV and farm at 95% plus.
  • AGM outcomes: All six resolutions at the 73rd Annual General Meeting held on 6th August 2026 were duly passed with the requisite majority, including declaration of a dividend of Rs. 4.00 per equity share of Rs. 2 each for FY ended 31st March 2026, re-appointment of Smt. Sunanda Singhania and Dr. Raghupati Singhania, and payment of remuneration to Shri Bharat Hari Singhania.

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from a Not Rated, risk-focused view on seasonality, Mexico, raw-material under-recoveries, and debt to a Buy call centered on pricing power, premiumization, deleveraging, and an upcoming CV upcycle. Premiumization/margin expansion and deleveraging persisted as themes; monsoon/Mexico concerns were replaced by EPR costs and a near-term CV demand correction before the expected FY26 upcycle.

Fears that came true

  • Raw-material under-recoveries remained a live issue and intensified into the last report’s warning of rising RM costs and all-time-high domestic Indian rubber prices.
  • Material-cost constraints also persisted, as the last report continued to cite input-cost and EPR pressures rather than relief from alternative materials or stabilizing costs.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that raw-material costs would stabilize and drive margin expansion/cost savings failed to materialize, with the last report instead highlighting RM and EPR cost pressures.
  • The optimistic broad demand/export outlook was undercut by the near-term flattish CV demand correction, and the Buy call’s +77.8% prediction turned into a -8.7% disappointment.
