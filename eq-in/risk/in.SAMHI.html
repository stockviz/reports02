SAMHI Hotels Ltd. — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical volatility: SAMHI operates in an increasingly volatile geo-political environment. Specific pressure points cited include the West Asia crisis and the IndiGo crisis, which disrupted operations during the period under review.
  • International travel softness: Geopolitical situations and limitations of Gulf carriers have affected international passenger traffic and inbound travel, weighing on demand from that segment.
  • GST regime change: A shift from 12% GST with input tax credit to 5% GST without input tax credit has caused margin compression.
  • Temporary disruption to international travel: The Middle East conflict caused temporary geopolitical headwinds and some disruption to international travel.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Committed room pipeline: A committed pipeline of 1,660 rooms across seven new hotels in the upper upscale and upscale segments.
  • Rebranding and renovation: About 450 rooms are being rebranded to upscale, with renovation initiatives underway.
  • Upscale mix shift: The share of upscale inventory is expected to rise from ~41% today to ~60% by FY2030, which is expected to support operating EBITDA margins of approximately 40%.
  • Domestic demand resilience: Domestic demand has been resilient, supported by sustained corporate travel and MICE activity.
  • RARE India platform: Expansion of the RARE India experiential leisure platform, following the acquisition of Itmenaan Lodges Private Limited for INR 12 crores.
  • Marriott distribution partnership: A proposed Marriott distribution partnership is noted as a growth lever.
  • Capital raise: Approval to raise funds up to INR 750 crores through equity shares or convertible securities, alongside an increase in authorized share capital to INR 29 crores, supporting a planned strong capital expenditure cycle to add significant room inventory in the next few years.
  • Cash flow visibility: Forecast cumulative cash flow of more than Rs. 3,000 crores over FY2027–FY2031, with visibility of strong internal accruals and attractive acquisition and growth opportunities.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued exposure to an increasingly volatile geo-political environment, with the Middle East conflict and Gulf carrier limitations posing risks to international passenger traffic and inbound travel.
  • Margin pressure from the GST transition to 5% without input tax credit.
  • Execution risk around the planned capital expenditure cycle, hotel additions, rebranding, and renovation initiatives.
  • Dependence of the fund infusion on prevailing market conditions, with timing and terms to be determined in consultation with the committee of the Board of Directors.
  • Leverage position: Net Debt to EBITDA of ~3.2x (~2.4x on Operating Assets), with management noting a comfortable level of ~3.0x.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Guidance provided: - Long-term revenue growth forecast of 9%–11%. - Upscale share of revenue expected to move from 40%–41% today to approximately 60% by FY2030. - Cumulative cash flow projected at more than Rs. 3,000 crores over FY2027–FY2031. - Operating EBITDA margins of approximately 40% expected as the upscale inventory share increases. - Net debt to EBITDA expected to remain at a comfortable ~3.0x, with interest outflows expected to remain stable to support long-term growth initiatives. - Timing and terms of the fund infusion depend on prevailing market conditions and will be in consultation with the committee of the Board of Directors.

Observed performance (quarter ended 30 June 2026): - Same-store RevPAR grew 9.6% year-on-year to approximately Rs. 5,220 (Rs. 5,219). - Portfolio occupancy stood at 79.3%. - Total income was Rs. 308.3 crores (Rs. 3,083 Mn). - Consolidated EBITDA was Rs. 1,013 Mn. - PAT stood at Rs. 249 Mn. - Net debt as of June 30, 2026 was approximately Rs. 1,490 crores. - Effective interest rate stands at 7.8%. - Net Debt to EBITDA of ~3.2x (~2.4x on Operating Assets).

Other developments: - The 16th AGM was held on Monday, 31st August 2026, through Video Conferencing, where five resolutions were approved by requisite majority: adoption of financial statements, re-appointment of director Mr. Manav Thadani, increase in authorized share capital and alteration of the Memorandum of Association, raising capital up to INR 750 crores through equity shares or convertible securities, and approval of remuneration for Non-Executive Independent Directors. - The Board meeting on August 3, 2026 approved the unaudited financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. - The Integrated Annual Report for FY2025-26 was made available via web-link, with the AGM notice and report sent electronically to members whose e-mail IDs were registered as on the cut-off date of 31st July 2026.

Broker Narrative

Initially, brokers framed headwinds as temporary 1H disruptions from heatwaves, elections, and festival timing, while expecting broad-based demand from MICE, weddings, concerts, and foreign tourist recovery. Over time, the narrative shifted toward structural margin pressure from lost input tax credit and a sustained shortfall in premium international travelers, compounded by West Asia conflict disruptions. Domestic demand resilience remained a persistent bright spot throughout, and the asset-light RARE India platform emerged as a new growth pillar by the latest report.

Fears that came true

  • Temporary 1H headwinds from heatwaves, elections, and an offbeat October due to early Diwali materialized and weighed on near-term operations, correlating with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in early 2025 reports.

Optimism that failed

  • Recovery in foreign tourist arrivals failed, as same-store ARR growth was constrained by a lower share of higher-paying international travelers and international business normalization was delayed.
  • MICE activities and high-scale concerts lost momentum, with event cancellations and postponements from the West Asia crisis dragging F&B revenue growth to just 3.7%.
