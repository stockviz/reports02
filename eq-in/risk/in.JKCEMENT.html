JKCEMENT: Recent Corporate Announcements — Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Cost pressure eroding profitability. The Q1 FY27 investor presentation (July 18, 2026) describes the quarter as showing “robust volume growth, however reduced profitability due to cost pressure.” Pet coke prices (6.5% Sulphur USA CFR) increased, driving fuel cost up 3% QoQ and 1% YoY on a grey standalone basis, with the impact only partly offset by mix change. Logistics cost rose 3% QoQ and 2% YoY, attributed to higher diesel prices amid a geopolitical situation. Other expenses rose 18% QoQ, mainly on higher maintenance and packaging cost.

Margin compression. Standalone EBITDA margin fell to 16.9% in Q1 FY27 from 18.5% in Q4 FY26 and 21.9% in Q1 FY26. Consolidated margin fell to 16.3% from 17.8% QoQ and 21.2% YoY. Standalone EBITDA per tonne declined to ₹982 from ₹1,012 QoQ and ₹1,229 YoY. Standalone profit after tax fell 16% QoQ and 12% YoY to ₹291 crore; consolidated PAT fell 17% QoQ and 15% YoY to ₹275 crore.

Regulatory penalty. On August 14, 2026, the company disclosed that it had voluntarily filed an application for adjudication under Section 454 of the Companies Act, 2013, admitting delays in filing Form BEN-2 in respect of Significant Beneficial Owner. The Registrar of Companies, ROC Uttar Pradesh I, imposed penalties under Section 90(11) for violation of Section 90(4) read with Rule 4 of the Companies (Significant Beneficial Owners) Rules, 2018: ₹5,00,000 on the company; ₹1,00,000 each on Dr. Raghavpat Singhania (MD), Mr. Madhavkrishna Singhania (Joint MD & CEO), Mr. Ajay Kumar Saraogi (Dy. MD & CFO), and Mr. Shambhu Singh (previous Company Secretary); and ₹64,600 on Ms. Bhumika Sood (present Company Secretary). The order was received on August 13, 2026 at 7.40 P.M. The company stated the order does not have a major financial impact.

Ongoing CCI litigation. Note 3(i) to the Q1 FY27 results records a CCI order dated August 31, 2016 imposing a penalty of ₹128.54 crore; the NCLAT upheld the order on July 25, 2018; a revised demand notice dated August 7, 2018 was issued for ₹154.92 crore (penalty plus ₹26.38 crore interest). The company’s appeal before the Supreme Court was admitted on October 5, 2018, with the NCLAT interim stay continuing. Note 3(ii) records a separate CCI penalty of ₹9.28 crore imposed on January 19, 2017, where NCLAT has stayed the operation of the CCI order and the matter remains pending. No provision has been considered in the books in either matter, backed by legal opinion. The statutory auditors included an Emphasis of Matter on the CCI matter in both standalone and consolidated review reports.

Subsidiary losses. The consolidated review report notes that nine subsidiaries reflected total revenues of ₹296.06 crore and total net loss after tax of ₹27.18 crore for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. Non-controlling interest was negative ₹2.85 crore for the quarter.

Governance-related dissent. At the 32nd AGM (July 17, 2026), the ordinary resolution to re-appoint Mrs. Sushila Devi Singhania as a director retiring by rotation (Resolution 3) passed with 76.34% in favour and 23.66% against; public institutions voted 44.73% in favour and 55.27% against. The special resolution for continuation of her directorship (Resolution 5) passed with 76.40% in favour and 23.60% against, with public institutions at 44.87% in favour and 55.13% against.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Sector demand drivers. The Q1 FY27 presentation cites GDP growth of 6.6% projected for FY27, India among the fastest-growing economies, infrastructure-led growth and strong domestic demand. It cites installed sector capacity of ~730 MTPA, demand growth of 7–8% annually, capacity expansion across India and healthy utilisation. Budget 2026-27 public infrastructure capital expenditure is cited at ₹12.2 lakh crore, with infrastructure capex for FY27 estimated 17.7% higher than the last fiscal. Road & Highways allocation is cited at ₹3.10 lakh crore, with ₹1.22 lakh crore for road infrastructure and ₹1.87 lakh crore to NHAI including pending Bharatmala projects. PMAY-G FY27 target is 20 million additional rural houses and PMAY-U FY27 target is 10 million additional urban houses. Average annual industrial capex is cited at around ₹7.1 lakh crore with PLI and emerging sectors as major contributors.

Volume growth. Q1 FY27 standalone grey cement volume was 5.96 MT, up 18%; white cement volume was 0.54 MT, up 29%. The presentation attributes double-digit volume growth to ramp-up of the 6 MTPA expansion commissioned in H2 FY26, with white business volumes also growing due to impact on imports from UAE. Standalone revenue from operations rose 21% YoY to ₹3,866 crore; consolidated revenue from operations rose 20% YoY to ₹4,032 crore.

Operating performance. Q1 FY27 grey standalone capacity utilisation was 75% for cement and 76% for clinker. Trade mix was 69% versus 68% in Q4 FY26; net sales realisation was ₹5,065/tonne versus ₹4,841/tonne in Q4 FY26; blended cement was 67% versus 65% in Q4 FY26; premium products were 18% of trade sales. Dispatch mix was 5% rail and 95% road. Lead distance was flat QoQ and 4% lower YoY, the latter on account of commissioning of the Bihar grinding unit.

Capacity expansion pipeline. The company states it is progressing towards 50 MTPA by FY30. Current grey cement capacity is 32.3 MTPA (including 0.42 MTPA in a subsidiary); white cement and wall putty capacity is 3.1 MTPA (including 0.60 MTPA in a subsidiary); green power capacity is 342.3 MW; captive solar and wind capacity is 223.0 MW; waste heat recovery is 119.3 MWh; and the network spans ~91,000 dealers and retailers.

Commissioned capacity. On August 12, 2026, the company commissioned a 0.60 MnTPA white cement-based wall putty manufacturing plant at Village Kajiyawas, Tehsil Nathdwara, Dist. Rajsamand, Rajasthan. Following this, overall white cement and wall putty production capacity stands at 3.65 MnTPA including the subsidiary’s capacity.

Projects in progress (all scheduled for H1 FY28 unless stated): - 7 MTPA grey cement expansion in North India: 4 MTPA clinker and 3 MTPA cement; project cost ₹3,630 crore; expenditure YTD Jun-26 ₹1,162 crore; ordering and engineering completed; civil, mechanical and erection work progressing as per schedule. - 2 MTPA split grinding unit: project cost ₹565 crore; expenditure YTD Jun-26 ₹181 crore; ordering and engineering completed; civil and mechanical work progressing. - 2 MTPA split grinding unit: project cost ₹610 crore; expenditure YTD Jun-26 ₹85 crore; 100% land acquired; EC/CTE approvals expected by Sep’26; major plant and equipment ordering completed. - Wall putty capacity expansion in North India: 6 lakh MT plant; project cost ₹195 crore; expenditure YTD Jun-26 ₹140 crore; construction nearing completion; commissioning expected in the month of the presentation (Q2 FY27 scheduled).

Raw material resources. The presentation lists limestone and coal reserves: Dommarnandyala Block-3, Andhra Pradesh (~550 MT); Parewar Sn-III, Rajasthan (~200 MT); Kishanpura Limestone Block, Rajasthan (~100 MT); Itauri-Jharkua, Madhya Pradesh (~50 MT); Mahan Coal Block, Madhya Pradesh (~70 MT); West of Shahdol Coal Block, Madhya Pradesh (~26 MT). Mahan Coal block development work is to start during the financial year.

Diversification. The presentation notes 17 RMC plants under operation, paints business net revenue above ₹125 crore, and the VAP business growing significantly.

ESG recognition. The presentation cites an ESG score in the S&P Global DJSI-CSA assessment, membership in the S&P Global DJSI Yearbook 2026, an A CDP supplier engagement assessment score, an A- CDP water security score, a B CDP climate change score, and an ESG score of 67 by NSE Sustainability Ratings Analytics, with JK Cement emerging as the highest rated cement company.

CSR spend. CSR spend in YTD June 26 was ₹11.7 crore, comprising ₹5.09 crore on education, ₹5.34 crore on health, and ₹1.27 crore on community development.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and fuel price risk: Pet coke and diesel price movements directly affect fuel and logistics costs, as evidenced by the Q1 FY27 cost increases.
  • Litigation risk: The CCI matters remain pending, with no provision recognised; the auditors flagged the uncertainty as an Emphasis of Matter. The Supreme Court appeal and the NCLAT matter remain unresolved.
  • Regulatory compliance risk: The BEN-2 filing delay resulted in penalties on the company and named officers, including the MD, Joint MD & CEO, Dy. MD & CFO, and current and previous Company Secretaries.
  • Execution risk on expansion: The 7 MTPA North India expansion and the two 2 MTPA split grinding units are scheduled for H1 FY28; the third unit’s EC/CTE approvals are expected only by Sep’26.
  • Subsidiary performance risk: Nine subsidiaries reported a net loss after tax of ₹27.18 crore for the quarter ended June 30, 2026.
  • Shareholder dissent risk: Significant public institutional votes against the re-appointment and continuation of directorship of Mrs. Sushila Devi Singhania.
  • Debt-related covenants: The company maintains compliance with DSCR of at least 1.10 times, Total Outside Liability/Total Net Worth of maximum 4.00 times, and security assets cover of at least 1.20 times as at June 30, 2026, per the auditor’s report on debenture covenants. Standalone debt-equity ratio was 0.91 times as at June 30, 2026 versus 0.87 times as at March 31, 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Capacity guidance. The presentation states the company is “progressing steadily towards 50 MTPA by FY30.” Observed: current grey cement capacity is 32.3 MTPA, with 7 MTPA of grey expansion plus two 2 MTPA split grinding units under construction, all scheduled for H1 FY28. The wall putty plant at Nathdwara was commissioned on August 12, 2026, taking white cement and wall putty capacity to 3.65 MnTPA including the subsidiary.

Project commissioning guidance. The wall putty expansion in North India was guided for Q2 FY27 commissioning with construction nearing completion; the Nathdwara 0.60 MnTPA plant was commissioned on August 12, 2026. The 7 MTPA grey expansion and the two 2 MTPA split grinding units are guided for H1 FY28; the presentation notes civil, mechanical and erection work progressing as per schedule for the first, and civil and mechanical work progressing for the second. The third unit’s EC/CTE approvals are expected by Sep’26.

Cost focus. The presentation states “razor sharp focus on cost.” Observed: fuel cost rose 3% QoQ and 1% YoY; logistics cost rose 3% QoQ and 2% YoY; employee cost was flat QoQ and 3% lower YoY due to higher volumes; other expenses rose 18% QoQ on higher maintenance and packaging cost. Lead distance was flat QoQ and 4% lower YoY.

Volume and revenue performance. Observed: standalone grey cement volume up 18% YoY, white cement volume up 29% YoY, standalone revenue from operations up 21% YoY, consolidated revenue from operations up 20% YoY. However, standalone EBITDA declined 5% YoY and consolidated EBITDA declined 6% YoY, with margins contracting YoY and QoQ.

Financial covenants. The auditor’s report on debenture covenants confirms compliance with DSCR of at least 1.10 times, TOL/TNW of maximum 4.00 times, and security assets cover of at least 1.20 times as at June 30, 2026. The company continues to maintain more than 100% asset cover for secured NCDs, and the long-term rating for debt instruments is maintained by CARE Ratings as CARE AA+.

Auditor opinion. The statutory auditors issued an unmodified opinion on the Q1 FY27 standalone and consolidated financial results, with an Emphasis of Matter on the CCI matter. The AGM confirmed the Auditors’ Report for FY2025-26 does not contain any qualifications, modified opinion or adverse remarks.

Broker Narrative

Early broker reports (2023) emphasized competitive intensity and rich valuations as headwinds against a backdrop of robust demand and sharply lower fuel costs, while the latest report (2026) shifted focus to fuel cost inflation, operating deleverage, and UAE disruptions as headwinds, with grey cement volume growth, improved realisations, and capacity ramp-ups as tailwinds. The theme of fuel costs persisted but inverted from a tailwind to a headwind, while demand and pricing dynamics remained central but with altered direction. Valuation concerns gave way to execution and cost-management risks over time.

Fears that came true

  • Elevated competitive intensity and aggressive capacity expansion by Adani Cement stoked a race for market share, pressuring realisations and contributing to multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes from mid-2025 onward.
  • Fuel cost savings were transient, as fuel cost inflation resurfaced by Q2FY27 (~INR100/t QoQ increase), keeping margins under pressure and aligning with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in H2FY26/FY27.
  • Weak pricing power persisted, with a likely realisation drop materialising in phases and limiting EBITDA/te gains, correlating with negative actual returns from late 2025.
  • UAE operations were impacted during H1FY27 with white cement volumes declining ~50% due to logistical disruptions, directly weighing on earnings and triggering DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.

Optimism that failed

  • Sharply lower global fuel costs generating Rs250-350/te savings failed to sustain, as fuel costs inflated again by Q2FY27, eroding the expected margin tailwind.
  • Strong underlying demand and operating leverage from high volumes did not translate into sustained EBITDA growth, as Q1FY27 EBITDA declined YoY despite 18% grey cement volume growth.
  • High volumes supporting operating leverage were offset by rising raw material costs (up 20% YoY), maintenance costs (INR50-60 crore), and a 24% YoY jump in other expenses in Q1FY27.
  • Paints business achieving EBITDA breakeven and FY27 revenue guidance of INR500-550 crore failed to offset broader margin pressures, with multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes despite this tailwind.
