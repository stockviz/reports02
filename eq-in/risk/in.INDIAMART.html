IndiaMART — Q1 FY2027 Results, AGM, and Related Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Paying supplier base has stalled. Paying suppliers stood at 218K at the end of Q1 FY2027, flat year-on-year (0%) and down 1% quarter-on-quarter from 220K in Q4 FY2026. The presentation discloses that 1,852 paying suppliers declined during the quarter. The five-year paying supplier CAGR is 8%, but the three-year CAGR is only 3%, indicating a marked slowdown in net additions.

Enquiry volumes are declining. Unique business enquiries were 26 million in Q1 FY2027, down 11% year-on-year and down from 27 million in Q4 FY2026. Business enquiries delivered fell 13% year-on-year to 85 million, and the multi-year trend shows a decline from 610 million in FY21 to 370 million in FY26 (negative 10% five-year CAGR, negative 8% three-year CAGR).

Active buyer base contracted. Active buyers over the last 12 months were 41 million, down 5% year-on-year and flat sequentially. The three-year CAGR for active buyers is 4%.

Collections and cash flow show sharp sequential declines. Standalone collections from customers fell 26% quarter-on-quarter to Rs. 402 crore, and standalone cash flow from operations fell 47% quarter-on-quarter to Rs. 153 crore. Consolidated cash flow from operations fell 44% quarter-on-quarter to Rs. 163 crore. The presentation notes that the fourth quarter has traditionally been the best quarter for collections, which partly explains the seasonality.

Associate losses are a recurring drag. Share in net loss of associates was Rs. 146 million in Q1 FY2027, Rs. 127 million in Q4 FY2026, Rs. 141 million in Q1 FY2026, and Rs. 548 million for FY2026. The consolidated results include the Group’s share of total net loss after tax of Rs. 127.84 million for seven associates, plus a further Rs. 17.69 million share of net loss for one associate (IB MonotaRO) for the period 1 April 2026 to 29 May 2026, whose financial information was unaudited.

Busy Infotech profitability is volatile. Busy’s EBITDA fell to Rs. 3 crore in Q1 FY2027 from Rs. 5 crore in Q4 FY2026, with EBITDA margin dropping to 9% from 16%. Busy’s segment result was negative Rs. 27 million in Q1 FY2027, compared with negative Rs. 7 million in Q4 FY2026 and negative Rs. 6 million in Q1 FY2026. Busy’s FY26 EBITDA margin was 17%, down from 24% in FY23 and 25% in FY22.

Other/emerging businesses are loss-making. The “Others” line within the legal entity view showed negative EBITDA of Rs. 6 crore in Q1 FY2027 and negative Rs. 11 crore for FY2026.

Labour Codes implementation is incomplete. The Government of India notified provisions of The Code on Wages, 2019, The Industrial Relations Code, 2020, The Code on Social Security, 2020, and The Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions Code, 2020, effective from 21 November 2025. However, corresponding rules have been notified only by four States; rules for the remaining States are yet to be notified. The Group recognised the resultant employee benefit expense in FY2026 and will evaluate the impact of remaining State rules when notified.

Governance-related dissent at the AGM. At the 27th AGM on June 29, 2026, the resolution to re-appoint Mr. Brijesh Kumar Agrawal as a director retiring by rotation passed with 94.0115% votes in favour and 5.9885% against. Public institutions voted 83.4697% in favour and 16.5303% against on this resolution, a notably higher dissent level than on the other resolutions.

Director cessation. Mr. Dhruv Prakash ceased to be a Non-Executive Non-Independent Director with effect from the conclusion of the 27th AGM on June 29, 2026, upon completion of his tenure.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Consolidated revenue growth. Consolidated revenue from operations was Rs. 414 crore in Q1 FY2027, up 11% year-on-year from Rs. 372 crore. Standalone revenue was Rs. 376 crore, up 9% year-on-year, driven primarily by improvement in realisation from paying suppliers.

Deferred revenue provides visibility. Consolidated deferred revenue rose to Rs. 2,014 crore as on June 30, 2026, up 16% year-on-year. Standalone deferred revenue was Rs. 1,858 crore, up 14% year-on-year. The presentation notes that approximately 20% of deferred revenue gets recognised within the next three months.

ARPU expansion. Standalone annualised revenue per paying supplier was Rs. 69K, up 9% year-on-year. Top 10% ARPU was Rs. 349K, up 10% year-on-year. The top 1% of paying suppliers (~2K) generate ARPU of Rs. 1,135K and contribute 16% of revenue; the top 10% (~22K) generate 51% of revenue.

Strong margins and cash generation. Standalone EBITDA was Rs. 149 crore, an 11% year-on-year increase, representing a 40% margin. Consolidated EBITDA was Rs. 146 crore at a 35% margin. Consolidated cash generated from operations was Rs. 163 crore, equal to 35% of collections. Standalone cash flow from operations was 38% of collections.

Large cash and investment balance. Consolidated cash and investments stood at Rs. 3,553 crore as on June 30, 2026, up 29% year-on-year. Standalone cash and investments were Rs. 3,316 crore. The company has returned approximately Rs. 1,650 crore of capital to shareholders, including the FY26 dividend payout concluded in July 2026.

Busy Infotech growth. Busy revenue from operations was Rs. 36 crore in Q1 FY2027, up 47% year-on-year. Busy billing was Rs. 59 crore, up 10% year-on-year. Busy deferred revenue and advances were Rs. 146 crore. Busy’s five-year revenue CAGR is 35% and billing CAGR is 42%.

Supplier storefront and listing growth. Indian supplier storefronts grew to 8.8 million, up 5% year-on-year. Live product listings grew to 132 million, up 8% year-on-year.

AI deployment. Management stated that deployment of AI — from standardized cataloging and intelligent matchmaking to advanced conversational tools — is streamlining user interactions and driving efficiency. The presentation describes AI applications including product category intelligence, content moderation, systemic user verification, contextual user experience, and multimodal and multilingual search.

New wholly owned subsidiary for financial services. On July 21, 2026, the Board approved incorporation of a wholly owned subsidiary, IndiaMART Finance Limited, in India, subject to necessary approvals. The primary objective is to strengthen trust, engagement, and retention of users on the platform by enabling business users primarily to meet short-term working capital requirements/financial needs. The initial paid-up capital is 50,000 equity shares of Rs. 10 each, amounting to Rs. 5,00,000, subscribed 100% in cash. The incorporation is subject to approval of the Ministry of Corporate Affairs and other relevant statutory/regulatory authorities.

Strategic investment portfolio. IndiaMART has invested approximately Rs. 730 crore in the accounting space. Investments include Busy Infotech (Rs. 509 crore, 100%), Livekeeping Technologies (Rs. 103 crore, 100%), Fleetx Technologies (Rs. 161 crore, 22.2%), IB MonotaRO (Rs. 118 crore, 18.7%), Baldor Technologies (Rs. 113 crore, 10.3%), Simply Vyapar Apps (Rs. 108 crore, 28.6%), Mobisy Technologies (Rs. 72 crore, 32.5%), Mynd Solutions (Rs. 53 crore, 9.6%), Truckhall (Rs. 38 crore, 34.5%), Agillos E-Commerce (Rs. 26 crore, 23.2%), Edgewise Technologies (Rs. 18 crore, 26.0%), Zimyo Consulting (Rs. 17 crore, 10.0%), Adansa Solutions (Rs. 14 crore, 26.0%), Legistify Services (Rs. 9 crore, 14.2%), and Instant Procurement Services (Rs. 1 crore, 13.1%).

Dividend. The 27th AGM approved a final dividend of Rs. 30 per equity share for FY2025-26 and a special dividend of Rs. 30 per equity share, aggregating to Rs. 60 per equity share.

Market position. IndiaMART describes itself as India’s largest online B2B marketplace for business products and services, diversified across approximately 98,000 categories, 57 industries, and 132 million products. The platform has 58% repeat buyers, 38% of suppliers are also buyers, and a 4.8 Play Store rating. Approximately 48% of buyers come from small cities, and 8.5 million suppliers are served free of charge.

3. Key Risks

Dependence on paying supplier additions. With paying suppliers flat year-on-year at 218K and 1,852 suppliers declining during the quarter, future revenue growth depends on ARPU expansion rather than volume growth. The three-year paying supplier CAGR of 3% contrasts with the five-year CAGR of 8%.

Declining enquiry and delivered lead volumes. Unique business enquiries fell 11% year-on-year and business enquiries delivered fell 13% year-on-year. The multi-year trend shows business enquiries delivered declining from 610 million in FY21 to 370 million in FY26.

Associate investment losses. The Group’s share of net losses in associates has been recurring: Rs. 548 million in FY2026, Rs. 146 million in Q1 FY2027, Rs. 127 million in Q4 FY2026, and Rs. 141 million in Q1 FY2026. One associate’s financial information (IB MonotaRO, for the period 1 April 2026 to 29 May 2026) was unaudited and furnished by the Board of Directors.

Reliance on other auditors for subsidiary and associate figures. The consolidated financial results include audited financial results of four subsidiaries reflecting total revenue of Rs. 27.25 million and total net loss after tax of Rs. 64.75 million, audited by other independent auditors. The Group’s share of net loss for seven associates (Rs. 127.84 million) is based solely on interim financial information, and for one associate (Rs. 17.69 million) on unaudited information certified by the Board.

Segment profitability divergence. The accounting software services segment reported a negative segment result of Rs. 27 million in Q1 FY2027, compared with negative Rs. 7 million in Q4 FY2026 and negative Rs. 6 million in Q1 FY2026. The web and related services segment result was Rs. 1,492 million in Q1 FY2027.

Regulatory and approval risk on new subsidiary. The incorporation of IndiaMART Finance Limited is subject to approval of the Ministry of Corporate Affairs and other relevant statutory/regulatory authorities. The entity belongs to the financial services industry.

Labour Code rule notification uncertainty. The Group will evaluate the impact of State-level rules under the new Labour Codes when notified and recognise consequential effects in the period in which the relevant rules are notified.

Seasonality in collections and cash flow. The presentation notes that the fourth quarter of the financial year traditionally has high cash flow from operations due to seasonal impact of collections from customers. This contributed to the sharp sequential declines in Q1 FY2027 collections and cash flow.

Governance dissent. The re-appointment of Mr. Brijesh Kumar Agrawal as a director retiring by rotation received 5.9885% votes against, with public institutions casting 16.5303% of their votes against. Mr. Brijesh Kumar Agrawal and Mr. Dinesh Chandra Agarwal are cousin brothers.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Management commentary. Mr. Dinesh Agarwal, Chief Executive Officer, stated: “We continued our emphasis on sustainable growth and elevating overall marketplace experience by building a highly trusted, reliable environment for our buyers and sellers. By prioritizing platform trust, we are fostering deeper, more meaningful engagement across our marketplace. Deployment of AI, from standardized cataloging and intelligent matchmaking to advanced conversational tools, is streamlining user interactions and driving efficiency. With a robust business model and strong cash generation, we remain confident in our ability to create long-term value for all our stakeholders.”

Observed performance against commentary. Revenue growth was positive: consolidated revenue rose 11% year-on-year and standalone revenue rose 9% year-on-year. EBITDA grew 11% year-on-year on a standalone basis at a 40% margin. Cash generation remained strong at Rs. 163 crore consolidated from operations, equal to 35% of collections. However, the commentary on “deeper, more meaningful engagement” coincides with a decline in unique business enquiries (down 11% year-on-year), a decline in business enquiries delivered (down 13% year-on-year), a decline in active buyers (down 5% year-on-year), and flat paying suppliers (0% year-on-year). The commentary on “sustainable growth” coincides with a 26% quarter-on-quarter decline in standalone collections and a 47% quarter-on-quarter decline in standalone cash flow from operations, though the presentation attributes this partly to Q4 seasonality.

Strategic outlook stated in the presentation. The presentation outlines a strategic outlook covering: (i) core SME business — keep expanding buyer and supplier network; (ii) brands strategy — focus on large and medium enterprises; and (iii) improve engagement — commerce enablement, fintech and accounting, and business enablement SaaS. The presentation describes a journey towards commerce and business enablement spanning discovery, conversation, commerce, and business enablement, including credit facilitation, logistics/tracking, procurement management, HRMS, order management, accounting/invoicing, inventory management, distributor management, and receivables management.

Busy Infotech stated priorities. The presentation states Busy’s priorities as “Invest in Team & Awareness” and “Accelerate Revenue growth” and “Increase New Licenses Sold.” Observed: Busy revenue grew 47% year-on-year in Q1 FY2027, but EBITDA fell to Rs. 3 crore from Rs. 5 crore in Q4 FY2026, and the segment result was negative Rs. 27 million.

No specific numerical guidance. The supplied material does not contain specific numerical revenue, margin, or earnings guidance for future periods. The forward-looking elements are qualitative statements about strategic priorities, AI deployment, platform trust, and long-term value creation.

Investor meetings. The company held investor meetings with Fidelity International and ICICI Prudential AMC Ltd. on September 16, 2026, and no unpublished price sensitive information was shared during these meetings.

Broker Narrative

The broker stayed Buy from the first report to the last, initially leaning on dominant market share, network effects, expected supplier additions, ARPU pricing power, and net cash as protection. By 2026 the narrative had to absorb a falling paying-supplier base, elevated Silver churn, soft buyer demand, and LLM/AI search disruption, yet it maintained conviction by shifting emphasis to Gold/Platinum retention, ARPU growth, margin expansion, cash generation, and AI-led efficiency. Persistent themes were pricing power, premium-tier stability, balance-sheet strength, and trust/quality investments; what changed was the acknowledgment that volume-led growth in suppliers and buyers had stalled.

Fears that came true

  • The early risk that Silver subscription price hikes would slow paid supplier additions materialized: paying suppliers later fell ~1,850 QoQ to ~218,000 and Silver-tier churn stayed high at ~7%, consistent with negative actual returns on Buy calls.
  • The warning that traffic growth was moderating after COVID-related enquiries faded materialized into soft buyer demand, a ~5% YoY decline in active buyers, and unique business inquiries around 26m.
  • The flagged competitive threats materialized as structural uncertainty from LLM/AI-driven search disruption, with traffic migrating to LLM-based search and contributing to disappointing Buy outcomes.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of adding ~8,000 paid suppliers per quarter failed, as the paying supplier base instead declined by ~1,850 QoQ by 1QFY27.
  • The assumption that the B2B classified model was less susceptible to disruption failed, with LLM/AI-driven search disruption becoming a key structural overhang.
  • The large headroom thesis from low MSME penetration and untapped T2-T4 cities did not translate into supplier/buyer expansion: paying suppliers fell and active buyers declined ~5% YoY.
