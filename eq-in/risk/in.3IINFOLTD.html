Headwinds and Challenges

  • Board composition and governance friction. On August 12, 2026, the Board disclosed that it had received Notices under Section 160 of the Companies Act, 2013 from certain members proposing 12 candidatures for appointment as Non-Executive Directors at the 33rd AGM (scheduled for August 28, 2026). The Board noted that if all 12 were elected, total Board strength would exceed the maximum permissible limit of 15 directors under Section 149(1) and Article 122 of the AoA, and would also require appointment of Independent Directors to remain compliant with Regulation 17 of the SEBI Listing Regulations. The Board stated that shareholder approval by special resolution would be needed to increase the maximum number of directors and amend Article 122, and that the matter could not be considered at the ensuing AGM; approvals would instead be sought via postal ballot or an Extraordinary General Meeting. The Board also flagged concerns about the overall size of the Board and the administrative, logistics and cost implications of managing a Board of that size, and asked the NRC to evaluate the optimal Board size.

  • Proposed removal of a director. The company received a Special Notice under Section 115 of the Companies Act, 2013 from a member for removal of Mr. Umesh Mehta as a Non-Independent Non-executive Director. The Board approved inclusion of this matter as additional special business (Item No. 5) at the AGM.

  • AGM resolutions not passed. At the 33rd AGM held on August 28, 2026, Ordinary Resolution No. 1, Ordinary Resolution No. 4, Ordinary Resolution No. 5, and Special Resolution No. 3 were not passed by members; only Ordinary Resolution No. 2 was passed.

  • SEBI complaint over voting pattern. The company filed a further follow-on complaint with SEBI on September 8, 2026, requesting an investigation into a coordinated voting pattern at the 33rd AGM, which the company stated may constitute conduct as persons acting in concert and acquisition of control under Takeover Regulations.

  • Cybersecurity incident. During Q1 FY27, the company managed a cybersecurity incident, activating its incident response framework with a CERT-In empanelled forensic auditor. Critical operations were restored within a short timeframe with no material impact on business continuity or client delivery, and regulatory obligations were complied with. The company subsequently strengthened monitoring, SIEM optimisation, endpoint protection, network segmentation and geofencing.

  • US revenue moderation. US revenue stood at ₹91.2 crore in Q1 FY27 versus ₹98.3 crore in Q4 FY26, a sequential moderation of 7.2% QoQ.

  • Profitability pressure. EBITDA was ₹11.5 crore in Q1 FY27 versus ₹18.1 crore in Q1 FY26, though the prior-year figure included a one-time other income from the US on ECS credit of ₹18.4 crore. PAT was ₹6.5 crore in Q1 FY27 versus ₹7.3 crore in Q4 FY26 and ₹7.5 crore in Q1 FY26. Standalone PAT was ₹6.6 crore in Q1 FY27 versus ₹13.7 crore in Q4 FY26.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Revenue growth. Operating revenue rose to ₹177.9 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹170.5 crore in Q1 FY26, a 4.3% YoY increase and 1.3% QoQ growth. Gross margin improved to 14.4% in Q1 FY27 from 11.2% in Q1 FY26.

  • Order bookings and client wins. The company secured order bookings of TCV ₹240.9 crore (ACV ₹195.6 crore) in Q1 FY27 and reported 25+ new client wins, which it stated broadened its customer mix and reduced concentration risk.

  • MEA momentum. MEA revenue rose to ₹15.9 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹9.7 crore in Q4 FY26, a 64.0% QoQ increase supported by new business wins. India revenue rose 4.9% QoQ to ₹66.6 crore.

  • Segment mix. AAA contributed 73.5% of revenue, IS 18.7% and BPS 7.8%. IT remained the largest industry vertical at approximately 46.0% of revenue, followed by BFSI at approximately 32.9%.

  • Deal wins across geographies and sectors. Q1 FY27 wins included Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited (IT facility management), VaporVM IT Services DMCC (UAE technology resourcing), a leading city gas distribution company (BPS call centre), Ohio Transmission LLC (US industrial BPS), NCDEX (managed services renewal), SBI General Insurance (DWBI renewal), a public sector general insurance company (biometric two-factor authentication), a global research and publishing company (AWS cloud billing), Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (application maintenance), Nathdwara Temple Board (document management), CG Power and Industrial Solutions (facility management and IT helpdesk), a leading Indian private sector bank (document digitisation of approximately 51 lakh credit card application records), PNB Housing Finance (infrastructure management renewal), Al Ain Farms (UAE resourcing), Flexible Plan Investments (Microsoft 365 migration) and J&B Medical (US healthcare technology).

  • New disaster recovery order. The company received a purchase order dated September 10, 2026 (PO No. NCDEX/2026-2027/EQ/062) from a leading commodity exchange in India for additional resources to support Disaster Recovery operations, providing 24x7 DR operations and datacentre management support for three years (FY 2026-27 to FY 2028-29), valued at ₹1,02,31,832 (approximately ₹1.02 crore), exclusive of applicable taxes.

  • Strategic initiatives. The company cited operationalisation of a Centre of Excellence-led operating model across all business segments, launch of an AI and new-age tech vertical, an SAP partnership, successful closure of a rights issue, and in-house development of new-age products. It described six strategic priorities — People, Process, Productivity, Platform, Partnering and Profitability — and Vision 2030 ambitions.

  • Recognition. The company was positioned as a Major Contender by Everest Group in its PEAK Matrix for BFSI IT Services 2025 and Next-Generation Quality Engineering Services 2024, and as an Aspirant in several other Everest Group assessments, and was featured in Gartner’s Market Guide, 2023 for several offerings.

Key Risks

  • Failure to resolve the Board composition issue could leave the company in breach of the SEBI Listing Regulations, the Companies Act or its Articles of Association, as the Board itself noted.
  • The Special Notice seeking removal of Mr. Umesh Mehta introduces potential Board-level disruption.
  • The unresolved SEBI complaint regarding the AGM voting pattern, and the possibility of regulatory findings on persons acting in concert or acquisition of control under Takeover Regulations, represent governance and regulatory risk.
  • The non-passing of multiple AGM resolutions (Ordinary Resolutions 1, 4 and 5 and Special Resolution 3) indicates shareholder dissent on certain matters.
  • Cybersecurity risk remains, given the Q1 FY27 incident, notwithstanding remediation measures.
  • Geographic concentration risk is evident, with the US remaining the largest geography at 51.2% of revenue and showing QoQ moderation.
  • The company’s own forward-looking statements are subject to risks and uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ materially.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Management stated that Q1 FY27 performance reflected disciplined execution of strategic priorities outlined at the beginning of FY27, and that over the past two years the focus had been on improving operational efficiency, enhancing profitability and building a scalable foundation for long-term growth. Observed performance: revenue grew 4.3% YoY and 1.3% QoQ, and gross margin improved to 14.4% from 11.2% YoY, while EBITDA and PAT declined YoY (EBITDA ₹11.5 crore vs ₹18.1 crore; PAT ₹6.5 crore vs ₹7.5 crore), with the prior year including the ₹18.4 crore one-time US ECS credit.
  • Management stated that many strategic initiatives will continue to mature over the coming quarters and that it remains focused on accelerating sustainable revenue growth and maintaining margin discipline. Observed performance: Q1 FY27 revenue growth was 4.3% YoY, and EBITDA margin was 6.4%.
  • Management highlighted continued operationalisation of the CoE-led operating model and reinforcement of the leadership team with experienced business and sales leaders. Observed performance: the company reported 25+ new client wins and TCV bookings of ₹240.9 crore (ACV ₹195.6 crore) in the quarter.
  • Management stated the company is well positioned to capitalise on AI-enabled, cloud-first and platform-led transformation through its integrated portfolio. Observed performance: AAA remained the largest segment at 73.5% of revenue, and the company announced an AI and new-age tech vertical and an SAP partnership.
  • Management stated it will continue to update the stock exchanges on significant developments related to the SEBI complaint matter.
