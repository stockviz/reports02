Headwinds and Challenges

  • The June 2026 quarter (1QFY27) was marked by a complex global operating environment, with developments in West Asia affecting supply chains and input costs more acutely during the quarter.
  • Consolidated revenues declined sequentially to ₹60,794 crores from ₹63,270 crores in 4QFY26, driven by a seasonal drop in volumes, particularly in India, partly offset by higher steel realisations.
  • India crude steel production (5.76 million tons) and deliveries (5.17 million tons) were affected by maintenance shutdowns at Meramandali and Kalinganagar. Production and deliveries are expected to normalise in coming quarters.
  • Netherlands operations were impacted by the closure of the Direct Sheet Plant. Liquid steel production was 1.55 million tons and deliveries 1.40 million tons; Netherlands EBITDA fell to €4 million for the quarter. The local environment authority has permitted trial runs, which are ongoing, ahead of restart of full operations.
  • UK operations reported revenues of £484 million and an EBITDA loss of £27 million, although the loss narrowed from £48 million in 4QFY26. The improvement came despite operational disruptions from a pickle line fire.
  • Raw material costs moved higher due to a rise in coking coal consumption cost in India and Netherlands. Other expenses rose on account of higher royalty and West Asia crisis-induced increases in power and fuel costs.
  • Working capital was impacted by inventory build-up due to operational and supply chain disruptions, and an increase in prices.
  • Net debt stood at ₹84,173 crores, with Net debt to EBITDA at 2.3x.
  • Exceptional loss of ₹345 crores related to an employee separation scheme in India and redundancy-related provisions in the UK.
  • The company reassessed useful lives of certain items of property, plant and equipment, resulting in an additional depreciation charge of ₹294.49 crore for the quarter and an expected additional charge of approximately ₹1,178 crore for the year ending March 31, 2027.
  • In the Netherlands, the Environmental Agency and Province of North Holland issued a letter on April 23, 2026 indicating intention to revoke operating permits and trigger early closure of Coke and Gas Plants 1 and 2. TSN is engaged with regulators on a safe, responsible and controlled closure process, and is exploring all options including legal recourse. TSN is also engaged with regulators on evolving standards for steel slag classification and disposal, with removal from the IJmuiden site temporarily disrupted, resulting in excess stockpiling. A material uncertainty exists surrounding the impact of these adversities on the financial stability of TSN as an entity.
  • India apparent steel demand continued to grow, but imports outpaced exports during the quarter. The government initiated an anti-dumping investigation on Hot Rolled steel imports from select countries.
  • EU and UK incorporated tighter safeguard quotas effective July 1, 2026, though in the UK there were deviations from the initial proposal with implications for product categories.

  • The completion of the divestment of JFSPL is subject to the satisfaction of certain conditions precedent, including receipt of necessary approvals from the All-India Football Federation and completion of other customary actions and activities as agreed between Tata Steel and Churchill.
  • The completion of the acquisition of 23% equity stake in TMILL is subject to customary conditions precedent, and approvals from regulatory authorities including Competition Commission of India and other stakeholders, as applicable.
  • The amalgamation of NINL into and with the Company is subject to certain conditions, including sanction of the Scheme by the Hon’ble National Company Law Tribunal. The draft scheme has been filed at the relevant bench of the NCLT and the sanction of the NCLT is awaited.
  • The amalgamation of RIL into and with the Company is subject to certain conditions, including sanction of the Scheme by the Hon’ble National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai bench.
  • In the Netherlands, the timing, nature and contents of a decision that may be issued by the EA and the Province regarding the potential revocation of operating permits and early closure of CGP 1 and 2 is still unclear, while discussions, exchange of technical information and independent validation continues. TSN is also exploring all options including legal recourse to ensure that the closure process is managed with due care and prudence.
  • TSN is engaged with regulators on evolving standards relating to classification of steel slag as a material and its disposal. In the interim, the removal of steel slag from the IJmuiden site is temporarily disrupted resulting in excess stockpiling. A range of mitigating actions is being pursued, including regulatory engagement, legal and technical assessments and the development of alternative storage and disposal solutions. But the resolution of the underlying regulatory conditions is not wholly within TSN’s control.
  • Given the significance of both the above matters to TSN’s operations, there exists a material uncertainty surrounding the impact of such adversities on the financial stability of TSN as an entity. The financial results of TSN as an entity have accordingly been prepared on a going concern basis while recognising the aforesaid material uncertainty.
  • The financial results of Tata Steel UK have also been prepared on a going concern basis, considering the UK Government funding being available under the Grant Funding Agreement (GFA) signed with the UK Government and a commitment to infuse equity into TSUK by the Company.
  • In view of the matters stated above, the Group has assessed its ability to meet any liquidity requirements of the Group, including at TSUK and TSN, as may be required, and concluded that its cashflow and liquidity position remains adequate.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from early optimism on domestic capacity expansion and robust pre-monsoon demand to managing acute cost pressures and European operational disruptions. While the goal of doubling domestic capacity to 40 MTPA and focusing on value-added products persisted, the optimism around demand and margins faded. The early warning of a UK/European drag materialized into active fires and regulatory shutdowns, with the European EAF transition now depending on massive government funding.

Fears that came true

  • The risk of sharp increases in coking coal prices adversely impacting margins materialized as coking coal costs rose ~$5/tonne QoQ in India and ~US$10/tonne QoQ in the Netherlands, correlating with the stock’s negative actual returns in 2026.
  • The UK operations drag and risk of shutdown materialized as a pickle line fire caused ~10 KT lost sales and ~£5 million EBITDA impact, correlating with DISAPPOINTMENT and PENDING outcomes with negative returns in 2026.
  • The risk of sharp declines in domestic steel realizations materialized as Indian net sales realization was expected to decline ~₹1,500/tonne QoQ, correlating with the negative actual returns and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in 2026.

Optimism that failed

  • The optimism around a robust domestic demand outlook supported by expected inventory restocking before the monsoon season failed to materialize, as a seasonal monsoon slowdown instead drove an expected ~₹1,500/tonne QoQ decline in net sales realization, correlating with the stock’s negative actual returns in 2026.
