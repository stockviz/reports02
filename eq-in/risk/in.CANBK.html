Canara Bank — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin and liability structure. Management describes Canara Bank’s NIM as being “towards the lowest side” among peers, with CASA below 30% (29.70% as of the Q1 FY27 call) and a higher dependence on bulk deposits. The MD & CEO noted that the bank’s NIM is around 2.52% against a guidance band of 2.50–2.60%, and that the bank has historically operated with a lower CASA share than peers such as Central Bank of India, Bank of Maharashtra, and his previous bank (around 38–39%).

Treasury income drag. Treasury income fell sharply year-on-year. In the June 2025 quarter, the bank earned ₹1,617 crore from treasury operations, aided by softer yields, RBI OMO operations, and arbitrage opportunities. In the June 2026 quarter, only ₹654 crore was earned through sale of investments — described as a “dent” of about ₹1,000 crore — because yields had hardened and no arbitrage was available.

Fee income softness. Fee income grew 5.35% YoY but declined sequentially by under ₹200 crore (from ₹2,513 crore to ₹2,342 crore), attributed to certain commissions and service charges that are typically booked in the March quarter.

SMA migration. SMA-0 rose from ₹862 crore to ₹3,315 crore, and SMA-2 rose from ₹1,394 crore to ₹3,482 crore, while SMA-1 declined. Management attributed this to three to four large consortium accounts that are government-guaranteed and oscillate between SMA-0, 1, and 2. One account shifted from SMA-1 to SMA-2 and has since corrected; another briefly entered SMA-0 and has since exited. Management stated there is no stress in the large corporate book, with total SMA below 3%.

ECL transition provisioning. Management estimates an incremental provisioning requirement of around ₹10,000 crore for Stage 1/Stage 2/Stage 3 under ECL, plus an uncertain mark-to-market component (illustratively ~₹2,000 crore), totaling roughly ₹12,000 crore. The bank has a five-year dispensation from RBI but plans to absorb the impact over two years. The estimated dent on capital adequacy, if provided in one year, would be 1.2–1.25%. Management also noted uncertainty around M2M on 1 April 2027, since market conditions at that date are unknown.

Agri/monsoon exposure. Management acknowledged potential stress in the KCC portfolio if districts are declared distress districts due to deficient rainfall, though it noted the KCC portfolio is not very large and that dispensations from SLVC, government, and RBI would help manage it.

Yield pressure from repo-linked book. About 53% of the portfolio is repo-rate linked, and the 100 bps repo rate cut during FY26 was spread across the year, contributing to a 29 bps decline in yield on advances in the quarter.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Business growth. Global business grew 14.37% YoY to ₹29,05,066 crore; global deposits grew 11.63% YoY to ₹16,11,685 crore; global advances grew 17.97% YoY to ₹12,93,381 crore. RAM credit grew 21.20% YoY to ₹7,64,675 crore; retail credit grew 35.88% YoY to ₹3,19,893 crore; housing loans grew 17.85% to ₹1,29,036 crore; vehicle loans grew 26.34% to ₹27,315 crore; MSME grew 15.12% to ₹1,68,815 crore.

Emergency Line of Credit 5.0. The bank identified ~₹90,000 crore of eligible accounts, with ~₹18,000 crore of positive advances based on eligibility. Of this, ₹11,000 crore+ has been sanctioned and ₹10,000 crore+ disbursed. Management expects ₹5,000–6,000 crore more, with disbursement through the Jan Samarth portal and minimal pendency. This is expected to support advances growth in coming quarters, with a 1% higher rate.

FCNRB/ECB/OFCB mobilization. Guidance of $2.3–2.5 billion; $775 million already raised in July against a $750 million target, with an endeavor to cross $1 billion within the month. These funds come at ~6.50%, with CRR/SLR dispensation reducing effective cost to ~6.20%, helping replace higher-cost bulk deposits.

Deposit mix improvement. Individual savings grew 12.48% and retail term deposits grew 9.10%. Management aims to grow deposits 11–12% and gradually replace high-cost bulk deposits with retail deposits. The bank plans to open 250 new branches this year (34 already opened), adding to its 10,131-branch network.

Corporate pricing traction. Management noted some improvement in corporate lending pricing (10–20 paisa here and there), with 86% of the book now A-and-above rated, up from before. RAM share of advances rose from 58% to 59%.

Gold loan franchise. Total gold loan book at ₹2.59 lakh crore (agriculture gold ₹1.51 lakh crore; retail gold ₹1.07 lakh crore), with LTV of 60–65%. Management described it as a growing, well-collateralized business with minimal credit cost and no capital provision requirement.

Subsidiaries. Two subsidiaries listed last year (Canara HSBC Life and Canara Robeco) are performing well, with the bank benefiting threefold: agency commissions, valuation gains, and stock price appreciation. Around ₹320 crore of profit came to the parent this year from subsidiaries/associates.

Digital spend. Over ₹3,000 crore earmarked for digital spend this year (~8% of total IT cost), with a substantial portion toward AI, to be implemented in a consolidated, calibrated manner.

Credit pipeline. Around 100 accounts worth ~₹50,000 crore: 45 accounts sanctioned but undisbursed (~₹18,000 crore) and 47 proposals in hand (~₹32,000 crore).

3. Key Risks

ECL transition uncertainty. The exact M2M impact on the investment book at 1 April 2027 is unknown. Management estimates ~₹10,000 crore for Stage 1/2/3 plus an illustrative ~₹2,000 crore for M2M, but acknowledges this is dynamic. The run-rate credit cost impact post-ECL is estimated at 4–5 bps (management caveated this as an assumption).

SMA volatility. Although attributed to government-guaranteed consortium accounts, the oscillation between SMA categories introduces reporting volatility. Management stated total SMA is below 3% and there is no large corporate stress.

Agri/monsoon risk. Potential stress in the KCC portfolio if districts are declared distress districts, though management considers it manageable given dispensations and the relatively small KCC book.

Margin sensitivity. With 53% of the portfolio repo-linked and deposit repricing described as “plateau,” further rate movements could pressure NIM. Management aims to protect NIM at 2.50–2.60% and aspires to improve it, but acknowledged the tough ecosystem.

Bulk deposit dependence. The bank’s reliance on bulk deposits (cost ~6.58% last month, improving monthly by 29–30 bps) remains a structural risk to margin, though management is working to replace it with retail deposits and FCNRB.

RBI penalty. On 10.08.2026, RBI imposed a monetary penalty of ₹1,49,450 on the bank for CC Remittance Shortage (₹1,24,100), Mutilated Notes in CC Remittance (₹23,350), and Counterfeit in CC Remittance (₹2,000). The impact is limited to the penalty amount.

Fee income seasonality. Fee income is concentrated in the March quarter, creating sequential volatility.

ECL guidance. Management estimates ~₹10,000 crore incremental provisioning plus ~₹2,000 crore illustrative M2M, to be absorbed over two years against a five-year dispensation. Run-rate credit cost impact estimated at 4–5 bps. PSLC income. No full-year target; PSLC income is concentrated in Q1 with some spillover into Q2. Q1 FY27 PSLC income was ₹1,947 crore vs. ₹1,684 crore in Q1 FY26. Management expects only ₹200–300 crore of additional traction in Q2.

LCR. Soft target threshold of 110%; reported LCR at 115%, with quarterly average at 119%.

Dividend. The Board recommended a dividend of ₹4.2 per equity share (210% of face value of ₹2 each) for FY2025-26, approved at the 24th AGM on 23.06.2026.

AGM outcomes. All four ordinary resolutions passed at the 24th AGM: adoption of standalone and consolidated audited financial statements for FY2025-26 (99.0771% in favour), declaration of dividend (99.2564% in favour), appointment of Shri Sunil Kumar Chugh as Executive Director (89.6463% in favour), and appointment of Ms. Shalini Pandit as Government Nominee Director (89.3213% in favour).

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from a cautious Accumulate in 2023, which saw near-term NIM support but worried about CoF, loan moderation and ECL, to a Buy in 2026 that argued NIM had bottomed, ECL was manageable and loan growth was strong and broad-based. Persisting themes were margin/CoF pressure, ECL provisioning risk and asset-quality improvement. The changed themes were loan growth (moderation fear replaced by 19% YoY growth) and NIM outlook (near-term expansion replaced by bottoming thesis).

Fears that came true

  • Rising CoF materialized: by Q1FY27 cost of funds reached 5.43% and NIM fell to 2.52%, with the 2026-01-30 Hold call ending as a DISAPPOINTMENT at -9.5% actual.
  • NII/estimate misses recurred: after Q4FY23 NII missed by 8%, Q1FY27 core PPoP was 7.5% below estimate, followed by the 2024-05-08 Buy call’s -12.2% actual vs +16.5% predicted.
  • ECL provisioning risk crystallized: the envisaged multi-year charge appeared as a one-time ~INR 120bn impact (~10% equity) in Q1FY27, and the last report’s +18.1% predicted return was tracking only +3.9%.

Optimism that failed

  • The near-term NIM expansion from 1-year MCLR re-pricing failed to sustain; NIM ended at 2.52% in Q1FY27, the lowest among peers.
  • Subsidiary value unlocking via planned IPOs/listings has not appeared in later reports.
  • The strong profitability/RoA momentum did not continue into Q1FY27, as core PPoP missed estimates and provisions rose on employee/tax items.
