Sudarshan Colorants India Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Revenue decline and market softness. FY2026 revenue from operations stood at ₹780.63 crore, down 5.4% from ₹825.06 crore in FY2025. The Managing Director attributed this to global market softness, customer inventory destocking, and the caution accompanying a year of organizational transition. Net profit fell 13.4% to ₹44.53 crore from ₹51.45 crore.

Margin compression. Operating EBITDA declined to ₹57.95 crore in FY2026 from ₹81.80 crore in FY2025. Operating profit margin fell to 7% from 10%, and Return on Net Worth declined to 8% from 10%. Return on Capital Employed declined to 11% from 13%.

US export pressure. The Managing Director noted that outside the US, markets grew 7%, but an uncertain tariff environment weighed on US export momentum.

Segment softness. Printing inks were softer, reflecting global destocking. Coatings remained the most stable segment, with specialty and high-performance grades showing better resilience than commodity pigments.

Raw material and currency volatility. Volatility in raw material costs, currency movements, and supply chains was described as an operating reality, managed through supply chain diversification, strategic inventory positioning, and long-term partnerships.

Competitive and structural pressures. The company’s risk disclosures cite industry structure changes leading to oversupply and increased competition from small players with varying costs and quality standards, as well as challenges in tapping business opportunities due to lack of innovation and insufficient customer engagement.

Operational risks. Lower production capacity utilization impacting efficiency, and inadequate planning for demand, supply, and logistics resulting in higher costs, are identified risk themes.

Attrition and succession risk. Challenges in attracting and retaining talent, and lack of a robust leadership succession plan at critical levels, are flagged as risks.

Governance and leadership churn. The year saw significant board and KMP changes: Mr. Ravi Kapoor resigned as Chairman effective July 16, 2025; Mr. Jugal Sahu resigned as Executive Director effective August 11, 2025 (continuing as CFO until March 09, 2026); Mr. Amitabha Mukhopadhyay resigned as Independent Director effective August 11, 2025 due to potential conflict, then was appointed as Non-Executive Non-Independent Director effective October 31, 2025; Mr. Kewal Handa resigned as Independent Director effective October 28, 2025; Mr. Sunirmal Talukdar ceased as Independent Director effective March 31, 2026 on completion of tenure. Ms. Ashwini Natekar ceased as Interim Company Secretary effective July 16, 2025, and Mr. Adwait Joshi was appointed effective August 01, 2025. Mr. Nilkanth Natu was appointed CFO (Interim) effective May 11, 2026.

Whistle-blower complaint. One complaint was received under the Whistle-Blower Mechanism; upon investigation it was found frivolous and motivated by malicious intent, and was closed.

EPF Trust matter. The Regional Provident Fund Commissioner-II, Nariman Point directed the Company’s EPF Trust to comply as an unexempted establishment effective March 1, 2024. The EPF Trust transferred securities and liquidated proceeds to the RPFC; a third-party audit found no material observations. The EPF Trust will be formally closed after final confirmation from the RPFC. The Company does not expect material adjustments.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

ONE Sudarshan integration. Following Sudarshan Chemical Group’s acquisition of the Heubach Group (completed March 03, 2025), the Company became part of ONE Sudarshan, described as one of the world’s leading pigment majors with 19 manufacturing facilities across 11 countries, 3,825 employees, and 4,000+ global customers. The name change from Heubach Colorants India Limited to Sudarshan Colorants India Limited was approved effective December 12, 2025.

Open offer and control. Post completion of the Open Offer on October 03, 2025, the Acquirer along with PAC-1, PAC-2 and PAC-3 exercise control over the Company constituting 70.25% of total voting share capital.

Destocking cycle largely run its course. The Managing Director stated the destocking cycle has largely run its course, and domestic demand, particularly in coatings and plastics, is expected to strengthen through FY2027. The company is targeting a return to growth, supported by new account wins and an expanded portfolio via the ONE Sudarshan group.

Portfolio breadth as commercial advantage. Customers who previously sourced certain specialty grades elsewhere have returned. Being a full-service colorant partner rather than a single-category supplier is described as a genuine commercial advantage showing up in the FY2027 pipeline.

Innovation opportunities. Near-term opportunities in India include recycling-compatible pigments driven by tightening packaging regulations, and low-migration, food-contact compliant grades. Efforts to localize the premium HP Azo range are expected to unlock new export opportunities FY2027 onward. New products include Hostatint 100-IN (ready-to-use lead-free solvent-based pigment preparations eliminating grinding), NIR-detectable black solutions, OK Compost-compliant color solutions, Novotex Red ARCY, and Polyfast PUF.

Manufacturing optimization. Select AZO products are being transitioned from Frankfurt to Roha, while Cuddalore is building a combination of commodity and specialty products for the broader industrial market.

Procurement leverage and supply chain resilience. Being part of ONE Sudarshan provides significant procurement leverage and supply chain resilience.

Sustainability progress. Renewable energy contributed 58% of total electricity consumption in FY2026. Roha’s solar installation meets approximately 50% of the facility’s electricity needs. At Cuddalore, the hybrid wind-solar model increased renewable electricity share from approximately 62% to 75%. The 7.5 MWp offsite solar plant at Roha achieved annual savings of ₹2.52 crore; Cuddalore’s wind + solar source achieved savings of ₹1.19 crore.

Debt-free balance sheet. The company operates from a largely debt-free balance sheet with no borrowings, giving flexibility to prioritize investments.

Macro tailwinds. India’s GDP growth accelerated to 7.7% in FY2026 from 7.1% in FY2025. FY2027 GDP is projected to expand between 6.8% and 7.2%. The global pigments market is nearly $8.6 billion with expected CAGR of 3%.

Governance strengthening. PricewaterhouseCoopers Services LLP was appointed as Internal Auditor effective October 31, 2025. The statutory audit by MSKA & Associates LLP closed with an unmodified opinion. The Secretarial Audit Report contains no qualification, reservation, adverse remark or disclaimer.

Customer recognition. Regional Customer Service & Delivery teams earned multiple ‘Best Supplier of the Year’ awards across key customers.

Culture and systems integration. Over 95% of colleagues identify with and live the new culture. SAP integration is underway, consolidating four legacy systems into one.

3. Key Risks

Business-related risk. Industry structure changes leading to oversupply and increased competition from small players with varying costs and quality standards; challenges in tapping business opportunities due to lack of innovation, insufficient customer engagement and partnerships; failure to plan for fluctuating costs affecting profitable growth. Mitigation: product innovation, operational efficiency, quality reputation, dynamic cost management including regular cost analysis, strategic sourcing, and contingency planning.

Environmental risk. Difficulty in creating environmentally friendly products through R&D. Mitigation: specialized R&D team focused on innovation and environmentally friendly practices.

Operational risk. Lower production capacity utilization impacting efficiency; inadequate planning for demand, supply, and logistics resulting in higher costs. Mitigation: detailed production optimization plan, demand forecasting, optimizing supply chain operations, improving logistics coordination.

Attrition risk. Challenges in attracting and retaining talent; lack of robust leadership succession plan at critical levels. Mitigation: dynamic inclusive workplace with competitive compensation, professional growth opportunities, clear career paths, identifying high-potential individuals, targeted development, mentorship culture.

Geopolitical and trade risk. Geopolitical volatility underlines the importance of global manufacturing footprints and truly global partners. The global outlook became more cautious in early 2026 amid escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, with growth projected to moderate to 3.1% in 2026.

Raw material, currency, and supply chain volatility. Managed through supply chain diversification, strategic inventory positioning, and long-term partnerships.

Regulatory risk. New Labour Codes notified November 21, 2025, consolidating 29 existing labour laws, led to recognition of incremental impact during the quarter ended December 31, 2025 of ₹12.80 crore as an exceptional item, primarily due to change in definition of wages. Pursuant to actuarial valuation and further assessment, the net incremental impact for FY2026 was revised to ₹4.49 crore (gratuity ₹3.81 crore and compensated absences ₹0.68 crore), with a net reversal of ₹8.31 crore recognized during the quarter ended March 31, 2026 under Exceptional Items.

Tax positions. Based on detailed review of income tax positions relating to long-outstanding tax balances from prior assessment years, the Company recognized reversal of ₹1.79 crore as on March 31, 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Revenue guidance vs. actual. The Managing Director stated FY2026 revenue of ₹780.63 crore was “below our expectations,” reflecting global market softness, customer inventory destocking, and transition caution. The Chairman characterized FY2026 as “a year spent building, not just performing.”

Growth outlook vs. current performance. Management is targeting a return to growth in FY2027, supported by new account wins and expanded portfolio via ONE Sudarshan. The Managing Director stated “FY2027 is when we begin to show the value of what we have built.” This follows an FY2026 revenue decline of 5.4% and PAT decline of 13.4%.

Destocking assessment. Management stated the destocking cycle has largely run its course, with domestic demand in coatings and plastics expected to strengthen through FY2027. Printing inks softness is expected to ease as inventory positions normalize through FY2027.

Margin trajectory. Operating profit margin declined to 7% in FY2026 from 10% in FY2025. The Managing Director noted that the company sometimes accepted near-term margin pressure to protect relationships worth far more over time, with a three-year horizon rather than a quarterly one.

Customer retention vs. revenue. Management stated customer confidence held up better than revenue numbers might suggest, and that where volume was lost in FY2026, it was to market conditions, not to any lapse in service or product quality.

Innovation commercialization. Management stated near-term opportunities in recycling-compatible pigments and low-migration food-contact compliant grades are being actively converted, and localization of the premium HP Azo range is expected to unlock new export opportunities FY2027 onward.

Sustainability targets vs. delivery. Management stated the Roha solar installation now meets approximately 50% of the facility’s electricity demand, and Cuddalore’s hybrid wind-solar model increased renewable electricity share from approximately 62% to 75%. These were described as “targets we set and delivered.” Renewable energy accounted for 58% of total electricity consumption in FY2026.

Value capture plan. The company stated initiatives spanning manufacturing, procurement, and organizational transformation are progressing as planned, with cumulative gains building year over year.

Capex. The Company incurred capex spend of ₹25.34 crore during FY2026 and capital investment on energy conservation equipment of ₹1.95 crore.

Dividend. The Board did not recommend any dividend for FY2026.

CSR spending. Against a prescribed CSR expenditure of ₹1,19,67,000, the Company spent ₹1,28,92,814, with excess amount for set off in succeeding financial years of ₹9,25,814.
