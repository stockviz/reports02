ICEMAKE: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

The company operates against a backdrop of global geopolitical uncertainty, volatile energy prices, and changing trade dynamics, which contribute to cautious investment sentiment. Higher interest costs are a further burden, alongside depreciation associated with newly commissioned facilities and initial ramp-up expenses.

On the cost side, higher commodity and raw material prices are pressuring margins. The company has cited war and dollar strengthening as factors causing indirect import impacts and foreign exchange fluctuation losses, and it has been unable to fully pass on price hikes immediately.

The most recent quarterly performance illustrates these pressures: for Q1 FY27, consolidated revenue from operations reached Rs 178.88 crores (up 60.4% year-on-year), but EBITDA was only Rs 3.09 crore, an EBITDA margin of 1.7%, with a profit before tax loss of Rs 2.23 crore and a profit after tax loss of Rs 1.65 crore.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Demand conditions are described as growing across cold chain, food processing, pharmaceuticals, industrial infrastructure, modern retail, and quick commerce, supported by government infrastructure investments and rising rural incomes.

The centrepiece development is the definitive agreements signed with Galilei Holdings Co. Ltd (formerly Galilei Co. Ltd), a Japanese company listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, announced on 24th July, 2026. These comprise a share subscription agreement for Galilei to subscribe to equity shares for approximately INR 1,800 million through a preferential issue, plus INR 100 million from other investors; a shareholders’ agreement among the company, its promoters, and Galilei; and a joint venture agreement for a newly incorporated company in which Galilei will hold 60% and ICEMAKE 40%. The JV will manufacture, assemble, distribute, market, and sell commercial upright and table type refrigerators and their derivatives, successors, modifications, upgrades, or enhancements.

The stated uses of the fund raise include capacity expansion, modernization of existing operations, scaling of integrated solutions across the refrigeration and cold room value chain, evaluation of select inorganic growth opportunities including potential acquisitions in refrigeration, cold chain, and allied ecosystems, investment in the proposed JV, completion of a new corporate office, Centre of Excellence, and a state-of-the-art development and testing laboratory, and repayment and/or prepayment of certain existing borrowings to strengthen the balance sheet.

Operationally, the company reports strong revenue momentum, an order book of over Rs 220 crore, and growth in newer categories such as chest freezers and continuous panels. Management expects peak revenue potential from existing capacity to exceed Rs 950 crores after the first phase of capacity building.

Key Risks

The transaction is subject to Ice Make’s shareholder approval, in-principle approval from stock exchanges, and customary closing conditions, so completion is not assured at announcement.

Margin risk is evident in the gap between revenue growth and profitability: despite roughly 60% year-on-year consolidated revenue growth in Q1 FY27, the company recorded an EBITDA margin of 1.7% and a net loss. The company’s ability to restore margins depends in part on price hikes taking effect, which it has said it could not immediately pass through.

Foreign exchange fluctuation losses and indirect import impacts from dollar strengthening represent ongoing exposure. Depreciation from newly commissioned facilities and initial ramp-up expenses weigh on near-term results. The JV introduces execution and partnership risk, with production targeted only by February 2028.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Management expects EBITDA margins for the whole of FY27 to be around 6 to 6.5%, with margin improvement anticipated in the second half of FY27 following price hikes. Against this, Q1 FY27 actual EBITDA margin was 1.7%, indicating a substantial gap between the guided full-year range and the starting quarterly run rate.

Management targets production under the Galilei JV by February 2028, and expects peak revenue potential from existing capacity to exceed Rs 950 crores after the first phase of capacity building. For context, standalone revenue for FY 2025-26 reached Rs 668.41 crores, with EBITDA of Rs 43.73 crores and PAT of Rs 10.89 crores, and a final dividend of Rs 2.25 per equity share was recommended.

On the fund raise, the company has realized application or allotment money aggregating to INR 190,00,01,010 from allottees (Galilei Holdings Co. Ltd., Bhumi Jayeshkumar Patel, and Shweta Samir Patel) against the allotment of 23,67,573 equity shares on September 10, 2026, and September 07, 2026, with statutory auditors Umesh Shah & Associates certifying compliance with Regulation 169(4) of the SEBI ICDR Regulations. The shares were allotted on a preferential basis to non-promoter investors at an issue price of INR 802.51 per share. The board also approved subscribing to 3,52,70,000 equity shares of Ice Make Horeca Private Limited for INR 35,27,00,000, representing a 40% shareholding in the joint venture.

Management states it expects to continue strengthening capabilities, expanding market opportunities, advancing innovation, and delivering dependable refrigeration solutions to create sustainable value for all stakeholders.
