MGL: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Regulatory and tax disputes. MGL faces a long-running transportation tariff dispute with GAIL relating to ONGC’s Uran–Trombay Natural Gas Pipeline (UTNGPL). GAIL’s demand for the period November 2008 to July 2021 totalled Rs. 331.80 crore. The matter has moved through PNGRB, APTEL and the Delhi High Court; the High Court stayed recovery in December 2022 while directing MGL to deposit Rs. 50 crore with GAIL, which was deposited on February 14, 2023. The next hearing is scheduled for August 13, 2026. Separately, a GST demand of Rs. 54.33 crore plus penalty and interest was raised in January 2025 on reverse-charge mechanism liability for road reinstatement charges; the Commissioner (Appeals) confirmed the demand in August 2025, and MGL’s writ petition is pending, with the next hearing on August 17, 2026. In both matters MGL states it has strong legal opinions and does not expect outflow, so no provision has been recognised.

Cost and margin pressure. In the quarter ended June 30, 2026, revenue from operations rose to Rs. 2,597.89 crore from Rs. 2,280.44 crore a year earlier, but purchase of natural gas and traded items rose to Rs. 1,733.38 crore from Rs. 1,310.07 crore. Profit before tax fell to Rs. 259.07 crore from Rs. 432.22 crore, and profit after tax fell to Rs. 193.70 crore from Rs. 319.56 crore. Employee benefits expense also increased, to Rs. 46.34 crore from Rs. 37.07 crore.

Customer grievance load. Customer complaints rose to 39,488 in FY 2025-26 from 27,433 in FY 2024-25, with 3,436 pending at year-end; MGL reports resolving 91% of FY 2025-26 complaints, with the remainder to be closed in FY 2026-27.

Operational and asset risks. MGL identifies asset integrity and critical incident management as a material risk, given pipeline and compressor infrastructure, the inflammability of gas, and potential consequences including leakage, fire, explosion, injuries, operational disruption and regulatory non-compliance. End-use safety risk arises from customer-side awareness gaps. Data security risk arises from large volumes of household customer data, with potential penalties, fines and reputational damage. Climate-related events such as flooding, heavy rainfall and heatwaves can affect assets and service continuity.

Energy transition risk. Shifts toward electrification, renewable adoption and technological change may influence long-term natural gas demand; delayed adaptation could result in demand risk and under-utilised assets.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Policy tailwind for natural gas. India has committed to Net Zero by 2070, and natural gas is about 6.3% of the country’s energy mix with a target of 15% by 2030. MGL is scaling operations to promote natural gas adoption and supplies PNG and CNG at rates below competing LPG, petrol and diesel.

Clean-energy diversification. MGL signed a Shareholders’ Agreement with FPEL Reliant Energy Private Limited and FPEL Saur Vidyut Private Limited on March 8, 2026 to acquire a 26% stake in FPEL Reliant, strengthening its renewable energy presence; Rs. 3.89 crore was invested in the current quarter. MGL, GAIL and Reliance Industries entered a tripartite agreement on August 8, 2025 under India’s CBG–CGD synchronisation scheme. A Concession Agreement was signed with Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation on June 1, 2026 for a 350 TPD Compressed Biogas plant at Mankhurd.

LNG for heavy trucking. Mahanagar LNG Private Limited (MLPL), a joint venture with Baidyanath LNG Private Limited formed in December 2023, has commissioned two LNG stations — Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Maharashtra) and Seoni (Madhya Pradesh) — with plans for two more at Amravati and Bhiwandi by October 2026.

Electric mobility. Under a Share Subscription Agreement with 3EV Industries Private Limited dated February 12, 2024, amended May 20, 2026, MGL’s proposed investment is Rs. 96.00 crore in tranches; Rs. 83.99 crore has been invested as on date for a 26.13% equity stake on a fully diluted basis.

Energy efficiency and renewables. MGL replaced IE2 motors with IE3 motors across five CNG stations, yielding annual energy cost savings of about Rs. 16.1 lakh. It signed an agreement for 6.5 MW of renewable solar power through Green Open Access (Phase-1), with operations expected from July 2026. HVAC integration with solar at the Mahape office is implemented. A zero-lube packing modification by the OEM was carried out on 22 KPCL compressors, improving packing ring life and reducing packing losses.

Digital and customer initiatives. Project Prism went live on the Salesforce platform; paperless digital CNG joint ticketing was rolled out across OMC, COCO and DODO outlets; paperless billing from April 1, 2025 achieved close to 95% success.

Green certifications and infrastructure. CGS and CNG RO Vilebhagad achieved IGBC Platinum rating. Green certification for 50% of office buildings and facilities completed Phase I, with Phase II site visits by the Indian Green Building Council in February 2026. STPs were installed at CGS Mahape, Taloja, Savroli and Ambernath, and rainwater harvesting at CGS Mahape, Taloja, Savroli and Ambernath.

CSR and community. Nineteen CSR project agreements for FY 2026-27 were signed before March 31, 2026. CSR projects reported 100% beneficiaries from vulnerable and marginalised groups across numerous programmes.

3. Key Risks

  • Asset integrity and critical incident management — pipeline and compressor infrastructure; mitigation includes an Asset Integrity Policy and Integrity Management Plan, risk audit framework, defect assessment and repair policy, Management of Change and Non-Routine Operation procedures, and mock drills under the Emergency Response and Disaster Management Plan.
  • Human rights — non-adherence could cause operational delays, lawsuits, reduced employee satisfaction, lost opportunities and reputational harm; mitigated through a Human Rights Policy, Code of Conduct and periodic affirmations.
  • Occupational health and safety — excavation, pipeline maintenance and traffic-area work expose employees and contract workers to hazards; MGL is ISO 45001:2018 certified with HIRA, PPE, Safety and Technical Competency training and health surveillance.
  • End-use safety — customer-side awareness gaps; mitigation includes pressure reduction to 21 millibar at cooking stoves, awareness campaigns, safety sessions and after-sales service.
  • Data security — Data Privacy Framework based on DPDP Act 2023, DLP, MDM and SOC implementation, vendor risk assessments, masking of mobile numbers and email IDs.
  • Enterprise risk management — policy framework covering operational and business risks, with a risk management tool.
  • Climate adaptation and mitigation — physical climate risks may raise maintenance and repair costs.
  • Energy transition and business model resilience — delayed adaptation may create demand risk and under-utilised assets.
  • Corporate governance — governance failures may attract regulatory action and investor concerns.
  • Digitalisation and cybersecurity of gas operations — greater reliance on SCADA, automation and smart meters increases cyber exposure; controls include network segregation, vulnerability assessments and incident response, with OT security upgradation under evaluation.

Safety performance in FY 2025-26 showed zero lost time injuries, zero recordable work-related injuries and zero fatalities for both employees and workers, compared with a worker LTIFR of 0.083 and three recordable worker injuries in FY 2024-25. No complaints were reported on sexual harassment, discrimination, child labour, forced labour or wages in FY 2025-26. No instances of data breaches were reported.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Green certification targets. MGL set a target to obtain green certifications for 50% of office buildings and facilities by FY 2025-26. Phase I was completed, and under Phase II physical site visits for Nahur and Seawoods offices were conducted by the Indian Green Building Council on February 25, 2026. CGS Vile Bhagad was awarded IGBC Platinum rating.

CBG plant target. A 1000 TPD CBG plant was targeted for Mumbai by FY 2026-27. A Concession Agreement between MGL and BMC was signed on June 1, 2026 for a 350 TPD CBG plant at Mankhurd.

Compressor efficiency target. Upgradation of motors with IE3 was planned at 5 CNG ROs by FY 2025-26. Upgradation to IE3 efficiency motors has been completed at five CNG retail outlets.

Pressure reduction skid target. Power generation through a pressure reduction skid at CGS Taloja was targeted by FY 2025-26. The project has been taken up as an R&D project, and installation is expected by December 2026.

ESG awareness target. Conducting ESG awareness sessions for the remaining 350 employees was targeted for FY 2025-26. Sessions covered 387 employees during FY 2025-26, with total participation of 566.

Solar canopy target. Installation of a solar canopy at Vile Bhagad and Usarghar CNG RO was targeted by FY 2025-26. Installation of solar panels on the admin and sales building at Vile Bhagad RO was completed; for Usarghar, initial PESO approval was received on May 8, 2026, so installation is expected by December 2026.

Financial performance versus prior periods. For FY 2025-26 (audited), revenue from operations was Rs. 9,059.77 crore, total income Rs. 9,178.97 crore, profit before tax Rs. 1,140.52 crore, profit after tax Rs. 846.82 crore, and basic and diluted EPS Rs. 85.73. In the quarter ended June 30, 2026, revenue from operations was Rs. 2,597.89 crore, profit before tax Rs. 259.07 crore, profit after tax Rs. 193.70 crore, and EPS Rs. 19.61, compared with revenue of Rs. 2,280.44 crore, profit before tax of Rs. 432.22 crore, profit after tax of Rs. 319.56 crore and EPS of Rs. 32.35 in the quarter ended June 30, 2025.

Amalgamation. Unison Enviro Private Limited, a wholly owned subsidiary, was amalgamated with MGL pursuant to a scheme sanctioned by the NCLT Mumbai bench on July 9, 2025 (certified copy received July 23, 2025), with appointed date February 1, 2024. Comparative financial information for the quarter ended June 30, 2025 has been restated to give effect to the amalgamation.

Assurance. MGL engaged M/s SGS India Pvt. Ltd. for external assurance, obtaining Reasonable Assurance on BRSR Core parameters, with the assurance statement dated July 13, 2026.

Broker Narrative

Early coverage was bullish on MGL, with Buy calls underpinned by falling spot-LNG costs, record margins, and strong CNG/PNG growth; the last report moved to Accumulate as the narrative shifted to supply constraints and cost inflation. The persistent theme was gas allocation/security: the early fear of lower APM allocation became explicit 80% domestic-gas curtailment by 2026. Growth optimism remained on domestic PNG penetration and network expansion, but was increasingly tempered by partial cost pass-through, EV adoption, and higher capex/debt concerns.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning of lower APM/domestic gas allocation materialized: the last report cites curtailed domestic gas allocation to 80% of average consumption, which hurt PNG-I/C volumes and coincided with a long run of DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Volume-weakness fears resurfaced at the segment level: by Q1FY27 PNG-I/C volumes were down 9.9% QoQ and 7.2% YoY due to constrained gas supply, matching negative actual returns around those reports.
  • The risk that reduced allocation would raise gas costs proved correct: gas cost climbed to INR39.9/scm and was not fully passed on, compressing margins and contributing to negative surprises.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of durable margin expansion from falling input gas costs failed: the record Rs18.7/scm gross margin did not persist, and EBITDA/scm fell to Rs7.9 by the last report as gas costs rose.
  • The projected industrial PNG growth in Raigad did not materialize as hoped: PNG-I/C volumes declined 9.9% QoQ and 7.2% YoY in Q1FY27 due to gas supply constraints, alongside negative actual returns.
  • APM pricing-policy reform did not fully protect pricing power: the last report notes recent gas-cost increases were not fully passed on, undermining the earlier stability/protection thesis and leading to DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
