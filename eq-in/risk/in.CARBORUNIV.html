Carborundum Universal — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost inflation in Abrasives. The US–Iran conflict that began in end-February 2026 saw a truce, then a re-escalation. April brought a nominal cost push; from May onwards the cost push became significant. The overall cost push was around INR 16 crores, concentrated in oil-linked inputs such as resins and related products, plus fuel. Oil reportedly touched USD 117 in May before easing to about USD 107. Grains and related materials (80–85% of Abrasives cost of goods sold) rose only 3–5% and were largely offset by normal price increases, so the pressure came from the non-grain, oil-based portion.

Abrasives profitability compression. Standalone Abrasives PBIT fell from INR 37 crores in Q1 FY26 to INR 34 crores in Q1 FY27, with margin down from 13.1% to 10.4%. Sequentially, PBIT dropped from INR 60 crores to INR 34 crores and margin from 17.1% to 10.4%. Management attributed roughly INR 15–16 crores of PBIT drop to cost push and INR 8–9 crores to volume decline, with Q1 seasonally weaker than Q4. Against FY26 full-year standalone Abrasives PBIT margin of 15.3%, the quarter represented a sharp step down.

Loss-making overseas operations being wound down. CUMI Awuko Abrasives GmbH (Germany) reported a loss after tax of EUR 1.69 million in Q1 FY27 versus EUR 0.9 million in Q1 FY26, though better than EUR 2.63 million in Q4 FY26 excluding exceptional items. Its holding company approved voluntary winding-up on 30 March 2026, with INR 119 crores of exceptional items recognised in Q4 FY26. Management aims to complete the process “in a quarter or so.” Foskor Zirconia (Pty) Ltd, South Africa, was determined commercially unviable due to sustained losses, rising input costs and marketing pressures; its board concluded on 13 May 2026 that it could not continue operations. Foskor sales fell 11.7% year-on-year to ZAR 107 million and 14.7% sequentially. Consolidated Electrominerals PBIT of INR 22 crores absorbed a INR 17 crores loss from Foskor. Management expects to reach a solution within a quarter.

VAW Russia sanctions overhang. VAW remains on the US OFAC Specially Designated Nationals list (added per a US Department of State press release dated 10 January 2025). Its US property and interests are blocked, and most US-currency and US-person transactions are not possible without authorisation. An impairment of INR 10,413 lakhs was recognised in prior years; no additional impairment was deemed necessary in the current period. Cash and cash equivalents of INR 36,090 lakhs at VAW are not available for use by other group entities due to temporary repatriation restrictions. VAW sales fell 14.1% year-on-year in ruble terms to RUB 1.58 billion, though they rose 24.1% sequentially; PAT fell to RUB 52.1 million from RUB 71.5 million. Management said it is “too difficult to predict” the geopolitical outcome and the aim is to stay put, comply with laws and local rules, serve the domestic market and “stay above the water.”

Ceramics margin pressure despite growth. Standalone Ceramics PBIT was flat at INR 59 crores even as revenue rose 15.2%, held back by fuel and alumina cost increases and unabsorbed costs in new semiconductor and aerospace & defence capacity lines. Consolidated Ceramics PBIT was broadly flat year-on-year at INR 74 crores.

Foreign exchange and other income swings. Standalone unallocable expense was INR 17 crores in Q1 FY27 versus unallocable income of INR 60 crores in Q1 FY26 and INR 11 crores in Q4 FY26, driven by dividend income in comparative periods and a INR 3.3 crores forex loss in the current quarter versus forex gains earlier. Consolidated unallocable expenses rose to INR 19 crores from INR 7.6 crores and INR 9.8 crores, attributed to forex loss and lower interest income.

Chinese import competition and unhelpful exchange rates. Management noted import competition from China had only “just started” easing, with multiple moving factors — possible withdrawal of Chinese export benefits and unfavourable exchange rates — making the cause unclear.

Seasonality. Q1 is generally a lower quarter than Q4 for both Abrasives and Refractories; refractories is project-based and saw a sequential drop.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Broad-based standalone growth. Standalone sales rose 21.2% year-on-year to INR 846 crores, with Electrominerals up 33%, Ceramics up 15.2% and Abrasives up 14.7%. Growth was predominantly volume-driven with very small price increases.

Electrominerals momentum. Standalone Electrominerals sales grew 33% year-on-year and 12.2% sequentially, largely volume-driven and export-centric, with product mix (higher treated product share) aiding margins. Export salience rose from roughly 20% historically to about 35% last year and is on a 40% trajectory. Management cited long-standing qualification efforts with European players, with similar efforts underway in the US and Asia, and noted European duties on Chinese alumina imports as a helpful opportunity. Standalone Electrominerals PBIT jumped to INR 39 crores from INR 7 crores year-on-year and INR 28 crores sequentially.

Ceramics guidance upgrade. Management raised consolidated Ceramics growth guidance to 23–25% from 15–15.5%, citing growth optimism across segments except wear ceramics (expected to grow normally), with improved momentum in Metallized Cylinders, Engineered Ceramics and SOFC ceramics. Growth is predominantly export-driven. After several quarters, Wear Ceramics also grew in the quarter.

New capacity programmes. Advanced ceramics for power electronics including substrates, metallized tubes, rings and brazed assemblies; expansion of brown-fused alumina; an integrated furnace facility for thermal spray powders; and a zirconia furnace are part of the INR 400 crores FY27 capex plan. The metallized substrate facility is being set up with an anchor customer and technology tie-up, with revenue expected from FY28. Semiconductor wafer fabrication equipment components are already being supplied post-qualification; FY27 will be small, FY28 should pick up, and management expects a peak in FY30. Aerospace and defence should be slightly better this year, with the programme kicking in from FY28.

Metallized cylinder structural demand. Management sees the demand trend from generation and distribution, coupled with changing technology choices (switchgear moving from gas insulators to vacuum interrupters), as lasting for some time.

Refractories and NBSiC acquisition. The silicon carbide products LLC acquisition is seen as helping secure critical businesses in NBSiC refractories for wear- and impact-based applications, and as an anchor into America. Refractory capacity expansion programmes are on track with no issues seen.

Rhodius improvement. Rhodius sales rose 18% year-on-year in euro terms to EUR 15.6 million and 33.9% in INR terms to INR 172 crores, helped by INR depreciation against the euro. Its loss after tax narrowed to EUR 0.7 million from EUR 1.6 million.

Sterling Abrasives gain. Q1 FY27 consolidated Abrasives PBIT of INR 40 crores included a INR 25 crores gain from Sterling Abrasives Limited arising from transfer of leasehold rights of immovable property and related buildings.

Strong balance sheet. Consolidated debt-to-equity was 0.05, with Q1 FY27 consolidated capex of INR 53–54 crores.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued uncertainty around VAW Russia from international sanctions and territory embargoes, with the impact assessment described as a continuing process requiring ongoing monitoring of material changes to the internal and external environment.
  • Repatriation restrictions trapping INR 36,090 lakhs of VAW cash and cash equivalents.
  • Execution and timing risk on the Awuko and Foskor closures, both targeted for resolution within about a quarter, with updates promised at the next quarterly meeting.
  • Commodity and fuel cost volatility tied to the geopolitical situation, which management said changes daily and makes pricing decisions difficult to model beyond one quarter at a time.
  • Forex volatility affecting unallocable income/expense lines.
  • Unabsorbed costs in new semiconductor and aerospace & defence capacity lines until volumes ramp.
  • Qualification and ramp-up risk in new businesses (metallized substrates, semiconductor fab equipment ceramics, aerospace and defence), where revenue is expected mainly from FY28 onwards.
  • Chinese import competition in Abrasives, where the easing trend is not yet firmly established.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Consolidated sales. Guidance was approximately 4–4.5% growth for FY27; excluding Foskor Zirconia and Awuko revenue (INR 343 crores in FY26), comparable growth was guided at 11–12%. Management now says growth could be 15% without Foskor and Awuko sales in both periods, given current order load and customer forecasts. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated sales grew 16.9% year-on-year to INR 1,411 crores and 2% sequentially.

Consolidated Abrasives sales. Guidance of 5.5–6%, or 11–12% excluding Awuko revenue of about INR 108 crores, was retained. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated Abrasives sales grew 20.1% year-on-year to INR 610 crores and were flat sequentially.

Consolidated Ceramics sales. Guidance was raised from 15–15.5% to 23–25%. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated Ceramics sales grew 16.5% year-on-year to INR 349 crores and fell 0.7% sequentially.

Consolidated Electrominerals sales. Guidance of a 6.5–7% decline (reflecting Foskor’s INR 235 crores FY26 revenue), or 8–9% growth excluding Foskor, potentially 9–10%, was retained. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated Electrominerals sales grew 16.8% year-on-year to INR 473 crores and 10.7% sequentially.

Consolidated Abrasives margins. Guidance of 9.5–10% was retained, against FY26 reported margin of 4.3% or 7.9% excluding the Awuko loss. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated Abrasives PBIT of INR 40 crores included the INR 25 crores Sterling gain; excluding it, the implied margin was around 2.5%, which management addressed by noting Awuko losses are still present in the quarter and reiterating the guidance.

Consolidated Ceramics margins. Guidance of 20.5–21% was maintained. Management said it expects to be at the higher end of guidance for the full year. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated Ceramics PBIT was INR 74 crores, broadly flat year-on-year and up 19.1% sequentially.

Consolidated Electrominerals margins. Guidance of 9–9.5% was retained, against FY26 reported margin of 5.6% or 9.1% excluding the Foskor loss. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated Electrominerals PBIT was INR 22 crores, including a INR 17 crores Foskor loss.

Rhodius. Guidance of about 5% growth in FY27 with a small PAT-level loss was maintained. Observed Q1 FY27 sales grew 18% year-on-year in euro terms, with a loss after tax of EUR 0.7 million.

Capex. Guidance of about INR 400 crores for FY27 was retained. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated capex was INR 53 crores (INR 54 crores per the press release), against INR 64 crores in Q1 FY26.

Overall. Management stated that all four businesses are doing well at standalone level, the closure programmes for the two businesses are well on track, long-term strategy programmes are being pursued, and capex programmes are proceeding as planned. Management acknowledged the growth rate targeted and communicated last quarter is now slightly higher, with more clarity expected next quarter.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative shifted from an abrasives-led industrial growth thesis (2023) centered on PLI-driven capex, acquisitions (AWUKO/RHODIUS), ceramics exports and electrominerals to a defense-focused story (2026) built on Atmanirbhar Bharat, Positive Indigenisation Lists and export growth. Import dependence persisted as a theme — first from Chinese abrasives competition (~30% of market), then from foreign defense equipment. Energy/fuel cost pressures and subsidiary underperformance were recurring concerns throughout, while the growth optimism migrated from industrial consumables demand to defense indigenisation.

Fears that came true

  • Acquisition integration costs and goodwill write-offs from AWUKO/RHODIUS materialized, with AWUKO posting a EUR3.7mn loss, correlating with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in late 2023 and early 2024.
  • Subsidiary challenges including fixed-cost burdens, logistics issues and cost pressures persisted, contributing to negative actual returns across multiple DISAPPOINTMENT calls.
  • Import competition from Chinese products (~30% of the Indian abrasives market) pressured margins, evidenced by repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes from Buy/Accumulate calls in 2023-2024.
  • Energy and fuel costs remained a significant drag on profitability throughout the period, weighing on EBIT margins as flagged from the outset.

Optimism that failed

  • AWUKO/RHODIUS were expected to add coated/bonded capacity and Europe-America reach to drive CUMI growth, but instead generated losses and integration costs, aligning with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • The industrial consumables market’s projected 9-10% CAGR on PLI-driven manufacturing capex uplift did not translate into positive stock returns, as multiple Buy/Accumulate calls resulted in DISAPPOINTMENT.
  • CUMI Industrial Ceramics’ export-led growth and value-added product expansion (SOFC, hydrogen, composites) failed to deliver expected outperformance, with the company pivoting to a defense thesis by 2026 instead.
