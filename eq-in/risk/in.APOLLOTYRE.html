Apollo Tyres Ltd (APOLLOTYRES) Analysis

Overview: Apollo Tyres reported strong Q2 FY26 results (ended Sep 30, 2025) with consolidated revenue up 6% YoY to ₹68.3 Bn (highest growth in 10 quarters), driven by India (6% YoY) and partial Europe recovery. EBITDA margin expanded to 14.9% (vs. 13.6% YoY), aided by GST rationalization, stable raw materials, and operating leverage. PAT was muted at ₹2.6 Bn due to exceptional restructuring costs (₹1.8 Bn in Q2 from Europe plant closure). Net debt stable at ₹26 Bn (0.8x EBITDA). Management outlook remains optimistic for H2 growth, premiumization, and profitability.

Below is a structured analysis of headwinds, tailwinds, growth prospects, and key risks, derived from the Q2 FY26 earnings, financials, analyst transcript, and disclosures.

Tailwinds (Positive Catalysts)
  • Robust India Demand & GST Boost: 6% YoY revenue growth; mid-single digit volume rise (replacement +2%, OEM +4%). Farm/2W/3W segments strong; October demand accelerated post-monsoon/infra rebound. GST cuts (7-8%) passed on but unlocked rural volumes (expected mid-high single digits in H2).
  • Margin Expansion: Consolidated EBITDA 14.9% (+130 bps YoY); India at 15.3%. Raw materials down 3% QoQ, expected stable/slightly down in Q3 (NR ₹210/kg, SR ₹175/kg). Favorable mix from premium Vredestein (highest Q2 volumes, 10k tyres/month).
  • Brand & Premiumization Momentum: Indian Cricket Team sponsorship to penetrate rural (agri/truck/car/2W). Podium finishes in Europe tests; new OEM wins (German marques). UHP mix up to 49% in Europe.
  • Balance Sheet Strength: Net debt flat YoY (India 1.1x EBITDA); free cash flow focus. Capex guided steady.
  • Sustainability Edge: ESG score up to 58/100; awards in CSR/safety.
Headwinds (Challenges)
  • Europe Weakness: 4% YoY revenue growth (volume-led, no pricing) amid soft demand (PCR stable, Spacemaster OEM decline). EBITDA margin 12.7% (below targets); restructuring drags (Enschede closure: additional €17 Mn exceptional hit; total ~€55 Mn cash cost, payback ~2 yrs).
  • Competitive Pressure: TBR replacement share stabilized at ~29% (after prior losses), PCR at 20% (industry-leading). New entrants (e.g., OHT-focused players) may intensify pricing/margins in TBR/PCR; no aggressive price cuts planned.
  • Minor Regulatory Hit: Customs ADD demand + penalty (₹12.69 Lacs); appeal planned, no material impact.
  • Demand Muted in Segments: Truck/PCR OEM soft; Sep GST transition caused 1-month destocking (now restocked).

Cricket sponsorship unlocks rural goldmine post-GST. Bear Case: Europe drags + competition erodes TBR share + raw mat spike caps multiples. Broker Narrative

The narrative started with a near-term recovery Buy thesis built on India replacement pickup, raw-material softening, deleveraging and a capex-light model. By the last report, the Buy thesis had shifted to pricing power and EU restructuring, with raw-material inflation (17% Q1FY27, +8% expected) and higher FY27-FY29 capex/FCF drag as the central challenges. Persistent themes were European/agri-tyre weakness, market-share gains and healthy utilisation; the main reversals were the RM cycle and capital-allocation story.

Fears that came true

  • The early European agri-tyre inventory concern materialised as an outright decline in agricultural tyres and the Dutch plant closure/production loss, which coincided with several later negative actual returns and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • The early weak-global-macro softness flagged alongside exports persisted in parts, as US tyre demand remained muted and macro/demand moderation was still a downside risk in the last report.

Optimism that failed

  • The raw-material softening/margin-expansion tailwind failed: the RM basket rose 17% in Q1FY27 and was expected to rise another 8%, while Europe price hikes were insufficient to offset it.
  • The capex-light/strong-FCF tailwind did not persist: the last report flagged higher capex intensity and free-cash-flow drag for FY27-FY29, reversing the earlier disciplined capital-allocation optimism.
