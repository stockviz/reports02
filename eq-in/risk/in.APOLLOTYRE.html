Headwinds and Challenges

  • Raw material inflation: In Q1 FY27, raw material costs escalated sharply by nearly 17%. Management expects a further sequential raw material inflation of about 8% into Q2, largely led by natural rubber, with average natural rubber prices moving from around INR 225 in Q1 to upwards of INR 260 in Q2.
  • Margin compression: Consolidated EBITDA margin fell to 11.7%, down about 150 basis points year-on-year, primarily on raw material cost pressures. India Operations EBITDA margin was 12% versus 13.6% a year earlier. Europe EBITDA margin was 8.9% versus 10.8%.
  • Geopolitical uncertainty in West Asia: This continued to create headwinds in select international markets, with heightened uncertainty and cost volatility across raw materials, energy and logistics. Commodity prices are expected to remain volatile until the geopolitical situation stabilises.
  • Europe transition costs and revenue loss: The Enschede (Netherlands) plant stopped production as planned in June 2026. Overlapping costs during the closure and transition weighed on Europe margins. Revenue was also impacted by the Enschede closure transition, the agri tyre transition, and the shifting of truck radial capacity from Hungary to India during a period of strong demand.
  • Export market softness: West Asia geographies were behind budget due to the war. The U.S. market underperformed expectations, with dealer inventory not moving off the shelf and hence reduced buying.
  • Agri business disruption in Europe: Agri business declined by high teens sequentially and a small single-digit decline year-on-year. About 20–25% of the agri business is being serviced through offtake, and management expects at least a couple of quarters to reach a stable situation.
  • Pricing gap versus cost inflation: In India, about 7–9% of price increases were implemented in Q1 against a requirement of roughly 15–16%, with only 3–4% flowing into revenue due to staggered implementation. In Europe, about 10% was needed for Q1–Q2, but only 3–4% had been implemented.
  • Capacity tightness: India capacity utilisation is running in the 90s, and management expects to remain very tight on capacity through FY27.
  • Rising leverage: CapEx has started (INR 500 crores in India in Q1; INR 650 crores consolidated), and management anticipates the net debt-to-EBITDA ratio will rise slightly in the current year, with the company becoming a net borrower.
  • Leadership transition: CFO Gaurav Kumar announced his departure after 22-plus years, stating he had completed the Enschede project and decided it was the appropriate time to leave.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong consolidated and India growth: Consolidated top line grew 12.8% year-on-year to INR 74 billion. India Operations recorded their highest ever revenue at INR 54.6 billion, up 15.6% year-on-year and 4.3% sequentially — the strongest year-on-year quarterly growth in 14 quarters.
  • Broad-based volume growth in India: Replacement grew 13%, OEM 10%, and exports 15%. All product categories except TBB delivered double-digit growth.
  • Healthy demand outlook: Demand remains healthy across categories and channels. July delivered a strong start, providing confidence that momentum will carry into Q2. Management noted demand has been surprisingly strong for a sustained period, with year-on-year growth continuing to be strong even in the seasonally weaker Q2.
  • Pricing actions underway: Further price increases into Q2 have been announced, with current cumulative increases of about 9% in TBR and 11% in other categories. One increase was announced in July (about 1–2%), with a similar quantum expected in August. Full impact of the five-odd price increases is expected to flow into H2 revenues.
  • Europe restructuring benefits: Financial and operational benefits of the restructuring are expected to start materialising from H2 of the current fiscal year. Management aims to deliver high-teens EBITDA in Europe on a full year basis once the manufacturing footprint is right. Excluding overlap costs, Europe would have been at about 11% margin levels.
  • Europe PCR replacement momentum: The PCR replacement segment continues to deliver healthy growth, with double-digit growth in the quarter, well ahead of the market, and positive momentum expected to sustain.
  • Anti-dumping duty on Chinese tyres: Expected to be positive, as Chinese tyres were taking share at the lower end. The Vredestein brand does not compete with Chinese tyres, but domestic players are expected to benefit.
  • Capacity expansion: Hungary capacity is being expanded by 4,000 passenger car tyres per day (from about 17,000 to 21,000), starting production and ramping up in H2. India capacity will start coming on stream towards the end of the year and ramp up through FY28, with double the capacity addition of Hungary.
  • Raw material relief expected: Rubber is not impacted by the West Asia crisis, and management expects rubber to start cooling down from Q3 onwards as the seasonal impact goes off. Crude basket benefits (including synthetic rubber) are expected with a quarter lag, providing a further boost from RM decline into Q3. Management noted the industry has historically recorded its best margins in a falling raw material scenario.
  • Strategic initiatives: Progress in R&D with OEM approvals and nominations including multiple EV platforms; global S/4HANA programme and scaled AI deployment in manufacturing delivering productivity, energy efficiency and cost benefits; brand campaigns including the ICC Women’s T20 World Cup generating over 220 million consumer reach and 500 million views.
  • Sustainability recognition: Exceeded FY26 environmental commitments, strengthened FY30 roadmap, and was recognised among India’s top 30 most sustainable companies. NSE Sustainability Ratings and Analytics Limited assigned an ESG rating of ‘68’ for FY2026.
  • Market share gains: TBR replacement market share is now upwards of 30%, and passenger car replacement is 21% plus, with some lost share regained.
  • Balance sheet strength: Net debt to EBITDA at consolidated level was 0.4, with absolute net debt of about INR 1,700 plus crores.

Key Risks

  • Continued volatility in commodity prices, particularly natural rubber, until the West Asia geopolitical situation stabilises.
  • Potential demand moderation in the replacement market for trucks, buses and farm segments due to a potential El Nino impact and a high base in the second half.
  • Possible ASP correction downwards in Q4 if raw material prices fall significantly amid a weaker demand scenario, depending on the magnitude of the RM decline and demand conditions.
  • Execution risk in the Europe transition, including stabilising the agri offtake arrangement, which management expects will take at least a couple of quarters.
  • Capacity constraints in India, with utilisation in the 90s and tight capacity expected through FY27.
  • Rising leverage as CapEx accelerates, with the company expected to become a net borrower and the net debt-to-EBITDA ratio rising slightly.
  • OEM pricing negotiations remain difficult, with OEMs facing their own pressures, and price increases from OEMs expected to roll in from Q2 and Q3.
  • Domestic natural rubber prices remain highly elevated despite international prices starting to cool down, though management expects price parity to eventually force domestic prices down.
  • Leadership transition risk following the CFO’s departure.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • India pricing: Management stated that in Q1, about 7–9% of price increases were implemented in a staggered manner, but only 3–4% flowed into revenue. This is consistent with the observed 15.6% India revenue growth being described as largely volume-led (about 12% from volume).
  • Raw material inflation: Management guided to about 8% sequential raw material inflation into Q2, with natural rubber averaging upwards of INR 260 versus INR 225 in Q1. This is a forward-looking statement; observed Q1 raw material escalation was nearly 17%.
  • Europe margins: Management stated that without the overlap costs, Europe would have been at about 11% margin levels, versus the observed 8.9% reported margin. Management aims for high-teens EBITDA in Europe on a full year basis once the footprint transition is complete.
  • Europe restructuring benefits: Management guided that financial and operational benefits of the restructuring would start materialising from H2 of the current fiscal year. The Enschede plant stopped production as planned in June 2026, and the transition is expected to be completed in September–October 2026.
  • Capacity expansion: Management guided that Hungary would start producing and ramping up in H2, going from about 17,000 to 21,000 car tyres, while India capacity would start coming on stream towards the end of the year and ramp up through FY28.
  • Leverage: Management guided that the net debt-to-EBITDA ratio would go up slightly in the current year and that the company would be a net borrower. Observed Q1 consolidated net debt to EBITDA was 0.4, with absolute net debt of about INR 1,700 plus crores.
  • Raw material outlook: Management guided that rubber would start cooling down from Q3 onwards as the seasonal impact goes off, and that crude basket benefits would flow with a quarter lag, providing a further boost from RM decline into Q3.
  • Demand outlook: Management guided that replacement momentum is strong and that good year-on-year growth should be delivered despite Q2 being seasonally weaker. Observed July sales volume increase year-on-year continued to be very strong.
  • Agri offtake: Management guided that about 20–25% of the agri business would be through offtake and that at least a couple of quarters would be needed to reach a stable situation. Observed agri business declined by high teens sequentially and a small single-digit decline year-on-year.
  • Advertisement and sales promotion: Management stated that the major reduction in other expenses was on advertisement and sales promotion, reduced by almost 50%, and that no further reduction should be expected, with quarters where spending would increase.

Broker Narrative

The narrative started with a near-term recovery Buy thesis built on India replacement pickup, raw-material softening, deleveraging and a capex-light model. By the last report, the Buy thesis had shifted to pricing power and EU restructuring, with raw-material inflation (17% Q1FY27, +8% expected) and higher FY27-FY29 capex/FCF drag as the central challenges. Persistent themes were European/agri-tyre weakness, market-share gains and healthy utilisation; the main reversals were the RM cycle and capital-allocation story.

Fears that came true

  • The early European agri-tyre inventory concern materialised as an outright decline in agricultural tyres and the Dutch plant closure/production loss, which coincided with several later negative actual returns and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • The early weak-global-macro softness flagged alongside exports persisted in parts, as US tyre demand remained muted and macro/demand moderation was still a downside risk in the last report.

Optimism that failed

  • The raw-material softening/margin-expansion tailwind failed: the RM basket rose 17% in Q1FY27 and was expected to rise another 8%, while Europe price hikes were insufficient to offset it.
  • The capex-light/strong-FCF tailwind did not persist: the last report flagged higher capex intensity and free-cash-flow drag for FY27-FY29, reversing the earlier disciplined capital-allocation optimism.
