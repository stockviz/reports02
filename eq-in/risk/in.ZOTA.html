Zota Health Care — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure from input costs. Gross margin declined sequentially from 63.46% in Q4 FY26 to 61.96% in Q1 FY27. Management attributed this to higher input costs arising from the ongoing geopolitical situation and war, citing packaging and bottle costs specifically. Management expects this pressure to persist for one or two quarters, with selective price increases and MRP revisions intended to offset it.

Sharp rise in other expenses. Other expenses rose to roughly 33% of revenue in Q1 FY27 versus about 21% in Q1 FY26. Management attributed this primarily to marketing investment following the onboarding of Mahendra Singh Dhoni as brand ambassador, with approximately INR 15–17 crore spent on marketing in Q1 alone against roughly INR 18 crore for the entire prior financial year. Management also noted that brand ambassador fees are paid in tranches and advertising film production is largely one-time, so the spend is not evenly distributed across quarters.

Cash losses. The company reported a PBT loss of approximately INR 43–44 crore for the quarter. Adjusting for depreciation and Ind AS impacts of roughly INR 14–15 crore, underlying cash loss was around INR 30 crore; excluding approximately INR 10 crore of incremental marketing spend, underlying cash loss was around INR 20 crore.

Reported EBITDA swung negative. EBITDA moved from positive INR 1,191.17 lakh in Q4 FY26 to negative INR 955.40 lakh in Q1 FY27. On a pre-Ind AS basis, operating profit was negative INR 2,985.83 lakh (negative 17% margin), with the Davaindia vertical at negative 19.58% and Everyday Herbal at negative 35.49%.

Store maturation drag. Roughly 1,000 stores (803 opened in FY26 plus about 200 added in Q1 FY27) remain in the maturation phase. The FY26 cohort generated average monthly revenue of about INR 1.60–1.61 lakh per store and is still loss-making. The FY25 cohort of 617 stores averaged about INR 2.28 lakh per month and is broadly above breakeven. Management noted dispersion within cohorts — around 10% of stores below average and around 10% above.

GMV-to-revenue conversion gap. The conversion ratio fell to about 87% in Q1 FY27 from roughly 93–94% in the prior two quarters. Management attributed this to FOFO stores, where GMV is recorded on store-level invoices while revenue reflects sales to franchisees; the gap may widen as store-level invoicing becomes more streamlined. For COCO stores there is no difference between GMV and revenue.

Moderated expansion. Management stated it intends to moderate the pace of store expansion during Q2 FY27 to focus on productivity, monitoring operating trends at recently added stores, and strengthening execution.

Annual report risk disclosures. The company noted it may incur losses due to uncontrollable reasons resulting in inadequate profits to pay managerial remuneration, and that it has incurred huge capital expenditure along with operational and working capital expenses for the DAVAINDIA project.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong revenue growth. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations grew 67.59% year-on-year to INR 17,360.23 lakh, driven by Davaindia network expansion and growth in existing stores. Davaindia sales were INR 14,342.33 lakh versus INR 7,562.90 lakh in Q1 FY26.

Network expansion. The company added 264 new stores in Q1 FY27 (201 COCO, 63 FOFO) and closed 18 (2 COCO, 16 FOFO), a net addition of 246, taking the total to 2,825 stores as of June 30, 2026 (1,855 COCO, 970 FOFO) across 23 states and 5 union territories. Management reaffirmed a full-year target of 600–650 store additions, expecting Q2 to be slower with additions picking up in Q3 and Q4.

Footfall and wallet share gains. Quarterly footfall rose to approximately 60 lakh from about 35 lakh in Q1 FY26. Average wallet spend increased to INR 262 for COCO (from INR 231) and INR 306 for FOFO (from INR 281). Total GMV reached INR 16,402 lakh.

Gross margin expansion year-on-year. Consolidated gross margin improved from 56.43% in Q1 FY26 to 61.96% in Q1 FY27.

Mature store economics. Stores opened between 2021 and 2024 (234 stores) generated average monthly GMV of approximately INR 4.13 lakh per store, with store-level EBITDA margins typically 12–15%. Management described a maturation path: breakeven at around INR 2.5 lakh monthly revenue within 12–18 months, then roughly INR 1 lakh per month revenue increase annually, with EBITDA margins improving around 10–12 percentage points per year — reaching roughly 3–4% in year two, 12–15% in year three, 20–22% in year four, and around 30% by year five. Management noted newer stores are reaching maturity in 12–15 months versus the earlier 15–18 months.

Ecosystem investments. The company increased its investment in Davaindia Health Mart Limited through a rights issue (39,794 equity shares), continued supporting KMHP Ventures Limited and Curexis Ventures Private Limited (UGO Generic and SKIA formats), acquired an 80% stake in Globotask IT Consultancy Services Private Limited for internal IT capability and data security, and raised its Everyday Herbal Beauty Care stake to 87.78% to support backward integration. Management stated UGO and SKIA are in early rollout and expects measurable progress from next quarter.

Brand ambassadors. Suniel Shetty and Mahendra Singh Dhoni represent Davaindia; Akshay Kumar represents Zota Health Care and UGO Generic.

Industry headroom. Management cited approximately 18–19 lakh pharmacies in India against roughly 14,000–15,000 organized retail players excluding Jan Aushadhi, describing substantial room for expansion, with cluster-based, state-by-state approaches rather than a uniform city target.

Annual report outlook. Management stated that with increasing numbers of matured COCO stores and sales volume, the company should be able to taper the effect of expenses, and performance is expected to improve further in coming years.

3. Key Risks

  • Input cost and geopolitical risk: Margin pressure from war-related input cost inflation, expected to last one or two quarters, with recovery dependent on price increases and normalization.
  • Execution risk in maturation: Approximately 1,000 stores remain below maturity; their ramp-up over the next two to three quarters underpins the profitability trajectory.
  • Marketing spend uncertainty: Management guided full-year marketing spend of INR 40–45 crore, but also stated spend depends on need and that the quarterly run rate should not be annualized. A shareholder requested separate disclosure of marketing expenses; management agreed to consider it from next quarter.
  • FOFO accounting dynamics: The GMV-to-revenue gap may increase over time as store-level invoicing becomes more streamlined.
  • Store-level dispersion: Within cohorts, around 10% of stores perform below average, with individual stores potentially loss-making even in cohorts that are broadly above breakeven.
  • Cash breakeven dependency: Achieving cash breakeven by Q4 FY27 or Q1 FY28 depends on store productivity improvement and operating leverage.
  • Annual report risk factors: Losses from uncontrollable reasons could result in inadequate profits to pay managerial remuneration; heavy capital expenditure and operational/working capital outlays for DAVAINDIA.
  • Regulatory/compliance matter: Unclaimed/unpaid dividend for FY 2018-19 and corresponding shares will be transferred to the IEPF on October 27, 2026, with a claim deadline of October 26, 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Store additions. Management had outlined a strategic milestone and growth objective for expansion; Q1 FY27 delivered 264 gross additions (246 net), which management described as in line with that objective. Full-year guidance remains 600–650 additions, with 200 already added and 400–450 planned over the next three quarters.

EBITDA. Management stated the company was EBITDA positive in the prior quarter (Q4 FY26 reported EBITDA of INR 1,191.17 lakh) but turned EBITDA negative in Q1 FY27 (negative INR 955.40 lakh). Management expects to return to reported EBITDA positivity from the next quarter and to exceed FY26’s 3.44% operating margin and 4.82% EBITDA margin on a full-year basis. On a pre-Ind AS basis, management expects EBITDA positivity around Q3/Q4 FY27 or at the latest Q1 FY28.

Cash breakeven. Management guided cash breakeven within two to three quarters, potentially Q4 FY27 or Q1 FY28, with the current quarterly cash loss of around INR 20 crore (excluding incremental marketing) to be eliminated as store productivity improves and operating leverage kicks in. Management also stated that next year the business should generate a meaningful level of cash profitability.

Employee costs. Management stated employee expenses are largely linked to store network growth, with hiring typically in the same month as store opening or about one month prior — not two quarters in advance as a shareholder suggested. Management does not expect disproportionate increases and expects costs to grow in line with store additions.

Marketing spend. Management guided full-year marketing spend of INR 40–45 crore versus approximately INR 18 crore last year, while cautioning that Q1’s INR 15–17 crore was lumpy due to ambassador fees and commercial production and should not be annualized.

Margins. Management expects margins to return to historical levels over the next two to three quarters as input cost pressure subsides and sales scale up.

Mature store profitability. Management’s stated maturation curve (breakeven at around INR 2.5 lakh monthly revenue, roughly 10–12 percentage point annual EBITDA margin improvement) was consistent with the disclosed cohort data: the 234-store 2021–2024 cohort at approximately INR 4.13 lakh monthly GMV with 12–15% store-level EBITDA margins, and the FY25 cohort at approximately INR 2.28 lakh monthly revenue broadly above breakeven.

Annual report guidance. Management stated that with increasing numbers of matured COCO stores and sales volume, the company shall be able to taper the effect of expenses, and performance is expected to improve further in the coming years.
