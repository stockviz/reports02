Headwinds and Challenges

  • Global growth faced headwinds from Middle East geopolitical tensions, higher energy prices, and stalled disinflation, with supply-chain risks and margin pressures resurfacing in sectors including consumer goods.
  • India’s vulnerability as a major energy importer was highlighted by higher crude oil prices, creating risks for inflation and external balances; the Rupee weakened sharply, touching a record low of ₹96.8/USD in May before recovering to ₹94–95/USD by quarter-end.
  • The primary challenge during the quarter was margin pressure from higher commodity and logistics costs, driven largely by Middle East crisis disruptions. Commodity cost inflation across major raw materials persisted.
  • Competitive intensity remained high, particularly in the value segment, where elevated price sensitivity led many competitors to use aggressive pricing as a key differentiator.
  • The export business faced a difficult quarter, as disruptions in key global shipping routes adversely affected freight availability, increased transit times, and created execution challenges for international orders. Export sales declined 17.9% to ₹12.8 Crores from ₹15.6 Crores.
  • Horwood Homewares (UK) reported an operating EBITDA loss of £0.6 million, with higher energy and import costs continuing to pressure margins; political uncertainty in the UK remains elevated and a period of stable governance is unlikely in the near term.
  • Ultrafresh Modular Solutions reported an operating EBITDA loss of ₹0.20 Crores, though improved from a ₹2.08 Crores loss in the prior year.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • India remained one of the fastest-growing major economies, supported by strong domestic demand, government capex, services exports, and private investment. Food inflation remained relatively contained.
  • India continued to benefit from the global AI boom and China+1 supply-chain diversification trends, creating growth opportunities in electronics, engineering, automotive components, and industrial manufacturing.
  • The kitchenware segment delivered stable-to-healthy growth, supported by increasing adoption of induction cooking, premiumization trends in cookware, and resilient urban consumer demand. Demand was encouraging across retail, distribution, and e-commerce channels.
  • Domestic sales grew 35.6% to ₹758.6 Crores; total sales grew 34.2% to ₹771.4 Crores. Operating EBITDA grew 74.3% to ₹88.8 Crores. Profit before tax (before exceptional) grew 74.6% to ₹82.4 Crores. Profit after tax grew 89.0% to ₹66.4 Crores.
  • Consolidated turnover grew 33.6% to ₹813.9 Crores; consolidated PBT (before exceptional) grew 108.3% to ₹73.4 Crores; consolidated PAT grew 130.2% to ₹59.0 Crores.
  • Growth was seen across categories with induction cooktop leading; cookware led growth followed by electrical kitchen appliances and cookers. E-commerce led channel growth, followed by trade, modern format, and exclusive stores.
  • The repositioned Judge brand grew around 89.9% to approximately ₹34.6 Crores (PY ₹18.2 Crores).
  • The Company introduced 26 new SKUs in Q1 and plans around 40 new SKUs in Q2 FY27. The Prestige Xclusive network of 709 stores across 337 towns contributes significantly to overall sales.
  • The Company maintained a strong free cash balance of over ₹870 Crores as of June 30, 2026, including short-term liquid investments, while continuing to fund capex and working capital.
  • Festive season demand buildup is expected to drive channel inventory replenishment across cookware, kitchen appliances, small appliances, home products, and other discretionary consumer categories.
  • Stable macroeconomic conditions and softer energy prices provide a supportive operating environment. India continues to attract investments across electronics, engineering, consumer products, and advanced manufacturing.
  • The Company is positioned to capitalize on growth opportunities through its brand portfolio, extensive market reach, and debt-free balance sheet. Strategic transformation initiatives undertaken since Q4 FY25 continue to strengthen operational capabilities.
  • Horwood’s long-term prospects remain positive, with 5–8% market CAGR over the decade driven by home-cooking, sustainability, and online retail trends; it continues to focus on digital channels, product innovation, retail performance, and European exports.
  • Ultrafresh continued to witness strong demand in retail sales, supported by steady momentum in project orders, with ongoing investments in talent, infrastructure, and systems.

Key Risks

  • Energy prices and geopolitical developments remained key risks for India’s outlook.
  • Rising fuel and transportation costs posed upside inflation risks.
  • Global geopolitical developments and export market uncertainties remain key watchpoints.
  • Competitive pricing pressure in the value segment.
  • Shipping route disruptions affecting export execution.
  • UK political uncertainty and elevated energy/import costs affecting Horwood.
  • The Company reassessed the estimated impact recognized as an Exceptional Item for the year ended March 31, 2026, following alignment of salary structures with the Labour Codes; a consequent reversal of ₹7.3 Crores was presented as an Exceptional Item for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. The original ₹16.94 Crores impact arose from the change in the definition of “wages” under the New Labour Code notified on November 21, 2025.
  • A Voluntary Retirement Scheme at the Hosur factory resulted in ₹9.98 Crores debited to the Statement of Profit & Loss in FY2025-26 as an Exceptional item.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Management stated that strategic transformation initiatives undertaken since Q4 FY25 continue to strengthen focused execution on innovation, operational excellence, and market expansion, expected to support continued growth while maintaining margin stability. Observed: Operating EBITDA margin was 11.5% (PY 8.9%), and 13.1% before the business excellence provision (PY 11.9%).
  • Management stated that encouraging demand trends witnessed in Q1 FY27 have largely continued in July 2026, reinforcing confidence in the business outlook for the remainder of the year. Management remains confident of sustaining business momentum and delivering consistent performance during FY26-27.
  • Management stated the Company is well positioned to capitalize on growth opportunities through its strong brand portfolio, extensive market reach, and debt-free balance sheet.
  • Management stated that through effective sourcing strategies and timely pricing interventions, the Company was able to mitigate a significant portion of commodity cost inflation impact. Organized brands were able to partially mitigate cost increases through selective price hikes, product mix improvement, and premium offerings.
  • Horwood management guidance: growth expected to remain modest for the remaining 2026-27, with Q2 wholesale volumes likely flat to slightly higher than expected. Management remains focused on disciplined cost control, SKU optimisation, and improved stock availability.
  • Ultrafresh management is actively implementing cost-efficiency initiatives and operational improvements aimed at enhancing EBITDA margins over the coming quarters. Observed: Ultrafresh operating EBITDA improved to ₹(0.20) Crores from ₹(2.08) Crores.
  • The Company’s other expenses for the quarter include ₹12.4 Crores (PY ₹17.7 Crores) attributable to ongoing efforts to achieve overall business excellence and bring in sustainable cost savings over the next few quarters.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a defensive stance on competition and market share loss to an optimistic outlook driven by strong domestic demand and premiumization. Input costs remained a recurring theme, initially expected to deflate but later becoming an inflationary headwind. The Judge brand and new product launches became central to the growth story, while export disruptions emerged as a new concern.

Fears that came true

  • The risk of a sharp increase in input prices materialized, as commodity inflation (aluminum, steel, copper, nickel up ~8%) pressured input costs by the last report.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected commodity price correction and margin tailwind failed to materialize, replaced by commodity inflation.
  • The anticipated recovery from H2FY24 on a favorable base did not occur, as stock returns remained negative throughout 2024.
