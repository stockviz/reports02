Manorama Industries — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost and margin pressure. Cost of goods sold rose 49.5% YoY in Q1 FY27, faster than the 39.5% revenue growth, and gross profit margin contracted 376 bps YoY to 43.6%. Management attributed the movement to freight cost timing and by-product (de-oiled cake) realisation, noting gross margin tends to move in a 45%–50% band and is better tracked alongside EBITDA margin than in isolation. Raw material inflation is passed through into costing models and finished goods pricing.

Freight, logistics and other expenses. Other expenses rose 15.8% YoY (and 13.0% QoQ), which management attributed primarily to higher freight and container costs. Geopolitical and macroeconomic disruption was acknowledged as having an indirect impact through freight and logistics, even where direct sourcing is diversified.

Export ban risk in Africa. Nigeria introduced a temporary export ban on shea nuts. Management characterised this as one of 22 African shea-growing countries and stated it does not materially affect sourcing strategy given the company’s presence across 10 wholly owned African subsidiaries and its Burkina Faso facility.

Subsidiary drag on consolidated profitability. Consolidated-minus-standalone revenue was negligible (a difference of roughly INR 4–5 lakhs versus standalone) while subsidiary losses were INR 3 crores in the quarter, down from INR 8 crores in Q4 FY26. West African entities are procurement vehicles with no standalone revenue, so consolidation carries operating costs without corresponding top line; Brazil is in a build-out phase. Management described these as deliberate funded investments whose drag should reduce and eventually turn into a contribution as they scale.

Supplier debit note / recovery uncertainty. A debit note was raised against a manufacturer/supplier, with the company claiming the full amount and stating it is in process and will update under SEBI regulations. Management said sourcing is diversified across multiple countries and suppliers and is not dependent on that particular supplier.

Seasonality and working capital intensity. Shea nut procurement was largely completed in the prior quarter; raw material requirements are around 50% of the business. Inventory rose to INR 7,278 million at 31 March 2026 from INR 5,505 million a year earlier, and trade payables rose to INR 908 million from INR 145 million.

Governance transition. Mr. Mudit Kumar Singh’s tenure as Independent Director completed effective September 05, 2026; Dr. Rohini Tiwari was appointed as Non-Executive Independent Director for a five-year term from September 04, 2026 to September 03, 2031.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record quarterly scale. Q1 FY27 revenue rose 39.5% YoY to INR 4,040.1 million, crossing the INR 4,000 million quarterly revenue and EBITDA milestone for the first time. EBITDA grew 42.2% YoY to INR 1,062.1 million with margin up 49 bps to 26.3%; PAT rose 67.6% YoY to INR 786.6 million with margin up 326 bps to 19.5%. Management stated approximately 85% of the YoY growth was volume-led.

Capacity expansion. Debottlenecking has already operationalised part of the additional 7,500 MTPA, with the balance targeted around Q3 FY27. A further ~4,500 MTPA of debottlenecking is expected in FY27, taking capacity from 47,500 MTPA to approximately 52,000 MTPA. The proposed INR 460 crore capex covers solvent fractionation 3, a new refinery, CBA/ESOS capacity in India, and extraction/expelling capacity in Burkina Faso, targeted for commissioning around Q3 FY28, with fuller impact expected in FY29.

Backward integration in Africa. Incorporation of wholly owned subsidiary Manorama Savannah Agro Chad SARL strengthens shea sourcing in Chad. Acquisition of ~10 hectares (24 acres) in Burkina Faso for a shea nut and mango nut processing facility is awaiting regulatory approvals. The Burkina Faso facility is expected to cost INR 120–130 crores with a payback of around three years once operational, improving efficiency and reducing seed freight.

Forward integration into CBA/ECBE. A CBA (cocoa butter alternative) product using an interesterified/enzymatic process (ECBE) will convert liquid fractions into hard fractions, using existing raw materials and by-products such as olein, targeting food, chocolate, confectionery and HoReCa applications.

Latin America ramp-up. First commercial production of CBE/specialty fats with Dekel in Brazil was achieved in Q3 FY26, with trial samples delivered to customers. Management expects gradual quarter-on-quarter ramp-up over the next two to four quarters, with production flowing from the Indian plant to the Brazilian processing entity.

Balance sheet strength. The QIP of INR 500 crores completed in July 2026 at ₹1,470/share. Management stated no further equity dilution is planned, with working capital to be supported by existing sanctioned bank limits (State Bank of India as lead banker) and INR 150-odd crores held in FDRs excluding the QIP component. CARE Ratings upgraded the company’s bank facilities from ‘A’ to ‘A+’.

Subsidiary capitalisation. The Board approved share capital increases in wholly owned subsidiaries: up to Rs. 20 Crore (Manorama Savanna Ghana), Rs. 10 Crore (Manorama Savanna Nigeria), Rs. 15 Crore (Manorama Burkina SARL), Rs. 10 Crore (Manorama Savannah Agro Chad Sarl), Rs. 15 Crore (Manorama Latin America LTDA) and Rs. 5 Crore (Manorama Mena Trading LLC), to be completed in a calibrated and phased manner aligned with business milestones and funding needs.

Structural demand drivers. CBE is trans-fat-free and fully compatible with cocoa butter; regulatory support permits up to 5% CBE in finished chocolate in the EU, India (FSSAI) and other markets while retaining the chocolate label; heat-stability benefits suit tropical markets; and plant-based ingredient demand supports shea, sal and mango butters in personal care.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material availability and policy risk in African origins, illustrated by Nigeria’s temporary shea nut export ban, mitigated by multi-country presence and diversified suppliers.
  • Freight, logistics and macroeconomic/geopolitical disruption, acknowledged as having at least an indirect cost impact.
  • Margin volatility from product mix, one-off items, freight timing and by-product realisation; management stated margins can see modest quarter-to-quarter movement.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk on the INR 460 crore capex, with commissioning targeted around Q3 FY28 and full impact only in FY29, and utilisation of technical plants building gradually.
  • Subsidiary losses continuing until West African procurement entities and Brazil scale.
  • Recovery risk on the supplier debit note, with the full amount claimed but recovery still in process.
  • Working capital intensity, with raw material at around 50% of the business and inventory build-up evident in the FY26 balance sheet.
  • Pricing stability depends on contracts of 9–12 months renewed on a rolling basis; management cannot quantify renewal timing by quarter.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Capacity and utilisation. Management guides 80%–85% utilisation of the expanded 52,000 MTPA capacity for FY27, with 80% as the figure stakeholders should use and any improvement to 85%–90% to be communicated. Observed: Q1 FY27 utilisation was around 80%, and the company reported 47,500 MTPA capacity in Q1 FY27 with the balance debottlenecking targeted for Q3 FY27.

Margins. Management guides the underlying margin range to hold broadly stable, with modest quarter-to-quarter movement depending on mix and one-off items, and directs stakeholders to historical performance as the directional benchmark. Observed: Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 26.3% versus FY26 full-year EBITDA margin of 26.4%, within the stated range; gross margin of 43.6% was below the 45%–50% band management cited, which management attributed to freight timing and by-product realisation.

Employee cost run-rate. Management guides INR 14–15 crores per quarter going forward, after Q4 FY26 included a performance incentive provision. Observed: Q1 FY27 employee expenses of INR 188.6 million (approximately INR 18.9 crores), down 20.7% QoQ from INR 237.8 million.

Other income. Management stated other income of INR 16-odd crores was largely FDR income and forex gains (INR 13 crores forex, INR 3 crores other), not related to the QIP proceeds received in the quarter, and that it should normalise. Observed: Q1 FY27 other income of INR 161.8 million versus INR 57.6 million in Q1 FY26 and negative INR 70.9 million in Q4 FY26.

Capex. Management guides INR 225–250 crores of investment in FY27, part of the proposed INR 460 crore programme, with INR 120–130 crores for Burkina Faso and the balance for Indian projects. Observed: around INR 20-odd crores spent in the quarter and around INR 70 crores spent to date, with approximately INR 220-odd crores more intended in FY27. Debottlenecking itself costs only around INR 5–6 crores.

Revenue guidance. Management declined to reaffirm the previously cited INR 1,600–1,800 crores guidance from a November conference, stating guidance is given on a utilisation basis (around 80% of 52,000 tons) and that stakeholders should model accordingly. Management expressed confidence in delivering healthy top-line growth in FY27 with further scope for improvement as the year progresses, supported by capacity ramp-up and debottlenecking.

Asset turnover. Management declined to commit to a specific 7x–8x–9x asset turnover for FY29–FY30, stating it aims to perform in the direction of historical capex investment performance and expects steady, healthy growth over a three-to-five-year horizon toward FY30–31. Observed: FY26 asset turnover of 7.4x and FY22–26 revenue CAGR of 48.5%.

Subsidiary losses. Management guides that the drag on consolidated margins should reduce and over time turn into a contributor as entities scale. Observed: subsidiary losses declined from INR 8 crores in Q4 FY26 to INR 3 crores in Q1 FY27.

Dividend and AGM. The 21st AGM is scheduled for September 21, 2026, with a record date of September 14, 2026 for the final dividend and the same date as the e-voting cut-off; remote e-voting runs September 18–20, 2026. The FY26 auditor’s report notes a final dividend proposed for the year subject to shareholder approval, and no interim dividend was declared during FY26.
