Anand Rathi Wealth Limited — Q1 FY27 Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

  • Volatile equity markets in Q1 FY27. Management repeatedly referenced a difficult market environment during the quarter, noting that Nifty slipped into a two-year negative return on 9 June 2026 and that “last quarter wasn’t easy from a sentiment standpoint.” Despite this, the company reported net inflows of ₹2,743 crore and equity mutual fund net sales of ₹1,900 crore.
  • Yield pressure in mutual fund distribution. On the earnings call, a question was raised about larger AMCs passing on the impact of changes in the TER expense ratio to distributors. Management acknowledged that “some degree of to and from happens” and that 1–3 bps of transmission “might happen here or there,” while noting the company’s scale (net inflow market share rising from 0.18% in FY20 to 2.30% in FY26) gives it some bargaining power.
  • RM attrition and client retention. Three relationship managers had left for a competitor that was hiring at high salaries after raising capital. Management stated that generally 70–80% of assets are retained in such cases, and for this specific group, of ₹758 crore managed, net flow out was ₹73 crore, leaving ₹658.5 crore as of June end — roughly 90% retention excluding market movement.
  • Concentration considerations in structured products. The company predominantly sells Anand Rathi Global Finance structured products over third-party products. Management framed this as deliberate de-risking, citing credit risk concerns and noting that two structured product issuers in India had nearly failed and one actually did.
  • Regulatory change affecting derivative volumes. An RBI circular on bank guarantees for capital market entities was expected to reduce exchange volumes. Management viewed this positively, expecting high-frequency trading volumes to be curtailed and market volatility to become “smoother,” with negligible or nil impact on the company.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • AUM milestone and growth. Total AUM grew 21% Y-o-Y to ₹1,06,300 crore as of 30 June 2026, crossing the ₹1 lakh crore milestone. Net inflows were ₹2,743 crore for the quarter.
  • Consistent, market-agnostic performance. Management highlighted 19 consecutive quarterly results, with mean Y-o-Y PAT growth of 31.7% and median of 33.1% over the last 17 quarters, and a standard deviation of 4.8%.
  • Client acquisition and retention. 1,611 new client families were added on a net basis over twelve months, taking the total to 13,941. Client attrition by AUM lost was 0.09% in Q1 FY27, and RM attrition was zero for the quarter.
  • Platinum segment scaling. Platinum clients grew from 211 to approximately 230, up from 40–45 when the segment launched about two and a half years ago. Management indicated the count could reach four digits in a few years.
  • Mutual fund market share ambition. The company targets a 4% share of Category II (growth/equity-oriented) mutual fund assets, up from around 1.5–1.75% currently, implying AUM of ₹6 lakh crore in 8–10 years, requiring 22–23% asset growth.
  • New business initiatives. The Board approved applying to SEBI to act as sponsor of a mutual fund, with plans to incorporate an AMC and Trustee Company upon approvals (announced 9 July 2026). The UK subsidiary commenced operations, and a GIFT City license application was in its second stage. A Dubai representative office exists.
  • Digital businesses. Digital Wealth AUM grew 23% Y-o-Y to ₹2,526 crore with clients up 16% to 7,320. OFA, the SaaS platform, had 6,890 subscribers with platform assets of ₹1.66 lakh crore.
  • Structured products expansion exploration. Management discussed exploring structured products on global indices, citing higher S&P 500 volatility and interest rate levels as favorable raw material for product design, though no full-fledged work had been done.
  • GIFT City fund management registration. Anand Rathi FME (IFSC) Private Limited, a wholly owned subsidiary, received a certificate of registration from IFSCA on 14 September 2026 (dated 11 September 2026) to act as a Registered Fund Management Entity (Non-retail) at GIFT City, Gujarat, enabling it to commence fund management activities.

Key Risks

  • Market-linked revenue sensitivity. Revenue and AUM are tied to market levels; management noted mark-to-market movements affect yields and that “Trump decides my yield” in reference to market-driven variability.
  • Client and RM attrition. While current attrition is low, management identified client attrition and RM attrition as the key metrics for the business, noting that in some wealth management firms client attrition runs at 4–10%.
  • Concentration in group company products. Structured product distribution is weighted toward Anand Rathi Global Finance, which management views as de-risking but which represents concentration exposure.
  • Execution risk in new ventures. The AMC license, UK operations, GIFT City entity, and potential Bahrain presence are at various stages; management emphasized building “brick-by-brick” and not scaling the UK subsidiary aggressively soon.
  • Regulatory dependencies. The mutual fund sponsorship, GIFT City operations, and other initiatives require regulatory approvals, with the AMC structure contingent on SEBI approval.
  • Typographical correction in financial results. The company filed a revised outcome on 9 July 2026 correcting the paid-up capital in the standalone and consolidated results to “8302.06,” with all other contents unchanged.

Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • FY27 guidance: Revenue of ₹1,415 crore, PAT of ₹460 crore, and AUM of ₹1,20,000 crore.
  • Q1 FY27 actuals: Revenue of ₹336 crore (24% of guidance), PAT of ₹116 crore (25% of guidance), and AUM of ₹1,06,300 crore.
  • Management stated it remains confident of achieving full-year guidance given the business built and opportunities ahead.
  • Longer-term framework: Management described a 20–25% AUM growth model comprising 10–12% mark-to-market and 10–12% from net sales, with an implied monthly net sales target of about 1% of AUM (roughly ₹1,060 crore at current AUM). Management assigned “90%, 95%” odds to achieving this over three to four years.
  • Growth cylinders cited: embedded portfolio returns (15–16% for long-term clients), new clients from existing RM capacity (33 clients per RM currently, with capacity for about 17 more per RM, implying roughly 6,500 additional client families), new relationship managers (490 people trained, next 100 RMs “60% ripe”), and wallet share penetration among existing clients.
  • Historical context: Management noted that any time Nifty delivered two-year negative returns over the last 26 years (50 observations out of 291), the subsequent three years were positive in all cases, with a mean return of 21.6% and median of 20.2%.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative shifted from a highly bullish structural-growth outlook focused on demographic dividends and low market penetration to a cautious stance prioritizing near-term earnings pressures. Persistent themes included strong RoE and the regulatory overhang on TER and yields, while the focus changed from volume-driven growth to margin compression from rising operating expenses and volatile market outflows.

Fears that came true

  • SEBI’s F&O regulations and peak-margin norms caused intermittent volume disruptions that correlated with the DISAPPOINTMENT outcome in July 2025 and the subsequent negative actual returns.
  • Telescopic TER structures and tightened commission norms pressured revenues and yields, correlating with the negative actual returns seen in the pending outcomes from October 2025 onward.

Optimism that failed

  • The optimism surrounding continuous AUM expansion driven by low penetration and high SIP run-rates failed as AUM declined 6% QoQ in 4QFY26 due to negative mark-to-market impacts and volatile markets limiting net inflows.
  • The expectation of sustained high turnover velocity failed to materialize due to intermittent volume disruptions from regulatory changes and negative market movements.
