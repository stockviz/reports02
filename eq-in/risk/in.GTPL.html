GTPL Hathway — Q1 FY27 Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Profitability divergence despite revenue growth. Total income grew 12% Y-o-Y to ₹1,020 crores in Q1 FY27, but PAT fell to ₹23 million from ₹105 million in Q1 FY26. Management attributed the roughly ₹8 crore Y-o-Y PAT decline mainly to higher depreciation and finance cost (up ~₹6 crores), driven by capitalization of right-of-use assets related to HITS infrastructure under conservative accounting practice. The full cost of HITS has been taken up front, while related cost savings and operational benefits are expected to flow in later quarters.
  • Digital TV revenue pressure. Subscription income from CATV declined 3% Y-o-Y to ₹2,913 million (though up 2% Q-o-Q). Management linked this to churn and to entry into new markets where ARPU is lower than in established markets, changing the ARPU mix.
  • Broadband subscriber additions lagging home-pass expansion. Homepass stood at 5.95 million with ~75% FTTX-ready, but subscriber additions have been modest (up 10K Y-o-Y to 1,060K). Extraction rate is ~16–17%, which management wants to raise to 19–21%.
  • New-market gestation. In Kerala and Jammu & Kashmir, management stated that making a cable business positive takes 6–12 months, with discounts needed initially to build scale, implying an initial investment phase before returns.
  • Content cost inflation. Management noted content cost increases every year or two, managed through negotiations with broadcasters and some on-ground price increases, with costs absorbed jointly by GTPL, broadcasters, and LCOs.
  • Regulatory/legal overhang. The standalone and consolidated results disclose a DoT demand of ₹9,754.15 million on the Parent (principal ₹2,286.50 million plus interest/penalty) relating to license fee on cable TV revenue, treated as a contingent liability with no provision, based on legal advice and pending TDSAT proceedings. Similar contingent matters exist for GTPL Broadband (₹3,749.12 million) and GTPL KCBPL Broadband (₹353.92 million) regarding license fee on pure internet services, with litigation pending before TDSAT and the Supreme Court.
  • Competitive intensity. Management acknowledged competition from DTH, local cable operators, and telecom broadband/AirFiber-type offerings across markets, including new ones.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • ACT Group Digital TV acquisition. A business transfer agreement was executed on June 23, 2026 with seven ACT Group companies for their cable TV business on a going-concern slump-sale basis for ₹362.30 million (₹36.23 crores), effective July 1, 2026, with completion expected by September 15, 2026. It adds ~6 lakh Digital TV subscribers across Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Odisha, and Karnataka, giving GTPL a leadership position in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana (taking its base there from over 1 million to ~1.6–1.7 million) and enabling revenue consolidation and cost optimization.
  • GTPL Infinity (HITS) platform. Launched November 30, 2025, backed by a C-Band teleport in Ahmedabad and Telkom-4 satellite capacity via PT Telkomsat. It enables pan-India reach, including rural, cable-dark, and hilly areas where point-to-point delivery was not financially viable, with fixed transponder costs that improve operating margin as subscribers scale. ~2.5 million existing subscribers have been converted to HITS and ~200,000 new subscribers added; bandwidth savings of ~₹4 crores were realized in Q1. Full benefits are expected by end of Q3/start of Q4 FY27, with 40–50% benefit this year and 100% next financial year.
  • New market entries. Kerala and Jammu & Kashmir entered in Q1 FY27 via HITS, completing GTPL’s presence across all southern markets. Addressable markets cited: ~6.5–7 million TV households in Kerala (GTPL already has ~75,000–80,000 base) and ~4.5–5 million in J&K. Broadband launches are planned in both.
  • Broadband growth levers. Broadband revenue grew 5% Y-o-Y to ₹1,425 million; ARPU rose to ₹470 from ₹465; average data consumption rose 6% Y-o-Y to 436 GB per user per month. Management sees potential to convert 12+ million GTPL Digital TV households, with Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra, and rural Gujarat as growth drivers, and >75% of homepass FTTX-ready.
  • Bundling and upsell. Combo products (Cable + Broadband + other services such as games) are already launched and growing, with a pilot in select cities; management sees this increasing stickiness and revenue. GTPL Buzz app, GTPL Genie, and cloud gaming are cited as differentiators.
  • Industry consolidation. MSO count fell from 1,702 (Dec 2020) to 818 (Sept 2025), with license cancellations of non-compliant MSOs creating consolidation opportunities; GTPL cites ~40 million cable TV households available for consolidation.
  • Industry runway. Traditional TV retains mass reach (745 million weekly), TV households expected to cross 200 million by 2028, and wired broadband penetration remains low (~14.5% of ~320 million households), with the India wired broadband market projected to grow at 15.43% CAGR FY23–FY28 and FTTH at 17.62%.

3. Key Risks

  • Contingent tax/regulatory liabilities. The DoT license-fee demands (₹9,754.15 million on the Parent; ₹3,749.12 million for GBPL; ₹353.92 million for KCBPL Broadband) remain unresolved, with outcomes dependent on litigation; no provisions have been recognized.
  • Execution risk on ACT integration. Revenue and EBITDA contribution figures were not disclosed pending deal closure; management deferred quantification to the next call. Benefits are expected to be accretive but are contingent on integration.
  • Margin trajectory risk. Operating EBITDA margin was 22% in Q1 FY27 (flat Y-o-Y, up from 17% Q-o-Q); management guides to 25%, dependent on HITS benefits materializing and new-market profitability.
  • New-market and expansion risk. Lower ARPU in new markets, discounting, and 6–12 month gestation periods could pressure near-term margins; broadband home-pass expansion is being resumed after a pause, with a target 20–21% extraction rate.
  • Competitive and content-cost risk. Competition across all markets and periodic content cost increases require ongoing negotiation and price adjustments.
  • Dependence on HITS for future economics. The platform’s fixed-cost model improves margins only as subscriber volumes scale; slower-than-expected subscriber growth would delay the benefit.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Operating margin. Management stated operating margin of 22% “will go up to 25%,” tied to full HITS benefits. Observed: operating EBITDA margin was 22% in Q1 FY27, same as Q1 FY26 and up from 17% in Q4 FY26; published EBITDA margin was 10.7% (vs 12.4% in Q1 FY26).
  • HITS benefits. Management guided that bandwidth savings would build through the year, with 40–50% of benefits in FY27 and 100% from FY28. Observed: ~₹4 crores of bandwidth savings realized in Q1 FY27; ~2.5 million subscribers migrated and ~200,000 new added.
  • Broadband extraction. Management targets 20–21% extraction (from ~16–17%) and said it will resume investing in new home passes. Observed: homepass stable at 5.95 million; subscribers up only 10K Y-o-Y to 1,060K; ARPU ₹470, which management said will “remain somewhere around ₹470 for right now.”
  • ACT acquisition contribution. Management declined to give revenue/EBITDA figures before closure (expected September 15, 2026), stating the full effect would begin mid-Q2 and be visible in Q3, with subscriber count rising by ~6 lakh and the deal being EBITDA-accretive. No contribution is reflected in Q1 FY27 results.
  • Capex. Management guided FY27 capex of ~₹400 crores, split ~50% Broadband and ~50% Digital TV. No capex figure for the quarter was disclosed in the supplied material.
  • New markets. Management guided a 6–12 month gestation to positive cable business in Kerala and J&K; no financial contribution from these markets was reported for Q1 FY27.
  • Digital TV revenue. Management attributed the Y-o-Y dip to churn and lower-ARPU new markets and said revenue “will come back” with aggressive expansion and acquisitions; observed Q1 FY27 CATV subscription revenue was down 3% Y-o-Y but up 2% Q-o-Q.
