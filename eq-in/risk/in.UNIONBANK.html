Headwinds and Challenges

  • Deposit mobilisation lagged advances. Total deposits grew 3.50% YoY in Q1FY27 while gross advances grew 12.50% YoY, pushing the credit-deposit ratio up to 86.10% from 79.17% a year earlier. On a sequential basis, global deposits declined 1.80% (from ₹13,06,891 crore in Mar-26 to ₹12,83,366 crore in Jun-26), with current account deposits falling 12.01% QoQ.
  • Margin and yield pressure. Yield on advances declined to 7.90% in Q1FY27 from 8.50% in Q1FY26, and cost of deposits, though down YoY to 5.05%, rose sequentially from 5.23% in Mar-26. Net interest income growth was 10.15% YoY but net profit growth was largely supported by lower provisions.
  • Non-interest income weakness. Non-interest income fell 14.95% QoQ to ₹4,603 crore in Q1FY27, and the cost-to-income ratio, while improved YoY to 45.34%, remains a drag relative to the operating profit trajectory.
  • Regulatory action. The Reserve Bank of India imposed a penalty of ₹1,33,850, received on 11.09.2026, for deficiencies in three Currency Chests relating to mutilated currency notes, shortage of currency notes, and counterfeit currency notes found in cash remittance during FY 2021-22 and FY 2022-23. The bank assessed the financial and operational impact as not significant.
  • Asset quality slippages remain. Fresh slippages of ₹218 crore in Q1FY27 were marginally higher than ₹214 crore in Q4FY26, and write-offs of ₹240 crore in Q1FY27 exceeded the ₹124 crore in Q4FY26, indicating continued reliance on write-offs to manage gross NPA levels.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Sustained profitability expansion. Net profit grew at a ~17% CAGR during FY24–26, reaching ₹1,975 million (FY26) and ₹563 million in Q1FY27. Operating profit hit a record quarterly high of ₹845 million in Q1FY27, up 15.83% YoY.
  • Improving returns. RoA improved 25 bps YoY to 1.36% and RoE expanded 208 bps YoY to 17.23% in Q1FY27.
  • Asset quality improvement. GNPA fell 87 bps YoY to 2.65% and NNPA fell 15 bps YoY to 0.47% as of Jun-26. Provision coverage ratio strengthened to 95.05%. Gross NPA declined from $4,553 million in FY24 to $3,073 million in Q1FY27.
  • RAM segment momentum. RAM advances grew 11.56% YoY, with retail up 12.06% and MSME up 16.49%. RAM constituted 57.31% of domestic advances, and the bank crossed the $100 billion mark in total advances in FY25.
  • Capital strength. CRAR stood at 18.46% and CET-1 improved to 16.38% as of Jun-26, providing headroom for growth. The bank carries investment-grade ratings (BBB/Stable from S&P, BBB-/Stable from Fitch).
  • Foreign currency funding initiative. The Board approved raising foreign currency funds up to USD 2.00 billion through a Medium Term Note Programme via the Dubai and/or Sydney branches, with proceeds earmarked for DIFC Branch funding requirements, business expansion, and general corporate purposes.
  • Digital and financial inclusion traction. Over 90% of transactions are conducted digitally, with digital sanctions of ~$12.7 billion in FY26. The bank opened 3.45 crore PMJDY accounts with balances of ₹15,943 crore as of 30.06.2026, up from 3.26 crore accounts and ₹13,089 crore a year earlier.
  • Green finance portfolio. Renewable energy sector sanctions reached ₹34,363 crore and Union Green Vehicle sanctions ₹2,819 crore as of 30.06.2026. The bank became the first major Indian bank to sign up to the Partnership for Carbon Accounting Financials (PCAF).

Key Risks

  • Deposit growth divergence. The widening gap between advance growth (12.50%) and deposit growth (3.50%) could pressure liquidity and funding costs if sustained. The average LCR was 121.30% and NSFR 118.72% as of Jun-26.
  • Sequential deposit contraction. The 1.80% QoQ decline in global deposits and 12.01% QoQ fall in current account deposits indicate volatility in the deposit franchise.
  • Yield compression. The 60 bps YoY decline in yield on advances, if not offset by cost of funds reduction, could compress NIM. NIM improved to 2.80% in Q1FY27 from 2.76% in Q1FY26 but remains below the FY25 level of 2.64% on a sequential basis.
  • Regulatory and compliance risk. The RBI penalty highlights operational lapses in currency chest management, though the bank has implemented preventive measures.
  • Asset quality sensitivity. Despite improvement, the bank continues to carry gross NPAs of $3,073 million and net NPAs of $530 million as of Q1FY27, with fresh slippages and write-offs remaining part of the NPA management cycle.
  • Concentration in RAM segments. With RAM at 57.31% of domestic advances, any stress in retail, agriculture, or MSME segments could disproportionately affect the portfolio.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. The investor presentation contains forward-looking statements subject to known and unknown risks, uncertainties, and factors beyond the bank’s control that could cause actual results to differ materially.

The offering summary indicates a benchmark USD issuance with 3 and/or 5-year tenor, rated BBB by S&P and BBB- by Fitch, listed on the NSE IFSC Debt Securities Market. This is a forward-looking capital/funding action; actual issuance terms and timing are not yet reflected in reported financials.
  • Preventive measures on currency chest operations. Management stated that necessary preventive measures have been taken to avoid recurrence of the deficiencies flagged by the RBI. No subsequent data on currency chest operations is available in the supplied material to assess the effectiveness of these measures.
  • Dividend. The AGM passed an ordinary resolution to declare a dividend of ₹5.00 per equity share for FY2025-26, with 99.8193% of valid votes cast in favour.
  • Capital raising authorisation. The AGM passed a special resolution for raising capital through fresh equity shares and/or Additional Tier-1/Tier-2 capital as per Basel III guidelines, with 99.9506% of valid votes in favour. This authorisation provides the framework for future capital actions but does not itself indicate a completed issuance.
  • Observed performance versus stated trajectory. The bank’s Q1FY27 results show net profit of ₹5,332 crore, operating profit of ₹8,003 crore, and NIM of 2.80%, with RoA at 1.36% and RoE at 17.23%. These are reported outcomes, not guidance. The investor presentation’s “Key Highlights (Jun-26)” cite total deposits of ~$135.6 billion, total advances of ~$115.8 billion, and market capitalisation of ~$15.0 billion as of 19 Aug’26.
  • Director appointments. Broker Narrative

Initially the broker’s Buy call emphasized strong profit growth, asset quality improvement, and a strong corporate pipeline, while cautioning near-term NIM pressure and elevated costs. By the last report, the narrative shifted to a more cautious Accumulate as NIM compression indeed persisted (NIM at 2.80% vs 2.98% at the start) and deposit growth slowed, but the bank’s core loan growth and capital strength were positive. The persistent theme was the pressure on margins; the changing themes were the fading recoveries-based non-interest income and a softer outlook on deposits.

Fears that came true

  • NII and margin contraction materialized as NIM fell from 2.98% (Q4FY23) to 2.80% (Q1FY27), coinciding with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes in early 2024.

Optimism that failed

  • Strong recoveries and non-interest income tailwind failed, as lower recoveries and higher write-offs hurt other income in the last report.
  • The expected MCLR repricing benefit failed to prevent NIM decline, as NIM remained below its earlier level.
