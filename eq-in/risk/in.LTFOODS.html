LT Foods — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Organic segment decline. Organic Foods & Ingredients revenue fell 13% YoY to ₹254 crore in Q1 FY27, with EBITDA margin dropping to 4% from 10%, attributed to the remodelling of the organic business. Management states underlying demand fundamentals remain intact and expects normalisation over coming quarters.

Margin pressure. Gross margin declined to 32.6% of revenue from 34.7%, attributed to a change in shipment terms with related parties from CIF to C&I. EBITDA margin fell to 11.5% from 12.1%, linked to the organic segment remodelling. Interest cost increased on account of supplier funding arrangements to optimise the working capital cycle, and interest coverage declined to 7.2x from 8.9x.

Europe revenue contraction. Europe revenue declined 12% YoY, attributed to reduced non-strategic B2B business.

US countervailing duty on organic soybean meal. Ecopure Specialities Limited received a preliminary US-DOC determination applying a CVD rate of 340.27% on organic soybean meal exports to the US (exports of approximately ₹50 crore during January–December 2023). The final order dated February 23, 2026 reduced the rate to 75.48%. Ecopure filed a summons with the US CIT on March 27, 2026 and an appeal on April 27, 2026; on May 11, 2026 the US CIT issued an injunction enjoining liquidation of entries until final outcome. Management states it believes it has a strong legal position and does not expect a material impact.

Insurance claim litigation. An insurance claim recoverable of ₹13,410.53 lakh relates to a FY2014-15 fire at Daawat Foods Limited. The District Court decreed in favour of the subsidiary in December 2023 (₹16,120.27 lakh plus ₹10,383.23 lakh interest). The insurer’s appeal on merits is admitted and pending before the Madhya Pradesh High Court; stay applications were rejected by the High Court and Supreme Court. The insurer deposited ₹26,503.50 lakh on March 4, 2025, released to the subsidiary on April 8, 2025 against a 100% bank guarantee valid until final disposal of the First Appeal. The amount received is recorded as a liability given the matter is sub judice. The bank guarantee was renewed in April 2026 for ₹26,503.50 lakh. The differential excess amount awarded over the carrying value has not been recognised.

Broader risk disclosures. The company’s risk disclosures cite escalating import tariffs (including US import tariffs reaching up to 50%), rising input costs and elevated freight rates particularly on Middle East routes, geopolitical tensions and trade policy uncertainty, climate variability and commodity price volatility affecting paddy yields and raw material availability, regulatory changes such as stricter Maximum Residue Levels and FSSAI norms, talent availability and retention, cybersecurity and data privacy threats, intense competition from established brands, regional players and private labels, and over-reliance on a single commodity (rice). Sustainability-related risks cited include climate change disruptions to agricultural productivity, workplace incidents, human rights violations in the value chain, business ethics non-compliance, and cyber threats.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Consolidated growth. Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue grew 26% YoY to ₹3,161 crore, with gross profit up 19% and EBITDA up 20% YoY. PAT grew 9% YoY to ₹183 crore. ROCE held steady at 21.1%, and Net Debt/EBITDA declined to 0.48x from 0.59x.

India business. India revenue grew 19% YoY, contributing 29% of revenue. Market share stood at 23.1% (MAT June 2026) and household penetration at 64.4 lakh (MAT March 2026) versus 45.6 lakh (MAT 2023). The company cites leadership in leading e-commerce and quick-commerce platforms.

International business. International markets contributed 71% of revenue. North America grew 49% YoY (27% normalised including Golden Star in Q1 FY26), with Royal commanding a 60%+ basmati import share and Golden Star now the #1 jasmine rice brand in the US. Middle East & Rest of the World grew 44% YoY, including ₹17 crore of revenue from Saudi Arabia in Q1 FY27. Daawat has set a four-year UK revenue target of £100 million.

Segment performance. Basmati & Other Specialty Rice revenue grew 34% YoY to ₹2,845 crore, led by 11% volume growth (branded volume up 10%), with EBITDA margin steady at 13%. RTH & RTC revenue grew 13% YoY to ₹53 crore, with 42% growth in Biryani Kits; the portfolio has scaled approximately 2.5x over FY21–FY26.

Brand and marketing activity. LT Foods onboarded Chef Ranveer Brar as brand ambassador and launched a new DAAWAT Delight campaign (announced August 4, 2026), rolled out across television, digital and social media. DAAWAT Delight is available across Q-Commerce, Modern Trade and General Trade. The company cites the 5th edition of Daawat World Biryani Day (50 crore+ impressions) and SRK-led campaigns across the US, Middle East and Far East.

Product launches. In Q1 FY27, the company launched DAAWAT Idli Rice, Ponni Rice and Matta Rice across Europe in 10 KG household packs, sourced from South India.

Market opportunity. The company cites estimated basmati and specialty rice market sizes of ₹50,000+ crore (India), ₹35,000+ crore (North America), ₹7,500+ crore (Europe) and ₹40,000+ crore (Middle East), with expected industry CAGRs of 10–12%, 10–12%, 6–8% and 9–10% respectively. The Indian FMCG market is estimated at approximately $105 billion with expected growth CAGR of 11%.

Strategic priorities. Management lists margin expansion (economies of scale, premiumisation, value-chain efficiencies), growth (solidifying the core business, expanding portfolio and geographies, widening distribution, exploring inorganic opportunities), capability building (digital transformation, talent) and ESG embedding.

Sustainability opportunities. The company cites sustainable sourcing to enhance supply chain resilience and brand value, boosting green energy share and energy efficiency to lower costs and reduce emissions, sustainable packaging, and strengthened governance and data security.

Other tailwinds cited. Rising consumer preference for premium branded packaged foods, structural shifts toward health-oriented, organic and clean-label foods, growing global acceptance of basmati and specialty rice, expansion of digital commerce/e-commerce/quick-commerce, rising disposable incomes and urbanisation, expansion of convenience-led categories (RTE, RTC, RTH), strategic consolidation including full ownership of Golden Star, and supportive government policies on food processing, agriculture and infrastructure.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity concentration: Over-reliance on a single commodity (rice).
  • Trade and tariff exposure: Escalating import tariffs (US tariffs cited up to 50%), geopolitical tensions and trade policy uncertainty affecting international trade.
  • Input and logistics costs: Rising input costs and elevated freight rates, particularly on Middle East routes.
  • Climate and yield risk: Climate variability and commodity price volatility affecting paddy yields and raw material availability; climate change-related disruptions to agricultural productivity and operations.
  • Regulatory risk: Stricter Maximum Residue Levels and FSSAI norms.
  • Litigation risk: The pending insurance claim appeal before the Madhya Pradesh High Court, with the carrying value of ₹13,410.53 lakh dependent on final verdict; the differential excess amount awarded has not been recognised. The US CVD matter on Ecopure’s organic soybean meal exports remains subject to proceedings, with Nature Bio Foods Inc. as importer of record liable for applicable duty upon final disposal.
  • Competitive risk: Intense competition from established brands, regional players and private labels.
  • Operational and people risk: Talent availability and retention; workplace incidents; human rights violations in the value chain; business ethics non-compliance.
  • Cyber and data risk: Increasing cyber threats and data privacy risks.
  • Financial risk indicators: Interest coverage declined to 7.2x from 8.9x; Return on Equity declined to 15.9% from 17.1%; gross and EBITDA margins contracted.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Margin guidance. Management states an expectation to deliver continued double-digit revenue growth while restoring normalised EBITDA margins to above 12%. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: revenue grew 26% YoY (double-digit growth achieved), but EBITDA margin was 11.5%, below the stated above-12% target, having declined from 12.1% in Q1 FY26 due to organic segment remodelling.

Organic segment normalisation. Management states underlying demand fundamentals remain intact with normalisation expected over coming quarters. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: organic revenue declined 13% YoY and EBITDA margin fell to 4% from 10%, indicating the normalisation had not yet materialised in the reported quarter.

FY27 strategic priorities. Management lists expanding the consumer franchise (household penetration in India, scaling quick-commerce and e-commerce), consolidating North America leadership (Royal position, Golden Star integration benefits), scaling RTH/RTC, expanding retail partnerships and operational leverage in Europe, and accelerating growth in Saudi Arabia and GCC markets. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: India revenue grew 19% with household penetration at 64.4 lakh (MAT March 2026); North America grew 49% YoY (27% normalised); RTH & RTC grew 13% YoY with Biryani Kits up 42%; Europe declined 12% YoY; Middle East & Rest of the World grew 44% YoY with ₹17 crore from Saudi Arabia.

UK target. Daawat has set a four-year UK revenue target of £100 million. No current UK revenue figure is disclosed in the supplied material for comparison.

2030 sustainability targets. Management has set 2030 targets including sourcing 50,000 MT of paddy sustainably, conducting 1 lakh farmer training sessions, achieving 100% green electricity and carbon neutrality, reducing specific energy by 10%, using 45 MT of recycled plastics, ensuring 82% of packaging is recyclable, maintaining a zero-fatality vision, having 100% of critical suppliers undergo value chain assessments, achieving 56% board independence and 30% female directors, and maintaining zero data breaches. The company reports a 72/100 ESG rating (Aspiring) awarded by NSE Sustainability Ratings & Analytics. No interim progress metrics against these 2030 targets are disclosed in the supplied material.

FY2026 reported results. Consolidated revenue reached ₹11,023 crore (up 26% YoY, or approximately 19% normalised excluding US tariff pass-throughs), EBITDA rose to ₹1,236 crore, and PAT stood at ₹625 crore. Net Debt/EBITDA was 0.6x, Net Debt/Equity 0.16x, and ROCE 20.4%. Basmati and Other Specialty Rice accounted for 88% of total revenue. A total dividend of ₹3.00 per equity share was declared for FY2026. The company operates an integrated Farm-to-Fork value chain across 9 plants and 39 offices, reaching over 85 countries. Paid-up capital was ₹34.72 crore, turnover ₹4,12,816.43 lakh, and net worth ₹1,92,216.36 lakh for FY2025-26, with 28% of turnover from exports.

Investor engagement. The 36th Annual General Meeting is convened for Tuesday, September 29, 2026 at 11:30 A.M. (IST) through video conferencing and other audio-visual means.
