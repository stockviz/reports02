Headwinds and Challenges

  • Input cost pressure in Q1 FY27. Sequential profitability was impacted by higher input costs from increased market sourcing of iron ore and elevated coal prices following the West Asia crisis. Consolidated EBITDA fell 24% QoQ to Rs. 334 crore and PAT fell 21% QoQ to Rs. 222 crore, with EBITDA margin at 19.1% and PAT margin at 12.7%.
  • Iron ore mining volume decline. Q1 FY27 iron ore mining production fell 20% QoQ and 16% YoY to 5,37,832 tonnes. The company attributes this to space constraints for overburden dumping, caused by delay in obtaining tree-cutting permissions on additionally allotted land. This forced higher market procurement of iron ore for pellet production.
  • Project delays and deferrals. The proposed 1 MnT Integrated Steel Plant and 250 MW captive solar power plant have been kept in abeyance due to delays in approvals and on-the-ground challenges. The 250 MW solar project is also affected by the CRM project’s relocation to Maharashtra and delay in land allotment.
  • CRM relocation and revised timeline. The 0.7 MnT CRM Complex is being relocated from Chhattisgarh to Sambhajinagar, Maharashtra. Land allotment approval is expected in August 2026, construction is expected to start from October 2026, and commissioning is now scheduled for December 2027.
  • Pellet segment softness. Q1 FY27 pellet production declined 7% QoQ and pellet sales declined 9% QoQ. FY26 pellet production was down 2% YoY and FY26 pellet sales down 30% YoY versus FY25.
  • Industry-level pellet constraints. India pellet capacity utilisation remains constrained at around 65%, weighed down by weak export demand, limited high-grade ore availability, and margin pressures. China’s steel consumption has peaked and is expected to gradually decline, with global iron ore prices facing downside risks as additional low-cost supply enters the market.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Revenue growth in Q1 FY27. Consolidated revenue rose 32% YoY and 9% QoQ to Rs. 1,750 crore, supported by healthy sales volumes and improved realisations. Realisations improved on both YoY and QoQ bases across key products.
  • Captive mining expansion. Ari Dongri mine capacity is set to expand from 2.35 MnT to 6 MnT, with Consent to Operate received from CECB in February 2026. Phased ramp-up begins Q3 FY27, with full-scale operations targeted from FY28. Total iron ore mining capacity more than doubled in FY26 to 6.7 MnT.
  • Beneficiation plant. The 5.4 MnT beneficiation plant remains on track for commissioning in Q3 FY27, enabling higher captive mining, improved raw material availability, and cost efficiencies. Full benefits of expanded pellet capacity and improved profitability are expected from Q4 FY27. Capex of Rs. 218 crore incurred up to 30 June 2026.
  • Pellet expansion commissioned. Pellet capacity increased 74% to 4.7 MnT with capex of Rs. 600 crore. Capacity utilisation stood at 77% in Q1 FY27 and is expected to ramp to 85% in FY27.
  • Renewable energy and BESS. A 25 MW captive solar plant was commissioned and grid-synchronised on 19 May 2026, replacing high-cost DISCOM power at captive mining operations. A 100 MW solar project is targeted for completion by end-September 2026. The 20 GWh BESS project, executed through Godawari New Energy Private Limited, is slated for commissioning in Q1 FY28, with capex of Rs. 501 crore incurred up to 30 June 2026. Maharashtra approved multiple incentives including electricity duty and stamp duty exemptions, power tariff subsidy, interest and capital subsidies, SGST-linked incentives, and ESI/EPF reimbursements.
  • Value-added steel foray. The 0.7 MnT CRM Complex has a planned capex of Rs. 1,100 crore, funded via debt of Rs. 550 crore (bank sanction received) and internal accruals. It is registered under PLI 1.1 and PLI 1.2 incentives. Orders placed for key equipment lines including Pickling Line, CGL lines, Colour Coating Line, and Acid Regeneration Plant.
  • WHRB and decarbonisation. A 6.91 MW WHRB plant commenced commercial operations at Siltara, raising total WHRB capacity to 49 MW. A 45 MWh BESS project for captive solar is targeted for commissioning by Q3 FY27. GPIL joined the India Green Steel Coalition and is targeting Net Zero Carbon Emissions by 2050.
  • Balance sheet strength. Net cash of Rs. 837 crore in FY26. CRISIL reaffirmed long-term and short-term credit ratings at AA-/Stable and A1+ respectively. The company exited Ardent Steel by reducing its stake from 37.85% to 0%.
  • Vision 2030. Targets approximately 4x increase in revenue and approximately 3x growth in EBITDA and PAT, from FY26 baselines of Rs. 5,381 crore revenue, Rs. 1,253 crore EBITDA, and Rs. 802 crore PAT.
  • Domestic demand outlook. India’s crude steel production rose approximately 3% YoY in Q1 FY27. Government infrastructure spending, housing, railways, and manufacturing investments are expected to sustain steel demand. India’s iron ore production is projected to rise to 340–345 MnT in FY27 from approximately 316 MnT in FY26.

Key Risks

  • Approval and land-related execution risk. Delays in approvals and on-the-ground challenges have already caused the Integrated Steel Plant and 250 MW solar project to be kept in abeyance, and contributed to the iron ore mining volume decline through delayed tree-cutting permissions.
  • Commodity price and input cost volatility. Elevated coal prices amid the West Asia crisis and increased market sourcing of iron ore pressured margins. Global iron ore prices face downside risks from additional low-cost supply, including Guinea’s Simandou project ramping up.
  • China demand decline. China’s steel consumption has peaked and is expected to be in gradual decline, which could affect global iron ore and steel markets.
  • Pellet industry utilisation constraints. Utilisation at around 65% is weighed down by weak export demand, limited high-grade ore availability, and margin pressures.
  • Project execution and funding risk. The CRM Complex, BESS project, and solar projects involve significant capex and are subject to revised timelines. The CRM project commissioning is now scheduled for December 2027.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. The company notes that statements concerning future growth prospects involve risks and uncertainties including fiscal policy, competition, inflationary pressures, and general economic conditions affecting demand, supply, and price conditions in domestic and international markets.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • FY27 production guidance. Management guided FY27 iron ore mining at 3.4 MnT, iron ore pellets at 4.0 MnT, sponge iron at 0.65 MnT, steel billets at 0.525 MnT, rolled products at 0.44 MnT, and ferro alloys at 95,000 tonnes. Q1 FY27 achievement was 16% for iron ore mining, 23% for pellets, 26% for sponge iron, 22% for steel billets, 22% for rolled products, and 29% for ferro alloys.
  • Margin recovery guidance. Management expects margins to recover from Q4 FY27 with the commissioning of the beneficiation plant, enabling use of captive iron ore for pellet production. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 19.1% versus 27.26% in Q4 FY26 and 24.49% in Q1 FY26.
  • Pellet utilisation guidance. Management expects the 4.7 MnT pellet plant to ramp up to 85% utilisation in FY27 from 77% in Q1 FY27.
  • Beneficiation plant timeline. Management stated the 5.4 MnT beneficiation plant remains on track for commissioning in Q3 FY27, with full benefits expected from Q4 FY27.
  • BESS timeline. Management stated the 20 GWh BESS project is progressing well and slated for commissioning in Q1 FY28.
  • CRM timeline. Revised expected commissioning is December 2027, with construction expected to start from October 2026 and land allotment approval expected in August 2026.
  • Solar timeline. The 100 MW solar project is targeted for completion by end-September 2026. The 45 MWh BESS project for captive solar is targeted for commissioning by Q3 FY27.
  • Vision 2030. Management targets approximately 4x revenue growth and approximately 3x EBITDA and PAT growth by 2030 from FY26 levels.
  • Q1 FY27 standalone and consolidated results. Standalone total income was Rs. 1,519.76 crore with net profit of Rs. 198.90 crore. Consolidated total income was Rs. 1,783.75 crore with net profit of Rs. 222.37 crore.

Broker Narrative

The broker started with a Buy on strong Indian infrastructure/commercial demand, export/VAP growth through Dubai, and APAT’s cost advantage, while flagging election, housing, Chinese-import, and US-election risks. By Dec-25 the call was reduced to Add, with near-term caution shifting to weak steel pricing, input-cost margin pressure, deferred Ari Dongri benefits, and pellet-plant shutdowns, while new growth drivers were added in BESS, pellet capacity, and mining backward integration. Persistent themes were steel demand recovery, cost/backward integration, and volume expansion; the early export/water-transport emphasis gave way to domestic energy-storage and pellet/mining catalysts.

Optimism that failed

  • The Ari Dongri mining expansion’s financial benefits, expected to aid cost control and backward integration, were deferred to FY27E/H2FY27E once the ore beneficiation plant starts, so the earlier near-term benefit did not materialize.
