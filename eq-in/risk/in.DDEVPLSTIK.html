DDEVPLSTIK — Q1FY27 Earnings Call and Related Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical and trade disruption: The quarter began amid a challenging global operating environment marked by supply chain disruptions, commodity price volatility and external uncertainties. Management specifically referenced the war situation and the Hormuz situation, and noted that shipments faced issues with vessel availability in the last week of March.
  • Raw material price volatility: Raw material prices were highly volatile, changing weekly and sometimes two or three times a week, making it difficult to predict price movements. Resin prices had risen sharply around March–April. Management indicated that prices had stabilised relative to March–April, but expected a gradual 3–6 month decline to earlier levels once the war and Hormuz situation cleared.
  • Softer domestic demand: The first quarter was described as full of uncertainty, with local demand falling because of very high prices.
  • Export pricing uncertainty: In exports, only indicative rates and timelines could be quoted, and a volatility/war-risk premium had to be added. Prices marginally came down in July, and average export selling prices were expected to be comparatively lower.
  • Margin sustainability question: The elevated EBITDA per ton of INR19.6 achieved in the quarter was described as potentially not sustainable every quarter, though it could persist for a few months. The volatility premium of roughly INR2.5–3 gained in the quarter was not expected to repeat at the same level.
  • Working capital and finance cost pressure: A sudden increase in raw material prices in March left March inventory and debtors comparatively higher, requiring alternative working capital financing and raising finance costs. The cash conversion cycle rose in Q1 before consolidating; management expects it to return to 55–60 days.
  • BESS timeline slippage: The BESS business was shifted from West to East and may be delayed by a couple of quarters. Separately, most BESS bids in the market were at low prices, with EMDs being forfeited and projects going to retendering, delaying those projects as well.
  • Capacity utilisation drag: Average capacity utilisation was around 66%, with the moderation primarily attributable to the new 48,000 MTPA Bhiwadi capacity having limited operating time during the period. Bhiwadi was running at roughly 20–25% utilisation.
  • Limited order visibility: Order books are generally spot and not beyond 10–15 or 20 days, limiting visibility to about July–August. Management flagged uncertainty over how long the export business would continue month-on-month, citing Iran-related developments.
  • Sector growth ceiling: The product category served by the company’s cable customers is growing at an average of 12–13%, with the additional 2–3% targeted only through market share gains.
  • No current trade protection: There is no anti-dumping duty investigation on XLPE, HFFR or PVC compounds, nor BIS discussion on the compounds, though BIS has been implemented on some raw materials such as polymers and PVC resin.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Electrification and infrastructure cycle: India is seen as entering a multi-decade electrification and infrastructure expansion cycle. India’s installed power generation capacity is expected to nearly double from approximately 442 GW in FY24 to around 900 GW by FY32, supported by INR9 trillion of transmission infrastructure investment, generating sustained demand for distribution, power and specialty transformers and the wire and cable ecosystem.
  • Digital economy demand: Investments in data centres, semiconductor ecosystems, renewable energy infrastructure, industrial corridors and advanced manufacturing are increasing demand for high-performance, specialised cable solutions, driving a shift from low-voltage to medium, high-voltage and extra high-voltage applications with superior realisations and entry barriers.
  • Trade architecture: The India-EU FTA is expected to provide preferential access to more than 99% of Indian exports. The European Union FTA is expected to directly benefit the company, which already exports to a couple of customers in Europe in good quantities; lower duties would increase competitiveness. The UK FTA is expected to benefit indirectly as Indian cable companies target the UK market aggressively.
  • Export track record: Despite geopolitical disruptions, exports grew at a 16% CAGR between FY22 and FY26. Q1 revenue growth of 29% year-on-year was driven primarily by robust traction in the cables and wire segment in the export market, with close to INR300-plus crores of exports mainly to the MENA region.
  • Leadership position and scale: As of June 2026, total installed capacity was 3,16,400 MTPA, with more than 200 specialised compounds, a 50% market share in Sioplas, a leading presence in HFFR compounds in India, and an approximately more than one-third market share in XLPE compounds.
  • Bhiwadi expansion: The greenfield XLPE compound facility at Bhiwadi, Rajasthan, with 48,000 MTPA capacity, was commissioned in April 2026, taking installed XLPE capacity to 2,14,500 MTPA. It is expected to contribute incremental revenue of close to INR500-odd crores as utilisation scales up, with average utilisation of 50-plus percent expected for FY27.
  • Market share opportunity: The company has historically had lower market share in the medium voltage XLPE category, and the Bhiwadi plant was set up close to North Indian customers to increase share in the Indian market. Bhiwadi will serve existing customers, including those who have added CCV line capacity.
  • Product mix improvement: The company continues to improve product mix and geographical market mix quarter-on-quarter, which is gradually leading to a better margin profile. Engineering plastics is a more complex, niche segment where the company aims to provide solutions rather than commodity products.
  • BESS entry: A focused strategic entry into battery energy storage systems is seen as a natural extension of the company’s understanding of the power ecosystem, with storage becoming increasingly critical for grid stability as renewable share rises. Phase 1 is a 1 GWh capacity with an expected investment within INR150–200 crores including capex and working capital margin, funded from internal accruals, with annual turnover potential of INR900 crores depending on sell prices. Initial model is supply, moving to EPC over time; BOO and BOOT models are not being eyed for an immediate start. Initial EBITDA margin expectation is 6–8%, improving by 2–3% at each stage, with a long-term target range of 11–15%. The 1 GWh timeline was indicated as mid-FY29.
  • East India expansion: The company is building capabilities in East for medium voltage cables, with capacity expected by end of the fiscal year or first quarter; investment is already committed. West Bengal has announced initiatives including addressing land availability issues, and an incentive policy was expected by end of August, with the company awaiting the policy before finalising location, though it is clear the location will be in Bengal.
  • Vapi consolidation: The company is consolidating products at a new Vapi site, gradually moving some products from multiple plants or sites with limited space.
  • Balance sheet strength: Growth has been achieved with zero rejections, a robust balance sheet and a consistent record of sustainable value creation.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued geopolitical uncertainty, particularly around Hormuz, the availability of material and prices, makes it difficult to predict how long export business will continue month-on-month.
  • Raw material price volatility and the pass-through nature of the business mean realisations can fluctuate; margins cannot expand in line with the 30% realisation increase.
  • Demand postponement risk if crude remains elevated, as project costs would rise and customers might slow down, though management did not see this happening at the time of the call.
  • BESS execution risk: competing with EPC players, the need to establish credibility and track record, and dependence on retendering of low-priced bids.
  • Dependence on customer capacity additions for Bhiwadi volumes, since the plant will supply existing customers.
  • Working capital and finance cost sensitivity to sudden raw material price spikes.
  • Limited forward visibility due to spot order books of 10–20 days.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • FY27 revenue growth guidance: Management guided to approximately 13–15% growth for FY27, with 15% volume growth targeted. Q1 volume growth was only about 1%, which management attributed to uncertainty and high prices. Management maintained confidence in achieving 15% volume growth for the year, citing the new capacity ramping up over another couple of months, and stated that the annual guidance would definitely be surpassed given Q1 was proportionately better than expected.
  • FY27 tonnage target: Management targeted 2,31,000 tons for the fiscal year against Q1 tonnage of 52,163 tons, expecting the remaining roughly 1,80,000 tons at about 60,000 tons per quarter. Utilisation was expected around 70% for the year against capacity of 3,34,400 MTPA by year-end.
  • EBITDA margin guidance: Management’s stated target is INR16–17 per ton, or 10–12% EBITDA margin. Q1FY27 delivered EBITDA per ton of INR19.6 and 10% margin, with EBITDA growing 27% year-on-year and crossing INR100 crores for the first time. Management said the INR19.6 level may not be sustainable every quarter but could persist for a few months, with the volatility premium of roughly INR2.5–3 not expected to repeat at the same level. The underlying range was described as INR15–16.5–17, improving quarter-on-quarter.
  • Bhiwadi utilisation: Guided at 50-plus percent average for FY27, against actual running at roughly 20–25% at the time of the call.
  • BESS margins: Guided at 6–8% initially in the supply model, improving by 2–3% at each stage, with a long-term target of 11–15%.
  • BESS capex and funding: Phase 1 requirement expected within INR200-odd crores, fully funded from internal accruals; the 1 GWh investment was described as within INR150–200 crores including capex and working capital margin.
  • Cash conversion cycle: Expected to return to 55–60 days, after rising in Q1 due to turmoil and uncertainty.
  • Raw material prices: Expected to gradually decline to earlier levels over 3–6 months once the war and Hormuz situation clear, though not overnight.
  • Q1FY27 reported performance: Revenue grew 29% year-on-year; EBITDA grew 27% year-on-year at 10% margin; profit after tax was INR64-odd crores, up 22% year-on-year. Total income for the quarter was Rs. 99,912.33 lacs and net profit was Rs. 6,379.34 lacs.
