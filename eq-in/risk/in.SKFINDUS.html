SKF India (Industrial) Limited — Q1 FY 2026-27 Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure from one-time and external factors. Q1 FY27 PBT margin came in at 9.0%, down 56 bps quarter-on-quarter from 9.5%. Management attributed this to two main causes: a forex loss of approximately INR 147 million (driven by rupee depreciation linked to the Middle East conflict, impacting imported products primarily from Europe), and approximately INR 150 million of one-time demerger-related IT infrastructure separation costs. Combined, these represented roughly a 3% adverse impact on margins.

Demerger transition costs. The company continues to incur IT separation expenses as it splits infrastructure from the automotive entity. These costs are expected to continue for the next two quarters until AB SKF globally separates its automotive business, after which they should stop. Additional one-time outflows included charges for name change and land transfer from SKF India Limited to SKF India (Industrial) Limited, which weighed on operating cash flow (INR 547 million in Q1 versus INR 1,864 million in Q4 FY26).

Weak monsoon and inflation. Irregular monsoon conditions adversely affected fuel and food prices, with FY27 inflation expected in the 4–6% range. Management noted inflation could worsen if monsoons remain weak.

Capacity constraints. The company is currently space-constrained, which limits how quickly new manufacturing lines can be added. Most new installations are tied to the new Pune plant, expected to begin production in 2028. Until then, the company is upgrading existing machinery to drive more output.

Margin moderation versus prior guidance. Management acknowledged that the current 14–16% PBT margin guidance is lower than an earlier 16–19% guidance, citing investments and localization-driven margin pressure over the next couple of years.

Early-stage new opportunities. Humanoid robotics and data center-related products remain early-stage in India, with limited domestic manufacturing and most OEMs located outside the country. These are not yet significant contributors.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong macro backdrop. India’s GDP growth is forecast at 6.5–7.5% (RBI forecast 6.7% for FY27). Industrial production rebounded strongly, signaling improved manufacturing momentum. Construction is expected to grow 10% year-on-year, steel production remains above 42 MT, and India is now the fourth-largest wind capacity in the world, growing at approximately 10% year-on-year.

Revenue growth. Revenue from operations was INR 9,708 million for the quarter, up 18.3% year-on-year (with management assumptions for comparability) and up 2.6% quarter-on-quarter. Strong performance was delivered in wind, general machinery, and agriculture. Rail was also solid.

Order wins. The company won a significant INR 140 crore order with a gearbox manufacturer (spread over approximately one year) and an INR 35 crore contract with a leading tractor OEM for a customized product. Management noted the order book continues to grow and has seen sustained growth over recent quarters.

Localization investments. A new Tapered Roller Bearing (TRB) line was operationalized in Pune, adding capacity of 3 million units annually, serving agriculture, gearbox, and aftermarket segments, and supporting both India and export markets. An additional TRB channel is planned by early 2027.

Innovation pipeline. New products include railway wheel-set bearing solutions extending maintenance intervals by up to 20%, bearings capable of operating up to 250°C for the food segment, energy-efficient hybrid ceramic bearings for electric drives, and customized cylindrical roller bearings for agricultural OEMs.

Solutions business. The solutions business (services, remanufacturing, condition monitoring, predictive maintenance, and O&M) currently represents around 6–7% of India business, with management seeing significant growth potential and investing in technology and go-to-market capabilities.

New Pune plant. A INR 900–950 crore investment is planned for a state-of-the-art Pune plant, progressing as per plan, with production expected to start in 2028. Payback is estimated at five to seven years.

Segment diversification. Management highlighted that SKF operates across all segments without overreliance on any single one, with major companies planning to double capacity by 2030 across infrastructure, steel, and cement. Railways and renewable energy (wind) were cited as key growth drivers.

Aspiration to double the business. Management expressed an aspiration to double the business within approximately five years, though no firm timeline was committed.

3. Key Risks

  • Forex volatility: Rupee depreciation against other currencies, particularly linked to geopolitical events such as the Middle East conflict, can adversely impact margins on imported products.
  • Demerger execution risk: IT separation and related one-time costs are expected to continue for two more quarters; any delay in the global AB SKF automotive separation could extend these costs.
  • Margin trajectory uncertainty: Management indicated continued margin pressure over the next couple of years due to investments and localization, with improvement expected beyond 2028.
  • Capacity timing: The new Pune plant is not expected to produce until 2028, limiting the ability to add new lines in the interim.
  • Inflation and monsoon risk: Weak monsoons could worsen inflation, affecting input costs and demand.
  • Early-stage opportunities: Humanoid robotics and data centers are not yet significant and depend on future market development in India.
  • Segment cyclicality: While diversified, all segments are cyclical, exposing the company to broader economic downturns.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Revenue: Management reported 18.3% year-on-year growth (with assumptions for comparability) and 2.6% quarter-on-quarter growth, describing it as a “great start” and “solid.” This aligns with the stated strong performance across wind, general machinery, and agriculture.

Margins: Management had earlier guided to 16–19% PBT margin, which was moderated to 14–16%. Observed Q1 FY27 margin was 9.0%, below the guided range. Management attributed the gap to one-time demerger costs and forex losses, stating that adding these back would bring margins to approximately 12%. Management expressed an ambition to reach closer to 17–19% over the longer term but declined to commit beyond 2028.

Cash flow: Operating cash flow was INR 547 million with 63% cash conversion. Management described cash generation as “solid” and noted the drop from Q4 FY26 was due to one-time name change and land transfer charges.

Order book: Management stated the order book is growing and has seen sustained growth over recent quarters, consistent with the announced order wins (INR 140 crore gearbox order and INR 35 crore tractor OEM contract).

Capex: The INR 900–950 crore Pune plant investment is progressing as per plan, with production expected from 2028. Management confirmed an additional TRB channel by early 2027 despite current capacity constraints.

Doubling aspiration: Management described doubling the business as an aspiration, ideally within five years, but did not commit to a firm timeline.
