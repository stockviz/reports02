Headwinds and Challenges

Coforge faces a demand environment in which enterprises struggle to connect intelligence across engineering, product management, support, operations, and enterprise workflows at scale. Hi-Tech companies are under growing pressure to accelerate product development, reduce engineering costs, improve customer experience, and respond quickly to market shifts.

The company has also experienced governance-related headwinds. Mr. DK Singh resigned as Non-Executive Independent Director and Chairperson of the Nomination and Remuneration Committee effective immediately, following internal audit review findings regarding the Board Evaluation Exercise. The resignation letter cited industry headwinds, differences and tension between Independent and Executive Directors, and the resignations of both Chairman Mr. OP Bhatt and NRC Chair Mr. DK Singh.

Internal auditor observations regarding the Board Evaluation Process included that relevant reports were not made available to other members of the Board (including independent directors) at the instruction of the Chairman of the Board; that presentation of findings to the NRC and Board did not cover all relevant aspects; and that the Chairman’s category received the lowest rating in the Reports, which was not disclosed or discussed before the NRC or Board. The internal audit/governance review remains ongoing.

At the 34th Annual General Meeting held on August 24, 2026, Resolution 4 regarding the re-appointment of Mr. OP Bhatt as an Independent Director was not passed by members, while Resolutions 1, 2, 3, and 5 were duly passed.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Coforge announced an expanded focus on helping Hi-Tech enterprises move beyond AI experimentation and isolated use cases to operationalize AI at scale. Its approach combines Coforge Nuuron, a composable enterprise AI operating layer, with Momentuum blue, a specialized operating model embedding Forward Deployed Engineers and AI agents.

The company is positioned to help Hi-Tech enterprises operationalize AI at scale across engineering, operations, and business decision-making by combining a next-generation AI operating system with Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) to convert AI investments into measurable business outcomes. This is expected to result in faster product development cycles, improved operational performance, more informed decision-making, and accelerated paths to growth.

The Board has worked in close cooperation with all business strategies and governance decisions being approved unanimously, enabling key decisions to be taken expeditiously. These include the divestment of the AdvantageGo business, exit from the data centre business, acquisition of Encora, execution of the Sabre contract, and the decision to exit the loss-making India Government business. The company has achieved significant growth despite industry headwinds and created significant shareholder value.

Key Risks

The internal audit observations regarding the Board Evaluation Process represent a governance risk, including the non-availability of relevant reports to independent directors, incomplete presentation of findings to the NRC and Board, and non-disclosure of the Chairman’s lowest rating. The internal audit/governance review remains ongoing.

The Board has stated that the Board evaluation process is entirely distinct from the Company’s financial reporting and audit processes, and that matters relating to the evaluation of Board members do not concern the Company’s financial statements, accounting policies, revenue, or profitability, having no bearing on the Company’s financial or operational performance or its business and growth outlook.

The resignation of Mr. DK Singh cited differences and tension between Independent and Executive Directors, which represents a board-level governance risk. The non-passing of Resolution 4 regarding Mr. OP Bhatt’s re-appointment as Independent Director at the AGM is a further governance development.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Management stated through Rajan Khattar that the next phase of AI transformation will be defined by business outcomes rather than AI features, and that the challenge now is operationalizing AI across engineering, operations, and customer-facing functions to improve productivity, accelerate innovation, and create sustainable competitive advantage.

Mr. Vivek Sharma has been appointed as interim Chair till January 31, 2027, and Ms. Beth Boucher has been designated as the Chairperson of the NRC. Mr. Vivek Sharma will lead a global search for adding additional independent directors to the board, oversee the process of electing the new Chair, and the Board will continue to act in the best interest of all stakeholders with integrity and transparency without any change in direction and priorities.

The broker narrative evolved from macro and industry-level concerns (US/Europe tech spending weakness, M&E and EU MDR-driven medical device moderation) alongside vertical-specific tailwinds (automotive electrification, aerospace, IT services demand) toward company-specific execution drivers (deal wins, cash conversion, Encora integration) and ultimately governance risk (O.P. Bhatt’s resignation). The long-term IT demand thesis remained a constant positive thread, while acute headwinds shifted from global macro pressures to internal corporate governance issues.

Fears that came true

  • Persistent weak macro environment in the US and Europe materialized, correlating with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes featuring sharply negative actual returns such as -25.6% (March 2025) and -19.0% (June 2025).
  • Continued pressure on near-term tech spending at large enterprises persisted, reflected in DISAPPOINTMENT results across late 2023 and recurring through 2025, including multiple Sell and Reduce calls that failed.
  • Media & Entertainment spending pressure and EU MDR-driven moderation in medical devices contributed to muted near-term results, consistent with the DISAPPOINTMENT cluster in early-to-mid 2025.

Optimism that failed

  • Very strong demand in the automotive vertical and aerospace did not consistently offset broader headwinds, as evidenced by the -25.6% actual return on Dolat Capital’s Accumulate call (March 2025) and the -19.0% return on Motilal Oswal’s Buy call (June 2025).
  • The expectation of strong long-term demand and senior leadership talent tailwinds translating into steady outperformance failed during periods of sharp drawdown, notably the -19.0% return in June 2025 and -3.2% in January 2025.
