Rajoo Engineers — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Margin pressure from input and logistics costs. The Managing Director’s message for Q1 FY27 states that the quarter witnessed pressure on margins due to elevated raw material prices and higher procurement and logistics costs arising from global geopolitical developments. Consolidated EBITDA margin (excluding other income) fell to 17.65% in Q1 FY27 from 21.84% in Q1 FY26, a decline of 419 bps, and PAT margin fell to 14.10% from 17.65%, a decline of 355 bps. Basic EPS declined 5.43% year-on-year to Rs. 0.87 even as revenue grew 44.66%.
  • Raw material volatility and geopolitical uncertainty. Management explicitly flags that global geopolitical uncertainties and raw material volatility may create near-term challenges, and states that commodity prices and supply chain dynamics continue to be monitored closely.
  • Cost base rising faster than revenue in some lines. In Q1 FY27, raw materials rose to Rs. 79.48 crore from Rs. 50.49 crore a year earlier, employee cost to Rs. 8.46 crore from Rs. 5.70 crore, and other expenses to Rs. 13.41 crore from Rs. 10.30 crore. Depreciation (Rs. 2.18 crore vs Rs. 1.05 crore) and interest (Rs. 0.62 crore vs Rs. 0.15 crore) also increased year-on-year.
  • Sequential comparison highlights volatility. Q4 FY26 was a weak quarter, with EBITDA (excluding other income) of Rs. 3.20 crore, EBITDA margin of 4.02%, PAT of Rs. 2.36 crore and basic EPS of Rs. 0.11, against Q1 FY27 EBITDA of Rs. 21.72 crore and PAT of Rs. 17.35 crore. Management attributes the Q1 FY27 improvement partly to dispatch of orders carried forward from previous quarters, indicating quarter-to-quarter lumpiness.
  • Accounting and regulatory transition items. The subsidiary transitioned from Indian GAAP to Ind AS effective April 1, 2026, requiring restatement of comparative consolidated figures for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026. Separately, four labour codes became effective from November 21, 2025; the financial impact is not considered material.
  • Industry structure. The plastics processing machinery industry is described as labour intensive while being highly technical, with 20+ major manufacturers and nearly 300+ small and medium manufacturers, indicating a competitive landscape.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong Q1 FY27 revenue growth. Revenue from operations was Rs. 123.07 crore in Q1 FY27 versus Rs. 85.07 crore in Q1 FY26, up 44.66% year-on-year, attributed to production and dispatches remaining in full swing throughout the period. EBITDA (excluding other income) rose 16.90% year-on-year to Rs. 21.72 crore.
  • FY26 full-year performance. Revenue from operations was Rs. 344.25 crore in FY26 versus Rs. 253.66 crore in FY25, up 35.72%, driven by improved demand across key markets, execution of a higher order backlog, normalization of supply chain conditions (excluding the last month of the year), increased volumes, better capacity utilization and a favourable product mix. EBITDA (excluding other income) rose 34.23% to Rs. 62.16 crore. PAT grew 29.67% to Rs. 49.43 crore and net profit 29.85% to Rs. 49.46 crore.
  • High capacity utilization and order pipeline. Management states production facilities operated at high-capacity utilization in Q1 FY27 and that the company is well positioned with a strong order pipeline, sound manufacturing capabilities and a resilient business model.
  • Demand drivers in flexible packaging and polymer processing. Management cites increasing demand for sustainable packaging solutions, modernization of manufacturing infrastructure and growing investments by customers. The industry overview notes extrusion machinery volumes growing at a 10.4% seven-year CAGR (FY19–FY25) and extrusion machinery investment growing at a 15.8% CAGR over the same period.
  • Southeast Asia partnership. A strategic partnership with Rieckermann covering Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines is intended to expand market reach and strengthen customer support across Southeast Asia, advance the international growth strategy, and create new opportunities for long-term growth. The partnership covers blown film extrusion, extrusion coating & lamination, sheet extrusion and thermoformers.
  • Capacity, capability and footprint expansion. Recent initiatives include a major upgrade of Shree Yantralaya with machines from Okuma, Huron and Jyoti, enabling end-to-end in-house production of large, complex, high-value components and 5-axis multitasking; a strategic investment of Rs. 300 million in Yantralaya; a 60% majority stake in Kohli Printing and Converting Machines Pvt. Ltd.; a signed NBIO for a strategic acquisition in the machinery segment; a foundation for a manufacturing park on 80-acre non-agricultural land; and fund raising through QIP (Rs. 160 crore raised, with authorization up to Rs. 225 crore). The company also reports high-value orders including Rs. 31 crore from Europe and a Rs. 137 crore order with Rs. 106 crore of export orders.
  • Product innovation and recognition. Launches cited include Proex series, PentaFoil (including PentaFoil POD and Proex Pentafoil), Lamina E (India’s first mono & multi-layer EVA/POE sheet line), Dispotilt, 5-axis machines, and India’s biggest 3-layer blown film with vertical spiral die. The company also cites awards including SIES SOP STAR AWARD 2025, Gujarat Best Employer Brand 2025, and a Women Entrepreneur of the Year award for Mrs. Khushboo Chandrakant Doshi.
  • Customer loyalty and global reach. Approximately 60% of business each year is generated through repeat orders, with marquee customers including Uflex, Reliance Industries, Indian Oil, Binny Wads, Suvi International, Synthetic Packaging, Abhinav Industries and S D International. Installations span more than 70 countries (the partnership announcement cites a customer base spanning over 78 nations).
  • Renewable energy opportunity. India’s total renewable energy installed capacity reached 288.58 GW as of June 2026, ranking 3rd globally, with a 500 GW target by 2030. The company has installed a 304.64 KW solar power plant in Gujarat and formed Shrutina Nexgen Solar LLP for captive green power.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity price and supply chain risk. Elevated raw material prices and higher procurement and logistics costs tied to global geopolitical developments pressured Q1 FY27 margins; management continues to monitor these dynamics.
  • Margin sustainability. Despite revenue growth, EBITDA margin contracted 419 bps year-on-year in Q1 FY27 and 20 bps for FY26 (18.06% vs 18.26%); PAT margin contracted 355 bps in Q1 FY27 and 67 bps for FY26. EPS declined 5.43% year-on-year in Q1 FY27.
  • Earnings volatility. The sharp swing between Q4 FY26 (EBITDA Rs. 3.20 crore, PAT Rs. 2.36 crore) and Q1 FY27 (EBITDA Rs. 21.72 crore, PAT Rs. 17.35 crore) illustrates exposure to order timing and dispatch schedules, including carry-forward orders.
  • Dependence on associate/JV and subsidiary performance. Share of profit of associate was Rs. 0.10 crore in Q1 FY27 versus Rs. 0.66 crore in Q1 FY26 and Rs. 0.92 crore in Q4 FY26. The subsidiary contributed total revenue of Rs. 4,638.70 lakhs and PAT of Rs. 454.24 lakhs in Q1 FY27; the joint venture contributed total revenue of Rs. 446.10 lakhs and PAT of Rs. 19.39 lakhs.
  • Accounting transition risk. The subsidiary’s Ind AS transition required restatement of comparatives, with adjustments including gratuity remeasurement (Rs. 97.44 lakhs), warranty provisions (Rs. 39.06 lakhs), expected credit loss provisioning (Rs. 3.67 lakhs), fair valuation of employee loans and financial liabilities, and related deferred tax, with goodwill and NCI adjusted accordingly.
  • Regulatory and compliance change. The four labour codes effective November 21, 2025 carry a financial impact not considered material, though draft Central Rules and FAQs are still being assessed.
  • Competitive and structural industry risk. The machinery building activity is described as labour intensive while highly technical, with a fragmented market of 20+ major and nearly 300+ small and medium manufacturers.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. The presentation states that forward-looking statements are not guarantees of future performance and are subject to known and unknown risks including Indian and international economic performance, industry performance, competition, strategy implementation, growth and expansion, technological change, and changes in revenue, income or cash flows.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Margin pressure acknowledged and observed. Management stated that Q1 FY27 saw margin pressure from elevated raw material prices and higher procurement and logistics costs, while noting EBITDA improved significantly on a sequential basis supported by higher capacity utilization and improved machine dispatches. Observed: EBITDA (excluding other income) rose to Rs. 21.72 crore in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 3.20 crore in Q4 FY26, while EBITDA margin fell to 17.65% from 21.84% year-on-year.
  • Execution and dispatch guidance versus results. Management attributed Q1 FY27 growth to robust execution, timely customer deliveries and dispatch of orders carried forward from previous quarters, with facilities at high-capacity utilization. Observed: revenue from operations rose 44.66% year-on-year to Rs. 123.07 crore.
  • Cost optimization focus versus results. Management stated continued focus on operational efficiency and cost optimisation and work towards improving procurement efficiencies and enhancing product mix. Observed: total expenditure rose to Rs. 101.35 crore in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 66.49 crore in Q1 FY26, with raw materials, employee cost and other expenses all higher year-on-year.
  • FY26 growth drivers versus results. Management attributed FY26 growth to improved demand, execution of a higher order backlog, normalization of supply chain conditions (excluding the last month of the year), increased volumes, better capacity utilization and favourable product mix, with EBITDA growth from improved operational performance and better absorption of fixed costs. Observed: FY26 revenue up 35.72% to Rs. 344.25 crore and EBITDA (excluding other income) up 34.23% to Rs. 62.16 crore, with EBITDA margin down 20 bps to 18.06%.
  • Long-term optimism versus near-term caution. Management expressed optimism about opportunities in flexible packaging and polymer processing supported by sustainable packaging demand, manufacturing modernization and customer investments, while cautioning that global geopolitical uncertainties and raw material volatility may create near-term challenges. Management stated the company is well positioned with a strong order pipeline, sound manufacturing capabilities and a resilient business model to deliver profitable growth over the long term.
  • Southeast Asia partnership guidance. Management described Southeast Asia as an important market and the Rieckermann partnership as a significant step in expanding regional reach, expressing intent to build a strong partnership and create new opportunities for long-term growth. No financial contribution from this partnership is quantified in the supplied material.
  • Dividend recommendation. The Board recommended a final dividend of Rs. 0.15 per equity share of Re. 1 each for FY 2025-26, subject to shareholder approval at the ensuing Annual General Meeting, with the 39th AGM scheduled for Tuesday, September 29, 2026 at 12:00 p.m. at the registered office in Veraval (Shapar), Rajkot, Gujarat.
