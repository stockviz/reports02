LMW Q1 FY26-27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance Versus Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Textile machinery demand remains subdued. Management noted that two years ago the company was running with delivery lead times of 14–15 months or more, but deliveries have since “come quite shrunk.” The current TMD order book stands at ₹3,200 crores, of which active orders are around ₹2,400 crores. Textile division capacity utilization “still hovers around 60 percent.”

Cost inflation across inputs and logistics. Commodity, fuel, gas, and transport costs have all risen. Management estimated costs could be up “anywhere up from between three to three and a half percent.” Specific operational dependencies cited include helium for sheet metal cutting and gas for special process units, where non-availability would halt production. Availability was “becoming an issue last quarter” and, while it has eased somewhat, remains a close watch item, with the company seeking four to five months of material pipeline visibility.

Currency pressure on imported content. The appreciation of the USD and euro against the rupee has impacted costs in both the Machine Tool Division and Textile Machinery. Management acknowledged this impact and said mitigation is being worked through cost savings, domestic substitution, and price discussions.

Machine Tool Division margin compression. Sequentially, MTD margins declined. Management attributed this to lower operational margin from reduced turnover rather than product mix alone, noting Q1 is seasonally lighter than Q4. The division is running at 75–80% capacity utilization.

Subsidiary losses. LMW Global posted a loss of ₹5.6 crores for the quarter (versus ₹5 crores in the prior quarter) on turnover of ₹51 crores. LMW China posted a loss of ₹7 crores (versus ₹11.5 crores in the prior quarter) on turnover of ₹11 crores, down sharply from ₹76 crores in the same quarter last year.

Weak export order book outside ATC. Management stated export order books are “not very strong” and that there is no long-term export order book. TMD subsidiary and MTD export orders are short-cycle, booked and delivered within three months on an LC/cash-and-delivery basis.

Middle East tensions. In response to a question on renewed Middle East tension, management confirmed impacts on raw material, commodity, and logistics costs, and emphasized supply chain resilience efforts over the prior two to three months.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong year-on-year growth. Revenue for the quarter ended June 2026 was ₹891 crores, up 24% from ₹722 crores in the quarter ended June 2025. PBT was ₹85 crores, up 151% from ₹34 crores a year earlier and up 18% from ₹72 crores in the prior quarter.

Secured TMD order book. The ₹3,200 crore TMD order book is described as secured, as only orders with a 10% deposit are included. Within this, approximately 70% is projects and 30% unitary machines.

State policy triggers. Management cited new state policies in Gujarat, Orissa, and Madhya Pradesh (including the Dhar region) as triggering activity. Gujarat’s new policy includes spinning within its subsidy scope. Management also referenced PLI-type schemes favoring large integrated players.

FTAs and export competitiveness. Management expects FTAs to support demand for spinning, noting that yarn spun in India is significantly exported and that other countries face similar energy cost challenges.

Spindle demand recovery expectation. Management stated that low spindle installation cannot persist across multiple years and that demand will return, though this year is expected to show gradual rather than sharp uptake. Cotton spinning margins are described as good; synthetic margins are under strain but still profitable, which management expects to drive modernization.

ATC order book and capacity expansion. ATC holds approximately ₹1,000 crores of orders for execution over three to three and a half years, fully tied up for execution. The order book mix is around 25% composite and 75% metallics. ATC revenue for the quarter was ₹60 crores (versus ₹57 crores prior quarter and ₹46 crores a year ago). A new ATC facility is planned with approximately ₹150 crores of land and building investment, with a timeline of 18–24 months.

ATC export orientation and stability. ATC is 90% export and 10% domestic; within turnover, 20% is composite and 80% metallics. Management noted export execution has been stable at 90% for at least the last four to five quarters, providing confidence in committing to the order book.

Machine Tool Division growth. MTD plus foundry revenue was ₹343 crores for the quarter (versus ₹352 crores prior quarter and ₹251 crores a year ago), with around 8% from foundry. Management said MTD has “done extremely well” year-on-year, driven by the machining center product portfolio. Automotive accounted for around 56% of MTD this quarter, with the balance across EMS, general engineering, and others. The J-series (six models, J1–J6) has good market acceptance, with J2 particularly well accepted in EMS.

Auto winder traction. Auto winder has been supplied in the south with good feedback; management expects to book more orders for further rollouts closer to the last quarter of the year.

Cost optimization. Management cited VRS costs in the current and prior quarters as part of ongoing cost optimization, and noted other expenses grew only 4% against 25% revenue growth due to tight cost control.

Enabling resolution for new divisions. An enabling resolution covers expansion into six new divisions, including pharma and specialty chemicals, EV, and an advanced technology center. Management characterized this as an enabling resolution covering multiple options, with any significant development to be disclosed to shareholders.

3. Key Risks

  • Demand timing uncertainty in textiles. Management explicitly stated the question is “how quickly it comes back,” with no sharp uptick expected this year.
  • Order book execution variability. Management noted historical concerns about “push ins and pull outs” in execution, though it stated the last two years have shown greater stability.
  • Cost inflation persistence. The 3–3.5% cost increase and supply chain availability concerns (helium, gas) pose ongoing operational risk.
  • Currency exposure. USD and euro appreciation against the rupee pressures costs in MTD and textile machinery, with mitigation dependent on domestic substitution and price pass-through.
  • Subsidiary performance. Continued losses at LMW Global and LMW China, with China revenue down sharply year-on-year.
  • Capacity utilization dependency for margins. MTD margin recovery is tied to higher turnover and capacity utilization; management declined to provide a timeline.
  • Interest rate sensitivity. A shareholder question raised the prospect of rising interest rates pressuring textile customers’ capex decisions; management did not directly address this.
  • Export order book concentration. Export orders are concentrated in ATC (approximately ₹900 crores over three to three and a half years) plus around ₹150 crores for immediate delivery, with no long-term export order book elsewhere.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Order book stability. Management stated that export execution has been stable at 90% for at least four to five quarters, supporting its ability to commit to the ATC order book of approximately ₹1,000 crores over three to three and a half years. This contrasts with its acknowledgment of historical execution variability.

Margin recovery. Management linked MTD margin recovery to higher turnover and capacity utilization, noting the division can comfortably do 15% more and has capacity for 20% more. It declined to provide a timeline for returning to the 12–14% EBIT margins seen historically, noting the current cost base is much larger and product mix more varied than when those margins were achieved on ₹300–400 crores of turnover.

ATC margins. Management attributed the current quarter’s higher ATC margin to a mix favoring assemblies over components, and stated it “will equate out to similar numbers of last year.” It also confirmed ATC margins are positively impacted by forex, with a significant forex impact in the prior quarter making comparisons less like-for-like.

Cost mitigation. Management stated it is working to offset currency and input cost impacts through savings, domestic procurement substitution where fit for purpose, and price revisions. It noted ATC and Foundry have clear contractual price revision clauses with customers, and similar actions are taken in Machine Tool.

Auto winder orders. Management guided that more orders for further rollouts would be booked closer to the last quarter of the year, following good feedback from southern supply.

New divisions. Management characterized the six-division expansion as an enabling resolution, stating that anything significant requiring disclosure will be published and informed to shareholders.

Capacity expansion. The ATC facility expansion is guided at approximately ₹150 crores for land and building over 18–24 months. MTD capex will be required to push capacity beyond current levels, with planning and investment to follow as machining center volumes grow.

Observed performance versus prior guidance. Revenue grew 24% year-on-year and PBT grew 151%, with management noting the Machine Tool Division “has done extremely well” year-on-year. However, sequentially MTD revenue declined from ₹352 crores to ₹343 crores and MTD margins declined, which management attributed to lower operational margin from reduced turnover. TMD revenue was roughly flat sequentially (₹482 crores versus ₹485 crores) but up 16% year-on-year. Consolidated revenue declined sequentially from ₹972 crores to ₹902 crores, and consolidated profit declined from ₹78 crores to ₹75 crores.
