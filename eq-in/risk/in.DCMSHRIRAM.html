DCM Shriram Limited — Q1 FY27 Analysis

Headwinds and Challenges

Geopolitical and macroeconomic pressures. The West Asia conflict disrupted energy markets and supply chains, embedding inflationary pressures and expectations of a prolonged higher interest rate environment. This volatility raised energy and freight costs across the chemical supply chain.

Monsoon disruption. An erratic and deficient start to the southwest monsoon, linked to El Niño, caused uneven regional rainfall distribution. Kharif sowing lagged by 15–20% nationally, and more in some of Bioseed’s markets. While July rains improved, the initial lag created a large void in sowing and strained rural consumption.

Chemicals. Excess capacity in China weighed on international chemical pricing. Caustic soda capacity utilization stood at 82% (up from 80% last year), with further improvement expected as downstream projects commission. Hydrogen peroxide faced oversupply across parts of Asia despite growing structural demand. Epichlorohydrin and epoxy markets saw volatility from feedstock movements and geopolitics; both plants operated at around 70% utilization.

Vinyl. A temporary basic customs duty waiver on PVC, combined with strong Chinese imports, caused a surge in imports that weighed on domestic PVC sales. PVC volumes fell 25% year-on-year despite prices rising 22%. Demand was subdued by labor shortages, heatwave conditions, and cautious buying, with softness expected to persist through the monsoon in Q2 FY27.

Sugar and Ethanol. Domestic sugar volumes fell 8% on lower offtake. Ethanol volumes were flat while prices declined 4% due to a change in sales mix. Higher sugar production costs from increased cane prices partly offset improved realizations. Management noted the ethanol blending ecosystem still requires decisive government policy intervention on feedstock pricing and alternate usage mandates, and that a clear implementation roadmap and periodic alignment of ethanol procurement prices with sugarcane cost remain important.

Fertilizer. Natural gas prices rose sharply following West Asia supply disruptions — gas was at $15.5/mmbtu versus $13.9/mmbtu last year. Subsidy outstanding rose to ₹292 crore as of June 30, 2026, versus ₹236 crore a year earlier and ₹189 crore as of March 31, 2026. Continued geopolitical uncertainty may impact LNG availability and lead to higher subsidy outstanding.

Bioseed. The Kharif season is the main season for this business and was particularly challenging. Delayed monsoon reduced demand, with revenues down 26% and PBDIT at negative ₹9 crore versus positive ₹42 crore last year, on lower corn and paddy volumes and lower cotton margins. Significantly higher productivity during the 2025-26 seed production season, owing to favorable climate, is pressuring margins and building higher inventory.

Fenesta. Order intake rose only 4%, which management described as somewhat lower than expected, partly due to West Asia crisis-driven delays in customer decisions. Margins were affected by product mix changes and upfront investments in new platforms and distribution.

Income tax penalty. On June 29, 2026, the Assessment Unit, Income Tax Department levied a penalty of ₹1.59 crore under Section 270A for FY 2021-22 (AY 2022-23) relating to additions on account of sale of buildings. The company stated it will take appropriate legal action, noting the underlying matter is under adjudication before the ITAT (heard May 26, 2026, order awaited) and that there is no material financial or operational impact beyond the penalty amount.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Chemicals downstream integration. Aluminum chloride (100 TPD) and calcium chloride (225 TPD) projects at Bharuch are in final pre-commissioning stages, with commercial production expected in Q2 FY27. After these projects, almost 50% of chlorine will be captively consumed; including pipeline partnerships and regional tie-ups, almost 85% of chlorine will be tied up once commissioned. The epichlorohydrin plant (52,000 TPA) was commissioned in April 2026. A 36 KTPA formulated resins expansion at HSCL (₹101 crore investment, total capacity 50 KTPA) is expected by Q2 FY28. The proposed acquisition of salt works with 208,000 MTPA capacity in Gujarat is under regulatory approvals, targeted for Q3 FY27.

Renewable energy. A definitive agreement with Serentica Renewables covers 58 MW peak hybrid renewable energy for the Bharuch chemicals complex, with related infrastructure investment of ₹235 crore, taking Bharuch’s total renewable capacity to 108 MW. Upon commissioning, peak renewable capacity across Bharuch and Kota is expected to reach around 176 MW. The 68 MW peak Kota project is under commissioning, with average power injection of 25 MW in July. The company has invested in captive power efficiency over years, including a new power plant in 2019 and a 120 MW coal-based plant in 2024-25, replacing less efficient plants.

Vinyl policy support. The government reinstated basic customs duty on PVC imports, and DGFT notified a minimum import price of US$766 per metric ton on suspension grade PVC for six months (introduced July 24, extendable). These measures should support domestic PVC prices.

Sugar and Ethanol. The global sugar market is expected to shift into a deficit of around 0.7 million metric tons in the 2026-27 season, versus a surplus of around 2.5 million metric tons last year, due to lower production in Thailand and Europe. India is expected to end SS 2025-26 with closing stock of around 3.75–3.8 million metric tons (versus 5.1 million metric tons last year). Current prices are around ₹4,450 per quintal and expected to remain firm. Policy developments signaled an intention to prepare for blending beyond E20.

Fenesta. Revenue grew 22% on higher volumes in both project and retail segments. The order book is close to ₹1,000 crore and up 4%. The business is setting up a wooden doors manufacturing facility and an aluminum extrusion plant at Kota (first phase expected Q2 FY27). It operates 8 fabrication plants, one uPVC extrusion unit, one hardware plant (DNV), 430 dealers across 273 cities, 9 company-owned showrooms, and serves 976 cities in India with presence in 7 countries.

Shriram Farm Solutions. Revenue rose 2% with PBDIT up 22%, driven by higher realizations and improved margins across verticals. Four new products were launched from in-house R&D. Management emphasized farmer reach, R&D-led new products, and market credibility as drivers.

Fertilizer. Management stated domestic urea manufacturers are governed by Fertilizer Industry Coordination Committee rules, and that international prices do not directly affect the domestic industry, though higher gas prices can create cash flow issues that are a pass-through under government policy. The government has been timely in subsidy payments.

Portfolio strategy. Management stated the group intends to grow consistently across all businesses except Urea and Cement, with a strong focus on value-added businesses, and to pursue acquisitions or stakes in affiliated companies (e.g., the epoxy factory acquisition in Gujarat in October last year, and the DNV Global stake in Fenesta’s hardware supply chain).

Demerger. Management confirmed it intends to proceed with a demerger/reorganization of businesses, with the objective of making the application to the government within this financial year, though timing depends on internal work.

Key Risks

  • Continued geopolitical conflict in West Asia triggering logistical bottlenecks, energy shocks, and feedstock disruptions, with potential for intense price volatility and margin compression in global chemicals.
  • Chinese excess capacity and imports weighing on chemical and PVC pricing.
  • Monsoon variability and El Niño projections creating uncertainty for agriculture-linked businesses (Bioseed, Shriram Farm Solutions) and rural demand.
  • Soft domestic PVC demand expected through the monsoon in Q2 FY27.
  • Ethanol policy uncertainty regarding feedstock pricing, allocation across feedstocks, and procurement price alignment with sugarcane cost.
  • Higher inventory in Bioseed from strong 2025-26 seed production pressuring margins.
  • LNG availability risk and higher subsidy outstanding in Fertilizer.
  • Ongoing income tax penalty matter under appeal.
  • Debt levels: net debt rose to ₹1,649 crore as of June 30, 2026, from ₹1,481 crore a year earlier, due to two acquisitions totaling about ₹450 crore and capex of close to ₹1,000 crore. Management targets debt-to-EBITDA not breaching 1.5; current debt-to-EBITDA is about 1.1, with credit rating at AA+.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Caustic soda pricing. Management declined to make forward-looking statements but stated current ECU is just below ₹30,000 and expected to remain in that range or higher. Chlorine prices are currently in the minus ₹7,000 to minus ₹8,000 range. Observed: ECU realizations for Q1 FY27 were ₹35,761/MT, up 6.6% year-on-year and up 15% quarter-on-quarter.

Caustic utilization. Management stated 82% utilization in Q1 FY27, expected to improve as downstream projects commission. Observed: utilization was 82% versus 80% last year.

PVC policy support. Management stated the reinstatement of customs duty and MIP should support domestic PVC prices. Observed: these measures were notified in July 2026, after the quarter ended.

Sugar prices. Management stated prices should remain firm for the next couple of months. Observed: current prices around ₹4,450 per quintal, with domestic realizations in Q1 FY27 at ₹4,137/quintal, up 1.7% year-on-year.

Ethanol optimization. Management stated the company can optimize grain-based ethanol up to 260 KLD depending on margins, and does not plan to grow capacity. Observed: ethanol volumes were flat year-on-year at 412 lakh liters, with prices down 4% due to sales mix.

Fenesta growth. Management stated robust growth is expected to continue, with an order book close to ₹1,000 crore. Observed: revenue grew 22% year-on-year, PBDIT grew 13%, and order booking rose 4%.

Debt. Management stated net borrowing should be around similar levels by year-end, with some reduction of about ₹200-odd crore, depending on the sugar season, and that debt-to-EBITDA will not breach 1.5. Observed: net debt at ₹1,649 crore as of June 30, 2026, with debt-to-EBITDA at about 1.1.

Tax rate. Management stated that following the MAT credit recognition, cash tax outgo will be 19% for at least the foreseeable five years, while the P&L will reflect 25%. Observed: the company recognized ₹474.3 crore tax adjustment relating to prior years, including ₹376 crore MAT credit, and moved from a 35% to a 25% tax bracket.

Demerger. Management stated the objective is to make the application to the government within this financial year, while noting the process takes time and no firm timeframe can be given.

Capex. Management stated no major new capex has been announced beyond ongoing projects, with about ₹1,000 crore capex this year. Observed: the investments update lists ongoing and expected projects through Q2 FY28, including the Serentica renewable agreement and HSCL formulated resins expansion.

AGM outcomes. All ten resolutions set out in the notice for the 37th Annual General Meeting held on August 18, 2026, were passed with requisite majority, including adoption of financial statements, a final dividend of ₹4 per equity share, appointment/re-appointment of directors and independent directors, ratification of cost auditor remuneration, cancellation of forfeited shares, and revision of maximum remuneration for relatives of a director.
