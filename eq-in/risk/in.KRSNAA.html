Krsnaa Diagnostics — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin compression from upfront infrastructure costs. EBITDA margin fell to 25% in Q1 FY27 from 27% in Q1 FY26 (a decline of 221 bps), and PAT margin fell to 7% from 11%. Management attributes this to the fixed cost base of Rajasthan laboratories, equipment, manpower and logistics being carried ahead of full utilisation. Around 4,000 people were onboarded in Rajasthan, along with logistics across collection centres, ahead of full revenue realisation. Management describes this as the normal rhythm of an infrastructure-led model where capex is front-ended, rather than pricing or structural pressure in the core business; like-to-like business delivered stable margins broadly in line with previous quarters.

Rajasthan ramp slower than earlier stated aspiration. Rajasthan contributed ₹257 million of revenue in Q1 (described on the call as around ₹26 crores). Management guided to ₹100–150 crores for the full year on a conservative basis, with steady state closer to ₹150–175 crores, while noting the earlier stated aspiration of ₹200–250 crores for FY27 still stands as an aspiration. Management expects Rajasthan revenue to roughly double in the coming quarter.

Radiology revenue mix pressure. Radiology/pathology mix was 41:59 in the quarter. Management noted radiology revenue has been broadly flat despite centre expansion, citing staggered operationalisation of MRI projects (so full utilisation not yet seen), radiology projects that completed their tenure in the prior quarter, and the larger Rajasthan pathology contribution. The presentation notes the platform “continues to expand despite the Tamil Nadu MRI discontinuation.”

Retail (RPL) still loss-making at EBITDA level. RPL was at negative EBITDA in Q1 due to deployed ground fleet and manpower across five states. Management expects RPL to be EBITDA positive by Q2, and expects the drag to reduce substantially.

Receivables from government projects. Karnataka money has started flowing in after state government approval of certain funds, but is still below expectations. Himachal Pradesh funds have been allocated and money has started flowing. Management expects Karnataka collections by Q2, citing procedural delay including a recent change in ministry. Himachal, Karnataka and a portion of Maharashtra are the three states where collections are not on track; all other projects are on track.

Higher “fees to hospitals” expense. This line rose to ₹406 million (17% of revenue) in Q1 FY27 from ₹154 million (8%) in Q1 FY26 and ₹143 million (7%) in Q4 FY26. Management clarified this reflects revenue-share arrangements with local partners handling manpower and operations for Rajasthan, Manipur and similar projects, not a change in core economics.

Income tax demands. Assessment orders from Income Tax Authorities resulted in demand orders totalling ₹626.90 million for undisclosed income and disallowance of deductions across multiple assessment years; appeals have been filed. Management believes the demands are not tenable and will not have a material impact on the Group’s financial position or performance.

Sector-level risks cited. Intensifying competition from organised diagnostic chains and hospital-based laboratories, regulatory and compliance changes, ability to attract and retain qualified radiologists and pathologists, and high dependence on government healthcare contracts and PPP-based projects.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Overall revenue growth. Revenue from operations grew 22% YoY to ₹2,355 million in Q1 FY27 (Q1 FY26: ₹1,930 million). Like-to-like projects grew approximately 12% YoY, which management highlights as evidence that growth is not dependent only on winning new projects.

Rajasthan transition to operations. By end of Q1 FY27, Rajasthan had 31 mother labs, 62 hub labs and 1,228 collection centres operational. Management describes this as moving decisively from implementation to go-live, providing scale to drive volumes, improve utilisation and unlock operating leverage.

New Himachal Pradesh CT award. Krsnaa was awarded the Himachal Pradesh CT project for 34 CT scanners across the state, expanding from the 12 centres where its PPP journey began almost 12 years ago. The project is predominantly cash-paying, strengthens revenue visibility for 10 years, and gives Krsnaa the opportunity to serve Himachal Pradesh for almost 25 years in total. Management indicated further revenue and investment details will be shared in coming quarters as discussions continue.

Retail as a second engine. Retail revenue grew approximately 64% YoY to ₹193 million (Q1 FY26: ₹118 million) and 22% sequentially, contributing about 9% of group revenue. The retail network expanded to more than 4,000 touchpoints (over 4,250 per the press release) across five to seven states, with contributions from home collection, wellness camps, digital channels and partner networks. Management targets retail contribution of 10–15% this financial year, rising thereafter. The model uses asset-light franchisee arrangements, Krsnaa Business Associates, Krsnaa Referral Centres, strategic alliances and existing PPP infrastructure, which management says keeps customer acquisition costs structurally lower than pure-play retail competitors.

Radiology expansion. Of a pipeline of 17 MRI centres, 8 MRI centres in Maharashtra were inaugurated and made operational during the quarter; the balance are expected to go live by end of Q3. Radiology footprint at Q1 FY27: 149 CT (+4 YoY), 42 MRI (+2 YoY), 1,516 teleradiology (+15 YoY).

New PET-CT segment in Punjab. Krsnaa executed a Concession Agreement with the Department of Health & Family Welfare, Government of Punjab (acting through PHSC), pursuant to a Letter of Award dated August 14, 2026, to develop, operate and maintain PET-CT diagnostic facilities at 4 Government Hospitals in Punjab under a PPP DBFOT format for a 15-year Concession Period. Consideration cannot be determined upfront as revenues depend on actual PET-CT scans and user charges. Management describes this as a meaningful expansion into a new diagnostic service line with long-term cash revenue visibility, strengthening the growing cash portfolio of government PPP partnerships.

Strategic partnerships. Apulki Hospital, Pune commenced operations, with Krsnaa holding exclusive diagnostic rights for radiology and pathology including oncology, cardiology and genetics, providing a platform across Apulki hospitals over the next 30-plus years.

Quality and accreditation. 12 new NABH accreditations were added in Q1, taking total accreditations including NABL, CAP and ACR to 124. NABH radiology accreditations rose to 66 from 31 YoY; NABL pathology accreditations held at 54. Krsnaa is described as the first company in India to receive American College of Radiology accreditation.

Innovation pipeline. Management plans to launch a first-of-a-kind proposition in India combining preventive diagnostics and financial protection, plus new specialty and wellness packages covering oncology, cardiac care, obesity, metabolic health and gastro-related testing. AI and technology are being expanded across consumer engagement, X-ray reporting and business analytics.

Policy tailwinds. Healthcare GDP share projected to rise from 3.3% to 5% by 2030; estimated Indian healthcare industry size of $1,522 Bn in FY29-30; MoHFW Budget of ₹1,017 Bn (Union Budget 2025-26); PM-ABHIM allocation of ₹42 Bn (FY 2025-26). Long-tenure contracts: radiology average tenure ~10+ years, pathology ~5+ years.

Promoter capital infusion. The Board approved the issue of up to 16,21,000 share warrants on a preferential basis to promoters Mr. Rajendra Mutha and Krsna Diagnostics (Mumbai) Private Limited. Management said funds will be used for capital expenditure on projects and certain acquisitions under consideration.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued margin drag if Rajasthan and MRI utilisation ramp slower than expected, given front-ended capex and fixed costs.
  • RPL remaining EBITDA-negative beyond the guided Q2 breakeven.
  • Government receivables concentration, with Himachal Pradesh, Karnataka and part of Maharashtra not on track for collections.
  • Income tax demand orders totalling ₹626.90 million across multiple assessment years, currently under appeal.
  • Dependence on government healthcare contracts and PPP-based projects, with associated tender, tenure-completion and rate-benchmarking exposure (management noted CGHS rate revisions do not immediately benefit existing contractually tendered rates, though forthcoming tenders may be benchmarked to new rates).
  • Competition from organised diagnostic chains and hospital-based laboratories.
  • Ability to attract and retain qualified radiologists and pathologists.
  • Regulatory and compliance changes.
  • Project tenure completions affecting reported segment revenue (radiology projects completing tenure in the prior quarter).
  • Execution and working capital management identified as key focus areas.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Rajasthan FY27 revenue. Earlier guidance was ₹200–250 crores for FY27. On the Q1 FY27 call, management guided conservatively to ₹100–150 crores for the full year, with steady state closer to ₹150–175 crores, while stating the previously stated aspiration still stands. Q1 actual Rajasthan revenue was ₹257 million (around ₹26 crores), with management expecting it to roughly double in the coming quarter. This represents a guidance reset below the earlier stated range, with the earlier figure retained only as an aspiration.

RPL EBITDA breakeven. Management guided RPL to become EBITDA positive by Q2 FY27. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: RPL at negative EBITDA, with the drag described as having come down substantially versus prior quarters and at a nominal level in Q1.

Overall margins. Management stated margins are expected to improve quarter-on-quarter as Rajasthan revenue doubles in Q2 and RPL turns positive, with margins expected to return to double digits at the end of the year as a whole. Observed Q1 FY27: EBITDA margin 25% versus 27% in Q1 FY26, with the moderation attributed to upfront Rajasthan fixed costs.

Rajasthan cost normalisation. Management stated the upfront cost impact has largely been absorbed within Q1 and is expected to normalise further as Rajasthan revenue scales through the rest of the year. Observed Q1 FY27: total expenses at 75% of revenue versus 73% in Q1 FY26, with fees to hospitals and others at 17% of revenue versus 8% a year earlier.

Retail mix. Management targets retail contribution of 10–15% of revenue this financial year. Observed Q1 FY27: retail contributed approximately 9% of overall group revenue, with 64% YoY growth.

Employee cost trajectory. Management indicated some further hiring is expected as labs are still to be operationalised, but that hiring will be in tandem with revenue growth and the manpower impact should not be significant in coming quarters. Observed Q1 FY27: employee benefits expense of ₹357 million (15% of revenue) versus ₹350 million (18%) in Q1 FY26 and ₹327 million (17%) in Q4 FY26.

Karnataka receivables. Management expects money to be collected by Q2. Observed: money has started flowing in following state government approval of certain funds, but remains below expectations.

MRI rollout. Management expects the balance of the 17-MRI pipeline to go live by end of Q3. Observed: 8 of 17 Maharashtra MRI centres inaugurated and operational during Q1.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative went from a defensive Hold (API price declines, zero WPI/DPCO price revisions, export/supply risks) to a sustained Buy despite repeated disappointments, with optimism shifting to US launches, biosimilars, NCE, consumer, and acquisitions. API pricing pressure and weak domestic/new-launch growth remained persistent concerns, while seasonal/gSuprep/regulated-market issues were replaced by INDR compliance and SASV online-pharmacy transition risks.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning on declining API prices materialized, as the last report still flags generic API pricing pressure and the stock delivered -20.9% after the first Hold and negative returns on the 2025 Buy calls.
  • The fear that zero WPI/DPCO price revisions and a lack of new launches would hurt domestic growth materialized; the last report attributes weakening domestic trends to lower inflation and relatively lesser new launch opportunities.
  • The early concern about antibiotic/export/regulated-market pressure was not resolved, as later reports continue to carry API and 7-ACA pricing/capex worries and the subsequent Buy calls ended in DISAPPOINTMENT.

Optimism that failed

  • The optimism around ceiling price revisions and NLEM introductions failed, because domestic growth weakened instead of benefiting from those pricing/launch opportunities.
  • The early expectations from US ramp-ups, gRevlimid share, CRAMS/contrast, and consumer/wellness recovery did not prevent -32.2%, -15.2%, and -16.8% actual returns on the later Buy calls.
  • The last report’s stated drivers of biosimilars, consumer business, NCE, acquisitions, and strong US performance failed to materialize in the near term, as that Buy was followed by -16.8%.
