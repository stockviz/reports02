Lokesh Machines — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

The sanctions-era quarter. The entire June 2026 quarter was operated under the OFAC sanctions regime — described as “the last sanctions-era quarter.” OFAC delisted the Company on 30 June 2026, the final day of the quarter, so no part of Q1 FY27 benefited from reopened channels. The designation dated back to 30 October 2024 under Executive Order 14024; wind-down licences issued in December 2024 expired during June–November 2025, and the delisting petition was pursued through US legal counsel with two OFAC questionnaires and detailed responses.

Machinery division contraction. Machinery revenue fell 13.2% YoY to ₹40.22 crore and 23.8% QoQ, explicitly attributed to blocked USD and EUR channels through the final sanctions quarter. Segment PBIT declined 16.8% YoY to ₹5.15 crore, with PBIT margin at 12.8% versus 13.3% a year ago.

Input-cost pressure. Cost of materials consumed rose to 62.7% of revenue from 47.2% a year ago, driven by commodity cost pressure across the capital goods sector and a revenue mix shifting toward the higher material-intensity Components division. Other expenses rose 26.0% YoY to ₹9.84 crore.

Inventory build. An inventory build of ₹9.72 crore — 17.5% of the quarter’s revenue — arose as production ran ahead of despatches. Converting this stock to despatches is flagged as a focus area for Q2.

Finance cost burden. Finance costs of ₹4.65 crore remain the largest item below EBITDA, up 5.7% YoY, even as the preferential equity raised in the quarter supports a deleveraging agenda.

Margin compression. EBITDA margin eased to 17.79% from 18.39% a year ago (−60 bps), despite pricing discipline and cost control.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

OFAC delisting and reopened markets. The 30 June 2026 removal from the SDN List unblocked all blocked property, reopened USD and EUR banking channels, restored the ability to transact with US persons and access the US financial system, removed secondary-sanctions exposure for counterparties, and made European and US end-markets addressable again for the Machinery division. The Ministry of External Affairs, Government of India, confirmed the delisting basis on 8 July 2026. Benefits accrue from Q2 FY27 onwards.

Defence and Components at scale. The division’s revenue rose 693% YoY to ₹15.55 crore, with PBIT of ₹2.15 crore at a 13.8% margin — now 27.9% of revenue against 4.1% a year ago and 11.7% in Q4 FY26. This followed the resumption of supplies to its principal customer.

Order book visibility. The order book stood at ₹271.00 crore as of 12 August 2026, exceeding FY26 revenue of ₹209.62 crore by ₹61.38 crore, over and above Q1 billings. Composition: General Purpose Machines ₹103.00 crore (38.0%), Defence ₹98.00 crore (36.2%), Special Purpose Machines ₹37.00 crore (13.7%), Forgings ₹30.00 crore (11.1%), Job works ₹3.00 crore (1.1%). GPM, a low lead-time product, converts to revenue within the year; SPM remains steady on critical replacements at OEMs; the Auto Components division is seeing new opportunities on recently commissioned hot forging lines.

Defence order inflows. A ₹58.21 crore order was received on 15 July 2026 from the Integrated Headquarters, Ministry of Defence (Army), for supply of MOD kits for 7.62 mm Medium Machine Guns — inclusive of all duties and taxes, domestic, and not a related-party transaction. Defence order inflows are driven by indigenous small arms orders from the Indian Armed Forces and security establishments.

Balance sheet strengthening. ₹23.62 crore of preferential equity was infused during the quarter; capital employed rose 16.3% QoQ to ₹265.55 crore, with the Components division up 33.4% QoQ to ₹91.67 crore as production ramped. A further ₹6.81 crore was received on 11 July 2026 on conversion of 5.00 lakh warrants, taking paid-up capital to ₹21,79,67,700 across 2,17,96,770 shares; ₹31.04 crore is contracted to follow on full conversion of the 22.78 lakh outstanding warrants, convertible within 18 months of allotment.

Stated way forward. Maximising production capacities across Machine Tools and Auto Components; expanding into new markets including export markets reopened by the delisting; scaling Defence division revenue through expanded capacity, new product lines and emerging opportunities; and accelerating ESG initiatives.

3. Key Risks

  • Sanctions compliance risk. The Company states it continues to work closely with legal counsel to maintain full, ongoing compliance with OFAC regulations and other applicable international trade laws.
  • Export channel dependence. The Machinery division’s performance is tied to the reopened USD and EUR channels; the Q1 contraction demonstrates the sensitivity of this division to access constraints.
  • Commodity and input-cost exposure. Raw material intensity remains elevated at 62.7% of revenue, with the mix shift toward Components adding to material intensity.
  • Working capital and conversion risk. The ₹9.72 crore inventory build must be converted to despatches; failure to do so is identified as a focus area rather than a resolved item.
  • Finance cost drag. Finance costs remain the largest below-EBITDA item and rose YoY.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. The presentation notes that forward-looking statements do not guarantee future performance and are subject to known and unknown risks, including economic performance, industry and competitive conditions, execution ability, capacity expansion, technological change, revenue/income/cash flow movements, market preferences and market risk.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Guidance and forward statements. Management states confidence in sustaining momentum through FY27, citing the OFAC delisting, a sound order book and a healthy order outlook. The delisting is positioned to benefit the business from Q2 FY27 onwards, with reopened export markets supporting Machinery recovery from Q2. Converting the inventory build to despatches is set as a Q2 focus area. The stated way forward targets full capacity utilisation, new market entry including exports, Defence revenue scaling, and accelerated ESG initiatives. The Company also states it assumes no obligation to update forward-looking statements.

Observed performance against prior positioning. Q1 FY27 total income rose 15.4% YoY to ₹55.77 crore (revenue from operations ₹55.46 crore), but declined 6.6% QoQ. EBITDA rose 11.6% YoY to ₹9.92 crore while margin contracted 60 bps to 17.79%. Gross profit grew 17.0% YoY, ahead of the 15.4% revenue growth, and PBT nearly doubled to ₹1.41 crore. PAT rose 121.7% YoY to ₹1.01 crore, with PAT margin at 1.81% versus 0.94%. Basic EPS rose to ₹0.49 from ₹0.23. The effective tax rate eased to 28.5% from 36.6%, with ₹0.35 crore of the ₹0.40 crore charge being deferred tax.

Segment divergence. The two divisions moved in opposite directions: Defence & Components delivered the guided scale-up (revenue +693% YoY, +122% QoQ; PBIT +1,854% YoY, +2,971% QoQ), while Machinery contracted (−13.2% YoY, −23.8% QoQ) — consistent with the explanation that export channels remained blocked through the quarter and that recovery is expected only from Q2.

Order book versus revenue. The ₹271.00 crore order book as of 12 August 2026 already exceeds the previous financial year’s total revenue, excluding Q1 billings, supporting the multi-quarter visibility claim. The ₹58.21 crore Army order of 15 July 2026 is a concrete inflow within the Defence portion of that book.

Corporate calendar. The Board approved the standalone unaudited results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 and the limited review report on 12 August 2026, alongside the FY26 Directors Report, Cost Audit Report and Secretarial Audit Report. The 42nd Annual General Meeting is scheduled for 22 September 2026 at 11:00 A.M. IST through video conferencing/OAVM, with a cut-off date for e-voting of 15 September 2026 and Mr. L.D. Reddy, PCS, appointed as Scrutinizer.
