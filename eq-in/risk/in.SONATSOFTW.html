Sonata Software (SONATSOFTW): Recent Corporate Announcements

Headwinds and Challenges

International services growth stalled sequentially. USD revenue was $82.0 million in Q1 FY27, representing 0.1% quarter-on-quarter constant currency growth and 0.5% quarter-on-quarter degrowth in reported currency. Rupee revenue of ₹777.2 crores was a 0.3% quarter-on-quarter degrowth.

EBITDA compression in international services. EBITDA before other income and forex fell to 15.4% in Q1 FY27 from 20.2% in Q4 FY26, a 4.8% quarter-on-quarter degrowth. Management attributed this to a one-time compensation benefit in the prior quarter (about 2% of the drop), an unexpected delay in a large deal ramp-up, AI talent transformation and advisory investments, cross-currency forex movement (about 50 basis points), and higher CSP bundled deal costs, partly offset by a favourable offshore mix shift to 70% from 68%.

Utilization decline. Utilization fell to 88.5% in Q1 FY27 from 91.8% in Q4 FY26, attributed to the delayed large deal ramp-up and AI capability incubation. Management expects the ramp-up to complete in Q2 FY27.

Forex swings hit profitability. International PAT was ₹62.2 crores, including a forex loss of ₹6.8 crores, versus ₹84.2 crores in Q4 FY26 which included a forex gain of ₹21.7 crores. Consolidated PAT of ₹108.61 crores included a forex loss of ₹7.4 crores, against ₹130.5 crores in Q4 FY26 including a forex gain of ₹28 crores; management stated forex fluctuation alone impacted PAT by about ₹35.4 crores. EPS was ₹3.9 versus ₹4.71 in Q4 FY26.

Domestic business model disruption. The domestic business had faced headwinds from a large OEM partner changing its business model to direct billing with selected large customers. Management stated that through modified plans and engagement, some customer contract renewals were retained and “some of these anticipated risks which were there are now behind us.”

Domestic DSO spike. Domestic DSO rose to 65 days in Q1 FY27 from 47 days in Q4 FY26, described as largely seasonal.

Top-account concentration concerns. Top 10 clients contributed 51% of revenue in Q1 FY27. An analyst noted weakness in top-10 revenue over the prior six quarters; management attributed the percentage shift to growth in the 11–20 account bucket rather than account losses, and stated there were no major issues or discounts in the top 10.

Client-count optics. The number of clients above $1 million annual revenue has been declining for several quarters; management attributed this to customers moving into higher buckets ($3–5 million and $5 million-plus) rather than customer losses.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

AI-led pipeline and order book momentum. AI-led pipeline improved 21% quarter-on-quarter to $340 million. AI order book was $21.4 million in Q1 FY27 versus $16.9 million in Q4 FY26, contributing 18.2% of overall order book. Total Q1 FY27 order book was $97.4 million with a book-to-bill ratio of 1.18x, better than 1.16x in Q4 FY26.

AI-native strategy and leadership. Sonata is positioning itself as “engineering the AI enterprise” across three dimensions (outcome-led business transformation, AI-first technology platforms, AI-native service delivery) and six value pools (customer engagement and operations transformation using AI, agentic AI, legacy modernization, AI-ready data, resilient AI backbone, and AI-native service delivery and ops), targeting nine micro-verticals. The company launched Workbench, an enterprise-grade agentic AI service delivery platform, and appointed Hariprasad Rebala as Chief AI Officer on 17 August 2026, with a mandate to accelerate the AI-native transformation.

Microsoft ecosystem positioning. Sonata was invited by Microsoft to join the Copilot agents and platform engineering Depth Partner Program, described as a select global group of system integrators. The company cites a 30+ year Microsoft partnership, membership in the Microsoft Inner Circle (top 1% of global Microsoft partners), and a Microsoft Frontier Partner Badge, alongside AWS Premier Tier status and partnerships with Salesforce and Snowflake.

Large deal wins. Q1 FY27 included a multi-year deal with a global beverage brand spanning over 800 locations across 11 countries for retail application and cloud infrastructure modernization; a multi-year contract with a global financial technology and payment solutions organization to modernize core digital wallet platforms (ramp-up delays now completed); and a three-year multimillion-dollar deal covering modernization, managed services, and data. Seven new customers were added in the quarter.

Domestic business recovery. Domestic revenue was ₹2,505.6 crores, up 42.4% quarter-on-quarter and 10.2% year-on-year; gross contribution was ₹78.5 crores, up 4.2% quarter-on-quarter and 14.5% year-on-year; EBITDA was ₹59.3 crores, up 34.9% year-on-year. The SMC and corporate segment grew about 82% year-on-year. Management expressed confidence in sustaining double-digit growth momentum.

Talent and skilling. Sonata University has trained 93% of the workforce in AI (the press release cites 90% AI-trained). The forward-deployed engineer pool is being expanded to about 100 people by August. The Sonata Modernization Engineering Program for 2026 was launched with AI at the center.

Cash generation. Closing gross cash was ₹567 crores with net positive cash of ₹67 crores, the third consecutive quarter of positive net cash. International DSO improved to 60 days from 64 days in Q4 FY26.

Capital return. The Board declared an interim dividend of ₹1.25 per equity share (125% on par value of Re. 1) for FY 2026-27, with a record date of 14 August 2026 and payment on or after 24 August 2026. The 31st AGM on 31 July 2026 passed all resolutions with requisite majority, including adoption of FY26 financial statements and confirmation of interim dividends and declaration of final dividend.

Key Risks

  • Concentration risk: Top 10 clients contributed 51% of international revenue; a single large client has been a focus of leadership augmentation and pipeline repositioning.
  • Ramp-up and execution risk: The delayed large deal ramp-up and related paperwork issues affected utilization and margin; management stated these are largely behind, with a small portion remaining.
  • Forex volatility: Cross-currency movements materially affected both PAT and EBITDA in the quarter.
  • Margin trajectory uncertainty: Management declined to provide a steady-state EBITDA margin range, calling it guidance-like, while stating confidence in positive EBITDA movement in coming quarters.
  • Domestic OEM model risk: The OEM direct-billing shift had previously disrupted the domestic business; while management states anticipated risks are behind, the business remains dependent on OEM and hyperscaler partnerships.
  • AI investment intensity: Continued investment in AI advisory, forward-deployed engineers, and talent transformation is expected to continue for a couple more quarters before recovery.
  • Working capital seasonality: Domestic DSO rose to 65 days from 47 days, described as seasonal, with vendor payout timing affecting gross cash balances.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Margin recovery guidance. Management stated it expects EBITDA to have positive traction every quarter this year, with one-timers normalizing and the large deal ramp-up completing in Q2 FY27. Observed: Q1 FY27 EBITDA fell to 15.4% from 20.2% quarter-on-quarter, with management attributing the decline to identified one-time and investment factors.

Domestic growth confidence. Management expressed confidence in sustaining double-digit year-on-year growth in domestic gross contribution. Observed: domestic gross contribution grew 14.5% year-on-year and 4.2% quarter-on-quarter in Q1 FY27, with revenue up 10.2% year-on-year.

Top-account stabilization. Management stated it sees stability in the top five accounts with no major threats or issues, and expects the top five to grow better in coming quarters, with growth returning in a couple more quarters. Observed: top 10 revenue share was 51%, with the 11–20 bucket growing; management did not disclose absolute top-10 figures.

AI order book trajectory. Management cited AI-led pipeline up 21% and AI-led order wins up 27% quarter-on-quarter as reinforcing conviction in the strategy. Observed: AI order book rose to $21.4 million from $16.9 million, and AI-led pipeline reached $340 million.

Medium-term outlook. Management stated it remains optimistic and expects gradual improvement in revenue and EBITDA over the medium-to-long term, citing the AI pipeline, AI order bookings, partner ecosystem, and focused vertical strategy, while noting the market environment remains mixed.

Broker Narrative

The narrative began with a Buy and bullish expectations of a H2FY24 spending rebound in BFSI, manufacturing and hi-tech, driven by a strong pipeline, cloud adoption and generative AI. By the last report, the stance had been cut to Hold, with AI repositioned from a broad growth driver to a specific US$340 mn pipeline/order-book story while core IT services revenue stayed muted and margins fell. Persistent themes were delayed client decisions and ramp-ups, but the focus shifted from cyclical recovery to margin repair, utilisation recovery and stabilisation of top-5 accounts.

Fears that came true

  • Discretionary spending softness persisted, as IITS revenue in US$ terms declined 0.5% QoQ and grew only 0.2% YoY.
  • Client delays and project ramp-downs materialised through delayed large-deal ramp-up and lower utilisation, which dropped to 88.5% from 91.8% QoQ.
  • Unfavourable macroeconomic conditions kept overall growth near flat and repeatedly turned Buy/Add calls into DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Extended decision-making cycles hurt conversion, reflected in muted revenue and a ~481 bps QoQ EBITDA margin decline in IITS.

Optimism that failed

  • The strong project pipeline did not convert into growth, with IITS revenue declining sequentially and growing only 0.2% YoY.
  • The expected H2FY24 rebound in BFSI, manufacturing and hi-tech spending failed to materialise, as large-deal ramp-up remained delayed and consolidated EBITDA margin fell ~300 bps QoQ to 5.2%.
  • Generative AI/cloud optimism did not revive financials; despite AI-led order book rising to US$21.73 mn, overall revenue stayed muted and margins deteriorated.
