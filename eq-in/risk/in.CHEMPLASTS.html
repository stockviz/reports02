Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost shock and profitability collapse. Q1 FY27 was shaped by a volatile global environment in which geopolitical events influenced raw material availability, feedstock costs and demand. Consolidated revenue was INR 1,125 crores, but a sharp increase in input costs produced an EBITDA loss of INR 115 crores and a net loss of INR 176 crores. The Managing Director described the quarter as marked by elevated input costs following the Middle East conflict and subdued demand in certain segments.

Suspension PVC. The segment faced declining prices, particularly in April 2026, reduced sales volumes and high VCM costs stemming from Middle East tensions. The company was carrying high-priced VCM inventory booked at close to USD 1,000-plus per ton, while realisations were around USD 700–750 per ton, producing a negative PVC-VCM spread in Q1. This high-cost inventory was expected to be consumed by July and part of August. Customs duty on PVC resin was exempted and duty on VCM reduced from 2% to nil from 2 April to 30 June; the PVC duty removal was extended to 15 July before being restored. Demand was expected to remain soft during the monsoon season.

Paste PVC. Demand was volatile through April and early May due to lower downstream operating rates caused by PNG/LNG supply issues and elevated input costs. Supplies from Europe and Asia were limited for much of the quarter before turning aggressive again from mid-June as market conditions normalised. On the regulatory front, the Ministry of Finance allowed the anti-dumping recommendation against imports from the EU and Japan to lapse, prolonging import pressure; the company filed a writ of mandamus before the Madras High Court and obtained an order directing provisional assessment of imports and execution of bonds by importers to facilitate retrospective recovery of ADD if ultimately imposed. Basic customs duty on Paste PVC was waived from 1 April 2026 to 30 June, extended to 15 July, and reinstated with effect from 16 July 2026.

Value-added chemicals. Caustic soda and chloromethane operated in a challenging market with pricing under pressure from weak regional demand and excess supply. Chloromethane volumes were impacted by softer demand in select end-use segments. Hydrogen peroxide volumes improved sequentially as raw material availability normalised, though prices moderated from elevated levels seen at the beginning of the quarter. Caustic production was lower due to a planned membrane changeover requiring plant shutdown, with productivity improving post-changeover.

Karaikal fire and regulatory orders. On 17 July 2026 a fire occurred at the EDC manufacturing plant of the Karaikal facility following a nitrogen supply disruption that led to accumulation of flammable vapours, EDC release and fire. Production was shut down safely, there were no injuries to employees or contractors and no spillage, and the fire was fully extinguished within 15 minutes. The Inspector of Factories, Puducherry issued a prohibition order dated 19 July 2026 prohibiting further usage/operation of the EDC plant pending a revoke order. The Puducherry Pollution Control Committee then issued directions dated 22 July 2026 that the EDC plant operation shall be closed down forthwith and shall not resume until further orders. The company stated it was undertaking corrective actions and submitting a compliance report, and was in the process of ascertaining the damage caused. An insurance claim was filed and surveyors were assessing the incident; downtime was being used for planned maintenance. No impact on Paste PVC operations was reported, with EDC being sourced through imports for the Mettur plant.

Agrochemical market backdrop. The overall agchem market continued to be subdued and was reviving, with significant competition from generics from China. New molecule launches were getting delayed and not taking off as customers originally expected because of price pressures on existing molecules and availability of cheap generics, which motivates farmers to continue using existing chemistries.

Leverage and cash flow. Interest cost was around INR 235 crores annually and free cash flows remained negative because of growth capex. Net debt rose from INR 741 crores in FY24 to INR 1,117 crores in FY25 and INR 1,419 crores in FY26. The company reported four consecutive quarters of losses.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Paste PVC recovery and debottlenecking. The market turned around in June as the Middle East situation eased, providing better visibility on raw material availability and helping downstream operating rates recover to 70%–80%. Management expressed confidence that demand recovery would continue in coming quarters. Productivity improvement initiatives at the Cuddalore Paste PVC facility were progressing well, and the 7,000-ton debottlenecking project remained on track for commissioning in October 2026. Paste PVC realisations were better than Suspension PVC, with a spread of roughly USD 200 between Paste and Suspension PVC. With reinstatement of customs duty and the ongoing legal proceedings on ADD, the company expected moderation in low-price dumping from Europe and Japan.

Custom Manufactured Chemicals Division (CMCD). The segment delivered a much improved performance, reflecting encouraging signs of recovery from the slowdown of past quarters, with Specialty Chemicals segment revenue of INR 427 crores and volumes registering 21% year-on-year growth. MPB 3 Phase 3 and pilot Phase 3 were successfully commissioned and on stream. A business development head for Europe and a representative in Japan were appointed and were on the ground, strengthening customer engagement and new product opportunities. The molecule pipeline expanded to close to 50 molecules, with 14 commercialised. Management stated it was on track for the INR 1,000 crores CMCD target, which would continue to be predominantly agchem in the near term. Utilisation on already-commissioned assets was close to 60%–70%. Pharma innovator projects were ongoing, mostly at development stage, with some expected to become commercial later in the year or early next financial year.

Refrigerant gases (R32). Commercial production of R32 from the swing plant commenced in May 2026, with positive customer feedback on product specifications. Commissioning of the new plant was underway and progressing as planned. All capacities were expected online by end of the fiscal, with full ramp-up in the last quarter of the year and first quarter of next year. The go-to-market strategy encompassed both domestic and international sales, with active partner discussions. Management expected this to be one of the profitable segments.

Suspension PVC support measures. The reinstatement of customs duty and introduction of a Minimum Import Price of USD 766/MT were expected to provide marginal respite for CCVL. Management noted the MIP was lower than prevailing market prices but served as a floor to calibrate against future market falls. Globally the market was recovering marginally.

Capacity expansion. The CMCD capacity was expanded from roughly 1,100 tons to 5,400 tons across phases, with civil and infrastructure work for the next MPB targeted for completion by Q2 FY27. Additional capex of approximately INR 160 crores was noted to further enhance capacity of the new multi-purpose production block. The addressable market size for CMCD was estimated at USD 2 billion.

Key Risks

  • Continued low-price dumping from Europe and Japan in Paste PVC and Suspension PVC, with the ADD recommendation allowed to lapse and the Suspension PVC ADD being re-examined as an industry proposal.
  • Exposure to high-cost VCM inventory and volatility in PVC-VCM spreads, which turned negative in Q1 FY27.
  • Geopolitical disruption to feedstock availability and costs, as seen with the Middle East conflict.
  • Regulatory and operational risk from the Karaikal EDC plant closure orders, with the financial impact still being ascertained and resumption subject to further orders.
  • Weak regional demand and excess supply pressuring caustic soda and chloromethane pricing.
  • Subdued agchem market and competition from Chinese generics, with delayed new molecule launches by customers.
  • Negative free cash flow alongside growth capex and annual interest cost of around INR 235 crores, with net debt rising to INR 1,419 crores in FY26.
  • Monsoon-season softness in Suspension PVC demand.
  • Onerous contract exposure: an exceptional charge of INR 149.92 crores was reported in Q4 FY26 for onerous contracts and inventory write-down, and a standalone exceptional impairment of INR 898 crores was recorded on investment in CCVL. A net provision of around INR 90 crores for CCVL and INR 30 crores for Chemplast was expected to be reversed during the current quarter.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • CMCD INR 1,000 crores target: Management stated it was on track as communicated in the last call, with the trend expected to continue in coming quarters. Observed: the segment delivered a much improved quarter with healthy revenue run-rate and 21% year-on-year volume growth in Specialty Chemicals.
  • R32 capacity timeline: Management stated all capacities would be online by end of the fiscal, unchanged from plan, with full ramp-up in the last quarter of this year and first quarter of next year. Observed: commercial production from the swing plant commenced in May 2026 and commissioning of new plants was underway as per plan.
  • Paste PVC debottlenecking: Guided for commissioning in October 2026. Observed: project remained on track.
  • Suspension PVC spread: Management guided to a spread of around USD 160 between PVC and VCM at replacement level, with high-cost VCM washed out by August and normal levels from September. EBITDA neutrality was indicated at roughly USD 120–130 per ton, and PBT positivity at another USD 20–30 of spread. Observed: Q1 FY27 spread was negative due to high-cost VCM, with the benefit expected from Q3.
  • Profitability turnaround: Management stated the worst was over and expected reasonable performance from Q3, with four positive triggers cited (Suspension PVC duty/MIP, Paste PVC duty and court order, CMCD order book, and refrigerant gas). Observed: Q1 FY27 recorded an EBITDA loss of INR 115 crores and PAT loss of INR 176 crores, with some challenges still expected in the current quarter.
  • Onerous contract provision reversal: Management indicated the net provision of around INR 90 crores for CCVL and INR 30 crores for Chemplast would be reversed during the current quarter.
  • Debt servicing: Management stated conserved cash over the years, along with current accruals, would take care of all debt servicing obligations, with enough liquidity in the system.
  • CMCD returns: Management indicated the business was reaching a stage of optimising cost levels and getting to industry levels of returns, expected to get there this year.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from an optimistic demand-driven growth story to a defensive one centered on import protection and recovery from a destocking cycle. Early themes of strong PVC demand, capacity expansion, and custom manufacturing growth persisted only in name, as repeated disappointments forced the broker to acknowledge lower volumes, negative spreads, and execution lags. By the final report, the broker still recommended Buy, leaning on restored duties, MIP, and a purported strong CMCD order book, while the stock continued to underperform.

Fears that came true

  • The surge in Chinese PVC imports persisted, leading to negative suspension PVC spreads and requiring the restoration of customs duty and implementation of MIP.
  • Specialty segment volume declines continued, reflected in lower volumes across all segments in the last report.
  • Long lead times in custom manufacturing materialized as lower-than-expected execution in that business.

Optimism that failed

  • Strong PVC demand failed to benefit the company as negative spreads and weak volumes eroded profitability.
  • Custom manufacturing traction did not hold up, as execution lagged and volume expectations were missed.
  • Capacity expansion projects (Paste PVC, CSM phases) did not translate into expected growth, with volumes remaining lower across segments.
  • The expectation of easing input costs proved wrong, as high-cost VCM inventory persisted.
