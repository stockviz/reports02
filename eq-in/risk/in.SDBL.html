SDBL Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Madhya Pradesh licence disruption. The dominant headwind in Q1 FY27 was the operational and regulatory disruption at the Bhopal (Madhya Pradesh) facility. Management described Q1 as “an extremely difficult quarter,” with the MP plant suspended and the matter sub judice. The company estimates it lost approximately ₹250 crore of revenue in the quarter due to the closure. The disruption has persisted for around six months, longer than the roughly 15-day timeline management had indicated on the prior call. Fixed costs of approximately ₹6–7 crore per quarter (including interest, salaries and electricity) continue to be incurred, with about ₹5 crore of employee cost attributable to Bhopal. Around ₹25 crore of finished goods remain at the plant, with roughly one month of shelf life remaining and the stock undergoing verification.

Volume and financial impact. Consolidated volumes fell to 45.79 lakh cases (down 48% YoY), total income was ₹268.8 crore (down 49% YoY), EBITDA was ₹15.2 crore (down 79% YoY) and PAT was ₹1.6 crore (down 96% YoY). Lower volumes reduced operating leverage, amplifying the impact on EBITDA and PAT.

Input cost pressure. Key raw material and packaging costs rose in Q1 FY27 versus Q1 FY26 — cans, malt and bottles increased by an average of about 7.5% to 8%. Management noted dependence on a single supplier (Ball Corporation) for cans across the industry, and acknowledged vulnerability to raw material price volatility.

Market share loss risk in MP. Management acknowledged that consumers who move to another brand during a prolonged non-availability period are difficult to win back, and that recovery in markets closed for a long time will take time.

Andhra Pradesh entry delayed. The planned AP launch, originally expected in Q1, was delayed by state excise and corporation permissions, which were received only by end-July. Export permissions from Karnataka were still being processed, with brands expected to be visible in AP by the first week of September.

Excise duty mechanics. Higher excise duty paid in Q1 relative to reduced sales reflects the fact that Karnataka and Odisha require excise duty to be paid by the manufacturer before dispatch, unlike Madhya Pradesh.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Karnataka and Odisha recovery. Karnataka saw a 30% increase in cases sold in Q1, and Odisha close to 40%. Management cited improving demand and market share, describing these as evidence of brand and distribution resilience. The Hassan facility operated at approximately 60% capacity utilization and the Odisha facility at around 70%.

Uttar Pradesh plant commissioning. Commercial production at the UP brewery began around June 9, 2026, adding approximately 10 million cases of annual beer capacity. Roughly ₹300 crore has been invested in Phase 1, with no pending Phase 1 capex and no external debt taken for this investment. Phase 2 (distillery) permissions have been applied for, with a firmer execution view expected in three to four months. Management expects it may take three to four years to reach peak capacity utilization at UP, and noted UP is a large, growing market with low per-capita consumption. Delhi, previously served from MP, is now being served from the UP plant.

Portfolio and premiumization. Beer remained the core earnings engine at 45 lakh cases, representing about 98.9% of total volume and 93% of revenue. IMFL realization improved approximately 3% YoY to ₹1,047 per case. The beer portfolio includes Hunter, Black Fort, Power Cool, Sunny Beaches, Legend and Woodpecker; Sunny Beaches reportedly performed well in Karnataka. Mahavat (IMFL) has been launched in Bhopal, Delhi and UP, and management indicated a possible entry into Indian single malt before the end of the financial year.

Balance sheet strength and cash generation. Despite commissioning the UP facility in a difficult environment, gross debt rose only about ₹10 crore, with gross debt/equity moving marginally from 0.30x in March 2026 to 0.31x in June 2026. Cash generated from operations was close to ₹28 crore in the quarter.

Proposed promoter funding. The Board approved a preferential issue of up to 32,12,955 convertible warrants to promoter Mr. Deepak Arora at ₹77.81 per warrant (face value ₹2, premium ₹75.81), aggregating up to ₹25,00,00,028.55, primarily for working capital and general corporate purposes. 25% is payable on allotment, with the balance 75% payable on conversion within 18 months, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals.

Contract manufacturing and footprint strategy. Management said it evaluates local contract brewing versus importing from its own plants on a margin and quality basis, and has mapped capacity so that MP served northern states, Odisha serves eastern states including West Bengal, Jharkhand and the Northeast, and Karnataka serves southern states.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory and legal risk: The MP licence matter is in the courts and with authorities, and is sub judice; timing of resolution is not within the company’s control.
  • Consumer attrition risk: Prolonged non-availability in MP may cause lasting market share loss.
  • Input cost and supply risk: Raw material and packaging inflation (7.5–8% YoY in Q1) and single-supplier dependence for cans.
  • Execution risk at UP: Ramp-up, capacity utilization and market penetration at a newly commissioned large facility, with peak utilization potentially three to four years away.
  • Import economics: Importing into MP from Odisha or UP was evaluated but found exorbitant due to high MP import fees, which would erode margins or cause losses.
  • Receivables: Management stated most markets have government wholesalers, so receivable risk is limited, and governments are currently prompt on payouts.
  • Competitive pressure: Acknowledged in the context of UP, where other players are present and UBL is reportedly planning a large brewery.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

MP restart timing. On the prior call, management had indicated Bhopal operations would restart within about 15 days; three months later the licence had not been restored. On this call, management said it is “very hopeful” resolution occurs within the month, while noting the matter is sub judice and has taken longer than anticipated.

Revenue guidance. Management revised its FY27 revenue guidance to ₹1,000–1,100 crore, down from earlier guidance of ₹1,400–1,500 crore, attributing the change to the MP situation. Management declined to provide a Plan B scenario or FY27 margin guidance, indicating a better view by the next call.

UP capacity utilization. Management declined to provide current UP utilization as not meaningful so soon after the June commissioning, and guided to three to four years for peak utilization. This contrasts with an investor’s expectation of 60–70% utilization, which management attributed to the plant only having been commissioned in June and the ongoing lean season.

AP entry. Previously guided to Q1 FY27; permissions were received end-July, with brands expected in AP by the first week of September.

Balance sheet discipline. Management’s stated approach of adding capacity without material leverage is supported by the observed ₹10 crore increase in gross debt and stable 0.31x gross debt/equity, alongside approximately ₹28 crore of operating cash generation.

Recovery trajectory. Management expressed confidence in a “meaningful revival” through FY27, contingent on MP normalization, sustained Karnataka and Odisha recovery, and UP ramp-up.

Broker Narrative

The first report was a bullish Buy, framing SDBL as a high-growth beer story driven by strong Q1FY24 results, capacity expansion, and market-share gains. The last report moved to Not Rated and shifted focus to margin pressure from higher bottle costs, higher working capital in newer duty-paid states, and sustained debt. Persistent themes were dominant market share in MP/Karnataka/Odisha and above-market growth; the change was from political/seasonality/competition risks to operational and balance-sheet constraints.

Fears that came true

  • Raw-material/bottle cost pressure flagged in the first report materialized, as the last report noted new bottle costs are ~50% higher and cap EBITDA margin at 12-13%, contributing to disappointing returns.
  • The risk of margin dilution from entering new states without local manufacturing capacity materialized, with the last report citing spends required to scale newer states and duty-paid states raising working capital.
  • The concern about aggressive expansion requiring extra funding appeared as elevated leverage, with the last report saying debt is likely to stay at current levels due to capex and working capital needs.

Optimism that failed

  • The first report’s expectation that strong Q1FY24 growth and demand catalysts such as warm weather and the Cricket World Cup would reward investors failed, as the Buy call’s actual return was -45.2%.
  • The optimism around capacity expansion and new-state entry translating into the predicted +10.5% upside did not materialize; the stock delivered -45.2%.
  • The continued growth and market-share narrative in the last report also did not prevent negative returns, with the stock falling -39.6% after the Not Rated report.
