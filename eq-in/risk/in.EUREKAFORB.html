Eureka Forbes — Recent Corporate Announcements: Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Inflationary and currency pressure. Management described the Q1 FY27 operating landscape as continuing to be impacted by inflationary pressures, currency volatility, and associated uncertainty. Gross margin for the quarter stood at 58.4%, lower by 131 basis points year-on-year, reflecting higher commodity costs and adverse currency movements. Management noted that the cost environment remains challenging and that no meaningful reduction in input costs had yet been seen.

Margin compression. Adjusted EBITDA margin came in at 10.5%, a decline of 46 basis points year-on-year, attributed to a moderation in gross margins and planned, deliberately higher growth investments.

Service bookings softness. Price increases of roughly 3% to 12% on single-year and multi-year AMCs led to deferral and postponement of AMC renewals, moderating bookings growth. Management characterised this as deferral rather than lost revenue, expected to mitigate and normalise over time, but acknowledged the impact would play out toward the later part of the year.

Rising cost lines. Employee costs rose 10.7% year-on-year to INR90 crores (normal annual increments); ESOP charges rose 16.2% to INR6.6 crores; service charges rose 2.7% to INR83 crores; other expenses rose 21.4% to INR162 crores, largely led by advertisement and sales promotion (A&SP) spending focused on in-store presence in modern retail.

Competitive intensity. Management acknowledged new entrants in the water purifier category and stated that the company must remain agile and aggressive rather than behave like an incumbent. A shareholder question noted FY23–FY26 revenue growth of 11.3% per year and asked whether market share had increased.

External risk factors. The disclosed risk set includes commodity price fluctuations, currency rate fluctuations, inflationary headwinds, uncertainty in the external environment, employee costs, changes in input costs, supply chain sources, government regulations, taxation, political stability, litigation, and competitive intensity.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Q1 FY27 growth. Revenue for the quarter grew 15.3% year-on-year to INR701 crores, described as a solid start to FY27 with accelerated and broad-based growth across the product business, especially water.

Water purifiers. The category grew by high teens on double-digit volume growth, driven by momentum in the economy range, an expanded range of stainless-steel products, and premium offerings in Hot, UTC, and IoT. Growth was ahead of the category, leading to market share gains.

Emerging categories. Robotics and softeners delivered strong growth. Robotic vacuum cleaners benefited from premiumisation and a shift toward fully automatic cleaning products; a new campaign featuring Shraddha Kapoor was launched. Water softeners reported strong double-digit growth. Management stated that by FY30 robotics would be an INR1,000 crores business.

Channel breadth. Growth was broad-based across retail, direct, and e-commerce, all delivering strong double-digit growth.

Filters and D2C. The filter portfolio grew well in Q1, supported by a simplified assortment (reduced from 65-odd filters to five universal filters), a new distribution system for the open ecosystem of filter and spares distributors, and stepped-up awareness campaigns including a “Stop, Check and Relax” digital campaign. The D2C engine saw strong growth, supported by first-party data on 15 million customers and nearly 2.5 million monthly active users; multiple-category ownership is currently less than 1%.

Structural demand drivers. Growing middle-class incomes, urbanisation, rising female labour force participation, higher piped water coverage, high electrification access, increasing importance of health and hygiene, unpredictable domestic help creating demand for cleaning backups, and low category penetration in water purifiers (about 7%), water softeners, vacuum cleaners, and air purifiers.

Cost-of-ownership innovation. The company launched two e-commerce products with four-year filter life (Glow 4X and Ritz Pro 4X, stainless steel, priced at roughly INR22,000 and INR25,000), described as a first in the country, about 45 days before the call. Earlier initiatives included tiered AMCs starting as low as INR699 and a range of water purifiers with two-year filter life, about 70% of whose buyers were first-time category entrants.

Balance sheet. The quarter ended with a net cash surplus of INR425 crores; net cash position of INR254 crores and ROCE above 350% were also reported.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity price and currency movements continuing to pressure gross margins, with no meaningful input cost reduction yet visible.
  • Consumer price increases in AMCs causing deferral of renewals, with the impact expected to play out later in the year.
  • Dependence of service revenue growth on in-year bookings, which management said would need to come in for revenue growth to translate.
  • Competitive intensity from new entrants, particularly in water purifiers and robotics, including the possibility of online-first entrants.
  • Execution risk on the FY30 ambition of 2x revenue from FY25 to FY30 and 3x EBITDA over the same period.
  • The external environment described as uncertain, with an ongoing geopolitical crisis showing no signs of closure.
  • A shareholder observation that FY23–FY26 revenue growth averaged 11.3% per year, below the 17%–18% CAGR implied by the FY30 ambition.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Full-year margin guidance. Management expects FY27 EBITDA margins to be broadly in line with last year. In Q1 FY27, adjusted EBITDA margin was 10.5%, down 46 basis points year-on-year. Management noted that Q1 typically carries an upfront step-up in A&SP spend, that the trajectory would not be similar in every quarter, and cited the prior year as a reference point: Q1 margins down 46 basis points year-on-year, with the year ending at a margin expansion of nearly 55 basis points.

Growth guidance. Management expressed confidence in delivering a clear step-up in FY27 full-year growth, given the strong Q1 start. Observed Q1 revenue growth was 15.3% year-on-year.

Service revenue guidance. Management stated that Q2 service revenue growth is expected to be similar to Q1 and not very different, with visibility beyond that dependent on bookings and filter business scaling. Observed Q1 service revenue growth tracked at levels similar to Q4 FY26, with a deferred liability balance increase providing an annuity stream into FY27.

A&SP guidance. Management expects FY27 A&SP spends to increase ahead of revenue growth, focused on in-store presence and consumer finance adoption. Observed Q1 other expenses grew 21.4% year-on-year, with almost the entire increase attributed to A&SP, largely in-store investment.

ESOP guidance. ESOP expense is expected in the range of INR25 crores to INR26 crores for the year. Observed Q1 ESOP charges were INR6.6 crores, up 16.2% year-on-year.

Pricing stance. Management stated it would be calibrated and measured about further price hikes given category momentum, noting that in absolute terms cost increases had been covered though not fully in percentage terms. Peers followed the company’s price increases with a lag of roughly 30 days, after which price equilibrium versus competitors was restored.

FY30 ambition. The 2x revenue and 3x EBITDA ambition from FY25 to FY30 remains the stated North Star, with management expressing confidence in delivering it. Product categories are expected to grow ahead of the average, and filter growth is expected to clock in ahead of AMC growth.

One-off item. Q1 FY27 reported PAT grew 44% to INR55 crores, including a one-time gain of INR19.5 crores from reversal of gratuity expense. Adjusting for this, pre-exceptional PAT grew 6.1% year-on-year to INR41 crores. Adjusted PBT for Q1 was INR61 crores.

Governance. At the 17th AGM held on August 19, 2026, all three resolutions — adoption of audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY2025-26, re-appointment of Mr. Sahil Dalal as a director retiring by rotation, and ratification of Cost Auditors’ remuneration — were passed with the requisite majority. The ESG rating assigned to Eureka Forbes for FY2025-26 was 63.4.

Broker Narrative

All 11 calls were Buy. The first report was built on brand moat (Eureka Forbes/Aquaguard, ~45% share), multi-channel distribution, entry-level pricing, margin expansion to 10%+, strong FCF, and limited MNC competition. The last report kept the underpenetration/margin/services themes but shifted emphasis to management FY30 targets, robotics scaling, digital/D2C, and service-platform/AMC recurring revenue. Persistent risks were commodity/input costs and competitive pressures; the changed story was one of new growth vectors and management targets replacing the earlier incumbent-brand moat argument.

Fears that came true

  • Steep commodity/input price increases, flagged in the first report and still in the last, were followed by negative actual returns (e.g., -17.4% on 2025-08-12 and -33.7% on 2025-11-28).
  • Higher competitive pressures, flagged from the first report and repeated in the last, correlated with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes (-2.5% on 2024-10-09 and -17.4% on 2025-08-12).
  • The risk of new product/plant launch failure or delay persisted into the last report, and the stock’s actual return after that report was -33.7% versus the +15.3% predicted.

Optimism that failed

  • Limited MNC competition / failure of new entrants to gain meaningful share did not materialise as a durable moat: competitive pressure remained a stated headwind and Buy calls subsequently delivered large negative returns.
  • EBITDA margin expansion to 10%+ / mid-high teens failed to show up as an achieved result; the last report still framed margin improvement as a future target via better mix, utilisation, and filter-kit simplification.
  • The early confidence that established brands/market share and multi-channel distribution would drive upside did not prevent negative actual returns, including -17.4% and -33.7% on later calls.
