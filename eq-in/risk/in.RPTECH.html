RPTECH: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Component shortage and pricing pressure. Management describes a fundamental shift in component economics, with AI data center demand relocating global memory capacity toward high-bandwidth memory and creating a sustained shortage of conventional DRAM and NAND flash, alongside firm and rising prices. Independent forecasts expect this to persist through 2028. Third-party reports indicate the total market may shrink by about 10% unit-wise, though rising prices are offsetting the quantity drop.

Consumer affordability pressure. Laptop prices have roughly doubled compared to the prior year. Management notes consumer demand is slowing because of affordability, and flags possible pockets of consumer affordability pressure in the second half. Entry-level laptops face almost 50% shortage, while medium and premium notebooks remain available if the price is accepted.

Supply allocation risk. Management characterizes supply constraints as a potential risk and an “orange alert” tracked weekly, though it notes Rashi’s 30-plus-year relationships and business agreements with global companies including NVIDIA, Intel and AMD have so far secured good allocation.

Working capital trade-off on large deals. Management states that pursuing large AI data center projects would roughly double the debt ratio from around 0.5, and that run-rate business offers higher margins and ROCE. This led the company to deprioritize large deals in the April–June quarter.

Channel inventory build-up. Inventory has built up across channel partners, who are holding stock to earn double-digit margins and selling slowly. Management states this is not a major concern given current and near-term demand, and that days of inventory at the company’s own level are marginally declining.

Contingent liabilities. GST-related contingent liabilities are in the nature of reconciliatory show-cause notices. Management states that historically such notices have closed at less than 1% of the total liability demanded, and that no significant liability has been incurred.

Gross margin mix. Gross margin was slightly lower than prior quarters, attributed to a higher share of the lower-margin PES segment in the product mix and an accounting impact from a subsidiary hived off in the prior-year first quarter.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Multi-year PC refresh cycle. The move away from Windows 10 has triggered a sustained hardware replacement wave across enterprise and commercial users, described as a multi-year cycle with room still to run.

AI PC inflection. AI-capable devices are rising as a share of every shipment and carry higher average selling prices, accelerating the incentive to refresh.

Value over volume. Unit shipments may face pressure, but market value is rising because pricing is firm and moving up. Management states the advantage flows to players with scale, deep OEM relationships, disciplined inventory and balance sheet strength to secure supply.

Structural India opportunity. The Indian ICT distribution opportunity is described as over ₹1.5 lakh crore with PC penetration still only in the mid-teens, with digitization deepening into tier 2, 3 and 4 India. Two new branches were opened in Udaipur and Dhule.

Q1 FY27 performance. Consolidated revenue grew 61.9% year-on-year to ₹5,100 crores (₹5,102 crores per the CFO), with EBITDA growing 50% to ₹155 crores (₹173 crores per the CFO) and PAT growing 69.5% to ₹105 crores, with diluted EPS of ₹15.25. Standalone revenue was ₹4,832 crores, up 58%, with EBITDA of ₹164 crores and PAT of ₹97 crores. Management describes this as the strongest quarter in the company’s history, with all-time highs on topline, EBITDA, PAT and annualized ROCE simultaneously.

Returns and working capital. Annualized ROCE and ROE were approximately 19.5% and 19.8%, the highest since listing. Working capital days improved year-on-year to 56 days, with inventory days at 55, debtor days at 41 and creditor days at 40. Net debt at end-June was ₹1,285 crores.

Growth composition. Of the roughly 60% growth, 30–35% came from price increases, 5–10% from new products and brands, 20–25% from higher quantity, and about 10% from improved market share. Unit growth was about 20% excluding a major unit dip in the SanDisk memory brand.

VDA Infosolutions acquisition. Announced June 23, 2026, RPTECH entered a binding agreement to acquire an upfront 67% stake in VDA Infosolutions for cash consideration of ₹3.68 billion, at an equity valuation of ₹5.5 billion. VDA is a Mumbai-based enterprise technology and digital infrastructure solutions company with FY26 revenue of approximately ₹8.5 billion, 1,000+ professionals across 140+ locations, and partnerships with IBM, Dell, Broadcom, Cisco, Palo Alto Networks, Red Hat, Nutanix and Checkpoint, among others. Management expects approximately 5% of revenue to come from integrated services and value-added solutions, and states VDA will bring value creation over 2–3 years.

Restar Corporation joint venture. Announced August 4, 2026, RPTECH will transfer its semiconductor business division into a wholly owned subsidiary headquartered in Bengaluru and a step-down subsidiary headquartered in Singapore, with Restar Corporation acquiring a 26% stake. The JV, named Rashi Restar Semiconductor Solutions Private Limited, is structured 74% RPTECH and 26% Restar, with a four-member board (three from RPTECH, one from Restar), headquartered in Bengaluru with a network across five major-city locations, and scheduled to commence operations in October 2026. Focus areas are advanced image sensing solutions for industrial and automotive applications, targeting automotive Tier-1s, industrial robotics, EMS/ECMS, enterprise surveillance, machine vision cameras, AV and home appliances. Restar is a Tokyo Exchange-listed semiconductor distribution company with approximately US$4 billion in revenue. Benefits cited include access to Restar’s distributed products and solutions, access to Japanese manufacturers in India, and design services support, including direct training of Indian engineers by Japanese design experts and 50+ local engineering hires over two years. Management targets more than US$100 million in revenue under the JV within three years, with a higher margin profile than the core distribution business.

Semiconductor momentum. RPTECH became the first ICT distributor in India to enter the semiconductor business in 2021 and operates a dedicated Embedded Lab in Bengaluru. Semiconductor portfolio revenue in Q1 grew 70% compared to FY26, and management states semiconductor revenue has already crossed 70% of the entire prior year’s revenue.

Data center pipeline. Management states there is a good pipeline of AI data center projects for at least the next three quarters, and that RPTECH participates in smaller component supplies of ₹20–70 crores across almost all such deals. The company is prioritizing neo-clouds, smaller cloud service providers and niche AI operators over large projects.

Dell commercial business. Approximately 5% of business came from the new Dell commercial business in the quarter, and management states it is above target.

Business reorganization. The Board approved transfer of the Embedded Business from Rashi Peripherals Limited and Rashi Peripherals Pte Limited to Rashi Semiconductor Solutions Private Limited and Rashi Semiconductor Solutions Pte Limited on a slump sale basis. The investment limit in Rashi Semiconductor Solutions Private Limited was revised from up to ₹80 crores to up to ₹150 crores. The transaction under the Business Transfer Agreement dated August 31, 2026 was completed after fulfilling all conditions precedent.

IPO proceeds fully utilized. The monitoring agency report for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 records nil deviation from the objects of the issue, with the full ₹554.14 crores utilized. The General Corporate Purpose allocation was fully utilized during the quarter, with no unutilized proceeds remaining.

Dividend and governance. The Board recommended a dividend of ₹2 per equity share (40% of face value of ₹5) for FY26, with a record date of August 14, 2026. All seven resolutions at the 37th AGM held September 9, 2026 were approved, including adoption of financial statements, declaration of the final dividend, re-appointment of directors and statutory auditors, revision of remuneration for the Managing Director, and approval for enhancement of borrowing limits and creation of charge on assets. The Board also approved allotment of 5,06,081 equity shares to eligible employees under the Rashi Peripherals Employee Stock Option Scheme 2022, increasing paid-up capital from ₹32,94,98,325 to ₹33,20,28,730.

3. Key Risks

  • Supply constraints on chips and components could impact volumes in the second half, described by management as an “orange alert” tracked weekly.
  • Elevated pricing and potential consumer affordability pressures in the second half.
  • The price increase trajectory is expected to slow, with management indicating the speed of price increase in Q2 should be about half that of Q1, which could be detrimental to growth.
  • Large AI data center projects carry higher working capital intensity and would roughly double the debt ratio, constraining participation.
  • Channel inventory build-up, though management states it is not a major concern given demand.
  • GST-related contingent liabilities, though historically such notices have closed at less than 1% of the demanded amount.
  • Forward-looking statements are subject to known and unknown risks including performance of the Indian and international economies, industry performance, competition, ability to implement strategy, growth and expansion levels, technological implementation, changes in revenue, income or cash flows, market preferences, and exposure to market risks.
  • Refurbished PCs represent a potential but limited threat; management estimates the refurbished market at 0.5–1 million units against a total PC TAM of 15–16 million units per year, and notes sourcing refurbished products within India is not easy.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Q2 FY27 guidance. Management states Q2 will continue a similar trend to Q1, with the only correction being that the speed of price increase should be about half. Management describes being on a “dream run” for the coming quarter, citing high consumer sentiment, feedback from LFR and online channels, and fear of further price increases triggering buying. July–August–September is traditionally a high quarter, competing with January–February–March for the highest.

FY28 and long-term growth. Management references a 20% CAGR delivered over the last 20 years despite industry ups and downs, and states this remains the base on which the company continues to work. The VDA acquisition and Restar JV are described as strategies for short-term and long-term value creation in topline and bottom line.

Semiconductor JV guidance. Management targets more than US$100 million in revenue under the JV within three years, described as a conservative minimum guidance, with higher margins than the core distribution business. Restar’s investment amount for the 26% stake will depend on fair market valuation at the time of entering the Share Purchase Agreement, and management states it is premature to estimate.

VDA guidance. Management states it is too early to comment precisely, expects approximately 5% of revenue from integrated services and value-added solutions, and will provide a clearer picture in Q2 when numbers are reported. VDA’s FY26 revenue was approximately ₹8.5 billion, with substantial growth planned in the current financial year. Management states it is not looking at topline at this moment and expects value creation over 2–3 years.

Data center guidance. Management states it did not prioritize large deals in the April–June quarter due to 60% growth momentum and working capital preference toward run-rate business, but states it is present in the race with strong credentials and can pick up projects if needed, with some expected in the JAS quarter. VDA and RPTECH will run independent operating strategies for the time being, with no guidance on joint bidding in the next few quarters.

Observed performance versus prior commentary. Management states the quarter unfolded almost exactly as described in previous calls, with the three converging forces—PC refresh cycle, AI PC inflection, and component economics shift—playing out as anticipated. The company delivered record results across topline, EBITDA, PAT and annualized ROCE, with working capital discipline maintained despite strong growth.
