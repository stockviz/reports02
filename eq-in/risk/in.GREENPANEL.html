Greenpanel Industries — Q1 FY27 Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

The quarter began amid heightened volatility following the war in the Middle East. Chemical costs rose significantly, prompting Greenpanel and the wider industry to implement price hikes of around 15% in a phased manner from April to protect margins. Some peers subsequently rolled back prices, forcing Greenpanel to react in kind. By the time of the August 11, 2026 call, management indicated that almost all of the price hike had effectively been rolled back, with only 1–2% retained in certain segments or markets, modified geographically depending on competition.

Export sales fell to zero during the quarter, and remained negligible in July and August. Management attributed this to Middle East markets being shut off, with container freight rates rising from a normal $400–500 per container to $5,500–6,000, making sales unviable. OEM volumes also declined, which management described as partly intentional: rather than supply OEMs at the cost of retail allocation during a chemical shortage, Greenpanel priced OEM sales at retail levels, accepting lost business. OEM volumes degrew 14% year-on-year.

Channel destocking compounded the pressure. Rapid price rollbacks left channel partners unwilling to hold inventory, expecting further reductions, and buying only hand-to-mouth. Management noted this sentiment was still in play at the time of the call.

On the ply side, margins fell from 7.5% to 2.7% quarter-on-quarter despite stable capacity utilization, which management attributed to product mix and higher fixed costs. A 5% plywood price hike was taken in Q1, but transmission was limited by competitive pressure and schemes.

Competitive intensity was a recurring theme. Management described MDF in India as a commodity-like, price-sensitive business with industry overcapacity and insufficient orders, where competitors actively pass discounts. Tier-2 players were cited as cumulatively impacting demand. New capacity was noted — an Action line in South India and another continuous press in Madhya Pradesh. Greenpanel was described by an analyst as having lost its number-one volume position in India to Century for two quarters, and as having the lowest India growth in the industry in Q1.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Retail MDF — described as the most sustainable and value-accretive revenue stream — grew around 20% year-on-year. Domestic MDF volumes grew 12% year-on-year, a level management said had been demonstrated consistently for the last four quarters. Domestic MDF ASP rose 7.4% year-on-year, aided by price hikes and by removing additional OEM discounts, bringing OEM realizations close to retail levels. Total MDF volumes degrew 2.3% year-on-year due to zero exports, but total MDF revenues still grew 8% year-on-year. Ply volumes rose 10.4% year-on-year with revenues up 5%.

Gross margin improved both sequentially and year-on-year, reaching 52.7%, attributed to timely price hike implementation and a change in distribution and product salience. MDF operating EBITDA margin expanded to 10.3% from 4.4% in Q1 last year. Consolidated operating EBITDA, excluding currency movement on the euro borrowing for the new plant, was INR 33.5 crore, or 9.6% of revenues. Total revenue for the quarter was INR 350 crore, up 8.5% year-on-year. Timber and efficiency measures saved 6–7% overall in Q1.

Brand and retail investments continued: participation at Bharat Buildcon, a relaunched website, and strategic television partnerships with six leading news channels.

Management expects OEM demand to return in the current quarter now that prices are back to competitive levels, since imports remain an alternative for OEMs if domestic pricing is uncompetitive. Export flows are expected to improve gradually once Middle East conditions normalize. The stated plan is to ramp up domestic volumes over the remaining nine months to improve relative market share for the full year, and to price at par with peers rather than at a premium to Century. Management also noted that no substantial industry expansions are projected beyond one or two lines, which could reduce price undercutting once demand catches up with supply.

On capex, no MDF capital expenditure is needed for 18–24 months beyond maintenance. On plywood, the plan is to fully utilize current capacity, with possible small machinery additions at the existing facility to enhance production by 30–40% at minimal investment.

Key Risks

  • Continued chemical cost volatility, moving on a day-to-day basis, with chemical costs still 4–5% above pre-war levels and again trending upward; further hikes could trigger another round of industry price increases.
  • Freight volatility affecting export competitiveness beyond the Middle East, where most other foreign markets already have their own MDF manufacturing.
  • Aggressive competitor pricing and discounting amid overcapacity, with Greenpanel acknowledging it must react to peer rollbacks.
  • Channel destocking persisting while price sentiment remains volatile.
  • Loss of domestic market share and the number-one volume position, with management committing to aggressive pricing to avoid losing volume options.
  • Import substitution risk for OEM customers if domestic prices become uncompetitive.
  • Ply margin pressure from product mix and elevated fixed costs.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Management declined to provide full-year margin or capacity utilization guidance, citing uncertainty over chemical costs and competitive pricing. On capacity utilization, management said the plan is to enhance it this year subject to market volume, but gave no accurate number given export uncertainty. The Q1 utilization referenced by an analyst was about 51%.

Where guidance can be compared with observed performance: management’s stated intent to protect margins through price hikes was reflected in the 52.7% gross margin and 10.3% MDF operating EBITDA margin, though the subsequent rollbacks mean the pricing benefit largely unwound. The decision to prioritize retail over OEM was reflected in 20% retail MDF growth alongside 14% OEM degrowth. Management’s expectation that OEM demand returns this quarter and that domestic volume ramp-up delivers improved relative market share remains forward-looking and unverified in the supplied material. Management’s objective to maintain current margins was stated with the caveat that there may be a lag in implementing future price hikes.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a 2023 wood-products thesis focused on MDF volume/export weakness and Plywood margins to a 2026 PVC/resin cycle thesis centered on channel destocking and import duties, while persistent themes of capacity oversupply, raw-material cost volatility, and weak domestic demand endured. Despite repeated Buy calls and tailwinds around capacity additions, duties, and cash flow, the stock delivered deep negative returns across nearly all reports, indicating that operational headwinds consistently overwhelmed optimism. The late-stage focus on PVC imports and Wavin integration mirrored early MDF/export concerns, but the structural margin pressure and volume weakness proved durable.

Fears that came true

  • Weak domestic MDF volumes (down 7% YoY) and higher mix of lower-margin MDF exports (up 89% YoY) materialized as sustained revenue pressure, correlating with repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Steep cut in MDF export realisations (down 22% YoY) and lower blended realisations persisted, contributing to consistent negative actual returns across Buy calls.
  • Poor Plywood results—volume/revenue down 17%/23% YoY and EBITDA margin of 0.4%—reflected ongoing operational weakness that translated into multi-year underperformance.
  • Raw material cost inflation, including ~25% YoY rise in timber prices, continued to pressure margins and align with sustained negative returns.
  • Rising MDF imports and large domestic capacity additions over the next 1-2 years created oversupply conditions that weighed on prices and volumes.
  • Inferior product mix and inventory losses (~4% of revenue) in Plywood materialized as a recurring drag on profitability.

Optimism that failed

  • FY24 guidance of MDF volume growth 12-15% and EBITDA margin 23-25% failed as actual returns were deeply negative across multiple reports.
  • Possible additional duty (ADD) on MDF imports did not materialize sufficiently to reverse the DISAPPOINTMENT trend.
  • New 231,000cbm MDF capacity expected operational during 1QFY25 added supply rather than driving growth, contributing to persistent price pressure.
  • Strong operating cash flow (Rs 540mn) and Rs 1.87bn net cash did not prevent the stock from correcting further, indicating the thesis failed to sustain.
  • Reinstatement of import duties, MIP, channel restocking, capacity additions, Wavin integration, and diversified portfolio touted in the last report failed to generate positive returns (-1.1% actual, DISAPPOINTMENT).
  • Stable domestic MDF prices despite volume pressure proved fragile, with later reports citing sharp resin/PVC volatility instead.
