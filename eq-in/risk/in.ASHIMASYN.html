Ashima Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements: Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Discontinued cotton textile operations remain a drag. The company’s cotton textile operations were discontinued in FY 2024-25, and their results continue to be reported separately as discontinued operations. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, the discontinued segment reported nil revenue and a segment loss before interest and tax of ₹(75) lakhs on a standalone basis, with segment assets of ₹2,721 lakhs and liabilities of ₹1,115 lakhs. The operational movable assets of the cotton textile business (excluding land and building) are to be disposed of and have been transferred to “assets held for sale” at the lower of carrying amount and fair value less cost to sell.

Real estate segment is loss-making. On a standalone basis, the Real Estate segment reported a segment loss before interest and tax of ₹(67) lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, compared with a loss of ₹(63) lakhs in the same quarter a year earlier and a profit of ₹15 lakhs in the immediately preceding quarter ended March 31, 2026. For the full year ended March 31, 2026, the segment loss was ₹(139) lakhs. Segment assets in real estate stood at ₹31,795 lakhs as at June 30, 2026, up from ₹30,324 lakhs as at March 31, 2026.

Finance costs are substantial relative to operating profitability. Standalone finance costs were ₹246 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, compared with ₹172 lakhs in the same quarter a year earlier and ₹264 lakhs in the preceding quarter. For the full year ended March 31, 2026, finance costs were ₹896 lakhs. These charges exceeded the standalone segment results total of ₹418 lakhs for the quarter, contributing to a modest profit before tax of ₹171 lakhs.

Prior-year losses. For the year ended March 31, 2026, the standalone loss for the period from continuing operations was ₹(601) lakhs, with a loss from discontinued operations of ₹(885) lakhs, and a total loss for the period. Basic and diluted EPS for continuing and discontinued operations for FY 2025-26 was ₹(1.13) on a standalone basis and ₹(1.06) on a consolidated basis.

Investment activity introduces earnings volatility. Net gain on fair valuation of equity shares held for investment activities is disclosed as part of revenue from operations, while losses on fair valuation are disclosed as a separate expense line. In the quarter ended March 31, 2026, a loss on fair valuation of ₹221 lakhs was recorded; for FY 2025-26 the loss was ₹362 lakhs. This asymmetry means investment mark-to-market movements can swing reported results.

Subsidiary not reviewed by the principal auditor. The consolidated results include the subsidiary Ashima Capital Management Limited, whose interim financial information was reviewed by another auditor. The parent’s auditor relied solely on that report for the subsidiary’s figures.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Sharp year-on-year improvement in standalone profitability. Standalone total income for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was ₹941 lakhs, compared with ₹350 lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. Profit before tax from continuing operations was ₹171 lakhs, compared with a loss of ₹(96) lakhs a year earlier. Profit for the period was ₹133 lakhs, compared with a loss of ₹(281) lakhs in the prior-year quarter. Basic and diluted EPS for continuing and discontinued operations turned positive at ₹0.07 for the quarter, against ₹(0.15) a year earlier.

Investment segment is the principal profit driver. On a standalone basis, the Investment segment reported segment results before interest and tax of ₹486 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, compared with ₹236 lakhs in the same quarter a year earlier. Investment segment revenue was ₹516 lakhs for the quarter, versus ₹244 lakhs a year earlier. Investment segment assets rose to ₹10,226 lakhs as at June 30, 2026 from ₹9,727 lakhs as at March 31, 2026 and ₹3,400 lakhs as at June 30, 2025.

Consolidated performance also improved. Consolidated total income for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was ₹1,009 lakhs, against ₹390 lakhs a year earlier. Consolidated profit before tax from continuing operations was ₹253 lakhs, versus a loss of ₹(58) lakhs a year earlier. Consolidated profit for the period was ₹193 lakhs, against a loss of ₹(253) lakhs in the prior-year quarter. Consolidated EPS for continuing and discontinued operations was ₹0.10 for the quarter.

Investment Management & Advisory Services segment contributes. On a consolidated basis, this segment reported revenue of ₹59 lakhs and segment results before interest and tax of ₹56 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, compared with revenue of ₹53 lakhs and results of ₹47 lakhs in the preceding quarter. Segment assets were ₹799 lakhs.

Real estate development activity continues. Land development and construction cost was ₹1,747 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, and ₹9,359 lakhs for FY 2025-26, indicating ongoing project execution. Real estate segment assets increased to ₹31,795 lakhs as at June 30, 2026.

Governance reconstitution. By circular resolution passed on July 1, 2026, the Board approved reconstitution of the Nomination and Remuneration Committee and the Stakeholders’ Relationship Committee with effect from that date. On the Nomination and Remuneration Committee, Mr. Neeraj Golas was replaced by Mr. Malay Jayendra Dalal as a member, with Mr. Nilesh Mehta continuing as Chairman, Mrs. Koushlya Melwani as member, and Mr. Chintan N. Parikh as member. On the Stakeholders’ Relationship Committee, Mr. Neeraj Golas was replaced by Mr. Krish Nachintan Parikh (Executive Director), with Mrs. Koushlya Vijay Melwani continuing as Chairman and Mr. Malay Jayendra Dalal as member.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued losses in discontinued operations, with the cotton textile segment reporting nil revenue and negative segment results, and remaining assets held for sale subject to valuation adjustments.
  • Real estate segment losses persisting across quarters, with the segment reporting negative results in the quarter ended June 30, 2026, the quarter ended June 30, 2025, and the full year FY 2025-26.
  • High finance costs relative to segment operating results, which absorb a significant portion of earnings.
  • Earnings dependence on investment fair value movements, which are volatile and asymmetrically presented between revenue and expense lines.
  • Inventory and cost dynamics in real estate, with changes in inventories of finished goods, work-in-progress and stock-in-trade reported as large negative figures (₹(1,586) lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026), alongside land development and construction costs.
  • Reliance on a subsidiary audited by a different auditor, with the parent’s auditor not reviewing the subsidiary’s interim financial information.
  • Deferred tax credits contributing to reported profits in some periods, which may not recur.
