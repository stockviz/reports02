Speciality Restaurants — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Tax litigation setback: On July 22, 2026, the company disclosed that the Tamil Nadu Sales Tax Appellate Tribunal (Additional Bench), Chennai, dismissed its six miscellaneous petitions seeking rectification of earlier orders dated August 28, 2015 and March 13, 2017. The dispute concerned the applicable VAT rate on supply of food and non-alcoholic beverages for assessment years 2006-07, 2007-08 and 2008-09. The order was dated April 27, 2026, signed May 18, 2026, and received by the company on July 21, 2026. The Tribunal held no error apparent on the face of the record existed. The company is evaluating the order with tax advisors and will pursue available legal remedies. It had already deposited ₹1,71,68,672 with the authorities, so no incremental cash flow impact arises, but the financial impact may extend to the deposited amount. No impact on operations was reported.
  • General operating environment: The company flags general market, macro-economic, governmental and regulatory trends, movements in currency exchange and interest rates, competitive pressures, technological developments, changes in the financial condition of third parties dealing with the company, and legislative developments as material risks.
  • Forward-looking statement caveat: The Q1FY27 press release notes that forward-looking statements reflect current management views based on currently available information and are not a guarantee of future performance; underlying assumptions could prove inaccurate.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong Q1FY27 performance (quarter ended June 30, 2026):
    • Standalone: Total income ₹125.79 cr vs ₹108.73 cr (up 15.69%); EBITDA ₹27.85 cr vs ₹23.69 cr (up 17.56%); PAT ₹6.88 cr vs ₹5.68 cr (up 21.13%); total comprehensive income ₹6.57 cr vs ₹6.09 cr.
    • Consolidated: Total income ₹131.30 cr vs ₹114.51 cr (up 14.66%); EBITDA ₹28.61 cr vs ₹23.59 cr (up 21.28%); PAT ₹7.11 cr vs ₹5.12 cr (up 38.87%); total comprehensive income ₹6.73 cr vs ₹6.56 cr.
  • Same-store sales growth: 11.35% SSSG across restaurants and confectioneries, described as evidence of healthy consumer demand and organic growth independent of new store contributions.
  • Walters QSR momentum: Revenue up 345% in the quarter to ₹1.56 crore from ₹35 lakh a year earlier; now 1.3% of consolidated revenue versus 0.3% previously. Plans for a minimum of 12–15 new Walters stores in Mumbai over the next three quarters, plus a cloud kitchen to widen delivery reach.
  • Brand portfolio and market position: Market leadership in the Pan-Asian/Oriental cuisine segment; growing dine-in sales; strong home delivery revenue; versatile brand portfolio with strong customer loyalty. Plans include new outlets of Oriental Cuisine, Siciliana, Sweet Bengal and Walters, and upgrades to existing Mainland China and Asia Kitchen restaurants.
  • Network scale: 73 restaurants, 34 confectionery stores and 11 cloud kitchens across 11 Indian cities as of June 30, 2026, plus 2 Asia Kitchen outlets in UAE, 1 in Muscat, Oman, and 1 Chourangi restaurant in London. The company reports 118 units as of June 30, 2026, and profitability for 20 consecutive quarters in Q1FY27.
  • Leadership transition: Avik Chatterjee took charge as Chief Executive Officer in Q1FY27, having spent much of his career within the organisation and played a hands-on role in building and scaling several brands.
  • Subsidiary funding: Speciality Hotels India Private Limited (SHIPL), a subsidiary, allotted 65,961 equity shares of face value ₹100 each at ₹838.57 per share for a total consideration of ₹5,53,12,916 via a rights issue to Esensos Services and Solutions LLP on August 24, 2026. SHIPL’s paid-up share capital rose to ₹2,66,57,600, with Speciality Restaurants’ shareholding at 51.21%.
  • Shareholder returns: The 27th Annual General Meeting on September 11, 2026 passed five ordinary resolutions, including adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements, declaration of a dividend of ₹1/- (10%) per equity share, and re-appointment of directors Mr. Avik Chatterjee and Mr. Aditya Ghosh.
  • Investor engagement: An Analyst/Institutional Investor one-to-one meeting is scheduled for Tuesday, September 22, 2026, at 2:30 p.m. IST in Vasant Kunj, New Delhi, with Mr. Chander Bhatia, Mr. Rajesh Seth and Mr. Shaleen Seth of Seers Group.

3. Key Risks

  • Adverse outcome of the Tamil Nadu VAT matter, with financial impact potentially extending to the ₹1,71,68,672 already deposited; the company is pursuing legal remedies.
  • General market, macro-economic, governmental and regulatory trends; currency exchange and interest rate movements; competitive pressures; technological developments; changes in financial conditions of third parties dealing with the company; and legislative developments.
  • Forward-looking statements are not guarantees of future performance and may prove incorrect if underlying assumptions are inaccurate.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Guidance: Management stated it remains focused on improving productivity of the existing network, enhancing customer experience, maintaining cost discipline and pursuing calibrated expansion, aiming to deliver sustainable revenue and profitability growth in the quarters ahead. The CEO outlined priorities to strengthen existing brands, expand into the right markets, create new concepts responding to evolving customer preferences, sharpen operations, use technology to improve efficiency, invest in customer experience, and explore opportunities overseas. Specific expansion guidance: a minimum of 12–15 new Walters stores in Mumbai over the next three quarters, alongside a cloud kitchen. Management also notes plans to open new outlets of Oriental Cuisine, Siciliana, Sweet Bengal and Walters, and to upgrade existing Mainland China and Asia Kitchen restaurants, while cautioning actual developments and results may differ materially.
  • Observed performance: Q1FY27 consolidated PAT growth of 38.87% outpaced revenue growth of 14.66%, which management described as reflecting progress in operating efficiency and profitability. SSSG of 11.35% was cited as evidence that established outlets continue to see healthy consumer demand and generate organic growth independent of new stores. Walters demonstrated early execution of the QSR expansion thesis, with revenue up 345% year-on-year to ₹1.56 crore and contribution rising to 1.3% of consolidated revenue from 0.3%. The company reported profitability for 20 consecutive quarters in Q1FY27 and standalone revenue from operations of ₹12,161.70 lakh for the quarter ended June 30, 2026.
