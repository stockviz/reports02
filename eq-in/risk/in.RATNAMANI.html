Ratnamani Metals & Tubes — Q1 FY2026-27 and Recent Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Weak infrastructure spending: Lower government spending on infrastructure projects, including the water segment, subdued demand, and lower sales volumes weighed on the quarter. Standalone revenue fell to ₹740.78 crores in Q1 FY27 from ₹1,062.40 crores in Q1 FY26.
  • Under-absorption of fixed costs: Lower volumes led to under-absorption of fixed costs and margin pressure. Standalone EBITDA margin declined to 14.2% from 21.3%, and PBT margin to 9.9% from 18.4%.
  • Geopolitical disruption: The ongoing geopolitical situation in the Middle East impacted demand, project execution, and raw material supplies, per the CEO’s message.
  • Profitability decline: Standalone PAT fell to ₹54.06 crores from ₹144.96 crores; consolidated PAT fell to ₹107.03 crores from ₹127.13 crores, despite subsidiary support.
  • Subsidiary losses: Ravi Technoforge reported a PBT loss of ₹6.20 crores and PAT loss of ₹7.49 crores in Q1 FY27. RFSS PAT declined to ₹2.66 crores from ₹3.92 crores.
  • Project delay: The Ratnamani Middle East Company, LLC (KSA) greenfield project timeline was revised to March 2028.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Subsidiary outperformance: RTL revenue rose to ₹97.35 crores from ₹64.4 crores, and RFSS revenue rose to ₹119.63 crores from ₹12.60 crores, supporting consolidated profitability.
  • Major export order: RFSS received export orders for spools/hangers aggregating USD 286 million (approx. ₹2,700 crores), to be executed over two to three years, partly in-house and partly sub-contracted on a mercantile trade transaction basis; these are not related-party transactions.
  • Order book: Standalone order book increased to over ₹2,000 crores; RFSS holds an active order book exceeding ₹300 crores (~USD 32 million), targeted for execution over nine months.
  • Capacity expansion completed: External 3LPE coating facility at the Odisha plant (25 lakh sq. metres p.a.) and the HSAW facility at Kutch (pipes up to 18 metres, 18”–143” diameters, 1,00,000 MT capacity) were completed in July 2026.
  • Ongoing expansions: RFSS capacity rising from 1,400 MT to 4,000 MT annually (ready by Q3, commercial production from Q4); RTL’s Pipaliya unit capacity expanded by 20%; RTL commissioned a 9 MW solar plant, with 50% of energy from green sources benefiting Q1 FY27; RTL’s long-term credit rating upgraded from CARE A to CARE A+.
  • Exports and enquiries: Exports witnessed encouraging growth, and enquiry levels across key markets continue to improve.
  • Financial strength: Company remains debt free on a standalone basis; market capitalisation of ₹17,500 crores as of June 30, 2026; AA/Positive CRISIL rating.
  • Dividend: Dividend of ₹10.00 per equity share (500%) for FY2025-26 was declared and passed at the 42nd AGM on August 18, 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued dependence on government infrastructure spending, including water projects, for demand recovery.
  • Geopolitical conditions in the Middle East affecting demand, execution, and raw material supplies.
  • Fixed-cost under-absorption and margin pressure when volumes are low.
  • Execution risk on the large RFSS export order, part of which relies on sub-contracting on a mercantile trade transaction basis.
  • Subsidiary performance divergence, with RTL reporting losses in the quarter.
  • Revised timelines on greenfield projects, such as the KSA facility now targeted for March 2028.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Management stated the Company remains confident of long-term growth prospects, backed by enhanced manufacturing capabilities, a strong order pipeline, and subsidiary momentum. Observed performance shows consolidated profitability supported by subsidiaries even as standalone results declined sharply year-on-year.
  • The RFSS export order guidance is execution over two to three years; the active RFSS order book of over ₹300 crores is targeted for execution over nine months.
  • RFSS expansion guidance: new capacity ready by Q3, commercial production from Q4.
  • At the 42nd AGM (August 18, 2026), management reviewed business performance, financials, business outlook, capex plans, opportunities, and future growth prospects, though specific forward-looking numerical targets were not detailed. All five resolutions were passed with the requisite majority.
