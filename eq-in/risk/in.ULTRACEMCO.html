UltraTech Cement — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Cost inflation across key inputs. Power and fuel expense rose to Rs. 5,418.65 crores in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 4,861.90 crores a year earlier, and freight and forwarding expense rose to Rs. 5,210.61 crores from Rs. 4,648.97 crores. The investor presentation attributes higher logistics cost partly to an increase in diesel cost, and higher raw material cost to an increase in industrial diesel prices. Other expenses also rose, with the presentation citing an increase in packing material cost.

Margin pressure relative to the preceding quarter. Consolidated operating margin was 21% in Q1 FY27 versus 22% in Q4 FY26, and net profit margin was 11% versus 12%. Sequentially, revenue from operations declined from Rs. 25,799.47 crores in Q4 FY26 to Rs. 24,648.20 crores in Q1 FY27, and net profit fell from Rs. 3,000.02 crores to Rs. 2,603.72 crores.

Overseas volume decline. While domestic grey cement volumes grew 13.1% and domestic white cement volumes grew 12.8%, the overseas category declined 11.2% year on year.

India Cements revenue softness. India Cements revenue was Rs. 1,013 crores, down 0.7% year on year, even as its normalised PAT turned to Rs. 52 crores from a net loss of Rs. 183 crores in Q1 FY25.

Comparability distortion from acquisitions. The results include Birla White WallCare from 29 May 2025, so Q1 FY27 figures are not comparable with the corresponding prior period. The company also recognised a statutory impact of the new Labour Codes of Rs. 88.48 crores (consolidated) as an exceptional item in the year ended 31 March 2026, relating to additional gratuity liability and compensated absences.

Working capital and leverage metrics. Consolidated long-term debt to working capital stood at 26.72 times in Q1 FY27, compared with negative 16.73 times in Q4 FY26 and negative 21.48 times in Q1 FY26. The current ratio was 1.02 times consolidated and 0.94 times standalone.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong top-line and profit growth. Consolidated net sales rose 16% to Rs. 24,465 crores from Rs. 21,040 crores, PBIDT rose to Rs. 5,146 crores from Rs. 4,591 crores, and PAT rose to Rs. 2,604 crores from Rs. 2,221 crores. Domestic sales volumes were 39.2 million tonnes, up 13.1%. Consolidated sales volume was 41.31 MnT, up 12.2%.

Operating efficiency gains. Operating EBITDA per tonne improved to Rs. 1,214 from Rs. 1,198 in the prior-year quarter. Power cost per tonne fell to Rs. 322 from Rs. 356, helped by a shift in the power mix toward renewable energy (26.0% from 19.1%) and waste heat recovery (22.2% from 20.4%), and by lower captive thermal power rates. Lead distance was reduced by 8 km year on year and 7 km quarter on quarter to 360 km.

Capacity expansion. The company crossed 200.1 MTPA domestic grey cement capacity and 205.5 MTPA global capacity in April 2026, commissioning 8.7 MTPA during the quarter. The roadmap shows all-India grey cement capacity rising from 191.4 MTPA in FY26 to 207.3 MTPA in FY27 and 237.1 MTPA in FY28, with overall capacity (including overseas) rising from 196.8 MTPA to 242.5 MTPA. FY27 additions of 15.9 MTPA and FY28 additions of 29.8 MTPA are planned across greenfield and brownfield projects.

New business lines. The wires and cables business has secured regulatory certifications, established a warehouse and supply chain network, appointed logistics partners, and begun trial production for LDC wires including E-beam. Launch is targeted for Q3 FY27, with planned investment of Rs. 1,800 crores and Rs. 888 crores committed till June 2026.

Sustainability progress. WHRS capacity rose to 434 MW after commissioning 20 MW during the quarter, renewable power capacity reached 1.46 GW, and the green power mix was 45.6% (47% per the press release). The company was recognised as an S&P Global Sustainability Yearbook 2026 Member for the Construction Materials Sector. It deployed 45 electric heavy-duty trucks, estimated to reduce over 8,900 tonnes of emissions annually.

Macro support. The presentation cites RBI FY27 GDP growth estimates of ~6.6%, a repo rate held at 5.25%, GST collection growth of 13.9% YoY in June 2026 and 8.4% YoY in Q1 FY27, IIP growth of 5.1% YoY in May 2026, and manufacturing and services PMI of 54.2 and 57.3 in June 2026.

Balance sheet strength. Consolidated net worth rose to Rs. 83,431.66 crores from Rs. 80,712.44 crores in March 2026. Net debt fell to Rs. 15,875 crores from Rs. 16,620 crores. The debt-equity ratio improved to 0.27 times from 0.28 times.

3. Key Risks

Competition Commission of India penalties. Orders dated 31 August 2016 (penalty of Rs. 1,804.31 crores consolidated; Rs. 1,616.83 crores standalone) and 19 January 2017 (penalty of Rs. 68.30 crores) are under appeal. The NCLAT disallowed appeals against the 2016 order, and the company has appealed to the Supreme Court, which granted a stay on 5 October 2018. The company has deposited Rs. 180.43 crores consolidated (Rs. 161.68 crores standalone), equivalent to 10% of the 2016 penalty, recorded as an asset. Backed by legal opinions, the company believes it has a good case and has recognised no provision.

Regulatory cost impact from Labour Codes. The Code on Wages 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020 and Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020 introduce a uniform definition of wages and enhanced leave benefits. Draft Central Rules and FAQs are still being issued, creating uncertainty around the full financial impact.

Input cost volatility. Diesel, industrial diesel and packing material cost increases have already affected logistics, fuel and raw material costs, and could continue to pressure margins.

Execution risk on expansion. The FY27 and FY28 capacity roadmaps depend on delivery of multiple greenfield and brownfield projects across states, alongside the wires and cables launch targeted for Q3 FY27.

Working capital sensitivity. The consolidated long-term debt to working capital ratio swung to 26.72 times in Q1 FY27 from negative readings in prior periods, reflecting the sensitivity of this metric to net working capital movements.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Capacity targets. The presentation sets an FY27 all-India grey cement capacity target of 207.3 MTPA and an FY28 target of 237.1 MTPA. Observed performance: the company crossed 200.1 MTPA domestic grey cement capacity and 205.5 MTPA global capacity in April 2026, and commissioned 8.7 MTPA during Q1 FY27, against a planned FY27 addition of 15.9 MTPA.

Wires and cables launch. Management targets launch in Q3 FY27. Observed progress: regulatory certifications secured, warehouse network established, logistics partners appointed, operating team in place, and trial production for LDC wires including E-beam underway. Product portfolio and design finalisation is near completion, and the loyalty and CRM platform launch is on track.

Green power mix. The FY30 target is 85%. Observed performance: green power mix reached 45.6% in Q1 FY27 (47% per the press release), with FY26 consolidated green power mix at 35.8%, up 28.8% YoY.

Scope 1 emissions. The FY32 target is 462 kg CO2/t cement. Observed FY26 consolidated performance was 538 kg CO2/t cement, a 2.0% YoY reduction.

India Cements turnaround. The press release describes rapid stabilisation and integration of acquired assets. Observed performance: India Cements Q1 FY27 normalised PAT was Rs. 52 crores versus a net loss of Rs. 183 crores in Q1 FY25, supported by 18.5% volume growth, though revenue was down 0.7% YoY at Rs. 1,013 crores.

Fundraising. On 23 July 2026, following a letter dated 20 July 2026, the Finance Committee approved issuance of up to 5,00,000 unsecured, listed, rated, redeemable, rupee-denominated, non-convertible, non-cumulative debentures of Rs. 1,00,000 each, aggregating up to Rs. 5,000 crores, on a private placement basis in one or more tranches.

Dalla Super resolution. Following a settlement and a final arbitral award on 26 March 2026, all rights and interests in the Dalla Super unit and mines vested fully in the company, and all claims, proceeds and liabilities relating to the Series A Redeemable Preference Shares were fully discharged on 2 April 2026.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from unconditional bullish optimism in 2023—citing capacity expansion, market share gains, and cost reductions with zero headwinds—to a cautious Hold in 2026 that explicitly warns of sharp cement price falls and fuel price surges, reflecting a shift from growth faith to risk-awareness as the stock deteriorated.

Fears that came true

  • The sharp fall in cement prices risk materialized through sustained negative monthly returns in 2025 (-5.1% to -7.4%), correlating with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes on Buy calls.
  • The major surge in fuel prices risk materialized as early fuel cost reduction tailwinds reversed, contributing to the stock’s decline in 2025-2026.

Optimism that failed

  • Domestic grey cement capacity/volume CAGR exceeding industry growth failed to support prices, with the stock declining -6.9% in July 2025 despite expansion narratives.
  • Cost reduction initiatives and green energy synergies could not prevent the -7.4% drop in September 2025, showing operational tailwinds were insufficient.
  • Grey cement realization CAGR outperformance and market share gains did not translate into sustained returns, as Buy calls in late 2025 resulted in DISAPPOINTMENT.
