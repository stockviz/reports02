Popular Vehicles and Services Limited — Q1FY27 and FY26 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Profitability pressure despite revenue growth. In Q1FY27, total income rose 44.6% year-on-year to Rs. 1,903.1 Crs and EBITDA rose 86.6% to Rs. 71.5 Crs, but PAT was only Rs. 1.4 Crs (0.1% margin), against a loss of Rs. 8.8 Crs in Q1FY26. For FY26, consolidated revenue from operations rose 15.16% to Rs. 63,810.96 million, yet the consolidated loss widened to Rs. 124.74 million from Rs. 104.63 million. The company attributes this to Ind AS-related acquisition accounting adjustments, integration costs, higher depreciation and finance costs, and a one-off cess provision.

CV moderation. CV volumes declined 5.9% quarter-on-quarter in Q1FY27 (3,495 units vs 3,716), with CV revenue down 12.5% QoQ, which the company links to the prevailing macroeconomic environment.

Service volume softness. PV service volumes fell 4.5% YoY in Q1FY27, attributed to rationalisation of low-value job cards. For FY26, total service volumes declined 5.8% to 9,82,100 jobs, partly reflecting the Honda and Piaggio exits.

Lower realisations. New vehicle average selling price fell 14.0% YoY in Q1FY27 to Rs. 8,40,955, with revenue growth trailing volume growth due to product mix and realisations. The YoY comparison also reflects GST rate reductions implemented in September 2025.

Higher leverage. Net Debt to EBITDA rose to 3.1x in FY26 from 2.2x in FY25, and Debt to Equity was 1.1x, reflecting borrowings for acquisitions and network expansion. ROCE fell to 5.7% from 7.2%.

Other pressures. Luxury volumes were temporarily affected by a cyber incident at one OEM partner. Select supply constraints in Keralam moderated PV revenue growth in FY26. The acquired dealerships are witnessing only gradual recovery in service throughput from subdued levels at acquisition.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

GST 2.0 demand revival. The September 2025 GST rate rationalisation improved affordability, particularly in the entry-level segment, and drove a sustained industry inflection. Management cites improving customer sentiment supported by these reforms as a driver of Q1FY27 demand.

Strong volume momentum. Q1FY27 new vehicle volumes were 17,300 units, up ~81.5% YoY and 16.2% QoQ. PV (incl. luxury) rose 82.6% YoY, CV 41.0%, and EV 152.7%. EV volumes grew 8.2% sequentially on an already high Q4 base.

Acquisition contributions. Q1FY27 revenue included Rs. 71 Crs from Globe CV (BharatBenz), Rs. 126 Crs from R.K.S Motors (MSIL), and Rs. 20 Crs from Olympus Motors (Audi). FY26 acquisitions spanned Punjab (BharatBenz from Globe CV, August 2025), Telangana (R.K.S. Motor slump sale effective 15 October 2025), and Audi operations in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh (commenced January 2026).

Geographic diversification. Revenue contribution from Keralam declined to below 50% in Q1FY27 (49%), with Tamil Nadu 22%, Karnataka 12%, Telangana 8%, Maharashtra 5%, Punjab 4%, and Andhra Pradesh 0.3%. Revenue from outside Keralam rose to ~47% in FY26 from 28% at IPO.

Network and portfolio expansion. New touchpoints in Q1FY27 included an MSIL service centre at Koyilandy, two Tata Motors CV sales outlets in Keralam, and a JLR sales and service facility at Nagpur (operations commenced 1 April 2026). Ather expansion continued into Karnataka and Maharashtra, and the BKT tyre distributorship commenced across Keralam and Karnataka. Zparex Digisolutions was incorporated as an e-commerce spares platform.

After-sales resilience. FY26 service revenue rose 8.3% to Rs. 968 Crore despite lower volumes, supported by higher-value jobs, collision repair, and CV/EV servicing growth. Q1FY27 service income rose 22.6% YoY with average selling price up 21.3%.

Inventory discipline. New vehicle inventory days reduced to ~32 days in Q1FY27 from ~50 days a year earlier, and to ~29 days in FY26 from ~41 days, broadly in line with industry benchmarks.

Industry outlook. FADA’s survey indicates close to 75% of dealers expect FY27 growth of 3–7%, and the RBI projects FY27 GDP growth of 6.6%.

3. Key Risks

  • OEM concentration and dependence. The company operates dealerships for a limited set of OEM partners (Maruti Suzuki, JLR, Audi, Tata Motors, BharatBenz, Ather), and the Chairman notes risks “from OEM concentration to evolving customer preferences.”
  • Acquisition integration and accounting drag. Ind AS-related acquisition accounting and integration costs weighed on FY26 profitability, with the company expecting normalisation as acquired businesses mature.
  • Leverage and return ratios. Elevated debt from acquisitions and network expansion has raised Net Debt/EBITDA to 3.1x and compressed ROCE to 5.7%; improvement depends on stronger cash generation and capital discipline.
  • Regulatory change. The four Labour Codes notified on 21 November 2025 created an incremental exceptional impact (gratuity Rs. 8.43 million, compensated absences Rs. 4.33 million, and other items), with final Central/State Rules still pending.
  • Macroeconomic sensitivity. CV volumes moderated amid the prevailing macroeconomic environment, and demand recovery is tied to factors such as GST reforms, monsoon, and interest rates.
  • Supply and operational disruptions. Select supply constraints in Keralam and a cyber incident at an OEM partner affected performance.
  • Pending regulatory approval. Central Government approval for Mr. John Kuttukaran Paul’s appointment as Whole-Time Director remains pending with regulatory authorities.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Service business normalisation. Management stated that acquired dealerships “continue to witness a gradual recovery in service throughput from the subdued levels at the time of acquisition” and expected these operations “to normalize progressively over the coming quarters.” Observed: Q1FY27 total service volumes grew 1.1% YoY, with organic service volume growth of -1%, indicating recovery is still early.

Acquisition profitability. Management stated that “acquisition-related Ind AS accounting adjustments continued to weigh on reported profitability” while “the operating performance of the acquired businesses has improved steadily.” Observed: Q1FY27 PAT was Rs. 1.4 Crs (0.1% margin), a swing from the Q1FY26 loss of Rs. 8.8 Crs but still thin.

Inventory management. Management highlighted “prudent inventory management” with inventory days at ~32 days versus ~50 days a year ago. Observed: absolute inventory increased ~14% YoY, substantially lower than revenue growth of 44.6%.

FY26 recovery expectations. Management described FY26 as “a year of strategic execution and recovery,” with Q4FY26 revenue growing approximately 28% YoY. Observed: FY26 consolidated revenue grew 15.16%, with reported EBITDA of Rs. 203 crore (up 16%) and adjusted EBITDA up 28% to Rs. 200.9 crore, while reported net loss was Rs. 12.5 crore.

Debt reduction. Management stated focus on “further reducing our Net Debt to EBITDA ratio, which stood at 3.1x in FY26.” Observed: no subsequent figure is provided in the Q1FY27 release to confirm progress.

Festive season outlook. Management expressed confidence “heading into the festive season,” citing “encouraging pre-festive enquiries and customer footfalls across all vehicle segments.” Observed: inventory levels increased sequentially during Q1FY27 as part of festive preparedness, consistent with this expectation.
