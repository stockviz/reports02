Deepak Nitrite (DEEPAKNTR): Recent Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material volatility and cost pressure. Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) saw significant raw material volatility, particularly in propylene, which management described as requiring intensive procurement effort. In April 2026, raw materials were procured at prices substantially higher than in Q4. Within Advanced Intermediates, pressure from high-cost raw materials persisted for certain products.

Commissioning delays. The alkylation plant and the multipurpose agrochemical intermediates facility were slated for commissioning a couple of months earlier than achieved. Management attributed the delay to unavailability of contractual manpower and a natural gas shortage across the country during Q1, which pushed some pre-commissioning activities later.

Logistics and trade disruption. Export operations faced escalating freight rates, insurance rates, and supply-chain disruption, including vessel and container availability issues. One customer in the Middle East faced a war premium. Management noted that these conditions indirectly support domestic sales realizations.

Competitive intensity in legacy chemistries. Management described a “red ocean” environment over recent years in chemistries such as nitration, diazotization, oximation, and reduction, where competitors used profits from other platforms to defend market share, eroding value for new entrants. China’s earlier ramp-up of capacity in these chemistries contributed to this pressure.

Regulatory/safety matter. A Settlement Order dated August 25, 2026 was passed against wholly owned subsidiary Deepak Chem Tech Limited for payment of Rs. 3,00,000 under Section 114 of the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020, regarding violation of Rule 102 of the Gujarat Factories Rules, 1963. The company stated there is no material financial impact beyond this payment.

Duty-free import window. For effectively the entirety of Q1 FY27, the Government had removed import duty on phenol, allowing duty-free imports during the quarter. Management noted the duty has since been reimposed.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record quarterly performance. Q1 FY27 delivered record Revenue, EBITDA, PBT, and PAT. Consolidated revenue from operations was INR 2,592 crore (up 35% YoY, 22% QoQ); EBITDA INR 554 crore (up 159% YoY, 45% QoQ) with margins at 21% versus 11% a year earlier; PBT INR 468 crore (up 202% YoY); PAT INR 345 crore (up 207% YoY). Standalone total income was Rs. 812.94 crore with net profit of Rs. 64.71 crore; consolidated total income was Rs. 2,591.61 crore with net profit attributable to owners of Rs. 344.69 crore.

Phenolics strength. Phenolics delivered its highest-ever quarterly performance: revenue INR 1,775 crore (up 36% YoY, 24% QoQ) and record EBIT of INR 418 crore (up 254% YoY, 46% QoQ), with EBIT margin of 24%. The phenol plant has reached close to 4 lakh tonnes capacity on an annualized run-rate basis, with periods in the quarter hitting a 1 lakh tonne quarterly run rate. Management indicated further debottlenecking investment of about INR 70 crore to reach 4 lakh tonnes, following INR 80–100 crore already invested in advanced process controls on the journey from about 2 lakh tonnes. Management stated there is no new phenol capacity coming up globally, China is largely self-sufficient, and India currently faces short supply with balanced demand.

Advanced Intermediates recovery. AI revenue rose to INR 804 crore (up 33% YoY, 14% QoQ) with EBIT of INR 67 crore (up 89% YoY, 100% QoQ), an 8% margin. Growth is supported by two drivers: demand recovery in previously softer products and capacity-led expansion in established product lines.

Integration and new capacity. The ammonia-to-amines integration chain has been completed and stabilized, alongside expansion of nitration and reduction capacity. Management framed this as a shift from being “a nitration company that buys nitric acid” to “a nitrogen company that nitrates.” MIBK, MIBC, and acetophenone were to be commissioned in August, with the remainder of projects within Q2. The multipurpose agrochemical intermediates facility and MIBK/MIBC projects remain on track. Nitration and reduction assets were commissioned in Q3 of the prior year and are already operating at relatively high utilization, manufacturing 8 products simultaneously.

Polycarbonate and downstream platform. India’s first integrated polycarbonate project is progressing on its execution roadmap. The company has qualified with marquee customers in diverse end applications for polycarbonate compounds, naming Mahindra, Panasonic, Goldmedal, and Tata AutoComp as examples. Management targets H2 FY28–29 for polycarbonate commissioning, with BPA a couple of months later. The new phenol asset in Deepak Chemtech will largely serve as an integration platform for downstream BPA and polycarbonate, with merchant market participation possible at each stage.

R&D pipeline. Products across fluorination, amination, nitration, and specialty chemistries are progressing through customer qualification and commercialization. Flow chemistry platforms within NOx chemistry are being developed; one product that takes 16–18 hours in batch/semi-batch completes in roughly 45–52 seconds in flow. Management expects flow chemistry products to come into play in the next financial year, with current focus on seed samples and market traction.

Renewable energy transition is expected to progressively improve sustainability and cost competitiveness.

Subsidiary operations and funding. Deepak Chem Tech Limited is operating plants for fluorination, nitric acid, nitration, and hydrogenation and pursuing projects across various sites in Gujarat. On August 26, 2026, it issued and allotted 95,00,000 9% Optionally Convertible Redeemable Preference Shares aggregating to Rs. 95 crore to Deepak Phenolics Limited and 80,00,000 such shares aggregating to Rs. 80 crore to Deepak Nitrite Limited.

Governance. At the 55th AGM held August 5, 2026, all 14 resolutions were passed with requisite majority, including financial statement adoption, dividend declaration of Rs. 7.50 per share, director appointments/re-appointments, executive remuneration approvals, and cost auditor remuneration ratification.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued geopolitical developments and pricing volatility influencing the operating environment.
  • Raw material availability and pricing, particularly propylene, benzene, ammonia, toluene, and hydrogen; Q1 required buying competitively priced intermediates alongside propylene procurement.
  • Execution risk on the large propylene and polycarbonate project of INR 11,500 crore, funded 60:40 debt-to-equity, with the entire debt tied up. Peak debt expected around INR 8,000–8,500 crore including working capital, with debt-to-equity not crossing 1.
  • Customer validation cycles for new products, which management noted can range from a couple of months up to 12 months, and historically exceeded 24 months in optical brightening agents.
  • Export market exposure to freight, insurance, and currency movements; AI exports are roughly 40–50% of that segment, with consolidated mix around 80:20 domestic to export.
  • Regulatory compliance risk illustrated by the Deepak Chem Tech settlement order.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance/forward statements: - MIBK, MIBC, and acetophenone to be commissioned in August; remaining projects within Q2. - Polycarbonate commissioning targeted for H2 FY28–29, with BPA a couple of months later. - Further debottlenecking of about INR 70 crore to reach 4 lakh tonnes of phenol; management expressed confidence in achieving the “big 4 number” in the short to medium term. - New AI products expected to have margin profiles in line with or better than the AI average. - Capex: around INR 1,200 crore spent to date on the INR 11,500 crore project; a further INR 1,000–1,600 crore planned this year, with total spend around INR 3,000–3,200 crore; another approximately INR 3,000 crore planned across 2027 and 2028. - Flow chemistry products expected to contribute in the next financial year, not the current one. - Management declined to quantify incremental top-line or EBITDA contribution from new products, and declined to comment on Phenolics spreads and integrated polycarbonate margins.

Observed performance versus prior expectations: - The alkylation and multipurpose plants were slated for commissioning a couple of months earlier than achieved, with the delay attributed to manpower unavailability and a natural gas shortage. - Phenol capacity: earlier viewed as maturing around 3,50,000 tonnes; the plant has since reached close to a 4 lakh tonne annualized run rate, which management attributed to confidence and investment in advanced process controls rather than new technology or additional site. - Q1 phenol volumes were uneven: April included a preponed maintenance shutdown aligned with improving propylene availability, with part of the quarter running on stockpiled intermediates and part on bought-out propylene and benzene. - MIBK/MIBC pre-commissioning runs already met designed consumption norms, with MIBC exceeding originally designed capacity; product quality has been approved by customers. - Nitration and reduction assets commissioned in Q3 of the prior year are already operating at relatively high utilization, ahead of the nitric acid plant commissioning that completes end-to-end alignment. - Management characterized Q1 as a strong start to the year and expressed confidence in sustaining momentum, while noting it refrains from commenting on spreads and would not treat the 40–50% AI export mix as fixed guidance given quarter-to-quarter variation.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from growth-and-capex-driven optimism (Rs 25bn capex, debottlenecking, value-added derivatives, high utilisation) in May 2023 to a margin-survival narrative dominated by the West Asia raw material cost crisis (3.6x sulfur surge, import dependence, subsidy lag) by August 2026, while the Accumulate call persisted throughout despite the stock delivering overwhelmingly negative actual returns. Themes of cyclicality, margin pressure, and execution risk persisted across both reports, while growth optimism gave way to cost-crisis headwinds.

Fears that came true

  • Higher opex costs (up 22.4% YoY) impacting EBITDA contributed to persistent DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes across the entire report history.
  • Advanced Intermediates EBIT margin contraction of 865 bps YoY and Phenolics EBIT margin contraction of 45 bps YoY signaled structural margin pressure that worsened as the stock declined severely over multiple years.
  • Execution and timely ramp-up of the ~Rs 25bn capex projects remained a key risk and failed to deliver the expected growth returns, correlating with sustained DISAPPOINTMENT calls.
  • Business cyclicality concerns materialized as the stock experienced severe and prolonged negative returns from mid-2023 through mid-2026.
  • Sharply rising raw material costs due to the West Asia crisis, including a 3.6x sulfur price surge from ~US$260/t to >US$1,000/t, materialized and squeezed manufacturer margins.
  • India’s structural import dependence for fertilizer raw materials and subsidy increases lagging behind raw material cost inflation created a viability gap that continued to squeeze margins into the last report.

Optimism that failed

  • The healthy outlook for the Phenolics business over FY24/FY25, supported by robust domestic demand, failed to translate into positive stock returns as the stock declined sharply across the period.
  • The Phenol plant debottlenecking expected to increase capacity by ~50% did not prevent the stock’s severe and prolonged negative returns.
  • The ~Rs 25bn capex plan for growth projects, backward integration, new downstream products, and forward integration into polycarbonates failed to drive positive returns despite being flagged as a key growth driver.
  • Entry into value-added downstream derivatives and advanced chemistries expected to reduce cyclicality failed to mitigate the stock’s deep cyclical downturn, which produced DISAPPOINTMENT across most reports.
  • Plants operated at high utilisation levels throughout FY23, yet the stock delivered overwhelmingly negative actual returns in the majority of subsequent reports.
  • Government increases in kharif’26 NBS allocation to INR415.3bn, nutrient-wise subsidy hikes, and lower-cost inventory aiding Q1FY27 realizations failed to improve the stock’s trajectory, with the last report still showing DISAPPOINTMENT.
