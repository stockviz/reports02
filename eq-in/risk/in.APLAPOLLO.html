APL Apollo Tubes — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Soft Q1 FY27 volume. Sales volume for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was 744,823 tons, a 6% year-on-year decrease and a 19% quarter-on-quarter decrease. Management described the quarter as “mixed” — volume below expectations, profitability above expectations.

Four factors cited for the volume shortfall (August 3, 2026 call): - UAE operations disrupted by geopolitics — roughly 25,000 tons lost quarter-on-quarter; the Dubai plant fell to 5,000–6,000 tons per month as incoming and outgoing flows were closed, with demurrage costs on ships and pricing pressure. - SG Premium brand volume decline — this brand competes with secondary (sponge iron/patra) material, and a wide primary-to-secondary price gap made it uncompetitive. SG Premium fell to 58,686 tons in Q1 FY27 from 90,504 tons in Q4 FY26. - Energy crisis in India — affected rust-proof pipes and roofing products, costing an estimated 25,000–30,000 tons. - High factory inflation — rising prices across construction materials led channel partners to destock and EPC contractors/developers to delay purchases, hitting both primary and secondary sales.

Primary–secondary steel price gap. Management flagged this as a major structural drag: with a gap of roughly ₹10–12 per kg between primary and secondary material, SG Premium volumes suffer. Management stated that without this impact, growth could have been closer to 25% (as seen in January–March), and that the pain may persist for another one to three quarters.

Competitive intensity. Upstream steel players (Tata, JSW, JSPL, AM/NS, Lloyd, JSL) are adding capacity, and Tata is reportedly expanding into pipes. Management acknowledged it cannot hold 100% market share and targets maintaining 60–65%.

Employee cost inflation. Employee costs rose to ₹1,061 Mn in Q1 FY27 from ₹962 Mn in Q4 FY26, attributed to annual increments and lower production spreading fixed costs.

Working capital swing. Operating cash flow was negative ₹302 Mn in Q1 FY27 versus positive ₹20,023 Mn in FY26, driven by inventory build (₹4,562 Mn) and other working capital movements. Net cash fell to ₹14.1 Bn from ₹15.3 Bn. ROCE declined to 32.0% from 37.3% in FY26; ROE fell to 19.4% from 25.3%.

Export constraints. Exports from India were described as closed due to high container prices.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

July recovery. Volumes rose 20% month-on-month in July, crossing 3 lakh tons. Management targeted 3.30–3.35 lakh tons in August and 3.50–3.60 lakh tons in September, aiming to cross 1 million tons in Q2.

UAE normalization. Materials in transit arrived from July; the company held ~70,000 tons of inventory in transit. July UAE volumes were 10,000–12,000 tons, with a target of 16,000–17,000 tons for the following month and a return to 24,000–25,000 tons by September. Local demand creation was described as strong.

Capacity expansion (2 Mn Ton greenfield/brownfield plus 1 Mn Ton debottlenecking, taking total to 8 Mn Ton by FY28): - Gorakhpur (200,000 tons) — expected to start September 2026, full ramp in Q3. - Siliguri (300,000 tons) — new eastern region capacity. - New Malur (~1 million tons) — a fully value-added plant targeted at ₹8,000+ per ton EBITDA, expected to bring volume and margin stability by Q3 FY28 (December 2027). - Western coastal plant (500,000 tons) — relocated from Bhuj to the Pune corridor, reducing freight cost from ₹1,100–1,200 per ton to ₹200–300 per ton; Mumbai plant to be dedicated to exports. - Debottlenecking — 1 million tons via plant modernization and replacement of conventional mills.

Value-added mix shift. Value-added products are currently ~65% of the portfolio, targeted to reach 75–80% by Q4 FY28, reducing sensitivity to steel price volatility and the primary–secondary gap.

Solar opportunity. Management cited 30–35 GW of annual solar additions translating to ~830 KT of addressable market, expecting solar to contribute 4–5% of total volume over two to three years.

PEB segment. Pre-engineered buildings use ~20% steel pipes; APL Apollo holds 65–70% share in this category.

Infrastructure budget allocation. The press release cited improved government budget allocation for infrastructure as a demand driver, with H2 FY27 expected to outperform H1 FY27.

Steel capacity build-out. Management views the coming wave of blast furnace HRC capacity as a tailwind that will eventually make secondary/sponge iron tubes uncompetitive, benefiting APL Apollo’s HRC-based addressable market.

Group shared services. On August 1, 2026, the Board approved participation in a Group Shared Services Company (up to 20% equity, not exceeding ₹1 crore) to centralize HR, IT, branding and other common functions across APL Apollo, SG Mart, SG Finserv and Apollo Pipes. Subsequently, on September 10, 2026, SG Enterprise Solutions Private Limited was incorporated, with APL Apollo acquiring 20% (10,000 equity shares of ₹10 each, totaling ₹1,00,000).

Manufacturing rationalization. The Board noted Apollo Metalex Limited’s approval to phase-consolidate production from its A-25 unit at Sikandrabad to other group facilities and dispose of the land and building, aimed at optimizing footprint, improving utilization and redeploying capital.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical disruption in the Middle East affecting UAE operations, shipping, demurrage and raw material line-up.
  • Primary–secondary steel price gap — a structural risk to ~30% of the business (SG Premium/patra-competing segment); management noted it may take one to three more quarters to normalize.
  • Commodity price volatility driving dealer destocking/restocking cycles that distort reported demand.
  • Competitive entry by large steel producers into pipes, potentially pressuring market share and pricing.
  • Execution risk on capacity ramp-up — Gorakhpur, Siliguri, Malur and the western plant must come online on schedule to support volume guidance.
  • Operating leverage sensitivity — lower volumes raise per-ton costs; employee and other costs rose in Q1.
  • Export market dependence on container freight rates — currently a constraint.
  • Working capital and cash flow volatility — Q1 saw negative operating cash flow and inventory build.
  • Margin risk from aggressive pricing — management acknowledged tweaking pricing on ~15–20% of the portfolio to regain volume, which could pressure spreads.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Guidance provided (August 3, 2026 call and August 1, 2026 press release): - Volume growth: 15–20% for FY27 (management expressed confidence in 15%, with 20% requiring tailwinds). - EBITDA growth: 20%+ on an absolute basis for FY27. - EBITDA per ton: ₹5,000–5,500 range for FY27; target of ₹6,000 per ton at 8 million tons over two to three years. - Quarterly volume trajectory: Q1 ~7.5 lakh tons, Q2 ~10 lakh tons, Q3 ~10.5 lakh tons, Q4 ~12 lakh tons. - H2 FY27 better than H1 FY27. - Value-added share: 75–80% by Q4 FY28. - ROCE: target to return to 30% and then 40%. - Stability: management expects volume and margin stability once the 7-million-ton capex plan completes, around Q3/Q4 FY28.

Observed performance vs guidance: - Q1 FY27 volume of 744,823 tons was below the company’s own expectations and represented a 6% YoY decline — against a full-year guidance of 15–20% growth, implying a steep ask rate for the remaining nine months. - EBITDA per ton of ₹5,522 was at the top of the guided ₹5,000–5,500 range and flat QoQ despite a 19% volume decline, reflecting pricing power and mix. - EBITDA of ₹4,113 Mn was 11% higher YoY but 20% lower QoQ. - Net profit of ₹2,631 Mn was 11% higher YoY but 26% lower QoQ. - July volumes (3 lakh+ tons) and the targeted Q2 run-rate of ~10 lakh tons are consistent with the guided quarterly trajectory, though management acknowledged monthly visibility is limited. - Management maintained the 15–20% volume and 20%+ EBITDA growth guidance despite the weak Q1, citing Gorakhpur, Siliguri, UAE recovery and export normalization as drivers. - ROCE of 32.0% in Q1 FY27 was below the FY26 level of 37.3%, against the stated target of returning to 30% and then 40%. - Net cash of ₹14.1 Bn remained strong, consistent with the stated focus on working capital prudence, though down from ₹15.3 Bn in FY26.
