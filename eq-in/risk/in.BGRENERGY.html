BGR Energy Systems — Summary of Recent Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Contract claims and cost write-offs. Two balance-of-plant contracts have generated large unresolved claims for work executed beyond the original scope:

  • NUPPL Ghatampur: Management raised a claim of Rs.162,042 lakhs. A Conciliation Committee admitted and recommended Rs.33,969 lakhs to the customer, with the balance pending admission. Until the customer approves and the work order is revised, costs continue to be charged to the profit and loss account — Rs.1,913 lakhs in Q1 FY27 and Rs.41,496 lakhs cumulatively from July 2024 to June 2026.
  • NTTPS Vijayawada: A claim of Rs.76,980 lakhs is pending admission, with the company pursuing it through legal course. Rs.2,266 lakhs was charged to P&L in Q1 FY27 and Rs.7,573 lakhs cumulatively from January 2026 to June 2026.

Severe financial losses. For the quarter ended 30 June 2026, standalone revenue from operations was Rs.1,530 lakhs against total expenses of Rs.32,108 lakhs, producing a net loss of Rs.22,607 lakhs. Finance costs alone were Rs.20,379 lakhs for the quarter. The year ended 31 March 2026 recorded a standalone net loss of Rs.127,982 lakhs. Consolidated Q1 FY27 net loss was Rs.22,329 lakhs.

Revenue contraction. Standalone revenue fell from Rs.8,861 lakhs in Q1 FY26 to Rs.1,530 lakhs in Q1 FY27. The Construction and EPC segment, the dominant revenue source, declined from Rs.7,744 lakhs to Rs.1,339 lakhs over the same comparison.

Debt and NPA overhang. Nine public sector banks assigned their outstanding dues to National Assets Reconstruction Company Ltd (NARCL) on 29 September 2025. ICICI Bank facilities remain under NPA and were not assigned. Axis Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank facilities are non-fund-based and not classified as NPA. Interest of Rs.17,177 lakhs for the June 2026 quarter was provided in the P&L. Terms with NARCL are not yet finalised.

Insolvency proceedings. An NCLT order appointing an IRP was made on 17 April 2026; the NCLAT stayed the order on 30 April 2026 and extended the stay till 30 July 2026.

Going concern uncertainty. The auditors flagged a material uncertainty related to going concern, noting that the terms and conditions of the NARCL assignment were yet to be finalised as at the reporting date. The consolidated review report includes a qualified conclusion because the financial results of subsidiaries BGR Boilers Private Limited and BGR Turbines Company Private Limited for the year ended 31 March 2026 are unaudited.

Operational creditor adjustments. Following an external consultant review, commercial debit notes of Rs.4,460.25 lakhs were raised against vendors, and operational creditor balances of Rs.3,056.75 lakhs were written back on the basis that they became unenforceable due to expiry of the limitation period under the Limitation Act, 1963.

Bank guarantee encashment. The notes record that there has been encashment of bank guarantees.

Project execution and funding delays. The AGM-related material cites delay in execution and delays in financial closure for certain projects, challenges in securing long-term funding, and adverse market conditions.

Filing compliance issue. A discrepancy in the Consolidated XBRL filing relating to non-disclosure of cash and cash equivalents at the beginning of the period in the cash flow statement was acknowledged as inadvertent, requiring a revised filing.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Promoter funding support. Promoters have infused unsecured loans of Rs.43,318 lakhs for business operations to date. The Board approved raising unsecured loans from the Managing Director up to Rs.29 crores in single or multiple tranches, and loans (secured or unsecured) from the Promoter and Promoter group up to Rs.150 crores, with an option to convert into equity, preference shares, or convertible securities. The stated purpose is to meet funding requirements for proposed capital expenditure, operational expenditure, working capital for projects to be undertaken, and general corporate purposes.

Anticipated debt resolution. Management is confident that an agreement will be signed with IDRCL/NARCL effecting substantial reduction in both principal and interest liability during the second quarter of 2026-27. The auditors note that the assignment is expected to improve net worth, liquidity position, and overall financial stability.

Continued contract execution. Despite bank guarantee encashment, the company states it is continuing to execute contracts and is hopeful of amicable solutions.

Sector opportunities. The AGM material cites opportunities in the power and infrastructure sectors, ongoing business operations, and participation in upcoming project tenders.

Leadership and organisational changes. The Board approved the reappointment of Mr. Arjun Govind Raghupathy as Managing Director for five years from 11 November 2026 (subject to shareholder approval at the 40th AGM), and the appointment of Mr. Rangarajan Mukunthan as President for Business Division effective 29 July 2026, bringing over 30 years of experience in strategic planning, operations, business transformation, and operational excellence.

Registered office shift. The Board approved shifting the registered office from Andhra Pradesh to Tamil Nadu, subject to shareholder and Regional Director (MCA) approval.

3. Key Risks

  • Going concern risk: The auditors identify a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the company’s ability to continue as a going concern, contingent on finalisation of NARCL terms.
  • Claim realisation risk: Large claims (Rs.162,042 lakhs at NUPPL Ghatampur and Rs.76,980 lakhs at NTTPS Vijayawada) remain pending admission or approval; costs continue to be expensed until work orders are revised.
  • Insolvency risk: The NCLT IRP appointment order, though stayed by NCLAT until 30 July 2026, represents an ongoing legal risk.
  • Liquidity and funding risk: Heavy finance costs (Rs.20,379 lakhs in Q1 FY27) and dependence on promoter loans and NARCL resolution for funding.
  • Dilution risk: The promoter loan conversion option could increase the Promoter Group’s shareholding, contingent on exercise of the option.
  • Governance and control: Amendments to the Articles of Association re-align rights, representation, and governance arrangements of the Raghupathy Group, including replacing Mrs. Sasikala Raghupathy with Mr. Arjun Govind Raghupathy as representative and permanent chairman of the Board.
  • Audit qualification: The consolidated review report carries a qualified conclusion due to unaudited subsidiary financials.
  • Operational creditor risk: Write-backs and debit notes rely on management judgment and legal assessment, which could be challenged.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance: Management states it is confident that an agreement will be signed with IDRCL/NARCL effecting substantial reduction in both principal and interest liability during the second quarter of 2026-27. Management believes the going concern basis remains appropriate based on future business prospects, proposed fund-raising plans, and other mitigating factors. Management is hopeful of amicable solutions on contracts despite bank guarantee encashment.

Observed performance: As at the reporting date (29 July 2026), the terms and conditions of the NARCL assignment were yet to be finalised, and the auditors noted this as a material uncertainty. The company continued to record substantial losses in Q1 FY27 (standalone net loss of Rs.22,607 lakhs; consolidated net loss of Rs.22,329 lakhs), with finance costs of Rs.20,379 lakhs exceeding revenue from operations of Rs.1,530 lakhs. Revenue declined sharply year-on-year. The NCLAT stay on the IRP appointment was extended only till 30 July 2026. The anticipated NARCL agreement and its financial impact had not materialised as of the results date.
