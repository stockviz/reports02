TARIL: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Capacity utilisation drag at Changodar. Revenue grew 10% YoY in Q1FY27, but QoQ revenue declined 26% (standalone) and 27% (consolidated). Management attributes this to comparatively lower capacity utilisation at the Changodar plant, where ongoing expansion activities affected operational throughput. The expansion is expected to be completed by August 2026, after which improved utilisation is anticipated.

Margin compression. Standalone EBITDA fell 10% YoY to ₹87.37 Cr in Q1FY27, with EBITDA margin declining to 15.62% from 18.94% in Q1FY26. PAT declined 17% YoY. Consolidated EBITDA was broadly flat YoY (up 1%), with margin at 19.16% versus 20.50%.

Raw material and geopolitical exposure. Availability of critical raw materials and unforeseen geopolitical disruptions are flagged as key monitorables. Industry-wide challenges cited include supply chain and lead time issues, high raw material cost volatility, and limited component suppliers.

Backward integration timeline slippage. Commercial commissioning of all backward integration facilities is now targeted by Q1FY28, after considering a delay of up to one quarter.

Working capital build. Trade receivables rose sharply from ₹443.15 Cr in FY25 to ₹853.32 Cr in FY26 (standalone), and inventory increased from ₹399.43 Cr to ₹560.88 Cr. Short-term borrowings including current maturities rose from ₹182.24 Cr to ₹370.97 Cr over the same period.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order book momentum. New order inflow of ₹2,114 Cr during Q1FY27, including an Ultra Mega Order from PGCIL exceeding ₹1,000 Cr (within 30 months), an order from GETCO of about ₹228 Cr (by August 2028), an order from RRVPNL of about ₹175 Cr (by March 2028), and an export order from PDC AK LPIV, LLC USA of about ₹150 Cr (by mid-2027). Un-executed order book stands at ₹6,630 Cr, up 26% from ₹5,246 Cr in Q1FY26, executable over 18–24 months. Inquiries under negotiation total ₹23,000 Cr, with a historical win ratio of 10%–15%.

Nuclear sector entry. TARIL secured its first order in the nuclear power sector for NPCIL’s Kaiga Units 5 & 6 project (two indigenous 700 MWe Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors adding 1,400 MW in Karnataka), received from Megha Engineering and Infrastructures Limited (MEIL). A separate large order from MEIL (₹100–500 Cr range) for transformers and related work is scheduled for delivery within 35 months.

International expansion. Maxwell Grid Transformers (UK) Private Limited was incorporated in the United Kingdom as a subsidiary, with planned investment equivalent to GBP 10 million for a 51% shareholding, to manufacture and repair transformers, sell and distribute TARIL transformers, and trade transformer components.

Industry demand backdrop. The global transformer market is projected to grow from $68 billion in 2025 to $137 billion by 2036 at 6.6% CAGR. India’s transformer market is projected at ~$5–5.6 billion by 2030–34 from ~$2.5–2.6 billion, growing at ~8.3% CAGR, driven by grid modernisation, renewable integration, data centres, railway electrification, EV charging, and hydrogen. TARIL cites a ₹7.9 lakh crore transmission opportunity and 500 GW renewable target by 2030.

Capacity and integration. Expanded transformer manufacturing capacity supports revenue potential of ₹5,000–6,000 Cr. Backward integration facilities for radiators, tanks, bushings, pressboard, CTC conductors, and CRGO are at various stages of commissioning, with CRGO core cutting and slitting already operational.

Investor engagement. The company will meet a group of investors in Ahmedabad for an in-person management interaction as part of JP Morgan’s India Conference Industrials Tour. An earnings conference call for Q1FY27 is scheduled for 21 July 2026.

Key Risks

  • Raw material price volatility and geopolitical uncertainty affecting input availability.
  • Execution risk on the large order book, particularly given the Changodar expansion overhang.
  • Working capital intensity, with elevated receivables and inventory and rising short-term borrowings.
  • Dependence on timely commissioning of backward integration facilities, already subject to a one-quarter delay.
  • Concentration of order book in power transformers (80% of orders).
  • Export and international expansion execution risk, including the new UK subsidiary.

Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 outlook. Management targets 25% revenue growth with 16% EBITDA margin and 9%–10% PAT margin. In Q1FY27, standalone revenue grew 10% YoY and consolidated revenue grew 8% YoY, with standalone EBITDA margin at 15.62% and consolidated at 19.16%. QoQ revenue declined 26%–27%, reflecting the Changodar disruption.

Capacity. Management states existing and expanded capacities are adequately positioned to support growth objectives, with no additional capacity constraints and execution momentum on track. The expanded capacity is stated to support revenue potential of ₹5,000–6,000 Cr.

Capex. Expansion at Changodar (₹150 Cr) and backward integration (₹900–1,000 Cr) are progressing in line with planned timelines. Commercial commissioning of all backward integration facilities is targeted by Q1FY28, after a delay of up to one quarter.

Funding. Capex is to be funded through a balanced mix of QIP proceeds, leasing arrangements, internal accruals, and debt if required. Approximately ₹145 Cr of unutilised proceeds from the 2024 QIP remains earmarked for backward integration. The Board approved variation or re-allocation of unutilised QIP proceeds and the UK subsidiary investment.

Raw material strategy. Management states TARIL is well positioned and well guarded against price volatility by securing inputs at the time of order receipt, mitigating exposure to geopolitical uncertainties.

Dividend. Management recommended a dividend of 25% (₹0.25 per share) on equity shares for FY26. The 32nd AGM is scheduled for 21 September 2026, with remote e-voting from 18–20 September 2026 and a cut-off date of 14 September 2026.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a cautious Hold to a Reduce: the January report highlighted strong Q3FY26 financials and order visibility but worried about order inflow sustainability, costs, and execution; by July, those execution and cost worries had crystallized into a Q1FY27 miss and a FY27 growth guidance cut. The biggest reversal was on capacity expansion, once seen as a growth driver but now a near-term disruption, while order inflows and the backlog improved enough to push the order-inflow concern into the background.

Fears that came true

  • Cost pressures and commodity volatility flagged in January materialized as Q1FY27 EBITDA/PAT margins contracted to 16.3%/11.2% from 16.7%/12.7%, and production cost inflation remained a listed risk; the actual -5.5% return on the April 2026 Hold call versus -4.6% predicted reflected this disappointment.
  • The execution-timeline risk flagged in January materialized at Changodar, where expansion-related lower utilization hurt revenue conversion and forced FY27 revenue growth guidance down to ~25% from ~30%.

Optimism that failed

  • The aggressive capacity expansion story failed to deliver near-term growth—it disrupted Q1FY27 operations through lower Changodar utilization and revenue conversion.
  • The expected ~200 bps margin benefit from backward integration beginning FY27 did not materialize; margins fell in Q1FY27 and the benefit was pushed to FY28.
  • The momentum from robust Q3FY26 financials did not carry into Q1FY27, with PAT declining 4.7% YoY.
