RSWM — Recent Corporate Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Volatile input costs and geopolitical disruption. The Q1 FY27 quarter (ended June 30, 2026) was described as highly volatile. The West Asia conflict, elevated crude oil prices and supply chain disruptions pushed polyester fibre prices up by as much as 30% in March, which weighed on volumes through April, May and June. Yarn prices were pushed up accordingly, and fabric downstream customers could not easily absorb the increases. Crude price volatility continued into the following quarter.

Soft export demand. Export revenue in Q1 FY27 was ₹336 crore, lower than the immediately preceding quarter, with international customers following a calibrated procurement approach. Demand from certain export markets, including parts of the Middle East, remained subdued. Longer transit times and high freight added to uncertainty.

Fabric segment pressure. The fabric business faced cost-led pressure from higher fibre, gas, freight and chemical prices alongside subdued demand, particularly in knitted fabric, where most customers are export-oriented garmenters. Segment profitability at the EBIT level was described as barely break-even, with knit more affected than denim (denim is more evenly balanced between export and domestic).

Discretionary spending and trade uncertainty. Consumer discretionary spending in several developed economies remained subdued, producing a gradual rather than broad-based recovery in textile and apparel demand. Evolving global trade dynamics, including recent US tariff developments, were cited as factors requiring prudent planning and operational agility.

Earnings volatility factors. The company’s own disclosure of material risks cites fluctuations in earnings, growth management, competition, international operations and regulatory changes.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Improving demand environment. Inventory correction in international markets has largely moderated and demand shows signs of stabilisation. Domestic demand was healthy across several customer segments, with domestic sales rising to ₹825 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹774 crore in Q4 FY26. Management noted positive demand and a better outlook for fabric in the months following the quarter.

Trade agreements and sourcing shifts. The concluded India–UK free trade agreement and ongoing India–EU discussions are expected to create meaningful opportunities for Indian textile exporters, particularly in value-added and forward-integrated products. RSWM is actively exploring forward integration to capture FTA-related potential. Diversification of global sourcing away from concentrated hubs and brand focus on reliable, sustainable suppliers reinforce India’s position as a preferred sourcing destination.

Cotton value chain. Improved cotton availability and softer cotton prices, supported by a favourable policy environment and better crop outlook, are expected to benefit the cotton value chain. The cotton and mélange yarn business had a strong Q1 FY27 on the spread between yarn and cotton prices, and continued reasonably well into Q2.

Graphene and new product development. The graphene initiative with Birla Cellulose for viscose application is progressing, with a graphene plant planned within the LNJ Bhilwara Group to supply fibre for both polyester and viscose applications. A first round of product from Grasim/Birla Cellulose has arrived, with further improvements targeted. Management expects the initiative to get rolling within the financial year, though the market opportunity is yet to be fully evaluated.

LNJ GreenPET bottle-to-bottle recycling. The food-grade recycled granule project is under construction, with civil work underway and orders placed for critical machines. Completion is expected in Q4 of the current financial year for trials, with commercial production likely in Q1 of the next financial year. The first phase is planned at 50,000 MT per year, with expected revenue of ₹500 crore and industry-typical EBITDA of around 15%. Ramp-up is planned at approximately 75% in year one, 85% in year two and up to 90–91% by year three, reflecting certification and trial requirements for food-grade product. No customer tie-ups have been entered into yet.

Garmenting joint venture. RSWM and Spain-based Noize Design Studio (NDS9) signed a Joint Venture Agreement to establish LNJ NDS9 Global Private Limited, with equity held 74:26 respectively. The venture will set up a green garment manufacturing facility built on Industry 5.0 principles — automation, digital manufacturing, AI, sustainability and human-centric operations — focused on premium denim and activewear for international brands. Phase 1 capacity is 500,000 denim garments per month with an initial investment of approximately ₹186 crore; Phase 2 adds 1,000,000 denim/activewear garments per month, taking total capacity to 1.5 million garments per month. RSWM will lead manufacturing, plant operations, procurement, quality systems and supply chain; NDS9 will lead design, trend forecasting, sales and marketing, product development, merchandising and international business development. The facility is to be funded through equity contribution and term loans in a proposed 30:70 ratio. Rooftop solar is built in from the start. Knit garmenting may be considered in later phases.

Knitting capacity expansion. A ₹92 crore investment is expanding knitting capacity from 650 tonnes to approximately 900 tonnes per month, including 150 tonnes of printing capacity that did not previously exist. Benefits are expected to reflect from Q3 FY27 onwards through an enriched product mix and a more complete customer offering.

Renewable energy and cost reduction. Renewable sources rose from the mid-20s percentage share last year to the mid-40s, reaching around 60% of power consumption in the current quarter. Power and fuel cost fell to ₹112 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹123 crore in the preceding quarter, a roughly 10% decrease. Management targets savings of about ₹40 crore for the year from lower power cost at similar utilisation, with per-unit cost expected to vary month to month due to solar and wind seasonality.

Sustainability positioning. RSWM cites recycling over 20.5 lakh KL of water annually, sourcing nearly 70% of energy from renewables, an 18.1% reduction in Scope 2 CO₂ emissions, and recycling approximately 6.5 million PET bottles per day.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued crude oil and polyester fibre price volatility affecting input costs and customer buying behaviour.
  • Geopolitical uncertainty, including the West Asia conflict, causing supply chain disruption, longer transit times and high freight.
  • Export demand softness and calibrated international procurement, with particular weakness in Middle East markets.
  • Downstream inability to absorb yarn price increases, pressuring fabric segment margins, especially export-dependent knit customers.
  • Evolving global trade policy, including US tariff developments, that could shift sourcing patterns.
  • Execution risk in new ventures: the garmenting JV is a new business for RSWM requiring new teams, skilling and quality standards; the GreenPET project requires food-grade trials and certifications before approval.
  • Competition, growth management, international operations and regulatory changes, as cited in the company’s own risk disclosure.
  • Dependence of the graphene initiative’s commercial timeline on product development progress, which management acknowledged is not yet precisely determinable.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Profitability trajectory. Management stated that Q1 FY27 performance was positive and that it expects all quarters to be similar or better. Observed: revenue of ₹1,161 crore (up 1.7% sequentially from ₹1,142 crore), gross profit of ₹466 crore with gross margin expanding to 39.8% from 37.4%, EBITDA of ₹94 crore (up 10.1% QoQ and 16.1% YoY) with margin at 8.0% versus 7.4% in Q4 FY26 and 6.9% in Q1 FY26, PBT of ₹24 crore (up 33.5% QoQ and 153.7% YoY) and PAT of ₹17 crore versus ₹7 crore in Q1 FY26. PAT was lower than the immediately preceding quarter, which had included a one-time tax benefit from adopting the concessional corporate tax regime under the Income Tax Act 2025; excluding that, the underlying earnings trajectory was described as healthy.

Capacity utilisation. Management reported mid-90s utilisation overall: approximately 92–93% in mélange, 96% in synthetic yarn, 98% in cotton yarn, 90% in denim and mid-80s in knit.

Power cost savings. Management guided to more than ₹1 per unit average reduction year-on-year and approximately ₹40 crore of savings for the year at similar utilisation. Observed so far: a roughly 10% reduction in power and fuel cost between Q4 FY26 (₹123 crore) and Q1 FY27 (₹112 crore), with further decreases expected.

GreenPET timeline and economics. Management guided to project completion in Q4 of the current financial year for trials, commercial production in Q1 of the next financial year, ₹500 crore revenue at 50,000 MT capacity, around 15% EBITDA typical of the industry, and a three-year ramp to 90–91% utilisation. These remain forward-looking; no production has commenced.

Graphene commercialisation. Management expects the initiative to get rolling within the financial year but declined to give a firm timeline, noting the product is not yet aggressively used commercially and that market opportunity assessment will follow mastery of the product.

Garmenting JV. At the time of the earnings call (August 5, 2026), management described the JV as at principal board approval stage with stake split still under discussion and RSWM expected to be majority shareholder, and Phase 1 envisioned at up to 5 lakh pieces per month. The subsequent press release of August 11, 2026 confirmed a 74:26 equity split, Phase 1 capacity of 500,000 denim garments per month, Phase 2 adding 1,000,000 garments per month, and an initial investment of approximately ₹186 crore.

Knitting expansion. Management guided that benefits from the ₹92 crore knitting investment would reflect from Q3 FY27 onwards, with capacity rising from 650 to about 900 tonnes per month including 150 tonnes of printing. This remains a forward expectation.

Strategic priorities. Management reiterated the RSWM 2.0 priorities: strengthening the product portfolio, operational excellence, expanding value-added segments, disciplined cost management and disciplined capital allocation toward productivity, modernisation and measurable returns.
