SPARC: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Regulatory delay on PDP-716. The regulatory approval process for PDP-716 has been delayed due to compliance issues at the finished product manufacturing site. SPARC states it is working with commercialization partners on remediation activities while simultaneously qualifying alternative manufacturing sites.

Industry-wide pricing and trade uncertainty. The Chairman’s AGM speech notes that proposed tariff measures on pharmaceutical products and APIs entering the United States prompted companies to reassess supply-chain strategies and accelerate investments to diversify manufacturing networks. Developments relating to the Most Favoured Nation (MFN) framework and broader drug pricing reforms increased uncertainty, particularly in developed markets, compelling companies to focus more rigorously on value demonstration.

Termination of the CMS licensing agreement. During the quarter ended June 30, 2026, SPARC and CMS Bridging DMCC mutually agreed to terminate the licensing agreement dated November 5, 2019. SPARC refunded USD 2 million (₹1,889 lakhs) to CMS and recognised revenue of ₹2,921 lakhs relating to the non-refundable consideration.

Return to losses after the PRV-driven quarter. After the March 31, 2026 quarter, which included ₹184,002 lakhs of other operating revenue from the PRV, the quarter ended June 30, 2026 shows a loss before tax of ₹2,098 lakhs (standalone) and ₹2,067 lakhs (consolidated), compared with a profit before tax of ₹1,76,070 lakhs (standalone) in the prior quarter.

Prior-year labour code impact. The New Labour Codes, effective November 21, 2025, introduced a uniform definition of wages with implications for gratuity, leave encashment and related obligations. SPARC recognised an incremental cost of ₹1,236 lakhs during the previous year as an exceptional item.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

PRV monetization and debt-free position. Following favourable legal proceedings, SPARC secured a Paediatric Rare Disease Voucher (PRV) from the USFDA, granted February 03, 2026 for Sezaby®. The PRV was sold on April 30, 2026 for USD 195 million. The Chairman states the monetization generated significant non-dilutive capital, enabled SPARC to be debt free, and provided significant cash to support planned milestones of existing pipeline assets.

Pipeline progress. - SPARC-121/SCD-153: A Phase 1b multiple-ascending-dose study in alopecia areata patients is underway, with interim data expected during FY2026-27. Development is being expanded into vitiligo. - SPARC-122/SBO-154: Currently enrolling patients across the U.S., Australia and India under a Phase 1a dose-escalation study. Following successful completion of dose escalation, SPARC intends to initiate targeted expansion cohorts in breast cancer, lung cancer and ovarian cancer. - Sezaby: Achieved the PRV milestone described above.

Strategic focus on oncology and immunology. SPARC’s portfolio is organized around three pillars: targeted multimodal cancer therapeutics (antibody-drug conjugates, bispecifics, T-cell engagers, immune-stimulating antibody conjugates, small-molecule drug conjugates); DNA Damage Response pathway inhibitors; and autoimmune and dermatological disorders focused on novel non-JAK pathways, rational combinations and topical approaches.

Cost optimization. During the year, SPARC rationalized its U.S. presence, streamlined operating activities and reduced manpower-related costs. Laboratory infrastructure was consolidated from four sites to two, which the Chairman states improved operational efficiency, resource utilization and collaboration across research teams.

Supportive Indian policy environment. The Government of India’s Promotion of Research and Innovation in Pharma-MedTech (PRIP) Scheme, with an approved outlay of ₹5,000 crore, aims to position India as a global hub for pharmaceutical and medical technology innovation, supporting early- and late-stage R&D in novel drugs, biosimilars, complex generics, precision medicine and next-generation medical devices.

Sector demand. The Chairman notes the global prescription medicines market grew approximately 10% year-on-year to nearly US$1.7 trillion, and the U.S. FDA approved 46 novel drugs during 2025, with oncology accounting for over one-third of new approvals.

Capital infusion via warrants. On May 19, 2026, SPARC allotted 3,85,10,000 warrants convertible into equity shares on a preferential basis to Shanghvi Finance Private Limited at an issue price of ₹155.80 each, having received 25% of the issue price (₹38.95 per warrant) as subscription money. The balance 75% (₹116.85 per warrant) is payable within 18 months from allotment at exercise.

3. Key Risks

  • Manufacturing compliance risk: The PDP-716 delay stems from compliance issues at the finished product manufacturing site, with remediation and alternative site qualification ongoing.
  • Regulatory and pricing risk: Proposed U.S. tariffs on pharmaceutical products and APIs, the MFN framework and broader drug pricing reforms have increased uncertainty in developed markets.
  • Pipeline execution risk: SPARC-121 interim data and SPARC-122 expansion cohorts are subject to completion of ongoing studies; the Chairman notes the need to balance scientific ambition with disciplined capital allocation, operational agility and risk management.
  • Dependence on non-recurring income: The large profit in the March 2026 quarter and the debt-free position derive from the one-time PRV sale; the June 2026 quarter returned to a loss.
  • Partner-related risk: The CMS Bridging DMCC licensing agreement was terminated during the June 2026 quarter, with a USD 2 million refund to CMS.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

SPARC-121 interim data. Management states it expects interim data during FY2026-27 from the Phase 1b multiple-ascending-dose study in alopecia areata. No outcome data has yet been reported in the supplied material.

SPARC-122 expansion cohorts. Management states an intention to initiate targeted expansion cohorts in breast cancer, lung cancer and ovarian cancer following successful completion of the Phase 1a dose-escalation phase. The program is currently enrolling patients across the U.S., Australia and India; completion of dose escalation has not been reported.

PDP-716 approval. Management previously anticipated a regulatory approval process; the observed position is that approval has been delayed due to manufacturing-site compliance issues, with remediation and alternative-site qualification underway.

PRV monetization. The PRV was granted by the USFDA on February 03, 2026, recognised as income of ₹184,002 lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026, and sold on April 30, 2026 for USD 195 million. The Chairman states this enabled SPARC to be debt free and provided significant cash for pipeline milestones.

Cost structure. Management describes rationalization of the U.S. presence, streamlining of operations, reduction of manpower-related costs, and consolidation of laboratory infrastructure from four sites to two. Observed standalone employee benefits expense was ₹2,416 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter versus ₹2,768 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter; consolidated employee benefits expense was ₹2,961 lakhs versus ₹3,132 lakhs over the same comparison.

Financial performance. Standalone revenue from contracts with customers was ₹3,992 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter, compared with ₹964 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter and ₹1,320 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter. Standalone loss before tax was ₹2,098 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter, compared with a loss of ₹5,203 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter and a profit before tax of ₹1,76,070 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter. For the year ended March 31, 2026, standalone profit before tax was ₹1,55,213 lakhs and basic/diluted EPS was ₹47.83 (annualised); consolidated profit before tax was ₹1,55,301 lakhs with EPS of ₹47.86 (annualised).

Governance and board changes. The 21st AGM was held on August 10, 2026, and all resolutions set out in the Notice dated July 16, 2026 were passed with requisite majority, including the appointment of Mr. Anil Kumar Raghavan as Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer (Special Resolution) and the retirement of Dr. Rajamannar Thennati, who did not seek re-election. On the same date, the Board approved requests for reclassification of certain promoter group individuals from the “Promoter Group” to the “Public” category under Regulation 31A, subject to necessary approvals. On August 13, 2026, SPARC submitted an application to the stock exchanges seeking no objection/approval for the reclassification, further to intimations dated July 27, 2026 and August 10, 2026.

Broker Narrative

The first report acknowledged US generics price/competition and elevated costs but leaned on specialty launches (g-Revlimid), margin expansion, and branded/specialty growth. By the last report, those US generics risks had materialized—g-Revlimid contribution fell, competition/price erosion hit base products, and costs compressed margins—so the narrative shifted to the global specialty/innovative portfolio, domestic formulations growth, semaglutide launches, and the Organon acquisition as the main drivers. Cost pressure and US generics competition persisted as recurring themes, while specialty/innovative traction and domestic formulations strength remained consistent tailwinds.

Fears that came true

  • Price erosion and increased competition in the US generics base portfolio, flagged with the Taro/dermatology concerns, materialized as lower g-Revlimid contribution and competition across select base products, contributing to DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes such as 2026-08-01 (-6.4% vs +16.0%).
  • Elevated cost pressures/sales and promotional spending concerns materialized in the last report as employee costs and other expenses rising as % of sales, causing a 155bp YoY EBITDA margin contraction and the 2026-08-01 DISAPPOINTMENT.

Optimism that failed

  • The g-Revlimid launch—a key early growth driver—failed to sustain, as the last report attributed the US generics decline to lower g-Revlimid contribution.
  • The expectation of continued EBITDA margin expansion failed, as the last report recorded a 155bp YoY margin contraction despite earlier margin gains.
