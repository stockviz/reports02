JARO: Q1 FY27 Corporate Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Customer acquisition cost pressure in the referral channel. Referral CAC per enrolment rose sharply from ₹4,394 in FY23 to ₹11,622 in FY26, and stood at ₹11,839 in Q1 FY27. Performance marketing CAC per enrolment also increased from ₹29,393 in FY23 to ₹46,562 in FY26, though it moderated to ₹44,783 in Q1 FY27.
  • Declining return on performance marketing spend. ROPM fell from 2.29x in FY23 to 1.33x in FY26, and was 1.51x in Q1 FY27, indicating rising cost of acquiring revenue through the performance marketing channel.
  • Shrinking partner institution count. The number of partner universities declined from 36 in FY25 to 33 in FY26, even as admissions grew from 31,434 to 32,236 over the same period.
  • Lower revenue from operations on a sequential basis. Revenue from operations was ₹7,075.05 lakhs in Q1 FY27, compared with ₹7,278.64 lakhs in Q4 FY26, although it grew year-on-year from ₹6,067.46 lakhs in Q1 FY26.
  • Higher employee and other expenses. Employee benefits expense rose to ₹2,013.45 lakhs in Q1 FY27 from ₹1,882.96 lakhs in Q1 FY26, and other expenses rose to ₹3,545.34 lakhs from ₹2,788.87 lakhs.
  • Reduced ROCE and ROE. The company attributes the decline in ROCE (to 19%) and ROE (to 15%) to an increase in share capital following the IPO in September 2025 (FY25: ROCE 33%, ROE 35%).
  • Lower gross bookings sequentially. Gross bookings were ₹19,029.76 lakhs in Q1 FY27, compared with ₹20,809 lakhs in Q4 FY26, though higher than ₹16,287 lakhs in Q1 FY26.
  • Lower net bookings sequentially. Net bookings were ₹7,075 lakhs in Q1 FY27 versus ₹8,041 lakhs in Q4 FY26, but above ₹6,067 lakhs in Q1 FY26.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong year-on-year growth across key metrics. Total income grew 19% YoY to ₹7,262.99 lakhs; EBITDA grew 21% YoY to ₹1,704.20 lakhs; PAT grew 48% YoY to ₹1,116.89 lakhs; gross bookings grew 17% YoY; and admissions grew 12% YoY to 8,169.
  • Segment traction. Degree admissions stood at 6,979 (85% of admissions) and certification admissions at 1,190 (15%), with certification revenue growing 17% YoY and degree revenue growing 11% YoY.
  • Improving monetisation. ARPU increased 4% QoQ to ₹86,609 in Q1 FY27, up from ₹85,995 in Q4 FY26 and ₹84,960 in Q1 FY26.
  • New programme launches with premier IITs. Six new programmes were launched in Q1 FY27: Sustainability Leadership & Practice and Application of AI for Business Managers (IIT Bombay); Autonomous Robotics & AI (IIT Delhi); Chief Technology & AI Officers (IIT Roorkee); and Blockchain Technology and Rust Programming & AI-Integrated Systems (IIT Madras).
  • Large addressable market. The online higher education and upskilling TAM in India is projected to grow from ₹13,200 crores in FY23 to ₹41,450 crores in FY28P, a CAGR of 26%, per the Technopak Report cited in the presentation.
  • Policy tailwinds. NEP 2020 targets increasing the online higher education GER from 29% to 50% by 2035, and the company positions itself as aligned with NEP 2020 and Digital India.
  • Revenue visibility. Multi-month and multi-year programmes, long-term institutional partnerships (3–7+ years), and a mix of degree and certification programmes support revenue visibility across the programme lifecycle.
  • Scale and reach. The company partners with 33 institutions (including IIMs, IITs, and NIRF-ranked universities), offers 285 programmes, has served over 350,000 learners, and operates across 26 cities with 42 offices and studios in FY26.
  • Dividend declaration. Shareholders approved a final dividend of ₹3 per equity share of face value ₹10 for FY2025-26 at the 17th AGM held on July 28, 2026.
  • IPO proceeds deployment. Of the ₹17,000 lakhs fresh issue proceeds, ₹13,995.95 lakhs was utilised up to June 30, 2026, leaving ₹3,004.05 lakhs unutilised, including ₹2,869.81 lakhs earmarked for marketing, brand building, and advertising.

3. Key Risks

  • Dependence on institutional partnerships. The business model relies on fee-sharing arrangements with partner institutions; the decline in partner universities from 36 to 33 between FY25 and FY26 illustrates exposure to partnership changes.
  • Rising acquisition costs. The upward trend in both performance marketing and referral CAC, alongside declining ROPM, poses a risk to unit economics if not offset by monetisation gains.
  • Concentration in a single segment. The company operates as a single reportable segment (education program services) with operations domiciled in India, with no geographical diversification disclosed.
  • Regulatory and policy dependence. Growth prospects are tied to NEP 2020 targets and the regulatory environment for online higher education.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. The presentation notes that actual results could differ materially from forward-looking statements due to factors including economic performance, competition, strategy implementation, technological change, and market risks.
  • ESOP trust structure. The company consolidates the Jaro Education Welfare Trust, with treasury shares recognised at cost and deducted from equity and reserves, following the trust’s borrowing from the company to purchase shares in the open market.
  • IPO expense revision. Estimated IPO expenses were revised from ₹3,665.39 lakhs to ₹3,313.39 lakhs, with the company’s share revised from ₹1,384.70 lakhs to ₹1,251.73 lakhs, approved by the Board on July 4, 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Management commentary (August 10, 2026). Dr. Sanjay Salunkhe stated the company began FY27 on a strong note with healthy growth across financial and operating parameters, citing sustained learner demand, improving monetisation, and continued expansion of the institutional ecosystem. He noted focus on expanding institutional partnerships, introducing future-ready programmes, increasing learner reach, and improving monetisation, while maintaining operating discipline.
  • Observed performance against commentary. Total income grew 19% YoY, EBITDA 21% YoY, and PAT 48% YoY in Q1 FY27, consistent with the stated growth narrative. Gross bookings of ₹19,029.76 lakhs and 8,169 admissions were reported for the quarter. ARPU rose 4% QoQ to ₹86,609, supporting the improving monetisation claim.
  • Programme expansion delivered. The six new IIT programmes launched during the quarter match the stated focus on future-ready programmes in AI, advanced technology, and sustainability.
  • Institutional ecosystem. While the commentary cites continued expansion of the institutional ecosystem, the disclosed partner university count declined from 36 in FY25 to 33 in FY26, with no Q1 FY27 partner count disclosed.
  • Operating discipline. EBITDA margin was flat at 23% in Q1 FY27 versus Q1 FY26, while PAT margin improved to 15% from 12%, reflecting the benefit of lower finance costs (₹15.98 lakhs versus ₹149.24 lakhs) and higher other income (₹187.94 lakhs versus ₹14.13 lakhs).
  • Auditor review. The statutory auditor, M S K A & Associates LLP, issued a limited review report dated August 8, 2026, stating nothing came to their attention indicating the unaudited results contain any material misstatement.
  • Shareholder ratification. All six resolutions at the 17th AGM (July 28, 2026), including adoption of FY26 audited financials, the ₹3 dividend, director appointments, and increased remuneration for the Whole-time Director and CEO for FY2026-27, were approved with over 99.99% of votes polled in favour.
