ZENITHSTL: Recent Corporate Announcements — Key Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Production disruption from zinc tank rupture. On 11/04/2026, a zinc tank ruptured at the plant during processing of zinc material, causing unintended loss and accumulation of zinc material on the ground. Production operations were temporarily suspended from 15/04/2026 to allow recovery and handling of the material. A portion of the recovered material was sold as Zinc Dross at scrap value amounting to Rs. 91.06 lakhs. The stoppage reduced production and affected availability of finished goods for dispatch and sale, adversely impacting quarterly sales. The company is working to restore normal operations and expects to be fully operational by 31/08/2026.

Suspected fraud involving a consultant. During Q3 of FY 2025-26, the company identified a suspected offence involving unauthorized access to bank payment processes by a consultant. The consultant’s access was discontinued, an internal investigation was initiated, and an FIR was lodged on 01/12/2025. The amount involved is estimated at approximately Rs. 758 lakhs, pertaining to various prior periods. Land and residential properties aggregating Rs. 131.07 lakhs were recovered as compensation and accounted as an exceptional item with a corresponding debit under Other Receivables. The investigation and internal review remain ongoing, and the final financial impact is yet to be determined.

Negative net worth and going-concern uncertainty. As at 30/06/2026, the net worth of the company and the group continues to be negative due to accumulated losses from prior periods. The auditors noted that accumulated losses exceed share capital and reserves, that net worth has been fully eroded, and that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the ability to continue as a going concern; the auditors stated that use of the going concern basis is not adequately supported.

Non-compliance with deposit-related provisions. The company has not fully complied with Section 74 and other applicable provisions of the Companies Act, 2013 read with the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 2014, in respect of non-repayment of public deposits and interest on due dates, non-maintenance of prescribed liquid assets, and non-compliance with Company Law Board orders. The auditors noted this constitutes material non-compliance and that reported liabilities, interest obligations, and disclosures would have been different had the company complied.

Balance confirmation and reconciliation gaps. Balances of trade payables, trade receivables, loans, advances, deposits, intergroup balances, current liabilities, and borrowings are subject to reconciliation and confirmation. Management has not issued direct balance confirmation requests to all parties, citing pending reconciliations. The auditors stated they were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence to verify accuracy, completeness, and recoverability/payability of these balances.

Frozen bank accounts. Certain current bank accounts were frozen and non-operating. Bank statements and balance confirmations could not be obtained despite follow-ups, and a provision of Rs. 40.99 lakhs was made as a prudent measure. The auditors could not verify completeness and accuracy of these balances.

Inventory valuation concerns. Inventory of Rs. 669.96 lakhs as at 30/06/2026 was valued using the weighted average cost method, but valuation of finished goods, work-in-progress, and scrap was carried out manually based on cost sheets and management estimates rather than system-generated reports. The auditors stated they were not provided adequate information or supporting documentation to verify the basis of valuation, including quantities, condition, and cost allocation.

Legal and enforcement proceedings. A consortium of banks initiated SARFAESI proceedings in February 2014 for dues of Rs. 19,319.00 lakhs as on 31/01/2014, took symbolic possession of Khopoli immovable assets on 29/05/2014, and sought physical possession. The loan was assigned to Invent Assets Securitization and Reconstruction Private Limited on 31/03/2018, and during the quarter ended 30/06/2026 the debt was further assigned to India SME Asset Reconstruction Company Limited. The matter is before the Debt Recovery Tribunal, Pune; the company sought an adjournment at the 30/06/2026 hearing, and the next hearing is scheduled for 19/10/2026.

Debt settlement dispute. The company received a notice from India SME Asset Reconstruction Company Limited regarding assignment of debt, stating total dues including interest as on 25/03/2026 at Rs. 1,189.41 crores excluding further accruals and charges. The company replied by email dated 17/04/2026 asserting that Rs. 9,295.00 lakhs, being the principal outstanding, is payable towards full and final settlement of the assigned debt under an agreement dated 21/01/2019 with amendments.

SEBI matter. The company was prohibited from accessing the securities market for three years pursuant to a SEBI order dated 31/03/2021 relating to alleged violations of the SEBI Act, 1992 and GDR regulations. On appeal, SAT’s order dated 21/02/2023 partially allowed the appeal, restricting debarment to the period already undergone and reducing the monetary penalty from Rs. 1,000.00 lakhs to Rs. 25.00 lakhs, which was recognized as a provision for the year ended 31/03/2023 but remains unpaid as of 30/06/2026. SEBI filed a civil appeal before the Supreme Court, admitted on 02/01/2024; no further directions or communications have been received.

GST demands. A demand notice of Rs. 1,836.95 lakhs in Form DRC-08 was received for FY 2018-19; a writ petition was filed before the Madras High Court on 27/08/2024 and an interim stay was granted on 04/10/2024. Following a remand order, a demand of Rs. 1,185.53 lakhs was raised, against which a rectification application was filed on 22/05/2026; the rectification order dated 10/07/2026 substantially rectified the demand, leaving only a penalty of Rs. 0.79 lakhs, which was paid on 07/08/2026. Other GST demands include Rs. 28.76 lakhs (FY 2020-21), and Rs. 27.78 lakhs and Rs. 25.28 lakhs (FY 2021-22 and 2019-20), all under appeal.

MSME compliance gap. The company has not obtained MSME status confirmations from all vendors and, in the absence of adequate vendor classification, has not made provision for interest liability (if any) under the MSMED Act, 2006, and related disclosures are not considered complete.

Disputed receivable. A legal notice was received on 29/06/2023 from Ess Jay Global Ventures Private Limited, with a response sent on 30/06/2023. The company determined a net amount receivable of Rs. 150 lakhs from the group, which is under reconciliation/legal dispute. Further communication was received on 12/08/2023 with responses provided on 27/09/2023, and a formal legal notice was sent to Mango Capital LLC on 04/10/2023.

Public offer proceeds and doubtful advances. Of the Rs. 13,500.00 lakhs in public offer proceeds, Rs. 13,289 lakhs has been spent, with the balance held in current/fixed deposit/loan/advances accounts. A provision for doubtful advances of Rs. 10,925 lakhs exists, and pending recovery, the amount available for deployment will be lower to that extent.

Discontinuing operations. Loss from discontinuing operations was Rs. 1.94 lakhs for the quarter ended 30/06/2026, Rs. 2.86 lakhs for the quarter ended 31/03/2026, Rs. 2.29 lakhs for the quarter ended 30/06/2025, and Rs. 6.99 lakhs for the year ended 31/03/2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Revival measures. Management has initiated strategic measures including engagement with key suppliers and customers, and steps to improve operational efficiency and enhance revenue generation. The Board is actively evaluating various business options to augment income from operations and ensure long-term financial sustainability.

MoU with Tribus Real Estate Private Limited (TREPL). The company entered into an MoU with TREPL for takeover and settlement of existing bank borrowings aggregating to Rs. 15,894.92 lakhs as on 31/12/2025. TREPL is authorized to negotiate and settle dues with lenders/ARCs through One-Time Settlement or other mutually acceptable terms. TREPL repaid Rs. 6,599.92 lakhs to lenders/ARCs by 24/03/2026, with Rs. 9,295.00 lakhs remaining unpaid as on that date.

Khopoli land and building development. The proposed development arrangement relating to the Khopoli land and building is under discussion with the developer and is expected to be concluded in the near future, upon which sufficient funds are expected to be realized for settlement of the obligations.

Recovered properties to be sold. The company intends to sell the recovered land and residential properties in the open market in the near future and adjust the realizable value against the amount receivable.

Production restoration. The company is in the process of resuming production operations and has initiated steps to restore normal production activities, expecting to be fully operational by 31/08/2026.

GST rectification outcome. The rectification order dated 10/07/2026 was passed in favour of the company, substantially rectifying the demand and leaving only a penalty of Rs. 0.79 lakhs, which was paid on 07/08/2026.

New Labour Codes assessment. The company provided an additional impact of Rs. 5.40 lakhs under Employee Benefit Expenses for the quarter ended 30/06/2026 arising primarily from the change in the definition of wages under the New Labour Codes, and will continue to monitor further developments.

  • Material uncertainty regarding going-concern status, with net worth fully eroded and accumulated losses exceeding share capital and reserves.
  • Material non-compliance with Section 74 and related provisions of the Companies Act, 2013 and the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 2014, which may result in regulatory implications.
  • Inability of auditors to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence on balances of trade payables, trade receivables, loans, advances, deposits, intergroup balances, current liabilities, and borrowings.
  • Inability of auditors to verify frozen bank account balances and inventory valuation, with consequent impact on profit/loss and financial position not ascertainable.
  • Ongoing SARFAESI enforcement and DRT proceedings, with the next hearing scheduled for 19/10/2026.
  • Dispute over debt settlement, with the assignee stating dues of Rs. 1,189.41 crores as on 25/03/2026 against the company’s position that Rs. 9,295.00 lakhs is payable towards full and final settlement.
  • SEBI civil appeal pending before the Supreme Court, admitted on 02/01/2024.
  • Ongoing investigation into the suspected fraud of approximately Rs. 758 lakhs, with final financial impact yet to be determined.
  • GST demands under appeal and past-period demands.
  • MSME interest liability and disclosure gaps due to lack of vendor confirmations.
  • Disputed receivable of Rs. 150 lakhs from Ess Jay Global Ventures Private Limited and Mango Capital LLC.
  • Provision for doubtful advances of Rs. 10,925 lakhs, reducing amounts available for deployment.
  • Losses from discontinuing operations in each reported period.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Going concern. Management believes the company will be able to realize its assets and discharge its liabilities in the normal course of business based on ongoing efforts, projected future cash flows, and revenue streams, and has prepared the financial results on a going concern basis. The auditors stated that the material uncertainty exists and that use of the going concern basis is not adequately supported.

Khopoli development. Management expects the development arrangement relating to the Khopoli land and building to be concluded in the near future, with sufficient funds expected to be realized for settlement of obligations. As of the reporting date, the arrangement remains under discussion with the developer.

Production restoration. Management expects the plant to be fully operational by 31/08/2026 following the zinc tank rupture and temporary suspension from 15/04/2026. The company is in the process of resuming operations.

Debt settlement. Management asserts that Rs. 9,295.00 lakhs, being the principal outstanding, is payable towards full and final settlement of the assigned debt. The assignee’s notice stated total dues including interest as on 25/03/2026 at Rs. 1,189.41 crores excluding further accruals and charges.

Recovered properties. Management intends to sell the recovered land and residential properties in the open market in the near future and adjust the realizable value against the amount receivable.

SEBI matter. Management believes, based on legal advice, that the likelihood of an adverse outcome is presently not probable, and no further adjustments are considered necessary. The matter remains sub judice before the Supreme Court.

GST dispute. Management challenged the demand and obtained an interim stay; the rectification order dated 10/07/2026 substantially rectified the demand, leaving a penalty of Rs. 0.79 lakhs, which was paid on 07/08/2026.

Internal controls. Management has initiated steps to strengthen internal controls relating to procurement, vendor verification, payment authorization, and related aspects, and is strengthening internal controls and system-based tracking for inventory valuation. Management does not expect any other material impact on the financial results or operations for the current period based on information currently available.

Observed performance. Standalone profit for the period was Rs. 33.12 lakhs for the quarter ended 30/06/2026, compared with Rs. 36.72 lakhs for the quarter ended 31/03/2026, Rs. 95.65 lakhs for the quarter ended 30/06/2025, and Rs. 311.09 lakhs for the year ended 31/03/2026. Consolidated revenue from operations was Rs. 717.74 lakhs for the quarter ended 30/06/2026, compared with Rs. 1,129.18 lakhs for the quarter ended 31/03/2026, Rs. 1,891.87 lakhs for the quarter ended 30/06/2025, and Rs. 5,337.61 lakhs for the year ended 31/03/2026. Consolidated profit for the period was Rs. 43.83 lakhs for the quarter ended 30/06/2026, compared with Rs. 139.18 lakhs for the quarter ended 31/03/2026, Rs. 95.65 lakhs for the quarter ended 30/06/2025, and Rs. 438.30 lakhs for the year ended 31/03/2026. Other income included a foreign exchange gain of Rs. 162.15 lakhs for the quarter ended 30/06/2026, compared with Rs. 30.42 lakhs for the quarter ended 30/06/2025.
