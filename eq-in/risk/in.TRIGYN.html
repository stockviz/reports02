Trigyn Technologies — Recent Corporate Announcements

Headwinds and Challenges

Standalone profitability remains weak. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, standalone revenue was Rs. 3,799.38 lakhs (up from Rs. 3,541.02 lakhs a year earlier but down from Rs. 4,733.01 lakhs in the prior quarter), and the company recorded a standalone Loss After Tax of Rs. (247.98) lakhs, compared with a loss of Rs. (499.02) lakhs in the June 2025 quarter and a profit of Rs. 1,084.71 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter.

Uncollected APSFL contract dues. Under the Andhra Pradesh State Fibernet Limited (APSFL) contract (total value Rs. 160 crores), revenue of Rs. 79.90 crores has been recognized on the supply portion, but against milestone billings of Rs. 79.40 crores only Rs. 17.90 crores has been received, leaving Rs. 61.50 crores outstanding for more than seven years. The company has not booked Quarterly Guaranteed Revenue of Rs. 80 crores on the operations and maintenance portion due to uncertainty of collection, relying on an independent expert opinion that revenue recognition may be postponed until there is reasonable certainty of realization. An Expected Credit Loss provision of Rs. 61.50 crores has been made as of March 31, 2026. Balance work at 59 schools, 1 District Studio and Central Studio remains pending due to non-allotment of sites by APSFL.

Nashik smart parking dispute. No tolls were collected in the prior financial year or the current quarter. NMSCDCL issued a termination notice in September 2023, invoked the bank guarantee on October 20, 2025, and Trigyn has filed for commercial arbitration. The unamortized capital cost carried in the balance sheet as of June 30, 2026 is Rs. 3.30 crores. A petition seeking removal/substitution of the arbitrator is pending before the Bombay High Court, with the next hearing on August 14, 2026.

BharatNet Phase III receivable. Under the BharatNet Phase III agreement (order value Rs. 101.61 crores), receivables from the customer as of June 30, 2026 were Rs. 25.01 crores from an invoice raised in 2025-26. An ECL provision of Rs. 1.08 crores was made for the quarter, taking the cumulative ECL provision to Rs. 1.57 crores.

Tax and regulatory demands. The GST department confirmed a demand of Rs. 9.08 crores for FY 2019-20 to FY 2022-23 disallowing input tax credit; the company filed an appeal on June 30, 2026. Separately, following an Income Tax search under Section 132 on August 29, 2018, block assessment orders for 2014-15 to 2019-20 raised a fresh demand of Rs. 3.14 crores; appeals/rectifications are in progress.

Subsidiary going-concern dependence. Leading Edge Infotech Limited and Trigyn Technologies (India) Private Limited have negative net worth, cannot meet commitments on their own, and are dependent on holding company support; management is exploring revival, merger/amalgamation or liquidation. Eight subsidiaries’ results are management-certified and not reviewed by the auditors.

Legacy balances awaiting RBI approval. Investments, receivables and loans relating to wound-up overseas subsidiaries (including eCapital Solutions (Bermuda) Ltd at Rs. 50,972.96 lakhs) remain carried forward pending RBI approval under FEMA; the matter has been carried forward for more than 11 years.

Pending legal matters. These include the AM Alloy Industries SDN BHD loan litigation (a $4 million unsecured loan fully reserved; lawsuit dropped in 2026 with the option to refile before October 28, 2028), the J. Kohli copyright suit against TTIPL (listed December 10, 2026), the ISYX Technologies matter (NCLT Mumbai listed September 28, 2026), the ESDS arbitration challenge, the Iram Technologies cheque case (criminal complaint listed September 28, 2026), and the TCIL termination dispute (arbitration listed August 19, 2026).

Related-party balances. Receivables from United Telecoms Limited and Priyaraja Electronics Limited (advances converted into inter-corporate loans) were pending formal loan documentation and balance confirmations as of June 30, 2026, though principal repayments of Rs. 500 lakhs and Rs. 200 lakhs respectively were received on August 10, 2026.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Consolidated revenue growth and return to profit. Consolidated revenue for the June 2026 quarter was Rs. 25,275.74 lakhs, up from Rs. 22,449.99 lakhs a year earlier and marginally above the Rs. 25,194.63 lakhs of the prior quarter. Consolidated Profit After Tax from continuing operations was Rs. 348.75 lakhs, compared with a loss of Rs. (460.87) lakhs a year earlier and a profit of Rs. 63.21 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter.

Large addressable contract base. The APSFL contract (Rs. 160 crores total), the BharatNet Phase III order (Rs. 101.61 crores), and the Nashik smart parking project represent significant awarded work, with the APSFL supply portion substantially complete and revenue recognized in line with Ind AS 115.

Working capital facilities in place. The company has sanctioned working capital facilities of Rs. 100 crores from banks (Rs. 40 crores fund-based, Rs. 60 crores non-fund-based bank guarantees against 100% FD), with utilization of Rs. 17.07 crores fund-based and Rs. 11.58 crores non-fund-based as of June 30, 2026.

Investment gains. The IIRM Holdings Limited investment (29,15,554 shares) was valued at Rs. 134.55 per share as of June 30, 2026, implying an unrealized gain of Rs. 1,619.15 lakhs shown under OCI.

Governance continuity. At the postal ballot concluded June 24, 2026, members approved the continuation of Dr. Raja Mohan Rao Potluri as Non-Executive, Non-Independent Director upon attaining 75 years (effective July 15, 2026), the re-appointment of Ms. Lakshmi Potluri as Independent Director for a second five-year term from August 11, 2026 to August 10, 2031, and renewal of the consultancy agreement with Mr. R. Ganapathi for one year from February 20, 2026 to February 19, 2027 at Rs. 12,00,000 per annum. On August 11, 2026, the Board approved the appointment of Mr. Narendra Bhandari as an Independent Director for five years from August 11, 2026 to August 10, 2031, subject to member approval at the 40th AGM.

Business profile. Trigyn describes itself as a multinational IT firm with 2,000 resources in 25 countries, a client base including Fortune 500 companies, intergovernmental organizations and US state governments, and service offerings spanning Unified Command & Control Centers, Smart Solutions, IoT, Smart Utilities, and Security and Surveillance.

Key Risks

  • Collection risk on long-outstanding receivables, particularly the Rs. 61.50 crores APSFL balance and the Rs. 25.01 crores BharatNet receivable.
  • Arbitration and litigation outcomes, including the Nashik parking dispute, TCIL termination, ISYX insolvency proceedings, and the J. Kohli suit.
  • Tax demands of Rs. 9.08 crores (GST) and Rs. 3.14 crores (income tax block assessment), subject to appeal.
  • Going-concern dependence of two subsidiaries on holding company support.
  • Regulatory approval risk on FEMA/RBI matters relating to wound-up overseas subsidiaries.
  • The company’s own forward-looking statement notes potential risks including general economic conditions, foreign exchange fluctuations, competitive product and pricing pressures, and regulatory developments.
  • The auditors’ limited review reports draw attention to multiple notes (revenue non-recognition, toll project, legal suits, going concern, RBI approvals, GST and income tax demands, discontinued operations, management-certified subsidiary accounts) without modifying their conclusions.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

The source material contains limited explicit management guidance. The clearest forward-looking elements are:

  • Revenue recognition stance: Management’s position, supported by an independent expert opinion, is to postpone recognition of the Rs. 80 crores Quarterly Guaranteed Revenue on the APSFL O&M contract until there is reasonable certainty of realization. Observed performance is consistent with this stance — no such revenue has been booked, and the Rs. 61.50 crores receivable remains outstanding.
  • Legal matters: Management states it has evaluated pending legal cases with legal counsel and believes it has a good case and expects a favorable outcome in most cases. Observed developments include the NMSCDCL bank guarantee invocation on October 20, 2025 despite the arbitration, and the company dropping its AM Alloy lawsuit in 2026 while retaining the option to refile.
  • Subsidiary revival: Management states it is taking steps to revive Leading Edge Infotech and TTIPL and is exploring merger/amalgamation/liquidation. No outcome has been reported in the source material.
  • Labour Codes: The company has considered restructured employee compensation effective April 1, 2026 and assessed impacts in line with the Labour Codes, draft rules, FAQs and legal opinions, and will account for further impacts as Central and State Rules are finalized.
  • Forward-looking disclaimer: The company notes that future results could differ materially from current views due to risks including economic conditions, foreign exchange fluctuations, competitive pressures and regulatory developments, and that responses can only be given to questions that are not price sensitive.

Observed performance versus prior periods shows consolidated revenue growth and a swing to consolidated profit from continuing operations, while standalone results remained in loss for the quarter.
