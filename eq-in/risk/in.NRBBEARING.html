NRB Bearings — Q1 FY27 Corporate Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Cost escalation: Other expenses rose sharply in the quarter. Management attributed the increase to escalation in electricity, logistics, and petroleum-product-related costs, and stated that a combination of value engineering (VAVE), plant-level cost reduction, and price increases is being used to address this.
  • Global logistics and geopolitical disruption: The Unitec joint venture location was shifted from Hyderabad to Aurangabad partly because the building start coincided with a war situation, and management cited the broader global scenario slowing things down and potential future logistics cost increases.
  • Aerospace revenue unpredictability: Management noted it is hard to predict aerospace revenue in a given year because outcomes depend on design and order decisions by partners such as HAL Nashik, and on whether those partners secure platform orders.
  • Quarterly margin variability: Management stated it is not a quarter-by-quarter driven company and that margins can fluctuate within the guided 18%–20% range depending on which quarter absorbs which cost.
  • Foreign exchange regulatory matter: Standalone and consolidated results disclose trade receivables of ₹2,512 lakhs from customers outside India pending settlement, with receipt delayed beyond the timeline stipulated under the FED Master Direction No. 16/2015-16 under FEMA, 1999. The company is recovering the dues, has provided where required, and is regularising the defaults with the appropriate authority; any penalty is not ascertainable, though management believes the exposure is not expected to be material.
  • Prior-year labour code impact: The statutory impact of the new labour codes (₹1,018 lakhs gratuity and ₹306 lakhs long-term compensated absences, totalling ₹1,324 lakhs) was recorded as an exceptional loss in the year ended 31 March 2026.
  • Waluj fire aftermath: A fire at the Waluj, Aurangabad plant on 8 May 2023 caused an assessed loss of ₹2,076 lakhs; insurance disbursements of ₹621 lakhs (FY26) and ₹265 lakhs (sanctioned 30 June 2026, received 29 July 2026) have been classified as exceptional gains.
  • Shareholder dissent on one resolution: At the 61st AGM (29 July 2026), the resolution under Section 185 of the Companies Act, 2013 for giving loan/guarantee/security passed with 95.5894% votes in favour and 4.4106% against; public institutions voted 88.6306% in favour and 11.3694% against on that resolution.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Q1 FY27 consolidated performance: Revenue from operations was ₹370 crores versus ₹310 crores a year earlier, a 19.2% YoY increase. Consolidated PAT rose to ₹38 crores from ₹33 crores, up 15% YoY.
  • Standalone performance: Sales rose 14.7% YoY, PAT rose 31.7% YoY, and standalone EBITDA grew 21.7% YoY.
  • Trailing twelve-month momentum: Management cited a trailing 12-month revenue growth of 14.38% (also referenced as 14.28% in the call) and stated that compounding this rate would imply approximately ₹2,730 crores by 2031.
  • Six growth vectors: Management described broadening the addressable market across aerospace; automotive adjacencies (tapered and ball bearing wheel hubs, spherical roller bearings, steering systems, one-way clutches, wheel bearings); electrification beyond EV mobility (high-frequency drives, commercial EV fleets, electric two-wheelers, off-highway e-mobility, industrial electrification); mobility beyond current segments (robotics, AGVs, urban air mobility, drones, automated transit, heavy construction equipment); and mission-critical friction solutions (industrial gearboxes, data center cooling architecture, renewable engineering, energy drives, precision gearboxes). Several of these were described as USD10–14 billion market opportunities.
  • Aerospace and defence: The Mahant Tool Room acquisition completed in July 2026 brought the AS9100D aerospace certification. Management projected ₹300 crores revenue and ₹90 crores profitability contribution from MTR’s aerospace defence and commercial applications by 2031. Current order book was stated at approximately ₹25 crores for MTR, or about ₹30 crores including spherical bearing orders, and approximately ₹50 crores including NRB’s regular defence business. NRB received its first bearing order for a mission-critical Sukhoi-30 application (plain spherical bearing), described as a breakthrough order open to very few companies globally.
  • New customer wins: JCB UK as a customer in heavy-duty off-highway (announced at the AGM); HAAS Automation USA in industrial applications; a production order for high-precision planet pins for a General Motors Corvette program through an existing tier-1 transmission manufacturer, described as the first win for the NRB USA Columbia, South Carolina facility, with peak volume of 300,000 units.
  • Automotive platform launches: Next-generation BMW Hybrid steering platform (3 Series, 5 Series, X1, X2, Mini Cooper, Countryman); BMW X5 and X7 hybrid transmissions; Mercedes-Benz next-gen premium-drive electric EQC, EQA, CLA Electric version; BMW i steering launch planned for 2027 across ICE, hybrid, and electric lines.
  • Industrial segment: Industrial business is now 14% of total revenue, up from 11%, with 34% growth in industrial.
  • Unitec JV: Incorporated NRB Unitec Friction Solutions Private Limited on 22 April 2026 for cylindrical roller bearings for industrial gearboxes. Investment of ₹110 crores with capacity covering ₹130 crores of turnover; commissioning targeted for April 2027; location moved to a partly ready facility in the Shendra region near Aurangabad. The Board approved a corporate guarantee of up to ₹50 crores in favour of HSBC Bank for term loan facilities of the JV.
  • Lifetime nominated business: Increased from ₹800 crores to ₹1,100 crores, with the Corvette order cited as a contributor.
  • Capex: Total capex programme of ₹270 crores; ₹60 crores already spent, with another ₹100 crores ordered or in the process of being ordered. Management stated ₹100 crores of capex corresponds to ₹130 crores of sales.
  • EV-agnostic positioning: Approximately 70% of applications are common across EV, ICE, and hybrid, focused on chassis, transmission, and steering applications.
  • Dividend: Interim dividend of ₹2.25 per share (112.5%) on face value of ₹2 declared on 7 May 2026 and paid on 11 May 2026.
  • AGM outcomes: All four resolutions passed with requisite majority at the 61st AGM held 29 July 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Cost inflation: Electricity, logistics, and petroleum-related cost escalation, with mitigation dependent on VAVE, cost reduction, and price increases.
  • Aerospace order timing: Revenue conversion depends on third-party platform decisions and partner order wins, making near-term aerospace revenue difficult to predict.
  • Regulatory/FEMA exposure: The pending settlement of ₹2,512 lakhs of foreign receivables beyond FEMA timelines, with penalty not ascertainable and regularisation ongoing.
  • Margin variability: Operating margins may fluctuate within the guided range amid volatile market conditions.
  • JV execution and location change: The Unitec JV shifted location and is targeting April 2027 commissioning; the share transfer of 25% to Unitec S.r.l. had not been completed as at 30 June 2026, with the entity remaining a wholly owned subsidiary.
  • Data center entry: Still in the research and development stage with nothing concrete to announce.
  • Dependence on global OEM programs: Growth prospects are tied to platform nominations and launches at customers such as BMW, Mercedes-Benz, GM, JCB, and HAL Nashik.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Revenue growth guidance: Management referenced a conservative stance and pointed to the trailing 12-month growth of 14.38% as the baseline, implying approximately ₹2,730 crores by 2031. The aspirational vision of ₹3,000 crores was affirmed, while the earlier ₹2,500 crores figure was described as looking lower than what is likely to be achieved. Observed Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue growth of 19.2% YoY was above the trailing 12-month rate cited.
  • Margin guidance: Management reiterated a margin range of 18%–20% on an annual basis, noting quarterly fluctuations. Q1 FY27 standalone EBITDA grew 21.7% YoY, and consolidated PAT grew 15% YoY.
  • Capex guidance: Total capex of ₹270 crores, with ₹60 crores spent and ₹100 crores ordered or being ordered; management linked ₹100 crores of capex to ₹130 crores of sales.
  • Unitec JV guidance: Investment of ₹110 crores, capacity covering ₹130 crores of turnover, commissioning by April 2027. The plant location was changed from Hyderabad to Aurangabad, and a partly ready facility was purchased to protect the timeline.
  • Aerospace guidance: MTR projected at ₹300 crores revenue and ₹90 crores profitability by 2031; current order book around ₹25–30 crores for MTR and approximately ₹50 crores including NRB’s regular defence business. Management declined to give specific FY27 aerospace revenue figures, citing unpredictability, but confirmed some order book will convert to revenue in FY27.
  • Lifetime nominated business: Increased from ₹800 crores to ₹1,100 crores, with the Corvette order cited as a contributor; management prefers to update this annually rather than quarterly.
  • Industrial mix guidance: Industrial was previously discussed as moving toward 20%–25%; observed level is 14%, up from 11%, with 34% growth.
  • Dividend: Interim dividend of ₹2.25 per share declared 7 May 2026 and paid 11 May 2026, consistent with the board action recorded in the results notes.

Broker Narrative

The early thesis centred on domestic auto recovery, China+1 export gains, and margin recovery, with auto cyclicality and steel costs as the main risks. By the last report, the narrative shifted toward industrial/aerospace/defence diversification, a larger lifetime order book, and EV-agnostic revenue, while still acknowledging auto/industrial slowdown and commodity cost pressures. The broker stayed Buy throughout, but the latest predicted +27.6% return has so far delivered only +1.5%.

Fears that came true

  • Cyclicality/auto slowdown materialised: the last report cited a slowdown in domestic automotive and industrial segment, and the stock returned just +1.5% against the predicted +27.6%.
  • Input cost volatility materialised: commodity prices increased and PAT margin moderated 31 bps YoY in Q1FY27, confirming the earlier steel-cost risk.

Optimism that failed

  • Domestic vehicle recovery failed to hold: the early expectation of 10% YoY domestic growth was contradicted by the later slowdown in domestic automotive and industrial segment.
  • The China+1 export growth driver was effectively replaced in the later narrative by industrial/aerospace/defence orders, with no evidence of the expected export-led traction materialising.
