Suryoday Small Finance Bank — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Microfinance cycle transition. Management describes the sector as having come through a down cycle, with the bank deliberately moving away from the traditional JLG model toward individual loans (Vikas Loan). The MD noted that while the cycle has turned, the bank must remain cautious because customers without good track records are being weeded out, and it should not treat this as a signal to “go aggressive.”
  • Competitive intensity. Management flagged that competition “will be intense,” with digital capability positioned as the differentiator on customer experience.
  • Deposit pricing pressure. Fixed deposit rates had not come down; the bank responded by optimising savings account rates across buckets, staying selective on bulk deposits, and focusing on granular SA and 2–2.5 year FD money.
  • Yield and NIM compression. Yield fell from 17.3% in Q4 FY26 to 16.2% in Q1 FY27, and NIM from 7.9% to 7.2%. The CFO attributed this to a 4% reduction in the Inclusive Finance share (48% to 44%), a roughly 0.75% impact, and lower MFI recovery income recognition versus a strong Q4 FY26.
  • Commercial vehicle stress. Vehicle finance PAR rose to 11.5% from 10.1% in the prior quarter, attributed to fuel price increases and load availability issues linked to the Middle East crisis. About 75–80% of the CV book is used vehicles.
  • Mortgage slippages. A couple of slippages in the mortgage business were reported, being worked on for resolution through legal process, with management noting the cases are well collateralised.
  • PSL income normalisation. Other income of ₹147.9 Cr in Q1 FY27 included ₹46 Cr of PSL income from selling ₹1,600 Cr of PSL certificates. Management guided PSL income down to ₹10–15 Cr for each of the next two quarters and ₹15–20 Cr in Q4, implying a step-down in other income.
  • CASA moderation. CASA ratio declined 160 bps QoQ to 21.0%, though it was up 326 bps YoY.
  • Cost-to-income uptick. CTI rose 72 bps YoY to 70.1%, though it improved 360 bps QoQ from 73.7%.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong growth momentum. Gross advances grew 32.5% YoY to ₹14,376 Cr; deposits grew 29.4% YoY to ₹14,634 Cr; disbursements rose 30.6% YoY to ₹2,954 Cr.
  • Vikas Loan pivot. VL is now 75% of the Inclusive Finance book at ₹4,775 Cr, growing 40.0% YoY. Individual loans constitute around 80% of monthly onboarding, with plans to further reduce JLG exposure over 1–1.5 years. VL disbursements were ₹1,040 Cr in Q1 FY27, including ₹327 Cr of New-To-Bank VL with 53,000 customers.
  • CGFMU support. Over 98% of the IF portfolio is covered under CGFMU. The bank received ₹387 Cr of claims in Q1 FY27, with ₹134 Cr receivable against NNPA of ₹170 Cr. A residual claim of ₹15–20 Cr is expected, plus ₹135–150 Cr of additional eligible claims in subsequent years. PCR stands at 81.8%.
  • Asset quality improvement. GNPA/NNPA at 6.5%/1.2%; adjusted for the CGFMU receivable, 2.9%/0.3%. Slippages fell from ₹106 Cr in Q4 FY26 to ₹92 Cr in Q1 FY27; MFI slippages fell from ₹73 Cr to ₹53 Cr. IF current-bucket collection efficiency was 99.2%, and the post-November 2024 book was ~99.4%.
  • Secured retail expansion. Retail assets grew 40.8% YoY; CV crossed ₹1,900 Cr and mortgage including MHL crossed ₹3,200 Cr. Management plans ~50 branch additions in the current and next quarter, focused on inclusive finance and commercial vehicles, plus micro-mortgage and CV expansion into existing branches.
  • Digital franchise. Digital deposits reached ₹2,222 Cr (15% of the book) with a ~₹6 Cr/day run rate. Credit on UPI crossed 9 lakh customers with ~20 lakh pre-qualified; CLOU scaled to ~20 lakh pre-qualified, 9.4 lakh sanctioned and 5 lakh utilised customers. Digital MSME disbursements were ₹59 Cr in the quarter. Management targets doubling digital deposits and Credit on UPI, and doubling CLOU users from ~6 lakh to ~12 lakh.
  • Funding flexibility. With MFI quality improving, the IBPC market reopened; the bank took IBPC funding at slightly under 5%. Capital adequacy was 20%, with plans to raise fresh Tier 2 (₹200 Cr resolution passed at the AGM) and evaluate Tier 1 timing (₹300 Cr resolution passed).
  • Profitability. PAT rose 113.1% YoY to ₹75.2 Cr; RoA/RoE at 1.6%/14.5%. Pre-POP was ₹138.6 Cr, up 27.2% YoY.

3. Key Risks

  • Microfinance cycle risk. Management explicitly stated it is not treating CGFMU as a shield for doing business, and is modelling an extreme scenario where 12–14% of the portfolio could be at risk. The bank intends to test whether the business model can sustain a 1–1.5 year down cycle without relying on insurance.
  • CGFMU scheme concentration risk. An investor raised whether widespread industry adoption of CGFMU insurance could strain claim payouts in a future event. Management noted NCGTC is well funded but emphasised the scheme is intended for financial inclusion, not as a business model substitute, and that premiums paid over the next couple of years may exceed claims made.
  • Commercial vehicle asset quality. PAR elevation tied to fuel prices and load availability; management expects normalisation but acknowledged the CV market has been elevated across the industry for 1.5 years.
  • Yield trajectory. Management expects yields to return to ~17% and stabilise in the 17.2–17.5% range, contingent on recovery income from IF NPLs returning to prior levels.
  • Deposit mobilisation. Management acknowledged deposits “may look like a challenge,” relying on retail granular deposits (average ticket under ₹2 lakh) and digital channels, with some rollovers repricing lower.
  • Earnings volatility. An investor noted historical volatility; management’s stated focus is on sustainable quarter-on-quarter improvement rather than guiding higher ROE, with visibility cited for 4–6 quarters.

  • Credit cost guidance maintained. The CFO reaffirmed the 0.8–1.0% FY27 credit cost guidance, with Q1 at 0.8%.
  • RoA guidance. Management maintained the 1.3–1.4% RoA guidance despite Q1 delivering 1.6%, with the CFO noting the PSL income boost would not repeat at the same level in the next two quarters. The MD indicated the endeavour is to inch ROE up by a meaningful and sustainable 0.5–1% year-on-year over the next couple of years.
  • PAT trajectory. Management referenced ~₹75 Cr per quarter, with Q2 potentially subdued due to lower PSL income, offset by IF paying-book growth of ₹120–150 Cr per quarter.
  • Asset mix. Management intends to maintain a micro-banking to retail assets mix of roughly 48:52; the actual Q1 FY27 mix was 44.4% IF : 55.6% RA, with IF down 53 bps QoQ.
  • Yield guidance. Management expects yields to return to ~17% and stabilise at 17.2–17.5%; Q1 FY27 yield on non-NPA advances was 17.4%, down from 18.4% in Q4 FY26.
  • Capital. Capital adequacy was 20%, with management targeting 20–22% given growth plans; Tier 2 raise targeted for closure during the current quarter, and Tier 1 timing still under evaluation.
  • Branch expansion. Guidance of 100 new branches for FY27; 718 branches as of June 2026 versus 717 in March 2026, with ~50 additions planned in the current and next quarter.
  • Digital targets. 2x growth in digital deposits and Credit on UPI, and CLOU users doubling from ~6 lakh to ~12 lakh during FY27.
