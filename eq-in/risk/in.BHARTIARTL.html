Bharti Airtel: Recent Corporate Developments and Q1 FY27 Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Digital TV segment under pressure. Digital TV revenues grew only 1.4% YoY in Q1 FY27, with EBITDA declining 14% YoY and EBIT turning negative at Rs (785) crore. EBITDA margin fell to 42.9% from 50.9% a year earlier, and net additions were just 6 thousand customers during the quarter, with monthly churn rising to 2.7% from 1.9% in the prior quarter.

Homes business cash flow remains negative. Despite 33.2% YoY revenue growth, the Homes segment reported negative operating free cash flow of Rs (7,757) crore, reflecting heavy capex of Rs 18,996 crore. EBIT declined 15% YoY and EBIT margin fell to 11.0% from 17.2%.

Rising depreciation and finance costs. Consolidated depreciation and amortization rose 14.2% YoY to Rs 142,350 crore, while net finance costs increased 1.2% YoY. Interest on lease obligations rose to Rs 19,106 crore from Rs 15,907 crore.

Africa capex surge. Africa capex jumped to $389 million from $121 million a year earlier, contributing to a 14% decline in operating free cash flow for the region.

Legacy complexity and cybersecurity risks. The ITI Limited partnership announcement explicitly cites challenges enterprises face from “fragmented legacy environments, rising cybersecurity risks, growing data demands, and the need for greater operational agility.”

Regulatory transition. New telecom licensing rules issued on June 23, 2026 require existing licensees to mandatorily migrate upon expiry, with applications due one year before expiry. New RBI directions on NBFC customer protection and governance take effect January 1, 2027.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong consolidated growth. Q1 FY27 revenues reached Rs 58,539 crore, up 18.4% YoY and 5.7% QoQ. EBITDA rose 19.3% YoY to Rs 33,599 crore with a 57.4% margin. Net income before exceptional items grew 35.5% YoY to Rs 8,057 crore.

Mobile momentum. India mobile revenue grew 9.2% YoY, with ARPU rising to Rs 264 from Rs 250. Postpaid net additions hit a record 1.0 million, taking the postpaid base to 30.0 million. Smartphone data customers increased by 21.1 million YoY, now representing 80% of mobile customers. Data consumption rose 36% YoY to 34.4 GB per customer per month.

Homes expansion. The Homes segment added 473 thousand customers, reaching 14.7 million, with revenue growth of 33.2% YoY. LCO partnerships are live in 1,570 cities.

Airtel Business growth. Revenue grew 12.0% YoY, led by connectivity solutions and digital services. The segment launched Airtel Secure Workforce, described as India’s first fully-managed Zero Trust Architecture security platform.

Africa performance. Africa revenues grew 21.1% YoY in constant currency to $1,836 million. Airtel Money revenue grew 25.8% to $400 million, with active customers up 23.3% to 56.5 million. Transaction value rose 37.4% to $61 billion.

Balance sheet strength. Net debt (excluding lease obligations) fell to Rs 818,521 crore from Rs 1,254,893 crore a year earlier. The Net Debt to EBITDA ratio improved to 1.17 from 1.70. S&P upgraded Airtel’s credit rating to ‘BBB+’.

Strategic partnership with ITI Limited. Announced August 10, 2026, the collaboration targets enterprise digital transformation across connectivity, data center and sovereign cloud services, IoT, AI-powered solutions, LEO satellite services, and cybersecurity, with focus on regulated sectors like banking and PSUs, plus defence and security applications under Make in India and Atmanirbhar Bharat initiatives.

Network expansion. Airtel deployed 1,579 towers and 14,540 mobile broadband base stations in the quarter. Over the past year, 7,631 towers were added and 45,171 kilometers of fiber deployed. Population coverage reached 97.4%.

Africa share buyback and Nigeria license renewal. Airtel Africa launched a share buyback of up to 1% of issued share capital, with approximately 10.2 million shares purchased for $46.6 million as of June 30, 2026. Airtel Nigeria renewed its 900MHz spectrum license for $37 million, valid for ten years.

Airtel Money listing progress. London has been identified as the preferred listing venue for the proposed Airtel Money IPO.

3. Key Risks

Customer churn. India mobile monthly churn rose to 2.6% from 2.4% in the prior quarter. Africa churn increased to 4.5% from 4.3%.

Capex intensity. Consolidated capex rose 61% YoY to Rs 13,386 crore, with India capex at Rs 9,698 crore. This pressures operating free cash flow, which grew only 2% YoY.

Exceptional items. A charge of Rs 3,534 crore was recognized for an in-principle settlement of a commercial dispute in a subsidiary, partially offset by a tax benefit of Rs 3,898 crore.

Regulatory compliance burden. New licensing rules and RBI governance amendments require enhanced compliance frameworks, including formal board approval for compliance officers and strengthened internal audit processes.

Africa currency exposure. While Africa revenues grew 21.1% in constant currency, reported revenues in INR terms reflect currency translation effects. The region’s net debt stands at Rs 537,622 crore.

Competitive intensity. The stock movement comparison shows Vodafone Idea shares rose 94.6% while Bharti declined 7.8% in the Q1’27 vs Q1’26 period, suggesting competitive dynamics remain fluid.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Portfolio premiumisation. Management stated focus on “sustained portfolio premiumisation in Mobile” and “quality customers and convergence agenda” in Homes. Observed: Mobile ARPU improved to Rs 264 from Rs 250 YoY; postpaid additions reached record 1.0 million; Homes added 473 thousand customers with 33.2% revenue growth.

Digital services momentum. Management cited “strong momentum in Airtel Business” and “healthy orderbook execution.” Observed: Airtel Business revenue grew 12.0% YoY with EBITDA margin expanding to 44.0% from 42.6%.

Balance sheet discipline. Management referenced “disciplined capital allocation” and “balance sheet strength.” Observed: Net Debt to EBITDA improved to 1.17 from 1.70; interest coverage ratio improved to 7.64 from 6.32.

Africa conviction. Management expressed “conviction in Africa’s long-term growth opportunity” following the share swap increasing stake to over 79%. Observed: Africa revenues grew 21.1% YoY in constant currency; customer base increased 11.6% to 189 million; Airtel Money active customers grew 23.3%.

Network investment. Management highlighted “focused investments to future-proof Airtel.” Observed: 45,171 kilometers of fiber deployed over the past year; 7,631 towers added; population coverage expanded to 97.4%.

Convergence agenda. Management noted IPTV “continues to support underlying growth” and focus on “driving convergence.” Observed: Digital TV customer base stable at 16.0 million; IPTV expansion ongoing but segment profitability under pressure with negative EBIT.
