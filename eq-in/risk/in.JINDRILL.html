Jindal Drilling & Industries — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Three rigs due for dehiring during FY27. Management stated that three of the company’s rigs are expected to be dehired within the current financial year. Once dehired, each rig faces a refurbishment period of 4 to 6 months during which no revenue accrues. Management confirmed that H2 revenue will decline as a result, with the three rigs out of revenue.
  • Refurbishment cost burden. Management estimated refurbishment cost at INR 90 crores to INR 110 crores per rig, citing inflationary trends, higher labour cost, and higher transit cost of materials. Cash is being conserved specifically for this purpose.
  • Weak redeployment track record. Management noted that while historically a dehired rig would be redeployed on a new ONGC contract, that has not been the case over the past two years, and all drilling contractors have had problems redeploying rigs.
  • Day rate pressure. The recent ONGC award for Jindal Pioneer came at an Effective Day Rate of Rs. 45,83,215.16 (equivalent to USD 47,893.99). Management stated it had participated at a rate of USD 62,000 and was pushed down to approximately USD 47,800, and observed that the outcome depends on the customer’s willingness to pay rates comparable to prevailing international day rates.
  • INR-denominated contract. The Jindal Pioneer contract is denominated in INR at a fixed day rate, removing currency depreciation benefit on that contract. Management said clarity on whether future tenders will be USD- or INR-denominated would come in the next quarter’s earnings call.
  • JV loss. A loss in one joint venture in the quarter was attributed to refurbishment expenses incurred by that entity — the seller of Rig Jindal Pioneer — to bring the rig into the condition required under the sale purchase agreement before Jindal Drilling takes delivery.
  • Long-running ONGC legal dispute. The dispute has continued for 14–15 years and is before the Supreme Court for the second time, with legal costs borne over that period. Funds have been received; the amount comprises an original receivable of INR 63 crores plus interest and forex appreciation, totalling close to INR 163 crores. Management stated the possibility of losing is remote based on its own and its lawyers’ assessment, but if the case were lost, the amount would have to be repaid.
  • International deployment constraints. Management cited first preference for existing participants in international geographies, differing rig criteria across geographies, counterparty risk, and country risk as reasons international deployment is not straightforward.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • New ONGC contract for Jindal Pioneer. ONGC issued a Notification of Award (announced 30 July 2026) for deployment of rig Jindal Pioneer for 3 years at an Effective Day Rate of Rs. 45,83,215.16 equivalent to USD 47,893.99. The rig is under refurbishment in UAE; refurbishment is expected to be completed by the first week of September, with deployment expected as early as October 2026 and contract commencement expected in Q3 FY27.
  • Order book visibility. The order book stands at INR 1,310 crores, bifurcated rig-wise, day rate-wise, and year-wise in the presentation, allowing assessment of revenue shape.
  • Confidence in redeploying the three dehired rigs. Management stated the likelihood of redeployment is fairly good and it remains fairly confident, supported by the Government of India’s Samudra Manthan exercise. Management acknowledged Samudra Manthan relates to deep and ultra-deepwater and does not benefit the company directly, but expects shallow water to benefit indirectly from a general increase in drilling activity, since existing country capacities are unlikely to be bypassed.
  • Expected day rate improvement. Management expects day rates to improve for the three rigs due for redeployment, while noting this expectation was also held for the contract just awarded and did not materialise.
  • EBITDA resilience relative to revenue. Management stated that although revenue will decline in H2, EBITDA is not expected to decline in proportion, because the decline in earnings would primarily come from two rigs rather than three. Discovery-I (owned) and Virtue-I (rented at a good rate) are the earnings contributors being dehired; Jindal Star is rented but not at a good rate. Management indicated EBITDA margin might even increase, though absolute earnings would decline in H2.
  • Cash position. The company remains cash-rich despite acquiring a rig in FY25 and refurbishing it, and expects the cash position to improve going forward.
  • Sector spending outlook. Management cited improvement in oil and gas sector expenditure in other sectors and expects improved expenditure in its own sector, with the company as a beneficiary.

3. Key Risks

  • Redeployment risk on three rigs. Each rig requires participation in a separate tender submission; tenders may cover one, two, or three rigs. Redeployment is subject to customer nuances across tenders.
  • Refurbishment execution and timing risk. The 4–6 month refurbishment window per rig creates a revenue gap; the Jindal Pioneer refurbishment and October 2026 deployment timeline is the near-term execution benchmark.
  • Day rate risk. The gap between expected and realised rates (USD 62,000 bid versus approximately USD 47,800 secured) illustrates pricing risk on future awards.
  • Litigation risk. The ONGC dispute before the Supreme Court carries a potential repayment exposure of close to INR 163 crores if lost, though management assesses the possibility as remote.
  • JV performance risk. The JV entity incurred losses on refurbishment obligations under the sale purchase agreement.
  • Currency and contract denomination risk. The Pioneer contract is INR-denominated and fixed, with no clarity yet on denomination of future tenders.
  • International expansion risk. Counterparty and country risk, plus incumbent preference, constrain international deployment.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Revenue trajectory. Management guided that H2 FY27 revenue will decline because three of six rigs will be out of revenue during refurbishment. This follows broadly constant total revenue over recent quarters, with the Q3 FY26 dip attributed to reversal of an other income item booked in Q2 FY26.
  • EBITDA. Management guided that EBITDA will not decline in proportion to revenue and that EBITDA margin might even increase, while absolute earnings decline in H2. Blended EBITDA guidance of 35% was referenced as the basis for assessing revenue shape. EBITDA has remained in line, with variation attributed to other expenses driven by forex fluctuation — more prevalent in the last quarter of FY26 and less prevalent in Q1 FY27.
  • Day rates. Management expected day rates to improve, but the actual ONGC award came in below the company’s bid (USD 62,000 bid versus approximately USD 47,800 secured), a divergence between guidance and observed outcome.
  • Redeployment. Management remains fairly confident of redeploying the three dehired rigs, while acknowledging that the past two years saw industry-wide redeployment difficulties and that outcomes depend on customer tender behaviour.
  • Cash. Management expects the cash position to improve going forward, after remaining cash-rich through the FY25 rig acquisition and refurbishment.
  • Capital allocation. Management stated no acquisitions are currently being pursued, with focus on redeploying the three rigs to minimise risk and conserving cash for refurbishment.
  • Jindal Pioneer timeline. Refurbishment completion guided for the first week of September, deployment in October 2026, and contract commencement in Q3 FY27, consistent with the 30 July 2026 award announcement.

Key Facts

  • The Board meeting held 7 August 2026 approved unaudited standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, with Limited Review Reports from M/s Kanodia Sanyal & Associates.
  • The 42nd Annual General Meeting was convened for Tuesday, 15 September 2026, at 03:00 P.M. IST via video conferencing or other audio-visual means; the Annual Report for FY2025-26 and AGM Notice are available at the published web link.
  • The earnings conference call for Q1 FY27 was held on 10 August 2026, and its transcript was filed on 14 August 2026.
  • The company describes itself as the largest offshore jack-up drilling contractor based in India with ONGC, with 5 rigs on long-term contracts deployed with ONGC and a 6th rig having recently received a new contract.
  • Discovery-I and Jindal Star received a slight extension in contract duration, as disclosed in the presentation.
