Headwinds and Challenges

Cost inflation and margin pressure. The quarter was marked by inflationary pressure from higher crude oil prices and sustained increases in packaging material costs, described as one of the most challenging cost environments for the sector in recent years. Input costs rose 360 basis points, of which roughly 200 basis points was attributed to the West Asia conflict and the balance to business mix following the integration of acquired businesses. Gross margin contracted 360 basis points to 65.8%. While measured price increases were taken, the sharp rise in input costs weighed on profitability.

International business disruption. International business declined 12% in the quarter, primarily because disruptions in the West Asia conflict constrained the ability to execute orders. The OTC pain management portfolio, produced in India, was particularly affected, with hefty orders unable to move pending approvals; personal care, produced in UAE, Germany and partly Thailand, was largely restored.

Seasonality and weather. The summer season saw significant regional divergence. A weaker and unseasonal summer softened demand for the talc portfolio, and delayed monsoons in western regions impacted pain management. The transition to the new GST regime caused temporary trade disruptions.

Category-specific softness. Skin Care grew only 3%, with Male Grooming and BoroPlus each registering low single-digit growth. Healthcare grew 2%, with the Medico range posting single-digit growth. Kesh King reported mid-single-digit growth, slower than its H2 FY26 trajectory.

Tax normalisation. Profit after tax fell 16% to ₹137 crore due to normalisation of the effective tax rate, as fiscal benefits enjoyed last year ended and the company returned to normal taxation of about 25%.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Consolidated and domestic growth. Consolidated revenue grew 15% to ₹1,039 crore. Domestic business grew 20%, or 12% like-to-like with 8% volume growth after considering prior-year numbers of Axiom and IncNut. For FY26, the company reported consolidated revenue of ₹3,779 crore, EBITDA of ₹964 crore at a 25.5% margin, PAT of ₹775 crore at a 20.5% margin, ROE of around 30%, a debt-equity ratio of 0.04x, a net cash surplus of ₹883 crore, and interim dividends of 1000% (₹10 per share) totalling ₹436.5 crore.

Strategic investments as a growth engine. On a like-to-like basis, the strategic investment portfolio grew 61% and now contributes 18% of the domestic business. The Man Company grew well north of 20%; Brillare had a fabulous year, aided by Rosemary oil; Axiom Ayurveda and IncNut started strongly within the Emami ecosystem. Management expects this portfolio to contribute around 16% of turnover in FY27 and approximately 25% by FY30. Gross margins across these businesses are above 55%, with TMC north of 60% and Brillare and IncNut north of 70%; at an aggregate level they are close to or at EBITDA neutral.

Category performance. Hair and Scalp care grew 11%, with Navratna Cool Oil posting strong double-digit growth and 7 Oils in One delivering robust growth. Talc delivered high single-digit growth. OTC grew in high teens.

Channel transformation. Organised channels grew 19% like-to-like and contribute 32% of the domestic business. Modern trade and e-commerce maintained strong momentum, with quick commerce contributing 35% of the e-commerce business.

Structural opportunity. Management cites significant headroom from low penetration in core brands and the convergence of rural recovery, urban premiumisation, rapid digital adoption, wellness-led consumption, and scaling strategic investments. The company is transitioning from brand-wise to category-wise reporting to reflect a more diversified portfolio spanning traditional FMCG, personal and healthcare, premium beauty and grooming, digital-first brands and emerging consumer platforms.

Technology and transformation. Project SETU, SalesCode.AI and the Analytics Hub are on track for completion within the current financial year, aimed at improving forecast accuracy, service levels, working capital efficiency, field force productivity and enterprise-wide data analytics.

Key Risks

  • Continued commodity inflation and geopolitical developments, explicitly flagged as areas to watch.
  • Further West Asia conflict disruption delaying movement of OTC pain management orders from India.
  • Mix-related margin dilution from faster-growing, lower-margin startup businesses, which management acknowledges is difficult to offset through pricing alone.
  • Execution risk on the transformation initiatives and on integrating multiple acquired businesses.
  • Seasonality and weather variability affecting talc and pain management categories.
  • International business recovery dependent on resolving supply reinstatement to Middle East markets.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Margins. Management stated it does not see margin pressure going forward, expects to more than offset the absolute increase in input cost during the financial year through further pricing actions, and expressed confidence that margins will not come down. Observed performance: gross margin contracted 360 basis points to 65.8% in Q1 FY27, with EBITDA growing 6% to ₹226 crore and PBT growing 4% to ₹195 crore — slower than the 15% revenue growth.

International business. Management said the decline is now done and expects significant growth in Q3 and Q4 FY27 after realigning international strategies. Observed performance: international business declined 12% in Q1 FY27.

Talc. Management said the company would recover and return to FY25 numbers, with substantially high numbers in the current quarter and significantly high numbers for the full year. Observed performance: the talc portfolio delivered high single-digit growth in Q1 FY27.

Kesh King. Management expects double-digit growth for the full year, noting degrowth has been arrested. Observed performance: mid-single-digit growth in Q1 FY27.

Strategic investments. Management expects the portfolio to end the year at ₹750–800 crore, growing 20–30% year-on-year, with the 61% like-to-like growth described as sustainable and the quarterly figure broadly annualisable. Observed performance: ₹160 crore in Q1 FY27, with Axiom carrying some juice-related seasonality and TMC and Brillare facing high season in the next two quarters. Management targets high single-digit EBITDA margin for the vertical at around the three-year point.

Tax rate. Management guided to approximately 25–26% for the year. Observed performance: Q1 FY27 effective tax rate was around 28–29%.

Q2 FY27. Management expects Q2 FY27 to benefit from healthy demand trends across the portfolio and continued momentum in strategic investment businesses, and states the company is well-positioned to deliver sustained and profitable growth through the remainder of FY27.

Broker Narrative

Early reports focused on digital disruption risks (liquidity, customer acquisition costs, inflation, venture funding) offset by internet/e-commerce tailwinds, while later reports shifted to margin compression, tax-rate spikes, and tepid category growth, with the D2C opportunity and international expansion remaining persistent optimists. The narrative evolved from macro/structural fears to company-specific operating headwinds as actual returns turned sharply negative.

Fears that came true

  • Higher inflation impacting unit economics materialized as gross margin contracted 360 bps YoY and input costs rose sharply with ~200 bps impact from West Asia conflict and ~160 bps from mix shift.
  • Venture funding drying up with rising interest rates was compounded by a 16.5 ppt YoY tax rate jump to 28.4% and an expected FY27E rate of 25-26%, leading to PAT decline and EPS cuts.
  • Lower liquidity fears were validated as the stock delivered a -1.2% actual return on the first Buy call and subsequent reports showed sustained negative returns with multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.

Optimism that failed

  • Post-pandemic digital adoption acceleration failed to protect margins as D2C sales grew 61% YoY but contributed to a mix shift toward lower-margin startups, contracting EBITDA margin 193 bps.
  • Robust demand in niche consumer areas did not prevent skincare (+3% YoY) and healthcare (+2% YoY) from turning tepid and IBD sales from declining 12% YoY.
  • International business growth expectations were deferred to Q3/Q4 and the IBD double-digit growth projection was pushed to 2Q27, failing to materialize within earlier predicted windows.
  • Hair and scalp care’s 11% YoY growth and Navratna’s double-digit performance could not offset the broader margin and profit deterioration, leaving multiple predicted returns unachieved.
