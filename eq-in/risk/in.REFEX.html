Refex Industries: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Discontinued operations weighing on consolidated results. The Board approved discontinuation of the Power Trading and Refrigerant Gases business segments to concentrate on core, higher-growth businesses. These segments are classified as discontinued operations under Ind AS 105, with results disclosed separately. In Q1 FY27, the standalone discontinued operations recorded a loss before tax of ₹32.98 lakh and a net loss of ₹24.68 lakh; on a consolidated basis, discontinued operations (including Green Mobility) recorded a loss before tax of ₹1,393.77 lakh and a net loss of ₹997.63 lakh for the quarter. For FY26, standalone discontinued operations recorded a net loss of ₹132.40 lakh, while consolidated discontinued operations recorded a net loss of ₹3,866.18 lakh.

Green Mobility segment losses. On a consolidated basis, the Green Mobility segment reported a segment loss (EBIT) of ₹1,360.79 lakh in Q1 FY27, compared with a loss of ₹862.47 lakh in Q1 FY26 and ₹3,823.51 lakh for FY26. The Windpower segment reported a segment loss of ₹34.25 lakh in Q1 FY27, against a loss of ₹373.21 lakh in Q1 FY26, though it recorded a profit of ₹222.69 lakh for FY26.

Lapse of convertible warrants. Warrants allotted on November 07, 2024 (1,11,70,000 warrants at ₹468 each) lapsed upon expiry of the exercise period ending May 06, 2026, as the balance 75% consideration was not received. The upfront amount of ₹30,68,90,000 was forfeited in accordance with SEBI ICDR Regulations.

Working capital absorption. Standalone cash flow for FY26 shows changes in working capital of negative ₹310.5 crore, with net cash generated from operating activities of only ₹30.8 crore. Contract assets rose sharply to ₹734.1 crore as at March 31, 2026 from ₹171.4 crore a year earlier, and trade receivables increased to ₹737.3 crore from ₹673.6 crore. Current borrowings rose to ₹132.8 crore from ₹60.0 crore, and current tax liabilities increased to ₹69.7 crore from ₹4.5 crore.

Revenue decline at the full-year level. Standalone continuing-operations revenue for FY26 was ₹2,039.2 crore, down 9.7% from ₹2,259.4 crore in FY25, even as profitability improved.

Regulatory compliance burden on customers. Thermal power plants face mandatory 100% ash utilization targets under the MoEF & CC notification dated December 31, 2021 and amendment dated December 30, 2022, with non-compliance penalties of ₹1,000 per ton. Deadlines vary by current utilization levels (three to five years), alongside a ten-year legacy ash cleanup roadmap effective April 1, 2022.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Standalone continuing-operations revenue rose 76.4% year-on-year to ₹619.3 crore; EBITDA rose 166.0% to ₹105.4 crore (17.0% margin); PAT rose 122.8% to ₹73.6 crore (11.9% margin). The company attributed profitability improvement to better business mix, operating efficiency and economies of scale.

Ash & Coal Handling order book. The business secured new orders worth ₹279 crore during the quarter, with an order book of ₹1,635 crore as at June 30, 2026. The company describes itself as the largest organized player in India, with 2,000+ owned or leased fleets, 70,000+ MT of ash handled per day, 40+ plants serviced, and 50+ million MT of ash utilized over eight years. Revenue CAGR from FY22 to FY26 is stated at 46.4% and PAT CAGR at 52.56%.

Policy-driven demand. Electricity demand is growing at 9%, with coal’s contribution projected to stabilize at 50% of the energy mix. Thermal capacity additions of 50 GW are projected over the next five to seven years, plus expected additions of 1,800 MW from companies taken over from NCLT. The integrated ash handling model combining O&M of the ash handling plant with complete ash utilization is positioned as a way to lock in multi-year tie-ups and exclusivity.

Wind business transition to execution. The company delivered its first 5.3 MW wind turbine at Torrent’s Koppal project and started deliveries across other customers. The Silvassa factory has been commissioned, ALMM listing for the 5.3 MW GWH turbine is complete, and technology teams are undergoing localization training. Order book exceeds 400 MW, with a confirmed order book of ₹1,860 crore and a 1.5 GW order. The company targets ~5 GW annual production capacity over the next five years. Tailwinds cited include a GST reduction on wind equipment from 12% to 5%, a national target of 60% non-fossil installed capacity by 2035, an MNRE wind capacity target of 140–150 GW by 2030, and India’s 500 GW non-fossil fuel capacity target.

Demerger to unlock value. The composite scheme provides for amalgamation of RGML into RIL, followed by demerger of the Green Mobility Business Undertaking into RML, with RML shares issued to RIL shareholders at a 1:1 swap ratio and listed as an independent entity. Stated benefits include focused management, independent access to investors and lenders, and sharper capital market access. BSE and NSE observation letters with “No Adverse Observations” were received on March 16, 2026; the NCLT application was filed on March 26, 2026; and the NCLT directed meetings of equity shareholders, secured creditors and unsecured creditors for August 05, 2026.

Financial position. The company describes a net debt-free balance sheet, with FY26 ROCE of ~22.0% and ROE of 15.6% (standalone). Shareholder funds rose to ₹1,571.7 crore as at March 31, 2026 from ₹1,250.2 crore a year earlier.

3. Key Risks

Scheme approval risk. The Scheme remains subject to subsequent sanction by the NCLT and other regulatory approvals. The equity shareholders’ meeting was held on August 05, 2026 through VC/OAVM with e-voting; the secured and unsecured creditors’ meetings were held physically the same day with postal ballot voting. The resolution requires approval by a majority in number representing three-fourths in value of secured creditors present and voting under Section 230(6).

Execution risk in wind. The wind business is described as having transitioned from development into active execution, with turbine deliveries progressing and localization training underway. Segment results show the Windpower segment at a loss in Q1 FY27.

Continued losses in mobility. The Green Mobility segment has recorded losses in each period presented, including ₹3,823.51 lakh for FY26 and ₹1,360.79 lakh in Q1 FY27.

Working capital and receivables. The sharp increase in contract assets and trade receivables, alongside negative working capital changes, represents a material use of cash.

Customer compliance dependence. The ash handling opportunity is tied to regulatory mandates on thermal power plants; the pace of customer compliance and capacity additions affects demand.

Discontinued operations drag. The Refrigerant Gases and Power Trading segments continue to be reported as discontinued operations with associated losses.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Demerger timeline. Management stated in the Q1 FY27 investor presentation that the demerger process “continues to advance as planned,” with the Equity Shareholders’ Meeting scheduled for August 5, 2026 following NCLT approval to convene meetings. Observed: the NCLT order dated June 18, 2026 directed meetings of equity shareholders, secured creditors and unsecured creditors on August 05, 2026; the equity shareholders’ meeting was held on that date through VC/OAVM with remote e-voting from August 02–04, 2026, and the creditors’ meetings were held physically the same day. The scheme remains pending NCLT sanction.

Wind business execution. Management stated the wind business had “successfully transitioned from development phase into active execution,” marking “the start of a new growth engine.” Observed: the first 5.3 MW turbine was delivered at Torrent’s Koppal project, deliveries started across other customers, the Silvassa factory was commissioned, and ALMM listing was completed. The Windpower segment, however, reported a segment loss of ₹34.25 lakh in Q1 FY27.

Profitability drivers. Management attributed improved profitability to better business mix, operating efficiency and economies of scale. Observed: standalone EBITDA margin rose to 17.0% in Q1 FY27 from 11.3% in Q1 FY26, and gross profit margin rose to 20.0% from 15.5%.

Return ratios. Management stated that as the wind business scales, it expects it to “strengthen our return ratios and enhance the overall quality of our earnings.” Observed: FY26 standalone ROCE is stated at ~22.0% and ROE at 15.6%; Q1 FY27 annualized figures are not provided for comparison.

Order book growth. Management cited a growing order book supporting revenue. Observed: Ash & Coal Handling secured new orders worth ₹279 crore in the quarter, with order book at ₹1,635 crore as at June 30, 2026; the wind confirmed order book is stated at ₹1,860 crore with a 1.5 GW order.

Capital structure. Management describes a net debt-free balance sheet providing financial flexibility. Observed: standalone borrowings (non-current plus current) stood at ₹158.9 crore as at March 31, 2026, up from ₹96.8 crore a year earlier, alongside cash and cash equivalents of ₹180.5 crore and bank balances of ₹61.0 crore.
