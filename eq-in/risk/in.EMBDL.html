Embassy Developments Limited (EMBDL) – Recent Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Reported losses and completion-based accounting. For Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026), revenue from operations was INR217 crores against INR681 crores in Q1 FY26, total income was INR241 crores versus INR694 crores, EBITDA was negative INR106 crores versus positive INR2 crores, and the net loss was INR234 crores versus a net loss of INR166 crores a year earlier. Management attributes the gap between reported results and operating performance to the accounting requirement that residential revenue is recognized only on project completion and handover, creating a timing difference versus presales and collections.

Negative operating cash flow in Q1. The company began the year with roughly INR1,165 crores of cash and recorded negative operating cash flow of about INR285 crores in Q1, which the CFO linked to the absence of launches in the quarter and the fact that most projects were launched in Q3/Q4 of the prior year, with collections following roughly six months later.

Launch slippage and approval friction in Bengaluru. No new projects were launched in Q1. Management described Bengaluru as “a little problematic” because the planning authority (GBA) had not been able to convene meetings following a change in Chief Minister, though Aditya Virwani did not attribute the company’s own non-launching to this. Embassy One North was pushed to Q2 after a TDR FSI issue was resolved and a building plan obtained in Q1; the delay was described as intentional, partly because Embassy REIT terminated Four Seasons and the company terminated the Four Seasons branding on the residences. Embassy Knowledge Park was described as close to receiving its building plan. Management acknowledged that some of the remaining Bengaluru launches targeted for Q3 could spill into Q4.

High-cost debt. Gross institutional debt was approximately INR4,500 crores as of June 30, 2026, with cash and equivalents of approximately INR1,200 crores, giving net institutional debt of approximately INR3,300 crores and net debt to equity of 0.35x. The average cost of debt was stated at around 14%, with Blackstone debt being serviced at 18%, accrued and added to gross debt rather than paid out. Outstanding shareholder debt was INR1,063 crores — INR700 crores from Blackstone and INR363 crores from Embassy Group.

Legacy Indiabulls portfolio and brand perception. Management discussed the work of taking over erstwhile Indiabulls projects, including Panvel (described as a “ghost site” with 40 laborers on site, now with 1,500), and completing projects such as Savroli and 109 Gurgaon Phase 1 without the Embassy brand. Some landlords in Gurgaon have asked whether the company works with Indiabulls; management said this is a matter of education and time, attributing confusion to the transaction being technically structured as a merger rather than a takeover.

Nashik land and land bank monetization. Of the 3,000-plus acre land bank, over 1,400 acres are in Nashik, where the company is seeking an amicable solution with MIDC alongside a debonding exercise involving five government agencies. Sachin Shah estimated the debonding process would take another six to nine months, noted that a sublessee of 80-plus acres has intervened in the application, and said the legal process would move slowly. Management stated the broader land bank is not the immediate priority because scattered parcels require capital for land assembly and access, and that it will be addressed when the company has sufficient surplus.

Legal matter. Canara Bank has appealed to the Supreme Court against the NCLAT order dated May 4, 2026, which was in favour of the Company. No interim order, stay, or other interim relief has been granted against the NCLAT order, which remains in force. The matter is listed for hearing on September 24, 2026. The Company stated it does not expect financial implications or similar claims based on the present status and remains confident of its stand.

General risk factors. The company cites economic and political conditions in India and abroad, volatility in interest rates and securities markets, new regulations and government policies, the general state of the Indian economy, and management’s ability to implement strategy as risks affecting business plans, financial condition, and growth prospects.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 operating momentum. Presales for Q1 FY27 were INR868 crores, up 338% year-on-year, and collections rose 54% to INR496 crores. Nearly 60% of inventory launched in FY26 has been sold, with approximately 72% of launch inventory sold within six months in Bengaluru. The OC-received portfolio is approximately 98% cumulatively sold, and the ongoing development portfolio of 11 assets is approximately 70% sold by saleable area.

Large available-for-sale portfolio and launch pipeline. The company entered FY27 with approximately INR10,500 crores of ongoing inventory, around INR400 crores of completed OC-received inventory, and an INR19,400 crores pipeline of fresh launches. The FY27 pipeline comprises 9 owned projects (GDV of INR13,300 crores) and 2 development management projects (INR6,000-plus crores GDV). Four of the 11 projects were expected in Q2. Named launches include Embassy One North tower in Bengaluru (400,000 sq ft, ~INR1,400 crores GDV), Embassy Knowledge Park villas and apartments in North Bengaluru (80-plus acres, ~INR4,450 crores combined GDV), the front parcel at Embassy Springs (1.7 million sq ft, ~INR1,900 crores GDV), the Whitefield JDA project (68.5% JDA share, 1.7 million sq ft, ~INR2,000 crores GDV), Embassy Hub plot A in Hebbal (1.2 million sq ft, ~INR2,100 crores GDV, 91% share), and 109 Commercial Phase 2 in Gurgaon (INR800 crores GDV, 0.5 million sq ft). RERA approval was received for Embassy Terazza, the Juhu development management project, in the week before the call.

Beyond-FY27 pipeline and land bank. A development pipeline of roughly 20.3 million sq ft with estimated GDV of approximately INR23,470 crores exists beyond 2027, alongside a fully paid 3,000-plus acre land bank. Cumulative unsold inventory across launched and under-construction residential and commercial launches is INR13,630 crores.

Approvals and execution milestones. Embassy One 09 in Gurgaon and 5 towers at Golf City Savroli received occupancy certificates during the quarter. Embassy Citadel in Mumbai has approval for all 81 floors upfront, which management described as a significant differentiator in a market where approvals are typically staged, and Leighton was appointed as civil contractor. Embassy Paradiso at Embassy Springs and the balance towers of Golf City Savroli are nearing completion and slated for OC in FY27; Embassy Edge and Embassy East Avenue are progressing toward completion in FY28 and are substantially sold.

Supportive demand environment. Management described demand as healthy across premium and luxury residential segments in core markets, with customers favouring developers with proven track records and trust, and expressed the view that India is in a multi-decade housing cycle with consolidation toward the strongest developers. The company’s strategic focus spans Bengaluru, the Mumbai Metropolitan Region, and the National Capital Region, with a presence in Chennai and Indore and a portfolio across mid-income, premium, and luxury segments, supported by the Embassy Group’s three-decade track record of delivering and managing more than 100 million sq ft.

Balance sheet actions. The Board approved a preferential allotment of 3,25,18,900 convertible warrants to Embassy Property Developments Private Limited at an exercise price of INR111.51 per warrant, aggregating up to INR362.62 crores, subject to shareholder approval. Proceeds are earmarked for repayment of outstanding shareholder debt owed to Embassy Group, which would reduce Embassy Group’s debt to nil. The promoter group voluntarily committed to convert all warrants within six months, against the maximum permitted 18 months. The price represents approximately an 80% premium over the minimum price determined under SEBI ICDR Regulations and matches the April–May 2024 preferential issue price. Promoter Group shareholding would rise from 42.65% to 43.96% on full conversion. The company is also in talks with Blackstone about converting its portion of debt to equity.

Leadership additions. The Board approved the appointment of Mr. Neel Virwani as senior management personnel effective October 1, 2026, subject to shareholder approval, to provide full-time senior oversight based in Mumbai given the expanded portfolio including Embassy Citadel, Worli. The Board recommended the re-appointment of Mr. Jitendra Virwani as Chairman and Non-Executive Director retiring by rotation. At the 20th AGM held September 8, 2026, shareholders approved all six agenda items, including adoption of financial statements, Mr. Jitendra Virwani’s re-appointment, cost auditor remuneration, revision in remuneration for CFO Mr. Rajesh Kaimal, Mr. Neel Virwani’s appointment as Chief Business Officer, and the preferential issue of warrants.

New launch announcement. Embassy Developments announced the launch of Embassy Origins, an approximately 85-acre residential development in North Bengaluru featuring Embassy Riverine (217 villas) and Embassy South Reserve (855 apartments), with a combined GDV of approximately INR4,500 crore.

3. Key Risks

  • Reported earnings remain subject to completion-based revenue recognition, so operating performance and reported financials can diverge significantly; management expects this timing difference to persist.
  • The company reported a net loss of INR234 crores in Q1 FY27 and negative operating cash flow of approximately INR285 crores, with collections dependent on construction progress and milestone-linked inflows.
  • Launch execution is exposed to approval timelines, including Bengaluru planning authority meetings and building plan approvals; management explicitly flagged quarterly variability in launches and possible spillover of Q3 launches into Q4.
  • Cost of debt is elevated at approximately 14% average, with Blackstone debt at 18% accruing into gross debt; the company’s plan to refinance and reduce debt depends on collections and project cash flows materializing.
  • The Nashik land matter with MIDC and the debonding process carry legal and timing uncertainty, with a sublessee intervention noted.
  • The Canara Bank appeal before the Supreme Court is pending, with a hearing listed for September 24, 2026; no stay has been granted against the NCLAT order in the company’s favour.
  • Legacy Indiabulls projects require completion and rectification, and brand perception in some markets such as Gurgaon may take time to align with the Embassy identity.
  • General risks cited include economic and political conditions in India and abroad, interest rate and securities market volatility, regulatory and policy changes, the state of the Indian economy, and management’s ability to implement strategy.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

FY27 guidance. Management reiterated guidance of INR6,000 crores in presales from owned developments, INR2,000 crores from development management projects, and approximately INR3,000 crores of collections. The FY27 launch pipeline is INR19,400 crores of GDV across 11 projects, with 4 expected in Q2 and the remainder targeted for Q3 with possible spillover into Q4.

Observed performance against guidance. Q1 presales of INR868 crores and collections of INR496 crores were recorded with no new launches in the quarter, which management described as deliberate. Collections of INR496 crores in Q1 compare with the full-year guidance of approximately INR3,000 crores; the CFO stated the company remains on track for that guidance and expects collections to accelerate from Q2 onward as projects launched in Q3/Q4 FY26 enter their collection cycles. Construction spend in Q1 was INR276 crores, approximately 56% of collections.

Collection trajectory commentary. Management stated that most of the INR3,000 crores of FY27 collections is expected from already-launched projects (Greenshore, Verde, Eden and others launched in the second half of FY26), with new launches contributing roughly 10–20% in Q2, 10–20% in Q3, and about 10% in Q4. Aditya Virwani said he feels “very, very strong” about the INR3,000 crores and that the company could potentially better it, with more accuracy expected by Q3. He described a collection-to-presales ratio of about 35% last year, a targeted 50% this year (INR3,000 crores against INR6,000 crores of owned presales), and an industry average of 70%, with an inflection point expected around April–May of the next calendar year as projects such as Greenshore, Verde, and Citadel come out of the ground and slab cycles begin.

Debt trajectory commentary. Management indicated the priority is to refinance high-cost debt first, then reduce overall debt as projects generate surplus cash beyond construction spend. The CFO said the company targets net debt to equity in the range of 0.3x to 0.35x. Aditya Virwani said the company expects to refinance the whole portfolio around March–April next year, with cost of debt coming down first and overall debt reducing thereafter.

Accounting change exploration. In response to an investor suggestion, the CFO said the company is actively exploring adoption of a percentage completion method and will review it over the next couple of quarters and make changes if needed. He also explained that accounting standards do not permit provisioning for anticipated future costs without a reason, in response to a question about pre-booking expected losses. On the approximately INR2,500 crores of goodwill arising from reverse merger accounting, the CFO explained it reflects the share price at the time versus project value, is tested for impairment every six months, and benefits from rising Indian land prices.

Forward-looking statement policy. The company states it is under no obligation to update forward-looking statements or to inform recipients of changes or developments arising after the date of the press release.

Broker Narrative

The broker started with a Buy in July 2023, acknowledging strong Q1FY24 operating metrics and leasing momentum but flagging risks around office leasing recovery, vacancy, and rising lease expiries. By October 2024, the call had softened to Add, with the same core headwinds persisting—slower office leasing, higher vacancy, and higher expiries—while adding interest-cost pressure. The narrative shifted from expecting H2FY24 demand triggers to reduce vacancy to a more cautious, extended recovery timeline. Actual returns flipped from large successes in 2023 to repeated disappointments through 2024.

Fears that came true

  • The early fear of slower recovery in office leasing by GCCs materialized, as the last report still flagged slower office leasing and the stock saw negative actual returns on every 2024 call.
  • Higher portfolio vacancy levels persisted, with the last report continuing to cite higher vacancy as a headwind despite a small QoQ occupancy improvement to 87%.
  • Scheduled lease expiries rose as feared: the early FY24 expiry increase to 2.8msf was followed by FY25 expiries climbing to 3.5msf due to fresh exits and early renewals.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected revival in GCC leasing decisions in H2FY24 did not materialize, as slower office leasing remained a persistent headwind through the last report.
  • The expectation that SEZ denotification and demand triggers would reduce portfolio vacancy by Mar’24 failed, since vacancy levels were still flagged as high and occupancy only reached 87% well after that timeframe.
