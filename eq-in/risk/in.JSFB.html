Jana Small Finance Bank — Q1 FY27 Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Deposit mobilisation and pricing. Total deposits were broadly flat quarter-on-quarter (₹35,784 crore at 31 March 2026 to ₹35,756 crore at 30 June 2026), even as they grew 21.5% year-on-year. Management attributed the flat quarter to a deliberate reduction in bulk deposits (down ~6% q-o-q) alongside retail term deposit growth of 2.3% q-o-q and CASA growth of 7.1% q-o-q. April and May were described as difficult months from a liquidity perspective, with deposit prices hardening; the bank raised deposit rates in June. Management stated the opportunity to reduce cost of funds further is now over and expects cost of funds to remain around 7.3%–7.4%.

Micro LAP contraction. Micro LAP declined 1.7% q-o-q and grew only 4.6% y-o-y as the bank shifted the sourcing model toward direct sourcing. Management described this as a model transition issue rather than a book-performance issue and expects a return to positive growth from Q2.

Term loans to NBFCs. This book contracted 3.7% y-o-y and 1.2% q-o-q by design, and management expects it to remain broadly flat through the year.

Seasonal SMA increase. Overall SMA rose to 3.9% as at 30 June 2026 from 3.5% as at 31 March 2026. Management characterised this as seasonal in nature, linked to Q1 collection focus and lower recovery activity in the quarter.

Promoter-level rating action. JHL and JCL, the promoters, had a technical default on NCD payment after extending the tenor by six months to allow time to sell their stake. India Ratings downgraded JHL and JCL, and the bank was placed under ‘watch’ consequent to that action. CARE Ratings retained the bank’s rating with no action taken. JHL’s stake has fallen to 16.9% from a peak of 44%; JHL has not contributed capital to the bank since June 2022, has no Board member on the bank, and there is no cross-default linkage between JSFB debt and JHL/JCL debt. JHL and JCL will apply for de-promoterisation to SEBI and RBI once the stake falls below 9.99%.

Government deposit loss not yet recovered. CASA-linked government deposits lost in Q4 FY26 had not returned as of the call. The relevant state government has since issued an empanelment list that includes the bank, and management expects flows to resume, with some fixed deposits already received, though the pace is difficult to predict.

Other income decline. Other income fell to ₹227 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹266 crore in both Q1 FY26 and Q4 FY26. This included lower insurance commission (₹28 crore vs ₹50 crore, attributed to a change in the insurance programme) and lower profit on sale of investments (₹7 crore vs ₹49 crore, with a one-off ₹41 crore in Q1 FY26). Bad debt recoveries also fell to ₹12 crore from ₹39 crore in Q4 FY26.

Cost-to-income ratio. The cost-to-income ratio was 67.0% in Q1 FY27, marginally above 66.6% in Q4 FY26 and 66.1% in Q1 FY26. Management guided that it will not fall below 60% and should land in the 63%–65% range during the year.

External risk factors monitored. Management stated it was closely monitoring the geopolitical situation and El Nino weather developments, with no material impact observed at the time of the call. The MFI portfolio, about 27% of the total book, is monitored through early warning indicators, stress testing and vintage curves, with calibrated underwriting and tighter portfolio monitoring available as responses.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Profitability normalisation. PAT was ₹155 crore in Q1 FY27, up 52% y-o-y from ₹102 crore and up from ₹140 crore in Q4 FY26. ROA was 1.4% (vs 1.3% in Q4 FY26 and 1.1% in Q1 FY26) and ROE was 13.6% (vs 12.8% and 9.8% respectively). Management described ROA and ROE as having returned to normalisation after two years of struggle.

Margin recovery. NIM improved to 7.5% in Q1 FY27 from 7.2% in Q4 FY26 and 6.6% in Q1 FY26. Management stated NIMs are back to where they would have been absent the microfinance stress, and expects them to remain steady around that level. Cost of funds declined to 7.4% from 7.97% in Q1 FY26, a ~50 bps y-o-y reduction.

Asset growth led by secured products. GLP grew 25.7% y-o-y and 3.6% q-o-q to ₹37,612 crore. Secured assets grew 28.8% y-o-y and 3.9% q-o-q, taking the secured share to 72.8%. Within secured: Gold loans grew 112.5% y-o-y and 13.5% q-o-q; vehicle loans 78.1% y-o-y and 12.6% q-o-q; affordable housing 29.1% y-o-y; MSME 27.4% y-o-y. Management expects the secured share to continue rising toward 80% over time, given five secured product lines versus one unsecured line.

Unsecured book stabilisation. Unsecured advances grew 17.9% y-o-y and 2.8% q-o-q to ₹10,240 crore. Slippages in Q1 FY27 were 43% lower than Q1 FY26 and 13% lower q-o-q; unsecured slippages fell from ₹155 crore to ₹125 crore q-o-q, a ~20% reduction. Collection efficiency has been above 99% B0 consistently for four months, and BC book B0 collections have crossed 99% since January 2026. Management targets 10%–12% y-o-y growth for the MFI portfolio in FY27.

Guarantee cover on unsecured. ~80% of the unsecured book (79.8%) is covered under guarantee programmes (CGFMU/CGTMSE). Of ₹214 crore net NPA in unsecured, ₹196 crore is under the guarantee programme, leaving ₹18 crore uncovered. The bank expects to claim ₹65 crore by Q3 FY27. Management framed the guarantee commission (about 1%) as now part of BAU costs, estimated at ₹80–100 crore for the year, providing a floor to future credit losses.

Credit cost stability. Net credit cost was 0.45% in Q1 FY27, matching Q4 FY26, which was the best quarter of the prior year. Management expects 0.45% to be maintained at minimum in the remaining quarters. Provisions and contingencies fell to ₹178 crore from ₹195 crore in Q4 FY26.

Asset quality improvement. GNPA was 2.24% and NNPA 0.85% on GLP, versus 2.33% and 0.87% in Q4 FY26 and 2.76% and 0.89% in Q1 FY26. Additions to gross NPA declined to ₹291 crore from ₹334 crore in Q4 FY26 and ₹515 crore in Q1 FY26. PCR was 62.64%.

CASA and retail deposit franchise. CASA grew 31.3% y-o-y and 7.1% q-o-q to ₹6,871 crore; the CASA ratio rose to 19.2% from 17.9% in Q4 FY26. Retail deposits rose to 64.9% of total deposits from 62.6% in Q4 FY26 and 60.2% in Q1 FY26. Retail term deposits grew 30.7% y-o-y and 2.3% q-o-q. 96.6% of retail deposits and 83% of bulk deposits are contracted at one year and above. Management targets a CASA ratio of close to 20% for the year.

Capital raise. Tier-1 capital of ₹728 crore was approved via share warrants on a preferential route; ₹103 crore was received in June 2026, with 75% to be received over 18 months if the conversion option is exercised. TVS Venu Group awaits RBI approval for a 9.99% stake. The warrant issue increased the capital adequacy ratio by 43 bps as at 30 June 2026. CAR was 20.2% with Tier-1 CRAR of 18.3%.

Cost discipline. Opex growth was nominal at 1.5% q-o-q (₹666 crore to ₹676 crore), with headcount flat from the previous quarter. Management cited AI and RPA-led automation showing early productivity benefits, and a decline in MFI collections staffing.

Branch and product expansion. FY27 plans include 8 new branches, 30 splits and 40 relocations (78 total), of which 7 were executed in Q1. Gold loan branches number about 568–570 of 820 total branches, with 50–75 more branches planned over the next two quarters. Credit Line on UPI is to go live in Q2 after more than a quarter of testing; loans against shares will also launch in Q2 following RBI approval of the ₹1 crore limit; Nostro setup was expected in July, with a possible small FCNR(B) offering in August or September.

Used car business. Launched October 2025, now at ~₹45 crore monthly disbursement across ~50 cities, with potential to expand to over 240 cities where affordable housing and Micro LAP are present. No BT or refinance programme has been launched.

Branding. The RCB official banking partnership during IPL, including RCB’s championship win, was cited as contributing to strong Q1 CASA growth and as one of the bank’s best programmes, with interest in similar future programmes.

Anchor bank strategy. Average active products per customer was 3.9 (6.0 including property and life insurance). Gold penetration rose to 3% from 2% a year earlier; two-wheeler penetration reached 1.1%.

3. Key Risks

  • Unsecured/MFI credit risk: MFI is the bank’s single unsecured product line and about 27% of the total book. Management noted that historical missteps (MFI stress, COVID) have always been in unsecured. The guarantee programme is presented as a floor to future losses, but claim timing remains uncertain.
  • Guarantee claim timing risk: Of ₹196 crore guaranteed net NPA, only ₹65 crore is expected to be claimed by Q3 FY27, with the balance claimed subsequently. Recovery timing is the open question.
  • Deposit pricing and liquidity risk: Deposit prices hardened in April–May; the bank raised rates in June. Cost of funds is expected to plateau at 7.3%–7.4%, limiting further margin expansion from funding costs.
  • Promoter-related risk: The India Ratings action on JHL/JCL placed the bank under ‘watch’, though CARE took no action and management described the situation as BAU with no cross-default linkage or common Board members.
  • Geopolitical and weather risk: Potential impact from prolonged geopolitical conflict or El Nino deterioration on the portfolio, particularly MFI, though no material impact was observed at the time of the call.
  • Gold price risk: Gold prices witnessed some correction. The portfolio LTV is 64% on average, within regulatory limits, with daily price feeds and margin call mechanisms in place; GNPA in gold is expected to remain around 0.5% and be fully covered by collateral realisation.
  • Slippage trajectory risk: Management expects Q2 slippages to be flattish to Q1, possibly with a marginal uptick, offset by better recoveries, with small drops in Q3 and Q4.
  • Cost-to-income risk: The ratio remains elevated at 67.0% and is guided to stay above 60%.
  • Concentration in secured mix transition: The shift from 72.8% toward 80% secured depends on continued growth in affordable housing, MSME, vehicle, gold and Micro LAP, with Micro LAP currently contracting.

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from a cautious/neutral assessment dominated by liability-side stress (deposit costs, weak CASA, high CD ratio, elevated C/I) to a bullish Buy call after Q1FY27 delivered strong NIM expansion, lower credit costs, secured-book growth and a capital raise. Persistent themes were opex/C/I discipline, asset quality and the shift to a secured book. The key change was the replacement of universal-bank/deposit-franchise worries with newer concerns around promoter NCD default, guarantee-programme recoveries and MSME/geopolitical risks, while growth guidance stayed intact.

Fears that came true

  • The expected yield compression from a higher secured-book mix materialised, with NIM at 7.5% in Q1FY27 versus 8% in FY24.
  • The elevated C/I and opex pressure flagged in FY24 persisted, as the last report still had management guiding for a C/I decline only by FY27-end.
  • The weak CASA/deposit-mix and high CD-ratio risks were followed by negative actual returns of -13.6% after the first report and -15.4% (DISAPPOINTMENT) after the Add call.

Optimism that failed

  • The RoA/RoE guidance of 1.8-2% and 19-21% failed to materialise by Q1FY27, when actual RoA was 1.4% and RoE was 13.6%.
  • The expectation of stable NIMs around 8% in FY25 did not hold, as Q1FY27 NIM was 7.5%.
