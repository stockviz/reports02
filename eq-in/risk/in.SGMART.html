SG Mart Limited — Q1 FY27 Corporate Announcements Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Geopolitical volatility and input cost uncertainty. Management flagged that the operating environment “remained influenced by global geopolitical developments and input cost volatility.” On the call, Anubhav Gupta noted that until roughly ten days before the July 20 call, there was a view that the Iran–U.S. conflict was ending, but oil prices had again started to shoot up, which would impact commodities including steel. He observed that in March, businesses suffered from shortages of fuel, gas, petrol and diesel, and that a repeat would hurt the broader business environment. He estimated it would take another two to three weeks before volatility starts hurting businesses, and that excessive volatility would impact sales and customer industries generally, not SG Mart specifically.

Seasonality in service centre volumes. Q1 FY27 service centre volume was 160,000 tons versus 189,000 tons in Q4 FY26 — a quarter-on-quarter dip attributed to seasonality. Management stressed that year-on-year, volumes rose from 121,000 tons in Q1 FY26 to 160,000 tons in Q1 FY27, and that Q2 volumes should pick up sequentially.

Working capital pressure. Net working capital days rose to 27 in Q1 FY27 from 20 in Q4 FY26 and 15 in Q1 FY26. Trade payables fell to ₹1,643 Mn from ₹3,650 Mn at FY26 end, and other current assets rose to ₹2,111 Mn from ₹1,887 Mn. Management explained this as advances paid to steel mills to secure credible supply amid geopolitical turbulence, plus initial inventory needs of new business verticals. Inventory value fell from ₹2,836 Mn to ₹2,090 Mn despite higher steel prices.

Execution and scale-up demands. The company plans roughly ₹1,500 crores of capex over two to three years, including 15–18 additional service centres at about ₹50 crores each and a backward integration line at Raipur. Each service centre takes 9–15 months to establish, depending on land clearance. Management acknowledged that achieving the stated 50% CAGR target depends on execution across four verticals, though it characterized execution risk as low given identified land parcels, installed capacity, and group experience.

Customer concentration in solar structures. While service centres, steel profiles and accessories have wide customer bases, the solar structures business caters to a limited set of top 20–30 EPC companies and IPPs. Management noted this segment currently contributes little to overall revenue.

Margin mix sensitivity. Management declined to guide on EBITDA percentage quarter-on-quarter, noting that if the lower-margin service centre business (₹1,800–2,000/ton) picks up, blended EBITDA per ton may decline even as absolute EBITDA grows.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Business model transition from trading to manufacturing. Q1 FY27 was described as the second consecutive quarter of sustained revenue and profitability. The model now rests on five pillars: manufacturing, branding, distribution, a network of service centres, and an online marketplace. Ten products have been launched across four categories (service centre products, steel profiles, renewable structures, accessories), with seven more in the pipeline for the next two quarters.

Segment performance in Q1 FY27. Service centres: 160,000 tons volume with profitability intact at ~₹2,000/ton. Steel profiles: 18,000 tons (annualized run rate ~75,000 tons) against installed capacity of 200,000 tons. Renewable structures: ~11,000 tons (annualized ~50,000 tons) against ~200,000 tons capacity. EBITDA per ton across steel profiles and renewables is ₹3,000–4,000, expected to rise to ₹6,000–7,000 after backward integration.

Service centre expansion. Seven service centres are operational (Pune, Bangalore, Dujana, Raipur, Dubai, Indore, Ahmedabad — with Ahmedabad and Indore on lease). Plans call for adding five per year to reach 25 by 2029. Each centre targets ~8,000 tons/month (~100,000 tons/year), ~₹500 crores annual revenue, ~₹20 crores EBITDA on ₹75–80 crores capital employed. Management sees ROCE potentially reaching 35% per centre once coated steel products are added.

Backward integration. Land acquired in Raipur; construction begun and some machinery ordered. The line will cover cold rolling plus metal coating (zinc, zinc-aluminium, zinc-aluminium-magnesium), expected operational in about 18 months. This is expected to improve margins by ₹3,000–4,000/ton and reduce working capital days to 20–25 within two years.

Financial strength. ROCE annualized at 23% for Q1 FY27 with net cash of ~₹690 crores. Q1 capex was ₹90 crores; full-year capex guided at ₹400–500 crores. The ₹1,500 crores multi-year capex is to be funded from existing cash plus operating cash flow, with no new capital raising or dilution anticipated.

New revenue streams. Contract manufacturing is under exploration, with specifics expected by the Q2 FY27 call. An online marketplace for SG Mart’s own manufactured and traded products is planned, described as without an existing comparable platform in India.

Market opportunity. The presentation cites market sizes including ₹14,000 crores (2 MTPA) and ₹35,000 crores (5 MTPA) for certain categories, plus smaller categories at ₹420 crores, ₹200 crores, ₹4,800 crores and ₹950 crores.

3. Key Risks

  • Macro/geopolitical disruption affecting steel prices, fuel availability and customer industries, which management explicitly named as the main caveat to its FY27 EBITDA guidance.
  • Commodity price volatility impacting sales and inventory outcomes; management aims to minimize inventory gains/losses through better churn, as done at APL Apollo Steel Tubes.
  • Execution risk in scaling service centres, commissioning the Raipur backward integration line, and launching new accessory products — mitigated in management’s view by identified sites, existing infrastructure and group experience.
  • ROCE dilution risk from new ventures such as contract manufacturing; management stated no vertical will be pursued if it dilutes ROCE below 20%.
  • Customer concentration in solar structures (top 20–30 EPCs/IPPs).
  • Working capital risk from advances to steel mills and new-vertical inventory build; management expects rationalization as scale improves and backward integration commences.
  • Dependence on steel supply continuity, which drove higher advances and lower trade payables in Q1.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 EBITDA guidance. On the Q4 FY26 call, management had indicated ~₹300 crores absolute EBITDA for FY27. On the Q1 FY27 call, management said that given Q1 momentum and assuming no drastic macro deterioration, the company should achieve that figure, with macro disruption as the only caveat. Q1 FY27 actual business EBITDA was ₹588 Mn (₹58.8 crores), with total EBITDA of ₹685 Mn including other income.

Revenue and profitability trajectory. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹13,086 Mn, up 14% YoY from ₹11,438 Mn, but down from ₹18,228 Mn in Q4 FY26. Business EBITDA rose 64% YoY to ₹588 Mn from ₹359 Mn, with margin expanding to 4.49% from 3.10% a year earlier and 3.10% in Q4 FY26. PAT rose 41% YoY to ₹456 Mn. Management characterized the margin improvement as structural rather than one-off, attributing it to higher steel prices (₹2,500–3,000/ton increase from April 1 to June 30), a richer mix of steel profiles and renewable structures (realizations ₹10,000–15,000/ton higher than HR coil-based products), and minimal inventory gains (inventory value fell from ₹2,836 Mn to ₹2,090 Mn).

Segment guidance vs. actuals. Service centre profitability was guided at ₹1,800–2,000/ton and came in within that range. Steel profiles and renewables were guided at ₹3,000–4,000/ton and ₹3,000–3,500/ton respectively, consistent with reported segment commentary. Management reiterated that backward integration should lift these to ₹5,000+/ton (₹6,000–7,000/ton cited in opening remarks) in about 1.5 years.

Long-term targets. Management reiterated a 50% CAGR visibility over three years and Vision 2030 of 4–4.5 million tons of value-added volume (3 million tons from service centres, 0.5 million tons each from solar structures and steel profiles), ₹25,000–35,000 crores revenue, and minimum ₹1,000 crores EBITDA. B2B metal trading is excluded from this core volume ambition, described as optional upside requiring no investment.

Service centre count. The target was described as 25 centres by 2029–2030 on the call, while the presentation cites a target of 16 service centres across India by 2028. Management stated the number matters less than pan-India coverage of every industrial cluster, with centres spaced 400–600 km apart.

Working capital guidance. Management guided net working capital days down to 20–25 within two years from the current 27, supported by backward integration reducing raw material storage needs.

Capex guidance. Q1 FY27 capex was ₹90 crores against full-year guidance of ₹400–500 crores; the cash flow statement shows capex of ₹896 Mn for the quarter. The three-year capex plan of ~₹1,500 crores is to be met from ~₹700 crores on the books plus operating cash flow, with no dilution planned.

Corporate actions on July 20, 2026. The Board approved the unaudited results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026; appointed Sanjay Gupta as Chairman & Managing Director for five years, Rohan Gupta as Whole-time Director for five years, Chakram Kumar Singh as Non-Executive Non-Independent Director, and Shruti Shrivastava as Independent Director for five years (all effective July 20, 2026, subject to member approval); and approved the acquisition of 100% of Tanwar Cargo Solutions Private Limited for ₹85 crores in cash, expected to complete by December 31, 2026. TCSPL has no significant business operations and primarily owns ~9.956 acres of freehold land at Aurangabad, Tehsil Hodal, District Palwal, Haryana, intended for manufacturing, warehousing, logistics and commercial use. The 41st AGM is convened for September 19, 2026, at 11:00 A.M. IST via video conferencing, with proposals including adoption of financial statements, re-appointment of Amit Thakur as Director, ratification of cost auditor remuneration for M/s HMVN & Associates, the four director appointments, and revision of Amit Thakur’s remuneration ceiling.

Broker Narrative

The first report was company-specific and balanced: it flagged tepid Q4FY23 results, Unlimited underperformance, margin contraction, and LimeRoad cash burn, while seeing growth in store additions, SSSG, and omni-channel potential. The last report abandons SGMART’s retail fundamentals entirely and frames the outlook around Indian IT services underperformance, small-cap PAT weakness, and macro tailwinds such as GST 2.0, lower rates, and fiscal stimulus. The only persistent theme is a broad concern about underperformance/profitability pressure; the specific retail drivers and risks changed completely.
