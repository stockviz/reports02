Punjab & Sind Bank — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Sequential moderation in Q1 FY27. While year-on-year growth was strong, the quarter-on-quarter picture was subdued: overall business grew 1.05%, credit 1.25% and deposits 0.89%. Management attributed this to Q1 typically being a slower quarter and to the bank pacing growth on a QoQ basis.
  • Lower non-interest income. Other income fell to ₹333 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹427 crore in the prior quarter. Treasury contribution was ₹80 crore in Q1 FY27 against around ₹200 crore in Q1 FY26, and recovery in written-off accounts was lower than both the prior quarter and Q1 FY26. Management noted recovery typically accrues more in the second half.
  • Higher provisions. NPA provisioning rose to ₹123.54 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹19.71 crore in Q4 FY26. Management stated this was driven by proactive ECL provisioning (around ₹150 crore provided on ECL in the quarter) rather than deterioration in asset quality.
  • MSME slippage. Slippages were ₹207 crore for the quarter with a slippage ratio of 0.18%. Management indicated the run rate may continue, as global developments can have trickle-down effects on MSMEs, though it is not a matter of alarm.
  • Treasury uncertainty. Management described the treasury outlook as difficult to call given global turmoil, crude movement (touched $90), and the possibility of yields creeping from 6.8% toward 6.90%.
  • Limited FCNR(B) upside. The bank lacks a foreign branch; its Gift City branch is expected only around November (third quarter). Management therefore has modest FCNR(B) aspirations of around $20–25 million, with total expected mobilisation across FCNR(B) deposits and the two borrowing routes at around $100 million.
  • Cost-to-income pressure. The cost-to-income ratio was 60.21% in Q1 FY27. Management linked ongoing costs to legacy under-investment in HR, technology and branch expansion, and described bringing the ratio below 50 as an aspiration for two to three years out.
  • Strike notice. The bank received a Notice of Strike from the United Forum of Bank Union for an All India Strike on 11.09.2026. In the event the strike materialises, the functioning of branches and offices may be affected, despite steps taken for smooth functioning.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. Actual performance may differ from forward-looking statements due to market conditions, economic assumptions, or operational factors.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong annualised growth. Total business grew 15.27% YoY to ₹2,66,420 crore, split between deposit growth of 12.16% and advances growth of 19.35%. Retail grew 36.44%, agriculture 25.85% and MSME 32.71%.
  • RAM focus. RAM advances grew 32.66% YoY and reached 60.02% of the total book, up 602 bps. Management aims to take this to 64–65% by year-end.
  • NII recovery. Net interest income grew 15.33% YoY to ₹1,038 crore, which management read as a sign the bank is moving forward on the NII front after repo-rate adjustments.
  • Profitability. Net profit grew 23.05% YoY to ₹331 crore. ROA improved to 0.73% and ROE to 10.85%.
  • Asset quality improvement. Gross NPA fell 113 bps YoY to 2.21% and net NPA 26 bps to 0.65%. PCR improved to 92.33%. Collection efficiency reached 95%.
  • Undisbursed pipeline. The bank has undisbursed sanctions of around ₹15,000 crore and plans to replace low-yielding assets (including a central government guaranteed exposure of ₹5,000 crore, largely shed) with higher-yielding ones.
  • Digital and product initiatives. STP journeys exist for KCC, education loan, personal loan, pre-owned vehicle and commercial vehicle; home loan STP limits raised to ₹2 crore, commercial vehicle and pre-owned to ₹50 lakh. Digital gold loans, loan against mutual funds, CBDC and a digital personal loan STP journey are planned or launched. The bank is the first public sector bank to offer online PAN generation.
  • Distribution and structure. Five zonal offices now exist (up from two), CENMARG back-office sanctioning consolidated into five zones, 141 housing projects approved, and OEM tie-ups with Maruti, Mahindra and Hyundai. A three-year plan targets ₹4,00,000 crore business by FY29, 2,000 branches, around 1,600 ATMs and 6,000–6,500 BCs.
  • Co-lending. A specialised co-lending cell is active; co-lending is a stated priority in gold and MSME segments.
  • Capital and funding options. CRAR stood at 17.61%. Management referenced QIP and infrastructure bonds as capital-raising routes, and noted the RBI window for FCNR(B), OFCB and ECB borrowings.
  • Gift City branch. Expected to open around November, enabling resource mobilisation and deployment.

3. Key Risks

  • Asset quality. MSME slippage run rate may persist; management aims to keep net slippage below the prior year’s ₹677 crore and hopes to bring it below ₹600 crore.
  • Provisioning trajectory. ECL provisions kick in from 1 April next year; the bank is front-loading provisions, which weighs on near-term profitability.
  • Treasury and market risk. Treasury income is a market phenomenon subject to yield movements and global conditions.
  • Funding cost pressure. A question was raised on NIM evolution given funding cost pressure.
  • Execution risk on expansion. Branch expansion, recruitment and technology investment carry costs that must be offset by higher income.
  • Operational disruption. The proposed 11.09.2026 All India Strike may affect branch and office functioning.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. Actual results may differ from guidance due to market, economic or operational factors.
  • Concentration in government-guaranteed exposure. One central government guaranteed account carried a ₹5,000 crore exposure at low yield; the bank shed 50% by 30 June and nearly the entire amount thereafter.
