Balkrishna Industries — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical uncertainty and supply chain disruption. Management flagged geopolitical uncertainty with impacts on both availability and cost of materials, as well as availability of vessels and containers and freight costs. These were cited as key variables for near-term performance.
  • Raw material inflation. The Q1 EBITDA margin of 20.61% was impacted by raw material prices due to the global geopolitical situation and its supply chain effects. Management indicated raw material prices rose about 5% on a cost basis, translating to roughly 3% impact on sales price, with an expected ~2% margin impact in the coming quarter; part of the inflation is expected to flow into Q2 and some into Q3.
  • Freight costs. Freight was around 5% of revenue. Management noted a mid-period dip followed by a rebound, and said further increases may occur if the scenario continues, with uncertainty over how best to pass it on.
  • Weather risks. Weather challenges in Europe and a sketchy monsoon expected in India were cited as key near-term variables. On European heat waves affecting crops, management said it was too early to assess impact, noting a good monsoon there as a counter.
  • Margin dilution from India mix. India’s contribution rose to 40% of volumes, which impacted margin slightly. India margins remain marginally lower than export margins, though not as low as previously.
  • FX loss. Standalone revenue for the quarter included a realized foreign exchange loss on sales of INR36 crores.
  • Carbon Black pricing pressure. Geopolitical challenges are expected to impact pricing and supply of Carbon Black on the back of increased crude prices.
  • U.S. tariff-related disruption. The U.S. share of overall revenue has fallen to around 11–12% versus the historical 15–16%. Management said it was too early to share details on U.S. duty refunds, though applications have been initiated.
  • Employee cost increase. Employee cost rose to INR153 crores (up 18% YoY), attributed to a combination of Gujarat minimum wage increases, staff increments, and added staff to support new businesses.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record OHT volumes. Q1 delivered the highest-ever quarterly OHT sales volume at 93,770 metric tons, up 16% YoY, despite challenges across international geographies, end markets, and supply chain.
  • Regional momentum. Europe saw a stable environment with a lower base and a good agricultural season; India performed exceptionally well despite a high base, aided by marketing efforts, superior product range, market share gains, and growing infra capex; the Americas improved with the tariff rate settling at 10%, led by the U.S.
  • Market share runway. India market share is around 18–19%, the U.S. about 3–4%, and Europe roughly 7–8%. Management sees a long runway of growth in the Americas given BKT’s brand positioning and focus on high-quality products, and expects the U.S. to return to 15–16% of revenue.
  • Carbon Black expansion. Phase II of the Carbon Black plant was commissioned, taking total capacity to 360,000 MTPA at a capital outlay of INR800 crores. Captive power capacity at Bhuj was increased from 40 MW to 64 MW at INR125 crores. Third-party sales were around 10% of the overall business, and demand continues to be robust.
  • On-Highway entry. On-road products were launched in the TBR and 2-wheeler segments in the domestic market. Supplies have begun in Truck Bus Radial with positive trade response; the 2-wheeler portfolio was introduced with select products. The “YOU FORWARD” 24X7 journey assistance program launched in June, supported by a digital campaign. Management described FY27 as a year of building the portfolio, with FY28 onwards as “serious business,” and referenced a vision of INR5,000 crores revenue from On-Highway tires by 2030.
  • New Carbon Black business head. Mr. Ashish Kumar Datta was appointed Business Head for Carbon Black effective July 3, 2026, bringing 36 years of cross-functional leadership experience.
  • Capex progress. Balance capex projects of approximately INR3,000 crores are progressing as per schedule. Total planned capex to 2030 is INR6,800 crores, of which roughly INR3,800 crores has been spent.
  • Dividend. The Board recommended a first interim dividend of INR4 per equity share for FY27; the AGM also approved a final dividend of INR4.00 per equity share for FY2025-26.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical uncertainty affecting material availability and costs, vessel and container availability, and freight costs.
  • Raw material and crude-linked inflation flowing through Q2 and Q3, with partial offset from price hikes.
  • Weather risk in Europe and monsoon variability in India.
  • Freight cost escalation if current conditions persist, with uncertain pass-through.
  • Margin dilution as India’s share of volumes rises.
  • Carbon Black pricing and supply pressure from higher crude prices.
  • U.S. tariff-related disruption and uncertainty over duty refunds.
  • On-Highway segment remains small; management declined to share revenues or volume details and expects only gradual ramp-up from Q2.
  • Management repeatedly declined to provide forward-looking margin or momentum guidance under current volatility.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Price hikes. Management had guided earlier that it was monitoring supply chain disruptions and had taken price hikes alongside efforts toward a superior product mix to partially offset impact. Observed: a ~5% price hike was taken scattered across Q1, with full pass-through expected in the current quarter; the impact was partially offset in Q1.
  • Volume momentum. Management stated it continued the momentum of the previous quarter into Q1. Observed: highest-ever quarterly OHT volume of 93,770 MT, up 16% YoY.
  • U.S. performance. Management had noted the tariff rate settling at 10% levels. Observed: better performance led by the U.S., with expectations of sustained growth momentum.
  • Capex. Management guided balance capex of ~INR3,000 crores progressing as per schedule. Observed: Q1 capex of ~INR1,000 crores; additional INR1,500–2,000 crores expected in FY27; total FY27 capex around INR2,500–3,000 crores.
  • On-Highway ramp-up. Management guided a gradual ramp-up starting Q2 after seeding the business in Q1. Observed: distribution network and channel infrastructure set in April, supplies started in TBR, and positive trade response; no revenue or volume details shared.
  • Carbon Black. Management guided leveraging Carbon Black capacities to improve efficiency and quality. Observed: Phase II commissioned to 360,000 MTPA, captive power raised to 64 MW, decent YoY volume growth, third-party sales ~10% of overall business.
  • Margin outlook. Management declined to comment on future margin profile under current volatility, and repeatedly declined forward-looking statements on momentum and margin trajectory.

5. Key Investor Facts from the Debenture Issuance

  • The Finance Committee of the Board approved issuance of 55,000 rated, listed, unsecured, redeemable, non-convertible debentures of face value INR 1,00,000 each, for an aggregate principal amount of up to INR 550,00,00,000 on a private placement basis.
  • The issuance is in three series across 1-year, 3-year, and 4-year tenures with coupon rates of 7.35%, 7.38%, and 7.40% p.a., respectively.

6. Other Financial and Governance Facts

  • Standalone revenue for Q1 was INR3,409 crores, up 24% YoY; standalone EBITDA was INR703 crores at 20.61% margin; PAT was INR432 crores.
  • As on June 30, 2026, gross debt was ~INR4,690 crores and cash and cash equivalents ~INR2,965 crores, giving net debt of ~INR1,725 crores. The euro rate for the quarter was around INR102.
  • The 64th AGM was held on July 29, 2026 via VC/OAVM; all resolutions were passed with requisite majority. Deloitte Haskins & Sells Chartered Accountants LLP was appointed as Joint Statutory Auditors for five years up to the conclusion of the 69th AGM.
  • The Board approved unaudited standalone and consolidated results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, reviewed by Jayantilal Thakkar & Co. 

Broker Narrative

Initially, the broker flagged near-term headwinds like inventory destocking and subdued OTR but was optimistic about export stabilization and a margin recovery. Over three years, the narrative shifted to a robust demand recovery and new growth segments, yet the margin story reversed as raw material and freight costs stayed elevated. The call moved from Add to Neutral as cost pressures persisted.

Fears that came true

  • The risk of higher raw material costs materialized, as raw material inflation and elevated freight costs caused a 320bp YoY EBITDA margin contraction, leading to the Neutral call and disappointing returns.

Optimism that failed

  • Expectations of freight and cost deflation failed, as freight costs remained elevated and raw material costs rose instead.
  • The projected margin expansion to 22% in FY24 and 24.5% in FY25 failed, with the latest margin at 20.6%.
