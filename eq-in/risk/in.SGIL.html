SGIL Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Revenue decline and dispatch delays. Q1 FY27 total income was ₹75.71 Cr against ₹85.38 Cr in Q1 FY26, an 11.32% YoY decline. Management attributed this to delayed customer material lifting, prototype approvals that took longer than expected with a particular customer, and West Asia conflict-related logistics disruptions affecting export rollouts. Ship availability was a specific constraint in the quarter.

Margin compression. PBDIT fell to ₹5.30 Cr from ₹13.15 Cr a year earlier, with margin moderating from 15.41% to 7.0% — a decline of 841 bps. The stated drivers were raw material inflation (~200 bps), consumable cost inflation (~300 bps), and higher electricity costs, policy changes, manpower increases and other items (~300 bps).

Input cost inflation. Furan Resin, described as the third-largest spending item, rose 60% on crude oil price increases. Pig iron and CRC saw inflation from material shortages, with Indian markets reliant on imported scrap and shipping constraints complicating raw material imports. PNG and LNG fuel costs rose 60–70%.

Electricity policy change. In April, MSEDCL revised its solar banking policy, ending the ability to bank power generated between 9:00 AM and 5:00 PM for use in other zones. SGIL lost around 30–40% of solar-generated energy as lapsed units, with a retrospective one-time impact recovered in the quarter. The company was losing 3–4 lakh units per month of its 12–14 lakh monthly generation.

Logistics and customer-specific delays. Export revenues saw a marginal drop. Domestic OEM delays included one prototype delay and another related to securing letters of credit.

Bottom line. Depreciation rose to ₹8.81 Cr (from ₹3.37 Cr) and finance costs to ₹7.18 Cr (from ₹4.65 Cr), contributing to a PBT before exceptional items of negative ₹10.69 Cr and a loss after tax of ₹10.11 Cr, against a profit after tax of ₹3.37 Cr in Q1 FY26.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Industry momentum. Global wind annual installations grew 40% in 2025 with 165 GW installed; cumulative capacity is projected to exceed 2 TW by 2030 on a 5.2% CAGR. India’s annual installations reached a record 6.34 GW in FY26, with installed capacity expected to reach 77 GW by 2030 from 51 GW currently.

Capacity expansion and utilization ramp. Foundry capacity has been expanded to 45,000 tons per annum with 20,000 tons per annum in-house machining. FY26 utilization was 93% on the previous 30,000-ton capacity; the new capacity reached 66% utilization in Q1. July utilization reached 85% at 3,200 tons of production, with 80–90% expected within 2–3 months. Machining hit 50% capacity utilization last month with 50% of product development complete.

Customer and product wins. Clearance was received for Nordex NX N series 5 MW platform prototypes in Q1, with purchase orders mainly for US market exports. The Vestas 4 MW platform contract was signed in mid-May with tooling development underway; samples are expected in Q4 FY27 and full production in Q1 FY28. The earlier Vestas 3 MW platform is being phased out. In power, orders were received from L&T and BHEL, with the BHEL PO received in the quarter. Terex mining orders are strong, aided by 20% Yen appreciation against the Rupee tilting sourcing toward Indian suppliers.

Energy cost mitigation. A 5 MW wind PPA under open access was signed, supplementing the 10 MW captive solar. Combined, 50–60% of power is expected from own sources, with the ₹10 Cr annual energy saving described as intact.

Pricing and recovery. Raw material indexation with customers carries a one-quarter lag, with recovery expected in Q2 FY27. Realizations are expected to move from 138–140 to 145-plus levels in upcoming quarters. Management stated SGIL is 5–10% cheaper than China on a landed cost basis for wind products.

Order book and capacity pipeline. The executable order book is positioned at ₹500 Cr for the current year, with ₹600 Cr seen as achievable next year on the same products and customers. Land for the 100,000-ton capacity expansion is targeted for acquisition by end of the financial year, with the next project potentially initiated in Q3 FY28 and fundraising in Q1 or Q2 of 2028. Greenfield capex typically takes 15–18 months.

Demand mix. Wind demand is described as much higher than solar, with night tariffs 25% higher and daytime solar tariffs at a 25% discount — a 50% tariff differentiation favoring wind. The waiver delay to June 30, 2028 under ISTS was described as immaterial.

3. Key Risks

  • Customer concentration: Vestas has been the largest customer for 15 years, at times over 40–45% of business. Management expects this to decline to around 30% as new clients are added, though absolute revenue is not expected to drop.
  • Export and logistics exposure: Export revenue is expected at 25–30%; global logistics disruptions and ship availability have already affected dispatches.
  • Commodity and energy price volatility: Furan Resin, pig iron, CRC, PNG and LNG price movements flow through with a one-quarter lag on the way up, creating timing mismatches.
  • Regulatory/policy risk: The MSEDCL banking policy change and retrospective recovery illustrate exposure to state electricity policy shifts.
  • Capacity constraints on larger castings: SGIL’s 30-metric-ton limit prevents participation in Adani’s 5 MW parts that exceed 30 tons, and offshore castings are seen as requiring infrastructure not currently available in India.
  • Execution and approval timing: Prototype approvals and customer material lifting have proven difficult to predict, as evidenced by the Q1 revenue shortfall.
  • Funding and leverage: Borrowing limits were increased from ₹200 Cr to ₹250 Cr at the AGM, and the next capex cycle would require fundraising.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Revenue guidance vs. Q1 actual. Management maintained FY27 revenue guidance of ₹500 Cr, implying ~33% growth, and stated the FY27 outlook remains strong on a ~33% growth order book. Q1 actual total income of ₹75.71 Cr declined 11.32% YoY, with management attributing the gap to timing — production was strong but did not convert to sales due to inventory build from shipping constraints and prototype approvals. Management stated it is already seeing prototype approvals and customer lifting ease in Q2, and that July utilization reached 85%.

Margin guidance vs. Q1 actual. Management guided to a full-year PBDIT margin improvement of more than 300 bps over the previous year, and reiterated this guidance during the call. Q1 PBDIT margin was 7.0% versus 15.41% in Q1 FY26 and 13.10% for FY26 as a whole. Management cited recovery of RM and consumable inflation from Q2, higher volumes, increased H2 export concentration, and growing in-house machining contribution as the drivers.

Capacity utilization guidance vs. actual. Management guided to 80% utilization of the new 45,000-ton capacity for the full year FY27. Q1 actual was 66%, with July at 85% and 80–90% expected within 2–3 months.

Machining guidance vs. actual. Management planned 40–50% in-house machining, with cost of machining expected to decrease as it ramps. Actual: 50% of product development completed and 50% capacity utilization reached last month, with product approval from 50% of customers and cycle times coming in as projected.

Order book framing. The previously cited ₹600–700 Cr order book is now described as an executable order book, positioned at ₹500 Cr for the current year considering hurdles, with ₹600 Cr seen as achievable next year.

Capex and fundraising timing. Land acquisition targeted by December or March; next project potentially initiated in Q3 FY28; fundraising in Q1 or Q2 of 2028. Management stated it wants to see two or three good quarters of performance first.

Other income. Q1 other income of ₹4.4 Cr was attributed to foreign exchange-related items or export incentives, not power sales.
