Univastu India Limited — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Raw material inflation driven by geopolitical conflict is identified as a pressure point.
  • Mobilization costs are flagged as a risk to short-term margins.
  • The company’s long-term business plans require sizeable loans and investments and the issuance of guarantees or securities to corporate bodies from time to time, implying balance-sheet and contingent-liability commitments.
  • The transition of external credit rating from Infomerics Valuation and Rating Limited to CRISIL Ratings Limited required administrative follow-through, including withdrawal of the Infomerics rating.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Order book: A strong order book of Rs. 1,854 crores across five states, providing 2 to 3 years of revenue visibility.
  • New segments: Entry into new business segments including data centers and tunnel ventilation.
  • Strategic partnerships: Partnerships linked to international events such as the 2030 Olympics.
  • Asset monetization: Asset monetization projects are cited as a growth avenue.
  • New work order: A work order dated 16th September, 2026, from M/s Larsen & Toubro Limited for the CCTV System for the CA-234 Mumbai Metro Line 4 & 4A (Wadala – Kasarvadvali – Gaimukh) Project, valued at Rs. 23,74,01,563/- including 18% GST. Scope covers design, manufacture, routine testing, quality assurance, supply, factory acceptance testing, packing and forwarding, installation, interface, configuration, integration, testing and commissioning, spare parts, site training, a 2-year Defect Liability Maintenance Period (DLMP), and a 5-year Comprehensive Maintenance Period (CMP).
  • Credit profile: CRISIL Ratings Limited upgraded the credit ratings for the company’s bank facilities, with long-term bank facilities enhanced to Rs. 113 Crore (from Rs. 35 Crore) and upgrades for both long-term and short-term facilities. The CRISIL rating is valid up to 15th July, 2027.
  • Capital raise: The Board approved allotment of 18,39,339 Fully Convertible Warrants on a preferential basis at Rs. 87/- per warrant, raising up to Rs. 16,00,22,493/-, with each warrant convertible into one equity share of face value Rs. 10/- within an 18-month tenure upon payment of the remaining 75% balance consideration.
  • Governance proposals: The 17th Annual General Meeting is scheduled for Monday, 21st September, 2026, at 11:00 A.M. (IST) at PYC Deccan Gymkhana, Pune. Proposals include ratifying cost auditor remuneration, approving material related party transactions with Univastu NUOS IoT Systems Private Limited up to Rs. 100 Crores, increasing investment/loan/guarantee limits up to Rs. 1000 Crores over prescribed limits, and approving borrowing powers up to Rs. 750 Crores over and above paid-up capital and free reserves under Section 180(1)(c).

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material inflation linked to geopolitical conflict.
  • Mobilization costs pressuring short-term margins.
  • Exposure arising from sizeable loans, investments, guarantees, or securities extended to persons or bodies corporate under the long-term business plan.
  • Execution risk on the Mumbai Metro CCTV order, which carries a 67-week Design & Build timeline plus a 2-year DLMP and 5-year CMP.
  • Related party transactions with Univastu NUOS IoT Systems Private Limited proposed up to Rs. 100 Crores.
  • Significant proposed increases in borrowing and investment/loan/guarantee limits (Rs. 750 Crores and Rs. 1000 Crores respectively), which expand financial leverage and contingent exposure.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • Guidance: Management targets revenues of Rs. 600 crores for FY27 and Rs. 900 crores for FY28, with EBITDA margins expected between 17% and 18%. For the Mumbai Metro CCTV order, the work is to be completed in 67 weeks for Design & Build (D&B), with DLMP of 2 years and CMP of 5 years. The company will complete formalities with Infomerics for withdrawal of its credit rating at the earliest.
  • Observed performance: For Q4 FY26, revenue from operations reached Rs. 109.44 crores with a PAT of Rs. 10.33 crores. For the full year FY26, revenue reached Rs. 243.35 crores with a PAT of Rs. 25.69 crores, representing 42.16% year-on-year growth and an EPS of Rs. 6.48.
  • Comparison: The FY26 full-year revenue of Rs. 243.35 crores is well below the FY27 target of Rs. 600 crores, implying a substantial step-up in scale is required to meet management’s guidance. The company has not provided EBITDA margin figures for FY26 in the supplied material, so the 17%–18% margin guidance cannot be directly compared against reported profitability.
