Headwinds and Challenges

  • Weak subsidiary financial position. The statutory auditors’ consolidated review report draws attention to three foreign subsidiaries of the Group having negative net worth as at June 30, 2026. These subsidiaries have incurred losses and depend on continued financial support from the holding company to meet obligations and continue operations. This indicates a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on their ability to continue as going concerns.
  • Loss-making overseas operations. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, six subsidiaries (including a relevant step-down subsidiary) not reviewed by the principal auditors reported total revenues from operations of Rs.1,798.92 lakhs and a total net loss after tax of Rs.346.23 lakhs.
  • Interest burden on subsidiaries. The Board approved reversal of interest receivable from two overseas wholly-owned subsidiaries — Elgi Rubber Company LLC, USA (Rs.2.09 million) and Borrachas e Equipamentos Elgi Ltda, Brasil (Rs.14.51 million) — aggregating Rs.16.60 million, recognized during the quarter ended June 30, 2026. The stated intention was to reduce the interest burden and related costs in those subsidiaries and improve their financial position.
  • Dormant and inoperative subsidiaries. Elgi Rubber Company Limited, Kenya was dormant and inoperative during the immediately preceding five financial years, with turnover of Nil (0.00%) and net worth of INR 7.51 million (0.42%) as on March 31, 2026. Pincott International Pty Limited, Australia was inoperative during the immediately preceding six financial years, with turnover of Nil (0.00%) and net worth of INR (60.76) million (-3.40%) as on March 31, 2026.
  • Prior-year losses and impairments. The standalone results for the year ended March 31, 2026 show a loss after tax of Rs.(11,494.55) lakhs, including impairment of investment in a subsidiary under liquidation of Rs.(3,658.47) lakhs and impairment/ECL on loans to a subsidiary of Rs.(5,441.64) lakhs. The consolidated results for the same year show a loss after tax of Rs.(24,023.78) lakhs, including impairment on loan from a subsidiary under liquidation of Rs.(13,254.27) lakhs and a loss on disposal of a subsidiary of Rs.(2,053.52) lakhs.
  • Sequential revenue decline. Consolidated revenue from operations declined from Rs.8,633.53 lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 to Rs.7,009.26 lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2026. Standalone revenue declined from Rs.5,947.95 lakhs to Rs.5,843.37 lakhs over the same period.
  • Higher finance costs. Standalone finance costs rose to Rs.613.05 lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2026 from Rs.413.16 lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 and Rs.419.62 lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. Consolidated finance costs rose to Rs.779.54 lakhs from Rs.695.89 lakhs and Rs.670.07 lakhs respectively.
  • KYC and dematerialisation compliance burden. The Company reminded physical security holders to update PAN, address with PIN code, mobile number, bank account details, specimen signature and nomination choice, and to dematerialise physical securities, in line with SEBI Master Circular No. HO/38/13/(4)2026-MIRSDPOD/I/4298/2026 dated February 06, 2026. Physical folios without these updates are eligible for payments only through electronic mode with effect from April 1, 2024.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Gain on sale of land and building. Standalone results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 include an exceptional gain on sale of land and building of Rs.4,325.52 lakhs, which drove profit before tax of Rs.4,644.83 lakhs and profit after tax of Rs.4,322.97 lakhs, compared with a loss after tax of Rs.(2,492.30) lakhs in the preceding quarter.
  • Consolidated return to profitability. Consolidated profit after tax for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was Rs.4,084.68 lakhs, compared with a loss after tax of Rs.(20,404.79) lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 and a loss of Rs.(151.45) lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2025.
  • Improved earnings per share. Standalone basic and diluted EPS for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was Rs.8.64, compared with Rs.(4.98) in the preceding quarter and Rs.1.21 in the prior-year quarter. Consolidated basic and diluted EPS was Rs.8.16, compared with Rs.(40.77) and Rs.(0.30) respectively.
  • Simplification of the subsidiary structure. The Board approved voluntary winding up of two non-material wholly-owned subsidiaries — Elgi Rubber Company Limited, Kenya and Pincott International Pty Limited, Australia — subject to requisite consents, permissions and approvals under applicable laws. The Company stated that neither is a material subsidiary and that closure would not have any material impact on business activities, revenue or financial position. Completion is expected on or before March 31, 2027, with any proceeds from winding up to be received by the Company following completion.
  • Reduced interest burden in subsidiaries. The reversal of interest receivable from the US and Brazil subsidiaries is intended to reduce their interest burden and related costs and help improve their financial position.
  • Unmodified audit opinion. The statutory auditors expressed an unmodified opinion on the unaudited standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. The CFO, S R Venkatachalam, confirmed this in a declaration under Regulation 33(3)(d) dated August 12, 2026.
  • Shareholder support. All five resolutions proposed at the 20th Annual General Meeting held on August 27, 2026 were unanimously passed with 100% of valid votes cast in favour, including adoption of financial statements, re-appointment of Sudarsan Varadaraj as Chairman and Managing Director and as a Director, re-appointment of Harsha Varadaraj as Whole-Time Director, and ratification of the Cost Auditor’s remuneration.
  • Annual report accessibility. The Company dispatched a letter providing the web-link to the Annual Report for the financial year 2025-26 to members who had not registered their email addresses, and the report is available on the Company’s website.

Key Risks

  • Going concern uncertainty at three foreign subsidiaries. The auditors’ emphasis of matter states that these subsidiaries have negative net worth, have incurred losses, and depend on continued financial support from the holding company. Management assessed that the Group continues to have adequate resources to meet its obligations as they fall due and to continue operations for the foreseeable future, and the consolidated financial results were prepared on a going concern basis.
  • Recoverability of loans and advances to subsidiaries. The standalone results for the year ended March 31, 2026 included impairment/ECL on loans to a subsidiary of Rs.(5,441.64) lakhs and impairment of investment in a subsidiary under liquidation of Rs.(3,658.47) lakhs. The quarter ended June 30, 2026 standalone results include impairment/ECL on loans to a subsidiary of Rs.(93.77) lakhs.
  • Dependence on exceptional gains. The standalone profit for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was driven by the Rs.4,325.52 lakhs gain on sale of land and building. Profit before exceptional items and tax was Rs.413.08 lakhs.
  • Foreign exchange and cross-border regulatory risk. The winding up of the Kenya and Australia subsidiaries is subject to due process and receipt of requisite approvals from regulatory authorities in those jurisdictions, with completion expected on or before March 31, 2027. Proceeds, if any, would be received only after completion.
  • Unreviewed subsidiary results. The consolidated results include one subsidiary whose interim financial results were not reviewed by its auditor, with total revenue of Rs. Nil lakhs and total net loss after tax of Rs.0.01 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. Management stated these results are not material to the Group.
  • Reliance on other auditors. Six subsidiaries (including a relevant step-down subsidiary) were reviewed by other auditors, not by the principal statutory auditors. The principal auditors’ conclusion on the consolidated statement relies solely on those reports for the amounts and disclosures included in respect of those subsidiaries.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Subsidiary support and interest reversal. Management stated that reversing interest receivable from Elgi Rubber Company LLC, USA and Borrachas e Equipamentos Elgi Ltda, Brasil would reduce the interest burden and related costs in those subsidiaries and help improve their financial position. The reversal, aggregating Rs.16.60 million, was recognized during the quarter ended June 30, 2026. The standalone results note that the reversal of interest income recognized during the current quarter from two wholly owned subsidiaries was on account of uncertainty of its recoverability.
  • Winding up of non-material subsidiaries. Management stated that the voluntary winding up of Elgi Rubber Company Limited, Kenya and Pincott International Pty Limited, Australia would not have any material impact on business activities, revenue or financial position, as neither is a material subsidiary. The disclosures show both had Nil turnover in the last financial year, with Kenya’s net worth at INR 7.51 million (0.42%) and Australia’s at INR (60.76) million (-3.40%) as on March 31, 2026. Completion is expected on or before March 31, 2027.
  • Going concern assessment. Management assessed that the Group continues to have adequate resources to meet its obligations as they fall due and to continue operations for the foreseeable future, despite the auditors’ emphasis that three foreign subsidiaries have negative net worth and depend on continued financial support from the holding company. The consolidated financial results were prepared on a going concern basis.
  • Observed performance against prior periods. Consolidated revenue from operations for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was Rs.7,009.26 lakhs, lower than Rs.8,633.53 lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 and Rs.8,657.82 lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. Standalone revenue was Rs.5,843.37 lakhs, compared with Rs.5,947.95 lakhs and Rs.6,373.72 lakhs respectively. The profitability improvement in the quarter was primarily attributable to the exceptional gain on sale of land and building rather than operational revenue growth.
  • Annual General Meeting outcome. All five resolutions proposed at the 20th Annual General Meeting held on August 27, 2026 were unanimously passed with 100% of valid votes cast in favour, including adoption of financial statements, re-appointment of Sudarsan Varadaraj as Chairman and Managing Director and as a Director, re-appointment of Harsha Varadaraj as Whole-Time Director, and ratification of the Cost Auditor’s remuneration.
