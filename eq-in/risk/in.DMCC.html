DMCC Speciality Chemicals — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Global sulphur supply disruption: Approximately 50% of global sulphur transits the Strait of Hormuz, and the company faced a severe supply disruption on this route.
  • Working capital strain: Working capital was stretched, with material growth in receivables and inventory balances, managed through short-term borrowings.
  • Subdued European market: Demand conditions in Europe remained weak, weighing on the overall demand picture.
  • Sustainability of reported profitability: A meaningful portion of reported profitability reflected inventory gains that will reverse as prices moderate.
  • Normalisation risk: Management characterised the results as optically strong and not a reflection of what is to come, indicating profits will normalise when prices moderate.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Sulphur and sulphuric acid price escalation: A significant escalation in sulphur and sulfuric acid prices led to inventory gains and sharp growth in revenue and profitability.
  • Improved Boron business conditions: Improved demand, pricing, and supply on the Boron front.
  • Geographic diversification: Exports to Latin America, China, and Japan compensated for the subdued European market.
  • Operational continuity: Both the Dahej and Roha facilities operated without interruption despite severe supply disruptions.
  • Structural strengthening: Management continues to work on making the company structurally stronger, and once the operating environment stabilises, it is confident of delivering performance that is more sustainable and more representative of DMCC’s true potential.

3. Key Risks

  • Supply-chain concentration risk: Heavy dependence on global sulphur flows through the Strait of Hormuz exposes the company to geopolitical and logistical disruption.
  • Working capital and liquidity risk: Stretched working capital, growing receivables and inventory, and reliance on short-term borrowings.
  • Earnings quality risk: Inventory gains inflating reported profitability are expected to reverse as prices moderate.
  • Demand risk: Continued weakness in the European market, only partly offset by other geographies.
  • Forward-looking uncertainty: Management explicitly cautions that current results are not indicative of future performance.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • Guidance: Management stated the results are optically strong and not a reflection of what is to come, with profits expected to normalise when prices moderate. It continues to work on making the company structurally stronger and, once the operating environment stabilises, is confident of delivering more sustainable performance representative of DMCC’s true potential.
  • Observed performance (Q1FY27, quarter ended June 30, 2026): Consolidated revenue from operations of ₹253.01 Cr, up 42.43% Q-o-Q and 99.15% Y-o-Y; EBITDA of ₹34.43 Cr, up 92.78% Q-o-Q and 99.58% Y-o-Y; EBITDA margin of 13.59%; profit after tax of ₹20.40 Cr, up 166.58% Q-o-Q and 163.11% Y-o-Y. Standalone figures show profit before tax of ₹2,721.14 lakh and net profit of ₹2,040.52 lakh for the quarter, with basic and diluted EPS of ₹8.18.
  • Reconciliation: The strong reported growth coincided with the sulphur/sulfuric acid price escalation and associated inventory gains that management identifies as non-recurring in nature, meaning the observed performance exceeds what management expects to sustain once prices moderate.
  • Governance and reporting: The Board meeting held on August 10, 2026 approved the unaudited standalone and consolidated results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, accompanied by limited review reports from the statutory auditors. All eight resolutions at the 105th Annual General Meeting held on September 11, 2026 were passed with the requisite majority.
