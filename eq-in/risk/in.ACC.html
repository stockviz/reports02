ACC: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost inflation from the West Asia conflict. The Indian cement sector faced cost pressures in Q1 FY’27 from higher prices of imported fuels such as petcoke and thermal coal, along with elevated freight and logistics costs. Packaging costs rose 25–30% during the quarter. Because of a 60–90 day fuel inventory cycle, the impact of peak fuel cost inflation is expected to coincide with the seasonally weaker Q2, potentially affecting industry profitability in the near term. Kiln fuel cost rose to Rs 1.67/’000 kCal in Q1 FY’27 from Rs 1.56/’000 kCal a year earlier.

Weak quarterly performance. ACC’s consolidated Q1 FY’27 sales volume was 10.0 MnT against 10.7 MnT in Q1 FY’26 and 11.9 MnT in Q4 FY’26. Revenue from operations was Rs 5,808 Cr versus Rs 6,328 Cr (YoY) and Rs 7,146 Cr (QoQ). Operating EBITDA was Rs 457 Cr with a margin of 7.9%, compared with Rs 779 Cr / 12.3% in Q1 FY’26 and Rs 626 Cr / 8.8% in Q4 FY’26. PAT was Rs 147 Cr against Rs 376 Cr (YoY) and Rs 238 Cr (QoQ). Management attributed the profitability impact to planned maintenance of larger integrated units and higher MSA volumes with parent Ambuja Cements.

Soft demand outlook. Cement demand is expected to remain soft at ~5% for FY’27, with near-term demand potentially impacted by seasonal monsoon softness, geopolitical uncertainties, and input cost volatility.

Temporary suspension of operations. During the quarter ended June 30, 2026, management reviewed and approved temporary suspension of manufacturing operations at certain facilities following a strategic review of production facilities across India, aimed at improving operational efficiency and capital allocation. The closures were evaluated as temporary, and no impairment provision was recognised.

Regulatory and compliance matters. SEBI issued an administrative warning (letter dated July 31, 2026, received via NSE email on August 4, 2026) to the erstwhile Company Secretary and Compliance Officer, relating to alleged failure to capture complete and accurate information of insiders with access to UPSI relating to financial results for the quarter ended December 2023 in the Structured Digital Database. The warning is administrative and cautionary and imposes no financial or operational restrictions; the Company stated it will strengthen compliance and monitoring mechanisms.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Capacity expansion. Cement capacity stood at 109 MTPA as on June 30, 2026, expected to rise to 119 MTPA by FY’27. Trial production commenced at Dahej (1.2 MTPA), Salai Banwa (2.4 MTPA), Bathinda (1.2 MTPA) and Jodhpur (2 MTPA). Kalamboli (1 MTPA) and Warisaliganj (2.4 MTPA) trials are slated for Q2, and the Maratha clinker line (4 MTPA) is to be commissioned in 2027. ACC-specific updates cite the Salai Banwa trial run and Kalamboli expansion adding 1 MTPA in the Sept’27 quarter.

Cost reduction programme. A sequential cost reduction of Rs 206 PMT was achieved in Q1 FY’27 despite the West Asia conflict. Ambuja remains on track to deliver a Rs 250 PMT cost reduction in FY’27, targeting a committed cost of Rs 4,250 PMT. Levers include fuel mix optimisation, greater renewable energy adoption, logistics efficiencies, higher blended cement share, lower clinker factor, and domestic coal usage.

Value-over-volume strategy. Trade share rose 5 pp YoY to 81% at ACC (78% at Ambuja consolidated), premium products reached 44% of trade sales (up 3 pp YoY), blended cement share rose 5 pp to 85% YoY, and clinker factor improved 2.1 pp to 63.7% YoY. Green power share at ACC increased from 26% to 31% YoY. Primary lead distance shortened to 254 km from 290 km.

One Cement Platform. SEBI NOC for the proposed amalgamation of ACC with Ambuja was received on June 4, 2026, and an application was filed with the NCLT on June 29, 2026. The transaction is expected to be completed during FY’27, subject to regulatory approvals. The scheme provides for 328 Ambuja shares (face value Rs 2 each) for every 100 ACC shares (face value Rs 10 each); ACC shares held by Ambuja will be cancelled. A shareholder meeting to approve the scheme is scheduled for September 29, 2026. Management expects the unified structure to drive operational efficiencies, elimination of duplicate functions, financial synergies, and economies of scale.

Renewable energy and captive power. The Board approved acquisition of 26% equity in Amplus Andhra Power Private Limited (9,58,548 shares for approximately INR 53.1 Mn, cash consideration), an infrastructure and renewable energy company, to offtake electricity as a captive user under the captive consumption framework. Completion is expected on or before October 30, 2026.

RMX growth. Concrete footprint increased to 119 plants; volume rose 17% YoY to 0.97 Mn m3 with EBITDA of Rs 33 Cr.

Macro and policy support. GDP growth was 7.7% in FY’26 and is projected at 6.6–6.8% in FY’27; the RBI maintained the repo rate at 5.25% in June 2026; INR 12 trillion infrastructure spend and India–US trade agreements are cited as demand supports alongside urbanisation, housing, and mega projects (high-speed rail, expressways, urban transit, mass housing).

ESG recognition. ACC was recognised among India’s Most Sustainable Companies 2026 in the Cement Sector by Business Today; received CII GreenPro certification for its blended cement portfolio and GRIHA certification across its blended cement range (including Buildcem and Buildcem Pro), with additional LCA and EPD-based innovation recognition. ACC’s S&P CSA net score was 72, Sustainalytics risk rank 21.8 (9/114), and CareEdge ESG 1+ rating.

Key Risks

Ongoing litigation and disputed demands. The CCI imposed a penalty of Rs 1,148 Cr on August 31, 2016 over alleged cartelisation; the appeal is before the Supreme Court, where final arguments began July 16, 2026, continued July 23, 2026, and were adjourned to July 29, 2026. A separate CCI order dated January 19, 2017 imposed a Rs 35 Cr penalty over alleged collusive bidding (Haryana reference); the NCLAT matter was adjourned to August 18, 2026. No provision (including interest) is recognised, based on external legal counsel advice that the Company has a strong case on merits.

Wadi limestone mining dispute. ACC has an ongoing dispute with the Karnataka Department of Mines & Geology over the basis of royalty calculation. The Revisional Authority set aside the demand and remanded the additional royalty demand of Rs 492 Cr (1995-96 to 2021-22) back to the State Government; the State filed a writ petition on December 2, 2025. A separate show-cause demand of Rs 482 Cr towards alleged illegal mining is pending before the Karnataka High Court. The DMG appointed a Deputy Commissioner for recovery on March 3, 2026, but the State assured no precipitative action following the April 21, 2026 hearing. The matter was adjourned to August 3, 2026. No provision is considered necessary based on independent legal opinion.

Tax and cess matters. During FY’26, the Company reversed Rs 594 Cr (net) of tax provisions following favourable High Court decisions, and received tax refunds with interest of Rs 205 Cr. It also recognised a receivable/credit of Rs 93 Cr (standalone) / Rs 94 Cr (consolidated) for Chhattisgarh Infrastructure Development and Environment Cess claims, following a High Court judgment holding such cess cannot be levied on mining leases; the matter remains pending adjudication.

Labour code implementation. The four Labour Codes notified November 21, 2025 resulted in a Rs 54 Cr increase in defined benefit obligations, recognised as an exceptional item in FY’26. Final Central Rules were notified in Q1 FY’27 with no material impact assessed; the Company continues to monitor State Rules and clarifications.

Amalgamation execution risk. The scheme remains subject to statutory and regulatory approvals, including NCLT sanction.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Cost reduction. Management targets approximately Rs 250 PMT cost reduction in FY’27 (Ambuja consolidated) toward a committed Rs 4,250 PMT. Observed: a sequential reduction of Rs 206 PMT in Q1 FY’27 was achieved despite West Asia conflict headwinds. ACC’s own release notes only a marginal sequential cost reduction.

Capacity roadmap. Management guides capacity rising from 109 MTPA to 119 MTPA by FY’27. Observed: trial production has commenced at four units (Dahej, Salai Banwa, Bathinda, Jodhpur), with Kalamboli and Warisaliganj trials due in Q2 and the Maratha clinker line in 2027.

Value-led growth. Management states a clear focus on value over volume and improving quality of earnings. Observed: trade share up 5 pp YoY to 81% at ACC, premium products at 44% of trade sales, blended cement at 85% (up 5 pp), and clinker factor at 63.7% (improved 2.1 pp). Volumes moderated (10.0 MnT, down 7% YoY) consistent with the stated strategy of rationalising low-margin volumes.

Near-term profitability. Management cited planned maintenance at larger integrated units and higher MSA volumes with Ambuja as factors in Q1 profitability, and stated it has “good visibility of improved performance in the coming quarters,” while also flagging that peak fuel cost inflation is expected to coincide with the seasonally weaker Q2. Observed: Q1 FY’27 EBITDA of Rs 457 Cr was down 41% YoY but up 8% versus Q4 FY’26 at the Ambuja consolidated level; ACC’s own Q1 PAT of Rs 147 Cr was down YoY and QoQ.

Amalgamation timeline. Management expects completion during FY’27. Observed milestones: SEBI NOC June 4, 2026; NCLT application June 29, 2026; shareholder meeting scheduled September 29, 2026.

Investor interactions. The Company is scheduled to meet investors and analysts at the JP Morgan Conference on September 21, 2026 and the Anand Rathi Conference on September 22, 2026 in Mumbai, in 1x1 and group meetings, with discussions based solely on publicly available information and no UPSI disclosed.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from early optimism on robust demand and sharply lower fuel costs to a later focus on soft demand, cost inflation, and margin compression, with caution on competitive intensity and valuation persisting throughout. The first report highlighted strong volume growth and fuel cost savings as tailwinds against competitive pricing risks, while the last report emphasized demand slowdown, monsoon disruptions, and elevated input costs as headwinds, with only long-term sector constructive outlook and cost-mitigation measures as remaining positives.

Fears that came true

  • Elevated competitive intensity and inability to pass on fuel cost surges materialized, as reflected in repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and steep negative actual returns across 2023-2025.
  • Aggressive capacity expansion by Adani Cement stoked a race for market share and pressured pricing, correlating with sustained downside in ACC’s stock performance.
  • Fuel cost savings coincided with seasonal weak cement prices and a ~5% realisation drop, which contributed to weak pricing power and rich valuation multiples limiting upside, as seen in DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Weak pricing power and rich valuation multiples provided little scope for upside earnings revisions, consistent with the stock’s persistent underperformance and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.

Optimism that failed

  • Strong underlying demand growth of 11-12% in FY24 and ~14-15% YoY Q1FY24 volumes failed to sustain, as later reports showed FY27 demand growth slowing to ~5% and 1QFY27 sales volume declining ~6% YoY.
  • Sharply lower global fuel costs generating Rs250-350/te savings were reversed, with later reports citing higher imported petcoke/thermal coal costs and peak fuel impact expected in 2QFY27.
  • High volumes supporting operating leverage did not materialize, as EBITDA/t dropped ~40% YoY to INR404 in 1QFY27 and opex/t rose 7% YoY.
