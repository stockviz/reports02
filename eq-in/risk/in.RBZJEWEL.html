RBZ Jewellers — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure despite strong topline growth. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was ₹121 crores, up 60% Y-o-Y, and EBITDA was ₹18 crores, up 39% Y-o-Y, with EBITDA margin at 14.9%. Management attributed the relatively softer margin expansion to several factors: the average gold rate remained stagnant, resulting in negligible inventory gain; amortisation of lease assets of ₹76 lakhs and a lease liability impact of ₹115 lakhs; and pre-operative costs for the upcoming Surat store, including employee hiring, training, BTL activities, advertising and stock planning booked ahead of launch. PAT came in at ₹9 crores, a 28% increase, translating to a 7.5% PAT margin.

Cost build-up ahead of store openings. Management indicated that marketing and employee expenses will remain on a rising trend as the company invests in brand building for the new stores. Approximately 52–60 people were hired for the Surat store, and new senior roles (head merchandisers, head supply chain, head vigilance and security) are being added. Management explicitly flagged that increased marketing spend should be expected in the coming quarters.

Gold price volatility and unhedged inventory. The company’s inventory of roughly ₹400 crores is currently largely unhedged, with management noting a gap of around 18% between book value and market value observed on a weekly basis. The transition to a hedged inventory position via Gold Metal Loans is expected to take about three years, with a phased approach (some portion this fiscal, potentially 50% next year, 75% in the third year).

Capacity underutilisation. The factory’s total capacity remains around 1.8–2 tons per year, while actual consumption is roughly 900–1,000 kgs, implying utilisation of about 50%. Utilisation rises to 70–90% during peak season months (July–September), but remains low off-season.

Limited near-term visibility on new store economics. Management declined to share revenue forecasts for the Surat store, stating it was “too early,” and also declined to disclose division-wise EBITDA (retail, wholesale, job work) for competitive reasons.

Dependence on converting B2B partners to job work. Job work is described as the highest-margin, most asset-light segment with a fast working capital cycle. However, management noted it cannot compel B2B players to convert to job work — it depends on consumer preferences — and the company is prepared to supply via outright wholesale sale if conversion does not happen.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue growth of 60% Y-o-Y was described as in line with prior guidance, driven by robust consumer demand, higher jewellery sales and strengthening brand recall. Retail revenue was ₹78 crores (up 70% Y-o-Y), wholesale revenue was ₹42 crores (up 47% Y-o-Y), and job work revenue was approximately ₹1.2 crores.

Retail expansion pipeline. Surat is slated to launch in Q2 (targeted for late September, ahead of Navratri on 11 October), with Rajkot in early Q3. Maninagar and Gandhinagar (both mid-format) are planned for Q3, potentially in November, to capture the wedding season. Large-format stores are ~10,000 sq ft with inventory deployment of ₹125–150 crores; mid-format stores are ~5,000 sq ft with ~₹50 crores (plus/minus 10–15%). Capex for the Surat store was approximately ₹10 crores, with capex break-even targeted within a year or less.

Demonstrated retail track record. The Ahmedabad Harit Zaveri Jewellers store opened in FY21 with ₹24 crores turnover and closed last fiscal year at ₹408 crores — growth achieved without legacy brand inheritance, which management cites as evidence the model can be replicated in Surat and Rajkot.

Strong order book and trade show response. Management described IIJS as “really warm,” with buyer growth, good order backing, and additions of family jewellers. July and August order books were described as full, with Q2 performance expected to be in line with earlier projections. Five major jewellery exhibitions and trade shows were attended during the quarter across Saurashtra, North Gujarat and Ahmedabad.

Caratage diversification. 18-karat sales, introduced in December, are expected to reach at least 20% of the corporate (B2B) mix by year-end, versus 100% 22-karat previously. Management noted 18-karat demand is region-specific and 22-karat is expected to remain dominant in occasion wear.

Structural industry shift. Management cited the shift from unorganized to organized jewellery retail, with 60% of the industry still unorganized and white space available in Gujarat, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh. The company positions itself in antique gold bridal/occasion wear, described as a profitable gold segment with more resilient demand than daily wear.

Funding capacity for expansion. A sanctioned debt facility of approximately ₹300 crores is in place and yet to be utilised, which management states is sufficient for the four stores planned this financial year. Debt-to-equity is expected to remain below 1:1 by fiscal year-end (approximately 0.8 debt to 1 equity), with a longer-term target of 1.5–2:1, largely via lower-cost Gold Metal Loans (~3–3.5% versus ~9% on existing borrowing).

Franchise optionality. Management indicated brand-building investment is intended to make the brand attractive for a future franchise route, though it stated it is “too early to say anything” and would stick to the brand-building phase for now.

3. Key Risks

  • Gold price and inventory risk: Unhedged inventory of ~₹400 crores exposes the company to gold price volatility; the hedging transition is multi-year.
  • Execution risk on store rollout: Four stores are planned this financial year, with three clustered in Q3, creating concentration of launch and pre-operative costs.
  • Margin trajectory risk: Management guided that marketing and employee costs will rise, with no commitment to near-term margin recovery.
  • Leverage risk: Debt-to-equity is targeted to rise to 1.5–2:1 over time, dependent on credit rating improvement (from BBB+ positive outlook toward an “A” band) and banking relationships.
  • Segment mix risk: The intended shift toward retail (targeting 75:25 retail:B2B long term) depends on consumer response in new cities where the brand has no legacy.
  • Job work conversion risk: The highest-margin segment’s growth depends on B2B partners’ willingness to supply gold in advance, which management cannot control.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The company’s own disclaimer notes that forward-looking statements involve known and unknown risks, uncertainties and other factors that may cause actual results, financial condition, performance or achievements to differ materially.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Guidance met: Management stated Q1 FY27 revenue growth was “in line with the guidance shared in the previous quarter,” and the reported 60% Y-o-Y revenue growth was described as delivering on that guidance.

Mix guidance vs. current mix: Management targets a B2B:B2C mix of roughly 50-50 “in a year or two,” and 75:25 (75 retail) in the long term. In Q1 FY27, retail revenue was ₹78 crores against wholesale of ₹42 crores and job work of ~₹1.2 crores, with job work described as 54% of the mix — indicating the retail transformation is underway but the long-term target remains ahead.

Store timeline guidance: Surat was guided for Q2 (late September, before Navratri), Rajkot for early Q3 (before October), and Maninagar and Gandhinagar for Q3 (possibly November). These are forward commitments against which actual launches have not yet been reported in the supplied material.

Break-even guidance: Capex break-even for new stores is guided at “a year or less,” reiterated when directly asked. No store has yet operated long enough to validate this.

Margin guidance: Management did not guide to margin recovery, instead indicating marketing and employee expenses will rise. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 14.9% and PAT margin of 7.5% reflect the pre-operative cost drag management described.

Capacity utilisation guidance: Management guided that peak-season utilisation reaches 70–90%, versus ~50% currently, with Q2 described as “very heavy for corporate job workload.”

Hedging guidance: Management guided a phased shift to GML-hedged inventory over roughly three years, with partial adoption this fiscal year. GML use has already begun “a little bit” in Q1 FY27, with sub-limit negotiations ongoing with banks.

Long-term profit mix guidance: Management stated that while the sales mix may be 50-50 or 60-40, “profitability will come from retail side” over the long term, approximately three to four years out.
