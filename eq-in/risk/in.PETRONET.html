Petronet LNG — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Middle East conflict and the Strait of Hormuz. Armed conflict and related hostilities in the Middle East commencing 28 February 2026 disrupted maritime navigation through the Strait of Hormuz and LNG loading operations in the region. No LNG cargo was loaded under the SPA during the quarter. Qatar Energy has been declaring force majeure on a month-on-month basis, typically at least a week before the end of each month, and as of the call it had been declared through end-August. Management noted that no one can say when the Strait will reopen.

Volume decline and capacity utilization. Dahej processed 192 TBTU in Q1 FY27 versus 207 TBTU a year earlier and 201 TBTU in the prior quarter. Company-level volumes were 207 TBTU versus 220 TBTU year-on-year and 219 TBTU sequentially. Because Dahej’s nameplate capacity rose from 17.5 MMTPA to 22.5 MMTPA effective 31 March 2026, utilization optically fell: Dahej at 66% (versus 92% year-on-year and 90% sequentially) and Kochi at 23.27%, with overall company utilization at 58% versus 76% in both comparative periods. April was described as the weakest month, with May and June picking up.

Vessel suspension and arbitration. Claims aggregating to Rs. 348.13 crore as at 30 June 2026 have been made by owners of the company’s dedicated LNG vessels and for other associated costs arising from suspension of vessel operations, leading to arbitration proceedings initiated by vessel owners. Management clarified that time charter vessel operations were suspended not under a force majeure clause but under other clauses in the time charter agreements enabling suspension.

Use or Pay dues. Trade receivables as at 30 June 2026 include Use or Pay dues of Rs. 661.03 crore gross and Rs. 312.17 crore net, after a provision of Rs. 348.86 crore.

Regulatory compliance. The company incurred and paid penalties of Rs. 75,400 each (Rs. 76,700 including GST) to BSE and NSE for non-compliance or delayed compliance with Regulation 17(1) of SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015 for the quarter ended 30 June 2026.

Other structural risks. Volatility in international LNG prices, foreign exchange fluctuations (particularly USD-INR), delays in gas infrastructure development and pipeline connectivity, regulatory uncertainties, and potential competition from renewable energy and alternate fuels.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Financial resilience despite lower volumes. Standalone PBT was Rs. 1,514 crore versus Rs. 1,136 crore year-on-year (up 33%), and standalone PAT was Rs. 1,133 crore versus Rs. 851 crore (up 33%). Consolidated PBT was Rs. 1,491 crore and consolidated PAT Rs. 1,137 crore — the highest ever PBT and PAT for any first quarter. Standalone revenue from operations was Rs. 5,554.14 crore; consolidated revenue from operations was Rs. 5,557.84 crore.

Trading and inventory gains. Gross margin improvement was driven by trading gains of Rs. 301 crore and inventory gains of Rs. 193 crore. Management characterized this as an established business model over the last five to six years: when spot prices are significantly above long-term prices and throughput falls, trading margins kick in even on small spot volumes (about 6 TBTU). This trading activity was described as independent of the Qatar contract and of spare capacity.

Replacement volumes and tolling. More than two-thirds of the missing Middle East volumes were compensated by volumes from other parts of the world, brought in by customers under tolling and service contracts. Management expects replacement volumes to continue flowing to the terminal if the Qatar force majeure persists, citing Indian gas demand and government efforts to ensure equitable gas supply across sectors.

New contracts commenced. Contracts with Deepak Fertilizers at Dahej (approximately 0.5 MMTPA) and increased ExxonMobil volumes at Kochi have both started; two cargoes were lifted under the Deepak Fertilizers contract, which commenced in May 2026.

Structural demand and expansion. Growth prospects include India’s target to raise natural gas’s share of the primary energy mix to 15% by 2030, expansion of City Gas Distribution networks, the Dahej expansion to 22.5 MMTPA, the greenfield 5 MMTPA LNG storage and regasification project at Gopalpur, Odisha, the petrochemical complex at Dahej, new long-term contracts, small-scale LNG infrastructure, and emerging opportunities in renewables and green hydrogen.

Sustainability targets. Management has approved a Net Zero target for Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 2040 and aims for 100% renewable energy adoption by 2035, alongside group captive renewable power projects and expanded truck-loading bays.

Shareholder returns. The Board recommended a final dividend of Rs. 3.00 per equity share for FY 2025-26 — the 20th consecutive year of dividend recommendations. The record date was Friday, 12 June 2026. The 28th AGM is convened for Monday, 28 September 2026 at 3:00 P.M. IST via video conference, with remote e-voting from 24 to 27 September 2026. A special window runs from 5 February 2026 to 4 February 2027 for transfer and dematerialisation of physical securities sold or purchased prior to 1 April 2019.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued Strait of Hormuz closure or prolonged Qatar force majeure, with declarations made only about a week before each month begins, limiting forward visibility.
  • Arbitration proceedings and claims of Rs. 348.13 crore from dedicated LNG vessel owners.
  • Recoverability of Use or Pay dues, with Rs. 348.86 crore provided against gross dues of Rs. 661.03 crore.
  • Pricing and margin normalization: management indicated the trading and inventory gain trend depends on spot prices remaining significantly above long-term prices, and that this dynamic can reverse.
  • Foreign exchange and international LNG price volatility.
  • Infrastructure delays, particularly pipeline connectivity, and regulatory uncertainty.
  • Competition from renewable energy and alternate fuels.
  • Compliance lapses of the kind that led to the BSE and NSE penalties.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Capacity utilization. Management linked the utilization trajectory to the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, stating that once Qatari and regional volumes become available, utilization should improve considerably. Observed performance: utilization fell to 66% at Dahej and 23.27% at Kochi on the expanded capacity base, though management noted the company is running at the May–June rate and expects a slight improvement.

Trading and inventory gains. Management guided that if the current market situation persists — spot prices well above long-term prices — similar trading and inventory gains could continue, describing this as the business model observed over the last five to six years. Observed performance: trading gains of Rs. 301 crore and inventory gains of Rs. 193 crore drove gross margin improvement in Q1 FY27. Management also indicated these gains are independent of spare capacity and Qatar volumes.

Kochi pipeline connectivity. Management stated that, per the latest information, mechanical completion of the final connectivity should occur by the end of the current quarter. This remains a forward-looking expectation.

Petrochemical project. Management stated the project is on schedule, with physical progress of about 40% completed. Q1 FY27 petchem capex was approximately Rs. 470–472 crore; cumulative petchem capex to date was stated as Rs. 679 crore. FY27 budgeted capex is Rs. 9,064 crore, with similar levels expected in FY28. Management declined to disclose updated equity IRR figures pending completion of commercial contracts, noting two contracts already done — 600 KTPA of ethane handling and the arrangement with Deepak Phenolics. Propane sourcing contracts are expected to be signed in 2027, with supply potentially from the Middle East and the USA. Useful life for such projects is generally assumed at 25 years.

Contract renewal with offtakers. Management stated there is no discussion on tariff revision and status quo prevails, but that talks with offtakers are ongoing almost daily, with closure expected in the next two to three quarters, ahead of the new Qatar contract starting in 2028. Current tariffs were stated as approximately Rs. 69 at Dahej and Rs. 98 at Kochi.

New Qatar contract. The contract from 2028 is on a DES basis rather than FOB, with a formula-driven price that includes shipping cost; the formula was not disclosed. Most LNG carriers’ time charters end when the SPA ends, except one.

Use or Pay recovery. Management expressed confidence in recovering outstanding UoP dues based on contractual arrangements and recovery mechanisms agreed with customers. Management also clarified the order of priority: current-year commitments must be adjusted first, and only then can offsets for prior years be taken; tolling cargoes can retire Use or Pay faster, depending on which offtaker holds the obligation. Long-term contracts carry take-or-pay clauses, distinct from Use or Pay.

Penalty disclosure. Management stated that information regarding the penalty and non-compliance shall be put up to the Board, and the Board’s comments shall be duly informed to NSE and BSE.

Ind AS impact for the quarter. At gross margin level a positive of Rs. 14 crore, forex loss of Rs. 5 crore, other expenses a positive of Rs. 8 crore, depreciation Rs. 66 crore, and finance cost Rs. 48 crore.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a cautious Hold in June 2023, centred on refining/OMC sector risk, rich valuations and high spot LNG prices, to an Accumulate in August 2026 focused on PETRONET’s LNG volumes, 3rd-party growth and trading/inventory gains. LNG price/supply risk and weak Dahej/Kochi utilisation remained persistent themes, while OMC/refining-margin concerns were replaced by capex/FCF and Qatar contract-renewal as the key swing factors.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning that high spot LNG prices would constrain India’s LNG imports materialised: Qatar’s Hormuz force majeure cut long-term Dahej volumes from 105 TBtu to 12 TBtu and total volumes fell ~6% YoY, contributing to utilisation cuts and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes for the stock.
  • The early risk of volatile energy prices/policy uncertainty also materialised in the LNG business: spot LNG stayed significantly above long-term prices and tax implications on direct trading made PLNG less competitive, forcing FY27E utilisation/estimate downgrades.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of modest/falling LNG prices lifting gas demand failed — spot LNG remained significantly above long-term prices and Qatar’s force majeure caused long-term Dahej volumes to collapse from 105 TBtu to 12 TBtu.
  • The expectation of strong Indian gas consumption growth translating into higher PETRONET volumes failed — total volumes declined 5.5% QoQ/6.0% YoY and Kochi utilisation remained weak at 23.3%.
