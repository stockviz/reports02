A2Z Infra Engineering Limited — Recent Developments and Outlook

1. Headwinds and Challenges

The company is navigating a difficult financial position. Accumulated losses stand at INR 1,07,659.78 Lakhs as of the year ended March 31, 2026, and have risen further to INR 1,07,683.55 Lakhs by the quarter ended June 30, 2026, resulting in substantial erosion of net worth. For the June 2026 quarter, the company incurred a net loss after tax of INR 23.77 Lakhs.

Liquidity is acutely strained. Current liabilities exceed current assets by INR 6,081.20 Lakhs, and the company cites delayed realization of trade receivables as a persistent problem. It has defaulted on repayment of principal and interest to banks and financial institutions, leading to certain loan accounts being classified as Non-Performing Assets (NPAs). Certain lenders have filed applications with the Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT), and other parties have approached the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) for recovery of dues.

The statutory auditors have issued a disclaimer of opinion on the company’s ability to continue as a going concern for FY2026, and a disclaimer of conclusion on both standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, citing multiple uncertainties including going concern assumptions, unrecognised interest on NPA borrowings, and pending lender confirmations.

The company also faces ongoing legal proceedings and investigations. Mr. Amit Mittal, Managing Director cum CEO and Promoter, was taken into judicial custody by the Economic Offence Wing–Anti Corruption Bureau (EOW-ACB), Chhattisgarh, in connection with a matter concerning the company and its material subsidiary, A2Z Infraservices Ltd. He was subsequently released on bail by the Hon’ble High Court of Chhattisgarh via order dated July 03, 2026. The court’s order noted that he was not named in the FIR, had cooperated fully with the investigation, that the charge-sheet had already been filed, and that operational control of the company appeared, prima facie, to have been exercised by Mr. N. Uday. The order also observed that no independent material demonstrated he personally instructed payment of illegal commission, prepared forged documents, handled alleged cash transactions, or participated in the alleged conspiracy.

Separately, disputes regarding cogeneration power plants have resulted in fully impaired assets. The company is also exposed to macro-economic, customer concentration, working capital, execution, legal, contractual, and talent risks.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Despite the stressed financials, the company reported year-on-year growth in consolidated turnover of 14.92% to INR 38,643.79 Lakhs for FY2026, alongside an improvement in consolidated net profit to INR 523.61 Lakhs. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, the company recorded exceptional gains of Rs. 527.79 lakhs.

The company operates through three Strategic Business Units: Engineering Services (ES), Facility Management Services (FMS), and Municipal Solid Waste Management (MSW). It sees expanding opportunities in the Power Transmission & Distribution (T&D) EPC market, driven by the planned integration of over 500 GW of renewable capacity by 2030. In telecom, 5G rollouts are expected to create demand. Increasing urbanisation is expected to drive demand for professional FMS, while government initiatives under the Swachh Bharat Mission and Smart Cities Mission support MSW opportunities.

On the debt front, the company has entered into One Time Settlement (OTS) agreements with certain lenders and is in discussions with the remaining lenders. Management is also in discussions with certain customers for immediate recovery of amounts due and believes a substantial portion of trade receivables will be realized within the upcoming year.

3. Key Risks

  • Going concern risk: Auditors have disclaimed opinions on going concern for FY2026 and on the June 2026 quarterly results.
  • Liquidity and receivables risk: Delayed realization of trade receivables and a working capital shortfall (current liabilities exceeding current assets by INR 6,081.20 Lakhs).
  • Debt recovery risk: DRT and NCLT proceedings by lenders, plus NPA classification of borrowings.
  • Legal and regulatory risk: Ongoing legal proceedings, EOW investigations involving a subsidiary and a director, and cogeneration power plant disputes with fully impaired assets.
  • Operational and market risks: Macro-economic, customer concentration, execution, contractual, and talent risks.
  • Unrecognised interest risk: Interest on NPA borrowings remains unrecognised, and lender confirmations are pending.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Management states it will continue to leverage technical expertise and execution track record to secure selective, high-value opportunities in the EPC segments of Telecom and Power T&D, while significantly expanding its presence in FMS and exploring viable MSW projects. It remains committed to continuous debt reduction through prudent financial management, operational efficiency, and constructive engagement with lenders to strengthen the balance sheet and support sustainable long-term growth, while noting actual results may vary due to economic, market, and regulatory conditions.

Observed performance shows some alignment with the growth narrative: consolidated turnover grew 14.92% year-on-year to INR 38,643.79 Lakhs and consolidated net profit improved to INR 523.61 Lakhs for FY2026. However, the standalone result for FY2026 was a net loss of INR 279.25 Lakhs, and the June 2026 quarter showed a net loss after tax of INR 23.77 Lakhs, indicating that profitability remains uneven across entities and periods.

On debt resolution, management’s stated intent to reduce debt through OTS agreements and lender engagement is partially reflected in the OTS agreements already entered into with certain lenders, though negotiations with remaining lenders continue. On receivables, management expects increased realization within the upcoming year and believes remaining debts can be settled in due course, allowing financial statements to be prepared on a going concern basis. The auditors’ disclaimers on going concern and the June 2026 results indicate that these expectations remain subject to significant uncertainty. Regarding subsidiary disputes, management believes the company has reasonable chances of succeeding before the Arbitral Tribunal and does not foresee any material liability, though the cogeneration plant assets have already been fully impaired.
