Headwinds and Challenges

Global shocks and input-cost disruption. Management describes five major shocks over five years that hit operations and projects: the Ukraine War distorting oil, feedstock, steel and machinery prices; a deep and continuing slowdown in Chinese domestic consumption combined with a flood of new chemical investments there; exports priced below cost that depressed prices across China, the Far East, Asia and Europe; Covid-19 driving up steel, machinery and engineering costs and delaying the Dahej and US projects; US tariffs rising from near 3% to 50% or more since early 2026, affecting Indian customers and the ongoing US investment; and the Gaza conflict and Iran war raising logistics, oil and feedstock prices.

Feedstock and working-capital strain. Over roughly eighteen months, operations suffered from very high raw material costs, shortages and long supply-chain lead times. The company moved from nearly 100% domestic feedstock supply to 100% imports almost overnight, requiring it to buy 150,000–200,000 tonnes per year of liquid raw material from uncertain global supply. High feedstock prices in India pushed the company against its credit limits, and reduced operations from procurement difficulty lowered capacity utilisation. Because surplus energy for internal use is a major product at each plant, lower operating levels sharply spiked energy and conversion costs.

Balance-sheet deterioration. With two large investments underway and depressed cash flow from the existing business, the company moved from being debt-free in 2021-23 to a high-debt situation with elevated finance costs.

Project execution delays. The Gujarat PA and fine chemicals project suffered significant machinery supply and construction delays and escalation, though it has since started up, stabilised and is operating at near 90–95% capacity, with raw material largely imported and logistics erratic. The US project was affected by every global event described, driving up equipment, logistics and construction costs, more so in the US.

Liquidity position at 30 June 2026. The Group incurred a net loss of ₹4,367 lakhs for the quarter and, as at that date, Group current liabilities exceeded current assets by ₹74,212 lakhs, primarily due to capital expenditure by TCL Specialties LLC, USA for its ongoing plant construction. Standalone current liabilities exceeded current assets by ₹32,528 lakhs (excluding loans/receivables from subsidiaries of ₹45,655 lakhs). Current liabilities include ₹10,236 lakhs of short-term working capital and cash credit facilities at Group level and ₹6,476 lakhs at company level.

GST show cause notice. On August 19, 2026, the company disclosed receipt of a show cause notice from the Office of the Assistant Commissioner, Central GST and Central Excise, Audit Commissionerate, Vadodara, under Section 65 of the CGST Act, 2017, issued under Section 74 for non-reversal of ITC on stock difference relating to the audit period April 2020 to March 2024. The tax amount stated is ₹33.47 lakhs; interest and penalty are not ascertained. The company states it believes the demands are erroneous and not sustainable, will respond within prescribed timelines, and believes the financial implication, if any, is likely to be very insignificant with no material impact on financial position.

Malaysian subsidiary restructuring. The Malaysian subsidiary has mothballed the front-end maleic plant, sharply reduced costs and is running the derivatives plant and business. It no longer needs parent support and is studying all options, to move when the environment improves.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Gujarat project stabilised. The PA and fine chemicals project in Gujarat, located within its consumption cluster and close to one of the largest global feed producers, has started up, stabilised well and is operating at near 90–95% capacity. The team there has begun improvement work with support from the South India production centre.

US market fundamentals. US butane prices have remained low and steady, among the lowest cost globally, and butane and water are the only raw materials for all US products. The US is the largest global market for the company’s food ingredients and continues to show steady growth; the US producer currently supplies only 25% of US domestic demand, with the rest imported. US prices remain the highest globally, far above Asia and EU prices, creating good margins. Tariff and supply-chain disruption have created an intense drive toward US domestic sourcing by customers in every segment. The company has served these markets for over 20 years and has intensified marketing and seeding in the US over the last five years, with positive responses.

US project nearing production. Construction is complete and stage-wise start-up is in progress. The target is to start production soon and ramp up progressively during 2027 and 2028, which would make the US subsidiary independent of India funding support and progressively bring the parent and group to low debt and finance costs over the next few years.

Steadier demand and margins in India. The company has managed to switch between domestic and imported feedstock at short notice, a situation expected to last until the global refining situation settles. Markets and customers have adjusted to disruptions, and management reports starting to see steadier demand with decent margins — not the best situation yet, but getting better.

Cost and efficiency initiatives. Across the company and subsidiaries there is a major initiative to improve efficiency and drive down costs, with good early results, healthy competition between teams and sites, and sharing of learnings. Management refers to early green shoots of better performance, with the challenge being to sustain this.

Working capital and debt reduction. Finance and operating teams have driven changes in working capital management through better structured credit lines from suppliers and bankers, described as work-in-progress and dynamic. Reducing debt is stated as a priority.

Fund raising and asset disposal. On August 4, 2026, the Board approved raising funds aggregating up to ₹750 crores by way of debt and/or equity shares, fully/partly convertible debentures, non-convertible debentures, warrants or other convertible instruments, through public offer, private placement including QIP, preferential issue, rights issue or other permissible modes, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals. Proceeds are intended for day-to-day operations and refinance/repayment of existing debts. The Board also approved the sale of four windmills with 3.2 MW installed capacity on 11.42 acres in Muthunaickenpatti, Dindigul District, Tamil Nadu, contributing revenue of ₹158 lakhs (0.11% of revenue) in the last financial year, for consideration of ₹10.35 crores to TN Oxygen Private Limited, a Chennai-based manufacturing company not belonging to the promoter/promoter group/group companies, with completion expected 30 days from signing.

Malaysian asset quality. The MAN plant is described as in excellent condition, with on-site butane access and exceptional access to ASEAN and India markets.

Management’s historical framing. Management notes that the company came out of the severe 2009-2012 meltdown, Covid and earlier crises stronger, healthier, with lower debt, better profits and value, and states it has stronger and more experienced teams now.

Key Risks

  • Continued uncertainty in global refining, feedstock prices and logistics, requiring continued switching between domestic and imported feedstock.
  • Elevated debt and finance costs, with debt reduction dependent on execution of the US ramp-up, fund raising and asset disposals.
  • Group and standalone current liabilities exceeding current assets as at 30 June 2026, with the going-concern assessment dependent on cash flow projections, renewal of revolving working capital facilities, unutilised credit facilities, disposal of non-core assets and completion of debt/equity raising.
  • US subsidiary financing: TCL Specialties LLC is engaging prospective lenders and financing partners to raise debt funding of up to USD 130–180 million to support the ongoing project, refinance existing borrowings and meet initial working capital. A top-tier investment banker has been appointed as arranger and bookrunner; multiple financing alternatives are being evaluated; the proposed financing is expected to be completed by October 2026, with indicative term sheets received so far under evaluation.
  • Tariff and trade policy uncertainty affecting Indian customers and the US investment.
  • Execution risk on the US start-up and progressive ramp-up during 2027 and 2028.
  • The GST show cause notice, with interest and penalty not ascertained, though the company believes the impact is likely very insignificant.
  • Subsidiary-level matters: the Malaysian front-end maleic plant remains mothballed pending an improved environment; one subsidiary’s interim results were reviewed by other auditors and two subsidiaries’ interim results were not reviewed by their auditors (the latter described as not material to the Group).
  • Auditor change at a material subsidiary: M/s. Walker Chandiok & Co LLP resigned as Statutory Auditors of TCL Intermediates Private Limited with effect from August 28, 2026, to align statutory auditors across the Group following mandatory auditor rotation requirements at the Holding Company.
