TruAlt Bioenergy: Recent Developments and Outlook

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Policy and procurement disruption in ethanol. Management notes that disruptions during ESY 2025-26 resulted in lower-than-anticipated allocations and temporary underutilisation of industry capacities. This is the clearest operational headwind described for the core ethanol business.

Underutilised capacity. Capacity utilisation stood at 60.57% in Q1 FY27. While framed as growth headroom, it also indicates that a substantial portion of the expanded 2,000 KLPD platform is not yet producing.

CBG earnings pressure from maintenance. In the CBG segment, the company prioritised long-term reliability over short-term profitability, undertaking scheduled maintenance of critical equipment and investing in plant infrastructure and process improvements. This raised operating expenditure, which was described as largely maintenance-led and non-recurring, and temporarily impacted earnings. Segment figures reflect this: CBG total income rose to ₹11.31 Cr in Q1 FY27 from ₹10.19 Cr, but EBITDA fell to ₹6.24 Cr from ₹6.65 Cr, PBT declined to ₹5.28 Cr from ₹5.90 Cr, and PAT declined to ₹4.37 Cr from ₹4.89 Cr.

Geopolitical and crude oil volatility. Heightened geopolitical tensions in West Asia and resulting volatility in crude oil markets led the company to defer accelerated rollout of its retail fuel network. The retail segment also faces highly competitive fuel pricing. Retail PAT fell to ₹0.05 Cr in Q1 FY27 from ₹0.11 Cr a year earlier, a decline of 54.55%, even as total income rose 132.80% to ₹4.40 Cr from ₹1.89 Cr.

Governance and compliance issues. - The special resolution for fixation of commission payable to Mr. Vishal Nirani, Executive Director, was not passed at the 5th AGM held on August 31, 2026, failing to secure the requisite majority. Five other resolutions passed, including adoption of financial statements, re-appointment of a director, ratification of Cost Auditors’ remuneration, and appointment of Secretarial Auditors. - NSE and BSE issued notices on August 25, 2026 imposing monetary fines of ₹4,55,000 each plus applicable GST for alleged non-compliance with Regulation 17(1) of the SEBI Listing Regulations, pertaining to Board composition and failure to appoint a woman independent director. The company stated the financial impact is limited to the fines imposed and that it is examining the notices and will place the matter before the Board at the ensuing Board Meeting.

Public narrative risk. The Managing Director flagged that greater public visibility for the biofuels sector has led to misconceptions and incomplete narratives about ethanol, which the company views as a reputational challenge requiring transparent, data-grounded engagement.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Dual-feed transformation. Installed ethanol capacity rose 43% from 1,400 KLPD in Q1 FY26 to 2,000 KLPD in Q1 FY27, with approximately 1,300 KLPD (65%) on dual-feed technology. This expanded the feedstock basket from predominantly sugar derivatives to sugar derivatives plus grains unfit for human consumption, enabling near year-round production. Grain-based operations delivered approximately 6% higher profitability than sugar-based production. The platform can produce roughly 5.5 to 6 crore litres of ethanol per month.

Policy momentum in ethanol. India achieved 20% ethanol blending five years ahead of schedule, with production capacity approaching 2,000 crore litres per annum. Introduction of E22, E25, E27 and E30 BIS standards, flex-fuel vehicle ecosystem development, and E100-compatible vehicle policy clarity are cited as long-term demand drivers.

CBG expansion. Construction is underway on four CBG plants under the JV with Sumitomo Corporation via TruAlt Gas Pvt. Ltd., with phased commissioning targeted from Q2 FY27. Preparatory activities are progressing for six additional plants with GAIL (India) Limited through Leafiniti Bioenergy. Total planned CBG capacity across the programme is 162.2 TPD. Phase I comprises four units at ₹85 crore capital investment per unit (₹340 crore total), with ramp-up of 40% in FY27, 85% in FY28, and ~90% from FY29. ₹180 crore is committed across three locations with NABARD financing, plus a separate ₹60 crore equity commitment for the same three locations. Government support includes Central Financial Assistance under SATAT/MNRE (up to ₹4 crore per 4.8 TPD, capped at ₹10 crore per plant, covering approximately 20–30% of project cost), Market Development Assistance of ₹1,500/MT for FOM/LFOM, CBG-CGD synchronisation, the CBG Blending Obligation commencing at 1% in FY2025-26, and the Union Budget 2026-27 exemption of central excise duty on the biogas and CBG component in blended CNG.

SAF opportunity. The proposed 100 million litres per annum (310 KLPD) Ethanol-to-SAF facility at Srikakulam, Andhra Pradesh, represents a proposed investment of approximately ₹2,250 crore. It has received a ₹150 crore grant under PM JI-VAN Yojana, approved by the Centre for High Technology under MoPNG, expected to incentivise approximately 7–10% of project capex. A technology licensing agreement with Honeywell UOP provides access to validated SAF conversion technology. India’s announced SAF blending targets are 1% by 2027, 2% by 2028, and 5% by 2030, alongside CORSIA frameworks.

Co-product monetisation. DDGS production commenced from Q3, establishing an additional revenue stream. The company also produces Fermented Organic Manure, Liquid Fermented Organic Manure, and captures liquefied CO2 for industrial applications.

Retail network. Seven outlets are operational, with four additional under construction and 76 additional locations shortlisted, under a phased 100-outlet strategy.

Structural tailwinds. India imports nearly 85% of its crude oil requirements; biofuels are positioned as a long-term import substitution opportunity. Global capital is increasingly flowing toward energy transition sectors, and India’s bioeconomy aligns with the Viksit Bharat 2047 vision.

3. Key Risks

  • Policy and allocation risk: Ethanol industry operates within an evolving policy and procurement environment; ESY 2025-26 disruptions demonstrated exposure to allocation decisions.
  • Execution and commissioning risk: CBG plants and the SAF project are subject to construction timelines, statutory approvals, and financial closure. SAF commissioning is targeted within 24–30 months, subject to necessary approvals.
  • Commodity and market risk: Crude oil volatility, competitive retail fuel pricing, and agricultural commodity supply-chain disruptions affect downstream and feedstock economics.
  • Regulatory compliance risk: The exchange fines and the failed special resolution on executive commission point to governance and board composition matters requiring resolution.
  • Project financing risk: The SAF project requires financial closure; the ₹150 crore grant is to be released in accordance with project milestones and a Memorandum of Agreement with the Centre for High Technology.
  • Feedstock risk: Although dual-feed integration mitigates this, the business remains exposed to feedstock availability and pricing for both sugar derivatives and grains.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance provided: - Immediate focus on increasing gross capacity utilisation, improving operational efficiencies, and driving higher production from existing assets. - CBG phased commissioning targeted from Q2 FY27 onwards; specific plant commissioning targets: Mudhol August 2026, Kedarnath September 2026, Badami December 2026, Bhima Patas construction to commence upon completion of documentation. - SAF project progressing towards Front-End Engineering Design stage with Honeywell UOP, targeting financial closure and commissioning within 24–30 months, subject to approvals. - Retail expansion to be calibrated and disciplined, with Phase I comprising 100 fuel stations; four additional outlets to launch in the coming months, reaching 11 operational outlets in Karnataka. - Confidence in delivering a successful FY27 and creating enduring stakeholder value. - Ethanol expected to remain the core cash flow and operating platform.

Observed performance against guidance: - The dual-feed integration programme was completed, and Q1 FY27 was the first full operational quarter following it, with capacity utilisation at 60.57% — consistent with management’s stated focus on raising utilisation from the existing base. - Consolidated total income nearly doubled to ₹641.41 Cr from ₹326.63 Cr; EBITDA rose to ₹132.76 Cr from ₹41.54 Cr; PBT rose to ₹78.45 Cr from ₹5.80 Cr; PAT rose to ₹59.27 Cr from ₹4.73 Cr. - Standalone total income rose 99% to ₹630.37 Cr from ₹316.49 Cr; EBITDA rose 263% to ₹126.61 Cr from ₹34.89 Cr; PBT rose to ₹73.31 Cr from ₹0.13 Cr; PAT rose to ₹55.01 Cr from ₹0.03 Cr. - CBG segment performance declined year-on-year on EBITDA, PBT, and PAT, attributed to maintenance-led, non-recurring expenditure — a temporary deviation from the growth trajectory management projects for the segment. - Retail segment PAT declined despite higher income, reflecting the competitive pricing environment management acknowledged. - The CBG commissioning timeline guidance (Q2 FY27 onwards) had not yet been validated by commissioning as of the Q1 FY27 disclosure; construction progress was reported at 90–95% civil completion and 65–70% mechanical erection across the Sumitomo JV sites. - The SAF project remained pre-financial-closure, with engineering, approvals, and stakeholder engagement advancing. - The failed special resolution on executive commission and the exchange fines for board composition represent governance matters not addressed in the forward-looking guidance.
