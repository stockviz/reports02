Strides Pharma Science Limited — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Geopolitical volatility and supply chain disruption. Management described the operating environment as fluid, with the ongoing Middle East conflict producing supply chain disruptions, elevated freight costs, longer transit times and inflationary pressure across multiple cost lines. In Q1 FY27, this translated into roughly ₹13.1 crore of incremental operating and freight costs. Freight costs rose to 6.2% of revenue from 5.3% a year earlier, an increase of ₹18.9 crore, while other operating costs excluding freight rose ₹29.0 crore year-on-year on higher manufacturing costs.

US business softness and competitive pressure. US revenue for Q1 FY27 was ₹6,282 million (about $68 million), described as stable despite increased competition in certain products launched over recent quarters. Management stated the first two quarters of FY27 would be “very soft,” with the bulk of launches expected from the second half. Delays were noted in a few product approvals, expected to materialise over coming quarters.

Controlled substances quota constraints. Quota allocations occur roughly twice a year, around June and December, and depend on past sales history. Management noted the June cycle response was still awaited, with an answer expected the following month. The controlled substances portfolio contributed about 5% of revenues in the prior year, and the ramp-up has been slower than anticipated.

Ex-US shipment timing. Ex-US revenue was $63 million in Q1 FY27 versus $70 million in Q4 FY26, impacted by supply chain disruptions causing shipment delays across a few markets. Management characterised this as a timing rather than demand issue, with spillover business expected to be recovered over coming quarters.

Regulatory inspections. A USFDA cGMP inspection of the flagship Bengaluru facility was conducted May 12–20, 2026 and concluded with a Form 483 containing five observations. The company submitted a comprehensive response within the stipulated timeline. Separately, a routine cGMP inspection at the Formulations Facility at Alathur, Chennai was conducted September 2–11, 2026 and resulted in a Form 483 with three observations; the company stated it will respond within the stipulated time and keep the exchanges informed of further updates.

Working capital and returns. The cash-to-cash cycle stood at 123 days, up 7 days year-on-year, driven by a 24-day increase in inventory as supply chain resilience was prioritised, alongside a 15-day increase in payable days. ROCE on a trailing-twelve-month basis moderated to 15.3% from 15.8% in FY26 as capital employed grew on recent investments and currency movements.

Proposed US generic tariffs. Management flagged evolving developments around proposed tariffs on generic pharmaceutical products starting August 2028, noting limited visibility on the final implementation framework.

Product recall-related costs. Exceptional items in Q1 FY27 included ₹110.10 million of expenses related to product recall and settlements, alongside a ₹742.07 million gain on the Pivot Path transaction. Provisions relating to the ranitidine, losartan and testosterone recalls continued to be recorded.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Ex-US as the growth engine. Ex-US revenue grew 17% year-on-year to ₹5,875 million in Q1 FY27, with growth across multiple geographies. Management reiterated that Ex-US should grow faster than the company average over the foreseeable future and is an increasingly important earnings driver, with a healthy margin profile. The business spans B2C markets (UK, Nordics, South Africa, Africa including Francophone Africa and Kenya), B2B markets (Europe, Australia), and LATAM, MENA and APAC, where regulatory filings are underway and new dollars are expected from FY28–FY29.

US growth levers and FY28 aspiration. Management maintained a $375 million North America revenue aspiration by FY28, from a $284–285 million FY26 base, requiring roughly $80–90 million of additional revenue. Five levers were cited: controlled substances, new channels, new geographies and partnerships, new product launches, and the OTC portfolio. The commercialised US portfolio stood at 72 products after two launches in the quarter, with top-three market positions in 37 products contributing approximately 70% of US revenues. About 100-plus products are yet to be launched, with roughly 10 launches targeted between the Q1 FY27 call and March 31, 2027.

Complex dosage domains. Nasal sprays, transdermal patches, thin films and controlled substances are being developed around the Chestnut Ridge, US platform. The first nasal spray filing has reached advanced stages of review, with approval expected in H2 (Q3–Q4 timeframe); a second was filed about a month before the call with a 12–15 month review expected; a third filing was planned within a few months. Five to six nasal spray programmes are underway, with significant filing traction expected over 12–18 months, positioned as the driver of growth beyond the $375 million aspiration.

US manufacturing footprint. Nearly one-third of US revenues are supplied from the Chestnut Ridge facility, and all key growth platforms are being developed around it, which management views as balancing the business model against tariff developments.

Africa and other markets. Africa delivered another strong quarter led by expansion of the Brands business. The UK prescription business performed strongly, and Branded and OTC portfolios across Europe including the Nordics delivered a strong quarter with portfolio expansion, customer additions and improved market penetration.

Sandoz acquisition. The acquisition was not part of Q1 FY27 numbers; closure was expected sometime in Q2 subject to permissions, with contribution beginning in H2.

ESG progress. The EcoVadis sustainability score improved to 68 out of 100, a 19-point year-on-year improvement.

Rating upgrade. CARE Ratings upgraded long-term bank facilities to CARE A+; Stable from CARE A; Positive during the quarter.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued geopolitical volatility with new developments emerging almost daily, affecting freight, transit times and input costs.
  • Regulatory risk from open USFDA inspections: the Alathur, Chennai Form 483 with three observations (September 2026) and the Bengaluru Form 483 with five observations (May 2026), the latter subsequently closed with a Voluntary Action Indicated classification and an Establishment Inspection Report issued August 19, 2026.
  • Dependence of the FY28 US aspiration on complex products and controlled substances; management was asked what the next structural growth engine would be if this opportunity takes longer than expected, and pointed to nasal sprays, transdermal patches and thin films.
  • Quota allocation timing and dependence on demonstrated past sales history in controlled substances.
  • Proposed US tariffs on generic pharmaceuticals from August 2028, with limited visibility on the final framework.
  • Working capital intensity from inventory build and return profile pressure as recent investments mature.
  • Currency movements: management noted the rupee moved from about ₹82 to ₹95–97 against the dollar, which masked debt reduction of almost ₹3 billion during the prior year in reported terms.
  • Ongoing litigation and settlement exposure related to recalled products.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Gross margin. Guidance: maintain gross margins within 58–60%. Observed: Q1 FY27 gross margin expanded 60 basis points year-on-year to 60.9%, with absolute gross margin up 14% to ₹770.2 crore, driven by favourable business mix in Ex-US markets.

EBITDA. Observed: ₹229.8 crore, up 5.4% year-on-year, at an 18.2% margin — down 130 basis points year-on-year but ahead of 18.1% in Q4 FY26, after absorbing about ₹13.1 crore of geopolitical-related costs. Management described EBITDA margins as broadly in line with Q4 FY26 exit levels.

Profitability. Operational PAT was ₹123.1 crore, up 8% year-on-year, with EBITDA-to-operational-PAT conversion at 54% and operational EPS of ₹13.4. Reported PAT was ₹165.5 crore, up 56.7%, with reported EPS of ₹17.0, including a ₹74.2 crore gain on the Pivot Path divestment contributing ₹53.4 crore to reported PAT net of tax.

US trajectory. Guidance: first two quarters very soft, H2 stronger on new approvals, launches and portfolio optimisation; $375 million North America by FY28. Observed: Q1 FY27 US revenue of ₹6,282 million ($68 million), stable despite competition; two launches in the quarter; approval delays noted.

Ex-US trajectory. Guidance: grow faster than company average; Q4 FY26 shortfall to be recovered over coming quarters. Observed: 17% year-on-year growth to ₹5,875 million, but a sequential decline from $70 million to $63 million due to shipment timing.

Product launches. Guidance: about 10 launches between the Q1 FY27 call and March 31, 2027, with the majority expected to be higher-revenue-potential molecules. Observed: two launches in Q1 FY27.

Nasal spray approvals. Guidance: first filing approval expected in H2, potentially Q3–Q4, with timing subject to change month-on-month.

Debt. Guidance: net debt neutrality in roughly two to three years if growth delivery continues. Observed: net debt reduced by ₹11.9 crore in the quarter to ₹1,424.6 crore, with net debt-to-EBITDA at 1.52x from 1.55x at FY26 end; net finance costs of ₹36.1 crore versus ₹40.6 crore a year earlier; weighted cost of debt about 7.6%.

Capex. Guidance: ₹2.5–3 billion per year combining maintenance capex, R&D and hard capex, focused on existing factories and high-impact line items, with no greenfield plans at present.

Dividend. The board proposed a final dividend of ₹5 per equity share on May 18, 2026, implying a cash outflow of approximately ₹461 million, subject to shareholder approval. The resolution was passed at the 35th AGM held August 14, 2026, where all six resolutions were approved with the requisite majority.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from a cautious Hold in Aug 2023, focused on pricey valuations, estimate cuts, and US/Africa/institutional declines, to a Buy in Oct 2024 centered on record US sales, margin expansion, and GLP-1 capacity growth. Persisting themes were US ramp-up and deleveraging as key drivers, while US slowdown/regulatory risk remained the main downside caveat. The earlier institutional weakness also persisted in a milder form, but the overall tone became far more positive as operating delivery and new growth optionality overtook valuation concerns.

Optimism that failed

  • The early expectation that healthy order book visibility would improve institutional sales did not materialize, as the last report still showed Access market sales down 4% YoY.
