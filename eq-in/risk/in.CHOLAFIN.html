CHOLAFIN — Q1 FY27 Earnings Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Seasonal asset-quality deterioration: Gross Stage 3 rose from 3.05% in March 2026 to 3.29% in June 2026, and Stage 2 rose from 2.47% to 2.72%. Management characterised the March-to-June increase as seasonal, noting the Q4-to-Q1 increase in vehicle finance Stage 3 was 20–25 bps this year versus 45 bps last year.
  • Disbursement recognition change: From Q1 FY27, the company moved to recognising disbursements only on cheque clearance/debit into the customer’s bank account, rather than at cheque handover. This depressed reported disbursements in LAP, home loans, SBPL and used vehicles, where there is a lag between cheque cutting and registration/hypothecation. Management stated the change was the company’s own prudence call, not driven by an RBI regulation, and that like-for-like growth in LAP and HL was around 20%.
  • Cost of funds uncertainty: Management flagged that market-side cost of funds has been slightly hardening and that a repo rate hike of 25–50 bps is assumed; cost of funds could rise by around 10 bps in H2, though the full-year cost of funds is expected to be flat as a percentage of average assets.
  • Macro uncertainties: El Niño/weak monsoon predictions (92% of normal versus 96%), the West Asia conflict, tariff issues and geopolitical risk were cited as factors limiting visibility beyond one or two quarters.
  • Insurance regulatory overhang: A forthcoming IRDAI circular was mentioned as a potential development whose impact management declined to quantify until the circular is issued; management indicated it is not expected to affect this financial year.
  • SBPL Stage 3 volatility: SBPL Stage 3 rose from 3.53% in March 2026 to 4.70% in June 2026. Management attributed this to the timing of ARC-based recoveries (SBPL lacks SARFAESI access due to small ticket sizes), with resolutions done in some quarters and not others.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong headline growth: Q1 FY27 disbursements rose 22% YoY to ₹29,612 crores; AUM grew 23% YoY to ₹2,54,392 crores. Business AUM was ₹2,33,586 crores, up 22%.
  • Margin expansion: NIM improved to 8.2% from 7.8% YoY, supported by lower funding costs. Cost of funds fell to 6.7% from 7.0%.
  • Profitability: PBT rose 45% to ₹2,220 crores; PAT rose 46% to ₹1,654 crores. PBT-ROTA improved to 3.7% from 3.1%; ROE was 21.2% versus 18.8%.
  • Credit cost improvement: Net credit cost fell to 1.5% from 1.8% YoY, with management noting the full-year FY27 target of 1.5% was achieved in Q1 itself.
  • Segment momentum: Consumer segment disbursements grew 52% YoY; CSEL disbursements grew 67% to ₹3,408 crores with PBT up 93%; SME AUM grew 39%; SBPL AUM grew 40%; gold loan AUM reached ₹2,143 crores.
  • Gold loan expansion: 360 additional branches planned; AUM targeted to reach ₹5,000 crores this financial year; per-branch AUM of ₹12–15 crores; 90% of gold customers are new to Chola.
  • Vehicle finance industry backdrop: HCV +13%, LCV +18%, SCV +31%, PV +26%, two-wheelers +20%, tractors +19% in Q1 FY27.
  • Capital position: CAR at 19.81%, Tier 1 at 14.81%; liquid assets of ₹23,984 crores including undrawn lines. Of the ₹2,000 crores CCD issuance, ₹1,370 crores converted in FY26, ₹200 crores in July 2026, and ₹430 crores expected in October 2026.
  • Fund raising: Board approved issuance of secured and/or unsecured NCDs aggregating up to ₹55,000 crores in one or more tranches on a private placement basis.

3. Key Risks

  • Monsoon and rural demand: Below-normal rainfall could affect Q3/Q4 performance; management said visibility beyond one or two quarters is limited.
  • Geopolitical and tariff risk: West Asia conflict and tariff issues cited as uncertainties that could affect borrower cashflows.
  • Rate risk: A repo hike of 25–50 bps is assumed; MCLR-linked bank borrowings (25–30% of bank loans, which are ~50% of overall borrowings) could reprice upward.
  • Asset quality trajectory: While management expects Stage 3 to improve by March 2027 versus March 2026, the seasonal Q1 uptick and SBPL/vehicle finance Stage 3 levels remain monitorable.
  • Regulatory risk: IRDAI circular impact unknown; RBI norms on interest reversal during cheque-clearing lag prompted the disbursement recognition change.
  • ALM: A question was raised on negative ALM mismatch; management stated there is no cumulative negative mismatch in any bucket based on investor presentation data, and that the annual report column is not a full-fledged ALM.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from broad structural optimism in 2023 — emphasizing India’s low vehicle penetration, demographic dividends, strong auto disbursement momentum, and well-capitalized NBFC balance sheets — toward a margin-conscious, near-term cautious stance by 2026. Persistent themes included the auto OEM revival and gold loan expansion, but the focus shifted to NIM compression from rising cost of funds, seasonal asset quality deterioration, and high base effects on disbursements. The early structural tailwinds of low penetration and demographics remained valid but were increasingly offset by profitability headwinds, prompting a downgrade from Buy to Accumulate/Equalweight.

Optimism that failed

  • Robust private capex revival as a tailwind faded from prominence in the last report, with the narrative shifting away from corporate deleveraging and capex strength toward cost-of-funds and margin headwinds.
  • Strong disbursement momentum continuing at early-report pace was undermined by the high base effect, with management warning that YoY disbursal growth could moderate in subsequent quarters despite Q1’s +22% YoY growth.
  • The structural growth narrative of very low vehicle penetration and rising per capita GDP translating into outsized returns failed to sustain, as evidenced by repeated DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes where predicted returns far exceeded actuals (e.g., Motilal Oswal Buy on 2024-07-08 with predicted +43584% vs actual -3.8%).
