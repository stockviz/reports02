Asian Hotels (East) Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Odisha lease termination and invoked bank guarantee (GJS Hotels Limited). On 2 November 2024, the Government of Odisha, through its General Administration and Public Grievance Department, issued an order citing non-compliance by GJS Hotels Limited — a wholly owned subsidiary — with certain terms and conditions of a lease deed. GJS was directed to vacate the leased premises in Odisha, and a performance bank guarantee of Rs. 350 lakhs furnished by the Company was invoked. GJS has filed a writ petition before the Hon’ble High Court of Orissa challenging the order, and as of the reporting date the next hearing date had not been scheduled. As at 30 June 2026, the Company’s exposure to GJS comprises Rs. 860.86 lakhs in equity shares and Rs. 483.39 lakhs in loans (including the invoked guarantee amount); at the consolidated level, GJS’s capital work-in-progress and right-of-use assets amount to Rs. 783.09 lakhs.

Unresolved Hyatt Regency Mumbai acquisition and competing Buy Option claim. Novak Hotels Private Limited, a wholly owned subsidiary, is in the process of acquiring Hyatt Regency Mumbai from Asian Hotels (West) Limited under a Framework Agreement dated 11 August 2023, as amended, pursuant to an NCLAT order dated 9 January 2024. Novak is in possession of the property, but completion of the legal formalities is pending. Robust Hotels Limited, a party to the Framework Agreement, asserted its own right to exercise the Buy Option through a Board Resolution dated 28 May 2026. Although Novak approved exercise of the buy option in a board meeting during the quarter ended 31 December 2025, and the Company’s Board approved sending a formal notice to AHWL on 9 July 2026, formal completion of the Buy Option exercise and registration of related loan and security documents remain outstanding, and AHWL continues to contest Novak’s possession of HRM. Discussions between the parties are ongoing.

Delayed interest recovery from AHWL. There has been a delay in recovering interest of Rs. 5,608.88 lakhs accrued up to 31 March 2025 and outstanding as at 30 June 2026 from AHWL. AHWL has not confirmed the amount as payable. As a matter of abundant caution, the Group has not recognised interest income for the year ended 31 March 2026 and the quarter ended 30 June 2025.

Ongoing income tax disputes. The Company has appeals pending before appellate authorities: a demand of Rs. 13,827.73 lakhs for FY 2019-20 (AY 2020-21), where the Company received a favourable CIT(A) order that the Income Tax Department has appealed before the ITAT; a demand of Rs. 1,420.18 lakhs for FY 2022-23 (AY 2023-24); and a demand of Rs. 350.49 lakhs for FY 2023-24 (AY 2024-25). No provision has been considered necessary in respect of these matters.

Goodwill impairment already recognised. During the year ended 31 March 2026, the Group reassessed goodwill impairment under Ind AS 36 and concluded it was not recoverable, recognising an impairment loss of Rs. 8,213.06 lakhs as an exceptional item.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Proposed acquisition of Hyatt Regency Mumbai. The Framework Agreement dated 11 August 2023, as amended, and the NCLAT order dated 9 January 2024 underpin Novak’s proposed acquisition of HRM. Novak is already in possession of the property. During FY 2025-26, Novak decided to exercise the option to acquire HRM, and an advance of Rs. 40,259.04 lakhs was reclassified as Capital Work in Progress. Novak has capitalised interest cost of Rs. 3,249.10 lakhs directly attributable to the acquisition up to the quarter ended 30 June 2026. Capital work-in-progress recognised by Novak stands at Rs. 47,482.07 lakhs (including capitalised borrowing costs of Rs. 3,249.10 lakhs). Management, based on legal advice, believes only Novak is entitled to exercise the Buy Option and that Novak’s rights are supported by the contractual arrangements and the conduct of the parties.

Hotel operations performance. The Company is primarily engaged in the business of hotel operations, with no separate reportable segment under Ind AS 108. Standalone revenue from operations for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 was Rs. 2,686.64 lakhs, with net profit of Rs. 529.98 lakhs and total comprehensive income of Rs. 530.99 lakhs. Consolidated revenue from operations for the same quarter was Rs. 2,751.76 lakhs, with net profit attributable to owners of Rs. 431.77 lakhs and total comprehensive income of Rs. 432.78 lakhs.

Financing structure supporting the acquisition. To facilitate funding, the Company raised borrowings secured by a mortgage over the entire Hyatt Regency Kolkata hotel property along with other assets as specified in the financing agreements. The outstanding balance of such borrowings in the Company’s books was Rs. 14,173.00 lakhs as at 30 June 2026. Finance costs for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 include interest expense of Rs. 300.82 lakhs on a loan from a financial institution, while other income includes interest income of Rs. 404.97 lakhs arising from the loan extended to Novak.

AGM outcomes. The 19th Annual General Meeting was held on 27 August 2026 via video conferencing/audio-visual means. All four resolutions — adopting standalone financial statements, adopting consolidated financial statements, appointing Director Mr. Devesh Saraf, and reappointing Independent Director Mr. Sandipan Chakravortty — were passed with the requisite majority. The Register of Members and Share Transfer Books were closed from 22 August 2026 to 27 August 2026, with the cut-off date for voting eligibility being 21 August 2026.

3. Key Risks

Impairment risk on GJS exposure. The auditor’s qualified conclusion states that the events and circumstances — the direction to vacate the property, invocation of the bank guarantee, and the financial position of the subsidiary — raise significant doubt regarding recoverability of the Company’s exposure in GJS. No provision for impairment has been recognised. Had impairment been recognised, standalone total expenses for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 would have been higher by Rs. 1,344.25 lakhs, reducing reported net profit after tax to Rs. (-) 814.27 lakhs from the reported Rs. 529.98 lakhs; total comprehensive income would have been Rs. (-) 813.26 lakhs instead of Rs. 530.99 lakhs; and EPS would have been Rs. (-) 4.71 against the reported Rs. 3.06. At the consolidated level, had impairment of GJS’s right-of-use asset and capital work-in-progress been recognised, total expenses would have been higher by Rs. 783.09 lakhs, reducing reported net profit after tax to Rs. (-) 351.32 lakhs from the reported Rs. 431.77 lakhs; total comprehensive income would have been Rs. (-) 350.31 lakhs instead of Rs. 432.78 lakhs; and EPS would have been Rs. (-) 2.03 against the reported Rs. 2.50.

Recoverability risk on Novak exposure. The Company’s aggregate exposure to Novak as at 30 June 2026 is Rs. 25,294.98 lakhs, comprising investment, loans, and accrued interest. Recoverability is substantially dependent on Novak’s successful acquisition of HRM. The auditor’s emphasis of matter draws attention to this dependency, including the competing Buy Option claim, execution and registration of loan and security documents, and ongoing discussions with AHWL regarding possession.

Recoverability risk on AHWL interest. The auditor states that in the absence of sufficient audit evidence to support recoverability of the Rs. 5,608.88 lakhs interest amount, it is unable to determine the possible effect, if any, on the consolidated statement.

Tax dispute risk. The Company believes its position is legally tenable and adequately supported in respect of the income tax demands, and no provision has been considered necessary.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

GJS matter. Management believes, based on the current legal status and its assessment, that the matter will be resolved in favour of GJS, and accordingly no provision for impairment has been considered necessary. Observed position: the writ petition before the Hon’ble High Court of Orissa remains pending, with the next hearing date yet to be scheduled as of the reporting date. The auditor’s qualified conclusion differs from management’s assessment, stating that no impairment provision is not in accordance with the applicable Indian Accounting Standard.

Novak/HRM acquisition. Management expects the outstanding matters — the competing Buy Option claim, execution and registration of related loan and security documents, and discussions with AHWL regarding possession — to be resolved in due course, and continues to pursue completion of the proposed acquisition. Management, based on legal advice, believes only Novak is entitled to exercise the Buy Option. Observed position: formal completion of the Buy Option exercise with AHWL and registration of related loan and security documents remain to be completed, and AHWL continues to contest Novak’s possession of HRM, with discussions ongoing.

AHWL interest recovery. Novak’s management is actively engaged in negotiations and, based on its assessment, expects the amount of Rs. 5,608.88 lakhs to be recoverable as at the reporting date. Observed position: AHWL has not confirmed the said amount as payable, and the Group has not recognised interest income for the year ended 31 March 2026 and the quarter ended 30 June 2025 as a matter of abundant caution.

Income tax matters. Management believes its position is legally tenable and adequately supported, and no provision has been considered necessary. Observed position: appeals remain pending before the ITAT (FY 2019-20) and the CIT(A) (FY 2022-23 and FY 2023-24).

Financial reporting and governance. The Board meeting of 13 August 2026, which commenced at 01:30 p.m. and concluded at 03:45 p.m., approved the unaudited standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 upon recommendation of the Audit Committee, and took note of the limited review report issued by M/s Singhi & Co, Kolkata, Statutory Auditors. The AGM held on 27 August 2026 passed all four resolutions with the requisite majority.
