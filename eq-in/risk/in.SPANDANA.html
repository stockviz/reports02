Company Overview
  • Full Name: Spandana Sphoorty Financial Limited
  • CIN: L65929TG2003PLC040648
  • Stock Exchange Listings: BSE (Scrip Code: 542759), NSE (Symbol: SPANDANA)
  • Sector: Microfinance / NBFC-MFI
  • Business Model: Rural-centric micro-lending with ~15 lakh active customers, serving low-income populations across South and Central India.
  • Geographic Focus: Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka; high concentration (~52%) in top 4 states.

Summary of Key Highlights (Tailwinds & Strengths)

✅ 1. Strong Improvement in Core Lead Parameters

  • Collections:
    • X-bucket net collection efficiency improved from 96.9% in Apr-25 to 98.8% in Sep-25, nearing 99%.
    • Over 99.9% net collection efficiency on FY26 disbursed loans — a sign of improved underwriting and portfolio quality post-strategic pivot.
  • Disbursements:
    • Q2 FY26 disbursement surges to ₹934 Cr from ₹280 Cr in Q1 FY26 — 3.3x quarter-on-quarter increase.
    • Signals recovery in lending momentum after shrinkage in prior quarters.

Implication: Recovery and risk management initiatives are bearing fruit, enabling reinvestment.

✅ 2. Robust Balance Sheet & Liquidity Cushion

  • Successful ₹400 Cr Rights Issue (partly paid): Fundraise via equity significantly de-risks the balance sheet.
  • CRAR at 47% (Sep-25): Very strong compared to typical NBFC thresholds (~15–20%). Offers ample headroom for scale-up.
  • Liquidity Position:
    • ₹1,179 Cr as of Sep-25; estimated at ₹1,270 Cr by end-Oct 2025.
    • Healthy positive asset-liability gap (ALM) across maturities.

Implication: SSFL has financial headroom to grow, refinance, and absorb shocks — a major tailwind in current environment.

✅ 3. Strategic Reset: Portfolio Quality & Credit Discipline

  • Tighter Credit Underwriting: New credit rules introduced — only customers with 0 DPD (no delinquency) qualify for repeat loans (previously allowed up to 30 DPD).
  • Portfolio Mix Shift:
    • 28% of AUM now from new portfolio (disbursed under tighter standards).
    • Only 124 out of 1.8 Lakh loans in this bucket classified as delinquent as of 30 Sep 2025 — impressive early performance.
  • Borrower Profile Optimization:
    • 76% of eligible borrowers have household debt < ₹1 lakh.
    • 75% have >2 years vintage with Spandana — sticky, seasoned base.

Implication: Portfolio reset is driving structural improvement in credit quality and sustainability.

✅ 4. Revenue Rebalancing: Higher APR & Fee Revisions

  • Interest Rate Revision: Effective 1-Oct-25, rates raised from 19.75–24.75% to 23–26%.
  • Loan Processing Fee Increased: From 1% to 1.5% (effective 1-Sep-25).
  • Expected Impact: Boosts yield and NIM outlook from Q3 FY26 onward, even if volume grows moderately.

Implication: Improves income per loan; supports P&L recovery.

✅ 5. Diversified Funding Mix & Borrowing Advantage

  • 48% of borrowings from banks as of Sep-25 — enhances credit credibility and lowers dependency on volatile capital markets.
  • Borrowing Outstanding: ₹7,849 Cr (up from ₹3,289 Cr Y-o-Y), with cost of borrowing down to 12.2% (from 14.3% in Q2 FY25) despite rising yields.
  • Marginal COB: Stable at ~11.4–12.3% — cost management evident.
  • Availability of private placement route for ₹4,000 Cr NCDs approved at AGM.

Implication: SSFL is regaining lender confidence and optimizing funding cost.

✅ 6. Operational Efficiency Improvements

  • Branch Rationalization: 101 branches closed/merged in H1 FY26 → expected cost savings.
  • LO (Loan Officer) Productivity: Target increased to 275 borrowers per LO, with redeployment and capacity optimization.
  • Digital Collections: 14% of Q2 collections via digital channels (SMS links, QR codes, BBPS).
  • Automation: AI chatbots, call tracking, automated outreach for recoveries.

Implication: Moving toward scalable, tech-enabled microfinance platform.

Headwinds & Risks

❌ 1. Mounting Losses in Short-Term: P&L Still in the Red

Despite improvement: - Q2 FY26 PAT: (₹249 Cr) loss (net outflow) - H1 FY26 PAT: (₹609 Cr) loss - PPOP Negative: (₹79 Cr) in H1 FY26 vs. ₹515 Cr positive in H1 FY25

Why?
- Shrinkage of loan book (AUM down 61% from ₹10,537 Cr to ₹4,088 Cr).
- High impairment costs (~₹729 Cr in H1 FY26), though partly due to one-time technical write-offs of ₹86 Cr for prudence.

Risk: Profitability remains distant unless disbursement volumes sustain pick-up and reversal of provisioning.

❌ 2. Persistent Pressure on Top-Line Income

  • Yields Down: Q2 FY26 at 19.6% vs 22.4% YoY, due to interest reversals.
  • Interest Reversals: ₹83 Cr in H1 (₹50 Cr in Q1, ₹33 Cr in Q2) due to technical write-offs.
  • Income Shrunk: Revenue down 62% Y-o-Y in H1 FY26.

Risk: Rebuilding top-line will take time. Even with new pricing, base income is still compressed.

❌ 3. Deteriorating GNPA & NNPA Trends (Standalone)

  • GNPA: 4.97% as of Sep-25, up 9 bps Q-o-Q from 4.88%.
  • NNPA: 0.97% (stable), but PCR (provision coverage ratio) is only 80.5%, meaning ~19.5% of GNPA not covered.

Risk: NPA pressure not fully resolved; requires continued cost of credit control.

❌ 4. Shrinking AUM & Scale Risk

  • AUM at ₹4,088 Cr (Sep-25) vs ₹10,537 Cr a year ago — nearly 61% decline.
  • Branch count down from ~1,800+ to 1,665 (Sep-25).
  • Though this was intentional (credit cleaning), regains scale slowly.

Risk: Risk of remaining a “small NBFC” if recovery in disbursals doesn’t sustain; may limit institutional investor appeal.

❌ 5. Credit Ratings - Mixed Outlook

  • Multiple downgrades:
    • ICRA & India Ratings: “BBB+” but Negative Outlook
    • CARE: “BBB+” Stable Outlook
    • Criss Financial (a subsidiary): Lower ratings (“BBB”) with negative outlook.

Implication: Negative outlooks affect pricing power in capital markets, delay access to cheaper funds.

❌ 6. Macroeconomic & Climate Risks

  • Business concentrated in rural parts of Andhra Pradesh, MP, Bihar, Odisha — all prone to floods, rains, crop failures.
  • The investor presentation acknowledges reduced working days due to festive season and rains impacting operations in Q2.

Risk: Climate vulnerability could distort quarterly results seasonally.

Broker Narrative

The first report framed SPANDANA as a post-COVID recovery story with a clean balance sheet, low credit costs, and planned diversification, while flagging unsecured lending, political interference, and geographic concentration as key risks. By the last report, the narrative had shifted to structural operational improvements (digital underwriting, risk management) and acknowledgment of persistent pressures such as over-leveraging, regulatory shocks, and elevated credit costs. Despite the evolution, the Buy call was maintained throughout, even as a long series of DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes showed the early optimism about asset quality and credit costs was misplaced.

Fears that came true

  • The political interference risk flagged in the first report materialized as the Karnataka ordinance and Tamil Nadu Bill, which hurt collections and were followed by large negative actual returns.
  • The vulnerability of unsecured microfinance loans to borrower distress was borne out by over-leveraging and monsoon disruptions, leading to elevated credit costs and write-offs.
  • The geographic concentration in Karnataka became a specific fault line, as the state ordinance disrupted the local book and contributed to the DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.

Optimism that failed

  • The guidance of benign credit costs (<2%) failed, as the last report explicitly lists ‘elevated credit costs and legacy write-offs’ as a headwind.
  • The expectation of a clean balance sheet after FY23 provisions proved premature, since legacy write-offs continued to weigh on performance into the last report.
