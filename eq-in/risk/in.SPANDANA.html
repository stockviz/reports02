Headwinds and Challenges

  • Monsoon and El Niño uncertainty. Management flagged the possibility of El Niño and weak monsoon conditions, and said credit and business teams had proactively designed a sourcing strategy for new-to-credit customers and those in rented premises in select likely-affected districts and states. As of late July 2026, no adverse impact on collection efficiency had been observed, but the situation was described as a wait-and-watch with controls in place.
  • Concentrated portfolio. The top five states (Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Odisha and West Bengal) contribute about 60% of the total book, and the top four states account for roughly 52% of AUM. This concentration exposes the company to region-specific shocks.
  • Low share in large markets. Spandana’s share in Tamil Nadu is only about ₹16 crore against an industry size of about ₹38,000 crore, and in Maharashtra about ₹288 crore against an industry of about ₹24,000 crore (roughly 1.1% share). Management wants to at least double the Maharashtra share to 2%.
  • Underperforming branches. About 100 branches were carved out where there was scope for improvement on business, collections, or both. A dedicated task force is being set up to revive rather than merge or close them, on the view that mergers or closures in microfinance rarely succeed.
  • Elevated 90+ DPD pool. The 90-plus pool is described as large, at roughly ₹2,500 crore, with monthly recoveries averaging ₹16–18 crore. Management noted this is the last year of focus on the older 90-plus book, after which customers two years or more past due are not expected to pay.
  • Low Stage 1 coverage. Stage 1 coverage is only about 40 basis points, versus peers at 1% or higher. Management acknowledged this and said the focus is to raise the bar to at least 50% in the 1–30 segment.
  • Borrowing cost still elevated versus prior year. Although the marginal cost of borrowing fell to 11.3% in Q1FY27 from 12.0% in Q4FY26, the overall cost of borrowing at 12.8% was still 48 bps higher year-on-year (12.3% in Q1FY26).
  • Disbursement moderation. Disbursements fell 11% QoQ to ₹1,371 crore in Q1FY27 from ₹1,539 crore in Q4FY26, though they were up sharply year-on-year from ₹280 crore.
  • Rating outlook still negative at one agency. While ICRA changed its outlook to BBB+/Stable from BBB+/Negative, India Ratings continued to carry a BBB+/Negative outlook for Spandana, and Criss Financial carried BBB/Watch Positive at ICRA and India Ratings.
  • Rights issue call obligation. The first and final call of ₹115.00 per partly paid-up share is payable between September 9 and September 23, 2026. Failure to pay triggers 10% per annum interest, potential appropriation of future dividends, and forfeiture of partly paid-up shares including amounts already paid. Trading in the partly paid-up shares (ISIN IN9572J01019) was suspended from August 19, 2026.
  • Execution and integration items. The amalgamation of Criss Financial Limited into Spandana requires NCLT sanction, shareholder and/or creditor approvals, and regulatory approvals including from the RBI. The LOS platform migration covering 1,250 branches is planned for the October–December 2026 window.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Portfolio growth. AUM grew 11% QoQ to ₹4,887 crore in Q1FY27 from ₹4,420 crore in March 2026. New member addition was 61% of disbursements versus 46% in the March quarter, and total members added rose to 1.38 lakh from about 1.1 lakh.
  • Improving asset quality. X-bucket collection efficiency stayed above 99.5%. PAR 30+ improved to 4.4% from 13.4% in June 2025 and 4.7% in March 2026. Consolidated GNPA improved 15 bps QoQ to 3.64% and NNPA to 0.68%; standalone GNPA fell to 2.91% from 3.33%. PCR was maintained at about 81%.
  • Recoveries. ₹51 crore was recovered in Q1FY27, with cumulative recovery of ₹326 crore over 18 months, supported by over 1,000 dedicated recovery associates at end-June 2026.
  • New-book discipline. Loans originated under new credit rules constitute 91% of microfinance AUM, reporting 99.4% net collection efficiency. Lender overlap of more than three lenders reduced to 3.7% from 18.2%, and 75% of customers had a maximum of one lender relationship beyond Spandana.
  • Funding and liquidity. Incremental funding of ₹1,597 crore was raised in Q1FY27, with about ₹200 crore drawn under the credit guarantee scheme and ₹500 crore sanctioned. Marginal cost of borrowing fell to 11.3% from 12.0% QoQ. Bank share of outstanding loans rose to 47% from 44%. Liquidity stood at ₹1,316 crore and CRAR at 33.8%.
  • Profitability inflection. PAT was ₹12 crore versus ₹5 crore in Q4FY26. NIM expanded to 12.5% from 9.9%. Yield rose to 24.6% from 22.8%. Opex fell to ₹155 crore in Q1FY27 from ₹209 crore in Q1FY26.
  • Growth levers identified. Growing in low-share but good-quality markets (Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu), engaging existing and new customers, launching an individual loan book, and graduating from micro-LAP to affordable LAP.
  • Efficiency levers. Migration to a new agile LOS platform with a state-of-the-art collection module, and revival of the bottom 100 branches through a dedicated task force.
  • Individual loan pilot. The individual loan product is ready to be piloted in 8 branches in Madhya Pradesh with 8–10 people over three months, initially for existing customers and later for new-to-Spandana customers with an external track record.
  • New leadership. A new Chief Business Officer for South (Andhra, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala) joined recently, completing the four-CBO structure (North, South, East, West).
  • Governance continuity. Mr. Neeraj Swaroop transitioned from Non-Executive Nominee Director (nominee of Kedaara Capital I Limited) to Independent Director for three consecutive years from August 14, 2026 to August 13, 2029, subject to shareholders’ approval.
  • Group simplification. The Board approved the amalgamation of Criss Financial Limited (wholly owned subsidiary) with Spandana, intended to reduce duplicity of costs, create revenue and cost synergies from a diversified secured and unsecured mix, improve capital deployment and capital adequacy, enable entry into untapped markets, simplify group structure, reduce compliance costs, and lower overall borrowing costs. For FY26, SSFL had revenue of ₹906.59 crore and net worth of ₹2,193.75 crore; CFL had revenue of ₹150.86 crore and net worth of ₹233.11 crore. No shares will be issued and the shareholding pattern will remain unchanged.

Key Risks

  • Credit cost risk. Management identified credit cost as top of mind. Underwriting was strengthened over the preceding 15 months, and the company aims to sustain that discipline.
  • Borrowing cost risk. Funding costs rose during the stress period; the focus is on bringing them down and protecting NIMs.
  • Operating cost risk. Growth is intended to come with limited or no increase in cost.
  • Cycle risk. A participant noted that in prior cycles Spandana scaled to ₹8,000–10,000 crore AUM and then consolidated more heavily than peers during downturns. Management cited process and controls, discipline, and calibrated growth as safeguards.
  • Regulatory and compliance risk. The amalgamation is subject to NCLT sanction, shareholder and/or creditor approval, and regulatory approvals including RBI. The rights issue call process involves ASBA-based payment mechanics, PAN-Aadhaar linkage requirements, and forfeiture consequences for non-payment.
  • Concentration risk. Top-state and top-district concentration, though branch-level concentration is low (largest branch 0.3%, top 10 branches 2.4%).
  • Monsoon/El Niño risk. Proactive sourcing controls were put in place for select districts and states likely to be impacted.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Credit cost. Management reiterated comfort with 2.5%–3% gross credit cost for FY27, with net credit cost closer to 2%. Observed: annualized credit cost was 2.1% in Q1FY27 versus 3.2% in Q4FY26, and net credit cost was negative in Q1FY27 due to stronger recoveries.
  • Disbursements. Management guided ₹6,000–6,500 crore for FY27. Observed: ₹1,371 crore disbursed in Q1FY27.
  • AUM. Management guided an exit of a little upwards of ₹6,000 crore for FY27 and broadly ₹10,000 crore or thereabouts by March 2028. Observed: AUM at ₹4,887 crore at end-June 2026, up 11% QoQ.
  • ROA. Management stated 3.5% ROA is feasible in a business-as-usual environment by FY28, with current ROA at 1% and improvement expected through FY27. Observed: ROA (on-book POS) was 1.1% in Q1FY27 versus 0.5% in Q4FY26; ROA (total assets) was 0.7% versus 0.3%.
  • Yield. Management said the ideal disbursal yield is around 25.25%, that current yield is close to that, and that further improvement should be marginal before stabilizing. Observed: yield was 24.6% in Q1FY27, up 182 bps QoQ. No rate increase had been taken since October 1, 2025, and no further rate increases were planned.
  • Cost of borrowing. Management expected the marginal cost to sustain and possibly improve further, with overall cost of funds also improving as incremental borrowing replaces higher-cost past borrowing. Observed: marginal cost fell to 11.3% from 12.0% QoQ; overall cost of borrowing fell to 12.8% from 13.2%.
  • Opex. Management guided full-year FY27 opex around ₹675 crore versus about ₹760 crore in the prior year, with roughly a 10% increase the following year. Observed: Q1FY27 opex was ₹155 crore versus ₹209 crore in Q1FY26.
  • Recoveries. Management guided ₹150–200 crore of collections from the 90-plus pool for the year, with an aspiration to exceed ₹200 crore, against monthly averages of ₹16–18 crore. Observed: ₹51 crore recovered in Q1FY27.
  • New book share. Management expected the new book to move to about 95% in the coming quarter. Observed: 91% of microfinance AUM at end-June 2026.
  • Branch count. Management said the 1,250-odd standalone branches will remain, with no further consolidation, and some branches may be added in Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu. Observed: no branch reduction was reported in the quarter.
  • Liquidity. Management said liquidity would be optimized as conditions improve but noted it is better to stay liquid in microfinance. Observed: liquidity of ₹1,316 crore at end-June 2026.
  • Stage 1 coverage. Management said the focus is to raise the bar to at least 50% in the 1–30 segment. Observed: Stage 1 (current) coverage was 0.4% at end-June 2026.

Broker Narrative

The first report framed SPANDANA as a post-COVID recovery story with a clean balance sheet, low credit costs, and planned diversification, while flagging unsecured lending, political interference, and geographic concentration as key risks. By the last report, the narrative had shifted to structural operational improvements (digital underwriting, risk management) and acknowledgment of persistent pressures such as over-leveraging, regulatory shocks, and elevated credit costs. Despite the evolution, the Buy call was maintained throughout, even as a long series of DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes showed the early optimism about asset quality and credit costs was misplaced.

Fears that came true

  • The political interference risk flagged in the first report materialized as the Karnataka ordinance and Tamil Nadu Bill, which hurt collections and were followed by large negative actual returns.
  • The vulnerability of unsecured microfinance loans to borrower distress was borne out by over-leveraging and monsoon disruptions, leading to elevated credit costs and write-offs.
  • The geographic concentration in Karnataka became a specific fault line, as the state ordinance disrupted the local book and contributed to the DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.

Optimism that failed

  • The guidance of benign credit costs (<2%) failed, as the last report explicitly lists ‘elevated credit costs and legacy write-offs’ as a headwind.
  • The expectation of a clean balance sheet after FY23 provisions proved premature, since legacy write-offs continued to weigh on performance into the last report.
