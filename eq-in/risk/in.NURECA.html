Nureca: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

GST demand and rectification. Nureca received an order DRC-07 under Section 73(9) of the CGST Act, 2017 and Section 21 of the UT GST Act, 2017, pertaining to tax period 2021-22. The company filed a rectification under Section 161 of the CGST Act, 2017. The Final Order of Rectification was received on 6 August 2026, and the matter was disposed of upon payment of dues by way of reversal of Input Tax Credit amounting to Rs. 15,40,311. The related GST demand was dropped by the authorities, but the ITC reversal represents a quantifiable monetary impact.

Volatility in quarterly profitability. Standalone results show a sharp swing: profit after tax of Rs. 24.32 million in the quarter ended 30 June 2026, compared with a loss after tax of Rs. 66.25 million in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 and profit of Rs. 5.52 million in the quarter ended 30 June 2025. The March 2026 quarter included fair value changes and net loss on investments of Rs. 83.37 million, which was the principal driver of that quarter’s loss. On a consolidated basis, the pattern is similar: profit after tax of Rs. 30.67 million in Q1 FY27, a loss of Rs. 60.92 million in Q4 FY26, and profit of Rs. 8.13 million in Q1 FY26.

Full-year profitability remains thin. For the year ended 31 March 2026, standalone profit after tax was Rs. 2.97 million on revenue from operations of Rs. 1,472.10 million, and consolidated profit after tax was Rs. 20.80 million on revenue from operations of Rs. 1,469.63 million. The year included the Rs. 83.37 million fair value loss on investments.

Inventory build. Changes in inventories of stock-in-trade were Rs. 67.57 million in Q1 FY27 standalone (versus a negative Rs. 10.26 million in Q1 FY26), and consolidated changes in inventories of work in progress, finished goods and stock-in-trade were Rs. 62.09 million (versus negative Rs. 8.33 million). This indicates inventory accumulation during the quarter.

Dissent on director appointments. At the 10th AGM held on 28 July 2026, the special resolutions for the reappointment of Mr. Saurabh Goyal as Managing Director and the appointment of Ms. Smita Goyal as Whole-time Director each received 4,807 votes in favour and 167 votes against, or 96.64% in favour and 3.36% against on votes polled. The ordinary resolutions (adoption of financial statements and reappointment of Mr. Rajinder Sharma) received 6,501,983 votes in favour and 167 against, or 99.9974% in favour.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Dr Trust 360 user base. A press release dated 20 June 2026 announced that the connected health platform Dr Trust 360 surpassed 2.4 million registered users. The release describes Nureca as the parent of the Dr Trust, Dr Physio and Trumom brands, with Dr Trust having more than 1.86 crore customers.

Launch of Dr Trust Intelligence. Nureca launched Dr Trust Intelligence, an AI-powered analytics suite within the Dr Trust 360 app. Capabilities described include plain-language interpretation of body-composition metrics, automatic detection of changes and longer-term trends, personalized health analysis, AI-based diet guidance, and reports intended to support conversations with doctors, nutritionists and fitness professionals. It is offered as a premium subscription on Android and iOS.

Device ecosystem and accuracy claims. Dr Trust’s 8-electrode smart scales measure through hands and feet, and company validation testing indicates up to 98% DEXA-level accuracy for selected metrics including body fat, muscle mass and visceral fat. Dr Trust 360 integrates compatible devices including smart body-composition scales, blood pressure monitors and smart kitchen scales.

India-based data infrastructure. User health data is encrypted and stored and processed on servers located in India, which the company states supports handling in line with applicable Indian data-protection requirements.

Revenue growth year on year. Standalone revenue from operations rose to Rs. 403.96 million in Q1 FY27 from Rs. 343.24 million in Q1 FY26. Consolidated revenue from operations rose to Rs. 403.43 million from Rs. 341.76 million over the same comparison.

Merger of wholly owned subsidiary. The company has filed a Scheme of Merger by Absorption under which Nureca Technologies Private Limited, a wholly owned subsidiary, will merge with the company, effective from a proposed appointed date of 1 April 2025, subject to sanction of the National Company Law Tribunal and other regulatory approvals. No accounting impact has been recognised pending sanction.

Buyback completed. During the previous year the company undertook a buyback of equity shares through the tender offer route at Rs. 330 per share, funded from free reserves including securities premium. The buyback was completed during the quarter ended 31 December 2025, shares were extinguished, and a Capital Redemption Reserve equal to the nominal value of shares bought back was created under Section 69 of the Companies Act, 2013. Paid-up equity share capital stood at Rs. 95.42 million as at 30 June 2026, against Rs. 100.00 million as at 30 June 2025.

Governance continuity. All four resolutions at the 10th AGM on 28 July 2026 were passed with requisite majority, covering adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY26, reappointment of Mr. Rajinder Sharma as rotating director, reappointment of Mr. Saurabh Goyal as Managing Director for three years, and appointment of Ms. Smita Goyal as Whole-time Director.

Key Risks

Tax and regulatory exposure. The GST matter for tax period 2021-22 required a rectification application and resulted in an ITC reversal of Rs. 15,40,311. The order was received under Section 73(9) of the CGST Act, 2017 and Section 21 of the UT GST Act, 2017, and was subsequently rectified under Section 161.

Investment valuation risk. Fair value changes and net loss on investments of Rs. 83.37 million were recognised in the quarter ended 31 March 2026, both standalone and consolidated, and contributed to the loss in that quarter.

Dependence on a single business segment. The company operates in a single business segment, home healthcare and wellness products, with no reportable segments under Ind AS 108, both standalone and consolidated.

Execution risk on the merger. The Scheme of Merger by Absorption remains subject to NCLT sanction and other regulatory approvals, and no accounting impact has been recognised pending sanction.

Subsidiary review scope. The consolidated review report notes that one subsidiary with total revenues of Rs. 68.19 million, net profit after tax of Rs. 4.26 million and total comprehensive income of Rs. 4.26 million for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 was reviewed by another auditor, and a foreign subsidiary with nil revenue from operations and a net loss after tax of Rs. 0.25 million was not reviewed by its auditor, with the parent’s management converting its results to Indian accounting principles.

Product claims and data handling. The accuracy statement regarding DEXA-level results is based on company validation testing of selected metrics, and actual results may vary based on user profile, hydration, device setup and usage conditions. Dr Trust 360 is positioned as supporting wellness tracking and not as a substitute for professional medical diagnosis or advice.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

The supplied material does not contain explicit forward financial guidance from management against which performance can be compared. The available statements of expectation and positioning are qualitative.

Management stated, through Saurabh Goyal, Chairman and Managing Director, that crossing 2.4 million registered users reflects trust in the Dr Trust ecosystem, that Dr Trust Intelligence is designed to help users understand what is changing and whether their health efforts are producing results, and that combining intelligent analytics with India-based data infrastructure makes connected preventive healthcare more useful, personal and secure. The observed performance in the June 2026 quarter is consistent with continued revenue expansion year on year on both a standalone and consolidated basis, alongside a return to profit after the March 2026 quarter loss.

On the merger, management has set a proposed appointed date of 1 April 2025 but has recognised no accounting impact pending NCLT sanction and other approvals, so no financial effect from the scheme appears in the reported results.

On the GST matter, the company pursued rectification under Section 161 after the DRC-07 order, and the outcome was that the related GST demand was dropped while the ITC reversal of Rs. 15,40,311 was paid.
