Oriental Aromatics Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material inflation. Management describes input costs as elevated and a continuing area of vigilance. Several natural, terpene-based and petrochemical-derived inputs remain firm. Alpha-Pinene prices rose roughly 70–80% over the five months preceding the 31 July 2026 earnings call and remain at that high level; Alpha-Pinene-based raw materials are expected to stay expensive. Petro-based inputs are described as moving in a “seesaw” pattern, firm for several months before a brief decline.

Competitive intensity in Specialty Aroma Ingredients. The global ingredients market remains highly competitive, with capacity additions across Asia (primarily China) pressuring selling prices for several products. Management characterises many ingredient categories as a buyer’s market, where customers expect competitive pricing alongside quality, innovation and supply security. This has constrained the ability to pass through cost increases, particularly in Specialty Aroma Ingredients and Camphor.

Camphor overcapacity. The domestic Camphor market faces structural overcapacity from significant manufacturing capacity additions in recent years, with expansion not matched to the finished product’s limited CAGR. Management indicated it is challenging for industry players to coordinate on pricing given the demand-supply gap.

Mahad facility drag. The Mahad facility is at roughly 50–60% utilisation and has not reached the level needed to absorb fixed operating costs, so it continues to weigh on consolidated profitability. Management noted an inventory build-up at Mahad following constrained access to a petro-driven raw material, with confidence that the inventory will be sold in the near term. Specialty Aroma Ingredients commercialisation cycles run long — management cited 500 to 1,000 days for new products to reach commercial light.

Margin pressure. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 7.62% was lower year-on-year than Q1 FY26’s 8.01%, attributed to higher raw material costs. Management described the margin outlook as “very fluid” given geopolitical impacts on input availability and pricing.

Volume-revenue divergence. Q1 FY27 sales volume rose 22% year-on-year while revenue rose about 15%, reflecting lower realisations. Management attributed part of the volume growth to customers taking allocated product earlier than scheduled due to geopolitical conditions.

Camphor import discussions. Management stated that after an earlier meeting with government representatives on Camphor imports and their impact, there were no further developments during the quarter.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Q1 FY27 performance. Consolidated revenue from operations was approximately Rs. 260 crore versus approximately Rs. 226 crore in the prior-year quarter, growth of around 15% year-on-year. Total sales volume rose 22% and production volume 18% year-on-year. EBITDA was Rs. 19.8 crore against Rs. 19.46 crore in Q4 FY26 and Rs. 18.06 crore in Q1 FY26. EBITDA margin improved 71 basis points sequentially to 7.62% from 6.89%. Profit after tax was Rs. 2.51 crore versus Rs. 3.98 crore in Q4 FY26 and Rs. 0.5 crore in Q1 FY26. Cash profit was Rs. 10.2 crore versus Rs. 8.72 crore year-on-year and Rs. 11.72 crore sequentially.

Diversified portfolio. Presence across Fragrances, Specialty Aroma Ingredients, and Camphor and Terpene Chemicals provides resilience across cycles. Each of the three verticals contributes roughly one-third of sales.

Structural premiumisation. Long-term trends toward premiumisation in Personal Care, Home Care and Fine Fragrances remain intact, which management links to the company’s backward-integrated model.

Backward integration into Fragrances. Management emphasised that investments in ingredient plants feed the Fragrance division, providing competitive strategic positioning and margin contribution without requiring further capex.

Mahad commercial progress. Customer feedback remains encouraging, commercial shipments are progressing, and the facility is participating in global sourcing programmes and RFQs. Management targets 75–80% utilisation, at which point Mahad is expected to become EBITDA positive.

Seasonality. Q2 and Q3 are seasonally important for Camphor and consumer Fragrances. Q1 marks the start of inventory build-up ahead of festive demand, and the Camphor and Terpene Chemicals division recorded strong year-on-year volume growth in the quarter.

Export mix. Exports were 35% of the quarter versus 33% for the previous financial year; management expects the proportion to remain in a similar range.

Financial position. Net debt-to-equity improved to 0.56x as of 30 June 2026 from 0.58x as of 31 March 2026.

ESG rating. Crisil ESG Ratings & Analytics Limited assigned an ESG rating of ‘Crisil ESG 56’ (revised from ‘Crisil ESG 51’) for FY 2025-26, based on public disclosures and without engagement by the company.

Leadership addition. The Board approved the appointment of Mr. Nitin Bhuduvalekar as Vice President – Sales Fragrance effective 30 July 2026 (per Annexure A, date of appointment 01 August 2026), with 16+ years of experience across Fine Fragrances, Personal Care, Air Care, Agarbatti and Industrial Fragrances.

3. Key Risks

  • Sustained elevation in Alpha-Pinene and petrochemical-derived raw material prices, with limited pass-through in competitive categories.
  • Continued Asian capacity additions pressuring ingredient and Camphor pricing.
  • Structural Camphor overcapacity with limited industry coordination.
  • Mahad utilisation remaining below the threshold needed to absorb fixed costs, prolonging its drag on consolidated profitability.
  • Inventory build-up at Mahad tied to raw material access constraints.
  • Foreign exchange movements and geopolitical developments affecting input availability and pricing.
  • Customer inventory and working capital discipline, alongside heightened emphasis on security of supply.
  • Longer-than-expected commercialisation cycles for new Specialty Aroma Ingredients products (500–1,000 days cited).

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Revenue growth. Management guides to a near-term trajectory of 10–15% sales growth over the next one year. Observed: Q1 FY27 revenue grew approximately 15% year-on-year, within the guided range, though revenue declined 8% sequentially due to product mix changes.

Margins. Management’s stated priority is improving operating margins while preserving volume growth, through product mix, process optimisation and cost discipline. Observed: EBITDA margin improved 71 basis points sequentially to 7.62% but was lower than Q1 FY26’s 8.01% due to higher raw material costs. Management declined to give specific margin guidance, describing the outlook as fluid.

Mahad. Management targets progressive movement to 75–80% utilisation, where Mahad is expected to become EBITDA positive. Observed: current utilisation is 50–60%, with the facility still not absorbing fixed operating costs and continuing to impact consolidated profitability. Management did not commit to a timeline for meaningful scaling, stating it would inform the investor community as and when it happens.

Capacity utilisation. Management stated that, excluding the hydrogenation facility, most plants in Specialty Aroma Ingredients and Camphor and Terpene Chemicals run at 85–90%, with substantial additional ability in fragrance compounding. Observed: consistent with the stated near-term growth trajectory, though no specific revenue ceiling was quantified.

Tax rate. Management guided to an effective tax rate of around 25% for the full year. Observed: Q1 FY27 current tax of Rs. 277.86 lakh on consolidated profit before tax of Rs. 527.09 lakh, with deferred tax credit of Rs. 2.19 lakh.

R&D spend. Management indicated R&D spend is currently around 2–2.5% of sales, against a prior range of 3–4%, reflecting optimisation.

Capital allocation. Management’s stated priority is maximising utilisation and profitability from existing assets before undertaking major expansionary investments, and maintaining prudent capital allocation. Observed: net debt-to-equity improved to 0.56x as of 30 June 2026 from 0.58x as of 31 March 2026.

Governance. All five resolutions at the 54th AGM held on 18 August 2026 were passed with requisite majority: adoption of audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY ended 31 March 2026; declaration of dividend of Rs. 0.50 (10%) per equity share of face value Rs. 5; reappointment of Mr. Satish Kumar Ray (DIN: 07904910) as a rotating director; ratification of remuneration of Cost Auditor Ms V. J. Talati and Co.; and appointment of Mr. John Fitzgibbon Gloster (DIN: 02421071) as an Independent Director (special resolution). Total votes polled represented 75.41% of outstanding shares, with 4 votes cast against each resolution.
