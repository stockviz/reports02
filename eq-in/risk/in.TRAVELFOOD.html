Travel Food Services (TRAVELFOOD) — Q1 FY2026-27 Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Passenger traffic disruption from the Middle East conflict. Management stated that passenger traffic — the single most important external driver of the business — was broadly flat year-on-year in Q1 FY27. Domestic traffic grew modestly, but a significant decline in international traffic offset this. The quarter began with an overall traffic decline in April 2026; May 2026 saw gradual stabilisation with domestic traffic returning to growth while international traffic continued to face headwinds; June 2026 softened again following renewed conflict-related disruptions. The company also cited inflationary pressures and higher input costs arising from the geopolitical situation.

Soft like-for-like (LFL) performance in specific markets. System-wide LFL sales growth was 0.8% for the quarter. Management attributed this to traffic migration to newer airport infrastructure in Mumbai and Guwahati, and to certain South India markets with relatively higher exposure to Middle East traffic. Excluding these markets, system-wide LFL sales growth was around 7% year-on-year. Consolidated LFL growth was 4.2%.

Margin compression from new-unit ramp-up. EBITDA margin moderated to 35.8% in Q1 FY27 from 38.9% in Q1 FY26 (a decline of 308 bps), even as EBITDA grew 11% year-on-year to INR1,619 million. Employee costs rose due to annual compensation revisions and onboarding of manpower for recently opened outlets at Noida and Cochin airports, both in ramp-up phase, plus staffing for the new passenger services business at Noida. Other expenses rose 22% year-on-year, including INR223 million of lounge aggregation cost classified under other expenses. Management described these as pre-operating and advance costs associated with a large opening cycle.

Contract renewals and expiries. The Delhi T3 licence agreement (held by Travel Food Services (Delhi Terminal 3) Private Limited, a 60%-owned material subsidiary) is scheduled to expire on 30 September 2026. The JV GHL has bid for the contract but results were not out as of the call. Management noted that the financial results of that subsidiary were prepared on a going concern basis, and that while the matter represents a material uncertainty at the subsidiary level, it does not give rise to a material uncertainty at the Group level. Chennai and Kolkata airports are expected to come up for renewal at the end of March 2027 and early Q1 FY2027-28. Management also noted that contract renewals bring some cost escalation and asset refresh, though typically less than fresh construction.

Contract retention rate moderation. The contract retention rate moved from approximately 94% to approximately 92%, which management attributed to a decision not to renew certain smaller highway pilot outlets, consistent with a strategy of pursuing larger wayside amenity investments.

Near-term traffic trend. Management indicated that as of mid-August 2026, traffic trends were similar to Q1, with international traffic again the area of weakness. Two of India’s largest airlines suspended a number of international routes, especially long-haul, around end-May 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Network expansion. TFS commenced operations at Noida International Airport (operations from 15 June 2026), taking system-wide presence to 21 airports. In the last 12 months, 87 Travel QSR outlets and 2 lounges were added, with significant additions at Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Cochin, Navi Mumbai and Noida. System-wide footprint as of June 2026: 580 Travel QSR outlets and lounges (541 Travel QSR outlets and 39 lounges), with 153 brands. Bhogapuram Airport opened on 17 August 2026, where TFS will operate multiple outlets under its JV, GHL.

Pipeline of new units. Over 50 outlets are under development across the network, expected to open largely during the fiscal year. Management expects these, as they mature over 12 to 18 months (longer for greenfield airports), to provide a meaningful uplift to revenue and earnings. Roughly 90 units mobilised over the prior 12 months are still in early ramp-up, with a significant part of their earning potential yet to be realised.

New business lines. Passenger services launched at Noida under the Elite Assist brand (meet-and-greet, porter services, with baby pram and wheelchair services also shown), to be integrated into the EATS technology platform, with phased rollout at Noida and expansion to additional airports. In FY2025-26, TFS completed integration for direct-to-lounge access with all banks/card network partners in India under Elite Assist Technology & Services Ltd.

International expansion. Success in Malaysia and Hong Kong (a second lounge won in Hong Kong in January 2026). Management stated focus on the Middle East and Asia, with Asia currently the greater focus until Middle East conditions normalise. Entities have been set up in Dubai and Indonesia to enable bidding in those markets.

Domestic opportunities. Bangalore T1 is undergoing a major upgradation, with phased opportunities expected over the next 12 months; TFS won a prominent KFC outlet there. Pune airport is expected in the next two years. Highways are described as a medium-to-long-term opportunity, with the government’s wayside amenities plan (about 1,000 WSAs announced) and expressway rollout; management compared highways today to airports when TFS entered in 2008-09, while stressing that returns must match portfolio benchmarks.

Structural industry growth. The presentation cites India as the world’s third-largest aviation market, with India passenger traffic expected to grow at 7-8% CAGR versus 3-4% globally (CRISIL Report 2026/2025; ICRA March 2025; Ministry of Civil Aviation). It cites 70-80% of Indian airport lounge passenger traffic being driven through credit cards, and credit card penetration trends. TFS holds a 30% QSR market share and 45% lounge market share (CRISIL Report 2026), is present in 14 of India’s 15 largest airports, and has a ~92% contract retention rate.

Balance sheet capacity. Debt-free balance sheet with consolidated cash balance of approximately INR9,698 million as of 30 June 2026, providing headroom to fund airport expansions, lounge and highway growth, passenger services and other strategic initiatives.

Recovery expectation. Management expects passenger traffic growth to improve strongly in H2 FY27 as operating conditions normalise, noting airline announcements of restoration of many long-haul international routes from September and October 2026, and citing the May 2026 rebound — the highest ever single month of domestic air traffic in India — as evidence of demand resilience.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued or renewed Middle East conflict prolonging weak international traffic and input cost inflation.
  • Concentration of exposure to airports with high Middle East traffic, particularly South India markets and the JV portfolio on the western side.
  • Expiry of the Delhi T3 licence on 30 September 2026, with the outcome of the GHL bid unknown; the subsidiary’s results were prepared on a going concern basis, with a material uncertainty noted at the subsidiary level.
  • Renewal risk at Chennai and Kolkata airports (end-March 2027 and early Q1 FY2027-28).
  • Ramp-up risk on new and greenfield airports (Noida, Navi Mumbai), where traffic takes time to build and pre-operating costs are incurred ahead of sales.
  • Execution and return discipline in competitive bidding for new airports and highways; management stated it will not chase growth unless it delivers sustainable long-term profits and returns that mimic the rest of the portfolio at maturity.
  • Cost structure: roughly 8-10% of costs are fixed, mainly occupancy-related CAM and other airport operator charges; labour is semi-variable. Cost increases at contract renewals and new openings can pressure margins until units normalise.
  • Lounge aggregation accounting: revenues recorded in sales while related service costs sit in other expenses, affecting reported gross margin (INR223 million reclassified in Q1 FY27; adjusted gross margin ~81%).
  • Dependence on external factors including geopolitical stability, fuel prices, airline route decisions, and regulatory/taxation changes.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

LFL growth. Management stated that LFL growth tends to run at least 5% to 7% above passenger traffic, and that in a normal year with passenger traffic around 8-9%, LFL growth of roughly 14-15% is the normalised outcome. In Q1 FY27, with passenger traffic broadly flat, system-wide LFL growth was 0.8% and consolidated LFL growth was 4.2% — below the stated 5-7% above-traffic range, which management attributed to specific market factors (Mumbai and Guwahati terminal migration, South India Middle East exposure). Excluding those markets, system-wide LFL growth was around 7%, within the stated range.

Net contract gains. Management described net contract gains as lumpy year-to-year and declined to give a specific target, noting historical performance in a similar ballpark. Q1 FY27 delivered 15.9% system-wide and 20.2% consolidated net contract gains.

Gross margin. Management stated the adjusted gross profit margin of around 81% (after reclassifying INR223 million of lounge aggregation cost) remains well within its guided range. Reported gross margin was 85.7%.

EBITDA margin. Management stated that higher employee and other expenses from recently commissioned airports and new business initiatives are strategic and aimed at supporting future growth, and that other expenses should return to traditional levels within about 12 months. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 35.8% versus 38.9% a year earlier.

Ramp-up timelines. Management guided that existing-airport units normalise in 12 to 18 months and greenfield units in 18 to 24 months. It stated that the roughly 90 units mobilised over the prior 12 months will largely hit normalisation over the next 12 months, with benefits expected particularly in H2 FY27, and that the 50-plus outlets under development will follow the same ramp-up pattern after opening.

H2 FY27 outlook. Management expects a good bounce-back in passenger traffic in H2 FY27, supported by airline restoration of long-haul international routes from September and October 2026 and independent research expectations of strong H2 traffic in India. It characterised current conditions as a temporary disruption and said long-term drivers of Indian aviation remain firmly intact.

Capital allocation. Management stated it will pursue returns at maturity that mimic the rest of the portfolio and will not chase growth unnecessarily; it described highways as a calibrated medium-to-long-term opportunity rather than an immediate one.

JV growth gap. Asked why associate/JV growth (about 15%) lagged consolidated growth and peers, management cited scheduled migration of units into or out of JV structures and the concentration of JV airports on the western side with strong Middle East exposure.

Delhi T3. Management confirmed the contract runs to 30 September 2026, that GHL has bid, and that results were not yet out; it declined to comment on H2. It clarified that Delhi T3 was historically a majority-owned consolidated JV, and that under the new GHL structure with 30% shareholding it would not be consolidated going forward, with profit accruing through profit pick-up. Management stated margins would not drop on account of the entity type, and that the tender covers a larger set of outlets than the current T3 arrangement.

Other clarifications. Management stated the new labour code impact is minimal, below INR8-10 crore, with over 5,000 employees mostly on company rolls. It stated contracts are predominantly minimum guarantee and/or revenue share (whichever is higher), with MGs typically escalating and revenue share percentages generally constant over the contract life, and that TFS typically pays the revenue share amount rather than just the minimum guarantee.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from an optimistic recovery story in February 2026, citing passenger traffic growth, margin expansion, and strong JV-led profit growth, to a more cautious tone by August 2026 as Middle East tensions persisted, passenger traffic went flat, and EBITDA margins contracted sharply. Persistent themes included geopolitical risk, reliance on new airport/concession wins, and expansion of outlets, lounges, and brands. The key change was that revenue growth increasingly came from net contract gains and new unit mobilisation rather than like-for-like passenger recovery, while the margin outlook deteriorated.

Fears that came true

  • The Middle East geopolitical tensions flagged in the first report persisted and constrained passenger traffic growth, corresponding with the last report’s -1.4% actual return. Passenger traffic across TFS-managed airports was broadly flat YoY, confirming the fear that airline/geopolitical disruptions would hurt volumes. EBITDA margin contracted 308bps YoY as mobilisation of new units raised costs, a downside not fully anticipated in the first report’s margin expansion optimism.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of continued passenger traffic recovery failed, as traffic across managed airports was broadly flat and LFL growth was only 0.8%. The optimism around EBITDA margin expansion from operating leverage and cost discipline failed, with margins contracting 308bps due to new unit mobilisation costs.
