Dharmaj Crop Guard Limited — Q1FY27 Announcements and AGM Notice

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Weather disruption to the Kharif season: The quarter ended June 30, 2026 was marked by an El Nino effect and a slow start to the monsoon. The monsoon onset was delayed across several key agricultural regions, postponing Kharif sowing activity and reducing product demand through the quarter.
  • Unfavourable base effect in Domestic Branded Formulations: The Domestic Branded Formulations business was flattish year-on-year. The prior-year quarter (Q1FY26) had benefited from an early monsoon start that supported off-take, a benefit absent in Q1FY27.
  • Weakness in Active Ingredients: Revenue in the Active Ingredients business declined 14% year-on-year for the quarter. Management attributed this to the ongoing West Asia crisis and resulting macro headwinds, which affected both input availability and demand patterns in the Technicals market.
  • Seasonality: The company’s business is seasonal in nature, and results and performance of every quarter can be impacted by weather conditions and cropping pattern.
  • Governance transition: Mrs. Amisha Shah resigned as Independent Director with effect from the close of business hours on August 07, 2026, citing other professional commitments, and ceased to be a member of the committees she served on. The Board approved the appointment of Mrs. Megha Joshi as an Additional Independent Director for five consecutive years from August 08, 2026 to August 07, 2031, subject to member approval, and reconstituted the Audit Committee and Nomination and Remuneration Committee by inducting her as a member.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Revenue growth despite a challenging environment: Q1FY27 revenue from operations was ₹3,841 Mn, up 5% year-on-year on a larger Q1FY26 base.
  • Margin expansion: EBITDA for Q1FY27 was ₹573 Mn (up 13% year-on-year) with EBITDA margin at 14.9%, compared to 13.8% in Q1FY26. This was driven by a better product mix within the Domestic Branded Formulations vertical and partially aided by price realisations during the quarter. Management views the mix improvement as the more durable outcome for the business.
  • Export momentum: The Exports vertical reported robust growth of 116% year-on-year, albeit on a smaller Q1FY26 base. The recovery seen through FY26 has carried into the current year, and management expects this vertical to continue building scale as the year progresses.
  • Active Ingredients strategy: The company is aligning Active Ingredients production with the captive requirements of its Formulations business, which improves blended profitability at the company level and allows better utilisation of existing capacity even while the external Technicals market remains uncertain.
  • New Herbicides facility: The new Formulations facility at Kerala GIDC, Ahmedabad, dedicated to herbicides manufacturing, remains on track. Herbicides are expected to play an increasingly important role in the Formulations product mix, and the facility will release capacity at the existing facility, improving throughput during the peak Kharif season.
  • Distribution and product expansion: Retail touchpoints increased to 19.5K+, supported by 5,300+ dealers and distributors, alongside product registration activities and new product launches such as ORMARA.
  • International footprint: The company is planning to obtain necessary approvals for incorporating a new Wholly-Owned Subsidiary in Brazil.
  • Operational improvement levers: Management points to sustained demand, stable business operations, efficient manufacturing processes, and effective procurement management as supports for profitability, and is focused on enhancing operational efficiency, optimising manufacturing and procurement, improving capacity utilisation, expanding market reach, strengthening customer relationships, focusing on higher-margin products, and maintaining prudent financial and working capital management.

3. Key Risks

  • Weather and cropping risk: As a seasonal agri-inputs business, quarterly results and performance can be impacted by weather conditions and cropping patterns, as demonstrated by the El Nino effect and delayed monsoon in Q1FY27.
  • Geopolitical and macro risk: The ongoing West Asia crisis and resulting macro headwinds affected input availability and demand patterns in the Technicals market, contributing to the 14% year-on-year decline in Active Ingredients revenue.
  • External Technicals market uncertainty: The external Technicals market remains uncertain, and management describes the industry situation as dynamic, requiring continued agility in the Active Ingredients business.
  • Concentration in a single segment: The company’s business activity falls within a single business segment, “Agri-Inputs,” with no separate reportable segment under Ind AS 108.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The company’s disclosures note that forward-looking statements are not guarantees of future performance and are subject to known and unknown risks, uncertainties, and assumptions that are difficult to predict, including the performance of the Indian and international economies, industry performance, competition, strategy implementation, growth and expansion levels, technological changes, changes in revenue, income or cash flows, market preferences, and market risk exposure.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Annual growth target reaffirmed: Management reaffirmed its annual growth target and expects to build on momentum through the rest of the year, resting on three pillars: the strength of the Branded Formulations business, the scale-up of the Active Ingredients business, and a resurgence in the Exports vertical. Observed Q1FY27 performance showed 5% year-on-year revenue growth, 13% year-on-year EBITDA growth, and 17% year-on-year PAT growth, with Domestic Branded Formulations flattish and Active Ingredients down 14% year-on-year, while Exports grew 116% year-on-year.
  • Export scale-up expectation: Management expects the Exports vertical to continue building scale as the year progresses. In Q1FY27, Exports delivered 116% year-on-year growth on a smaller Q1FY26 base, consistent with the stated recovery carrying into the current year.
  • Active Ingredients alignment: Management stated it remains focused on aligning Active Ingredients production with captive Formulations requirements to improve blended profitability and utilise existing capacity. In Q1FY27, this business recorded a 14% year-on-year revenue decline amid the West Asia crisis and Technicals market headwinds.
  • Herbicides facility timeline: Management stated the new Formulations facility at Kerala GIDC, Ahmedabad remains on track, with the facility intended to support herbicides growth and release capacity at the existing facility to improve throughput during the peak Kharif season.
  • Profitability improvement expectations: Management expects that enhancing operational efficiency, optimising manufacturing and procurement, improving capacity utilisation, expanding market reach, strengthening customer relationships, focusing on higher-margin products, and maintaining prudent financial and working capital management will further improve productivity, operational performance, and profitability in the coming financial years. In Q1FY27, EBITDA margin stood at 14.9% versus 13.8% in Q1FY26, aided by a better product mix in Domestic Branded Formulations and partially by price realisations.
  • AGM and governance items: The 12th Annual General Meeting is scheduled for Thursday, September 24, 2026, at 11:30 a.m. IST through video conferencing or other audio-visual means. The cut-off date for sending letters to shareholders who have not registered their email addresses is August 21, 2026. Agenda items include adoption of the audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY 2025-26, reappointment of retiring director Rameshbhai R Talavia, ratification of cost auditor remuneration for M/s. Dalwadi & Associates at ₹65,000 plus GST and expenses, appointment of Mrs. Megha Joshi as an Independent Director for five years from August 8, 2026, and re-appointment of Mr. Jamankumar H. Talavia and Mr. Jagdish R. Savaliya as Whole-Time Directors for a three-year term starting August 1, 2027.
  • Employee stock options: As on June 30, 2026, no stock options were granted to eligible employees under the Dharmaj Employees Stock Option Plan 2023, which permits a maximum of 300,000 options convertible into an equal number of equity shares.

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from a cautious Hold (Jul 2023) worried about inflation-hit consumer sentiment, rain-affected cooling demand, LED price deflation and soft kitchen appliances, while relying on C&W/lighting as offsets, to a Buy (Oct 2024) driven by post-summer channel refill, festive pickup, softer input costs and EMS growth. Persistent themes were weak consumer sentiment, kitchen-appliance softness and B2C lighting price erosion. The main change was the growth engine: early optimism centred on C&W outperformance and a low-base margin recovery, whereas the final report looked to EMS acceleration and a seasonal demand rebound.

Fears that came true

  • Inflation-led weak consumer sentiment, flagged from the first report, persisted through to the last report and preceded the -17.3% DISAPPOINTMENT on the Oct 2024 Buy.
  • Soft kitchen-appliance demand was an early warning and was still cited in the last report, coinciding with weak outcomes such as the -25.8% DISAPPOINTMENT.
  • LED/lighting price erosion, first flagged as a risk to FY24 revenue, remained a headwind as B2C lighting price erosion and fed the margin pressure before negative returns.
  • Competitive pricing/margin pressure flagged for fans and white goods materialised as an increasing high-volume low-margin mix, dragging margins in later reports.

Optimism that failed

  • The early expectation that a low base would help normalise margins did not materialise, as later reports continued to cite margin dips.
  • The last report’s post-summer inventory dry-up/channel-filling optimism for cooling products did not translate into positive returns, with the stock falling -17.3% after the Buy.
  • Encouraging early Navratri/Dusshera trends and softer QoQ input material prices failed to prevent the last report’s DISAPPOINTMENT outcome.
