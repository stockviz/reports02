SSWL: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Export disruption from tariffs: The Q1 FY27 investor presentation notes that while reported numbers were domestic-demand led, exports only began showing signs of recovery in June as “tariff-related disruptions normalized.” This indicates exports faced tariff-driven pressure during the quarter.
  • Margin pressure at the gross level: Gross profit margin slipped to 34.7% in Q1 FY27 from 35.0% in Q1 FY26. On a full-year basis, gross margin declined to 34.4% in FY26 from 35.5% in FY25.
  • Declining full-year profitability trends: FY26 EBITDA margin fell to 9.9% from 11.0% in FY25; EBIT margin fell to 7.6% from 9.0%; PBT margin fell to 5.2% from 6.4%; and PAT margin fell to 3.9% from 4.7%. PAT declined to Rs. 202.1 Crs in FY26 from Rs. 210.0 Crs in FY25, and EPS fell to Rs. 12.86 from Rs. 13.38.
  • Rising finance costs: Finance cost increased to Rs. 123.2 Crs in FY26 from Rs. 117.2 Crs in FY25, continuing an upward trend from Rs. 102.7 Crs in FY24 and Rs. 83.5 Crs in FY23.
  • Working capital strain: Changes in working capital were negative Rs. 147 Crs in FY26, contributing to net cash from operating activities falling to Rs. 331 Crs from Rs. 517.6 Crs in FY25. Inventories rose to Rs. 952.5 Crs in FY26 from Rs. 743.1 Crs in FY25, and trade receivables increased to Rs. 609.0 Crs from Rs. 486.4 Crs.
  • Rising leverage on the balance sheet: Current borrowings stood at Rs. 550.0 Crs in FY26 versus Rs. 548.8 Crs in FY25, and trade payables rose sharply to Rs. 1,001.8 Crs from Rs. 752.1 Crs.
  • Capital requirements for expansion: The company states it anticipates future business requirements and potential expansion initiatives requiring additional funds, leading to a proposed increase in borrowing limits to INR 3,500.00 Crores.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record monthly turnover: SSWL recorded its highest ever monthly net turnover of Rs. 592.92 Crs in August 2026, a growth of 53.62% YoY versus August 2025, with gross turnover of Rs. 687.51 Crs, up 44.72% YoY — marking the second consecutive record month.
  • Demand drivers cited by management: Sustained commercial vehicle offtake, continued ramp-up of volumes for passenger vehicle and EV platforms, strong demand traction in the 2 & 3 wheeler segment, improved capacity utilization across manufacturing facilities, and continued strengthening of OEM relationships.
  • Export recovery: Exports began showing signs of recovery in June as tariff-related disruptions normalized, described as a potential tailwind for coming quarters.
  • Industry growth outlook: The steel wheel market is expected to grow at 4% p.a. while the alloy wheel market is expected to grow at 12% p.a. over the next five years.
  • Sales mix shift to high-margin segments: A deliberate shift of sales mix towards alloy wheels and exports, supported by increased alloy wheel volumes.
  • Aluminium product diversification: Scaling up capacity for aluminium knuckles — approximately 0.80 lac knuckles sold in Q1 FY27 generating Rs. 23.0 Crs in revenue — with the company working with two leading OEMs and plans to expand its customer base as capacity increases. The company is also exploring avenues to foray into alternate aluminium products.
  • Operations optimization: Development of robotic automated operation processes for operating cost rationalization.
  • Q1 FY27 momentum: Revenue from operations grew 27.2% YoY to Rs. 1,509.8 Crs; EBITDA rose 33.0% YoY with margin expanding 40 bps to 10.7%; PAT grew 43.3% YoY with PAT margin improving to 4.7%.
  • Capacity footprint: Steel wheel capacity of 20 Mn, alloy wheel capacity of 5.0 Mn, and aluminium knuckle capacity of 0.5 Mn, across facilities in Dappar (Punjab), Bhuj (Gujarat), Jamshedpur (Jharkhand), Mehsana (Gujarat), Chennai (Tamil Nadu), and Saraikela (Jharkhand).
  • Strategic partnerships: Tata Steel Limited holds a 6.9% stake (strategic alliance since January 2008) and Nippon Steel Corporation (formerly Sumitomo Metal Industries) holds a 5.4% stake (strategic alliance since December 2010), providing supply chain flexibility, priority support for new grade developments, and international expertise in steel quality and technology exploration.
  • Strong OEM relationships: High share-of-business positions with key customers, including Honda (96%), MG Motors (100%), Suzuki Scooters (96%), Mahindra 3W (81%), Ashok Leyland (64%), JCB (62%), Escorts (60%), and TATA Motors (58%) in respective segments.
  • Dividend continuity: The Board recommended a final dividend of Rs. 1.50 per equity share (150%) for FY 2025-26, continuing a consistent dividend payment record.

3. Key Risks

  • Tariff-related export disruption: Export volumes were affected by tariff-related disruptions, with recovery only emerging in June.
  • Margin compression: Full-year FY26 margins declined across gross, EBITDA, EBIT, PBT, and PAT levels compared to FY25.
  • Working capital and cash flow volatility: Negative working capital changes in FY26 reduced operating cash flow substantially versus FY25.
  • Rising debt and finance costs: Finance costs have risen for consecutive years, and the company is seeking to increase borrowing limits to INR 3,500.00 Crores, which would require member approval and authorization to create security on assets.
  • Customer concentration: The business has high share-of-business dependence on a limited set of OEM customers across segments.
  • Cyclicality of end markets: Performance is tied to commercial vehicle, passenger vehicle, EV, and 2 & 3 wheeler segment demand, as well as the performance of the Indian economy and international markets, as noted in the presentation’s forward-looking statement caveats.
  • Execution risk on expansion: The company’s growth plans depend on its ability to successfully implement strategy, ramp up capacity, and execute on aluminium product diversification and robotic automation initiatives.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Guidance on borrowing limits: Management proposed increasing borrowing limits under section 180(1)(c) of the Companies Act, 2013, from Rs. 2,000 crore to Rs. 3,500 crore, citing anticipated future business requirements and potential expansion initiatives requiring additional funds. This is subject to member approval at the ensuing AGM.
  • Guidance on growth drivers: Management cited sustained commercial vehicle offtake, ramp-up of PV and EV platform volumes, strong 2 & 3 wheeler demand, improved capacity utilization, and strengthened OEM relationships as tailwinds.
  • Observed performance versus guidance: The August 2026 record monthly net turnover of Rs. 592.92 Crs (up 53.62% YoY) and gross turnover of Rs. 687.51 Crs (up 44.72% YoY) — the second consecutive record month — is consistent with the demand drivers management cited. Q1 FY27 revenue growth of 27.2% YoY, EBITDA growth of 33.0% YoY, and PAT growth of 43.3% YoY also align with the stated growth trajectory.
  • Export recovery guidance versus observed: Management indicated exports began recovering in June as tariff-related disruptions normalized and described this as a potential tailwind for coming quarters. The subsequent record turnover in August 2026 is consistent with this recovery narrative.
  • Margin guidance versus observed: Management attributed Q1 FY27 margin improvement (EBITDA margin up 40 bps to 10.7%) to favourable product mix, cost optimization, and a rising share of premium products. However, full-year FY26 margins had declined versus FY25, indicating that the margin recovery is a recent development.
  • Capacity expansion guidance versus observed: Net capex of INR 196 Crs was incurred through FY26 towards incremental capacity expansion, largely focused on aluminium wheels and aluminium knuckles. Aluminium knuckle sales of approximately 0.80 lac units generating Rs. 23.0 Crs in Q1 FY27 reflect early-stage ramp-up consistent with the stated diversification strategy.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from a domestic-demand-led recovery story in FY23 to an export-and-capacity-driven story by FY26, but export weakness remained a recurring theme. Early tailwinds around alloy wheel capacity expansion and FCF/deleveraging persisted and scaled up, while the export risk morphed from sluggish American demand and Chinese aftermarket competition to US tariff disruptions and competition from Vietnam/Thailand. Raw-material price anxiety remained, though the last report added commodity pass-through as a mitigation.

Fears that came true

  • The early fear of a gradual/stalled export recovery materialized: exports stayed weak, the Oct-2023 Buy call (+24.5% predicted) ended as a -24.9% DISAPPOINTMENT, and FY26 exports still declined 19% due to US tariff disruptions.
  • The early concern over higher raw-material/aluminium prices persisted, with the last report still citing West Asia-driven aluminium price volatility as a key margin risk.

Optimism that failed

  • Management’s FY24 export revenue target of Rs5-6bn (vs Rs2.9bn FY23) did not materialize on that timeline, as FY26 exports fell 19% and only a ~Rs600cr FY27 target was being guided.
  • The expected tailwind from antidumping duties did not prevent export disruption, since US tariff-related trade conditions caused a 19% export decline in FY26.
