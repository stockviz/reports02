EMS Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Weather and seasonality. Heavy rains in Uttarakhand disrupted execution across the prior Q2 quarters, and management notes that Q2 is always a rainy season. Because establishment costs, labour, and plant and machinery costs are largely fixed, lower revenue from weather-related stoppages compresses margins. Management also flags that district administration restrictions imposed for safety during rains can halt sewerage work entirely, and that revised estimates, escalation claims, and government sanctions for such losses take considerable time to work through.

Election-related restrictions. The Kolkata project was impacted by the election period and the model code of conduct. Management states that restrictions have now been lifted, but that the Rs. 70–80 crore run rate sought by an analyst cannot be achieved in Q2 for a sewerage network project and will only be reached from Q3, with Q3 and Q4 expected to be much better.

Working capital stretch. An investor noted that the working capital cycle has increased drastically relative to peers. Management described a self-reinforcing cycle: when work stops, government payments are delayed, which further slows work and reduces billing and revenue generation. Management indicated working capital of roughly 120 days of turnover is typically required, and that Rs. 300–350 crore would be sufficient for a Rs. 1,000 crore turnover.

Margin compression and competition. Margins shrank because fixed costs continued while revenue fell. Management also stated that competition is increasing and that margins may remain slightly below the levels seen in 2023–2024, though it expects to return to levels at par with that period.

Execution lag on new orders. For EPC projects, it typically takes six to nine months after a work order is issued to begin real revenue, as engineering, estimates, and departmental approvals are completed first. Project timelines are typically two to three years, with revenue conversion from a work order taking about 18 to 24 months.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order inflows. The company secured work orders of roughly Rs. 317 crore in Q1 FY27. In Q2 to date, it has received work orders of about Rs. 158 crore, and it is L-1 for a Varanasi project exceeding Rs. 100 crore. The consolidated order book stood at Rs. 2,328.91 crore as of June 30, 2026 (described as Rs. 2,329 crore as of July 2026 in the call).

New awards and L-1 positions. EMS emerged as L-1 for a Rajasthan sewerage project awarded by the Directorate of Local Bodies, Government of Rajasthan, valued at approximately Rs. 218.65 crore including GST, covering six Urban Local Bodies across Ganganagar, Hanumangarh, and Churu districts. Scope includes sewerage systems with STPs, laying, jointing, testing, and commissioning of networks, a one-year defect liability period, 10 years of O&M, and an 18-month execution period. The company also secured L-1 status for a Rs. 102.85 crore sewerage project in Varanasi in June 2026.

NHAI award. EMS received a Letter of Award from the National Highways Authority of India for operations at the Varshamedi Fee Plaza and upkeep/maintenance of adjacent toilet blocks, with an estimated order value (excluding TCS) of approximately Rs. 2,838.88 lakh and an execution period of one year.

Geographic expansion. The company continues bidding in other states, including Bihar and Madhya Pradesh, and is exploring projects in Maharashtra and Karnataka.

Sector opportunity. Ongoing focus on urban infrastructure, sanitation, and wastewater management is described as creating meaningful opportunities for specialised EPC players, with management pointing to India’s urban infrastructure development as a growing opportunity.

Sequential recovery. Q1 FY27 standalone operating income was Rs. 125.72 crore, up 50.27% sequentially; standalone EBITDA rose 39.81% to Rs. 25.53 crore; consolidated operating income was Rs. 157.24 crore, up 30.49% sequentially; and consolidated EBITDA rose 31.62% to Rs. 28.14 crore. Standalone PAT was Rs. 15.03 crore (up 184.65% sequentially) and consolidated PAT was Rs. 15.49 crore (up 1.28% sequentially). Management attributed the sequential improvement to bouncing back from a slower quarter.

3. Key Risks

  • Weather-driven execution risk, particularly in Uttarakhand, with fixed establishment and machinery costs magnifying the margin impact of revenue shortfalls.
  • Regulatory and administrative risk, including election model code of conduct restrictions (as seen in Kolkata) and district administration safety stoppages.
  • Working capital and cash flow risk, with delayed government payments feeding a cycle of slower work and lower billings; an investor noted the cycle has stretched significantly versus peers.
  • Margin risk from rising competition, with management indicating margins may stay slightly below 2023–2024 levels.
  • Revenue conversion timing risk, given the six-to-nine-month lag from work order to revenue commencement and 18–24 month conversion timelines.
  • Client and geographic concentration, with revenue described as about 42% from Uttar Pradesh and about 61% from Uttarakhand in the quarter discussed.
  • Dependence on government authorities and bodies as customers, exposing the business to payment and approval timelines.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Revenue guidance. Management guided to full-year FY27 revenue at par with FY2024–2025, of the order of Rs. 900–950 crore, representing growth of at least 50% over the prior year. Sequentially, management expects Q2 to be about 30–35% higher than Q1, with growth of more than 50% quarter-on-quarter thereafter, and Q4 as the strongest quarter and Q2 the weakest. Management confirmed confidence in delivering around Rs. 300 crore of revenue in each of Q3 and Q4 to meet the Rs. 950 crore target.

Observed performance. Q1 FY27 standalone operating income was Rs. 125.72 crore, which management characterized as a bounce-back from a slower quarter. An investor observed that meeting the Rs. 900–950 crore guidance would require roughly Rs. 250–270 crore per quarter over the next three quarters, and management affirmed confidence in achieving this.

Margin guidance. Management expects to return to 25% EBITDA and 15% PAT margins, and to be at par with FY2024–2025 numbers in revenue, EBITDA, and PAT by the end of the year. Management stated the current order book was built with similar margins in mind, and that margins should bounce back as revenue conversion resumes. Observed: PAT margin improved from 6.3% in Q4 FY26 to 11.95% in Q1 FY27, which management cited as evidence of margin recovery.

Order book and pipeline. Management referenced a Rs. 2,500–3,000 crore bidding pipeline across Delhi and Maharashtra. Observed conversion: Rs. 317 crore of work orders in Q1, about Rs. 158 crore in Q2 to date, and L-1 status on a Varanasi project exceeding Rs. 100 crore, with management noting time remains in the quarter to convert more projects.

Capex. Management stated there are no CAPEX or investment plans for FY27 as of yet.

Capital return. The Board recommended a final dividend of Rs. 1.50 per equity share (15%) for FY2025–26, with a record date of September 19, 2026, and the 16th Annual General Meeting scheduled for September 26, 2026. M/s. Jatin Sharma & Co. were re-appointed as Cost Auditors for FY2026–27.
