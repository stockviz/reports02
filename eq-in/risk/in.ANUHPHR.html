Anuh Pharma Limited — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Income tax scrutiny and penalty order: On 18 June 2026, the Income Tax Department (Assessment Unit) issued a Tax Demand Order under Section 156 and a Penalty Order under Section 270 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, against the company. The order relates to the Scrutiny Assessment for Assessment Year 2020-21 and concerns disallowances of certain deductions claimed under various provisions of the Income Tax Act. The disclosed monetary impact is Rs. 17,46,026/-, which includes penalty under Section 270A(9) read with Section 270A(8). The company stated it is in the process of filing an appeal before the Higher Appellate Forums within the prescribed timelines and believes it has adequate factual and legal grounds to substantiate its position; it stated there is no impact on financial operations or other activities.
  • Withdrawal of promoter group reclassification requests: At the board meeting held on 7 August 2026, the board considered requests from three shareholders forming part of the promoter group — Vikram Kirtilal Shah (200 shares, 0.00%), Kiran Piyush Shah (15,388 shares, 0.02%), and Kinjal Siddharth Jhaveri (8,16,160 shares, 0.81%), totalling 8,31,748 shares or 0.83% — seeking to withdraw their applications for reclassification. These shareholders will continue to remain part of the promoter group.
  • Concentrated promoter holding and low public participation in voting: Of 100,224,000 total shares, the promoter and promoter group held 71,956,044 shares. At the 66th AGM, promoter and promoter group votes polled were 71,803,484 (99.79% of their holding), while public institution votes polled were 1,390 (3.17% of their holding) and public non-institution votes polled were 573,646 (2.03% of their holding). Total votes polled represented 72.22% of outstanding shares. Only 45 shareholders attended the AGM through video conferencing (17 promoter group and 28 public), against 28,020 shareholders on the record date.
  • Dissent on certain resolutions: While all resolutions passed with the requisite majority, some public institution votes were cast against the adoption of new Memorandum of Association (1,182 votes against, 85.04% of public institution votes polled) and the new Articles of Association (1,182 votes against, 85.04%). Public non-institution votes were also cast against several resolutions, including the MoA adoption (557 votes), AoA adoption (1,787 votes), and the remuneration revisions for both Joint Managing Directors (1,787 votes each).

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Volume expansion and regulated market growth: Extensive volume expansions across API lines, high-value regulated market growth, and a diversified product portfolio improving operational leverage.
  • Market diversification: Strategic market diversification focusing on exports to reduce currency risk.
  • Compliance and capacity investments: Continued investments in compliance, operational efficiency, and capacity enhancement. The company has completed expansion projects and holds USFDA and EU GMP clearances, with manufacturing and R&D facilities at Tarapur and Mahape.
  • Zero-debt balance sheet: The company operates with zero-debt balance sheet strength.
  • Dividend continuity: The company declared a dividend of Rs. 1.50/- per equity share of face value Rs. 5/- each for the financial year 2025-26, approved at the 66th AGM on 12 August 2026.
  • Governance and structural updates: The 66th AGM approved the adoption of a new set of Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association as per the Companies Act, 2013, and the board adopted a revised Policy on Related Party Transactions on 7 August 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Tax litigation risk: The penalty order under Section 270A for Assessment Year 2020-21 carries a disclosed impact of Rs. 17,46,026/-, with the company pursuing an appeal. The outcome of the appellate process remains pending.
  • Promoter group concentration: The promoter and promoter group continue to hold a dominant share of the equity, and the three shareholders who sought reclassification will remain part of the promoter group, keeping the public float and institutional participation limited.
  • Low institutional participation: Public institution holding is 43,854 shares (3.17% of their holding voted), and public institution votes were cast against key structural resolutions, indicating limited external shareholder engagement.
  • Dependence on regulated market clearances: The company’s growth prospects rely on maintaining USFDA and EU GMP clearances and continued compliance investments.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Guidance: Management anticipates a steady growth rate of 15-20% per annum.
  • Observed performance (Q1 FY27): Operating revenue was Rs. 193.81 Crores (+3.93% YoY), EBITDA was Rs. 19.36 Crores (+41.56% YoY), PAT was Rs. 11.42 Crores (+37.63% YoY), and ROCE reached 19.18%. The Q1 FY27 revenue growth of 3.93% YoY is below the guided 15-20% range, while EBITDA and PAT growth exceeded it, indicating margin expansion alongside slower top-line growth.
  • Capital structure: The zero-debt balance sheet and ROCE of 19.18% are consistent with the company’s stated operational efficiency focus.
  • Dividend: The declared dividend of Rs. 1.50 per equity share for FY 2025-26 was approved by members at the 66th AGM held on 12 August 2026, with 72,378,263 votes in favour and 257 against.
