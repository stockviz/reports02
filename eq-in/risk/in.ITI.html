ITI Limited: Recent Corporate Announcements

Headwinds and Challenges

Weak quarterly performance. For the quarter ended 30 June 2026, ITI reported a consolidated net loss of ₹3,225 lakh and a standalone net loss of ₹3,247 lakh. Consolidated revenue from operations was ₹42,503 lakh, down from ₹49,801 lakh in the quarter ended 30 June 2025 and from ₹62,765 lakh in the quarter ended 31 March 2026. Consolidated exceptional items of ₹3,233 lakh were recorded in the quarter.

Auditor disclaimer of conclusion. The statutory auditor, B K Ramadhyani & Co. LLP, disclaimed its conclusion on both the standalone and consolidated results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 (review reports dated 13 August 2026). The disclaimer stems from matters carried over from the auditor’s report dated 29 May 2026 on the FY2025-26 financial statements, which the auditor states continue to affect the quarter’s results and which management has neither quantified nor ascertained.

Revenue recognition and documentation gaps. The auditor notes that for multiple composite contracts, the company recognised revenue including unbilled portions without furnishing documentation substantiating compliance with Ind AS 115 preconditions or transfer of control, and did not assess probable losses from cost overruns, liquidated damages, warranty expenses and additional completion costs. The company is also assessing changes to terms of certain procurement contracts, with effects on costs not assessed. Clear breakups of unbilled payables were not provided.

Internal controls and compliance. The auditor states the company did not have adequate internal financial controls, including absence of a sound information systems security policy and general controls over access, passwords, change management, database changes and audit trails. Non-compliances with SEBI Listing Regulations and the Companies Act were reported, including quorum for board meetings, constitution of audit and risk management committees, and the specified proportion of independent directors; the consequential effect, including non-provision for penalty and interest, is not known. The company’s own note 8 attributes the board composition issue to insufficient independent directors and states a proposal for appointment of requisite independent directors is under process with the administrative ministry.

Going concern uncertainty. The auditor flags a material uncertainty on going concern, referencing the net loss for the quarter, while noting management’s view that the going concern basis remains appropriate.

Unit-level audit observations. Branch auditor reviews (dated 7–11 August 2026) for Palakkad, Naini, Raebareli, Mankapur and Srinagar units identified: overdue receivables from Government of India/PSUs of ₹8,986.69 lakh aged over three years against which only ₹331.07 lakh provision was made; old inventory not assessed for ageing, usefulness or obsolescence, with valuation not confirmed at lower of cost or net realisable value; inadequate processes for identifying MSMED Act suppliers and delayed-payment interest; unbilled revenue of ₹1,165.86 lakh at one unit (₹427.17 lakh from earlier years); revenue not recognised on certain optical fibre contracts while ₹89.39 lakh of related expense was recorded; non-provision of gratuity, paid leave, sick leave and LTC at Naini; PF Trust contribution defaults at Mankapur with outstanding liability of ₹2,721.43 lakh and late payment interest of ₹52.18 lakh; expired NIFT lease at Raebareli since November 2018 with GST burden on accrued rent and ₹16.07 crore of NIFT rent recoverable under discussion with the Ministry of Textiles; and long-pending advances, claims and balances without provisions.

Public shareholding shortfall. Promoter shareholding is 90.02% (including Government of Karnataka’s 0.03% and Special Investment Fund of 7.90%) against public shareholding of 9.98%. The Ministry of Finance granted an extension until August 2026 to comply with the 25% minimum public shareholding requirement, and the company has requested DoT regarding the manner of achieving it, which remains under consideration.

Sick company status and revival dependence. The company states it is under a revival plan after being referred to BIFR and declared sick. The CCEA-approved revival plan involves financial assistance of ₹4,15,679 lakh, of which ₹3,08,435 lakh has been received as of date.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Land monetisation. National Land Monetization Corporation issued an RFP for e-auction of ITI’s 44.03-acre land parcel at Krishnarajapuram, Bengaluru, at a reserve price of ₹1,685.40 crore (announcement dated 19 August 2026). Management cites land monetisation plans among the supports for the going concern basis.

Airtel Business strategic partnership. A press release dated 10 August 2026 announced a strategic collaboration between Airtel Business and ITI Limited to accelerate digital transformation for enterprises. The partnership is to combine ITI’s manufacturing and engineering capabilities with Airtel Business’s connectivity, data centre and sovereign cloud services, IoT, AI-powered solutions, LEO satellite services and cybersecurity. Target segments include regulated sectors such as banking and PSUs, with stated intent to support defence and security needs under Make in India and Atmanirbhar Bharat. Chairman and Managing Director Rajesh Rai described the MoU as paving the way for joint identification and fulfilment of customer digital transformation needs.

ASCON Phase IV defence order. ITI signed a contract on 10 October 2020 with the Ministry of Defence for the Army Static Switched Communication Network (ASCON) Phase IV project worth ₹8,280.36 crore, covering installation, commissioning, telecom equipment, NMS, mobile nodes and civil works, with a three-year implementation period and ten-year maintenance including two-year warranty. A Proof of Concept test bed has been set up at Army Headquarters 5 Signal and two Indian Army premises; the PoC is in process and expected to be completed by 31 December 2026.

Order book and unbilled revenue. Management cites an existing order book of ₹13,88,281 lakh and unbilled revenue of ₹2,34,615 lakh expected to convert into billed revenue within the next 12 months.

Diversification. The company describes focus on defence and railway sector products, solar equipment, NGN products, GPON, LED lighting, optical products, HDPE, OFC, smart cards, defence encryptors and Wi-Fi, alongside an operating data centre at Bengaluru serving government institutions and banks.

Key Risks

  • Inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence across revenue recognition, contract costs, inventories, receivables, inter-unit transactions and bank confirmations, with effects unquantified.
  • Material uncertainty over going concern, dependent on government support, order book execution, working capital borrowings from consortium banks, unbilled revenue conversion and land monetisation.
  • Potential unrecognised losses from cost overruns, liquidated damages, warranty expenses and contract completion costs.
  • Receivable recoverability risk, particularly overdue Government of India/PSU dues aged over three years with limited provisioning.
  • Inventory obsolescence risk where ageing and net realisable value assessments are incomplete.
  • Statutory dues and PF Trust liabilities, including Mankapur’s ₹2,721.43 lakh outstanding and related interest charges.
  • Regulatory non-compliance risk relating to SEBI Listing Regulations and the Companies Act, including board composition, committee constitution and quorum, with unquantified penalty and interest exposure.
  • Minimum public shareholding compliance risk, with the extension running to August 2026 and the manner of achievement still under consideration.
  • Litigation and claims risk where unit auditors could not verify pending litigation status or provision adequacy.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Going concern. Management states the going concern basis is appropriate, citing the ₹13,88,281 lakh order book, conversion of ₹2,34,615 lakh unbilled revenue into billed revenue within 12 months, recovery of billed dues, sanctioned working capital borrowing from consortium banks, continued Government of India support and land monetisation plans. Observed performance for the quarter shows a consolidated net loss of ₹3,225 lakh and standalone net loss of ₹3,247 lakh, with revenue declining year-on-year and sequentially. The auditor notes the material uncertainty without modifying its conclusion on this matter.

ASCON Phase IV timeline. The contract envisaged implementation in three years from October 2020. The PoC, a precursor activity, is now expected to be completed by 31 December 2026, indicating the project remains at the proof-of-concept stage well beyond the original implementation window.

Unbilled revenue conversion. Management expects ₹2,34,615 lakh of unbilled revenue to convert to billed revenue within 12 months. Unit-level observations show unbilled revenue balances from earlier years still unbilled, including ₹146.88 crore at Raebareli pertaining to prior years and ₹427.17 lakh at another unit from earlier years.

Receivable collection. Management states trade receivables and advances are realisable in the ordinary course of business and that MSME vendor identification is progressing. Unit auditors note ₹8,986.69 lakh overdue over three years with only ₹331.07 lakh provided, and a collection plan still being formulated.

Public shareholding. The company states the matter of achieving 25% minimum public shareholding is under consideration with DoT, following the Ministry of Finance extension to August 2026. Current public shareholding stands at 9.98%.

Independent directors. The company states a proposal for appointing requisite independent directors is under process with the administrative ministry. The auditor reports the board composition remains non-compliant with SEBI Listing Regulations.
