1. Headwinds and Challenges

Macro and operating environment (per Q2 2026 analyst presentation, July 21, 2026): - India’s GDP growth is expected at 6.6% in the current fiscal versus 7.6% last fiscal, amid headwinds including higher crude oil and commodity prices, softer global growth, and a below-normal monsoon forecast. - Inflation is projected to rise to 5.1% in fiscal 2027 from 2.0% last fiscal, driven by higher global energy/input costs, food price pressures, and a low-base effect. - Input cost pressures are expected to impact manufacturing and construction, even as growth is supported by government capex, services exports, and private consumption. - The rupee remains under pressure from a widening current account deficit and volatile capital flows amid the West Asia conflict. - The energy supply shock is driving up global inflation, prompting a renewed bias toward raising policy rates across several central banks, including a recent 25 bps hike by the ECB. - Global GDP growth is forecast at 3.1% in 2026, a 10 bps markdown from previous forecasts, as the negative impact of the Middle East energy supply shock is only partially offset by the AI investment boom.

Broker Narrative

The broker’s Neutral stance persisted from August 2023 to June 2024, with the narrative continuing to revolve around global bank spending caution and margin pressure in global businesses. The first report framed uncertainty and spending delays as near-term but expected calibrated margin expansion and strong domestic/GAC growth; by the last report, those headwinds had crystallized into a GR&RS growth slowdown and an EBITDA margin trough, while the broker leaned on an expected H2 CY24 recovery, resilient domestic Ratings, and GBA momentum. The main shift was from cautious optimism on GAC/ESG as growth areas to acknowledging slow ESG traction and treating Q1 CY24 as the low point.

Fears that came true

  • Near-term uncertainty in global businesses materialized as global banks/FIs slowed discretionary spending amid macroeconomic uncertainty.
  • The flagged risk of a global business growth slowdown materialized, with GR&RS growth moderation causing a material fall in EBITDA margin to 26% in Q1 CY24.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that newer areas like ESG would drive Global Analytical Centre growth did not pan out, as the ESG Ratings subsidiary saw slow initial traction.
  • The assumption that calibrated margin expansion and modular employee costs would manage margins failed in the near term, as Q1 CY24 EBITDA margin fell to a trough.
