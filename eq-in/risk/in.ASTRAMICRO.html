Astra Microwave Products — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Q1 FY27 performance was below the prior-year quarter. Standalone revenue from operations was Rs. 176 crores in Q1 FY27 against Rs. 197 crores in Q1 FY26, a decline of 10.7%. Standalone EBITDA fell 13.7% to Rs. 33 crores (margin 18.8% versus 19.5%), and standalone PAT fell 24.4% to Rs. 10 crores (margin 5.6% versus 6.6%). On a consolidated basis, revenue declined 11.5% to Rs. 177 crores, EBITDA declined 19.3% to Rs. 33 crores, and PAT declined 24.0% to Rs. 12 crores.

Management attributed the soft quarter to temporary, execution-related factors. On the August 11, 2026 earnings call, S.G. Reddy described the quarter as “on the expected lines,” with revenue and profitability “modest” primarily due to temporary delays in customer approvals and closing of last-stage technical issues, expected to normalize in coming quarters. Dr. M.V. Reddy elaborated that certain programs planned for Q1 booking were deferred because of inspection issues, delays in proving specifications during the development cycle, and supply chain issues.

Working capital remains a structural pressure point. Management stated that Q1 and part of Q2 typically show a better working capital cycle due to comfort carried from the prior year’s execution, but pressure builds in Q3 and Q4. For the full year, management does not expect any significant change in the working capital position compared with the previous year. The investor presentation shows debtor days of 216 in FY26 (down from 273 in FY21) and inventory days of 157 in FY26 (down from 213 in FY21), with the cash conversion cycle at 274 days in FY26 versus 459 days in FY21.

Supply chain stretch is flagged as a risk. Atim Kabra noted that supply chains are stretched across borders and countries because of global restocking, and that purchase and other departments are being strengthened to ensure materials arrive within defined timelines.

Export revenue has historically been limited. Management acknowledged that the export segment had not been growing, explaining that the company previously had no complete solutions to export — earlier “export” activity was largely offset/deemed export business (BTP), from which the company has moved away toward BTS. Sizable component (MMIC) orders from the U.S. and Europe may take at least another couple of years.

Quarterly guidance inconsistency was raised by analysts. An analyst noted that the press release cited 10%–15% top-line growth while the presentation cited 15%–20%, and that the same discrepancy existed in the prior quarter. Management confirmed that “in excess of 15%, that is 15% to 20% growth, is the right one.”

Management also pushed back on quarterly framing. Management stated the business is not quarterly driven and that yearly targets are what the company focuses on, while acknowledging that quarterly numbers are internal estimates that are constantly fine-tuned.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

A landmark order from HAL for the Uttam Radar. In July 2026, the company secured an order worth approximately Rs. 2,205 crores from Hindustan Aeronautics Limited for procurement of 122 AAAU and 121 Interface Frames for the Uttam Radar. Management described this as the single largest order in Astra’s history and noted its value exceeds the entire standalone order book as of June 30, 2026. On the call, Atim Kabra clarified the value is around Rs. 1,870 crores excluding GST. Execution is expected over about five years, with completion before FY32; Phase 1 of 12 numbers is to be completed by September 2027 (about 14 months from the order date), followed by an average of 25-plus numbers per year, with a possibility of completing the total quantity by FY31.

Order book reached record levels. Standalone order book was Rs. 2,156 crores as of June 30, 2026, and consolidated order book was Rs. 2,849 crores (including service orders of about Rs. 244 crores, described as more accretive to margins). With the Uttam order, Dr. M.V. Reddy stated the total order book reached a record Rs. 4,300 crores as of the call date. Standalone order book composition was approximately 66% defence and 34% space, meteorology and hydrology.

Order inflow guidance for the year. Management guided to order booking of about Rs. 1,600 crores for the full financial year, excluding Uttam, and stated it stands by that figure, with a possibility of improving it over coming quarters. Orders received in Q1 FY27 were Rs. 194 crores (standalone Rs. 185 crores booked in Q1, matching guidance).

Multi-year order intake visibility of Rs. 8,000–9,000 crores. Atim Kabra outlined expected order intake over the next 3–4 years to be executed over 5–6 years, comprising: the Rs. 4,300 crores current order book (including a small portion of QRSAM expected partly before year-end); at least Rs. 700–800 crores to be added from QRSAM for the first three regiments; at least Rs. 500–750 crores from Astra Rafael Comsys; and at least Rs. 3,000 crores from Su-30, Virupaksha and Angad. This excludes chip sales, counter-UAV solutions, the electronic wall, area denial systems, and exports. This updates the earlier QIP-era commitment of Rs. 8,000–10,000 crores of orders over five years.

AMCA program positioning. The company emerged as the lowest bidder (L1) in the AAAU portion of the AMCA program, alongside BEL as L2, for development of the Active Antenna Array Unit. Management expects the contract in about a month’s time and noted the program has a DcPP structure with three shortlisted consortium partners.

Platform participation across airborne programs. Management cited participation in Tejas (Uttam Radar; AATRU subsystem qualified for LCA Mk1A EW pod jammer), AEW&C Mk-2 supply chain, Su-30 upgrades (Tier 1 on radar, Tier 2 on EW), Angad EW suite for Su-30 as a consortium partner, Virupaksha, and LCH subsystems. An additional Rs. 500–600 crores may be added to order book projections from Tejas Mk1A.

New product demonstrations. In July 2026, the company conducted technology demonstrations in Jalandhar for two first-of-their-kind products: an electromagnetic wall and a vehicle-mounted anti-drone system. Management stated these are initial first-of-kind versions that can be customized. Atim Kabra stated none of these products are included in the numbers given to investors — “that’s all upside.” The company expects to have at least two complete radars of its own and one drone-mounted intelligence/electronic intelligence product around Diwali.

Other program opportunities. Management expects approximately Rs. 100–120 crores of orders from Shatrughat and Samaghat programs combined, where the company is part of the DRDO development program and expects production orders from BEL. In BrahMos, the company supplies gimbal-based RF seeker components and telemetry products, and is developing a seeker for the BrahMos NG missile; new-generation development numbers are not factored into order inflow projections.

Space and meteorology demerger. The Space and Weather divisions are to be spun off as a separate listed company with the same shareholding pattern as Astra Microwave (shareholders owning 5% will get 5% in the new company). S.G. Reddy has accepted the mandate to groom the new company. The entity is expected to operate independently from April 1, 2027. Management guided first-year standalone revenue for the space and weather entity at Rs. 300-plus crores with at least 18%–20% PBT margin, described as profitable from day one with its own cash flows. The company plans to launch its own satellite within the first six months of the new entity being listed, as part of a constellation, with focus beyond optics and on data monetization. Management stated the space business currently focuses solely on Government of India programs, with intent to participate in global supply chains for LEO satellites.

Meteorology outlook. The company has supplied more than 45 Doppler weather radars across frequency bands over four years and is the first Indian company to develop a Doppler weather radar supplied to IMD. Management sees visibility to continue production for the next 4–5 years, expects more orders under Mission Mausam in the current financial year (for execution over 3–4 years), and is developing new products including Avalanche Radar. Management stated demand is expected for the next 8–10 years.

Joint venture performance. Astra Rafael Comsys (ARC) had an order book of Rs. 836 crores as of the call date, with planned sales of Rs. 360 crores in the current year. ARC is described as India’s first private sector military-grade Software Defined Radio manufacturing facility, spread across 48,000 sq. ft.

Capital allocation stance. Management stated it will not dilute equity for working capital, will only consider accretive non-organic opportunities, and highlighted positive operating cash flow and improving return on equity. Interest cost fell substantially in Q1 due to significant positive cash on the books at the end of the last financial year, resulting in minimal overdraft utilization.

3. Key Risks

Execution risk on a rapidly scaling order book. Atim Kabra stated the company expects to do at least 6–7x its last year’s annual turnover over the next five years, with every single year exceeding what Astra achieved in its 33-odd years. He identified execution as the focus and flagged stretched supply chains across borders as a risk, noting the company is beefing up purchase and other departments.

Margin uncertainty on new solution products. Management declined to commit to a margin trajectory, stating that solution product pricing will be a function of the market and that there may be a trade-off between order size and initial margin. Management expects profitability across the product life cycle (including AMCs and upgrade cycles) to be “healthier, if not as healthy” as the current business.

Working capital pressure in H2. Management expects no significant change in working capital versus the previous year, with pressure building in Q3 and Q4, and indicated a clearer update would come at the end of Q3.

Export ramp-up timelines. Sizable component orders from the U.S. and Europe may take at least another couple of years; complete export solutions may take about a year or two to reach a stage where the company can approach foreign OEMs and services with a comprehensive offering.

Related party concentration and governance. The company is seeking shareholder approval for material related party transactions with Astra Rafael Comsys (a 50%-owned joint venture) for FY 2026-27, with an aggregate value not exceeding Rs. 200 crores. Proposed transactions are stated to be 16.93% of the company’s consolidated turnover for FY 2025-26. All related parties, irrespective of whether they are party to the transaction, must abstain from voting. Directors with interests include Mr. S. Gurunatha Reddy, Mr. P. A. Chitrakar and Dr. M.V. Reddy (directors on the boards of both companies), and Mr. Atim Kabra and Mr. Suresh Kumar Somani as shareholders of ARC.

Leadership transition. Mr. S. Gurunatha Reddy ceased to hold office as Managing Director and Director with effect from the close of business hours on September 30, 2026. Dr. M.V. Reddy is proposed to be redesignated from Joint Managing Director to Managing Director with effect from October 1, 2026, for the remainder of his tenure up to April 29, 2028. On the call, S.G. Reddy described it as his last interaction as Managing Director, passing the baton to M.V. Reddy and Atim.

Regulatory and compliance matters. The AGM notice includes ratification of cost auditor remuneration, re-appointment of directors retiring by rotation, and approval of material related party transactions. The company has fixed September 11, 2026 as the record date for dividend entitlement, with the register of members and share transfer books closed from September 12 to September 18, 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Revenue guidance versus Q1 outcome. Management guided to top-line growth of more than 15% year-on-year, translating to about Rs. 1,350 crores for FY27, with a healthy bottom line. Q1 FY27 standalone revenue of Rs. 176 crores was 10.7% below Q1 FY26. Management attributed the shortfall to temporary delays in customer approvals, last-stage technical issues, inspection issues, and supply chain issues, and expressed confidence in meeting the full-year guidance, with Q3 and Q4 expected to see good execution as material for those quarters has largely been received.

Order inflow guidance versus Q1 outcome. Management guided to order booking of about Rs. 1,600 crores for the full year (excluding Uttam). Q1 FY27 orders received were Rs. 194 crores (standalone Rs. 185 crores), which Dr. M.V. Reddy described as matching the performance guidance exactly.

Margin guidance versus observed margins. Management stated margins for the year are expected to be similar to last year, with a positive delta increase. Q1 FY27 standalone EBITDA margin was 18.8% versus 19.5% in Q1 FY26, and FY26 standalone EBITDA margin was 28.0%. Consolidated EBITDA margin was 18.7% in Q1 FY27 versus 20.5% in Q1 FY26 and 28.7% in FY26.

Next-year revenue guidance. Management guided to next year’s revenue of about Rs. 1,600 crores plus/minus Rs. 50 crores, with a different trajectory from FY29 onwards as production orders for Uttam and QRSAM play out.

Segment revenue guidance versus observed mix. Management guided that out of Rs. 1,350 crores projected sales for FY27, close to Rs. 120–150 crores would come from the space sector. Q1 FY27 revenue split showed space at 7.7%, defence at 80.4%, exports including deemed exports at 5.0%, meteorological at 4.9%, and others at 2.0%.

Space entity guidance. Management guided first-year standalone revenue for the demerged space and weather entity at Rs. 300-plus crores with at least 18%–20% PBT margin. An analyst referenced a current run rate of about Rs. 150 crores, and management responded “possibly, more than that, combined.”

Order book guidance versus observed order book. Management guided to an order book of about Rs. 1,600 crores for the full year (excluding Uttam). Standalone order book was Rs. 2,156 crores as of June 30, 2026, and consolidated order book was Rs. 2,849 crores. With the Uttam order, total order book reached Rs. 4,300 crores as of the call date.

Working capital guidance versus observed metrics. Management guided to no significant change in working capital versus the previous year. Q1 FY27 interest cost was down substantially due to positive cash on the books at the end of the last financial year and minimal overdraft utilization. FY26 cash conversion cycle was 274 days versus 459 days in FY21.

Dividend. The Board recommended a dividend of Rs. 2.40 per share for the year ended March 31, 2026, subject to member approval. The dividend history table shows dividend percentages of 12.5% (FY19), 60% (FY20), 60% (FY21), 70% (FY22), 80% (FY23), 100% (FY24), and 110% (FY25).

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from early optimism about INR78bn of AoN approvals and robust defence spending to a more execution-driven Buy case. Persistent themes were large defence order pipelines, government spending support, and eventual order-book acceleration; what changed was acknowledgment of near-term revenue/EBITDA misses, execution slippage (inspection, clearances, supply chain), and the early stage of direct exports. The final report kept a Buy rating, relying on the INR22bn Uttam Radar order, an INR80-90bn pipeline, and demerger value.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning that AoN approvals would take 18-24 months to turn into final orders was borne out by the last report’s ‘delays in awarding larger platforms’ and execution slippage, with 1QFY27 revenue declining 12% YoY and missing estimates by 22%.
