Pritika Auto Industries — Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure from input costs. In Q1 FY27, margins were impacted by higher raw-material prices from March and June, along with increased costs of chemicals and industrial gases. Consolidated EBITDA margin declined to 13.45% in Q1 FY27 from 15.22% in Q1 FY26 (a fall of 177 bps), and PAT margin declined to 4.91% from 5.32% (down 41 bps), even as revenue grew 26.49% year-on-year to ₹144.97 crore. On a sequential basis, however, EBITDA margin improved from 12.02% in Q4 FY26 and PAT rose from ₹4.77 crore to ₹7.11 crore.

Annual margin compression. On a consolidated annual basis, EBITDA margin fell to 14.71% in FY26 from 16.01% in FY25 (down 130 bps), and PAT margin declined to 4.80% from 6.70% (down 190 bps). Basic EPS declined 1.56% to ₹1.26. Return ratios also weakened: RoE fell to 6.22% in FY26 from 8.97% in FY25, RoA fell to 2.97% from 3.89%, and RoCE declined to 11.40% from 12.70%. Net Debt/Equity rose to 0.67x in FY26 from 0.63x in FY25.

Cost recovery timing. Customer compensation for the raw-material cost increase has only been received partially; the remaining impact is expected to be substantially recovered in the coming quarter, meaning margin normalization is still pending.

Dependence on customer qualification for new business. The KION USA order requires sample submission in August 2026 and completion of the customer’s qualification and approval process before regular production is expected to commence from November 2026 — a timeline outside the company’s sole control.

Industry transition risk. Management notes it is closely monitoring the evolving automotive landscape, including the transition towards electric and hybrid mobility.

General forward-looking risk factors. The company’s disclosures note risks and uncertainties including regulatory changes, local political or economic developments, performance of the Indian and international economies, competition, ability to implement strategy, future growth and expansion, technological implementation and advancement, changes in revenue, income or cash flows, and market risk exposure.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong revenue momentum. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was ₹144.97 crore, up 26.49% year-on-year and 4.7% sequentially. EBITDA grew 11.83% YoY to ₹19.50 crore and PAT grew 16.69% YoY to ₹7.11 crore.

Record dispatch milestone. The company achieved its highest-ever monthly dispatch in July 2026 at approximately 4,800 metric tonnes, which management describes as reflecting increasing business scale and execution capability.

New export order. An order has been received from KION USA, with samples expected to be submitted in August 2026 and regular production expected from November 2026, subject to qualification and approval. This is positioned as an opportunity to strengthen international presence.

Repeat and incremental orders. Orders were received from customers including Mahindra & Mahindra Swaraj and CNH Industrial, demonstrating continued customer confidence.

LFC plant ramp-up. After three years of developing and stabilizing the lost foam casting technology, the focus is on scaling utilization. The LFC plant is expected to reach approximately 65%–70% capacity utilization by the end of the year, with a progressively larger contribution to overall business and profitability as utilization improves.

Solar power MOU. Pritika Engineering Components Limited, a subsidiary, signed a 25-year solar power MoU with Spark Grid Private Limited on behalf of the Pritika Group of Industries, targeting approximately ₹110 crore in savings over the 25-year tenure — about ₹70 crore benefiting Pritika Engineering Components Limited and Meeta Castings Limited, with the balance benefiting Pritika Auto Industries Limited. An SPV will be established with Pritika Engineering Components Limited expected to hold 26% equity on behalf of the Group. The arrangement is expected to improve operational efficiency, reduce long-term energy costs, increase renewable energy contribution, and strengthen long-term cost competitiveness.

Related party transaction expansion. Material modifications to related party transactions with Pritika Engineering Components Ltd., Meeta Castings Ltd., and Pritika Industries Ltd. are on the AGM agenda, covering a material increase in the volume and value of related party transactions with subsidiaries and related entities for FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31.

Industry tailwinds. The foundry sector outlook cites the Vehicle Scrap Policy, a government-mandated 20–25% increase in load capacity per axle for goods vehicles, a new manufacturing policy targeting 25% manufacturing share of GDP, infrastructure project emphasis, and foundry sector investment of over INR 6–8 billion to upgrade facilities. The tractor industry outlook cites good farming conditions, government support, subsidies and mechanization schemes, and India’s position among the world’s largest tractor markets. Long-term growth is expected in the Earth Moving and CV industry, and major railway modernization is expected to boost demand in the next decade.

Strategic positioning. Approximately 60% of India’s tractors are manufactured in the region where Pritika’s plants are located, and the company describes itself as the only major tractor components manufacturer in this region. Long-standing customer relationships include Escorts (50+ years), TAFE (27+ years), and M&M (19+ years).

Capacity expansion and diversification. The company is on course to achieve a target of 1,00,000 tons installed capacity (from 72,000 tons currently), is pursuing geographical diversification, new product development including value-added products, and export market expansion, and sees opportunity to increase presence in the LCV segment (currently ~7% of volumes).

Subsidiary capital raise. The Board approved a preferential issue by Pritika Engineering Components Limited of up to 64,00,000 equity shares and up to 4,00,000 convertible warrants, alongside Pritika Auto’s investment of up to 50,00,000 equity shares in PECL via conversion of outstanding unsecured loans into equity. The Board resolved that the company’s shareholding will not lead to loss of majority control of PECL.

3. Key Risks

  • Input cost volatility: Raw material, chemical, and industrial gas cost increases compressed Q1 FY27 margins; full recovery depends on customer compensation.
  • Customer approval dependency: KION USA production commencement from November 2026 is subject to completion of the customer’s qualification and approval process.
  • Margin and return erosion: FY26 saw declines in EBITDA margin, PAT margin, EPS, RoE, RoA, and RoCE, with Net Debt/Equity rising to 0.67x.
  • Technology transition: The shift toward electric and hybrid mobility could affect the automotive component landscape.
  • Execution risk on capacity and utilization targets: The LFC plant utilization target of 65%–70% by year-end and the 1,00,000-ton capacity target are forward-looking.
  • Related party exposure: Materially increased related party transactions with subsidiaries and related entities are proposed for FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31.
  • General business risks: Regulatory changes, political or economic developments, competition, strategy implementation, technological change, and revenue/cash flow variability, as disclosed in the company’s forward-looking statements.
  • Shareholder compliance matters: Unclaimed dividends for FY 2018-19 remain subject to transfer to the IEPF, with shares liable to be transferred if not claimed by 15 October 2026.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Revenue growth guidance vs. delivery. Management reported “a strong start to FY27” with Q1 FY27 revenue of ₹144.97 crore, representing 26.5% YoY growth and 4.7% sequential growth — consistent with the stated momentum across the business.

Margin recovery guidance vs. observed. Management stated margins were impacted by higher raw-material prices from March and June plus increased chemical and industrial gas costs, that partial customer compensation has been received, and that the remaining impact is expected to be substantially recovered in the coming quarter to support margin normalization. Observed Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 13.45% was down 177 bps YoY but up from 12.02% in Q4 FY26, and PAT margin of 4.91% was down 41 bps YoY but up from 3.44% in Q4 FY26 — showing sequential improvement while the YoY recovery remains pending.

Dispatch momentum guidance vs. observed. Management stated momentum continued into the current quarter, evidenced by the highest-ever monthly dispatch in July 2026 of approximately 4,800 metric tonnes.

KION USA timeline. Management expects to submit samples during August 2026, with regular production expected to commence from November 2026, subject to customer qualification and approval.

LFC plant utilization guidance. Management expects the LFC plant to achieve approximately 65%–70% capacity utilization by the end of the year, with a progressively larger contribution to overall business and profitability as utilization improves.

Solar savings guidance. Management expects the 25-year solar arrangement to result in savings of around ₹110 crore for the Group over 25 years, including approximately ₹70 crore for Pritika Engineering Limited, enhancing energy efficiency and creating sustainable cost advantages.

Capacity target. The company states it is on course to achieve the target of 1,00,000 tons installed capacity, against current installed capacity of 72,000 tons per annum.

Forward-looking statement caveat. The company notes that certain statements are forward-looking and subject to risks and uncertainties such as regulatory changes, local political or economic developments, and other factors that could cause actual results to differ materially.
