Sangam (India) Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Power cost pressure: Power is described as among the company’s largest cost elements. The Q1 FY27 standalone results show Power & Fuel at ₹8,755 lakh, up from ₹6,784 lakh in Q1 FY26, indicating a meaningful year-on-year increase in this cost line.
  • Sequential revenue softness: Revenue declined 1.5% quarter-on-quarter in Q1 FY27 (₹867 crore vs ₹880 crore in Q4 FY26), even as it grew 8.1% year-on-year.
  • Rising finance costs: Finance costs increased sequentially in both standalone (₹2,877 lakh vs ₹2,603 lakh in Q4 FY26) and consolidated (₹2,983 lakh vs ₹2,692 lakh) results, and full-year FY26 interest stood at ₹114 crore versus ₹95 crore in FY25.
  • Debt-funded expansion: The ~₹1,500 crore capex plan is to be funded through internal accruals and term debt, with net debt/equity at 1.1x in FY26. The company notes it operates “within a firm internal leverage discipline.”
  • Labour intensity of garmenting: Garmenting is described as the most labour-intensive stage of the textile chain, with the company mitigating this through a high degree of automation from the design stage.
  • Comparability caveat: Depreciation for Q1 FY27 is not directly comparable with Q1 FY26 because useful lives of certain assets were revised effective 1 April 2025.
  • Execution timeline risk: The capex plan runs to March 2029, with garment units to be “ramped up in phases, in step with customer commitments.”

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong Q1 FY27 profitability: Revenue ₹867 crore (+8.1% YoY), gross margin 43.6% (+640 bps YoY), EBITDA ₹112 crore (+59.6% YoY, 12.9% margin), PAT ₹41 crore versus ₹2 crore in Q1 FY26.
  • High capacity utilisation: Denim fabric at 98% and PV fabric at 97% in Q1 FY27; existing utilisation across spindles, rotors, denim, recycled polyester fibre and synthetic processing is above 90–95%.
  • ~₹1,500 crore growth capex (to March 2029): Completes integration from fibre to garment across both cotton and synthetic value chains, including entry into garmenting. Proposed additions include 80,000 spindles, 2,700 rotors, 2 denim lines, 10 lakh pieces/month denim garmenting, 40 TPD recycled polyester fibre, and 4 chambers plus 5 lakh pieces/month PV garmenting.
  • Garmenting earnings potential: ~₹300 crore annual EBITDA at full utilisation (estimated).
  • Renewable energy savings: Existing 36 MW (19 MW solar + 5 MW wind + 12 MW hybrid); 40.7 MW under implementation (18 MW solar from Q2 FY27, 2.7 MW solar from August 2026, 20 MW hybrid from April 2027). Expected annual savings of ₹48 crore (₹22 crore solar + ₹26 crore hybrid) at full operation; on completion, 76.7 MW will cover a substantial share of power requirement at fixed cost.
  • Diversified, de-risked portfolio: Five revenue segments and a two-thirds domestic base; exports across 50+ countries.
  • Circular economy expansion: Recycled polyester fibre capacity rising from 45 TPD to 85 TPD, consuming close to 50,000 MT of plastic waste annually at full capacity.
  • Balance sheet improvement: Cash conversion cycle improved to 55 days in FY26 from 80 days in FY25; net debt/equity held stable at 1.1x through the investment phase.
  • Promoter capital infusion: Board approved issuance of up to 18,00,000 warrants convertible into equal equity shares at ₹555.56 per warrant, aggregating up to ₹100,00,08,000, to promoter/promoter group on a preferential basis (subject to member and regulatory approvals). Warrants convertible within 18 months from allotment. Promoter holding would rise from 70.52% to 71.54% post-conversion.
  • Recognition: Four-star export house recognised by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade.

3. Key Risks

  • Leverage and interest burden: Expansion is partly term-debt funded; interest costs are already elevated and rising sequentially.
  • Commodity and input cost exposure: Cost of materials consumed rose YoY (standalone ₹49,341 lakh vs ₹44,068 lakh), and power costs increased sharply.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk: Garmenting is a new business for the company; units are to be ramped in phases tied to customer commitments, and the full plan extends to March 2029.
  • Demand cyclicality: Textile demand and export markets across 50+ countries expose the business to global demand conditions.
  • Dilution: The preferential warrant issue, if converted, would increase share count by up to 18,00,000 shares and raise promoter holding to 71.54%.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The presentation itself notes known and unknown risks and uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ materially from management expectations.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Guidance: ~₹1,500 crore capex to be completed by March 2029, fully funded through internal accruals and term debt, within a firm internal leverage discipline. Garmenting expected to generate ~₹300 crore annual EBITDA at full utilisation. Renewable additions expected to deliver ₹48 crore annual savings at full operation. Warrants convertible within 18 months from allotment.
  • Observed performance: Q1 FY27 delivered revenue growth of 8.1% YoY with EBITDA margin expansion of 418 bps to 12.9% and PAT of ₹41 crore versus ₹2 crore in Q1 FY26. Capacity utilisation remained high (denim 98%, PV 97%). FY26 full-year revenue was ₹3,243 crore with EBITDA of ₹329 crore and PAT of ₹83 crore. Cash conversion cycle improved to 55 days in FY26 from 80 days in FY25, and net debt/equity was held at 1.1x in FY26, consistent with the stated leverage discipline. Capital work in progress declined from ₹172 crore in FY25 to ₹82 crore in FY26, while property, plant and equipment rose from ₹1,273 crore to ₹1,500 crore, indicating ongoing capital deployment.
