TD Power Systems — Recent Corporate Announcements

Headwinds and Challenges

  • Execution pressure amid buoyant demand. Management describes the current high rate of order inflow as placing pressure squarely on execution, and states the focus is heavily on execution.
  • Domestic demand remains subdued. India demand is described as fairly subdued, growing around 10–12%, supported by metals and other sectors, with no explosive growth in the economy or in power generation demand. Management does not expect more than this and expects it to continue.
  • Limited AI/data-centre opportunity in India. Management states it does not see meaningful hyperscaler-type AI demand in India, citing the need for gas and large quantities of water for baseload power, and views diesel engines as unsuitable for large-scale 500 MW to 1 GW hyperscaler facilities.
  • Railways segment under review. No fresh orders are being taken in railways; sustainability of the segment will be reviewed at the end of the year, and production space will be redeployed to generator and motor products once the Indian railway contract is completed.
  • Turkey business pipeline weak. About EUR 3–3.5 million of orders are to be executed this year, with no big pipeline visible for next year; the facility continues as an insurance/service backup with a cost to keep it alive.
  • Working capital and receivables constraints. Payment terms are fixed with customers and cannot be altered dramatically without risking business; working capital needs and funding requirements may increase. Other current liabilities rose on customer advances and taxation provisions.
  • No tariff refunds. As products are exported from India, the company is not importer on record and will not receive US tariff refunds.
  • Regulatory/listing risk (from the in-principle approval disclosure). Non-compliance with trading regulations, incomplete or incorrect information submitted to exchanges, and failure to meet listing requirements can result in withdrawal of in-principle approval or impact share listing.
  • General forward-looking risk factors. Known and unknown risks, uncertainties and assumptions that are difficult to predict, including performance of Indian and international economies and industries, competition, strategy implementation, future growth and expansion levels, technological implementation and advancements, changes in revenue, income or cash flows, market preferences, and exposure to market risks.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Very strong demand across verticals. Management describes a very buoyant market in all segments, with an extremely buoyant pipeline and the highest-ever quarterly order inflow and sales in Q1 FY27.
  • Structural demand drivers. AI data centres, grid stabilization, basic power generation, and the push towards renewables driving geothermal, hydro, and waste-to-energy demand.
  • Equipment shortage. A tremendous shortage of power generation equipment, specifically gas turbines, gas engines, transformers and generators, with very strong demand forecasts.
  • Segment momentum:
    • Steam turbine: steady market with around 10–12% growth in captive power plants, biomass, waste-to-heat recovery; multiple orders from leading turbine OEMs across steel, cement and sugar industries, CPP orders, and export orders for USA energy-infrastructure projects.
    • Gas engines and gas turbines: massive, uninterrupted growth; large volume orders and large-volume repeat orders for US data-centre applications; significant volume orders from a major global gas-turbine OEM in the US.
    • Hydro: one of the highest years for TDPS; active in refurbishment in India and abroad; large orders such as 4x16 MW vertical hydro generators for an African hydropower project; high-value orders expected in the quarter.
    • Motors: key area with teams working to increase footprint.
  • Large generator segment opportunity. The company is close to signing agreements with relevant parties for opportunities above the current below-100 MW business, with an announcement expected in August. Management notes this is a very large segment dominated by very large players.
  • Combined cycle potential. Management expects open cycle gas turbines to eventually move towards combined cycle, which would naturally improve the opportunity size; TDPS works with all major steam turbine companies globally.
  • Capacity expansion path. Sufficient capacity for FY28 of around INR 22 billion, with debottlenecking investments of around INR 500 million; plans to move capacity to INR 40 billion and above for FY29 and FY30, with details in the next earnings call.
  • Demand inelasticity. Power plant cost is a very small percentage of overall project cost (less than 5% for data centres), implying high demand elasticity and compulsive buying power.
  • Order book diversification. Order book of Rs. 22,073 million as of 30 June 2026, spanning generator and motor manufacturing, railway, spares and aftermarket, and Turkey.
  • Fundraise and promoter participation. Board approval on August 14, 2026 for a preferential issue to promoters (Nikhil Kumar and Mohib Nomanbhai Khericha) of up to 6,25,000 equity shares at Rs. 1,200 each pre-split (up to 12,50,000 shares at Rs. 600 each post-split), and a QIP of up to Rs. 600 crores. In-principle approval from BSE and NSE (letters dated September 11, 2026) for the preferential issue of 12,50,000 equity shares of face value Rs. 1 each at not less than Rs. 600 per share. Shareholders passed all resolutions at the EGM held September 10, 2026, including the preferential issue for aggregate consideration not exceeding Rs. 75.00 crore and the QIP.

Key Risks

  • Execution risk from the current high rate of order inflow, as flagged in the company’s own risk disclosure.
  • Demand sustainability uncertainty. Differing media views on the sustainability of the buoyancy; management counters this by citing non-refundable advances taken by OEM customers from buyers as the basis for confidence.
  • Customer concentration/relationship risk. Payment terms cannot be altered significantly without losing business; business continuity with customers is stated as the number one priority.
  • Geopolitical and supply chain disruption. Referenced in the context of current geopolitical issues, though management states no disruption is seen in order inflow and no let-up in demand.
  • Execution delays on the data centre side. Noted by management, though products are shipped to multiple sites, data centres and customers, and all customers are taking delivery as ordered.
  • Regulatory/listing risk tied to the in-principle approval, as described above.
  • Forward-looking statement risk covering economic and industry performance, competition, strategy implementation, growth and expansion, technology, revenue/income/cash flow changes, market preferences, and market risk exposure.

Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • FY27 revenue guidance upgraded to Rs. 2,600 crores, with a small chance the company may cross this number. This guidance does not include any revenue from large turbine generators, as large generators take 18–20 months to make.
  • Q1 FY27 observed performance (standalone): total income of INR 6.3 billion versus INR 3.63 billion in the same period last year, up 74%; EBITDA margin of 19.34% including other income, excluding exceptional and treasury income, versus 18.7%; PAT and comprehensive income of INR 853 million versus INR 471 million, up 81%.
  • Q1 FY27 observed performance (consolidated): sales of INR 6.43 billion versus INR 3.76 billion, up 71%; PAT and other comprehensive income of INR 860 million versus INR 500 million, up 72%. Total revenue of Rs. 6,401 million (up 71% YoY), EBITDA of Rs. 1,217 million at 19.0% margin (up 72% YoY), PAT of Rs. 860 million at 13.4% margin (up 72% YoY).
  • Order inflow: INR 7.34 billion in the quarter, up 87% QoQ; direct and deemed exports of INR 6.84 billion versus INR 2.57 billion, with 93% of quarterly inflows from exports and 7% domestic. Order inflow of Rs. 7,341 million, up 87% YoY.
  • Order book: INR 22.08 billion for the manufacturing segment — INR 19.29 billion generator and motor manufacturing, INR 2.11 billion railway, INR 0.22 billion spares and aftermarket, INR 0.46 billion Turkey. Total order book of Rs. 22,073 million as of 30 June 2026. Export and deemed exports excluding railway is 57%.
  • Order inflow expectation: around INR 700 crores per quarter, implying INR 2,800 crores plus for the full year.
  • Capacity guidance: sufficient capacity for FY28 of around INR 22 billion, with debottlenecking investments of around INR 500 million; FY27 capex anticipated at INR 50 crores; capacity targeted at around INR 32 billion for FY28 (described as approximate, not a hard ceiling); plans for FY29 and FY30 to move capacity to INR 40 billion and above, with details to be provided in the next earnings call.
  • Margin guidance: maintain gross contribution margin within approximately plus/minus 1%, with levers including pricing, cost reductions, product mix, exchange rate gains, raw material prices, and factory loading/capacity utilization. EBITDA margin guidance of 18–19% maintained.
  • Service revenue: remains around 5–6% of overall sales, growing at the same rate as sales (30–40% per year); generators are not expected to require service in the first 10 years.
  • Cash position: strong at INR 2.4 billion.
  • Employee costs: employee expenses rose 20% QoQ as headcount scales from 1,750 to 2,600; wage hikes for the year are already built in.
  • Working capital: expected to remain in line with current levels given significant growth; other current liabilities increased on customer advances and taxation provisions.
  • Turkey guidance: EUR 3–3.5 million of sales for this year, with no outlook for next year; facility retained as service backup for the European market.
  • Hydro refurbishment: a few orders won and in the Q1 order book, with more jobs in the pipeline for Q2 and Q3; no current plans to take refurbishment global.
  • Large generator revenue timing: no revenue expected in the current guidance period; management declined to comment further until the deal is announced.
  • Fundraise: Board meeting held August 14, 2026 approved the preferential issue and QIP; EGM held September 10, 2026 passed all resolutions; in-principle approval from BSE and NSE communicated by letters dated September 11, 2026.

Broker Narrative

The initial 2023 note was unrated with no downside risks, built around management’s 17-18% revenue CAGR guidance, a INR13.9bn order backlog, hydro order momentum, and capacity-led growth. By the 2026 Buy report, the narrative had become far more execution-driven: Q1FY27 revenue/PAT growth of 71%/72%, order inflows up 87%, and a raised FY27 revenue guidance to Rs26bn, while explicitly adding risks such as commodity volatility, execution delays, working capital stretch, and demanding valuation. Persistent themes included order-book strength, hydro/steam/gas demand, and earnings visibility; new themes were exports, global equipment shortages, AI/data-centre-linked power demand, and management guidance upgrades.
