HT Media Group — Q1 FY2026-27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Newsprint and currency pressure. Elevated newsprint prices, a weaker rupee and global supply-chain uncertainties are flagged as concerns going forward. Management describes newsprint as the single largest cost line for the Print business, at roughly 25%–40% of the total bill of material including direct and indirect costs. Post-COVID prices had fallen substantially but have since peaked at about $650–$700 per metric ton — the highest level since the pandemic disruption. Because newsprint is priced in US dollars and the rupee is at a lifetime high (weak), the company faces what management calls a “double whammy” on this cost line. Newsprint has no forward market, so forward prices cannot be predicted with certainty.

Print margin compression sequentially. Print operating EBITDA margin was 13% in Q1 FY27, down from 23% in Q4 FY26, attributed to higher commodity rates. Management indicates Q2 will see slightly higher newsprint prices versus Q1, with the exact margin outcome depending on advertiser categories and pricing achieved.

Radio softness. Radio revenue was broadly steady year-on-year (up 3% to INR 32 crore) but operating EBITDA remained negative at INR (3) crore, with a negative 11% margin. The segment continues to operate on a leaner footprint following the surrender of licenses for non-viable stations.

Digital revenue decline. Digital operating revenue fell 28% year-on-year to INR 27 crore, with operating EBITDA of negative INR (3) crore and a negative 12% margin. Management attributes this to a deliberate portfolio reset around leaner, more focused offerings.

Debt at the HT Media and Digicontent level. Although the consolidated net cash position is INR 922 crore, management clarifies that this cash is parked in HMVL. HT Media Limited and Digicontent Limited are described as indebted companies running net debt positions, with debt building in HT Media over the last four to five years post-COVID because of Print English and Radio operations. Shareholders on the call challenged the preferential issue pricing and dilution, and questioned consistency in prior statements about debt usage.

Circulation pricing constraints. Management notes Hindi papers are already well priced, and further cover price increases would be difficult; pricing is described as offering limited leverage, with actions instead taken on the volume side without compromising product or reach.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Consolidated revenue and profitability expansion. Total revenue grew 15% year-on-year to INR 497 crore, EBITDA rose nearly 3x to INR 90 crore with a 12-percentage-point margin expansion, and PAT improved substantially to INR 47 crore (9% PAT margin). Net cash position was INR 922 crore.

Print advertising growth. Print ad revenue grew 15% to INR 295 crore, with English Print ad revenue up 12% to INR 156 crore and Hindi Print ad revenue up 20% to INR 139 crore. Management attributes a substantial part of the growth to yield/pricing improvement, supported by the government advertising rate increase implemented in November of the prior year — the first such revision in seven years. Commercial revenues held on volume while benefiting from yield improvement; government revenues grew on both volume and pricing.

Circulation resilience. Print circulation revenue was virtually flat at INR 52 crore (up 1%). English Print circulation revenue grew 14% to INR 13 crore, described as mostly pricing and mix-related, with steady-state copy levels maintained market by market. Hindi circulation revenue was INR 38 crore, down 3% year-on-year.

Cost discipline and right-sizing. Employee cost declined 11% year-on-year to INR 99 crore at the consolidated level, reflecting ongoing efficiency drives and organizational right-sizing across HT Media and HMVL.

Portfolio rationalization. The decision to discontinue loss-making verticals — including the OTTplay streaming business (decision taken circa 31 March 2026) and the surrender of non-viable FM radio frequencies — is credited with improving profitability, with management expressing confidence that the improvement is sustainable.

Preferential issue to strengthen capital structure. The Board approved a preferential issue in July, subject to regulatory and shareholder approval, aimed at strengthening the capital structure, streamlining debt, and providing capital for general business requirements. Management states that 30%–50% of debt will be retired once the capital comes in, which is expected to be accretive to EPS, long-term interest coverage ratio and credit rating. The issue is priced strictly per the SEBI formula (higher of 10-day or 90-day VWAP), with no premium added, and promoters subscribing on the same terms as third-party investors up to their regulatory maximum.

Other income support. Other income rose 52% year-on-year to INR 60 crore at the consolidated level. In HMVL, other income rose 75% to INR 47 crore, driven substantially by treasury gains (mutual funds, NAV-based, not mark-to-market valuation) and profit on sale of assets. HMVL other operating income also increased, including job work income and scrap sales.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and currency risk: Newsprint prices and the USD/INR rate are the key swing factors for Print margins; management notes that if the dollar moves to 100 and the commodity to $700, margins would dilute.
  • Treasury income volatility: Other income from treasury gains is a function of yield curve movements and market volatility, influenced by the adverse global situation and US markets; management states it cannot be predicted.
  • Advertiser sentiment and pricing pressure: Noted as a risk in the Radio segment, alongside structural and distribution challenges in Print circulation.
  • Macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty: Evolving global conflicts and uncertainties in the external economic and geopolitical environment are cited as risks.
  • Financial risk management exposures: Interest rate, currency and credit risks are identified.
  • Capital structure and dilution risk: The preferential issue will dilute existing public shareholders (a shareholder on the call cited approximately 15% dilution) and reduce book value per share; the pricing at around INR 24–24.7 against a stated book value of about INR 70 per share was contested by shareholders, who also questioned why a rights issue was not preferred. Management cited the shorter timeline (2–3 months faster), greater certainty of fundraising, and no under-subscription risk as reasons for the preferential route.
  • Cash trapped at HMVL: The consolidated net cash of INR 922 crore sits in HMVL, a separately listed company with its own Board and shareholders; deployment of that cash is at HMVL Board’s direction and not freely available to HT Media or Digicontent.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Management explicitly states it will not give specific guidance on revenue or earnings projections. The Chairperson’s message and management commentary nonetheless contain forward-looking elements that can be compared with observed results:

  • Chairperson’s framing: “We began the financial year on a steady note, with consolidated revenue growing year-on-year and profitability improving in tandem.” Observed: consolidated total revenue up 15% to INR 497 crore, EBITDA up 224% to INR 90 crore, PAT up 991% to INR 47 crore — consistent with the stated steady start.
  • Print as anchor: The Chairperson stated Print remained the anchor with advertising revenue growing year-on-year and circulation resilient. Observed: Print ad revenue up 15%, circulation up 1%, Print operating EBITDA up 244% to INR 50 crore at a 13% margin.
  • Radio footprint: The Chairperson stated Radio is now operating on a leaner, more sustainable footprint following license surrenders. Observed: Radio revenue up 3% to INR 32 crore, operating EBITDA negative INR (3) crore versus negative INR (7) crore a year earlier — an improvement but still loss-making.
  • Digital reset: The Chairperson stated digital revenue moderated as the portfolio was deliberately reset around leaner, more focused offerings with the intent of driving sustainable and profitable growth. Observed: Digital revenue down 28% to INR 27 crore, operating EBITDA negative INR (3) crore, broadly unchanged from negative INR (3) crore a year earlier.
  • Newsprint outlook: Management stated it believes newsprint prices have peaked and should plateau before coming down, and that if prices do not worsen, Print operating margins should be maintainable. Management indicated the 13% Print EBITDA margin can broadly be used as a modelling baseline, while flagging that Q2 newsprint prices will be slightly higher than Q1 and that exact margins depend on advertiser categories and pricing.
  • Sustainability of profitability: Management expressed hope of repeating the quarter’s performance going forward, while declining to provide formal guidance. A shareholder noted that even if the current run-rate is maintained, EPS would be around INR 6 on a book value of INR 70, implying a return on equity below 10% and below the cost of capital.
  • Debt reduction: Management stated that once the preferential capital comes in, at least 30%–50% of debt will be retired, which is expected to be accretive to EPS and the long-term interest coverage ratio. This remains subject to regulatory and shareholder approval.
  • Capital deployment: Management stated that all options to maximize capital, cut losses or increase EPS are on the table and discussed up to Board level, without providing forward statements.
