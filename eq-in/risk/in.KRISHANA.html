Krishana Phoschem Limited — Q1 FY27 Announcements Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Monsoon and Kharif Sowing Disruption The quarter opened with the IMD forecasting the southwest monsoon at 92% of the Long Period Average, alongside anticipated El Niño conditions. By end-June, cumulative rainfall was nearly 40% below normal, and national Kharif sowing fell 22.7% year-on-year to 182.7 lakh hectares from 236.5 lakh hectares a year earlier. This weighed on fertilizer demand during the early part of the quarter.

Supply-Side Constraints and Raw Material Inflation Industry-wide production declined 28% year-on-year during April–May 2026, while SSP production dropped about 7% year-on-year. The decline was attributed to raw material availability and logistics challenges rather than weak demand. Geopolitical tensions in West Asia, particularly around shipping lanes through the Strait of Hormuz, raised freight costs, delayed vessel movement, and increased procurement costs. Prices of ammonia and sulfur rose, while phosphatic acid and rock phosphate remained elevated. Sulphur prices climbed from roughly ₹65,000–₹70,000 per tonne in April to almost ₹1 lakh per tonne in June and July.

Raw Material Availability Constrained Q1 Operations Management stated that the primary challenge in Q1 was raw material availability, which limited NPK-DAP utilization to 43%. The company also had to temporarily halt production when switching between different NPK grades, which affected volumes.

Finance Costs and Depreciation from New Capacity PAT margin declined sequentially from 11% to 8.9% despite improved revenue and EBITDA margins. This was attributed to a sharp rise in finance costs (from around ₹13 crore to ₹20 crore) and depreciation (from around ₹8.7 crore to ₹13 crore) following commissioning of the new capacity.

Sequential Revenue Decline Revenue declined about 30% compared to the previous quarter, which management attributed to the seasonal nature of the fertilizer business, where Q4 is historically stronger than Q1.

Trading Business Margin Dilution Trading contributed ₹173 crore of the ₹532 crore quarterly revenue, with EBITDA margins of approximately 7%–8%, lower than the roughly 16% manufacturing margin. Management noted imported products complement manufactured products and create value for farmers despite lower margins.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Monsoon Revival Monsoon activity revived strongly in early July, cutting the all-India rainfall deficit from nearly 40% at end-June to approximately 24% by the first week of July. This triggered accelerated Kharif growing and fertilizer offtake across key markets.

Strong Sales Despite Lower Production During April–May 2026, NPK sales rose 33% year-on-year and SSP sales grew 28% year-on-year, indicating underlying demand strength.

Government Support Measures - Minimum Support Prices were hiked for all 14 mandatory Kharif crops for the 2026-27 marketing season, with meaningful increases for soybean, groundnut, cotton, and pulses. - The Nutrient Based Subsidy framework continued to support the phosphatic fertilizer sector through higher subsidy rates despite global raw material inflation. - The Government initiated work on India’s first strategic Natural Gas Storage System, reinforcing long-term feedstock supply security.

Capacity Ramp-Up The NPK capacity commissioned in the previous quarter is expected to ramp up steadily. Total NPK-DAP capacity now stands at 495,000 MT (330,000 MT existing plus 165,000 MT added). Management expects higher utilization combined with a growing higher-margin complex fertilizer portfolio to drive revenue growth and operating leverage.

Portfolio Expansion During the quarter, the company introduced additional complex fertilizer variants: 12:32:16, 16:20:0:13, 15:15:15, 8:21:21, and 9:24:24. These additions are expected to strengthen market presence, improve product mix, and support volume growth.

Green Ammonia Agreement The company signed a 10-year 70,000 MTPA Green Ammonia Agreement with SECI. The third-party capacity is expected to start in FY29. The arrangement provides that the company’s cost price will match international grey ammonia prices — if grey ammonia prices are higher, the contractual price applies; if lower, the grey ammonia price applies. Management stated this will make Krishana Phoschem the lowest-cost raw material buyer for green ammonia in the country after the facility commences.

Backward Integration Backward integration into sulphuric acid and phosphoric acid continues to provide cost cushion compared to competitors.

Share Capital Optimization The company executed a 5-for-1 stock split, subdividing face value from ₹10 to ₹2 per share, aimed at enhancing market liquidity, improving retail affordability, and broadening the shareholder base. The record date was 3 July 2026.

3. Key Risks

Geopolitical Uncertainty The situation in West Asia remains uncertain, with the Strait of Hormuz having reopened and closed again over a 7–8 day period. This continues to affect shipping routes, freight costs, and raw material procurement.

Raw Material Price Volatility Sulphur prices reached ₹1 lakh per tonne, described as the highest price level, with refinery sulphur production at a lean stage. Management noted difficulty in forecasting sulphur or other raw material prices.

Partial Cost Pass-Through Management stated that overall, approximately 25%–30% of the increase in input costs has been passed on through a combination of higher subsidy support and MRP revisions. The remaining cost increase has been absorbed.

Seasonality The fertilizer business is seasonal, with Q4 historically stronger than Q1, creating sequential revenue volatility.

Dependence on Imported Inputs India’s dependence on imported rock phosphate, phosphatic acid, sulfur, and ammonia leaves the phosphatic industry vulnerable to global supply chain disruptions.

Capacity Utilization Uncertainty While management expressed confidence in ramping up utilization, Q1 NPK-DAP utilization was only 43%, and the company faced raw material availability constraints.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Revenue Growth Guidance In the Q1 FY27 call, management guided to achieving an additional 30%–35% turnover over last year, later referencing a target of around 25% of last year’s revenue. Observed Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was ₹532 crore, representing 35% year-on-year growth. Management also stated it expects to achieve a quarterly turnover of more than ₹500 crore over the remaining three quarters of FY27, supported by improved capacity utilization.

EBITDA Margin Guidance Management had previously indicated EBITDA margins would remain under pressure in Q1. Observed Q1 FY27 EBITDA was ₹89 crore with a 36% year-on-year growth, and EBITDA margin came in at approximately 16.7%, which management described as better than expected. Management attributed this to new NPK product variants and stated confidence in maintaining healthy EBITDA margins in coming quarters. Management also noted that inventory carried forward from the previous quarter at lower costs contributed to higher EBITDA margins.

Capacity Utilization Guidance Management stated that raw material availability has smoothened and expects production to operate at planned levels in coming quarters, with NPK and DAP facility utilization higher than Q1. Management stated capacity utilization is not a problem and that the company is confident of achieving optimal capacity utilization during the remaining quarters.

Receivables Collection Management referenced prior guidance of ₹700 crore receivables to be collected in Q1. In the call, management stated that collection was more than ₹700 crore, against a previous quarter debtor balance of around ₹300 crore.

Capex Guidance Management stated it is evaluating certain capacity expansion opportunities but is not in a position to share details as proposals have not yet been approved or disclosed through statutory channels. Once finalized and approved by the Board, necessary public disclosures will be made.

Green Ammonia Contribution Timeline Management clarified that the green ammonia capacity will start in FY29, after approximately two years, and will provide cost benefits linked to international grey ammonia prices.

Full Capacity Revenue Potential Management stated that at 100% utilization of the current asset base, with NPK-DAP capacity of 495,000 tons at around ₹60,000 per tonne and SSP at around ₹20,000 per tonne, total revenue potential can exceed ₹3,000 crore.
