INOXGREEN: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Strategic pivot disruption at INOX Wind. INOX Wind has pivoted from turnkey/EPC execution toward equipment supply. Management stated this shift creates disruptions, and Q1 FY27 revenue was broadly flat year-on-year as a result. The strategy is expected to reflect meaningfully in financials only from Q3 onwards.

Execution and site-related delays. Management cited recurring EPC challenges: right-of-way issues, equipment movement, customer site readiness, and weather. These were described as long-standing and partly beyond the company’s control.

Receivables and working capital. An investor noted trade receivables significantly higher than peers, linked to the legacy EPC business. Management attributed this to Ind AS 115 risk-transfer accounting, where receivables remain until commissioning, and said receivables should fall materially over the next three to four quarters as equipment supply rises.

Quarterly guidance misses. An investor observed that guidance had been missed in recent quarters despite management confidence. Management acknowledged “certain misses” but pointed to EBITDA margin improvement (from 18% a year earlier to 27% in the reported quarter) and maintained full-year guidance.

Silent period constraints. Management repeatedly declined to comment on fund-raising, future guidance, and acquisition-related details, citing the silent period.

General risk factors (from the AGM notice). Potential risks include loss or inadequacy of profits in any financial year and uncertainties regarding business requirements and transaction values in related party transactions.

Macro/policy risks (from the presentation). Actual results could differ materially due to economic and political conditions in India, volatility in interest rates, new regulations, and government policies.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order book and visibility. INOX Wind’s order backlog stood at approximately 4.4 GW as of July 2026, providing execution visibility of more than 24–36 months. As of July 2026, equipment supply was ~59% of the third-party order book, with 41% turnkey.

Group synergies. INOX Clean plans 3 GW+ of IPP capacity annually, which management expects to drive orders for INOX Wind, INOX Green, and INOX Renewable Solutions. An MOU for 1.5 GW with INOX Clean was signed in June 2026, with firm orders for 500 MW signed so far. INOX Wind also received a 200 MW LOA from NLC India in July 2026 (a repeat order).

Industry macro tailwinds. 1.4 GW of wind capacity was commissioned in India in Q1 FY27; total installed wind capacity was 57.4 GW as of June 2026. Management expects 8–10 GW of annual wind additions over the next few years, driven by RTC, FDRE, and hybrid capacity. Of 9.34 GW of renewable capacity awarded in Q1, 2.35 GW (25%) was standalone wind. Power demand in the first four months of FY27 was the highest in four years.

INOX Green’s O&M growth. The O&M portfolio stood at ~13.3 GWp as of June 2026 (~10.5 GW wind, balance solar), including investments to acquire ~6.5 GW of operational wind O&M assets. Machine availability averaged ~96.3%. Value-added services (turbine overhauls, life extension) are a growing revenue stream; life extension is being targeted up to 35 years versus the standard 25.

Wind World India acquisition. NCLT Ahmedabad approval was received; formalities are expected to complete in Q2 FY27, with financial consolidation thereafter. Wind World’s O&M portfolio is ~4.5 GW with clients including Tata, ReNew, Greenko, Apraava, and Hindustan Zinc, spread across Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, and Andhra Pradesh. It generated ~INR 580 crores revenue in FY26 with contracted annual price escalations of ~5%.

Demerger and asset-light transition. The demerger of the power evacuation infrastructure business from INOX Green into INOX Renewable Solutions was completed with record date August 1, 2026. INOX Green becomes an asset-light O&M player with improved ROE and ROCE. IRSL is expected to list after regulatory approvals (management indicated 2–3 months).

IRSL expansion. The Jaipur transformer factory is gearing up for 4.9 MVA transformers for the 4X series, with plans for 8–20 MVA and 100 MVA+ transformers, plus power electronics (inverters, unit substations, energy capacitor systems). USS commercial launch is expected in FY27. The company owns four cranes in operation, with more planned this financial year.

4X turbine. Foundation work is complete; tower and main components are ready. First prototype installation was targeted for August 2026, with commercial launch expected by end of FY26.

ALMM indigenization. Management stated 80–90% of wind turbine components are already indigenized, targeting near-100% by end of the calendar year, and expects this to provide an advantage for at least three years.

Other growth levers (from the presentation). Asset-light model post-deleveraging, long-term O&M contracts with steady cashflows, multi-year residual contract duration, and group targets: Inox Clean 3 GW+ annual capacity addition, Inox Solar 11 GW solar cell/module manufacturing, Inox Neo 14 GW hybrid RE capacity by FY29.

3. Key Risks

  • Guidance execution risk. Full-year revenue growth guidance of 75% with 20–22% consolidated EBITDA margin requires a heavily H2-weighted delivery (70–75% of business in H2), and Q1 FY27 showed flat year-on-year revenue.
  • Customer/site readiness risk. Turnkey execution remains exposed to right-of-way, site readiness, and weather delays.
  • Working capital risk. Elevated receivables tied to legacy EPC and Ind AS 115 commissioning-linked recognition.
  • Related party concentration. INOX Clean is a major and growing customer; management stated all contracts are at arm’s length with no preferential pricing, and that dependence is mutual.
  • Regulatory/process risk. IRSL listing and acquisition completions depend on regulatory approvals and NCLT processes.
  • Macro/policy risk. Interest rate volatility, new regulations, and government policy changes could adversely affect results.
  • Profit adequacy risk (AGM notice). Potential loss or inadequacy of profits in any financial year.
  • Force majeure. Management cited events beyond control (e.g., Middle East crisis) as the principal risk to guidance.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

INOX Wind revenue and margin guidance. Management maintained full-year guidance of 75% revenue growth over the previous year and 20–22% consolidated EBITDA margin, noting the business is H2-heavy (70–75% in H2). Observed: Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue was INR 872 crores, adjusted EBITDA INR 237 crores, PBT INR 95 crores, PAT INR 64 crores, cash profit INR 153 crores. Revenue was broadly flat year-on-year. Management stated operations showed resilience despite the pivot and that the strategy should reflect meaningfully from Q3 onwards. An investor noted the implied ask rate of ~INR 6,500 crores over the next three quarters (~100% growth); management did not quantify quarterly growth but maintained annual guidance.

Deferred revenue. Management had previously indicated ~INR 400 crores of revenue deferred from Q4, expected to be recognized in Q1 and Q2. Update: it was partly recognized in Q1, with the balance to be covered within FY27.

INOX Green financials. Q1 FY27 total income INR 101 crores (+17% YoY), EBITDA INR 57 crores (+19% YoY), PBT INR 54 crores (+74% YoY), PAT INR 41 crores (+86% YoY), cash PAT INR 55 crores (+25% YoY).

INOX Green EBITDA guidance. Management maintained a ~50% EBITDA margin guidance for the wind O&M portfolio and an INR 600 crores EBITDA guidance on an annualized basis, expected to be reflected from Q3/Q4 onwards post-consolidation of acquisitions. Observed: Q1 FY27 EBITDA was INR 57 crores. Management attributed quarterly variation to one-time expenditures, infrastructure enhancement, and life-extension costs, and said value-added services previously expensed without separate billing will be billed separately going forward.

Per-MW revenue. Management reiterated INR 9–10 lakh per MW (excluding GST) for the ~4 GW organic wind portfolio; the two acquired portfolios are expected to be substantially higher but are not yet consolidated (currently accounted as investments under Ind AS 109).

Consolidation timing. Management guided that Wind World India consolidation would occur post-Q2 FY27, with other investments expected to complete and consolidate in FY27. The INR 600 crores EBITDA guidance was described as annualized from Q3/Q4 onwards, not a quarterly figure.

Working capital. Management maintained its annualized working capital guidance and stated receivables should drop significantly over the next three to four quarters as equipment supply increases.

4X turbine timeline. First prototype installation targeted August 2026; commercial launch expected by end of FY26. Management also said it is contemplating higher models if demand exists.

IRSL listing. Management indicated the listing process should take approximately 2–3 months post record date, subject to regulatory approvals, and declined to guide on IRSL numbers until closer to listing.
