Naga Dhunseri Group Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Volatile quarterly performance driven by treasury operations. The company’s standalone results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 show total revenue from operations of ₹934.42 lakhs, swinging sharply from ₹(646.00) lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 and ₹462.78 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2025. The company explicitly notes that the nature of the capital market in which it operates is such that quarterly results do not indicate likely annual performance.

Realised losses on fair value changes. Standalone realised net gain/(loss) on fair value changes was ₹(167.24) lakhs for the June 2026 quarter, compared with ₹(1,058.69) lakhs in the March 2026 quarter and ₹(62.56) lakhs in the June 2025 quarter. For the year ended 31 March 2026, realised losses stood at ₹(994.74) lakhs.

Weak dividend income. Standalone dividend income fell to ₹1.20 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter from ₹2.35 lakhs in March 2026 and ₹27.46 lakhs in June 2025. For FY 2025-26, dividend income was ₹39.72 lakhs.

Tea segment losses in the prior quarter. In the consolidated segment results for the quarter ended 31 March 2026, the Tea segment recorded a total loss of ₹(6,316.79) lakhs, with India at ₹(4,752.13) lakhs and Outside India at ₹(1,564.66) lakhs. For the year ended 31 March 2026, the Tea segment recorded a total loss of ₹(2,627.68) lakhs.

Hyperinflationary conditions in Malawi. The Group has applied Ind AS 29 “Accounting for Hyperinflationary Economies” to step-down subsidiaries operating in Malawi with effect from 1 April 2024, since the Malawi kwacha is the functional currency of these subsidiaries in a hyperinflationary economy. The restatement resulted in a net loss of ₹39 lakhs for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, recognised in the consolidated financial results and described as non-cash in nature.

Seasonality. The Group notes that cultivation and production of tea and macadamia nuts is seasonal in nature, and combined with the nature of the capital market in which the parent operates, the Group’s performance varies from quarter to quarter.

Finance costs. Consolidated finance costs were ₹523.53 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter, compared with ₹622.48 lakhs in March 2026 and ₹444.25 lakhs in June 2025. For FY 2025-26, finance costs were ₹2,063.04 lakhs.

3. Key Risks

Dependence on capital markets. The parent company’s business activity falls within a single operating segment, “Treasury Operations,” and its results are directly exposed to capital market movements, as reflected in the sharp swings in realised and unrealised fair value changes across quarters.

Seasonality and commodity exposure. The Group’s tea and macadamia nut operations are seasonal, and segment results show significant volatility between quarters, including large losses in the March 2026 quarter.

Geographic concentration in hyperinflationary economies. Step-down subsidiaries operate in Malawi, a hyperinflationary economy, exposing the Group to currency translation and restatement effects under Ind AS 29.

Reliance on equity-accounted investees. A substantial portion of consolidated profit derives from the share of profit of equity-accounted investees (₹2,875.60 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter), whose interim financial information in respect of an associate was not reviewed by the parent’s auditors.

Unreviewed components in consolidated results. The auditors did not review the interim consolidated financial statements of one subsidiary (contributing total revenues of ₹8,608.71 lakhs and net profit after tax of ₹451.89 lakhs for the quarter), and the Group’s share of net profit after tax of ₹4,190.90 lakhs in respect of an associate was not reviewed by the parent’s auditors.

Finance cost burden. Consolidated finance costs of ₹523.53 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter and ₹2,063.04 lakhs for FY 2025-26 represent a recurring charge against earnings.
