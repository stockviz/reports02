Viviana Power Tech — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin compression at scale. Q1 FY27 revenue grew 246% YoY to ₹71.86 crore, but EBITDA margin slipped to 16.08% from 16.75% in Q1 FY26, and PAT margin eased to 9.65% from 10.05%. On the call, management addressed whether the margin decline was temporary or structural, indicating margins would remain in the ~9%–9.5% range annually.

Working capital and funding timing. Management noted that during December–January, discussions with banks for enhancement of working capital limits took longer than expected. To avoid project execution being impacted by funding delays, the company proactively arranged an NCD facility as a contingency source of liquidity. This was accompanied by a proposed alteration of the Articles of Association to allow appointment of a Nominee Director by the Debenture Trustee.

Receivables build-up. An investor flagged rising receivables alongside increasing cash flows. Management responded that a major portion of revenue came in March, that major amounts were received from debtors, and that more than ₹40 crore of receivables outstanding as of 30 June were received in July. Management stated there were no delayed payments from clients.

Regulatory filing deficiencies. NSE raised observations (email dated 29 June 2026) on the financial results submission. The company clarified that designations of Mr. Nikesh Choksi and Mr. Richi Choksi were inadvertently shown only as “Director” instead of “Managing Director” and “Whole-time Director”; that annual EPS was omitted from the Standalone XBRL due to a technical glitch; and that a PAT mismatch between Consolidated XBRL and PDF arose from minority interest treatment. Revised XBRL filings were submitted on 2 July 2026.

Execution dependence on external schedules. Management noted that execution in the infrastructure business naturally depends on project milestones and customer schedules. BESS projects are awaiting approvals — land has been received in Rajasthan with further approval pending, and in Gujarat the company awaits notice to proceed from the utility (GUVNL) for a battery energy storage park to be developed alongside another developer.

Restructuring execution risk. The disinvestment of 90% of Viviana Life Spaces and 75% of Aarsh Transformers is subject to definitive agreements, regulatory approvals, and customary closing conditions. Valuation was stated as still in progress, with no amount disclosed.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Sector capex cycle. Management described the Indian power sector as entering one of its most significant investment cycles in decades, driven by industrialization, renewable adoption, rising electricity demand, and transmission and distribution investment. The investor presentation cites ₹9.2 trillion of transmission capex anticipated in India between FY25–32E, transmission line length rising from 5.07 lakh CKm (Mar-26) to 6.48 lakh CKm (2032E), transformation capacity from 1,451 GVA to 2,342 GVA, peak demand from 238 GW to 458 GW, and inter-regional transmission capacity from 120 GW to 168 GW. All-India AT&C losses declined to 15% in FY25.

Order book and pipeline. The committed order book stands at ₹1,312.53 crore. On the call, management stated the EPC order book alone exceeds ₹800 crore, and combined with BESS exceeds ₹1,300 crore; unexecuted order booking plus L1 positions is around ₹840 crore. Bids under evaluation exceed ₹1,400 crore, and bids in participation exceed ₹2,600 crore, with Gujarat bids alone exceeding ₹6,000 crore in which the company can be eligible. Management expects a considerable portion of these to convert.

New awards and L1 positions. A turnkey contract of ₹9,74,02,648 from GETCO was received on 27 August 2026 for laying, erection, testing and commissioning of 66kV XLPE cable for Rajkot Zone (Package-2), to be executed within 24 months; this took overall unexecuted order value to over ₹1,700 crore. Separately, the company received L1 bid confirmation of ₹139.49 crore for conversion of overhead 11 kV HT lines into underground cable networks with Ring Main System at Mota Mava and Madhapar Sub-Divisions of Rajkot City Circle under the SI Scheme of PGVCL; with these awards and L1 positions, total unexecuted project value was stated at over ₹1,850 crore. On the call, management also indicated expectation of an LOA of around ₹100 crore from DGVCL for a single tender where it was L1.

Transformer manufacturing expansion. The company is setting up a 220kV-class power transformer manufacturing unit on 14 acres in Vadodara district, Gujarat, expandable to 400kV in phases. An IDT transformer facility on lease is being inaugurated by end-August 2026, with eligibility to manufacture up to 20 MVA; the greenfield first phase targets up to 132 kV. Total first-phase greenfield capex is around ₹90 crore, of which ₹3.5–4 crore has been spent, with around ₹10 crore planned in the current financial year and the major capex in the next financial year. Product plans include solar inverter duty transformers from 500 kVA to 20 MVA, BESS-specific designs, units up to 200 MVA, and a 400 kV testing facility with in-house HV laboratory.

New verticals. The company is pursuing renewable energy integration, battery energy storage systems, and emerging opportunities including data centers. It has bagged two BESS projects — one in Gujarat and one in Rajasthan.

Geographic expansion. Projects have been executed across more than 10 states. Current focus is mainly Gujarat, with bidding also in Haryana, Rajasthan, Bihar, and Uttarakhand.

Restructuring rationale. Management framed the disinvestment as proactive and growth-driven rather than stress-driven, intended to sharpen focus on the core power infrastructure business, improve operational efficiency, simplify the corporate structure, strengthen management accountability, enable sector-specific partnerships and fundraising, and reduce conglomerate discount. The company stated the transformer manufacturing expansion remains a key strategic initiative and the Aarsh disinvestment is not an exit from the segment. The hard collateral strategy is stated to remain unchanged, with around ₹100 crore of collateral expected from Viviana Life Spaces by 2030 for bank facilities.

Institutional engagement. Following the main board listing on 2 June 2026, the company initiated meetings with domestic mutual funds, PMS houses, and family offices, alongside quarterly investor presentations, earnings calls, and regular disclosures.

3. Key Risks

  • Order conversion uncertainty. The ₹1,400+ crore under evaluation and ₹2,600+ crore of bids in participation are not yet awarded; management noted technical and commercial parts are yet to open for the under-evaluation bids.
  • Margin sustainability. Management guided to maintaining current profitability for at least the next four to five years, but acknowledged the Q1 margin level and indicated annual margins of ~9%–9.5% at the PAT level.
  • Funding and liquidity dependence. Execution of the targeted revenue scale depends on existing working capital limits, which management stated are sufficient; the NCD was arranged as contingency liquidity.
  • Approval dependencies. BESS projects await notice to proceed from GUVNL in Gujarat and further approval in Rajasthan.
  • Restructuring completion risk. The disinvestment remains subject to definitive agreements, regulatory approvals, and customary closing conditions; valuation is ongoing.
  • Related party exposure. A material related party transaction limit of up to ₹50 crore with Viviana Life Spaces Private Limited is proposed as advance against capex for purchasing office premises, even as that entity is proposed to be divested.
  • Disclosure and compliance processes. The NSE observations on signing designations and XBRL discrepancies, subsequently re-filed, point to reporting-process risk.
  • Dependence on government-backed funding. Management stated projects are mainly backed by Central Government funds, tying demand to public capex cycles.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Revenue guidance. On the prior earnings call, management guided to a revenue trajectory of approximately ₹900-plus crore for FY27, supported by an order book that had crossed ₹1,000 crore. In the Q1 FY27 call, the medium-term target was reiterated at ₹875–910 crore for FY27, with a long-term objective of a ₹2,000–2,200 crore revenue platform with ₹200-plus crore PAT by FY30. Observed Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹71.86 crore, which annualizes well below the FY27 target, indicating heavy dependence on execution ramp-up through the remaining quarters. Management stated execution momentum continues to improve quarter-after-quarter.

Order book guidance. Management had highlighted an order book crossing ₹1,000 crore. The current committed order book is ₹1,312.53 crore, and subsequent announcements took unexecuted order value to over ₹1,700 crore (GETCO order, 27 August 2026) and then over ₹1,850 crore including L1 positions. When asked whether FY27 order book additions would be ₹500–600 crore, management said “conservatively maybe yes,” with internal expectations higher.

Margin guidance. Management stated on the call that margins would remain nearly 9%–9.5% annually, and that current profitability levels would be maintained for at least four to five years. Observed Q1 FY27 PAT margin was 9.65%, within that range, while EBITDA margin of 16.08% was slightly below Q1 FY26’s 16.75%. The investor presentation guides FY27E EBITDA margin of ~14%–16% and PAT margin of ~8.50%–10%, and FY30E EBITDA margin of ~13%–15.50% with PAT margin of ~8.50%–10%.

Transformer facility timeline. Management had previously indicated BESS and data center transformers by end of the year. On the Q1 call, management confirmed being on track, with the IDT transformer facility inauguration by end of August 2026, and stated the greenfield land has been identified with construction work soon to commence.

Capital allocation guidance. Management stated the disinvestment proceeds will come to Viviana Power Tech, with the amount to be updated as valuation is ongoing. On promoter dilution or QIP, management stated development is ongoing including investment from promoters and promoter groups, and that any development would be published.

Dividend. A final dividend of Re. 1 per equity share (10%) has been recommended for the financial year ended 31 March 2026, to be considered at the 12th Annual General Meeting scheduled for 29 September 2026.
