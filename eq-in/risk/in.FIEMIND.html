FIEMIND: Q1 FY27 Earnings Call and 37th AGM — Key Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Input cost inflation and currency pressure. Management described a significant increase in input costs and continued pressure from a weaker rupee. Raw material cost rose about 80 basis points and total cost increase was around 120 basis points quarter-on-quarter. Roughly 20% of input content is affected, and the pressure was described as industry-wide, not company-specific.
  • Employee cost step-up. Q1 saw a sharp jump in employee cost, driven by minimum wage revisions and annual increments. Haryana was the first state to implement the revision (about a 35% increase), affecting the Rai and Kundli factories, with other states such as Tamil Nadu following. Management indicated the Q1 employee cost run-rate would persist through the next three quarters.
  • Margin below full-year guidance in Q1. Q1 EBITDA margin was 13.5%, against full-year guidance of around 14%, with Q1 described as a quarter that generally carries cost increases.
  • 4-wheeler ramp-up delayed. Revenue contribution from 4-wheelers remained around 2.5%, similar to the past year. Management acknowledged that the earlier expectation of meaningful contribution from FY27 has slipped, with the revenue target pushed out by roughly two quarters and spillover into FY28. The conversion cycle with customers is taking longer than expected, and the development timeline for an average 4-wheeler program is about three years. Management also noted that competition is adding capacity, making the segment challenging.
  • Yamaha export softness. Yamaha’s India volumes were significantly higher than FIEM’s volumes in the quarter. Export models were slower, and the Fascino model — where FIEM does not participate — performed well, while models such as R15, XSR and Aerox (where FIEM has full presence) were slower. Export demand was described as cyclical, with Europe stronger in winter and the U.S. generally softer.
  • LED share stagnation. LED lighting’s share of total automotive lighting has ranged between 61% and 64% over the past seven quarters, which management attributed partly to continued demand for entry-level 2-wheelers with halogen lamps.
  • Hub motor initiative discontinued. The hub motor and motor controller initiative with Gogoro was stopped, as Gogoro is no longer in India, and FIEM is not currently working on that segment.
  • New technology still awaiting customer feedback. Hands-off detection, light control module (LCM), focused/projection lighting and ambient lighting remain at proof-of-concept or customer-evaluation stages, with adoption dependent on customer decisions.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record industry quarter. The Indian 2-wheeler industry delivered its highest-ever first quarter at 7.25 million units, with growth supported by GST rationalization, retail financing and strong rural demand. Exports and electric vehicles grew faster than the overall market.
  • Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Sales grew 18.6% to INR770 crores, EBITDA margin was stable at 13.5%, and PAT rose 16.31% to INR65.19 crores. LED lighting’s share in total automotive lighting was 63%.
  • EV adoption as a structural shift. EVs accounted for more than 9% of 2-wheeler volume in Q1, up from around 6% a year earlier. EV platforms typically carry higher LED-intensive lighting content. FIEM commenced supplies for the Ather new model (Konarc) as sole supplier for that model, the River RX02, and the Royal Enfield EV Flying Flea.
  • Customer capacity expansions. TVS is expanding 2-wheeler capacity from 6.8 million to 8.3 million units by year-end. Hero is tripling Vida capacity from 15,000 to 45,000 units a month. From the next quarter, FIEM will supply all Vida lamps for Hero’s new Andhra Pradesh plant from its Hosur facility.
  • New program wins and launches. TVS’s iQube crossed 1 million cumulative sales; the second Norton Manx model was launched for export to the U.K.; the Hero X440, exported to the U.S., features FIEM front and rear lighting. FIEM is working with more than 100 models across Honda, Yamaha, Suzuki and TVS.
  • 4-wheeler progress. Supplies to Mahindra & Mahindra are scaling up as planned, development programs with Force Motors and other leading OEMs are progressing, and Mercedes prototyping samples have been submitted for testing and evaluation, with a validation timeline of about 1.5 to 2 years before RFQ/RFI for mass production.
  • Capacity expansion. FIEM is expanding its Hosur footprint at both the Kelamangalam and Thally Road plants, largely to serve EV demand from TVS and other OEMs. Capex for the year is targeted at around INR100 crores, versus about INR110 crores last year, with most of it directed to Hosur and some to Tapukara.
  • Festive season and premiumization. The festive season was described as starting on a strong note, and the shift toward premium and electric vehicles is increasing content opportunity. Management cited a strong order book and pipeline.
  • New product development. The in-house EMC/EMI lab is expected to speed up testing and validation and reduce development time. LCM is being worked on closely with two or three customers for near-term launch. Ambient lighting using optical fiber is under development, with a possible breakthrough in the next year.

3. Key Risks

  • Pass-through lag on raw material costs. Recovery of raw material cost increases from customers happens with a lag of a couple of quarters, creating interim margin pressure.
  • Dependence on customer launch and production cycles. Revenue timing depends on OEM model launches, export seasonality and customer validation cycles, as seen in the 4-wheeler delay and Yamaha export softness.
  • Competitive intensity. Management acknowledged that competition keeps adding capacity, particularly in 4-wheelers, and that entering the segment was never expected to be easy.
  • Customer confidentiality limits disclosure. Management stated it cannot share advance wallet-share or model-level details because customers launch first and FIEM declares volumes and models afterward.
  • New technology adoption uncertainty. The timing of adoption for hands-off detection, projection lighting and ambient lighting depends entirely on customer decisions and cost considerations at the vehicle level.
  • Execution risk on capacity. The company stated it will not allow a situation where demand exists without capacity, but capex is being done judiciously rather than aggressively.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Revenue growth. Guidance is 15% to 20% top-line growth. Q1 FY27 sales grew 18.62% year-on-year to INR769.9 crores, within the guided range.
  • EBITDA margin. Full-year guidance is around 14%, with Q1 at 13.5% and management noting Q1 typically carries cost increases. Management expects to average around 14% for the full year, supported by recovery of raw material costs with a lag.
  • 4-wheeler revenue. In the Q4 FY26 earnings call, management had guided INR100 crores to INR150 crores of revenue from the 4-wheeler lighting segment. In Q1 FY27, management indicated this target is pushed out by about two quarters, with FY27 4-wheeler revenue now expected to remain around 2.5% of total revenue, slightly lower than previously indicated, and meaningful contribution now expected from FY28.
  • Capex. Full-year capex guidance is around INR100 crores, compared with about INR110 crores last year. Q1 capex was INR41.15 crores.
  • Employee cost. Management indicated the Q1 employee cost run-rate will remain in the same range for the next three quarters.
  • LED share. Management expects LED’s share to move toward 70% over the next 24 to 30 months, with new models increasingly LED-based and the market potentially reaching 90% to 95% LED by 2030, while older halogen models may persist.
  • Yamaha. Management expressed confidence in recovering Yamaha business in coming quarters, citing export seasonality and model cycles, while noting Yamaha India volumes were significantly higher than FIEM’s volumes in the quarter.
  • 4-wheeler strategy horizon. Management framed the 4-wheeler business as a 10-year opportunity, stating that whether it takes another 12 or 18 months, the company remains committed to the segment.

5. AGM Outcomes (July 31, 2026)

The 37th AGM was held on July 31, 2026 at 10:30 a.m. through video conferencing and concluded at 11:45 a.m. All 11 resolutions were passed with the requisite majority. Key items included:

  • Adoption of audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for the year ended March 31, 2026 (99.93% assent).
  • Declaration of a final dividend of INR40 per equity share for FY2025-26 (100.00% assent).
  • Re-appointment of Mr. Rahul Jain as a director liable to retire by rotation (99.91% assent).
  • Re-appointment of Ms. Aanchal Jain as a director liable to retire by rotation (98.56% assent).
  • Ratification of cost auditors’ remuneration for FY2026-27 (100.00% assent).
  • Re-designation and appointment of Mr. Jagjeevan Kumar Jain as Executive Chairman (99.83% assent).
  • Re-designation and appointment of Mr. Rahul Jain as Managing Director and revision in his remuneration (99.86% assent).
  • Re-designation of Ms. Aanchal Jain as Joint Managing Director, re-appointment for five years and revision in remuneration (98.52% assent).
  • Re-appointment of a Whole-time Director for another five-year term (99.37% assent).
  • Re-appointment of Mr. Kashi Ram Yadav as Whole-time Director for three years and revision in his remuneration.

The cut-off date for e-voting was July 24, 2026, with total paid-up equity share capital of INR26,31,96,600 divided into 2,63,19,660 equity shares of INR10 each. Remote e-voting ran from July 27, 2026 to July 30, 2026.
