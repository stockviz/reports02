IKIO Technologies — Q1 FY27 Conference Call and 10th AGM: Key Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical and war-led supply chain disruption: Management described the current environment as similar to COVID, with sharply extended lead times and volatile input prices. Electronic component lead times moved from a normal 4–6 weeks to, in some cases, over a year. One semiconductor part ordered in January was still not received at the time of the call, against a usual lead time of about four weeks.
  • Raw material inflation: Prices for metals (MS, aluminium, copper) and semiconductors rose sharply over the preceding four to five months, with semiconductor prices in some cases doubling or more. This forced a return to spot buying and repeated design changes to keep production and deliveries on track.
  • Gross margin compression: Gross margin fell from 44–45% to 41%. Management attributed this to the geopolitical situation and raw material volatility, while noting that in-house R&D and purchasing efforts prevented a larger hit.
  • Sequential profitability pressure: Although EBITDA and PAT rose year-on-year, profitability was sequentially impacted by higher raw material prices and higher employee expenses following a revision in minimum labour wages.
  • Middle East / UAE revenue decline: The UAE, Middle East and Gulf business saw a revenue decline due to the war situation, and was the only segment that did not grow year-on-year. A planned opportunity in another country was also delayed by the war.
  • Sequential dip in other business revenue: Other business revenue was marginally lower sequentially amid global trade tensions. Q1 was also noted as generally being one of the weaker quarters.
  • Tax rate variability: The tax rate was around 35% in the quarter, which management linked to deferment of tax reversal, with expectations of stabilisation going forward.
  • Margin mix dilution: Management noted that some high-growth categories carry lower margins, which constrains the blended margin profile compared with the earlier, less diversified business mix.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Revenue growth: Revenue from operations grew 41% year-on-year to Rs. 169 crores in Q1 FY27, supported by the diversified portfolio, improving home lighting ODM momentum and expanding international contribution.
  • Other business scale-up: Other business revenue grew 53% year-on-year to Rs. 124 crores, with approximately 54% CAGR over FY23–FY26, driven by new customers and an expanding product portfolio.
  • Home lighting ODM recovery: Revenue was Rs. 45 crores, up 16% year-on-year and 18% quarter-on-quarter, reflecting improving momentum and new customer additions.
  • Profitability improvement: EBITDA rose 94% year-on-year to Rs. 22 crores, with margin improving to 13% from 9.4%. PAT rose to Rs. 11 crores from Rs. 2 crores.
  • Diversification progress: Dependence on the traditional ODM home lighting business fell from around 60% at one point to below 20%, with growth across refrigeration, in-store, hearables and wearables, automotive lighting, energy solutions and electronic components.
  • Geographic expansion: Presence across more than 20 countries, with diversification across countries in the Middle East limiting the impact of the war.
  • Manufacturing capacity: Block 1 (~2 lakh sq ft) is operational; Block 2 was partially commercialised in Q2 FY27; Block 3 construction is progressing. Together these support new-age products, exports and backward integration.
  • R&D and product breadth: Over 3,000 SKUs, in-house R&D and product design, and capabilities across design, tooling, component manufacturing, assembly and testing.
  • Hearables and wearables transition: Two to three new customers added in the last three to five months; approximately 50% of products in the category are now ODM products designed by IKIO, improving margins. The segment contributes roughly 15–17% of topline, expected at 16–18% for the year.
  • Automotive lighting: Actual production began around May–June, supplying tier-1 brands for the aftermarket LED segment, with OEM onboarding planned as Phase 2, targeted by FY28.
  • Honeywell relationship: Production started on amplifiers, public address systems and fire alarm panels, with more products in the pipeline and plans to expand to regions beyond India. SKU count is targeted to increase up to 4x before the end of the year.
  • New initiatives: Solar inverters and energy storage (battery, BMS, EMS) are being developed; one order of good volume and value was completed. Gravus and Luminati brands are early-stage B2B initiatives with limited current revenue contribution.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical escalation: Management stated that if the war escalates or more countries become involved, the outlook would change materially.
  • Raw material and component volatility: Continued erratic pricing and extended lead times could further pressure gross margins; the 41% gross margin is considered sustainable only if prices remain at current levels or ease.
  • Customer and geographic concentration: Although dependence on ODM home lighting has fallen sharply, the Middle East decline shows exposure to regional conflict.
  • Execution risk in new verticals: Automotive lighting OEM onboarding, solar/energy storage and new brand initiatives are early-stage and depend on customer approvals and product development timelines.
  • Margin mix: Growth in lower-margin categories may keep blended EBITDA margins below historical peaks.
  • Capacity utilisation timing: Asset turns returning to pre-IPO levels depends on stabilisation over roughly two to two and a half years and on utilisation of new blocks.
  • Depreciation impact: Depreciation from Tower 2 is expected to begin from Q2 FY27 as floors are utilised, affecting reported figures.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

  • Revenue growth guidance: Management reiterated the Q4 guidance of approximately 18–20% revenue growth for the year, citing market volatility and geopolitical issues, and said an update may come around Q2 or Q3 if needed. This compares with 41% year-on-year growth actually delivered in Q1 FY27.
  • EBITDA margin guidance: Group EBITDA margin guidance of 13–14% for the year was maintained, with management noting it would have been around 14% or slightly better under normal conditions. Q1 FY27 actual EBITDA margin was 13%.
  • ODM EBITDA margin: Q1 FY27 ODM EBITDA margin was 8.4%, with management indicating the full year should be close to this figure, allowing for about half a percent variation if raw material prices remain stable or ease.
  • Home lighting ODM full-year outlook: Management indicated the full-year number should not be drastically different from the prior year’s approximately Rs. 170 crores, with a possible uptick of Rs. 10–15 crores. Q1 FY27 revenue of Rs. 45 crores was close to the prior-year quarterly average of about Rs. 42 crores.
  • Other business growth: Management attributed Q1 FY27 growth to a combination of all segments, with the UAE/Middle East the only declining area, and declined to single out one vertical as the most promising.
  • Asset turns: Management maintained that pre-IPO asset turn levels should be reached, contingent on stability over roughly two to two and a half years, and stated that 17–18% EBITDA margin is what seems feasible at peak utilisation rather than the earlier 20–23% range.
  • Capex: FY27 capex is expected at approximately Rs. 20–25 crores, with about Rs. 20 crores pending for Tower 3; Tower 2 capex is largely complete and entering the utilisation phase.
  • Tax rate: Management expects the tax rate to stabilise going forward, without specifying a level.

Additional Corporate Development

  • The 10th Annual General Meeting was held on 30 July 2026 at 3.30 P.M. IST through video conferencing. All six resolutions were passed with requisite majority, covering adoption of standalone and consolidated audited financial statements for FY2025-26, re-appointment of Mr. Hardeep Singh as a rotating director, appointment of M/s Agarwal & Saxena as statutory auditors, approval of commission to non-executive independent directors for FY2025-26, and appointment of Ms. Madhu Pandit as a non-executive women independent director.
  • The total voting capital as on the cut-off date of 23 July 2026 was 7,72,80,701 equity shares of face value Rs. 10 each. The company had 98,135 shareholders on the record date.

Broker Narrative

The narrative began with a Buy on the back of PLI/China+1, broad electronics verticals, and margin/return expansion, while flagging valuation and product-delay risks. By the last report the rating had been cut to Hold and the story had shifted to operational headwinds (slow railway/metro offtake, Avalon’s US losses, RAC seasonality) and narrower domestic tailwinds (AC restocking, BEE norms, washing machines, display modules). Government ‘Make in India’ support and electronics growth persisted as themes, but the early export and margin-expansion optimism was replaced by a more cautious, recovery-led view after four negative return outcomes.

Fears that came true

  • The valuation/result-deviation warning materialized: the stock gave negative actual returns on every call (-52.4%, -45.7%, -37.9%, -30.6%), so a sharp de-rating followed the earlier surge.
  • The product-delay/downtrading risk materialized: the last report still cites slow lifting by Railways/Metro and the seasonal RAC drag, keeping demand/revenue weak.

Optimism that failed

  • The China+1 export opportunity did not materialize for IKIO: the last report’s tailwinds are domestic (AC inventory, BEE norms, washing machines, Make in India) and there is no export ramp-up to offset negative returns.
  • The expected margin/return expansion from operating leverage and backward integration failed to show up, as actual returns were deeply negative and the last report still flags continued losses in the US business for Avalon.
