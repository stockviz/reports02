WeWork India: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

The company is in the early stages of a new capital expenditure cycle, which management acknowledges has moderated sequential financial performance. Q1 FY27 revenue of ₹698.0 Cr was lower than Q4 FY26 revenue of ₹709.9 Cr on an IGAAP-equivalent basis, and EBITDA declined sequentially from ₹164.7 Cr to ₹138.3 Cr. Management attributes this to investments made to build capacity ahead of demand.

Operating expenses per square foot increased by 5.6% year on year, even as rent per square foot remained stable. The company also reported a net loss under Ind AS reporting standards for Q1 FY27, with a loss after tax of ₹4.2 Cr on a reported basis, though this turned to a profit of ₹53.2 Cr on an IGAAP-equivalent basis after adjustments primarily related to non-cash rent straight-lining under Ind AS 116.

Free cash flow to the firm turned negative at ₹(46.1) Cr in Q1 FY27, compared to positive ₹126.0 Cr in Q4 FY26, reflecting higher capital expenditure of ₹188.0 Cr during the quarter. Net debt increased to ₹31.6 Cr from a net cash position of ₹(11.7) Cr in Q4 FY26.

The company carries accumulated losses of ₹20,501.60 million as of March 31, 2026, which the Board has approved to set off against the Securities Premium Account, subject to shareholder and NCLT approval.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

The flexible workspace sector has become the largest occupier segment for three consecutive quarters, accounting for 27% of total office leasing in Q2 CY26. Flex leasing grew 8.4 times from 2.2 MSF in 2017 to 18.6 MSF in 2025, with flex share doubling from 12% in Q1 2025 to 27% in Q2 2026. H1 CY2026 flex leasing reached 11.4 MSF, representing 89% year-on-year growth.

Structural forces supporting demand include record commercial real estate absorption of 24.6 MSF in Q2 2026 (21% YoY growth), domestic firms driving 46% of all leasing, approximately 55% of occupiers using flex in some form (on track to reach 65% by 2027), and premiumisation with 76% of Q2 2026 completions being green-certified.

WeWork India added 6.6k desks in Q1 FY27 and has committed supply for future capacity addition. Locked-in supply shows growth from 128.2k desks in March 2026 to 135.8k desks in June 2026, with 155.3k desks projected by March 2027 and 179.4k desks beyond March 2027. The company plans to add nearly 28,000 desks in FY27.

Sales velocity increased 28.3% year on year from 9.9k to 12.7k desks sold. April was the biggest sales month ever recorded at 7.5k desks. More than half of Q1 sales came from existing members. Contract backlog is outpacing rent commitments, with 4.7x year-on-year growth in incremental contract value compared to rent cost.

The company launched Member Services on July 15, 2026, a business services platform exclusively for members, providing access to a curated marketplace of B2B service partners across functions such as transportation, hiring, gifting, and payments.

The company plans to develop a 10 MWp captive solar power plant in Karnataka, approved by the Board on September 3, 2026. The plant is expected to generate 15-16 million units of clean electricity annually, support 10 centres in Bengaluru, and increase the share of renewable electricity from close to 40% currently to approximately 50%, supporting the goal of 100% renewable electricity by March 2028.

Key Risks

The company operates in a competitive flexible workspace sector where demand visibility depends on enterprise and GCC demand. The business requires significant capital expenditure to build capacity ahead of demand, which moderates near-term financial performance and can result in negative free cash flow to the firm during investment cycles.

The company’s growth plans depend on successfully executing its capacity expansion, including adding nearly 28,000 desks in FY27 and commissioning the solar power plant by Q1 2027. The solar plant is targeted for commissioning in FY27, specifically expected by Q1 2027.

The reduction of share capital involving utilisation of the Securities Premium Account to set off accumulated losses requires approval of Members by Special Resolution and sanction of the National Company Law Tribunal. The alteration of the Objects Clause and reclassification of authorised share capital also require shareholder approvals.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Management stated that FY27 would be a year of growth, with the outlook described as promising. The company began a new capex cycle in Q1 FY27, adding 6.6k desks during the quarter, and plans to add nearly 28,000 desks in FY27. Management indicated that the pipeline of committed supply sitting behind this is deeper than at any point in the company’s history.

Management guided that the solar power plant is targeted for commissioning in FY27, specifically expected by Q1 2027, and will generate 15-16 million units of clean electricity annually. The company aims to transition to 100% renewable electricity by March 2028.

Observed performance shows Q1 FY27 revenue grew 28.5% year on year to ₹698.0 Cr, EBITDA grew 69.3% to ₹138.3 Cr, and PAT surged 533.3% to ₹53.2 Cr. EBITDA margin expanded to 19.8% from 15.0% in Q1 FY26. Free cash flow from operations increased 176.1% year on year to ₹141.9 Cr. ROCE stood at 28.6%. The company maintained its A+ credit rating and cost of borrowing at 8.5%.

Portfolio occupancy stood at 84.9%, with mature centre occupancy at 87.5%. Net Promoter Score remained strong at +78. The company added approximately 7,000 desks during the quarter, taking operational desk capacity to 133.6k desks, up 17.1% year on year, while occupied members grew 29.9% to 113.4k, outpacing capacity growth.

The company held its 10th Annual General Meeting on August 24, 2026, where all six resolutions were approved by members with the requisite majority. The company will participate in the Jefferies 5th India Forum on September 18, 2026, in Gurugram, for group and one-on-one meetings with investors and analysts, with no Unpublished Price Sensitive Information to be shared.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from a confident structural-growth story centered on Indian office leasing momentum, flex-space demand, and Embassy Group backing to a more cautious tone acknowledging office leasing slowdown, weak flex demand, and Q1FY27 QoQ revenue/EBITDA dips. Yet the broker persisted in its Buy call, emphasizing YoY growth, occupancy recovery, and expansion plans, so the positive long-term thesis stayed intact.

Fears that came true

  • The flagged risk of an office leasing slowdown materialized, and the stock returned only +2.7% versus the predicted +38.2%, a clear disappointment.
  • The flagged dependence on member retention materialized as mature portfolio occupancy dipped 100bps QoQ to 88%, contributing to muted actual returns.
  • The flagged risk of weak flexible workspace demand materialized, with Q1FY27 revenue and EBITDA declining 2-3% QoQ due to lower VAS customisation revenue.

Optimism that failed

  • The predicted Indian office leasing momentum and GCC-led absorption growth did not sustain, as the last report cited an office leasing slowdown and weak flex demand.
  • The forecast ~18-20% CAGR growth in pan-India flex stock did not translate into robust near-term results, with the company reporting QoQ declines instead of continued acceleration.
