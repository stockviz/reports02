Aequs Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements: Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Profitability pressure despite revenue growth. For Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026), revenue from operations grew 55% YoY to ₹3,955 million, but EBITDA fell 46% YoY to ₹215 million (5% margin versus 16% in Q1 FY26), and PAT swung to a loss of ₹532 million from a profit of ₹39 million in Q1 FY26. The company attributes the YoY EBITDA decline to Consumer Electronics operating costs being expensed in Q1 FY27, compared with their capitalisation in Q1 FY26.

Consumer segment losses and low utilisation. The Consumer segment reported an EBITDA loss of ₹361 million in Q1 FY27, narrowed by approximately 24% sequentially from ₹473 million in Q4 FY26. Consumer capacity utilisation stood at just 22%, and the segment remained impacted by low utilisation.

Sequential EBITDA decline. Reported EBITDA fell 33% QoQ from ₹321 million in Q4 FY26 to ₹215 million in Q1 FY27. Aerospace EBITDA declined sequentially from ₹1,010 million in Q4 FY26 to ₹731 million in Q1 FY27, which the company attributes to an elevated Q4 base that included a higher contribution from other income.

Higher depreciation and finance costs. Depreciation and amortisation rose to ₹453 million in Q1 FY27 from ₹246 million in Q1 FY26, and finance costs rose to ₹189 million from ₹101 million, reflecting the expanded Consumer Electronics asset base.

Negative operating cash flow and working capital build. Cash flow from operations was negative ₹414 million in Q1 FY27 versus positive ₹274 million in Q1 FY26, with a working capital outflow of ₹653 million. Closing cash declined to ₹2,340 million from ₹3,015 million at the start of the period.

Balance sheet gearing. Total borrowings increased, with non-current borrowings rising from ₹1,002 million at Q4 FY26 to ₹1,818 million at Q1 FY27, alongside current borrowings of ₹3,040 million.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Aerospace momentum and record order book. Aerospace revenue grew 40% YoY and 6% QoQ to ₹3,222 million, supported by higher customer build rates and additional parts progressing into production. The Aerospace order book crossed USD 1 billion, rising 13% sequentially from USD 889 million to USD 1,004 million.

Consumer scale-up. Consumer revenue nearly tripled YoY, growing 190% YoY and 16% QoQ to ₹734 million, with the segment’s contribution to consolidated revenue rising to 19% from 10% in Q1 FY26.

Improving underlying operating performance. Operational EBITDA (excluding other income) rose from ₹42 million in Q4 FY26 to ₹148 million in Q1 FY27 — an approximately 3.5x sequential improvement — supported by narrowing Consumer losses and improving cost absorption.

New customer and programme wins. During the quarter, Aequs signed long-term agreements with two new Aerostructures Tier-1 customers and its first contract for fully assembled Airbus A320 wheels with Safran Landing Systems, with 100% in-country manufacturing value addition on the flight-critical product.

Capacity headroom. Capacity utilisation stood at 70% in Aerospace (78% for Aerospace in India) and 22% in Consumer, indicating room to scale without commensurate new capacity.

Growth investment. The company incurred capital expenditure of ₹830 million during the quarter to support future growth.

Long-standing customer relationships. The company cites a 15-year average relationship with its top three customers, six Airbus Detailed Parts Awards (D2P) between 2016 and 2023, and a portfolio of 5,740 qualified aerospace parts.

3. Key Risks

Execution risk on the path to breakeven. The company’s stated milestones — Consumer EBITDA breakeven by Q4 FY27 and consolidated PAT breakeven by H1 FY28 — depend on continued volume ramp-up and cost absorption in the Consumer segment, which currently operates at 22% utilisation and remains loss-making.

Consumer segment concentration of losses. The Consumer segment’s EBITDA loss of ₹361 million in Q1 FY27 continues to weigh on consolidated profitability, and the segment’s performance is tied to production volumes across the portfolio.

Cash flow and liquidity pressure. Negative operating cash flow, a working capital outflow of ₹653 million, and a ₹702 million net cash decline in Q1 FY27, alongside increased borrowings, indicate dependence on financing to fund capex and operations.

Depreciation drag from expanded asset base. Higher depreciation on the expanded Consumer Electronics asset base contributed to the PAT loss, and this burden persists as volumes scale.

Forward-looking statement risk. The company’s own safe harbour disclosures note that its statements are subject to known and unknown risks and uncertainties beyond its control — including industry dynamics, economic conditions, regulatory requirements, competition, execution ability, technological changes, cyber-attacks, pandemics, natural disasters, litigation, and labour strikes — that could cause actual results to differ materially.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Guidance provided. Management stated that FY27 is “the year we committed to translating expanded capacity into financial returns,” with milestones of Consumer EBITDA breakeven by Q4 FY27 and consolidated PAT breakeven by H1 FY28. The company also referenced a Vision 2031 roadmap of 4–6x revenue growth, 18–22% EBITDA margin, and 20% steady-state RoCE.

Observed performance against guidance. Q1 FY27 revenue growth of 55% YoY and the 3.5x sequential improvement in operational EBITDA to ₹148 million are presented by management as “the first proof point on that path.” The Consumer segment EBITDA loss narrowed 24% sequentially, consistent with the stated trajectory toward breakeven, though the segment remains loss-making. Consolidated PAT remained negative at ₹532 million, improving sequentially from an adjusted Q4 FY26 loss of ₹631 million (excluding a ₹90 million exceptional gain). Reported EBITDA margin of 5% in Q1 FY27 remains well below the 18–22% Vision 2031 target, and the company has not reached the stated breakeven milestones as of the quarter reported.
