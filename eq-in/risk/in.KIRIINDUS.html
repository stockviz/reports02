KIRIINDUS: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost pressure in the existing chemicals business. The company’s major raw materials are linked to crude oil prices, which increased during Q1 FY27, resulting in higher input and operating costs. Operating expenses also rose on higher fuel, freight and logistics costs amid elevated crude oil prices and transportation expenses. Management noted that sustaining margins will remain dependent on the ability to pass through input cost movements.

Mixed demand and partial capacity utilisation. Demand trends remain mixed across product categories. Average capacity utilisation in the dyes/chemicals business was about 60% in the last quarter, leaving roughly 40% unutilised across plants combined. Management described a cautious approach of ramping up profitably rather than chasing volume.

Project execution and vendor timelines. The integrated copper and fertilizer project has moved from design into a structured construction phase, but management acknowledged that installation and delivery of machinery depends on vendor timelines, which keep moving. The previously guided INR 20,000–25,000 crore FY28 revenue figure was stated not to hold, with projections described as dynamically moving.

Financial closure not yet complete. Complete financial closure for the copper project has not been achieved; more than 50% of debt commitments have been received, with the rest in process. Management hoped to reach complete financial closure in the next few months.

Litigation and shareholder dispute at the MCB Copper-Gold project. Development of the mine has not started; design is complete. There is a shareholder disagreement, with Celsius objecting to Kiri’s off-take, and the matter is before the court. Kiri continues to remain a lender to the project and stated it would not speculate while the matter is sub judice. Kiri’s position is that it has structured 70% of the off-take to come to it, but access remains unresolved pending judicial clarity.

No dividend. Management confirmed no dividend has been declared and there is no Board decision to declare one yet, citing the capital requirements of the new growth trajectory. A shareholder raised concerns about returns after a long investment period; management said the point would be taken positively and noted equal numbers of shareholders have expressed alignment with retaining capital for growth.

Tax leakage concern raised by an investor. A participant questioned whether passing margins to the JV (Lonsen Kiri) results in effective double taxation on distributions. Management responded that transactions are arm’s length, profits are taxed where generated, and each level pays applicable tax.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Improved pricing environment in dyes and intermediates. Pricing across Reactive Dyes, Vinyl Sulphone, H-Acid and certain basic chemicals strengthened during the quarter, supported by tighter global supply, higher efficiency, and continuing impact of environmental compliance measures on manufacturing in China. H-Acid prices were cited in the range of INR 800–900 versus INR 350–400 earlier; Vinyl Sulphone prices were described as almost 70–80% higher, though raw materials have also increased substantially.

Margin expansion. Standalone material margin improved to 31.9% in Q1 FY27, compared with 23.5% in Q1 FY26 and 20.4% in Q4 FY26, driven by a favourable pricing differential as average selling prices rose faster than average raw material prices.

Revenue growth. Standalone revenue from operations for Q1 FY27 was INR 295 crore, up 63% year-on-year, primarily realisation-led. Consolidated revenue from operations was INR 312 crore, up 55% year-on-year, again led principally by improved price realisations rather than volume.

Profitability and balance sheet strength. Standalone EBITDA was INR 17 crore (5.86% margin); consolidated EBITDA including share of profit of associates and JVs was INR 37 crore (7.84% margin). Consolidated PAT was INR 270 crore, primarily driven by other income of INR 286 crore (interest on inter-corporate loans and realised/unrealised treasury gains). Finance costs reduced sharply following repayment of borrowings at Claronex Holdings Pte. Ltd., leaving the group substantially free of external debt. The company received proceeds of USD 689 million from resolution of the DyStar matter, strengthening the balance sheet. Share of profit from associates was INR 21 crore, up 30% over the preceding quarter, primarily from Lonsen Kiri (40% equity interest, management control).

Headroom in H-Acid capacity. As one of the largest global producers of H-acid, management believes existing capacity provides headroom to participate in rising demand without significant additional capital expenditure. If prices sustain and utilisation reaches 75–80%, management indicated revenue could potentially double from around INR 1,200 crore toward close to INR 2,000 crore.

Integrated copper and fertilizer project. The project progressed from design into structured construction, with orders placed for several long-lead mechanical, electrical and utility packages. Enabling infrastructure includes a captive jetty, desalination facility, dedicated raw material conveying system, and long-term power infrastructure. Downstream copper facilities are planned to be commissioned in phases: copper tube targeted by Q1 FY28, copper rod plant FY28, and copper refinery in Q3 FY29. Total project cost for the copper complex is approximately INR 8,100 crore; the fertilizer project is estimated at INR 3,600 crore; capital expenditure for the renewable power project and jetty is approximately INR 1,600 crore. Total capital requirement for the project itself was cited at close to INR 12,000 crore including supporting infrastructure, with outflow spread over the next two years. Total capital deployed in the project to date is INR 1,400 crore, all equity.

Phased commissioning detail (per CEO Indo Asia Copper). A 35 KT tube plant targeted operational June 2027; a 2.25 lakh tonne CCR (8 mm continuous rod) plant targeted August–September 2027, operating on imported Japanese cathodes; stabilisation expected by January 2028. A part refinery (1.75 lakh tonnes) converting anodes to LME-grade cathode, plus a scrap melting furnace, targeted December 2027–January 2028. Copper foil trial production of 5,000 KT (system built for 10 KT Phase 1, ambition of 40 KT across phases, with application to the central government under the ECMS scheme) targeted around March 2028. The balance of plant — smelter, sulphuric acid plant and fertilizers — targeted first quarter of 2029. Management described this schedule as around 5–6 months faster than typical 36–40 month builds.

Raw material sourcing. MoUs covering approximately 1 million tonnes of copper concentrate are in place, against a requirement of around 1.5 million tonnes for 100% production. Contracts are typically firmed 3–4 months prior to requirement, with bonded contract costing expected around October 2028. Management expects visibility to rise to 1.2–1.4 million tonnes at the coming October LME Week, citing physical progress at site (about 1,000 people working on civil and mechanical works).

Import substitution demand. India consumed 1.8 million tonnes of copper in the last year, with almost two-thirds met by imports. Management views the entire output as import replacement, with sales not seen as a constraint provided production and raw material security are achieved. The fertilizer opportunity is supported by India’s continued dependence on imports, particularly DAP and key intermediates such as phosphoric acid and ammonia. Diversification spans copper smelting, refining and downstream products (tubes, foil, rods) plus fertilizer production utilising sulfur dioxide gases from the smelter. Structural domestic deficit in copper and growth in AI, data centers and electronics were cited as supporting demand.

MCB Copper-Gold project. Design completed; many financing offers received; management hopes for financial closure and start of development before the end of the year. Kiri is a passive player, not actively involved in management or operations, and has structured 70% of off-take to itself.

Odisha foray. Preliminary-stage discussions on a large specialty chemicals complex near Dhamra Port, with government support indicated on incentives. Management stated execution will be sequential, not parallel, and the project is currently far off.

Financing flexibility. Management confirmed equity issuance remains a valid option, having tested investor interest (including an equity infusion into Indo Asia of around INR 230 crore, with warrants issued at INR 278). The stated trade-off is that deferring dilution until after project execution could create higher value for existing shareholders; equity funding remains a Plan B.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material and crude-linked cost volatility in the existing chemicals business, with margin sustainability dependent on pass-through.
  • Execution and vendor timeline risk on the copper/fertilizer project; revenue projections described as dynamically moving and dependent on machinery delivery and installation.
  • Financial closure risk: complete debt closure not yet achieved; more than 50% commitments received, remainder in process.
  • Litigation risk at MCB Copper-Gold: shareholder dispute with Celsius, court proceedings, and objection to Kiri’s 70% off-take; Kiri’s lender position and off-take access unresolved pending judicial outcome.
  • Demand mix risk across product categories in dyes and intermediates.
  • Capacity underutilisation risk: ~40% unutilised capacity; ramp-up contingent on demand support and margin discipline.
  • Tax structuring risk raised by an investor regarding margin allocation to the JV and potential effective double taxation on distributions.
  • Dilution versus leverage trade-off: the decision to fund with debt rather than equity defers dilution but introduces future debt servicing obligations; debt repayment is scheduled to begin around 2029, with no drawdown yet and a moratorium in place.
  • Dependence on imported cathodes for initial downstream operations (primarily Japanese LME-grade cathodes) until the refinery and scrap furnace are commissioned.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance revised downward/withdrawn. The previously guided INR 20,000–25,000 crore FY28 revenue was explicitly stated not to hold, with management citing dynamically moving numbers and vendor-dependent timelines. Similarly, an earlier guidance of INR 1,000–1,200 crore EBITDA for FY27–28 was questioned by an investor against the economics of rod and tube plants; management responded that projections change with commissioning timelines and pointed to LME-linked downstream pricing structures.

Commissioning schedule reaffirmed. Management reaffirmed a phased schedule: copper tube Q1 FY28, copper rod Q2 FY28, copper refinery Q3 FY29. First revenue from the copper business was indicated around end of Q1 FY28 (May–June 2027), with commercial sales from Q2 FY28. Full operational revenue capture is targeted for FY2029–30. The project is projected to deliver an IRR of ~25% and an expected ROE of ~22%–30% based on conservative assumptions.

Observed performance versus guidance context. Q1 FY27 delivered what management described as one of the strongest quarters in the company’s history, with standalone revenue up 63% year-on-year and material margin at 31.9%. However, the PAT of INR 270 crore was primarily driven by other income of INR 286 crore (treasury and inter-corporate loan interest), not by operating performance; consolidated EBITDA including JV share was INR 37 crore. Management linked the improved operating environment to the resolution of an 11-year legal/litigation period and the start of a growth phase.

Capacity utilisation guidance. Management targets average capacity utilisation of 60% to 70–75% during the current year, contingent on market support, with a stated preference for profitable ramp-up over volume-led utilisation.

Debt repayment guidance. No drawdown has been made on project debt; considering the moratorium, debt repayment is guided to start around 2029, not before.

Financial closure guidance. Management hopes to reach complete financial closure in the next few months, with more than 50% of commitments already received.

MCB guidance. Management hopes for project financial closure and start of development before the end of the year, subject to resolution of the shareholder dispute.

AGM and fundraising proposals. The 28th AGM is convened for Tuesday, September 29, 2026, via video conferencing. Key proposals include raising up to INR 288,92,35,000 via preferential issue of 60,82,600 warrants at INR 475 per warrant to promoters and promoter group (Manishkumar Kiri, Anupama Manishkumar Kiri, Hemil Manishkumar Kiri), each convertible into one equity share of face value INR 10, constituting up to 4.98% of post-issue paid-up equity capital; increasing borrowing limits up to INR 10,000 crore under Section 180(1)(c); increasing limits for loans, guarantees and security up to INR 15,000 crore under Section 185 and up to INR 25,000 crore under Section 186; and approving material related party transactions with Indo Asia Copper Limited, Kiri Infrastructure Private Limited, Indoasia Agrotech Fertilizers Limited, and Lonsen Kiri Chemical Industries Limited. Management stated the proposed investments and financial support will help IACL meet initial capital expenditure for the copper manufacturing facility, strengthen business operations, and enable diversification into new lines of business while supporting long-term growth and value creation.
