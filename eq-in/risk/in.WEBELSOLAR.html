Websol Energy System — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Mix-led margin compression. EBITDA margin fell to 34% in Q1 FY27 from 47% in Q1 FY26, even as absolute EBITDA rose 21% to ₹126 crore. Management attributes this directly to sales mix: substantially more modules were sold, and module margins are lower than cell margins. Sequentially, revenue declined about 7% and EBITDA about 14% versus Q4 FY26. Management cited three causes: the module-heavy mix, softer cell realisations during the quarter, and higher BOM cost, particularly silver.

Soft realisations and input-cost pressure. Q1 cell realisations were around USD 0.00125 per watt peak and modules around ₹20.50 per watt. Management noted prices were soft in the quarter, with some increase later on the back of the ALMM mandate (subsequently deferred to December). On silver, imported silver paste carries a lag between market prices and absorbed cost; management expects that lag to produce a cost reduction in the current quarter. Wafer prices were broadly stable. The conflict in West Asia was cited as raising BOM costs for solar modules, though not for cells.

Inventory build. Inventory rose roughly 7% versus Q4 FY26. Management characterised this as cyclical — monsoon slows installations and offtake while production continues, and because the company consumes its own cells in module production, cell cost remains blocked in unsold modules. Management stated it does not foresee an alarming inventory position as of 30 June 2026.

Policy timing uncertainty. The ALMM cell mandate was postponed from June to December, which management read as an indication that domestic cell capacity remains well below module capacity. How the deadline moves further depends on how much capacity comes on the ground by then.

Location change for Phase 3. The planned expansion has moved from Andhra Pradesh (Naidupeta) to West Bengal. Management said there was no financial outflow on the Andhra Pradesh land and that the change was driven by expected synergies in supply chain, manpower and execution speed near the existing Falta facility. Land has been shortlisted and approvals are awaited. Investors on the call questioned the timeliness of disclosure and the six to eight months spent on the Andhra Pradesh plan; management acknowledged the point on investor relations and said it would increase engagement.

Valuation and institutional interest. An investor noted the stock trades at a discount to peers and that institutional interest is limited. Management said operations are within its control, that it has increased investor interactions, and that it is not aware of any information requiring disclosure to exchanges that it has withheld.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Operating ramp-up. Cell production rose to 259 MW in Q1 FY27 from 126 MW a year earlier, with cell utilisation at 92%. Module production more than doubled to 103 MW from 50 MW, with module utilisation at 81% versus 39%. Management expects to operate close to full run-rate utilisation across lines, aside from downtime related to the TOPCon upgrade.

Order book visibility. The confirmed order book stood at ₹1,278 crore as on 30 June 2026, against ₹1,161 crore at end-March 2026. Management said this provides healthy visibility into coming quarters and that the company discloses only firm purchase orders, with much routine business coming from repeat customers without fixed-term contracts.

TOPCon upgrade. One existing Mono PERC cell line is being upgraded to TOPCon, taking that line to 750 MW and total cell capacity from 1,200 MW to 1,350 MW, with roughly 55% of cell capacity on TOPCon. Completion is expected by March 2027, at an estimated capex of about ₹270 crore, fundable through internal accruals with possible debt evaluation to optimise liquidity. Expected cell efficiency is around 25%. Management cited three sources of incremental revenue: higher watt-peak per cell (from 7.6–7.7 in Mono PERC to above 9.5 expected in TOPCon), higher output from the larger line, and higher realisation per watt peak for TOPCon. Payback was indicated at two to three years.

Phase 3 expansion. The plan remains a 4 GW cell and 4 GW module expansion in two phases of 2 GW each, with no change to capex plans, project cost or funding strategy. Machinery lead time is four to six months, with no advance capital blocking required yet; equipment has been finalised. Construction is expected to start mid-September, with equipment ordered around December, delivered around April–May, two months of trials, and commercial production targeted around July. Management said the TOPCon upgrade serves as practical learning ahead of the greenfield project.

Demand environment. Management said its products largely serve DCR projects (PM-Surya Ghar and PM-KUSUM) and it sees no demand decline on that front. It expects solar demand to rise, citing AI, BESS and night-time solar requirements. The supplied material also cites India’s 500 GW non-fossil target by 2030, a structural shortfall in domestic cell capacity under ALMM List-II favouring integrated and TOPCon-ready producers, falling storage costs enabling solar-plus-storage, PLI support, and export opportunities from supply-chain diversification.

West Bengal industrial direction. Management cited recent state direction toward industrialisation, including a proposed new industrial policy, simpler access to industrial land and a stronger focus on attracting manufacturing investment, as encouraging for an existing operator in the state.

3. Key Risks

  • Supply-chain dependence: reliance on imported polysilicon and wafers, with silver paste imported from China and other countries.
  • Global oversupply and aggressive overseas pricing in cells and modules.
  • Rapid technological change shortening product life cycles; the phased 2 GW-at-a-time approach is explicitly framed as managing technology risk.
  • Policy implementation timing, notably the ALMM cell mandate, whose deferral to December reflects domestic cell capacity lagging module capacity.
  • Currency movements and input-cost inflation, including silver and module BOM costs affected by the West Asia conflict.
  • Execution and approval risk on Phase 3: land approval is pending, and timelines depend on receipt of approvals; management said it does not anticipate delays but acknowledged interim delay risk from the location change.
  • Growth management, competition, talent retention, contract overruns, government policy, fiscal deficits, regulations and prevailing economic costs, as set out in the forward-looking statement risk list.
  • Investor relations and disclosure timing, raised repeatedly by participants, including the concern that poor valuation could complicate future equity fundraising.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Margin guidance. At the end of FY26, management had said it did not see margin pressure for the next one to two years. In Q1 FY27, EBITDA margin fell to 34% from 47% year-on-year and declined sequentially. Management explained the fall as mix-driven and, when asked directly, said the current margin level should be sustainable for a year or two, while noting some expected fall in cell margins that would not be aggressive. On the sequential decline, management also cited softer cell prices and higher BOM cost, particularly silver.

Utilisation guidance. Management indicated 92% cell utilisation is a level it is comfortable holding, with only marginal gains available from process optimisation at current average cell efficiency of 23.3%, and that the next step-change in cell output comes with the TOPCon upgrade. For modules, it noted industry-average comfort at 70–75% and that Q1’s 81% is already close to full effective utilisation.

Debt and pledge guidance. Management had said it would strengthen the balance sheet. On 4 August 2026, the company prepaid the entire ₹110 crore outstanding IREDA term loan from internal accruals, without raising fresh capital and without slowing growth investments. Collateral, including promoter shares pledged against the facility, is being released, with the promoter pledge reducing from 80% to 16% of promoter holding. Management said paperwork is being pursued aggressively but release timing also depends on the lender.

Capacity and timeline guidance. The TOPCon upgrade is guided for completion by March 2027, consistent with the press release. For Phase 3, management stated no change to previously announced timelines, capex plans, project cost or funding strategy despite the shift to West Bengal, and expressed confidence in executing the 4 GW capacity in phases. The longer-range roadmap in the supplied material points to approximately 5.35 GW of cells and 4.55 GW of modules by FY29, including the TOPCon upgrade to 1.35 GW by early 2027, a greenfield 2,000 MW TOPCon facility targeted for 2027, and subsequent 2 GW lines.

Order book and mix. The order book rose to ₹1,278 crore from ₹1,161 crore at end-March 2026. Mix shifted from 60% module / 40% cell in Q4 FY26 to 52% module / 48% cell in Q1 FY27. Management declined to predict the FY27 mix, citing realisation uncertainty, but said it is confident of selling everything it produces. Of 259 MW of cell production, roughly 153 MW was sold to external customers.

Governance and board changes. On 10 August 2026, the Board approved the Q1 FY27 results, appointed Mr. Sanjay Kumar as an Additional Non-Executive Non-Independent Director and Mr. Dinesh Agarwal as an Additional Non-Executive Independent Director for five years (subject to shareholder approval), noted Mr. Rajeewa R Arya’s unwillingness to be reappointed at the ensuing AGM, and appointed Mr. Ashok Purohit as Company Secretary and Compliance Officer with effect from 10 August 2026. Board committee compositions were also reconstituted on 10 August 2026, with Mr. Dinesh Agarwal chairing the Audit Committee, Mr. Vishal Patodia chairing the Nomination and Remuneration Committee and the Stakeholder Relationship Committee, and Ms. Sanjana Khaitan chairing the CSR & ESG Committee and the Risk Management Committee. The CRISIL rating of BBB+ stable was stated to remain unchanged.
