Medi Assist Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Retention drag from the Paramount acquisition. Group retention stood at 90.2% in Q1 FY27, below the historical 93–94% range. Management attributed this to the post-acquisition transition of a large book, portfolio rationalisation, and a higher base effect from the acquired company’s older losses. Management expects this near-term PHS retention drag to normalise through FY27.

Margin dilution from integration. EBITDA margin was 20.3% in Q1 FY27 versus 22.0% in Q1 FY26, a decline of 175 bps. Management linked this to the Paramount integration and stated the immediate priority is to complete remaining integration activities and return toward the 23% margin level historically achieved around the IPO, targeting that trajectory toward the end of FY27.

International business decline. International revenue was ₹10.1 Cr, down 5.2% YoY. Management described this as a temporary impact from industry-wide moderation in student, leisure, and marine volumes occurring in the same quarter, and expects growth to improve as other projects kick in.

Retail TPA-model moderation. TPA-model retail revenue grew 13.1% YoY to ₹23.4 Cr, which management framed as part of a structural shift of retail toward a hybrid model rather than a disappearance of the traditional model. Reporting only captures contracts where Medi Assist is formally named on the policy, while platform-administered retail premiums reached ₹4,254 Cr.

Industry-level softness in PSU group health. Management noted the PSU group health business for the industry was down 1.5% YoY, while overall group health industry growth was about 14%. Medi Assist’s PSU PUM growth was 28.9%, which management read as share-of-wallet improvement.

Same-store growth moderation. Same-store growth of retained customers was about 7–8%, down from the ~20% highs just after COVID, with large IT companies growing slower and the rest faster.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Core India TPA franchise. Group revenue grew 25.5% YoY to ₹166.0 Cr, with group PUM at ₹8,454 Cr (+29.5% YoY) and group market share at 37.6% (+440 bps YoY). India Health PUM (Group + Retail) was ₹8,975 Cr (+26.8% YoY).

Government business. Revenue grew 35.3% YoY to ₹28.5 Cr, covering over 31 crore members across 12 states and 4 union territories. Management described government as margin accretive with the safest collections, coming from state and central governments, and noted DSOs are consolidated across the business including government.

Technology platform monetisation. Technology revenue grew 55.5% YoY to ₹7.8 Cr, now 3.3% of consolidated revenue. The ~₹24.5 Cr AI platform investment over six quarters is complete. Seven insurers are contracted across combinations of MAven, MAtrix, and MAgnum stacks, including AI components. The first outcomes-based contract was signed, with compensation tied to fraud, waste, and abuse outcomes. MAven Guard delivered over ₹183 Cr of fraud savings in Q1. Management stated technology contracts could deliver margins roughly double usual margins and that the technology business is already margin accretive.

Operational scale and speed. Over 186,000 pre-authorisations were processed within five minutes in Q1. Raksha Prime delivered zero-wait discharge for over 87,000 members across 6,000+ hospitals. MAtrix implementation at Star Health crossed 90%.

NPS Swasthya. Medi Assist acts as Health Benefits Administrator for the scheme, which allows NPS subscribers to draw down on a portion of their corpus for health expenses in real time and integrate with top-up covers. Management hopes the scheme extends to a substantial part of NPS membership.

International platform. Ownership in Mayfair We Care was increased to 91.75% post Q1, converting the seed investment into a majority-owned subsidiary. Nikhil Chopra, former Chief Business Officer, now leads the international business full-time. The first technology deployment contract went live in Thailand from 1 July 2026, with multiple corporates onboarded. Indian retail sign-ups with Mayfair provide visibility to over 50% of travel premiums placed in India.

Balance sheet strength. Debt-free with free cash of ₹245.5 Cr, net worth of ₹884.1 Cr, and contract liability of ₹337.4 Cr. Both technology and international tracks are funded from operating cash flows.

3. Key Risks

Client concentration. The top five clients contributed 64.8% of revenue.

Technology and cybersecurity. Complex IT systems and integration of acquired systems carry risk. A cyber-security incident at Paramount TPA in the prior year impacted certain systems and services; it was contained at the Paramount TPA level and did not affect the Company or other subsidiaries. Costs of ₹37.68 million up to 31 March 2026 were presented as an exceptional item, and an insurance claim was lodged under Paramount TPA’s cyber-insurance policy.

Regulatory and enforcement matters. A search and seizure operation was carried out by the Directorate of Enforcement at certain offices of MAITPA in Ranchi, Jharkhand, related to the Ayushman Bharat-linked health scheme in Jharkhand. MAITPA has cooperated and responded to clarifications; as of the date of the financial results, no communication on the outcome had been received, and management assessed no adverse impact.

Operational scale risk. Managing a large healthcare provider network and processing claims efficiently at scale.

Talent management. Attracting and retaining skilled professionals.

Integration and structural risk. Integration risks related to acquisitions such as Paramount TPA, including the pending Scheme of Merger by Absorption of Paramount TPA with the Company, for which IRDAI has sought clarifications and a joint response has been submitted. The merger of IHMS with MCSI is pending before the NCLT.

Macro and pricing. Rising medical inflation, macroeconomic volatility, potential regulatory changes, and pricing pressures.

Competitive landscape. Management addressed the trend of PSU insurers creating in-house TPA entities, stating every insurer has some work in-house and some with TPAs, and that Medi Assist’s focus is on delivering better than others.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Paramount integration. Management guided that full migration of balance claims and operations to the Medi Assist stack would occur within Q2 FY27. Observed: at the end of Q1, over 95% of group claims and over 80% of retail claims had migrated to MAtrix. Management also guided that the near-term PHS retention drag would fully normalise through FY27. Observed: retention was 90.2% in Q1, below the historical 93–94%.

Margin trajectory. Management guided that the immediate priority is to finish remaining Paramount integration activities and get back toward the 23% margin level through FY27, toward the end of FY27. Observed: operating EBITDA margin was 20.3% in Q1 FY27, versus 22.0% in Q1 FY26, with a 330 bps improvement over the last four quarters from the lowest point in Q2 FY26. Management stated a very insignificant part of deployment remains and expects completion in about one quarter.

Technology and international as EBITDA contributors. Management guided that both growth tracks are funded from operating cash flows and are expected to become meaningful contributors to growth and EBITDA margins over time. Observed: technology revenue grew 55.5% YoY to 3.3% of consolidated revenue and is described as already margin accretive; international revenue declined 5.2% YoY in Q1.

Core business growth. Management guided that the core business would grow at par with or faster than the market in group and retail segments. Observed: group revenue grew 25.5% YoY versus industry group health growth of about 14%; group market share rose 440 bps YoY to 37.6%.

Same-store growth. Management referenced same-store growth of about 7–8% blended, similar to the end of the prior year, with IT companies slower and others faster.

Receivables and cash flow. Management guided focus on reducing receivables by increasing collections faster than historical trends. Observed: DSOs have been consistently reduced on a quarterly basis, and the company remains debt-free with a free cash position of ₹245.5 Cr.

Governance transition. Dr. Vikram Chhatwal transitions from Executive Chairman to Non-Executive, Non-Independent Director and Chairman, effective August 8, 2026, subject to shareholder approval at the AGM on September 8, 2026. Management stated nothing about who runs Medi Assist changes on that date, and the strategy and domestic and international growth agenda are unchanged. Gaurav Bhatnagar joined as Chief TPA Officer and was designated as SMP effective August 8, 2026.
