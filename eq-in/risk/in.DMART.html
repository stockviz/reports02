DMart (Avenue Supermarts Limited) — Recent Corporate Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Metro store saturation and competition. Management stated that older, dense metro stores in cities such as Mumbai and Bangalore are reaching saturation, with high throughputs and footfalls. Same-store growth in these stores was flat in Q1FY27. Management attributed this to a combination of capacity constraints, store maturity, new stores opening nearby (which cannibalises same-store growth), and competition. Quick commerce was cited as a factor, with some customers preferring faster delivery for lower basket sizes; management expects this impact to persist over the next few years.

Same-store sales growth moderation. Two-year-plus stores grew 5.5% in Q1FY27 versus 7.1% in Q1FY26. For FY26, like-for-like growth for stores older than two years was 8.1%. Management indicated SSSG should hover around the FY26 level and is not expected to rise considerably, absent inflation.

E-commerce losses. Avenue E-commerce (DMart Ready) sales grew 16.9% in FY26, but EBITDA declined 43.5% and PBT fell 23.9% to a net loss of Rs. 307 crore. Contract labour cost rose 24% and transport cost rose 34%. Management stated losses had grown as the business expanded into more cities, prompting a decision to consolidate.

Employee cost inflation. Standalone employee cost rose 33.5% in FY26 (27 bps as a percentage of total income), attributed to capability build-out ahead of scale and the wage code implemented from December 2025. Management said it is difficult to say whether this build-out is complete.

Margin compression. Standalone PAT margin fell 25 bps to 4.81% in FY26; PBT margin fell 26 bps to 6.44%. Return on net worth was approximately 13.5% and ROCE approximately 17.1%, both slightly lower than the prior year. Finance costs rose 125% standalone.

Inventory days. Inventory days rose to 33.2 in FY26, attributed to more warehouses opened during the year.

Regulatory penalty. A penalty of Rs. 28.28 lakhs was imposed by the Greater Bengaluru Authority on August 25, 2026, for non-compliance related to construction activities at a project site, with no material impact on operations.

Real estate constraints. Land acquisition and store construction take two to three years, and securing clean land parcels meeting requirements remains a challenge. Management noted NCR as a market where owned property is difficult to obtain.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Store expansion. DMart crossed the 500-store milestone in FY26, adding 85 stores (including entry into five new states). As of June 30, 2026, the company operated 503 stores with 20.7 million sq. ft. of retail business area. Management’s internal benchmark is to add approximately 15% of the store base annually, though actual additions may be a few percentage points higher or lower depending on real estate readiness. In FY26, additions exceeded internal expectations because some properties were ready faster than anticipated.

Leasing flexibility. Of 85 stores added in FY26, 15 were leased; total leased stores stand at 68 out of 500-plus. Management is open to long-term leasing where owned land is unavailable, which may increase the leased proportion over time.

Cluster-based expansion. The company plans to build store density in the five new states entered last year while continuing to expand in established markets.

Tier 1 and Tier 2 performance. Stores in non-metros continue to grow well, with throughput and ramp-up in line with expectations. Management stated SSSG in these stores is better than the company average.

DMart Ready focus. The company is concentrating on 11 key cities where the majority of online business originates, targeting tailored assortment, delivery within six hours, and improved user experience. Operations were discontinued in seven cities that were marginal contributors. As of June 30, 2026, DMart Ready operates in 11 cities with 165 pickup points.

Subsidiary performance. Align Retail Trades grew sales 16.5% with PAT up 24%. Avenue Food Plaza grew sales 35.5% and returned to profit with PAT of Rs. 9 crore, versus a loss of Rs. 10 crore in the prior year.

Fresh category pilots. Pilots with fresh fruits in some Mumbai stores are showing strong results, though no full-fledged fresh portfolio is planned at present.

Organised retail runway. Management repeatedly noted that organised trade (including brick-and-mortar, e-commerce, and quick commerce) remains in the early teens as a share of total retail, leaving significant headroom for all formats.

Vendor relationships. Management stated FMCG vendors are increasingly focused on DMart, with the company being the number one retailer for some vendors as an individual entity.

3. Key Risks

  • Quick commerce competition, particularly in dense metros, with management expecting the impact on lower-basket convenience purchases to persist.
  • Same-store growth remaining at current levels, with older stores unlikely to return to double-digit SSSG absent inflation.
  • Continued e-commerce losses if the 11-city model does not demonstrate sustainable profitability.
  • Employee cost escalation from capability build-out and wage code implementation, with no certainty that the build-out is complete.
  • Real estate execution risk, including land availability, regulatory approvals, and construction timelines.
  • Margin pressure from inflation, wage costs, and competitive pricing, with management targeting a gross margin range of 14%–15% and net margin around 5%.
  • Regulatory and compliance risk, as illustrated by the Greater Bengaluru Authority penalty.
  • Debt increase, with total debt at Rs. 2,267 crore (including AS 116 adjustment) and net borrowing at approximately Rs. 965 crore as of March 2026; the board approved issuance of NCDs up to Rs. 1,000 crore on July 11, 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Store additions. Management’s internal benchmark is approximately 15% of the store base annually. In FY26, the company added 85 stores, exceeding its own initial expectations. In Q1FY27, 3 stores were added, taking the total to 503. Management stated the 15% figure could vary by a few percentage points higher or lower.

Same-store sales growth. Management guided that SSSG should hover around the FY26 level (8.1% for stores older than two years) and is not expected to rise considerably. Observed Q1FY27 SSSG for two-year-plus stores was 5.5%, versus 7.1% in Q1FY26, with flat growth in large metros.

Gross and net margin. Management stated a gross margin range of 14%–15% and a net margin around 5% as the “North Star.” Observed FY26 standalone gross margin was 14.30% (up 16 bps) and PAT margin was 4.81% (down 25 bps). Q1FY27 standalone EBITDA margin was 8.3% versus 8.2% in Q1FY26, and PAT margin was 5.1% versus 5.2%.

E-commerce profitability. Management stated the focus is on proving a sustainable, profitable model in 11 cities before expanding further. Observed FY26 Avenue E-commerce EBITDA declined 43.5% and net loss widened to Rs. 307 crore. In Q1FY27, operations were discontinued in seven cities.

Debt and funding. Management indicated availability of funds will not hinder expansion and that existing stores should generate sufficient capital within two to three years. Observed FY26 finance costs rose 125% standalone, and the board approved NCD issuance up to Rs. 1,000 crore in July 2026.

Quarterly growth volatility. Management attributed variation in recent quarterly growth (13% in December, 19% in March, 15% in June) to grocery deflation, panic buying in March with spillover into the next quarter, and inflation/deflation conditions. Q1FY27 standalone revenue grew 15.1%.

Technology investment. Management stated near-term margin trade-off from tech stack upgrades would be marginal. No quantified impact was provided.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a Buy predicated on rapid store additions, strong revenue growth, and a long 1,500+ store runway to a Neutral focused on tempered expansion, regulatory/land-acquisition bottlenecks, and rising quick-commerce competition. The early emphasis on high growth and D’Mart Ready scaling was replaced by a more cautious productivity/profitability lens, with SSSG expected near historical lows and FY27-29 estimates cut. Persistent themes included the EDLP/EDLC value framework, private-label growth, and balance-sheet strength.

Fears that came true

  • Early earnings-cut warnings materialized: after FY24/25 EPS cuts, FY27-29 revenue/EBITDA/PAT estimates were again trimmed ~1-2% due to slower store additions and metro growth drag, alongside multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes such as 2024-05-05 (actual -12.9%) and 2024-07-30 (actual -21.3%).
  • The fear of weaker expansion quality/growth in emerging clusters came true: the last report notes Tier-2/3 stores operate at lower sales per square foot and store-add targets were toned down to ~75/85 in FY27/FY28 versus 85/90 expected earlier.
  • The early discretionary-demand and average-bill-value weakness was not reversed: volume growth stayed muted, with SSSG at ~8% near historical lows and quick-commerce competition hurting dense metro volumes.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of continued rapid store expansion did not sustain: FY26’s +20% YoY pace was called not feasible, with FY27/FY28 additions guided lower due to regulatory and acquisition bottlenecks.
  • D’Mart Ready’s fast-scaling story failed to translate into aggressive profitable growth: by the last report, the focus shifted to rationalization in top 11 core cities and slotted deliveries rather than geographic expansion.
  • The strong footfall/bill-cuts momentum flagged early faded: SSSG is expected to stay near historical lows and elevated quick-commerce competition is impacting volume growth in dense metro catchments.
