UFLEX: Recent Corporate Announcements — Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material and supply-chain disruption from the West Asia crisis. The conflict is disrupting packaging film raw material production and transit routes, driving up freight costs. India’s import dependence on MEG, Homo Polypropylene and other key feedstocks further tightens supply and elevates prices. Over one-third of India’s MEG consumption is imported, with 90% of those imports sourced from West Asia; MEG supply tightened sharply post the war crisis, driving up MEG and PTA prices and, in turn, PET chip prices in India. Chemicals operations were impacted in March and April 2026 by shortages of solvents, monomers and adhesives, with availability improving only towards the end of Q1.

Input cost inflation. PTA, MEG and MELT prices rose sharply from March 2026 (PTA from Rs 66.59/kg in Feb-26 to Rs 90.16/kg in Mar-26; MEG from Rs 43.02/kg to Rs 57.86/kg; MELT from Rs 71.9/kg to Rs 97.2/kg), with Brent crude spiking to USD 117.3/barrel in Apr-26. Copper prices rose sharply by 51% from Q4 FY25 to August 2026, with only partial pass-through to cylinder sales prices, causing a marginal decline in cylinder volume and sales in Q1; higher metal costs also encouraged customers to extend use of existing cylinders, and fewer FMCG product launches and design changes reduced demand for new cylinders.

Aseptic packaging pressure. Domestic demand softened amid increased duty-free imports at aggressive prices, resulting in lower volumes and pricing pressure; export volumes declined due to a non-lucrative mix of pack sizes versus the prior year’s large-pack sales. Overseas volumes were also hit by supply disruptions and delayed consignment deliveries due to the West Asia crisis.

Competitive and demand softness in specific markets. Europe witnessed stable sales volumes amid continued pressure from low-priced imports, with demand expected to moderate next quarter due to seasonal holidays. CIS CPP volumes were impacted by increased low-priced imports. Hungary’s base films faced low-priced imports and cautious customer demand. Domestic flexible packaging faces highly price-competitive large FMCG tenders, prompting a strategic shift of capacity toward higher-margin products.

Cost and balance-sheet items. Blended spread/power costs and commissioning of new plants increased power costs in Q1; energy costs rose in Europe (Poland/Hungary), partly offset by green energy initiatives saving 4% of power costs. Gross debt rose to Rs 104,390 million at Jun 30, 2026 from Rs 98,526 million at Mar 31, 2026, though net debt reduced by Rs 343 million QoQ to Rs 85,875 million. Net debt/normalized EBITDA (TTM) stood at 3.77x.

Tax matter. Income tax demands aggregating Rs 50068.58 lacs were raised for Assessment Years 2020-21 to 2023-24 pursuant to Section 143(3) orders, including the impact of a Section 132 search conducted in February 2023. The Company received substantial relief at the first appellate authority for two years and has filed appeals before the Hon’ble ITAT; the matter is presently pending adjudication, and management believes it has a good case.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Consolidated revenue grew 37.6% YoY to Rs 53,972 million; EBITDA rose 92.1% YoY to Rs 9,198 million with a 17.0% margin — described as the highest EBITDA level in the last 21 quarters. Normalized PAT was Rs 4,233 million (7.8% margin). Growth was driven by operating leverage, stronger realizations, improved product mix and forex gains.

Regional demand momentum. India’s packaging films demand improved for the second consecutive quarter, with domestic packaging film sales volume up 9.1% sequentially; the war crisis is expected to keep raw material supply controlled across Asia, lowering packaging film imports into India and supporting higher price realization, especially BOPET, in FY27. MEA saw a surge in BOPET and BOPP demand, with UFlex sales volume growth of 14.9% YoY and 16.5% QoQ; converters are prioritizing local/regional sourcing to overcome supply-chain disruption, higher sea-freight costs and port inaccessibility. Americas delivered 18% YoY sales volume growth, led by steady BOPET demand, with the US Government’s aversion towards imports expected to remain a growth catalyst as imports from West Asia and India are expected to be limited. CIS BOPET sales benefited from steady demand.

Capacity additions and new facilities. The 39,600 MTPA Noida Sector 155 recycling facility was commissioned (April 30/May 1, 2026), and the 80-million-unit WPP bags facility in Altamira, Mexico was commissioned on July 31, 2026. The Egypt aseptic packaging facility (12 billion packs, Ain Sokhna) is scheduled for commissioning in H1 FY27, with residual capex of Rs 1,630 million. The Dharwad brownfield BOPP line (54,000 MTPA) is scheduled for FY27-28, with residual capex of Rs 6,154 million.

Backward integration and raw material security. The 168,000 MTPA virgin PET chips plant at Panipat offers raw material availability and supply predictability; integrated Panipat operations and domestic sourcing of PTA and MEG from IOCL Panipat provide greater raw material security and supply continuity. Virgin PET chips third-party sales volumes increased 31.4% QoQ to 29,471 MT, led by India (96.9% of sequential growth; India volumes up 41.0% QoQ).

Value-added mix and other businesses. A shift toward value-added packaging films and higher-margin products is a key growth theme. Engineering achieved 81% YoY growth in Q1 on strong export traction and new FMCG converting clients in MEA and the Americas. Holography sales growth was strong in mid-teens with operating profit above 33% in Q1. Chemicals export volumes grew with further opportunities in Egypt, East Africa, Nigeria and CIS.

Sustainability and regulation. Recycling volumes increased significantly — 319 million PCR PET bottles recycled in Q1 FY27 versus 93 million in Q4 FY26, plus 2,926 MT of PCR MLP and MLAP waste recycled. India’s EPR mandate effective April 1, 2026 requires brand owners to incorporate recycled content by FY28: 40% in rigid (Category-1), 10% in flexible plastic (Category-2), and 5% in multi-material flexible formats (Category-3).

Macro backdrop. India’s consumption environment is showing signs of broad-based recovery with improving FMCG volumes; rainfall was +108% LPA (1 Mar’26–31 May’26); rural FMCG volume growth continued to surpass urban.

ESG recognition. Crisil ESG Ratings & Analytics Limited voluntarily assigned an ESG rating of 58/100 (Category: Adequate) on 18 August 2026, based on publicly available data; the Company did not engage Crisil for this rating.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued West Asia conflict disrupting raw material production, transit routes and freight costs, with India’s import dependence on MEG, Homo Polypropylene and other feedstocks tightening supply and elevating prices.
  • Low-priced imports pressuring Europe, CIS CPP and domestic aseptic packaging, with duty-free imports at aggressive prices in the domestic aseptic market.
  • Seasonal demand moderation in Europe in Q2 FY27 due to holidays, plus continued import pressure and competitive pricing.
  • Price-competitive large FMCG tenders in domestic flexible packaging.
  • Volatility in industrial metal prices (copper up 51% from Q4 FY25 to August 2026) with only partial pass-through.
  • Pending income tax adjudication before the Hon’ble ITAT relating to demands for AY 2020-21 to 2023-24, including the impact of the February 2023 search.
  • Elevated gross debt (Rs 104,390 million at Jun 30, 2026) and net debt/normalized EBITDA (TTM) of 3.77x.
  • Forex exposure: Q1 FY27 included a Rs 825 million foreign currency/derivative adjustment; the average USD/INR rate moved from 85.37 in Q1 FY26 to 95.08 in Q1 FY27.
  • Execution and ramp-up risk on ongoing capex (Egypt aseptic, Dharwad BOPP), with residual capex of Rs 1,630 million and Rs 6,154 million respectively.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance provided:

  • CFO Arun Kumar Sharma: Q2 FY27 is expected to see some normalization from the exceptionally strong realization in Q1, while the underlying growth trajectory remains intact, positioning the Company for robust FY27 growth and continued improvement in earnings quality. Higher utilization led by localized sourcing to de-risk unscheduled supply chain disruption and a shift toward value-added packaging films will remain key growth themes.
  • Chairman Ashok Chaturvedi: the Company is well positioned to deliver sustainable and profitable growth in FY27 and beyond.
  • Domestic flexible packaging: management expects better product mix and realization to increase Packaging sales by 15-20%, while volume is to be maintained comparably at par or marginally lower going forward.
  • Cylinder business: management expects better sales realization as engagement with customers continues to achieve full cost pass-through.
  • Chemicals: improved raw material availability towards the end of Q1 will reflect better domestic sales performance in Q2.
  • Engineering: plans to expand into the non-FMCG segment for better profitability from premium clientele.
  • Capex schedules: Egypt aseptic H1 FY27E; Dharwad BOPP FY27-28E; Mexico WPP bags commissioned July 31, 2026; Noida recycling commissioned April 30/May 1, 2026.

Observed performance against prior periods:

  • Q1 FY27 revenue of Rs 53,972 million (+31.7% QoQ, +37.6% YoY) versus Q4 FY26 Rs 40,973 million and Q1 FY26 Rs 39,219 million.
  • EBITDA of Rs 9,198 million (+46.8% QoQ, +92.1% YoY); EBITDA margin 17.0% versus 15.3% in Q4 FY26 and 12.2% in Q1 FY26.
  • Normalized EBITDA of Rs 8,373 million (+37.0% QoQ, +78.2% YoY) with 15.5% margin versus 14.9% in Q4 FY26 and 12.0% in Q1 FY26.
  • Normalized PAT of Rs 4,233 million versus Rs 1,960 million in Q4 FY26 and Rs 580 million in Q1 FY26.
  • Total sales volume of 173,471 MT (+4.0% QoQ, +1.7% YoY); packaging films volumes +6.4% QoQ and +4.9% YoY; packaging volumes -4.0% QoQ and -8.4% YoY.
  • Packaging films production utilization improved to 85.4% in Q1 FY27 from 79.3% in Q4 FY26 and 82% in Q1 FY26; total production 135,873 MT (+7.8% QoQ, +6.2% YoY).
  • Overseas revenue contribution rose to 62% from 56% in Q1 FY26.
  • Blended cost of debt declined to 8.5% in Q1 FY27 from 8.7% in FY26; net debt reduced by Rs 343 million QoQ.
  • Capex incurred in Q1 FY27 was Rs 4,782 million, allocated across Egypt aseptic (Rs 1,236 million), Mexico WPP bags (Rs 205 million), Noida Sector 155 recycling (Rs 320 million) and Dharwad BOPP (Rs 215 million).
  • The CFO noted UFlex barely benefited from inventory gains in Q1 despite higher realization, and that Q1 growth comprised 38% realization growth, 2% volume growth and 4% currency tailwinds.
