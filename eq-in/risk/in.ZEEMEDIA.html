ZEEMEDIA: Q1 FY27 Earnings Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Profitability pressure despite revenue growth. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, consolidated operating revenue rose 4.7% year-on-year to ₹1,908.5 million (from ₹1,823.6 million in Q1 FY26), but operating expenditure grew faster at 7.4% to ₹1,745.9 million. As a result, EBITDA declined 17.8% to ₹162.6 million from ₹197.8 million. Operating costs alone rose 51.3% to ₹511.9 million, while employee benefit expenses were broadly flat at ₹663.7 million.

Losses and balance-sheet strain. The consolidated group incurred a loss of ₹1,212 lakhs for the quarter, with significant accumulated losses and negative working capital as at June 30, 2026. On a standalone basis, the loss was ₹882 lakhs. The statutory auditors flagged a material uncertainty related to going concern on both the standalone and consolidated results, noting these conditions may cast significant doubt on the company’s ability to continue as a going concern.

Revenue mix shift. “Other sales and services” revenue fell to nil in Q1 FY27 from ₹505.6 million in Q1 FY26, a 100% decline, even as advertising revenue grew 47.7% to ₹1,797.1 million and subscription revenue rose 9.9% to ₹111.4 million.

Competitive and structural pressures. The company faces market competition risks from traditional and regional brands, proliferation of local operators, and free news content. Other challenges include technological shifts (AI, Connected TV, OTT), potential variability in advertising revenue, intellectual property rights enforcement, feasibility risks of new initiatives, macro-level and geopolitical risks, litigation and regulatory risks, and manpower retention challenges in a competitive media landscape.

Regulatory matter. On February 18, 2026, the company and one of its subsidiaries received a SEBI Show Cause Notice (dated February 12, 2026) as co-noticees for alleged violations of certain SEBI Regulations in the matter of Zee Entertainment Enterprises Limited. The company states it believes it is in compliance with applicable laws, has filed its response subsequent to the quarter, and has filed a settlement application under the SEBI (Settlement Proceedings) Regulations, 2018, which is under consideration. No material adverse impact is expected.

Warrant conversion pullback. FPIs holding 13,33,33,333 convertible warrants allotted on November 7, 2024 communicated their unwillingness to exercise the conversion option due on or before May 7, 2026. The Board, on May 8, 2026, approved cancellation of the warrants and forfeiture of the upfront subscription amount of ₹5,000 lakhs.

Promoter warrant overhang. 135,000,000 warrants allotted January 5, 2022 to Asian Satellite Broadcast Private Limited (later transferred to Elitecast Media Limited) remain subject to status quo directions from the Delhi High Court, DRT and other courts. SEBI advised on August 24, 2023 that relaxation/extension may be sought after the final DRT order, and the company must abide by DRT directions.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Advertising momentum. Advertising revenue grew 47.7% year-on-year to ₹1,797.1 million in Q1 FY27, the strongest component of the revenue mix.

Digital and content monetisation. Zee Media’s YouTube ecosystem recorded video views surging nearly 3X growth in Q1, reflecting expanding digital reach and engagement. The company has been pursuing monetisation of its content archives, having recognised ₹8,019 lakhs from licensing of content archives during the year ended March 31, 2026, included under “Revenue from Operations.”

Capital raising progress. The Board approved FCCB issuance of up to USD 465,90,000 (5% coupon, 10-year maturity) on April 8, 2025, with a conversion price of ₹13.50 per share. Subsequent to the quarter, on July 30, 2026, upon receipt of subscription money from one investor, the Securities Issue and Allotment Committee approved allotment of 3,960 FCCBs of US$1,000 each.

New warrant issuance and conversion. The Board on May 18, 2026 approved issuance of 14,00,00,000 fully convertible warrants for up to ₹11,900 lakhs. On June 25, 2026, the company allotted these warrants to three FPIs at ₹8.50 per share, receiving 25% subscription (₹2,975 lakhs). On June 30, 2026, one warrant holder converted 3,00,00,000 warrants into equity shares, remitting the balance 75% exercise price.

Editorial and event properties. The company executed unmatched election coverage in West Bengal (Zee News and Zee 24 Ghanta) and across state elections in Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Assam and Puducherry. WION expanded global footprint through the Global Leadership & Innovation Summit in Sri Lanka. Zee Media extended infotainment into Purvanchal with Bhojpuri Samman, initiated a younger brand refresh for Zee Bharat, and executed 36 high-impact events across healthcare, travel, public policy, business, leadership, education, culture and community.

Zee Business leadership. Zee Business ranked #1 among competitors in YouTube VOD video views in Q1 (Source: Tubular), with properties including Money Alpha, Wealth Summit and Bharose Ki Choice.

AI-powered newsroom. The company integrated AI-led tools across newsrooms to enhance reporting, accelerate content workflows, strengthen data-driven storytelling and enable sharper journalism.

Broader growth prospects. Growth prospects cited include rapid digital media adoption, expansion into Connected TV (CTV) and live events, monetisation of archival content libraries through strategic licensing, new platform launches (Soul’s Own Lab, Zeefluence, Arena of Champions, SW!TCH, Pinewz), integrated marketing solutions (R.I.S.E.), and expanding regional and multilingual audience reach.

3. Key Risks

  • Going concern uncertainty: Auditors flagged material uncertainty on both standalone and consolidated results given losses, accumulated losses and negative working capital.
  • Competition: Traditional and regional brands, local operators, and free news content.
  • Technology disruption: AI, Connected TV and OTT shifts.
  • Revenue variability: Advertising revenue volatility.
  • Intellectual property: IP rights enforcement risk.
  • New initiative feasibility: Execution risk on new ventures.
  • Macro and geopolitical: Broader external risks.
  • Litigation and regulatory: Compliance and statutory oversight risks, including the SEBI Show Cause Notice and settlement application.
  • Talent: Manpower retention challenges in a competitive media landscape.
  • Capital raise execution: FCCB drawdowns have been amended from time to time at investor request; the Elitecast warrant monies remain subject to court/DRT directions.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Management commentary. CEO Raktim Das stated that Q1 FY27 performance “has set the runway to enter the next phase of our growth journey,” describing a strategic and transformative leap from a conventional advertising-led news company toward a diversified revenue stream media organisation. Management intends to continue investing in technology, strengthening digital capabilities, expanding regional and multilingual reach, unlocking value from its content portfolio, and maintaining disciplined capital allocation to drive sustainable long-term value. Management notes certain statements concerning future growth prospects are forward-looking and subject to risks and uncertainties.

Observed performance versus stated direction. The diversification intent is visible in the revenue mix: advertising revenue grew 47.7% while “other sales and services” fell to nil, and subscription revenue grew 9.9%. The company’s stated cost rationalisation and new revenue generation strategies are set against a quarter in which total expenditure grew 7.4% (faster than revenue at 4.7%) and EBITDA declined 17.8%. The company reported a profit of ₹1,693 lakhs (standalone) and ₹190 lakhs (consolidated) for the year ended March 31, 2026, and continues to have positive net worth, while the Q1 FY27 quarter returned to a loss. The company states its Board-approved business plan for the current financial year exhibits plans to secure higher revenues and improve operational cash flows, and that capital infusion plus monetisation through new revenue streams and cost rationalisation will enable it to settle liabilities as they fall due — the basis on which the results have been prepared on a going concern basis.
