Menon Bearings Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Competitive sector pressure in brakes: The Brakes Division capacity expansion is described as supporting revenue growth “in a competitive sector,” indicating competitive intensity in that business.
  • Working capital build-up: Trade receivables rose from ₹60.63 crore in FY25 to ₹90.77 crore in FY26, and inventories increased from ₹27.11 crore to ₹30.99 crore over the same period, tying up capital as the business scales.
  • Rising borrowings and finance costs: Total borrowings increased (non-current borrowings ₹21.25 crore and current borrowings ₹24.60 crore in FY26 versus ₹21.89 crore and ₹20.11 crore in FY25), with finance cost rising to ₹5.08 crore in FY26 from ₹3.76 crore in FY25 and to ₹1.37 crore in Q1FY27 from ₹1.16 crore in Q1FY26.
  • Margin moderation sequentially: EBITDA margin declined to 23.8% in Q1FY27 from 25.26% in Q4FY26, and PAT margin declined to 15% from 15.71%, even as absolute profits hit record highs.
  • Operating expense growth outpacing revenue growth: In Q1FY27, operating expenses grew 49.4% YoY against revenue growth of 37.2%, and employee cost grew 14.4% YoY.
  • Approval and registration dependencies: Railway registration of brake segment products is “under progress,” and dynamometer installation for railway approvals and OEM orders is still underway, making revenue timing dependent on external approvals. Components developed for several clients have received only 50% approval.
  • Long product approval cycles: The company notes higher gestation in product approval cycles among suppliers due to the technical nature of its products, which acts as an entry barrier but also delays revenue conversion.
  • EV transition exposure: The company explicitly positions itself in segments it describes as having low impact from the shift to electric vehicles, acknowledging the EV transition as a structural consideration for its end markets.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record quarterly performance: Q1FY27 delivered the highest-ever revenue from operations (₹94.04 crore, up 37.2% YoY), highest-ever EBITDA (₹22.38 crore, up 56.8% YoY), highest-ever net profit (₹14.11 crore, up 67.3% YoY), and highest-ever EPS (₹2.52, up 68.0% YoY).
  • Bi-Metal Products: Increasing exports and domestic orders for larger diameter bearings, bushes, and washers; samples received from a prominent US company for aftermarket; targeting high-volume, high-value parts for additional annual business.
  • Alkop: 38 parts developed and tested for a major customer, with substantial orders anticipated for domestic and export markets; expected to generate ₹50–60 crore annually within two years. In-house melting, die casting (pressure and gravity), machining, and powder coating capabilities handle components up to 12 kg.
  • Brakes: Growing export footprint and domestic aftermarket; dynamometer installation underway for railway approvals and OEM orders; significant turnover increase expected in FY26-27 post dynamometer commissioning.
  • Capacity expansion: Bi-Metal production capacity increased from 486 lakh units in FY25 to 580 lakh units in FY26 (~19% uplift), with ₹0.58 crore capex fully deployed in FY26 and ₹2.50 crore additional planned over the next two years. Brake capacity increased from 30 lakh pcs in FY25 to 50 lakh pcs in FY26 (~67% uplift).
  • Alkop capex: Additional ₹7 crore capex planned over the next two years to enhance machining capacity; commercial production of thrust washers ongoing with enhanced capacity at a new manufacturing plant.
  • EV segment entry: Entered the EV segment in Alkop for supply of components to Porsche, a German luxury and sports car maker, through Eaton, an Ireland-based supplier. Targeting EV segment to be 8–10% of Alkop by FY27. Also supplying to Tesla (via Concentric).
  • Export growth: Exports currently ~34% of revenue, projected to reach 37% by FY27. Exports to USA expected to increase significantly, with revenue per year expected around ₹30 crore. Plans to enter Africa via a large distributor network. Components developed/under development for several clients have received 50% approval, with production commenced in Q1FY26, holding revenue potential of approximately ₹55 crore annually.
  • Alkop revenue visibility: Alkop segment alone expects revenue of ₹95 crore by FY27 (from ₹40 crore now), including ₹25–30 crore from 38 parts developed and tested for a major customer. Order-to-production lead time of ~9 months provides forward visibility.
  • Sustainability and cost savings: Solar installation with output of ~53,67,828 kWh/year; energy-efficient motors, LED lighting, electrically operated furnaces, and boilers with pre-heat chambers each saving 1,800–2,000 kWh/year. Cost is nil for energy conservation equipment, maximizing ROI on existing infrastructure.
  • Market opportunity: Domestic market size per annum — engine bearings ~₹1,000 crore, brake segment ~₹2,000 crore, aluminium casting products ~₹6,000 crore.
  • Industry growth drivers: Growing per capita income (breach of $2,480 level by FY24 triggering auto demand; middle class anticipated to constitute ~38% of India’s population by FY31); PLI schemes in auto and auto-component sector with financial outlay of ₹57,042 crore; GOI infrastructure spend of $1.4 trillion by 2025; vehicle scrappage policy; rising indigenization by MNCs; India as global automotive R&D hub.
  • Strong balance sheet: Net Debt/Equity continues to stay almost nil. Last 10 years average ROE and ROCE are highlighted. Average dividend payout for last 10 years is highlighted.
  • Diversified clientele: No single customer contributes more than 10–12% of business. Exports to over 24 countries. Robust aftermarket network of 1,000 dealers and distributors, products at 10,000+ retail outlets, serving 30,000+ mechanics and reborers, with 40 marketing executives.
  • Dividend: Board declared interim dividend of ₹2 per share (200% of face value of Re. 1 each) for FY2026-27, absorbing ₹1,120.80 lakh, with record date of 22nd July, 2026, and payment from 27th July, 2026 to 4th August, 2026.

3. Key Risks

  • Customer concentration risk is mitigated but present: No single customer contributes more than 10–12% of business, but the loss of any major OEM relationship could still materially impact revenue.
  • Approval and regulatory risk: Railway registration and dynamometer commissioning are pending; brake segment revenue growth in FY26-27 is contingent on these approvals. Components with only 50% approval carry execution risk.
  • Export market concentration and geopolitical exposure: Exports are ~34% of revenue and growing, with significant planned increases to the USA. Changes in trade policies, tariffs, or geopolitical conditions in export markets could affect performance.
  • EV transition risk: While the company targets segments with low EV impact, the broader shift to electric vehicles could affect demand for traditional engine bearings and related components over the longer term.
  • Raw material and input cost risk: Cost of materials consumed rose 46.9% YoY in Q1FY27 (₹40.66 crore vs ₹27.68 crore), and operating expenses rose 49.4% YoY, indicating sensitivity to input costs.
  • Execution risk on capacity expansion and new initiatives: The company has multiple simultaneous initiatives (Alkop capex, thrust washers, EV components, railway approvals, Africa expansion) that require successful execution.
  • Working capital risk: Rising receivables and inventory levels could strain cash flows if not managed effectively.
  • Interest rate and finance cost risk: Finance costs are rising with increased borrowings.
  • Forward-looking statements are subject to risks: The safe harbour statement notes that actual results could differ materially from forward-looking statements due to known and unknown risks, uncertainties, and assumptions.
