TAKE Limited: Recent Corporate Announcements — Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Procedural deficiencies flagged by NSE. On June 22, 2026, TAKE clarified two observations from NSE regarding the outcome of its Board Meeting financial results for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026: the Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications was not in the SEBI-prescribed format and was not signed by the CFO and Chairman of the Audit Committee, and a machine-readable/legible copy of the financial results had not been submitted. The company resubmitted both on June 22, 2026, stating the deficiencies were purely procedural and that there was no change to the audited results approved by the Board on May 20, 2026.
  • Repetitive qualified audit opinion. The statutory auditor issued a qualified opinion on both standalone and consolidated annual financial results for FY2025-26. The qualification concerns income tax assets — ₹875.80 Lakhs in standalone and ₹1,247.60 Lakhs (per the auditor’s report; ₹1,291.90 Lakhs per the Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications) in consolidated — relating to assessment years up to March 31, 2021 (standalone) and March 31, 2026 (consolidated), where refunds are withheld or under process due to disputes pending before various forums. The auditor stated it could not confirm the ultimate realisable value of these tax assets, and the consequential impact on total assets and profits is not ascertainable. This qualification has been reported since the year ended March 31, 2023.
  • Contingent tax liabilities. Standalone contingent liabilities for direct tax matters stood at ₹108.03 Lakhs as at March 31, 2026; consolidated contingent liabilities for direct tax matters aggregated to ₹720.99 Lakhs.
  • Prior-year losses and discontinued operations. Standalone FY2024-25 recorded a net loss of ₹6,973.56 Lakhs, including a loss of ₹7,986.50 Lakhs from discontinued operations. Consolidated FY2024-25 net profit of ₹3,746.71 Lakhs was primarily driven by the gain on divestment of Ecron Acunova Limited.
  • Cash position decline. Standalone cash and cash equivalents fell to ₹24.74 Lakhs as at March 31, 2026 from ₹315.66 Lakhs a year earlier, with net decrease in cash of ₹290.92 Lakhs for the year.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Return to profitability and revenue resumption. Q1 FY2026-27 consolidated revenue from operations was ₹3,037.33 Lakhs (₹30.37 Crore) against Nil revenue in Q1 FY26, with total income of ₹3,170.11 Lakhs. Consolidated net profit reached ₹106.26 Lakhs, reversing a net loss of ₹(91.00) Lakhs in the prior-year period. Standalone revenue from operations rose to ₹1,196.90 Lakhs (vs ₹0.65 Lakhs in Q1 FY26), with standalone net profit of ₹96.83 Lakhs versus a net loss of ₹216.45 Lakhs.
  • Debt elimination and lower finance costs. Consolidated finance costs fell 99.62% YoY to ₹0.07 Lakhs in Q1 FY27 from ₹17.50 Lakhs in Q1 FY26. The company attributes this to debt elimination, interest burden reduction, and a clean capital structure. Standalone borrowings were nil as at March 31, 2026.
  • Improved equity base. Consolidated total equity improved to ₹3,100.14 Lakhs as at March 31, 2026 from ₹2,052.75 Lakhs a year earlier. Standalone net worth rose to ₹2,848.77 Lakhs from ₹2,576.86 Lakhs.
  • FY2025-26 turnaround. Standalone net profit for FY2025-26 was ₹271.91 Lakhs (continuing operations ₹270.91 Lakhs; discontinued operations ₹1.00 Lakh) against a net loss of ₹6,973.56 Lakhs in the prior year. Consolidated net profit was ₹1,084.91 Lakhs (continuing operations ₹425.61 Lakhs; discontinued operations ₹659.30 Lakhs), with consolidated revenue from operations of ₹5,418.53 Lakhs.
  • Strategic entry into longevity, biohacking, and nutraceuticals. TAKE announced a strategic entry into India’s longevity, biohacking, and preventive healthcare market, combining science-backed nutraceuticals and biohacking consumer products with technology-enabled predictive digital tools for metabolic health, sleep, cognition, and biological aging. The company cites a global longevity market projected to grow from USD 27.61 Billion in 2025 to USD 67.03 Billion by 2035 (9.41% CAGR); an Indian preventive healthcare market of USD 197 Billion in 2025; anti-aging supplements expanding from USD 168.6 Million in 2025 to USD 341.4 Million by 2033 (9.5% CAGR); and anti-aging services reaching USD 1.06 Billion by 2035. The model targets high repeat-purchase wellness categories for predictable, margin-accretive revenue, leveraging existing clinical and regulatory capabilities.
  • Going-concern support. The auditor’s Emphasis of Matter notes that divestment proceeds were used to substantially clear immediate statutory and debt obligations, and that ongoing business partnership and non-cash M&A initiatives, together with the improved financial position, provide reasonable grounds for continued preparation on a going concern basis.

3. Key Risks

  • Tax asset recoverability. The unresolved realisability of income tax assets (₹875.80 Lakhs standalone; ₹1,247.60 Lakhs consolidated per the auditor) and the timing/quantum of recovery remain contingent on appellate outcomes, with the consequential impact on assets and profits unascertainable.
  • Contingent tax exposures. Direct tax contingent liabilities of ₹108.03 Lakhs (standalone) and ₹720.99 Lakhs (consolidated) as at March 31, 2026.
  • Execution risk in the new longevity/biohacking segment. The strategic pivot is described as a recent entry; the cited market projections are external market estimates, and the company’s ability to scale the dual business model (nutraceuticals plus digital tools) is not yet evidenced in reported results.
  • Dependence on non-cash M&A and partnerships. The going-concern rationale references ongoing business partnership and non-cash M&A initiatives, which carry execution and completion uncertainty.
  • Liquidity tightness at the standalone level. Standalone cash and cash equivalents of ₹24.74 Lakhs as at March 31, 2026, alongside a net cash decrease of ₹290.92 Lakhs for the year.
  • Regulatory/compliance process risk. The NSE deficiencies, though described as procedural, indicate lapses in submission format and signatory compliance under Regulation 33.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • Profitability and revenue. Management characterized Q1 FY27 as reflecting “successful stabilization and revitalization” with “profitable operations” and a “clean balance sheet.” Observed: consolidated Q1 FY27 revenue of ₹30.37 Crore, net profit of ₹1.06 Crore, and finance costs of ₹0.07 Lakhs — consistent with the stated stabilization and near-zero finance costs.
  • Debt and capital structure. Management cited “debt elimination” and “near-zero finance costs.” Observed: standalone borrowings of nil as at March 31, 2026, and consolidated finance costs down 99.62% YoY in Q1 FY27.
  • Growth phase execution. Management stated it is “now executing our next growth phase” into longevity and anti-ageing. Observed: the strategic entry was announced alongside Q1 FY27 results (August 11, 2026); no revenue contribution from this segment is reported in the supplied Q1 FY27 figures.
  • Going concern. Management’s assessment (supported by the auditor’s Emphasis of Matter) that improved financial position and diversification initiatives provide reasonable grounds for going-concern preparation. Observed: FY2025-26 standalone net profit of ₹271.91 Lakhs versus a prior-year net loss of ₹6,973.56 Lakhs, consolidated revenue resumption to ₹5,418.53 Lakhs, and total equity improvement to ₹3,100.14 Lakhs.
  • Audit qualification continuity. Management maintains the tax assets are fully recoverable and expects a favourable outcome, with no impairment considered necessary. Observed: the auditor’s qualification on these balances is repetitive since FY2022-23, and the auditor states the realisable value cannot be confirmed absent final adjudication.
