Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost shock from the West Asia conflict. The quarter opened with the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed, crude crossing $100, and coal costs rising sharply. Fuel cost per ton rose from INR874 to INR915 (a 5% increase), with INR25–40 per ton attributable to fuel alone. Packing bag costs moved from an average of about INR9 per bag to INR12 per bag for the quarter, having spiked to INR14–15 before settling near INR10. Industrial diesel rose from INR78–80 per litre pre-war to INR150s during the war period. Ocean freight insurance premiums rose to 4–5% versus less than 1% normally.

Sequential cost pressure guided for Q2 FY27. Management expects costs to rise by INR130–140 per ton sequentially in the July–September quarter, reflecting the full impact of war-related costs from 1 July, monsoon-related maintenance across a large number of kilns, and operating deleverage. Pet coke has become more expensive than coal in energy terms, making domestic coal more attractive.

Monsoon and regional weather risk. July onwards brought wet spells across most of the country, though Rajasthan and some other pockets face a dry patch. Management noted that demand impact from dry conditions in some states may be felt next year through a water crisis. Q2 FY27 was described as likely to look optically softer due to the seasonal monsoon slowdown and cost effects.

Near-term data points being watched. Coal sector growth slowed in May, coal and refinery output were lower, and aggregate state capex in April–May grew a modest 2% year-on-year. Management characterised these as timing effects rather than trend changes.

East region lagged in Q1. East was the slowest-growing region in the April–June quarter, partly due to elections and labour availability. South and North were a shade below 15% growth; West and Central were above 15%.

India Cements-specific legal and regulatory matters. Demand Notices dated 15 July 2026 were received from the District Collector, Tirunelveli, Tamil Nadu, seeking recovery of compensation aggregating to ₹18.95 crore towards alleged Environmental Clearance violations pertaining to eight limestone mining leases, based on the Supreme Court judgment in Common Cause vs. Union of India. The company is reviewing the notices. Separately, the auditor’s review report draws attention to a provisional attachment order dated 25 February 2015 under which assets of ₹120.34 crore have been attached, which the company is disputing, and to a CCI order imposing a penalty of ₹187.48 crore, where the Supreme Court has admitted the company’s appeal and directed that the interim order of the Tribunal will continue; no provision has been made pending outcome.

India Cements Q2 FY27 optical softness. Management stated that Q2 FY27 may look optically softer for India Cements as the seasonal monsoon slowdown and West Asia cost effects weigh on the quarter.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong demand environment. Q1 FY27 was described as reaffirming conviction on demand, with a rich pipeline across infrastructure, housing and urban real estate. UltraTech reported 13.1% domestic grey cement volume growth, which management believes will be well ahead of industry growth (industry volume growth estimated at 7–8% for the quarter), translating into market share gains. Capacity utilisation was 81% versus 76% a year earlier on an enlarged 200-million-ton base.

Record financial performance. Q1 was the highest-ever first quarter for UltraTech across volumes, revenues, EBITDA and profit. Revenues grew 16%, EBITDA rose 12% to INR5,146 crore, and PAT rose 17.2% to INR2,604 crore. Operating EBITDA per ton was steady above INR1,200.

Announced infrastructure and capex pipeline. Maharashtra is planning a INR20,000 crore greenfield shipbuilding cluster around Mazagaon Dock; Odisha announced a deep seaport at Ganjam and a shipbuilding cluster at Paradip with over INR50,000 crore investment; Tamil Nadu signed ~INR18,000 crore of MoUs for data centres and shipbuilding. The Cabinet approved a ~INR20,000 crore-plus Ahmedabad–Dholera semi high-speed rail corridor. Metro programmes continue to expand across Ahmedabad, Bangalore, Mumbai, Pune and Uttar Pradesh. An additional ~INR30,000 crore infusion into NIIF is planned across roads, ports and urban infrastructure. Vadhavan port packages have been awarded. River linking (Ken-Betwa) is expected to be cement-intensive.

Housing and urban real estate strength. Housing and urban real estate account for roughly 55–60% of India’s cement consumption. Mumbai property registrations grew about 6%. Top-8 cities saw strong unit sales in Q1 FY26. Mumbai’s Slum Rehabilitation Authority is set to redevelop about 850 acres. Commercial real estate and grade A office markets opened strongly.

Urbanisation runway. India is about 35% urban, projected to reach about 39% by 2030, compared with Indonesia at 59%, indicating a long construction and development runway.

Brand premiumisation. Kesoram and India Cements brands were converted to 100% UltraTech. The brand grew 21.3% year-on-year. Customers buying B or C category brands were converted to an A category brand willing to pay a price premium. Retail remains the dominant channel: about 65–66% of sales are retail, 35% institutional, and RMC is about 3.5% of sales despite 477 RMC plants.

Capacity expansion. About 12 million tons of new capacity was commissioned in the quarter, of which 8.7 million tons by UltraTech (Shahjahanpur, Visakhapatnam, Patratu), taking domestic capacity to 200.1 million tons and total capacity to 205.5 million tons. Projects under execution are backed by about INR17,000 crore capex over the next 2–2.5 years, taking consolidated capacity beyond 242 million tons, with grey cement capacity reaching 212.7 million tons by end-FY27 and the balance the following year. Every ton of committed expansion is backed by secured limestone. Management targets exiting March 2028 with 235 million tons in India, implying 37 million tons added across FY27 and FY28.

Green power and cost levers. Green power stands at 1,897 MW, meeting about 47% of power requirements at quarter-end; 71 MW of renewables and 19 MW of WHRS were commissioned in the quarter. Management expects to reach 2.5–3 GW shortly. Lead distance fell from 367 km to 360 km. AFR substitution continues to ramp up. Clinker conversion improved to 1.5x.

India Cements turnaround. India Cements EBITDA per ton climbed from roughly INR386/t in Q2 FY26 to INR400, INR509 and INR603 in the current quarter. On a like-for-like basis (net of freight, with ex-factory reporting from Q1 FY27), revenues were INR993 crore versus INR821 crore, a 21% growth, backed by 19% volume growth. Cost improvement capex of about INR2,000 crore is being deployed into WHRS, preheater upgradation and cooler upgradation, with green power planned to rise from around 3% to about 86% of power requirements by end-FY28. Management targets INR1,000/t EBITDA for India Cements, with full capex benefits flowing through the P&L from Q4 FY28.

Cables and wires new business. The INR1,800 crore investment programme is on schedule and on budget; INR888 crore has been spent or committed. Channel partner onboarding is rapid, facility setup is complete, trial runs have commenced, key regulatory approvals are in place, leadership is on board, and SAP/ERP systems are in place with CRM under testing. Commissioning and product launch are reaffirmed for Q3 FY27 (October–December 2026).

Balance sheet strength. Net debt to EBITDA ended the quarter at 0.87x versus 0.94x at the start of the year; management is confident of ending the year below 1x. All growth capex and cost improvement initiatives are funded through internal accruals.

Pricing environment. All-India exit prices improved through June. East and South led price improvements; Central and West were steady; North was consistent. Industry expects prices to hold broadly steady through the monsoon quarter due to cost increases. Cost escalations of the past two quarters are still to be fully passed through.

Key Risks

  • Demand slowdown risk. Management identified a structural slowdown in demand — if people stop buying houses, industrialisation stalls, or data centres move elsewhere — as the principal risk. This was described as not foreseen.
  • Fuel and energy price volatility. The West Asia crisis was described as a “yoyo” with uncertain duration. Pet coke versus coal economics have shifted. Ocean freight insurance premiums remain elevated.
  • Monsoon variability. Dry pockets in some states could affect rural demand and potentially create water stress next year.
  • Regulatory and legal exposures at India Cements. The Tirunelveli demand notices (₹18.95 crore), the provisional attachment of ₹120.34 crore, and the CCI penalty of ₹187.48 crore remain unresolved, with no provision made for the CCI matter.
  • Execution risk on expansion. The 240-million-ton-plus growth programme and the India Cements cost improvement capex are under execution; benefits are expected to flow through FY27–FY28.
  • Industry capacity addition uncertainty. Management noted some industry players are revisiting expansion plans, making industry-level capacity forecasts difficult.
  • Working capital ramp in cables and wires. Higher working capital is expected for the first six months after launch, stabilising toward about 30 days thereafter.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Volume growth. Management targets double-digit volume growth for FY27. Observed: 13.1% domestic grey cement volume growth in Q1 FY27, following 9.3% in Q4 FY26.

Capacity milestones. Management stated it would cross 200 million tons; this was achieved a year early, with domestic capacity at 200.1 million tons and total at 205.5 million tons. Brand migration of India Cements and Kesoram was completed a quarter early.

India Cements EBITDA per ton. Management had guided to a trajectory of improvement. Observed: INR386/t in Q2 FY26, then INR400, INR509 and INR603 in the current quarter — sequential and unbroken. The INR1,000/t target remains in sight, with full capex benefits from Q4 FY28.

India Cements revenue optics. Management clarified that the printed revenue of INR1,013 crore versus INR1,021 crore reflects an accounting adjustment from reporting ex-factory sales from Q1 FY27; on a like-for-like net-of-freight basis, revenues grew 21% to INR993 crore from INR821 crore.

Q2 FY27 cost guidance. Management guided to a sequential cost increase of INR130–140 per ton, covering maintenance, operating deleverage and fuel. This was framed as a usual July–September quarter impact rather than solely war-related.

Net debt to EBITDA. Management is confident of ending the year below 1x. Observed: 0.87x at quarter-end versus 0.94x at the start of the year.

Cables and wires. Commissioning and product launch reaffirmed for Q3 FY27, precisely as committed when the investment was announced. No specific financial guidance was provided for the segment; management stated “sky is the limit” and that it would grow profitably with the market.

Dividend. Management indicated the Board looks at dividend as a percentage of profits and expects good dividends.

EBITDA per ton target. Management reiterated the INR1,400/t milestone for the January–March 2028 quarter, conditional on no war.

Capital allocation. Operating cash flows will be ploughed back into growth, with dividends for shareholders; no further investment in cables and wires is foreseen as the business matures.

India Cements merger timing. Completion of the journey toward potential merger is expected around Q4 FY28 or a quarter earlier, pending completion of the capex programme, disposal of non-core land assets, and bringing operating parameters in line with UltraTech stand-alone performance.

Broker Narrative

The broker kept a Sell stance from the first to the last report, but the narrative shifted from a distress story centered on operating losses, high debt and market-share erosion to a valuation-and-cost story that acknowledged a major operational turnaround—EBITDA jumped ~90%, PAT turned positive, volumes grew ~18%, and efficiency/brand initiatives delivered. Persistent themes included expensive valuation, soft realizations and fuel/cost pressures.

Fears that came true

  • Realization pressure flagged early materialized: net sales fell ~1% YoY and realization/t was ~2% below estimates in the last report, with recent Sell calls producing negative actual returns.
  • Fuel/cost pressure reappeared: variable cost/t rose ~3% YoY and fuel cost/kcal climbed to INR1.96 from INR1.70/1.75 in 1Q/4QFY26, validating the earlier cost-pressure warning.
  • Valuation remained restrictive: at 16x FY28E EV/EBITDA, the stock already priced in expected operational improvements, limiting re-rating scope and coinciding with negative recent returns.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of cement price realization improvements failed: realization/t stayed ~2% below estimates and net sales declined ~1% YoY, despite early hopes that prices would hold firm and improve.
