Marathon Nextgen Realty — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Execution and delivery obligations remain the central challenge. Management states that “execution continues to remain our foremost priority,” with construction activity across Monte South, Bhandup and Nexzone described as progressing “as planned.” The emphasis on timely delivery implies delivery slippage is a live risk the company is managing against.
  • Unsold inventory is substantial. The pre-merger portfolio shows total unsold area of 46,69,365 sq.ft. (MNRL share: 33,51,429 sq.ft.), with unsold GDV of ₹12,013 crore pre-merger and ₹13,483 crore post-merger. Converting this pipeline into sales is a continuing task.
  • Cost to complete ongoing projects is significant. Expected cost to complete ongoing projects is ₹1,461 crore on a 100% share basis (₹928 crore on MNRL’s share), against a surplus of ₹958 crore (₹727 crore MNRL share). This requires sustained funding and execution.
  • Dependence on approvals and process. The composite scheme of amalgamation and arrangement has received ‘No Adverse Observations’ from BSE and NSE and now “awaits NCLT approval.” Completion of the scheme is outside the company’s sole control.
  • Concentration in the Mumbai Metropolitan Region. The portfolio is entirely MMR-focused (Byculla, Panvel, Bhandup, Mulund, Lower Parel, Dombivli, Versova, Sewri), exposing performance to the Mumbai market cycle.
  • Segment mix shift. Commercial realisation per sq.ft. fell from ₹41,462 in Q4 FY26 to ₹29,047 in Q1 FY27 on the existing portfolio, and residential realisation moved from ₹20,976 to ₹22,487, indicating quarter-to-quarter variability in realisations.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Two new redevelopment projects added. Versova (premium society redevelopment, ~1.5 acres, estimated GDV ₹450+ crore) and Sewri (cluster redevelopment, ~7,500 sq.mts., estimated GDV ~₹450 crore), together ~₹900 crore GDV, in well-connected Mumbai micro-markets.
  • Large post-merger development pipeline. Post-merger developable potential of 4.20 crore sq.ft. across Dombivli (83 acres, ~1.0 crore sq.ft.), Panvel (205 acres, 1.26 crore sq.ft.), and Bhandup (130 acres, 1.75 crore sq.ft.), plus Lower Parel assets (Futurex 2, Chambers, two Central Mumbai projects) and Nextown.
  • Infrastructure drivers. Panvel benefits from the NAINA zone, Navi Mumbai International Airport, Sion–Panvel Highway widening, Atal Setu, and the Panvel–Karjat Rail Corridor. Dombivli benefits from Smart City Mission, Airoli–Katai Tunnel, Kalyan–Taloja Metro, Vasai–Alibaug Multi-Modal Corridor, and the Mumbai–Ahmedabad Bullet Train corridor. Bhandup benefits from Metro Line 4 (Wadala–Kasarvadavali), GMLR, and a 39 km cycling/jogging track.
  • Upcoming launches. Monte South Commercial (7.5 lakh sq.ft., ₹3,400 crore GDV), Monte South Residential Tower D (4 lakh sq.ft., ₹1,600 crore GDV), and the Marathon Neo Series (15.82 lakh sq.ft., ₹2,792 crore GDV).
  • Occupancy Certificates achieved. Full OCs for Cedar and Daffodil towers at Nexzone, enabling customer handovers and collections; OC received for NeoSquare; part OC for Monte South Tower B.
  • Strong balance sheet. Net debt-free with a positive net cash position following the ₹900 crore QIP completed in June 2025, with ₹340 crore earmarked for debt reduction; ~₹200+ crore acquisition capital available.
  • Diversified product mix across affordable housing, mid-size and luxury residential, and commercial, which the company presents as risk mitigation across market cycles.
  • In-house capabilities across design, engineering (slab cycles as fast as 40 hours), marketing, sales, and CRM, with 250+ active monthly channel partners.

3. Key Risks

  • Approval and regulatory risk: the amalgamation scheme awaits NCLT approval; all projects are RERA-registered, and the business is subject to real estate regulation.
  • Execution risk: timely completion of ongoing projects (Monte South at 58% completion, Nexzone at 50%, NeoValley at 67%, NeoPark/NeoSquare at 94%, Millennium at 93%) is required to realise the stated surplus.
  • Market risk: performance depends on sustained end-user demand and the Mumbai real estate cycle; the company notes the market “continues to demonstrate resilience” but this is not guaranteed.
  • Funding risk: cost to complete and acquisition plans require capital deployment; the company cites disciplined capital allocation as a priority.
  • Concentration risk: portfolio is concentrated in MMR micro-markets.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: the company’s own disclaimer notes actual results may differ materially due to industry structure, political and economic environment, tax laws, duties, litigation, and labour relations.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Guidance: Management stated FY27 commenced “on a healthy note with another quarter of profitable growth,” prioritising “timely execution of our existing projects, disciplined capital deployment, expansion of our redevelopment platform, building our PTC sales portfolio as an additional growth vertical, and continued focus on creating sustainable long-term value.” It expressed confidence in Marathon’s “growth trajectory in the years ahead.”
  • Observed performance: Q1 FY27 total income was ₹217 crore (up 43% Q-o-Q from ₹152 crore in Q4 FY26), EBITDA ₹66 crore (up 5%), and PAT ₹52 crore (up 15%). Collections were ₹118 crore (existing portfolio) / ₹146 crore (merged portfolio), and booking value ₹86 crore / ₹108 crore. Area sold was 0.38 lakh sq.ft. (existing) / 0.46 lakh sq.ft. (merged).
  • Where guidance and performance align: The company delivered profitable growth and maintained a net debt-free balance sheet, consistent with its stated priorities. OC achievement at Nexzone Cedar and Daffodil supports the stated handover and collection objective.
  • Where observed trends differ from prior periods: Area sold, booking value, and collections in Q1 FY27 were lower than Q4 FY26 (0.46 vs 0.48 lakh sq.ft. merged area sold; ₹108 vs ₹156 crore merged booking value; ₹146 vs ₹203 crore merged collections) and lower than Q1 FY26 (0.58 lakh sq.ft., ₹203 crore, ₹249 crore). Total income, however, rose to a multi-quarter high.
  • Scheme progress: The NCLT-convened meetings of Equity Shareholders and Unsecured Creditors held on September 7, 2026, passed the special resolution for the Composite Scheme of Amalgamation and Arrangement with the requisite majority, following the NCLT order dated July 2, 2026. The scheme still awaits NCLT approval.
  • Investor engagement: Officials will hold a Group Meeting with Investors/Analysts on September 17, 2026, from 3:00 pm to 4:00 pm in Mumbai, based on publicly available information. The 49th Annual General Meeting is scheduled for Monday, September 28, 2026.
