Thermax: Q1 FY 2026-27 — Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Industrial Infra project cost overrun. The dominant drag on the quarter was a Rs. 91 crore increase in the estimated cost to complete a specific project in the Industrial Infra segment, arising from events identified during the quarter. This single provision swung the segment to a PBIT loss of Rs. 71 crore (negative 8.7% PBIT margin) against a PBIT of Rs. 83 crore (9.9%) in Q1 FY 2025-26. Consolidated PBT before exceptional items fell to Rs. 42 crore (1.8% margin) from Rs. 211 crore (9.8%), and PAT fell to Rs. 22 crore (0.9%) from Rs. 151 crore (7.0%).

Industrial Products margin pressure. Segment performance was impacted by higher input costs and reduced export sales. PBIT fell to Rs. 64 crore (6.1% margin) from Rs. 79 crore (8.3%) despite sales rising to Rs. 1,058 crore from Rs. 950 crore.

Green Solutions project overruns. The Green Solutions business incurred project overrun costs during the quarter, with PBT of negative Rs. 17 crore (negative 6.9%) versus negative Rs. 5 crore (negative 2.2%) a year earlier, even as sales rose to Rs. 245 crore from Rs. 238 crore.

Input cost volatility. During April–June 2026, flat and structural steel prices, tubes and pipes hardened on uneven demand-supply dynamics. Non-ferrous metals saw significant price volatility from global market conditions, supply concerns and changing industrial demand. US dollar–Indian rupee movements further raised imported material costs and overall cost volatility.

Middle East softness. The West Asia conflict continues to dampen trade sentiment and regional capex, indicating softer near-term momentum in the Middle East. Raw material volatility and Middle East supply chain disruptions were also noted in the global resin market update.

Chemicals volume softness. New business wins of Rs. 5 crore in the Power segment helped offset volume softness across key end-user industries, particularly the SMI segment, though gross margins remained resilient at an average 14% price realisation.

Industrial Infra order decline. Order booking and backlog in the Industrial Infra segment declined primarily due to lower demand, even though reported order booking rose 24% to Rs. 863 crore and order balance rose 31% to Rs. 6,887 crore.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order book expansion. Consolidated order balance rose 23% year on year to Rs. 14,045 crore (from Rs. 11,376 crore), while order booking grew 2% to Rs. 2,809 crore. Order booking and balance for the current year are higher primarily driven by improved performance in TBWES, Industrial Products and Green Solutions.

Data centres as a growth vector. Data centres are gaining traction as a high-potential growth sector. During the quarter, the Industrial Products segment secured an order worth more than Rs. 400 crore for supply of boiler pressure parts for a data centre project in the USA. Data centre investment is accelerating, reinforcing demand for reliable power, cooling and efficiency solutions.

Green Solutions momentum. Green Solutions order booking rose 149% to Rs. 382 crore, attributed to a combination of improved business performance at TOESL and a change in its order book reporting methodology. TOESL adopted a rolling 12-month forecast model, replacing the earlier practice of reporting only the first year’s revenue from long-term contracts that typically run around 10 years; this methodological change increased the reported order book by Rs. 139, with no impact on underlying contracts, revenue recognition or financial results.

Segment order booking growth. Industrial Products order booking rose 8% to Rs. 1,394 crore with order balance up 16% to Rs. 5,370 crore. Chemicals order booking rose 1% to Rs. 169 crore with order balance up 45% to Rs. 220 crore.

Chemicals profitability improvement. Chemicals PBIT rose to Rs. 26 crore (11.2% margin) from Rs. 16 crore (9.3%), driven by higher volumes and better product mix.

Project execution milestones. In Green Solutions, 12 of 17 WTGs and transmission were completed for the 60 MW Trichy project; the 100 MW Karur project commenced with foundations completed for 10 of 17 WTGs; the 9.1 MWp Tuticorin solar expansion is nearing commissioning. At the Jafrabad, Gujarat hybrid project, PSS charging was achieved, 69.5 MWp of solar modules were installed, and 12 of 21 WTGs were erected. A CBG plant exceeded guaranteed production capacity, achieving 22+ TPD against a guaranteed 20 TPD with proven multi-feedstock operations. TOESL commissioned a 12 TPH biomass boiler in Coimbatore, reducing approximately 11,000 tonnes of CO2 emissions annually.

Product and order wins. TBWES secured a CFBC boiler order for a captive power project in India’s steel sector (designed to fire dolochar and Indian coal) and delivered a 60 TPH Flexisource boiler for the paper industry using multiple waste fuels. A 4.9 MW biomass-based multi-utility plant was commissioned in Thailand. Industrial Products commissioned a 60 TR steam-driven vapour absorption chiller for a Sri Lankan rubber glove manufacturer, a first-of-its-kind multi-utility plant in Thailand, high-capacity WHR ESPs for 650 TPD kiln lines, and a 30 KLD VapoNovae+ with ATFD for an auto component manufacturer.

Macro demand resilience. India’s Manufacturing PMI remained above 50 during Q1 FY27 (54.7 in April, 55.0 in May, 54.2 in June), indicating sustained manufacturing growth despite moderating momentum towards the end of the quarter. Global manufacturing remains in expansionary territory, while India’s private sector growth stays resilient despite persistent cost pressure. Consistent order demand continues across the chemicals, F&B and metal & mining sectors.

Restructuring initiatives. The Board on July 30, 2026 approved a Scheme of Arrangement and Amalgamation involving Thermax Bioenergy Solutions Private Limited (demerged company), Thermax Cooling Solutions Limited (transferor company) and Thermax Limited, with an appointed date of April 1, 2026, subject to NCLT and regulatory approvals. The demerger of TBSPL’s EPC business (turnover Rs. 239.35 crore as on March 31, 2026, 3.67% of the resulting company’s total turnover) is intended to let TBSPL focus on O&M. The TCSL merger is expected to simplify group structure, reduce administrative overheads, improve certain key financial ratios, deliver annual cost savings and enhance long-term shareholder value. No shares will be issued and there will be no change in Thermax’s shareholding pattern.

Capital return. The 45th AGM on July 30, 2026 approved a dividend of Rs. 14 plus a special dividend of Rs. 6, aggregating Rs. 20 (1000%) per equity share of face value Rs. 2 for FY 2025-26, with 99.9997% of valid votes cast in favour.

3. Key Risks

  • Project execution and cost estimation risk. The Rs. 91 crore Industrial Infra cost overrun and Green Solutions project overruns demonstrate exposure to cost escalation on fixed-price or long-cycle projects. The Industrial Infra key metrics show contribution variance from as-sold margins: for projects above Rs. 100 crore, 3 private projects and 5 PSU projects are in the below-negative-5% bucket, while 7 export and 7 private projects are in the 0% to 5% bucket and 7 export and 1 private project are above 5%.
  • Input cost and currency risk. Steel, tube and pipe price hardening, non-ferrous metal volatility and USD-INR movements increase imported material costs and overall cost volatility.
  • Geopolitical and regional demand risk. The West Asia conflict dampens trade sentiment and regional capex; Middle East supply chain disruptions affect the resin market.
  • Demand risk in Industrial Infra. Order booking and backlog declined primarily due to lower demand.
  • Export demand risk. Reduced export sales weighed on Industrial Products performance.
  • End-user industry softness. Volume softness persisted across key chemicals end-user industries, particularly SMI.
  • Regulatory and approval risk. The Scheme of Arrangement and Amalgamation requires statutory and regulatory approvals, including from the jurisdictional NCLT.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. The investor presentation notes numerous risks and uncertainties beyond the company’s control that could cause actual results to differ materially from forward-looking information, including volatile global economic and political conditions, compliance costs, market acceptance of new products and services, and changes in governmental regulations and currency exchange rates.

The original industrial themes largely disappeared from the last report, replaced by defense-sector framing. Despite optimistic early turnaround expectations, the stock experienced prolonged negative returns through 2024–2026.

  • Downward Estimate Revisions—EBITDA margin cuts of 20–40 bps and PAT cuts of ~5%—were validated by subsequent negative actual returns and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes across multiple reports from 2024 onward.
  • Losses in International Subsidiaries (Danstoker and PTTI) persisted beyond the expected recovery window, as the stock continued to decline sharply through 2024–2026 instead of recovering.
  • Margin Contraction in Green Solutions (EBIT margin dropping to 2.0%) proved lasting, as the stock’s prolonged downturn suggests green segment profitability did not improve meaningfully.

Optimism that failed

  • Turnaround Outlook for International Units expected within 12–15 months (by mid-2024) failed, as the stock recorded deeply negative actual returns (-28.7%, -28.4%, -23.8%) well into 2025–2026, indicating Danstoker and PTTI did not recover as projected.
  • Robust Financial Performance cited in 4QFY23 (revenue +16%, EBITDA +47.8%, PAT +52.3%) did not sustain, as evidenced by a string of DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes and negative returns from 2024 onward.
  • Easing Commodity Costs & Stabilizing Chemical Business failed to provide sustained tailwind, as multiple DISAPPOINTMENT results in 2024–2026 suggest commodity or chemical margin pressures re-emerged or were insufficient to offset other headwinds.
  • Strong Small & Medium Order Pipeline optimism failed to prevent prolonged underperformance, with the stock posting negative actual returns exceeding -20% on several occasions through 2025–2026.
  • Green Solutions Expansion plans (Rs5–6bn equity in FEPL, solar projects in Gujarat and Tamil Nadu) did not translate into sustained positive returns, as the stock declined significantly in the periods following these announcements.
