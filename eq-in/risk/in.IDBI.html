Headwinds and Challenges

  • Sequential contraction in core income. Net interest income fell 9% quarter-on-quarter (₹3,851 crore in Q4 FY2026 to ₹3,486 crore in Q1 FY2027), and interest income declined 3% QoQ. Non-interest income fell sharply — down 36% QoQ and 28% year-on-year — pulling non-interest income to total income down to 12.03% from 17.12% QoQ and 16.99% YoY.
  • Margin compression. NIM declined to 3.61% in Q1 FY2027 from 4.15% in Q4 FY2026 (down 54 bps QoQ) and from 3.68% in Q1 FY2026 (down 7 bps YoY).
  • Cost pressure. Cost to Net Income Ratio rose to 52.02% from 44.28% QoQ and 48.86% YoY (up 774 bps QoQ, 316 bps YoY). Operating expenses increased YoY.
  • Deposit and CASA attrition. Deposits fell 6% QoQ (₹3,47,163 crore to ₹3,25,757 crore) and CASA fell 8% QoQ. CASA ratio declined to 43.64% from 44.59% QoQ and 44.63% YoY. Total balance sheet contracted 5% QoQ.
  • Return ratios below prior-year levels. ROA at 1.89% was down 12 bps YoY (though up 14 bps QoQ), and Return on Equity at 14.80% was down 311 bps YoY.
  • Net NPA edged up sequentially. Net NPA rose 1 bp QoQ to 0.16% (though down 5 bps YoY), and Provision Coverage Ratio slipped 8 bps QoQ to 99.31%.
  • Stressed asset overhang. The Bank holds ₹3,543.43 crore book cost of Security Receipts, with a marked-to-market loss of ₹862.11 crore at portfolio level, and maintains 100% provision on the carrying value. Of this, ₹2,432.33 crore relates to Government-guaranteed SRs where provision is held on a prudent basis pending actual recoveries or Government approval of claims.
  • Project finance exposure under resolution. 23 accounts with outstanding of ₹315.30 crore are under a resolution process involving extension of DCCO, with resolution plans under implementation.
  • Senior management transition. Shri Sunit Sarkar, Executive Director (Senior Management Position), ceased to be Senior Management Personnel due to superannuation effective end of business hours on August 31, 2026.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Profit growth. Net Profit rose to ₹2,115 crore in Q1 FY2027, up 5% YoY and 9% QoQ. Basic EPS improved to ₹1.97 from ₹1.81 QoQ and ₹1.87 YoY.
  • Balance sheet expansion YoY. Total business grew 15% YoY to ₹5,84,725 crore; total deposits grew 10% YoY; net advances grew 22% YoY (and 2% QoQ); CASA grew 7% YoY.
  • Improving asset quality YoY. Gross NPA fell to 2.30% from 2.93% YoY (down 63 bps) and 2.32% QoQ. Net NPA fell to 0.16% from 0.21% YoY. PCR has been consistently above 99% since September 2023.
  • Strong capital position. CRAR improved to 26.92%, up 153 bps YoY and 27 bps QoQ. Tier 1/CET I ratio improved to 26.38% from 23.71% YoY and 25.56% QoQ. Net worth grew 27% YoY and 6% QoQ to ₹59,024 crore.
  • Lower funding costs. Cost of Deposits fell to 4.59% (down 25 bps YoY, 1 bp QoQ); Cost of Funds fell to 4.68% (down 30 bps YoY, 3 bps QoQ).
  • Credit-deposit ratio improvement. Rose to 79.50%, up 810 bps YoY and 644 bps QoQ, indicating better deployment of deposits.
  • Retail-weighted book. Gross advances composition was 30:70 Corporate:Retail.
  • Digital and innovation initiatives. Launch of ‘IDBI Innovate 2026’, a nationwide hackathon inviting start-ups, fintechs, developers and innovators to build technology solutions for banking challenges, aimed at enhancing customer experience and operational efficiency.
  • Recognition and inclusion initiatives. ‘APY Annual Award of Ultimate Achiever’ from DFS, Ministry of Finance for meeting Atal Pension Yojana enrolment targets for FY 2025-26 (awarded May 2026); ‘Internal Audit Excellence Awards 2026’ for the AI-enabled iNetra internal audit system.
  • Capital reserve strengthening. Discontinuation of Investment Fluctuation Reserve following the RBI Second Amendment Direction dated May 18, 2026, with ₹1,277.83 crore transferred from IFR to General Reserve during the quarter.
  • New CSR subsidiary. IDBI Foundation incorporated May 19, 2026 as a wholly owned Section 8 subsidiary; 100% of paid-up equity (10,000 shares of ₹10 each) subscribed on July 8, 2026.
  • Co-lending expansion. 9 CLA partners, 28,070 loan accounts, ₹734.26 crore outstanding, weighted average ROI 9.05%, spanning MSME, housing, agri, gold and commercial vehicle loans.

Key Risks

  • Margin and income volatility. The sharp QoQ declines in NII, non-interest income and NIM indicate earnings sensitivity to funding costs and non-core income.
  • Deposit franchise sensitivity. The 6% QoQ deposit decline and 8% QoQ CASA decline, alongside a 95 bps QoQ fall in CASA ratio, point to potential funding mix pressure.
  • Cost escalation. The 774 bps QoQ rise in Cost to Net Income Ratio signals limited operating leverage if income does not recover.
  • Stressed asset recovery uncertainty. Recovery on ₹3,543.43 crore of SRs (including ₹2,432.33 crore Government-guaranteed) depends on actual recoveries or Government approval of claims; the Bank carries a ₹862.11 crore mark-to-market loss at portfolio level.
  • Project finance DCCO extensions. 23 accounts (₹315.30 crore) remain under resolution plans involving DCCO extension, with implementation pending.
  • Concentration and related-party considerations. Voting on the LIC Mutual Fund related-party transaction was restricted to non-related parties under Regulation 23(4) of SEBI LODR; voting rights of shareholders holding above 26% of paid-up capital are capped at 26% under Section 12(2) of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
  • Key personnel changes. Superannuation of the Executive Director (Senior Management) effective August 31, 2026.
