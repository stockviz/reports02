SAGCEM Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Demand-side disruptions in Q1 FY27 - Heat waves across several parts of the country temporarily affected construction activity. - Election-related labour shortages in parts of Eastern and Southern India created short-term execution challenges. - April was negative in Tamil Nadu because of elections; the first six months post-election tend to be slower.

Pricing pressure - Realisations improved at the start of the quarter on price hikes across several markets, but momentum moderated towards the end amid competitive intensity and regional market dynamics. - Prices from the March exit to July were flat; June to July was also flat. Price increases that occurred in between were largely moderated. - Management noted that competitive intensity sometimes flares up and that most industry players are “losing patience.”

Cost inflation - Elevated input prices across energy, fuel and packaging amid geopolitical tensions in West Asia. - Power and fuel cost rose to ₹1,484 per tonne in Q1 FY27 from ₹1,450 per tonne in Q1 FY26, driven by higher pet coke and coal prices. - Price increases undertaken during the quarter only partially offset cost inflation. - Q2 FY27 is expected to see maintenance shutdowns at three plants, including a substantial shutdown at Mattampally and at Jeerabad, plus inventory cost adjustments from stocked-up clinker. Q2 is seasonally difficult due to rains and shutdowns.

Profitability deterioration - Consolidated operating EBITDA fell 40% year-on-year to ₹7,242 lakhs; EBITDA per tonne fell 47% to ₹451 from ₹851. - EBITDA margin declined 800 bps to 10%. - Loss after tax for the quarter stood at ₹2,810 lakhs (₹28 crore) versus a profit of ₹749 lakhs in Q1 FY26. - Standalone (Sagar) EBITDA per tonne fell 49% to ₹304; Andhra Cements EBITDA per tonne fell 57% to ₹161.

Andhra Cements cost structure - Variable cost at Andhra is around ₹100–125 per tonne higher than Mattampally, largely because Andhra lacks waste heat recovery and sources substantial electricity from the grid. - Bulk of the debt sits at Andhra, while Mattampally is debt-free.

Land monetisation delay - The Vizag land sale is awaiting final Government approval. A company-specific GO was earlier expected, but the Government now intends to issue a more generic GO. A similar GO was issued in Telangana a few months back.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Volume growth and demand - Q1 FY27 sales volume rose 13% year-on-year to 16,06,209 MT; production rose 13% to 16,30,407 MT. - Management remains confident of ~7 million MT volumes in FY27 (excluding clinker sales), and expects similar double-digit percentage growth in FY28. - South demand grew ~6–6.5% in Q1; management expects 8–10% for the year. AP and Telangana together grew ~11%; Tamil Nadu ~4% (June alone ~20%); Kerala ~12%; Karnataka flat. - Maharashtra, Odisha and Madhya Pradesh (9–10% growth) markets were described as healthy. - Amaravati capital development is a prime demand mover in Andhra; Telangana’s Indiramma Pathakam (six lakh houses) is another government-led driver. Private demand was also described as strong. - Labour that was missing in Q1 has returned; July demand expected similar to June.

Capacity and efficiency initiatives - Jeerabad 0.50 MTPA cement grinding expansion commissioned on June 10, 2026 (capex ₹120 crore, funded with ₹74 crore debt and internal accruals). - Remaining 1.55 MW WHRS at Gudipadu commissioned; total 4.35 MW WHRS fully commissioned on June 18, 2026. - Dachepalli 6-stage preheater commissioned October 23, 2025, raising clinker capacity from 1.85 MnT to 2.31 MnT; cement capacity expansion from 2.25 MnT to 3.00 MnT expected by September 2026. - New cement mill at Andhra due for commissioning end-September 2026. - WHRS at Gudipadu expected to deliver ~₹25 per tonne saving on consolidated volume (₹100–125 per tonne at Jeerabad volume). Andhra WHRS targeted over the next couple of years. - Green power usage at 22% in Q1 FY27 versus 16% in the prior year; solar projects at Mattampally and Jeerabad (4 MW each, ₹18 crore each) planned for FY27; WHR at Dachepalli (9 MW, ₹144 crore) planned for FY29. - Syngas-as-alternate-fuel project (India’s first) and AFR pre-processing facility at Mattampally, both with Q2 FY27 timelines. - Super fine building material work has started; operational plan to be shared by end of Q2.

Balance sheet and deleveraging - Gross debt at ₹1,704 crore as of June 30, 2026 (₹1,434 crore long-term); net debt ₹1,59,887 lakh; debt-equity 0.78:1; net worth ₹1,833 crore; cash and bank balances ₹105 crore. - No major capex planned for the next couple of years beyond maintenance capex of ₹30–40 crore per year; focus is on Vizag land monetisation and debt reduction. - Land monetisation: ₹150 crore expected in the current year and another ₹200 crore in FY28; management indicated buyer interest given buoyancy in the Vizag market. - Ongoing capex of ~₹240 crore for the current year, spread over this year and next.

3. Key Risks

  • Input cost risk: If the West Asia crisis escalates further, power and fuel costs could rise beyond the ₹100 per tonne inflation currently factored. Management stated it cannot control inflation, only the quantity of usage; inventory covers up to mid-October.
  • Pricing risk: Realisations depend on competitive intensity and regional dynamics; price hikes have repeatedly failed to hold. Management’s EBITDA guidance is explicitly conditional on prices remaining stable.
  • Seasonality and maintenance risk: Q2 FY27 will see elevated maintenance and inventory costs and seasonally weak operational metrics; recovery is expected only in Q3 and Q4.
  • Regulatory/approval risk: Vizag land monetisation depends on the Andhra Government issuing the generic GO; timelines have been outside the company’s control.
  • Andhra Cements drag: Higher variable cost (₹100–125 per tonne above Mattampally) and debt concentration at Andhra weigh on consolidated profitability; ramp-up to 60% utilisation is targeted by end of FY27, with 60–70% expected in coming years despite high competitive intensity in the neighbourhood.
  • Limestone auction premiums: Recent Telangana auctions saw premiums of 50–75%; new blocks in Tamil Nadu, Andhra and Telangana are due but not yet concluded.
  • Demand volatility: Month-to-month demand volatility has repeatedly prevented price increases from holding.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a bullish Buy in 2023, predicated on strong volume growth, cost savings from fuel softening, and rapid de-leveraging, to a cautious Hold in 2026 as cost inflation, high leverage, and margin pressure overshadowed those benefits. Persisting themes included capacity expansion, Vizag land monetization, and demand growth in South India, but the earlier confidence in near-term operational improvements gave way to concerns over temporary shutdowns, input cost inflation, and a slower-than-expected turnaround.

Fears that came true

  • Elevated cost structure from high power and fuel expenses persisted, with FY27E costs guided up ~₹100/tonne and Q1FY27 EBITDA/ton down 47% YoY.
  • Weak pricing environment materialized as realizations fell 6.4% YoY in Q1FY27, leading to a net loss.
  • High financial leverage remained a drag, with Net Debt/EBITDA still ~3x in FY28E, limiting re-rating as originally feared.
  • Demand disruption risks persisted, as the last report cited demand slowdowns and high regional competition as key risks.

Optimism that failed

  • Fuel cost softening expected from Q2FY24 failed to materialize as power and fuel expenses continued to rise, pressuring margins.
  • The de-leveraging pipeline to net cash by FY28E failed, with leverage still elevated at ~3x Net Debt/EBITDA.
  • Vizag land monetization has not yet materialized, with the last report still expecting ₹150 crore in FY27 and ₹200 crore in FY28 pending government approvals.
  • Operational cost savings from initiatives like the pre-heater upgradation were insufficient, as seen in the sharp 47% YoY decline in EBITDA/ton in Q1FY27.
