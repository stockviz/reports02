SIGACHI Industries — Recent Developments Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Capacity loss and market share erosion. The company lost approximately 6,000 metric tons per annum of MCC capacity following an incident at its Hyderabad facility. Management stated this shortfall prevented it from supplying existing customers, who then moved to approved alternative suppliers to avoid halting production. Q1 FY27 MCC revenue was INR82.74 crores, and management acknowledged the company is “limping back” to the best capacity achievable at its other two facilities.

Revenue run-rate below full-year guidance. Q1 FY27 total operating income was INR121.27 crores, against a full-year guidance of INR650–675 crores, which implies a quarterly run-rate of roughly INR160–170 crores. Several analysts questioned how the target would be met given capacity utilization of 76.8% overall (Dahej 76.5%, Jhagadia 77.16%).

Margin gap versus guidance. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 13.6% (EBITDA INR16.5 crores), against full-year guidance of 18%. Management indicated Q4 would need to exceed 20% for the full-year figure to reconcile.

Project delay. The 12,000 MTPA MCC expansion at Dahej-2, previously indicated for Q1 FY28, is now targeted for Q2 FY28. Management confirmed the revised timeline and, when asked, said no further delay is anticipated as of the latest project review.

Insurance claim timing. The property-loss insurance assessment is complete and an e-auction has been initiated. Management expects either the full claim (with some discount) or an ad-hoc amount by September 2026. Business continuity insurance of INR16.5 crores (against a INR25 crore policy) is expected to take a further three to four months.

Compensation matter sub-judice. INR42 lakhs has been paid to each beneficiary of the deceased. The matter remains before a court, and management declined to comment further, stating it has provided for the entire amount in the books.

Shareholder rejection of fundraising resolutions. At the Extraordinary General Meeting held September 15, 2026, shareholders did not approve the resolutions for increasing the Authorised Share Capital and for the preferential issue of up to 11,00,00,000 convertible warrants. The Board had earlier approved increasing Authorised Share Capital from Rs. 43,00,00,000 to Rs. 60,00,00,000 and issuing warrants at Rs. 26.40 each to promoters and other identified persons.

Prior warrant forfeiture. An investor noted that promoter shares were forfeited previously because amounts were not deposited in time; management attributed this to last-minute misplanning due to unexpected holidays.

Working capital. Receivable days stood at approximately 93–94 days, with a stated aim of reducing to 90 days by year-end and eventually 75–80 days.

Investor communication feedback. Participants raised that results and the conference call were separated by only about 10 minutes, and that the presentation’s guidance agenda item did not contain the guidance discussed on the call.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

MCC realization improvement. Average MCC realization rose to INR241.36 per kg in Q1 FY27 from INR216 per kg in Q4 FY26. Management attributed structural industry margin improvement since 2019 to consolidation (including ownership changes involving the global leader and an Indian player acquired by a European player) and innovation-driven product mix shifts toward higher-grade MCC.

Capacity expansion. The 12,000 MTPA Dahej-2 expansion, targeted for Q2 FY28, would take total cellulose-based excipient capacity to 30,000 MTPA. Current cellulose-based excipient capacity is 18,000 MTPA, with exports over 53.5% of production. Management expects rapid ramp-up of the new facility given 30 years in the supply chain across 65 countries.

CCS entry. The 1,800-ton Croscarmellose Sodium facility at Dahej SEZ is progressing, with operations expected in FY28. Management stated CCS margins are “far more healthier” than MCC and confirmed 25%+ margins are achievable even with CMC imported from China. The India CCS market is estimated at about $100 million, with realizations of INR1,200–1,500 per kg for certain grades. Export inquiries are already being received from customers who buy MCC and require CCS as a complementary disintegrant.

API momentum. Q1 FY27 API revenue was INR21.68 crores. New molecules cited include Sparsentan, Zestrapin, and Bempedoic acid, described as higher-margin. Management expects INR100–110 crores or more from API, with quarter-on-quarter improvement. A cystic fibrosis product is in the pipeline, with revenue possibly at year-end or next year.

New product launch. HiCel SMCC Nutra, combining microcrystalline cellulose and magnesium aluminometasilicate for nutraceutical formulations, was launched in the quarter.

Debottlenecking. Management plans to add capacity through debottlenecking, additional shifts, and small equipment to raise output without major new lines.

Smokeless tobacco demand. Management described the segment as nascent, with a shift from smoking to smokeless tobacco creating MCC demand, though it declined to give growth guidance and called 30–40% growth rates unrealistic to sustain.

ESG targets. Management targets a 20% reduction in energy consumption intensity by 2032, 30% reduction in coal consumption intensity by 2030, 10% reduction in Scope 1+2 emissions intensity by 2031, 30% reduction in freshwater consumption intensity by 2032, and 20% reduction in landfill intensity waste per unit by 2028.

3. Key Risks

  • Oversupply risk in MCC. An analyst noted most Indian players are expanding capacity and asked about potential price declines. Management argued regulatory compliance and quality approvals, not capacity alone, determine export market access, and cited historical Asia Pacific CAGR of 7–8% with the market expected to reach $1.4 billion by 2035.
  • Customer re-approval timelines. For CCS, customers must formulate and place products under stability before switching suppliers. Management expects acceleration given existing approved-vendor status.
  • Raw material dependence. CCS production will rely on imported CMC, primarily from China, rather than backward integration. Management judged in-house CMC capacity of a couple thousand tons uneconomic versus buying from large Chinese producers.
  • Funding risk for capex. FY27 capex is expected to exceed INR100 crores; FY28 capex is projected at INR150–200 crores. Management said funding could come from bank finance, internal accruals, or preferential equity/warrants, with no option finalized. The company stated it is currently debt-free with no term loan and that bankers are ready to fund.
  • Insurance timing risk. If the claim is delayed beyond September, management said capex would not be hampered, citing available term loan and equity options.
  • Litigation and regulatory exposure. The compensation matter is sub-judice. The EHS document lists regulatory compliance, cybersecurity and data privacy, supply chain disruptions, workplace accidents, and business ethics among risks.
  • Governance/execution risk on fundraising. The rejection of the preferential issue and Authorised Share Capital increase at the September 15, 2026 EGM removes a contemplated funding route unless revisited.
