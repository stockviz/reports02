Sasken Technologies — Q1 FY27 Earnings Call and 38th AGM: Key Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Product Solutions margin pressure. Product Solutions revenue of ₹120 Crores grew 23.6% year-on-year but moderated 3.9% sequentially following a strong Q4. Segment margins fell to 5.9%, down 310 basis points sequentially. Management attributed this to industry-wide pressures relating to memory availability, component pricing, and program mix. On memory pricing, the CFO explained that while costs are passed on to customers, the incremental margin does not come through, so the percentage margin is affected. A change in product mix also contributed, as the silicon business moves toward productization, with some products expected to ship through the year.

Working capital and cash consumption. Operating cash flows have been negative for the last two years despite profit growth. The CFO stated this reflects investment in the business rather than collection challenges, with consumption across working capital and fixed assets, and noted that Q1 included inventory build to manage the memory shortage. Management expects large cash consumption again this year and will use a mixture of internal accruals and external funds to finance growth.

Customer concentration. Revenue contribution from the top five customers remained broadly stable at approximately 56%, up from 52%, even as the active customer base expanded from 79 to 93 year-on-year.

60x4x3 timeline slippage. The strategy to bring customers to a US$4 million run rate within three years has completed nine of twelve quarters. Mr. Mody stated the three-year frame was “metaphoric” and that it may take one or two more years, while affirming the strategy and focus remain intact. The count of US$4 million-plus customers has stayed at six for four consecutive quarters.

Automotive module commercialization. On the two-wheeler instrument cluster, Mr. Ramanna noted the reference design was created for Qualcomm and adopted by module suppliers such as Quectel, but the two-wheeler market continues to buy modules from China due to cost sensitivity. Commercial conversion depends on shifts away from importing Chinese modules, and management sees potential in other modules such as NAD rather than clusters.

Order book tenure. A participant observed that the US$47 million quarterly TCV is roughly a quarter of revenue, shorter than the 9–12 month TCV typical of comparable companies, requiring a fast-forward, continuous sales approach. The CFO clarified that the published figure is orders booked in the quarter, not total backlog, and that the US$40.5 million annual contract value reflects delivery over the next 12 months, with backlog typically providing 9–10 months of carry.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Revenue momentum. Consolidated revenue for Q1 FY27 was ₹339 Crores, up 24% year-on-year and 1.6% sequentially. Software services revenue was ₹219 Crores, up 24.3% year-on-year and 4.8% sequentially. Product Solutions grew 23.6% year-on-year.

Order bookings and new logos. Total contract value booked in the quarter was approximately US$47 million, including US$34 million from new deal wins, with five new logos added. Annual contract value corresponding to orders won was approximately US$40 million, including US$24 million from new wins.

Margin expansion in software services. Gross margin for the quarter was 21.9% (₹74 Crores), up 48% year-on-year. Software services gross margin improved 240 basis points to 30.6% on improved utilization, disciplined cost management, and benefits of scale. EBIT margin of 6.3% improved 430 basis points year-on-year.

Customer and people metrics. Active customers rose to 93 from 79 a year earlier. Headcount reached 2,658, with attrition at 9.8% and utilization at 85%. CSAT was 4.5 out of 5 against a stated industry benchmark of 3.75. Cash and investments stood at ₹356 Crores.

AI and hyperscaler engagements. The quarter included a multi-year capability engagement with a global hyperscaler and a strategic engagement with a global technology company covering agentic AI, LLM validation, and intelligent testing. Mr. Mody confirmed the two disclosed hyperscaler engagements are with different hyperscalers. Management stated all work delivered for customers is AI-led.

Chip-to-Cognition positioning. Sasken describes itself as a full-stack “Chip-to-Cognition” engineering partner spanning semiconductor design, embedded software services, connected devices, and AI-native engineering. The Borqs acquisition broadened smart devices and ODM capabilities and strengthened relevance with global hyperscalers and automotive customers. Mr. Mody stated the Borqs integration has worked well over the last 12 months, with benefits in both services and product solutions.

Demand vectors. Management cited software-defined vehicles, centralized compute architectures, 5G, V2X, 5G Advanced, secure private networks, Open RAN, on-device AI, custom silicon, chiplets, memory integration, and package design as drivers. Engagements cited include massive MIMO, beamforming, and Open RAN. The China Plus One shift was cited as a vector, with European and US customers asking for products designed outside China. Make in India was also cited as a demand driver.

Silicon business. Dr. Savla described sustained semiconductor investment across AI, edge computing, automotive electronics, and intelligent connectivity, with scarce analog and mixed-signal talent pushing customers toward specialized partners. He noted GlobalFoundries IP certification, Intel certification, and a formal TSMC relationship are all in progress, with significant progress made and additional design activity resulting, though specifics cannot be announced. He also noted revenue mix already reflects implementation of these technologies.

Capacity expansion. Sasken inaugurated a Silicon Incubation Center in Hubballi for university collaboration and semiconductor talent development, and opened a new center of excellence in Hyderabad.

Sales bandwidth. Mr. Mody stated the company expects to add at least five to six sales heads during the rest of the year, alongside delivery-led growth and leadership-led selling.

3. Key Risks

  • Memory availability, component pricing, and program mix pressures in Product Solutions, with margin stabilization expected over the year but no guidance provided.
  • Continued negative operating cash flows and expected large cash consumption in FY2027, with growth financed through internal accruals and external funds.
  • Customer concentration, with the top five customers contributing approximately 56% of revenue.
  • Dependence on continued order booking to sustain revenue, given the short tenure of quarterly TCV relative to revenue.
  • Execution risk in converting bookings to revenue, which management identified as a priority for the remainder of FY2027.
  • Silicon business is at an early stage, with productization timing affecting margins and multi-year order offtake dependent on customer schedules.
  • Automotive module commercialization contingent on cost competitiveness versus Chinese imports and potential policy shifts.
  • No margin guidance provided; management stated it will maintain margins while sustaining growth momentum, without quantifying.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Growth versus margins. When asked whether growth might come at the cost of margins, Mr. Mody stated the company will maintain margins while maintaining growth momentum, citing a prior turnaround in Software Services EBIT from roughly 2–3% to 11.5–12% over about six quarters. Observed performance shows software services gross margin expanding 240 basis points year-on-year to 30.6%, while Product Solutions margin declined 310 basis points sequentially to 5.9% and EBIT margin declined 50 basis points sequentially to 6.3%.

60x4x3. Management acknowledged the three-year timeframe may extend by one or two years, with the count of US$4 million-plus customers unchanged at six for four consecutive quarters. Mr. Mody referenced crossing US$125 million in two years from a US$50 million base, achieved in FY2026, as evidence of direction, while stating no formal guidance is given.

Product Solutions margin stabilization. The CFO stated the Q1 actions should see margins starting to stabilize over the year and that margins should be viewed on a full-year basis. Observed Q1 Product Solutions margin was 5.9%, down 310 basis points sequentially.

Borqs break-even. The CFO stated the break-even point is approximately US$12 million in quarterly revenue, from which scale would add to the bottom line. No quarterly Borqs revenue figure was disclosed in the call.

Cash flow. Management stated the effort is to get to cash flow positive despite growth challenges, describing this as work in progress, while expecting large cash consumption this year. Observed performance shows negative operating cash flows for the last two years.

Headcount as a growth metric. The CFO noted that with AI embedded in delivery, headcount may no longer be the only vector to measure growth. Observed headcount rose from approximately 2,200 at the end of Q1 FY2026 (including roughly 300 from Borqs) to 2,658, with the increase described as organic.

Segment disclosure. Management declined to disclose revenue by offering or vertical, stating it would become more relevant as the business scales and could distort the picture on a quarterly basis. A participant requested future segment disclosure.

AGM outcomes. At the 38th AGM held on July 31, 2026, shareholders approved all five resolutions: adoption of audited financial statements for FY2026 (99.9970% in favour), a final dividend of ₹13 per equity share (99.9970% in favour), re-appointment of Mr. Pranabh D. Mody as director (99.8136% in favour), re-appointment of M/s. M S K A & Associates LLP as statutory auditors for five years (99.9936% in favour), and appointment of Mr. V. Suryanarayanan as Independent Director for up to five years (99.9955% in favour).
