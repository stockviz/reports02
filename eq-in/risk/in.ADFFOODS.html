ADF Foods — Q1 FY27 Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Geopolitical uncertainty and shipping disruption: The West Asia conflict, trade-route disruptions, vessel shortages and container non-availability constrained the business through the quarter. Management noted that March was a particularly bad month with no shipments, and that shipments resumed only from end-April. Even in June, roughly 30% of goods that were ready could not be shipped due to lack of ships and containers, with the risk that Q2 volumes slip into Q3.
  • Elevated freight and fuel costs: Ocean freight and fuel costs remained high, with the CFO quantifying the consolidated margin impact at roughly 3%. This was the principal reason consolidated EBITDA margin, adjusted for the tariff refund, sat at about 13.4% rather than the high-teens level.
  • Tariff uncertainty in the US: US import tariff developments remained fluid. The 10% rate was noted as having expired on 24 July but was still being applied at the time of the call, with the future path uncertain. Management also noted that during September–March, most mainstream customers had absorbed the tariffs, with the rest shared across the chain.
  • PLI sunset: FY27 is the last year of the company’s PLI enrolment (Category 3, brand marketing and promotion reimbursement), with about INR16 crores accrued for the full year. Management acknowledged the benefit will not continue in the same form.
  • Seasonality and sequential decline: Q1 was described as historically the weakest quarter. Consolidated revenue fell 15.0% Q-o-Q and PAT fell 33.3% Q-o-Q, with distribution segment margin at 11.5% versus 17.4% a year earlier.
  • Soul brand underperformance: The domestic Soul brand has been slower than Truly Indian; management said strategy changes have been made and expects results to improve this year.
  • Labour: No significant labour shortages were reported; any shortages were described as seasonal around elections and wedding season.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Fourth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth: Consolidated revenue rose 25.9% Y-o-Y to INR167.3 crores, with stand-alone revenue up 20.5% to INR120.9 crores, driven by shelf-space gains, category diversification, prior listings and execution in international markets.
  • Brand momentum: Ashoka grew over 30% in the quarter and has delivered a revenue CAGR above 20% over five years. Truly Indian is now in more than 3,000 US stores with marquee mainstream clients, with growth split roughly 60% same-store and 40% new listings. Repeat orders and new listings are cited as validation.
  • Surat greenfield facility: Commercial deliveries and initial container shipments have begun, with about 15 containers shipped in the quarter. The facility expands frozen food capacity; full capacity is expected to support just under INR300 crores of revenue, with FY27 contribution guided at INR40–50 crores and about 30% utilisation this year.
  • AEO-T3 certification: Received from CBIC in May, expected to deliver faster customs clearance, reduced inspections, better inventory turns and working capital efficiency, with benefits visible in shipments from June onward.
  • Freight pass-through: From the current quarter, the company has begun passing on freight increases — close to 75% in the US and upwards of 65–70% of business overall — which should support margin recovery.
  • Tariff refund: The US subsidiary received USD2.08 million (INR19.69 crores), of which USD0.77 million (about INR7 crores) was booked in Q1 P&L, with the balance held on the balance sheet pending evaluation of customer and commercial arrangements.
  • Europe expansion: A step-down subsidiary in Ireland is being set up to address the European market, alongside existing UK and Europe teams, with the region expected to grow at high double digits. New FTAs with the UK, EU, New Zealand and Australia are seen as positive.
  • Litigation recovery: The company won a US case awarding close to USD2.8 million, which it is now seeking to recover through lawyers’ discussions.
  • Capacity runway: With greenfield and brownfield combined, management indicated capacity to reach upwards of INR1,250 crores of revenue excluding agency business, with full utilisation seen around FY30.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued container and vessel shortages delaying conversion of a record order book into revenue, with possible spillover from Q2 into Q3.
  • Persistence of elevated freight and fuel costs, and the extent to which pass-through can be sustained market by market.
  • US tariff trajectory remaining uncertain, with the 10% rate’s status unresolved.
  • PLI benefit ending after FY27, with the company relying on brand investment declining as a percentage of sales to offset the impact.
  • Surat ramp-up risk: full capacity takes 2–3 years, and new product acceptance depends on consumer trials and repeat orders.
  • Brand-building investment requirements for Truly Indian and Soul continuing to weigh on near-term margins.
  • Competition for Truly Indian comes mainly from local US companies producing Indian-style foods, not Indian players.
  • Subsidiary-level items: three subsidiaries were not reviewed by their auditors (revenue INR177.02 lakhs, net loss INR115.82 lakhs), and one Indian subsidiary under voluntary liquidation was excluded from consolidation as immaterial.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

  • Revenue: Management guides FY27 revenue upwards of INR900 crores, contingent on geopolitical conditions easing. Q1 delivered INR167.3 crores consolidated, with the shortfall against potential attributed to shipping constraints rather than demand; the order book was described as the strongest in the company’s history for June, July and August.
  • EBITDA margin: Guidance is high teens, explicitly excluding the tariff refund. Q1 reported 17.7% consolidated, but adjusted for the refund this falls to about 13.4% due to the ~3% freight impact. Management expects recovery toward 16–17% over the remaining nine months as freight pass-through takes effect, and reiterated high-teens as the sustainable range.
  • Surat contribution: Guided at INR40–50 crores for FY27, with about 30% capacity utilisation this year and ramp-up over 2–3 years; commercial deliveries have commenced.
  • Depreciation: Q1 depreciation reflects Phase 1 of Surat; Phase 2 in Q3 is expected to add INR25–30 crores of capex, mostly plant and machinery, with incremental depreciation flowing through once projects go live.
  • PLI: INR16 crores accrued for FY27, roughly 2% of margin on FY26 revenue of INR683 crores; management expects this to be offset by brand investment declining as a percentage of sales, and hopes the government extends the scheme for Category 3.
  • Segment margins: Distribution segment margin of 11.5% in Q1 was below the prior-year 17.4%; management attributed this to quarterly seasonality and guided that full-year segment margins will remain in historical high-teens ranges.
  • Capacity: Full utilisation of expanded capacity expected around FY30, supporting upwards of INR1,250 crores of revenue excluding agency business.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative remained bullish with Buy calls throughout, but evolved from a first report with no risks and only broad TAM growth drivers to a last report that explicitly acknowledged currency, raw-material, geographic-exposure, and supply-chain risks. The optimism also became more concrete in the last report, citing specific brand initiatives, store listings, and revenue targets, while still maintaining confidence in growth and margin guidance.

Fears that came true

  • Currency fluctuation risk materialized as the actual +1.5% return was far below the +32.2% predicted.
  • Raw-material price pressure and the inability to fully pass on input-cost hikes likely contributed to the disappointing +1.5% actual return versus the +32.2% forecast.
  • Macro challenges in the UK, US, and Middle East exposure appear to have materialized, dragging actual returns to only +1.5%.
  • Supply-chain challenges remained, as reflected in the large shortfall between the predicted +32.2% and actual +1.5%.

Optimism that failed

  • Strong demand across all brands and new product additions did not translate into the forecasted +32.2% return; actual return was only +1.5%.
  • Expected $1 million revenue from new US, Germany, and UK store listings did not materialize enough to support the predicted return.
  • Management confidence in 20% topline growth and 18-20% EBITDA margin in FY25 was not reflected in the actual +1.5% return versus +32.2% predicted.
