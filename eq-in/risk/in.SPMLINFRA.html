SPML Infra — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Legacy order drag: Of the ~Rs 5,094 crore order book as of 30 June 2026, Rs 1,251 crore relates to legacy projects structured on a back-to-back basis with lower margin profiles. The company states that margins will improve only as these legacy projects taper.
  • Legacy debt overhang: The company carries a total debt (inclusive of interest) of ~Rs 700 crore covered by a settlement structure, of which Rs 325 crore has been repaid and Rs 375 crore remains outstanding. The balance repayment is stated to be backed by arbitration awards of Rs 678 crore, with the company expecting ~40% of claims filed (Rs 4,526 crore filed till date) to convert into awards.
  • Execution and working capital risks: The company identifies receivables and working capital risks, financial/leverage obligations tied to the NARCL settlement, new business risks in BESS, and potential litigation and contingent liabilities.
  • External risk factors: Geopolitical disruptions (such as the West Asia conflict affecting crude prices and fund availability), commodity price volatility, evolving regulatory and labour requirements, climate and environmental events affecting project execution, and execution dependency on project fund availability.
  • BESS execution dependencies: The NTPC BESS order is subject to design and drawing approvals, IEC/UL certifications for the battery pack, and NTPC’s contractual approval timelines. Billing to NTPC is targeted for Q4, subject to approval.
  • Audit scope limitations: The limited review reports note that certain joint operations, subsidiaries, associates, and joint ventures were not reviewed by the statutory auditor, with some financial information being management-certified or unavailable; the auditors state these are not material to the statements.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Union Budget 2026–27 allocations: Total water sector outlay of Rs 1,09,029 crore (including Rs 67,670 crore for Jal Jeevan Mission, Rs 8,000 crore for AMRUT 2.0, Rs 5,226 crore for river interlinking and irrigation, and Rs 3,100 crore for the National Ganga Plan). Energy sector allocation includes Rs 1,000 crore Viability Gap Funding for BESS, Rs 1,775 crore for grid-connected solar projects, and customs duty exemptions on BESS capital goods and solar inputs.
  • BESS opportunity: India’s accelerating renewable energy deployment is creating demand for energy storage. The company cites a market potential of 208 GWh by 2030, up from 34.72 GWh in FY27, and a BESS market estimated at ~$8.4 billion growing to 236.22 GWh by 2031–32 (~$57 billion). NTPC is targeting ~22 GWh of BESS capacity by 2032.
  • Landmark order wins: A Rs 1,128 crore BESS contract from NTPC for a 1 GWh (250 MW / 1,000 MWh) Battery Energy Storage System at Barauni Thermal Power Station, Bihar — the company’s first large-scale grid-scale BESS project, with an 18-month delivery period followed by 15 years of O&M. A Rs 165.4 crore order from RRVPNL for a 400 kV grid substation and transmission infrastructure at Kota, Rajasthan.
  • Order inflow: Rs 1,293 crore inflow till June 2026; total new order wins of Rs 5,616 crore across water, irrigation, and power projects, including Jal Jeevan Mission and AMRUT 2.0 projects in Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand, Tamil Nadu, and Chennai.
  • Strategic partnership: An exclusive 10-year partnership with Energy Vault (US) providing access to advanced storage technologies.
  • Manufacturing scale-up: A dedicated BESS manufacturing facility at SUPA MIDC, Pune, with Phase 1 capacity of 2.5 GWh fully ready, targeted to scale to 5 GWh and 600 container units annually by H1 FY28, alongside in-house container manufacturing. The NTPC order ties up ~40% of Phase 1 capacity even before commissioning.
  • Credit profile improvement: CRISIL assigned and ICRA upgraded a rating of BBB (Stable). The company has an Rs 860 crore credit facility enhancement and a surety bond drawl of ~Rs 300 crore.
  • Sectoral outlook: GOI allocated pipeline of Rs 17 lakh crore+ water projects; power investments expected to double to ~Rs 2.3 lakh crore over FY26–FY28.
  • Capital raising: Rs 819 crore of equity raised since 2022 (of which promoters contributed ~Rs 400 crore), with proceeds to be deployed towards Water, Power, and BESS growth, CAPEX, and working capital.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical disruptions affecting crude prices and fund availability.
  • Commodity price volatility.
  • Evolving regulatory and labour requirements.
  • Climate and environmental events affecting project execution.
  • Execution dependency on project fund availability.
  • Receivables and working capital risks.
  • Financial/leverage obligations tied to the NARCL settlement.
  • New business risks in BESS.
  • Potential litigation and contingent liabilities.
  • Dependence on NTPC approval timelines and certification completion for BESS billing.
  • Reliance on arbitration award conversion (~40% of claims expected to convert) to fund remaining debt repayment.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance provided: - FY2026-27 will be principally a year of delivery, focusing on accelerating the renewed water portfolio while maintaining commercial and working-capital discipline. - In BESS, focus on establishing assembly platforms meeting utility-scale safety and quality standards. - Healthy topline growth expected to continue into FY2026-27 and subsequent years, prioritizing profitability and execution discipline over pure order book expansion through fully-funded, high-margin, low-complexity projects under the SPML 2.0 strategy. - New orders expected to carry 10–12% margins with 3–4 year execution speed. - BESS billing to NTPC targeted in Q4, subject to approval. - Capacity scale-up to 5 GWh and 600 container units targeted for H1 FY28. - The company will continue to develop and expand its energy storage technology portfolio; management’s ambition is to build an integrated Indian energy storage platform spanning proprietary product development, manufacturing, system integration, and large-scale project execution.

Observed performance (Q1FY27, quarter ended 30 June 2026): - Revenue from operations: Rs 28,428.11 lakh (Rs 284.28 crore) standalone, versus Rs 15,805.53 lakh in Q1FY26 and Rs 29,050.73 lakh in Q4FY26. - EBITDA: Rs 28.27 crore with 9.90% margin, versus Rs 15.58 crore / 9.49% in Q1FY26 and Rs 24.65 crore / 8.39% in Q4FY26. - PAT: Rs 22.70 crore with 7.95% margin, versus Rs 12.15 crore / 7.40% in Q1FY26 and Rs 21.60 crore / 7.35% in Q4FY26 (PAT excludes a tax reversal of Rs 6.77 crore). - Order book as of 30 June 2026: Rs 5,094 crore, with new orders at 75% (Rs 3,843 crore) and legacy orders at 25% (Rs 1,251 crore). - Order inflow till June 2026: Rs 1,293 crore. - Net worth: ~Rs 999 crore (FY26), with debt-to-equity at 0.34x as of June 2026 (down from 0.40x in FY25 and 0.50x in FY24). - Debt to EBITDA: 3.82x as of June 2026, versus 4.41x in FY25 and 6.52x in FY26. - Current ratio: 1.75x as of June 2026, versus 1.89x in FY25 and 1.90x in FY26. - NARCL debt: Rs 410 crore as of June 2026, versus Rs 381 crore in FY25 and Rs 377 crore in FY26. - ROE: 8% in FY26, 6% in FY25, 4% in FY24. - The company has developed a proprietary 104.4 kWh battery pack under its own intellectual property, which has completed UL9540A, IEC 62619, IEC 63056, IEC 60730, IEC 61000-6-2, IEC 61000-6-4, and UN38.3 testing and certification. - The 45th AGM is scheduled for 25 September 2026, and a virtual Analyst Meet is scheduled for 9 September 2026.
