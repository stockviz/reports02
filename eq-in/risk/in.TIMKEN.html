Timken India — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and challenges

Cost inflation and pass-through limits. Management described continuing cost pressure, with the steel industry pushing through increases of roughly INR1,500–1,600 per ton in January and a further INR3,500 per ton by April — around INR5,000 per ton in total. These are being recovered from customers, with most having started paying, but recovery is uneven: heavy truck and tractor customers have largely absorbed the escalation, while railway contracts are fixed and public sector units and large cement plants operate Annual Rate Contracts that cannot be varied mid-term. Cost escalation in carbide and grinding media, and volatility in base oil used in grease, were also flagged. Management noted steel capacity utilisation remains low, and that further escalation depends on how the geopolitical situation develops.

Railway demand sluggishness. Government railway buying was described as slow, with tenders delayed by a couple of months, which management attributed to funds being diverted toward infrastructure and defence. Rail revenue growth in the quarter was in single digits, around 3%.

Regional export weakness. While the American market was described as resilient and up, Europe was down, South Africa was okay, Australia was flattish, and ASEAN and China were down.

Geopolitical uncertainty. Management characterised geopolitical challenges and war as the “new normal,” noting the situation affects the export outlook even as large projects such as a 300–400 km pipeline in Oman continue.

Cyclical and seasonal mix. The bearing business is cyclical, and Q1 typically comes off a very high Q4. Q1 is also an unfavourable mix quarter from a margin standpoint, in contrast to Q4.

Depreciation drag from new capacity. The prior-year quarter did not carry depreciation on the new capacity investment that has since been capitalised, which affects year-on-year margin comparability.

Legal contingency. The High Court of Gujarat at Ahmedabad, in its 17 June 2026 judgment (received by the company on 22 June 2026), upheld the Labour Court’s order in the matter of Antifriction Bearings Corporation Limited (amalgamated with Timken India Limited) vs Anil Ambalal Modi. The court directed payment of regular wages from January 2005 until the workman attained superannuation, at the salary applicable to Account Clerks, with adjustments for amounts already paid, to be computed and paid within six weeks of receipt of the order. The company stated it is in the process of determining the financial impact and is likely to file an appeal.

Organisational restructuring. Three senior management persons — Mr. Vijay Pratap Singh, Mr. Gajanan Bidkar and Mr. Tarun Beniwal — ceased to be part of Senior Management Persons on 4 August 2026 due to organisational restructuring, while continuing in employment with the company.

2. Tailwinds and growth prospects

Strong FY26 base and steady FY27 start. FY26 closed with the highest stand-alone revenue of INR3,147 crores, with a Q4 above INR1,000 crores. Q1 FY27 stand-alone revenue was INR929 crores, almost 15% growth year-on-year, with PBT of INR150 crores versus INR130 crores, PAT of INR115 crores, and EBITDA margin of 19.6%. Consolidated revenue was INR943 crores, consolidated profit INR156 crores and consolidated net profit INR119 crores. PBT margin was 16.2% against 16.1% in Q1 FY26, achieved despite higher depreciation from capitalised capacity.

Segment performance. Rail was INR200 crores (22% of revenue), mobile others INR184 crores, distribution approximately INR154 crores, process INR186 crores, and exports almost INR200 crores with export incentives at about 1% of the total. Process grew nearly 30% year-on-year, driven largely by wind — both gearbox exports from India and domestic wind mill and wind farm additions — with some contribution from metal customers and project business rather than only MRO. Exports grew about 21%, driven by intercompany taper supply into a resilient American market.

Bharuch ramp-up. The new Bharuch plant is ramping progressively, described as one of the fastest ramps management has seen, with top-quality output. Q1 revenue from the facility was approximately INR50 crores, growing every quarter and close to breakeven. Spherical roller bearing utilisation was close to 40–45% in the last quarter, expected to ramp to 70% in the August–September timeframe, while the CRB line is lower and expected to ramp toward the end of Q2 and into Q3. SRB revenue currently exceeds cylindrical roller bearings at Bharuch.

Capacity investments on track. Rail expansion at Jamshedpur and plain bearings at Bharuch are on track, with commercial production from the rail investment expected around the calendar year-end, after which it can be ramped up, also serving rail markets in other parts of the world. Capex is expected in the previously indicated range of 8–10% of sales, with possible timing differences between financial years but projects broadly on track.

Certification and structural initiatives. BIS certification was secured during the quarter for CRB and CRB rollers and TRB rollers. The scheme of amalgamation of Timken GGB Technology Private Limited with Timken India Limited has been approved by the Board and is now with the NCLT Bangalore Bench; the merger is expected to drive synergies, efficiencies and lower overall cost.

Global 80/20 strategy. The parent’s 80/20 portfolio approach — including divestment of the belts business and indications regarding the automotive OE business — was discussed. Management said the strategy is a performance enhancer that should help India through improved service levels, speed, cost reduction, consolidation, made-to-forecast versus made-to-order decisions, greater use of distribution for small customers, and digitisation. On India specifically, mobile is almost 20% of the market, focused on tractors and heavy trucks; the company does not play passenger cars, two-wheelers, three-wheelers or washing machine ball bearings, and remains focused on off-highway equipment such as backhoes and excavators, and on leadership in rail freight applications.

LPG-to-natural-gas conversion. The company converted its plants and supply chain from LPG to natural gas at what management described as record speed, addressing part of the cost pressure already in the system.

3. Key risks

  • Input cost escalation beyond current levels, contingent on how the geopolitical situation and war play out, with base oil in grease identified as the currently volatile input.
  • Inability to pass through costs in tender-based, fixed-contract and Annual Rate Contract customer segments, including railways, public sector units and large cement plants.
  • Railway procurement timing risk, with government buying slow and tenders delayed due to fund diversion toward infrastructure and defence.
  • Export demand concentration and regional weakness, with Europe, ASEAN and China down and geopolitics affecting the outlook, partly offset by US strength.
  • Tariff uncertainty on US exports. Management could not recall the exact applicable tariff, noting it had historically moved between roughly 5.6% and 5.8% and subsequently up and down, while noting China faces materially higher taper tariffs than India.
  • Legal exposure from the Gujarat High Court order, with financial impact not yet determined and an appeal likely.
  • Execution and approval risk in the Bharuch ramp-up, given that bearing plants are tool- and customer-specific and require customer approvals, with transitional time between PPAP production and delivery.
  • Portfolio strategy read-through from the parent’s 80/20 actions, including the belts divestment and indicated automotive OE divestment, though management stated India remains focused on its existing off-highway and rail applications.

4. Management guidance versus observed performance

Capex. Guidance of 8–10% of sales for FY27 was reiterated, with plant-wise breakup not disclosed. Management stated projects are almost on track, with possible timing differences spilling between financial years.

Bharuch utilisation. Management had previously indicated ramp-up to 70% in the August–September timeframe. Observed: SRB utilisation was close to 40–45% in the last quarter, with CRB lower and expected to ramp toward the end of Q2 and into Q3. Q1 revenue from the plant was approximately INR50 crores, growing every quarter and close to breakeven.

Bharuch commercial supplies. Management had previously commented that PPAP and commercial supplies for additional SKUs would progress around July–August. Observed: PPAP production is increasing every week, with revenues growing weekly and the ramp described as among the fastest seen.

Cost pass-through. Management had indicated in Q4 that about 10% of the required price hike had been taken against total cost inflation in grinding, coolants and similar inputs. Observed in Q1: management said it is difficult to state an exact percentage because behaviour differs by customer and input, but heavy truck and tractor customers have largely absorbed the escalation, while railway, PSU and cement ARC contracts will only reflect increases as new contracts are made. Gross margin was 39.9% in Q1, up 100 basis points year-on-year and broadly flat sequentially despite Q1 being an unfavourable mix quarter versus Q4 — which management attributed to the price increases obtained from heavy truck and off-highway customers.

Rail investment timing. Management stated the rail investment will begin commercial production by the calendar year-end, after which it will be ramped up immediately, also serving rail markets outside India.

Merger. The amalgamation of Timken GGB Technology Private Limited with Timken India Limited has moved from Board approval to being with the NCLT Bangalore Bench, consistent with the stated intent to drive synergies, efficiencies and lower overall cost.

Manufactured versus traded mix. Management indicated the mix remains in a similar range to prior quarters, at approximately 75/25, without disclosing exact figures.

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from a defensive Sell rooted in sustained export weakness, sluggish domestic revenue growth and MHCV cyclicality to a Buy that acknowledged persistent export/domestic/geopolitical risks but emphasised demand recovery, capacity expansion, localisation and margin improvement. Export and domestic slowdown themes persisted from the first report to the last, but were increasingly treated as transient headwinds rather than reasons to avoid the stock. The early bearish call was wrong as the stock gained, yet later positive calls also initially disappointed, showing the narrative became more bullish while near-term returns stayed weak.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning of sustained export decline materialised: the last report still flags slowdown in exports and geopolitical uncertainties weighing on exports, and the intervening Add call produced a -27.5% disappointment.
  • The early concern about inadequate domestic revenue growth materialised: the last report flags a slowdown in domestic industrial segments, coinciding with negative actual returns after subsequent Buy calls.
