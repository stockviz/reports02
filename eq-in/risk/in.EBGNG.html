GNG Electronics (EBGNG) — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Memory and component cost inflation. Management flagged that memory prices rose a further 5–10% in the quarter, with 8GB DDR5 at $126 and 16GB DDR5 at $231 as of June 30, 2026. Sharad Khandelwal corrected his own opening remark during the call, clarifying that DDR prices have risen nearly fivefold since October 2025 rather than doubling. He noted expert expectations of continued 5–10% quarterly increases, with no meaningful relief expected before end-2027.

Rising new-PC prices suppressing new-device demand. The price of an entry-level 8GB RAM / 512GB SSD laptop rose from ₹40,000 to ₹48,000. IDC now projects global PC shipments to decline 11.3% for full-year 2026, with a 20% year-on-year decline in the second half — a unit decline of roughly 30 million in two quarters.

Procurement-side pressure. Management acknowledged that rising new-device costs may cause enterprises to defer purchases or extend leases, potentially shrinking the overall disposition pool. Sharad noted that used-device prices are trailing rather than moving in tandem with new prices, and that the company must pre-empt these conditions by expanding buying partners and purchasing in advance.

Working capital intensity and higher net debt. Net debt rose by roughly ₹100 crore, from about ₹399–400 crore at end-March 2026 to ₹406 crore. Inventory stood at about ₹700 crore at end-Q1, down from roughly ₹740 crore at year-end. Management described the business as procurement-heavy and working-capital-heavy by design, with no significant fixed capital.

Opex running ahead of revenue. Other expenses rose about 68% year-on-year, which management attributed partly to incentivisation for better realisation and sales. Employee costs also increased. Management said hiring runs ahead of productivity because new production staff require a training curve in a niche industry.

Seasonality and growth deceleration. Q1 was described as a seasonally low quarter. Revenue growth of 32% year-on-year compared with roughly 40% in the prior two quarters, and revenue declined 36.7% sequentially from ₹651.7 crore in Q4 FY26.

Geographic and channel concentration considerations. India contributed 36% of revenue, the Middle East 12%, the US 24%, Europe 23%, and others 5% — meaning roughly 59% of revenue came from the US and Europe combined. India standalone gross margin was 21% versus close to 30% for overseas operations.

Middle East situation. Management was asked about regional disruptions and stated there was no disruption to UAE facilities, with shipments continuing by air and employee count rising.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Structural shift toward refurbished computing. Management framed the opportunity as structural rather than cyclical, citing used-car sales at more than twice new-car sales and refurbished smartphones moving from niche to meaningful share as precedents for PCs.

Affordability gap driving demand. As new devices become less affordable, management said consumers and enterprises are turning to premium refurbished devices with warranty. Sharad emphasised the proposition remains relevant even if memory prices were to correct.

Large untapped computing market. India has roughly 1 billion mobile users but an installed PC base of only 55–60 million. Management cited approximately 300 million students in India and close to 250 million in Africa, most without computer access, plus demand from developed-market students needing high-powered machines for AI workloads.

Enterprise and education demand. Businesses facing technology cost pressure are positioned as a core segment, served by devices with 1–3 year warranties (3-year replacement warranty in India, 1-year internationally).

Distribution expansion. The company held dealer meets in Surat, Chennai, Pune, Mumbai, Lucknow, Delhi, and Nagpur; launched the “EB Elite” program and “Chai Pe Charcha” initiatives; and cited its strategic partnership with Redington Limited as a milestone in India’s organised distribution network. Management referenced Redington as India’s number one distributor, Ingram as number two, and Supertron as approximately fifth.

Global footprint growth. As of end-Q1 FY27, Electronics Bazaar supplies 49 countries (up from 46 at end-FY26), with 5,130 customer touchpoints, 773 suppliers, and 2,420 employees (up from 2,148).

Pricing headroom versus new devices. Sharad stated refurbished products are currently pegged at around 30% of new-device prices, versus roughly 50% in refurbished phones and cars, and described closing that gap as a margin lever as trust grows.

Strategic inventory as a competitive moat. Management described holding 30–40 days of finished inventory to enable instant delivery of single models, citing a 3,000-laptop single order from an Australian customer and a potential ~60,000 desktop opportunity from a large US bank as examples where inventory control confers pricing power.

3. Key Risks

  • Memory price trajectory uncertainty. Experts reportedly hold differing views on how far prices will rise; management stated prices are not coming down to old levels.
  • Shrinking disposition pool. Deferred enterprise purchases and extended leases could reduce available refurbished supply.
  • Margin mix dependence on overseas operations. Overseas gross margins near 30% versus 21% in India mean blended margins are sensitive to geographic mix.
  • Working capital and balance sheet strain. Management acknowledged the tension between fiscal prudence and business requirements, stating it will “strike a balance” while continuing to hold elevated inventory when opportunities justify it.
  • No peer comparison. Management noted the absence of listed peers as a disadvantage requiring heavier spend on visibility and marketing.
  • Execution risk in expansion. Continued hiring, training, and channel investment are required ahead of productivity gains.
  • Legal and governance items. The company approved material Related Party Transactions for FY 2026-27 subject to shareholder approval, reappointed Ajay Pancholi as Non-Executive Non-Independent Director, appointed M/s. N N J & Co. as Secretarial Auditor for FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31, and scheduled its 20th AGM for September 24, 2026.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Guidance raised. Management revised revenue guidance upward from 25% to 30%, and PAT margin growth guidance from 0.5% to 0.75%–1%. Sharad stated the company is “comfortably ahead of our FY27 guidance” and characterised the approach as under-promising and over-delivering.

Gross margin expanded to 24.65%, up 329 bps year-on-year and 542 bps sequentially. Revenue growth of 32.1% exceeded the prior 25% guidance, and PAT growth of 56.2% exceeded the prior 0.5% PAT margin growth guidance.

Operational parameters versus prior periods: Total laptops were 1,08,000 units and desktops/others 42,000 units, with laptops contributing 81% of revenue. Unit growth was approximately 18% against 32% value growth. Laptop ASP was ₹30,763 versus ₹27,500 a year earlier (about 12% increase) and ₹30,000 sequentially (about 2.5% increase). Desktop/other ASP was ₹19,326 versus approximately ₹17,500 a year earlier (about 10% increase) and ₹19,090 sequentially (about 1.5% increase).

Sequential comparison: Revenue declined 36.7% from ₹651.7 crore in Q4 FY26, EBITDA declined 17.3% from ₹64.0 crore, and PAT declined 31.3% from ₹42.1 crore. Management attributed this to Q1 seasonality.

Tax guidance: Management guided to an effective tax rate of 10–12% on a consolidated basis for FY27; Q1 tax expense was ₹6.8 crore on PBT of ₹35.7 crore.

Forward-looking priorities: Management stated priorities remain disciplined execution, sustainable growth, steadily improving profitability, and long-term value creation, leveraging refurbishment capabilities across India, the UAE, and the USA with an expanding sourcing network and distribution strength. Management declined to give specific guidance on India blended gross margin or on the contribution expected from Redington, Supertron, and Ingram over the next 12–18 months.
