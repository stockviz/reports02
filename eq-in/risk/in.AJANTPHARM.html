AJANTPHARM: Q1 FY2027 Corporate Announcements Summary

Headwinds and Challenges

Asia branded generics decline. The Asia branded generics segment fell 16% year-on-year in Q1 FY2027, from ₹304 crore to ₹255 crore. The investor presentation attributes this to the continuing impact of war in the region, describing Asia as “war impacted continues.”

Cardiology underperformance in India. Within the India branded generics business, cardiology grew 9% against IPM growth of 15%, making it the only major therapy listed where Ajanta lagged the Indian Pharmaceutical Market. Ophthalmology (15% vs 10%), dermatology (14% vs 8%), and pain management (15% vs 10%) all exceeded IPM.

Forex drag on reported EBITDA. Foreign exchange losses of ₹30.59 crore were recorded in other expenses for Q1 FY2027, alongside forex gains of ₹50.47 crore in other income. Reported EBITDA of ₹424 crore (26% of revenue) was lower than adjusted EBITDA of ₹454 crore (28% of revenue), which excludes the forex loss.

Tax search proceeding. The auditor’s limited review report draws attention to a search conducted on 6 April 2026 for the block period 1 April 2019 to 17 November 2025. The consequent impact on the consolidated financial results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 is stated as currently not ascertainable pending communication from authorities. The auditor’s conclusion is not modified in respect of this matter.

Subsidiary losses. Three subsidiaries whose interim financial results were not reviewed reflected total revenues of ₹94.05 crore and a net loss after tax of ₹18.92 crore for the quarter, before consolidation adjustments.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Broad-based revenue growth. Revenue from operations rose 25% to ₹1,626 crore in Q1 FY2027 from ₹1,303 crore in Q1 FY2026. Profit after tax rose 31% to ₹334 crore, with PAT margin at 21%. ROCE stood at 37% and RONW at 28%.

US Generics outperformance. US generic revenue grew 57% to ₹487 crore from ₹310 crore. Three ANDA approvals were received and two products launched in the quarter, taking total commercialized ANDAs to 51, with 17 awaiting US FDA approval and 5 tentative approvals. The filing target is 8–12.

Africa strength. Africa branded generics grew 30% to ₹295 crore, and the Africa Institution business grew 83% to ₹70 crore.

India branded generics momentum. India revenue grew 24% to ₹509 crore. Per IQVIA MAT June 2026, Ajanta’s India branded generic performance exceeded IPM growth by 36%, with new launches exceeding IPM by 76% and volumes by 40%. Eight new launches occurred in Q1. The portfolio is 65% chronic, with 3,750+ medical representatives, 17 brands above ₹25 crore, and 53% of sales from the top 10 brands.

Pipeline and launches. Six new launches in emerging markets during Q1 FY2027, with a healthy pipeline of product registrations across diabetes, ophthalmology, gynaecology, antibiotics, cardiology, pain, dermatology, and antimalarial therapies. The company holds top-5 positions in major emerging markets.

Shareholder returns. The Board approved a first interim dividend for FY2027 of ₹32 per share on face value of ₹2, amounting to approximately ₹400 crore (₹399.79 crore per the financial results notes). Record date is 5 August 2026, with payment on or after 18 August 2026. The company notes a three-year Revenue and PAT CAGR of 14%.

Strategic priorities. Management lists new product launches across markets, market share gains in existing products, field force productivity, new countries and therapies, cost optimization, and digitalization across functions.

Key Risks

  • Geopolitical disruption in Asia, which management states continues to impact the Asia branded generics segment.
  • Regulatory and approval uncertainty in the US generics business, with 17 ANDAs pending approval and 5 tentative approvals.
  • Forex volatility, evidenced by the swing between forex gains in other income and forex losses in other expenses, which affects reported EBITDA relative to adjusted EBITDA.
  • Tax authority search outcome, with the financial impact for the quarter not currently ascertainable.
  • Concentration in branded generics, which represents two-thirds of the business, with India, Asia, and Africa as the three regions.
  • Dependence on new launches for India growth, given that new launches exceeded IPM by 76% while overall India growth exceeded IPM by 36%.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Dividend. The Board approved a first interim dividend of ₹32 per share (₹400 crore) for FY2027, consistent with the company’s stated focus on earnings and pay-out. The AGM on 16 July 2026 confirmed the interim dividend for FY2025, with 99.9992% of votes polled in favour.

US filing target. Management targets 8–12 ANDA filings. In Q1 FY2027, no ANDAs were filed, while 3 approvals were received and 2 products launched. The 8–12 target remains for the full year.

R&D spending. R&D expense rose to ₹66 crore in Q1 FY2027 from ₹56 crore in Q1 FY2026, representing 4% of revenue.

Growth trajectory. The company describes Q1 FY2027 as an “excellent performance” and a “great beginning,” with revenue up 25% and PAT up 31%. The investor presentation frames the quarter as “performance at best” and notes the company’s performance is “among best in industry.” The three-year Revenue and PAT CAGR of 14% provides context for the current quarter’s higher growth rates.

Segment mix. Branded generics India and Africa grew exceptionally, while Asia declined and the US generics and Africa Institution businesses delivered strong growth. The overall result reflects this mixed segment performance, with total revenue growth of 25% achieved despite the Asia decline.

Capital structure. The paid-up capital increased to ₹24,99,18,048 consisting of 12,49,59,024 equity shares of ₹2 each following the allotment of 23,400 shares upon exercise of options on 30 July 2026.

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from a cautious post-4QFY23 stance—subdued 17% EBITDA margin, high-cost inventory, forex losses, and US/export weakness—to a confident FY27 outlook of high-teen revenue growth and ~27% EBITDA margin. India branded strength, Africa recovery, and free cash generation remained anchors, while US price erosion, African tender lumpiness, and export branded volatility stayed recurring caveats. The rating remained Buy throughout, with the last report adding new levers like GLP-1 launches and a ₹1,000 crore domestic war chest.

Fears that came true

  • US generics price erosion did not abate; the latest report still guided only mid-single-digit US growth due to price erosion and increased competition.
  • Africa tender business remained lumpy, exactly as flagged in the first report.
  • Margin/cost pressures recurred—higher other expenditure, raw material cost escalation, and a ₹31 crore forex loss caused a 92bps YoY EBITDA margin decline.
  • Export branded weakness resurfaced, with Asia branded sales down 16% YoY due to shipment deferrals tied to Middle East geopolitical issues.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that US price erosion had stabilised and would be offset by 5–6 new FY24 launches failed: the latest report pushed new US launches to Q4FY27 while still citing erosion/competition.
  • The broad export branded recovery did not fully materialise, as Asia branded revenues declined 16% YoY even though Africa rebounded strongly.
