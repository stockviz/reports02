SG Finserve: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Geopolitical uncertainty affecting business momentum. Management acknowledged that geopolitical uncertainty is a reality. While it has not translated into credit cost pressure, it has affected business because incremental working capital requirements from traders and end users have come down. In response, the company is resorting to acquiring new customers and expanding into new products and geographies.

Growth normalization after a front-loaded quarter. Management indicated that the 27% quarter-on-quarter growth seen in Q1 FY27 will not be maintained, partly because ₹337 crores of equity was received toward the end of March and the beginning of April, requiring normalization of its impact. Management suggested quarterly AUM growth of around 15–16% and PAT growth of around 8–10% as more realistic run rates.

Conservative stance despite a small base. When questioned on why guidance remains at 25–30% AUM CAGR despite an 82% year-on-year growth rate and a small base, management cited the need to build net worth to a level that can absorb sudden shocks, and stated it would rather be conservative and sustain growth.

Insurance broking timeline pushed out. The insurance broking subsidiary is not expected to launch before Q4 FY27, with management explicitly stating it does not envisage it in Q2 or even Q3, given the need for IRDAI licensing, team, infrastructure, processes and policies.

GIFT City venture subject to dual approvals. The proposed Finance Company in GIFT City is a long-term plan subject to dual regulatory approval from RBI and IFSCA.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Record quarterly and loan book performance. Q1 FY27 delivered the highest ever quarterly PBT of ₹72 crores (27% QoQ growth) and a record loan book of ₹4,552 crores (16% QoQ, 82% YoY). PAT stood at ₹53.68 crores, up 119% YoY and 27% QoQ. Net worth was ₹1,539 crores with leverage of 2.2x and capital adequacy of 32%.

Digital lending partnership. On July 20, 2026, SG Finserve announced a digital lending solution with BharatPe Money (Resilient Digi Services Private Limited) as Lending Service Provider and Succesship Technologies as technology partner, aimed at expanding formal credit access for merchants through a fully digital, paperless process built in accordance with RBI Digital Lending Guidelines.

Factoring and TReDS commercialization. The company became one of less than 1% of financial institutions in India offering Factoring and TReDS solutions, commercialized during March and April. Factoring outstanding rose to ₹225 crores in June from ₹175 crores in March. Factoring is around 5% of total AUM.

Product and geographic expansion. Management outlined plans to launch LAP and digital lending programs, expand from Tier 1 to Tier 2 cities, and pursue cross-sell opportunities. Five new anchor mandates were acquired in Q1, taking total anchor mandates to 52 with ₹7,700 crores of MOUs signed.

Adjacent financial services build-out. The board granted in-principle approval to evaluate acquisition of 51% stake in Succesship Technologies for a maximum investment of ₹20 crores, subject to due diligence and valuation. A wholly owned subsidiary, SG Alternative Investment Fund Limited, was incorporated on July 16, 2026 to act as Investment Manager and/or Sponsor for a Category III AIF. SG Insurance Brokers Limited was also incorporated as a wholly owned subsidiary.

Target market opportunity. Management estimated the target market at approximately ₹25 lakh crores, based on receivables on the balance sheets of the top 1,000 corporates, focusing on unaddressed and untapped channel financing and factoring opportunities.

Key Risks

Credit risk in a lending business. While the company maintains nil NPAs, management stated it cannot be sure of maintaining this in future, noting that accidents and losses may occur in the lending business. The stated aspiration and guidance remain nil NPAs.

Concentration in the APL Apollo ecosystem. Around one-third of AUM comes from the APL Apollo ecosystem, with the stated steady-state mix being one-third APL Apollo supply chain, one-third non-APL Apollo working capital, and one-third beyond supply chain.

Dependence on anchor relationships and MOU conversion. Management noted that MOU numbers do not provide visibility on the actual book or AUM and may not translate into actual business.

Regulatory dependencies. The GIFT City entity requires dual approval from RBI and IFSCA; the insurance broking business requires IRDAI licensing; and the Succesship acquisition remains subject to due diligence, independent valuation and final board approval.

Key managerial transition. Kush Mishra resigned as Company Secretary and Compliance Officer effective close of business on July 18, 2026, due to personal reasons. Ankit Sharma was appointed as Company Secretary, KMP and Compliance Officer effective July 19, 2026.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Q1 FY27 performance versus stated objectives. Management’s stated RoA guidance range is 4.5%–5.0%; Q1 FY27 annualized RoA came in at 5.1%. RoE guidance is 14%–16%; Q1 FY27 annualized RoE was 14%, having expanded from 12% in FY26. Cost/Income guidance is 13%–17%; Q1 FY27 cost-to-income was reported at 7%, which management attributed to the growing asset base and said should be viewed as approximately 1% of average book. Nil NPAs were maintained in Q1 FY27.

FY27 PBT guidance. Management stated it looks to achieve PBT of ₹300 crores in FY27, which it said translates to around ₹225 crores of PAT and an equity base of approximately ₹1,700 crores at FY27 close. Q1 FY27 PBT was ₹71.6 crores.

Longer-term AUM and PAT guidance. Management guided to 25–30% AUM CAGR and 30–35% PAT CAGR over three to four years, with a target of ₹10,000 crores AUM by FY30 (excluding FY26). This compares with the Q3 FY26 investor presentation guidance of ₹7,500 crores AUM and ₹375 crores PAT for FY30, which management acknowledged as an increase in guidance.

Leverage and RoE trajectory. Management intends to transition from 2x leverage toward 3x over about two years, which it expects to move RoE from the present 14% toward 16%. No equity raise is planned in FY27, with management stating it does not need to raise equity.

Yield stability. Management stated average yield on overall AUM remains around 12.5%, unchanged across Q3, Q4 and Q1, with factoring neither diluting nor enhancing portfolio yield.

Disbursement velocity. Management noted disbursements of more than ₹7,000 crores were made to build an AUM of ₹4,552 crores, reflecting the high-churn nature of supply chain finance.
