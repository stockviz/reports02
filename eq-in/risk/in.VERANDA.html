Veranda Learning Solutions — Q1 FY27 Announcements Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin pressure despite revenue growth. Revenue from operations grew 42% year-on-year to about INR 150 crores in Q1 FY27, but reported EBITDA grew only 10% year-on-year to INR 54 crores. Management attributed this to two factors: a one-time other income of INR 17 crores in Q1 FY26 (from remeasurement of financial liabilities under Ind AS) that did not recur, and deliberate spending in Q1 FY27 on advertising and marketing to establish the commerce vertical as a standalone brand ahead of its demerger and listing, plus initial costs for adding 15 managed commerce colleges. Excluding the prior-year one-time item, management stated EBITDA growth would have been about 30%.

Sequential margin erosion. An investor noted that EBITDA margin (excluding other income) has drifted from 36.44% in September 2025 to 35.96%, then 35.5%, and 34.71% in the current quarter, against full-year guidance implying roughly 38.8%. Management attributed the decline to expansion-related spending (college additions, advertising) being expensed in the respective quarters, with benefits expected in later quarters.

Regional concentration. Management explicitly flagged the need to reduce regional concentration, noting the plan to establish offline presence in North and West India (UP, Bihar, Rajasthan, Gujarat) to unlock an underserved student base.

K-12 scale gap. Management acknowledged that managing six to seven schools does not place the company in the top-10 in the K-12 leaderboard, and expected K-12 to attract lower multiples than the commerce or government test prep verticals.

Demerger timeline slippage. The demerger process has taken longer than originally anticipated. The Board approved the composite scheme in late 2025; NOC from exchanges came in January 2026; the NCLT hearing on July 20, 2026 concluded with orders reserved; a further hearing was scheduled for August 17, 2026, with the company expecting completion in the first half of September 2026. An investor explicitly noted the delay was on the part of the court.

Debt overhang. Total debt stands at INR 125 crores in the commerce entity and INR 145 crores in the non-commerce entity. Management noted that acquisitions were funded through structured debt that was “quite pricey,” with the cost of debt reduced from 17.5% to about 9%–9.5% through refinancing. Sustainable quarterly finance cost is guided at INR 7.5–8 crores.

Investor scepticism on guidance delivery. An investor questioned why this cycle’s growth expectations would be met when similar expectations had been communicated earlier. Management responded that the prior period involved acquisitions, consolidation, deleveraging and refinancing, and that consistent delivery is now building confidence.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Structural sector tailwinds. Management cited growing emphasis on employability-linked learning, rising aspiration for competitive government examinations, deeper digital-first delivery penetration, and increasing willingness of students and parents to invest in outcome-oriented programs across commerce, academics and test prep.

Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Revenue grew 42% year-on-year to about INR 150 crores; PAT rose 472% year-on-year to INR 34 crores from INR 5.9 crores, marking the sixth consecutive PAT-positive quarter. Enrollments grew 35% year-on-year to about 1.03 lakh students; collections grew 27% year-on-year.

Segment momentum. - Commerce Test Prep: revenue INR 108.6 crores, up 53% year-on-year; EBITDA INR 42.7 crores, up 58% year-on-year; EBITDA margin around 40%. The segment holds the number one market position across CA, CS, CMA, ACCA and other programs, with 105+ centers and 50 new commerce colleges under management in the pipeline. - Government Test Prep: revenue INR 32.5 crores, up 41% year-on-year; EBITDA improved to nearly INR 4 crores from near breakeven in the previous quarter. Growth was driven by new offerings including Group 1 offline programs, Junior IAS, and subscription-based magazines, alongside the RACE platform’s network and a diverse exam portfolio (UPSC, SSC, banking, TNPSC, KPSC and other state PSCs). - Academics/K-12: revenue INR 12.2 crores, up 22% year-on-year; EBITDA INR 9.2 crores, up 53% year-on-year. The managed schools opportunity is described as a large underpenetrated market worth over INR 10 lakh crores across South India; the company currently manages six schools with over 5,400 students on an asset-light end-to-end managed services model.

Commerce Virtuals launch. A live and recorded digital delivery format for Class 11 and 12 students, enabling pan-India reach without additional physical infrastructure.

Demerger as a value-unlock catalyst. Upon completion, JK Shah Commerce Education Limited will be separately listed, with every Veranda shareholder receiving 1 share in the newly listed entity for every 1 share held, at no additional cost. Management expects the commerce entity to command a superior multiple given its leadership position, and stated the demerger enables sharper strategic focus, more efficient capital allocation and long-term value creation. Post-demerger, the commerce business targets significant revenue growth over three to four years, with a long-term aspiration of crossing INR 1,000 crores of revenue by FY30.

SNVA Veranda stake. The shareholding in SNVA Veranda (which houses the divested vocational segment — Edureka, Veranda Higher Ed and Six Phrase) will sit in the non-commerce entity, with management expecting significant value unlocking. SNVA Veranda targets FY27 revenue of INR 250+ crores, EBITDA of INR 50+ crores, a learner base of 2 lakh+ per year, and presence in 60+ countries, with growth outlooks of 25% revenue CAGR and 35% EBITDA CAGR (FY26–FY30).

Strategic priorities for FY27. Four focus areas: expanding government test prep into Karnataka; entering preschool managed operations to deepen the K-12 value chain; expanding the offline commerce college network into 15 new managed locations; and establishing offline presence in North and West India.

Structural margin levers. Lower customer acquisition cost through stronger brand equity and higher organic enrollments (FY26 additional learners up 21% year-on-year); lower finance cost via QIP-led deleveraging (about INR 357 crores raised in July 2025) and debt refinancing (about 67% year-on-year reduction in finance cost, from INR 26.2 crores in Q1 FY26 to INR 8.7 crores in Q1 FY27); and higher earnings quality from focus on profitable segments and better platform utilization. Ad spend as a percentage of revenue improved from 31.2% in FY25 to 4.8% in FY26.

Government test prep ambition. Management is working toward INR 100 crores of EBITDA in this vertical over the next four to five years.

3. Key Risks

Execution risk on expansion. The FY27 guidance depends on adding 15 new managed commerce colleges, increasing student count by 10%, raising ARPU by 7%–8% annually, entering new online markets (Class 11 and 12 commerce, digital aid programs, CMA India), and expanding into Karnataka and North/West India. Management acknowledged that the benefit of Q1 spending will only be visible in subsequent quarters.

Demerger completion risk. Although orders were reserved at the NCLT on July 20, 2026, and the company expressed confidence in completing the process by end of September 2026, the timeline remains subject to NCLT pronouncement, ROC filing, record date and listing procedures. Management stated it would not presuppose the NCLT’s decision.

Valuation and capital allocation risk post-demerger. An investor raised whether placing all non-commerce debt in the residual entity while listing JK Shah debt-free could structurally lower the multiple for the residual business or effectively transfer value to JK Shah shareholders. Management’s response was that the commerce entity, as an undisputed market leader, should command a higher multiple, government test prep should get a good multiple, and K-12 should not get the best multiples.

Tax expense volatility. Q1 FY27 tax expense was negative (a reversal of INR 7.35 crores related to earlier years) due to a merger scheme involving Veranda Administrative and Veranda K-12 subsidiaries, which allowed use of accumulated losses. This is a one-time item and not indicative of the run-rate.

Forward-looking statement risk. The company’s safe harbour notes that statements are subject to known and unknown risks, uncertainties and other factors beyond its control that could cause actual results to differ materially.

Deferred consideration. Management stated there are no deferred consideration payouts due in the next 12–15 months; structured payouts fall in FY28 and beyond, over five to seven years, and are based on respective EBITDA achievements.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

FY27 guidance. Management guided to approximately INR 670 crores in revenue, over INR 250 crores in EBITDA (INR 260 crores in the detailed table), and about INR 140 crores in PAT (INR 144 crores in the detailed table). Within this, commerce is expected to contribute about INR 450 crores revenue, INR 215 crores EBITDA and INR 110 crores PAT; non-commerce about INR 220 crores revenue, INR 45–46 crores EBITDA and INR 34 crores PAT. This compares to FY26 actuals of INR 482 crores revenue, INR 204 crores EBITDA and INR 130 crores PAT.

Q1 FY27 performance against guidance. Management stated the quarter closed “in line of guidance.” Q1 FY27 revenue of INR 150 crores represents about 22% of the full-year revenue guidance. The segment-wise quarterly mix table indicates commerce is expected to contribute 21% of its annual revenue in Q1 (actual), with 27% in Q2, 26% in Q3 and 27% in Q4; non-commerce is expected to contribute 24% in Q1 (actual), 36% in Q2, 22% in Q3 and 18% in Q4.

EBITDA margin trajectory. Management guided to roughly 38.8% full-year EBITDA margin, while Q1 FY27 reported EBITDA margin was approximately 36% (34.71% excluding other income per an investor’s calculation). Management attributed the gap to front-loaded expansion and advertising spending and stated margins would reach guided levels in Q2 and Q3.

Commerce segment guidance detail. Management indicated the offline/online revenue split for commerce is expected at roughly INR 330 crores offline and INR 120 crores online (about 3:1), with offline EBITDA margins at peak of 35% and online at 45%–48%.

Demerger timeline guidance vs. actual. The Board approved the scheme in September 2025; the scheme was filed with NSE and BSE in September 2025; NOC from exchanges was received in January 2026; the NCLT-directed court convened meeting was held in March 2026 with shareholder approval in April 2026; the second motion was filed with NCLT in April 2026; the NCLT hearing on July 20, 2026 concluded with orders reserved; the next hearing was scheduled for August 17, 2026. Management guided to completion in the first half of September 2026 and listing in September 2026. The NCLT subsequently sanctioned the Composite Scheme of Arrangement on August 20, 2026, with the amalgamation of Veranda XL Learning Solutions Private Limited into Veranda Learning Solutions Limited effective August 31, 2026, and the demerger of the Commerce Business into J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited effective September 1, 2026. The company stated it will separately intimate the Record Date for determining shareholders entitled to receive shares in J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited.

Long-term guidance. Management reiterated the aspiration of crossing INR 1,000 crores of revenue by FY30 for the commerce business, and INR 100 crores of EBITDA for the government test prep vertical over four to five years.
