Headwinds and Challenges

US tariff disruption. The quarter was affected by the US tariff situation, which management said slowed orders in March, April and May. The CFO attributed lower capacity utilisation to “order slowness because of U.S. tariff issue during the month of March, April and May.” Young Brand Apparels, described as a 100% American market business, saw customers hold back purchase orders for one to two months in anticipation of a tariff reversal, with the impact falling in March, April and partly May.

Softer top line. Consolidated revenue was broadly flat year-on-year at INR 401 crores versus INR 403 crores. Standalone adjusted operational revenue declined 7.6% year-on-year to INR 265.9 crores. Garment division adjusted operational revenue fell to INR 337.3 crores from INR 372.9 crores. Young Brand Apparels adjusted operational revenue declined 16.2% year-on-year to INR 71.4 crores, and its export volume slipped sequentially from 5.2 million pieces to 5 million pieces.

Lower capacity utilisation. Utilisation fell to 68.0% in Q1 FY27 from 82.0% in Q1 FY26, even as 750 machines were added in India during the year.

SPUK losses. SPUK revenue more than doubled year-on-year to INR 33.3 crores, but EBITDA was negative at INR 1.04 crores, attributed to small air shipments and the shift of certain shipments into the subsequent period. Air freight cost was around GBP 50,000. Management said that but for the deferred shipment, SPUK would have been EBITDA positive.

Higher finance cost and forex drag. Consolidated finance cost rose to INR 148.6 million from INR 117.8 million year-on-year. The CFO said the quarter included a hit of around INR 1.75 crores from restating INR packing credit due to exchange volatility, and guided to a full-year consolidated interest cost of INR 30–35 crores excluding the exchange loss.

Sri Lanka gestation. The Sri Lanka platform is still absorbing initial pre-operative losses, though management expects the factories to manage themselves by end of March.

US policy uncertainty. Management said customers are not currently expecting the proposed US bill to be a problem, but are cautious about what may happen three to four months out. The order book is covered until January.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Sourcing shift. International brands are diversifying under China Plus-One and Bangladesh Plus-One strategies, with customers evaluating India and Sri Lanka. Bangladesh’s textile sector is described as under pressure from energy shortages, high costs, lower incentives and reduced mill utilisation.

India–UK FTA. The agreement has recently become effective, and customer engagement from the UK has improved. Management said the FTA removes a roughly 12% tariff disadvantage and improves competitiveness against Bangladesh, Pakistan and Cambodia. India is currently the UK’s fourth-largest textile supplier with about 6.1% market share. Three new UK brands have been added to the customer base, and SPUK is adding three more customers, one of which is Marks & Spencer. Management guided SPUK to GBP 13 million plus in three years and GBP 50 million in five years, with a longer-term aspiration of GBP 50–60 million.

EU FTA pipeline. Management expects the EU–India FTA to be signed off by end of the financial year, which would bring more EU business into India.

Customer diversification. The group now operates with over 15 customers, reducing concentration risk. Two of the three new customers added in the quarter are from the UK and one from the EU.

Sri Lanka platform. The dual-country India–Sri Lanka base gives customers sourcing flexibility. Sri Lanka has around 1,650 machines, of which 1,300 are used for exports, and can go up to 2,000. Management guided Sri Lanka revenue of roughly INR 150–200 crores by end of March, booked within S.P. Apparels. Sri Lanka also offers duty-free trade to Europe and the UK, overnight raw material shipments from Tuticorin to Colombo, and labour availability. Management also flagged an asset-light job-work opportunity with customer-approved factories that could add 500–600 machines within a year without investment.

Young Brand Apparels expansion. Production has commenced at the Palladum facility, commercial production from another approved facility is expected in the coming months, and by October all planned units are expected to be in commercial production. A luxury moulded bra line is being added, with orders sufficient for 200 machines, investment starting September–October, full-fledged revenue expected next financial year, and investment capped at around INR 10 crores. Management guided Young Brand revenue to INR 340–350 crores in FY27 from INR 300 crores last year.

Retail turnaround. SP Retail Ventures revenue grew 26.7% year-on-year to INR 18.83 crores, with EBITDA of INR 0.4 crores versus a loss of INR 2.07 crores in Q1 FY26. Angel & Rocket continues to perform well. The company is seeking a strategic investor for the retail business and plans to raise funds from the capital market.

Capacity readiness. Management said capacities are in place for a 10,000-sewing-machine footprint: roughly 6,000 in India, 2,000 in Sri Lanka and 1,750 in Young Brand Apparels, sufficient for the next two to three years. The Sivakasi factory has started, with plans to add about 400 machines over two years. The Salem facility began trial production and is expected to run at planned Phase 1 capacity after Diwali.

Policy support. RoSCTL continuation to March 2026 and the Export Promotion Mission with an outlay of about INR 25,060 crores till FY31 are cited as supporting export competitiveness.

Shareholder returns. The Board recommended a final dividend of INR 3 per share (30% on face value of INR 10) for FY26 and proposed a 1:5 stock split from INR 10 to INR 2 face value, with the stated rationale of enhancing liquidity and encouraging retail participation.

Key Risks

  • US tariff and trade policy uncertainty, including a proposed bill whose passage management cannot rule out, with customers unable to be certain about the next three to four months.
  • Concentration of Young Brand Apparels in the US market, making it directly exposed to US demand and tariff shifts.
  • Execution risk in Sri Lanka, where initial pre-operative losses persist and integration is ongoing.
  • SPUK’s path to sustainable profitability depends on shipment execution and volume scaling; the quarter showed negative EBITDA despite strong revenue growth.
  • Dependence on the EU–India FTA being signed off by end of the financial year for the anticipated EU opportunity.
  • Forex volatility affecting reported finance cost and margins.
  • Capacity utilisation risk: utilisation fell to 68% despite machine additions, and the FY27 revenue guidance requires a materially stronger second half.
  • Order book coverage is limited to roughly four to five months, with November onwards still open.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

FY27 revenue guidance of INR 2,000 crores. Management reiterated confidence in achieving this, stating FY27 growth would be weighted to the second half. Q1 consolidated revenue was INR 401 crores, which management acknowledged leaves the balance to be delivered in H2. Management said Q2 would be better than Q1 on the top line and that what was lost in Q1 would be achieved in H2.

Garment division margin guidance of above 15%. Management guided consolidated garment division EBITDA margin (including Young Brand and Sri Lanka) of above 15% for the full year, and separately guided the garment division at 15%. Observed Q1 performance was higher: garment division adjusted EBITDA margin was 17.6%, standalone adjusted EBITDA margin was 17.5% versus 15.2% a year earlier, and Young Brand adjusted EBITDA margin was 17.7% versus 12.5%. Management said the higher margin reflected improved efficiency and a change in product mix, not one-offs.

Young Brand revenue guidance of INR 340–350 crores. Q1 adjusted operational revenue was INR 71.4 crores, down 16.2% year-on-year, with management attributing the decline to US tariff-related order deferrals and stating the held-back orders are materialising in Q2 and Q3.

Standalone export value guidance of INR 1,300–1,400 crores. Management cited INR 1,100 crores as the prior-year number. Q1 standalone adjusted operational revenue was INR 265.9 crores.

SPUK profitability. Management said EBITDA is sustainable and that but for the deferred shipment the quarter would have been EBITDA positive. Observed Q1 EBITDA was negative INR 1.04 crores.

Sri Lanka revenue of INR 150–200 crores by end of March. Observed Q1 Sri Lanka top-line contribution was INR 25 crores.

Order book. Management cited a total order book of close to INR 550–600 crores, comprising about INR 430 crores for SPAL India, around INR 100 crores for Young Brand Apparels and about INR 70 crores for SPUK. Management said the company is fully booked until October and open for November onwards.
