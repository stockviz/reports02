360 ONE WAM — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Sequential softness in operating profit. Operating Profit Before Tax declined 9.9% quarter-on-quarter to Rs 376 crore in Q1 FY27, even as total revenue rose 11.5% QoQ to Rs 870 crore. Total expenses grew 6.9% QoQ to Rs 446 crore, with employee costs at Rs 328 crore (+28.7% YoY) and admin costs at Rs 118 crore (+22.7% YoY). The cost-to-income ratio stood at 51.3% in Q1 FY27, versus 48.4% in Q1 FY26 and 53.5% in Q4 FY26.

Declining ARR retention. Combined ARR retention fell to 74 basis points in Q1 FY27, from 79 bps in Q1 FY26 and 78 bps in Q4 FY26. Wealth Management retention was 71 bps (versus 78 bps in Q1 FY26 and 74 bps in Q4 FY26), and Asset Management retention was 83 bps (versus 79 bps in Q1 FY26 and 86 bps in Q4 FY26).

Asset Management net outflows. Asset Management ARR net flows were negative at Rs -2,564 crore in Q1 FY27, compared with positive flows of Rs 2,028 crore in Q4 FY26 and Rs 973 crore in Q1 FY26. Discretionary PMS AUM declined 13.9% YoY to Rs 29,835 crore, and Institutional Mandates within Discretionary Portfolio Manager fell from Rs 22,316 crore in Q4 FY26 to Rs 21,599 crore in Q1 FY27.

Carry income volatility. Carry income dropped to Rs 55 crore in Q1 FY27 from Rs 87 crore in Q4 FY26 and Rs 110 crore in Q3 FY26. Within Asset Management, carried interest revenue fell to Rs 32 crore in Q1 FY27 from Rs 49 crore in Q4 FY26.

Transactional/brokerage income decline. Transactional/brokerage income fell 9.5% QoQ to Rs 208 crore, though it rose 37.3% YoY. Within Wealth Management, TBR revenue declined to Rs 208 crore in Q1 FY27 from Rs 230 crore in Q4 FY26, with the UHNI segment falling from Rs 177 crore to Rs 143 crore.

Loss-making segments. The HNI segment reported an operating loss before tax of Rs 13 crore in Q1 FY27 (versus a loss of Rs 17 crore in Q4 FY26), and the Mass Affluent segment reported a loss of Rs 7 crore (versus a loss of Rs 9 crore in Q4 FY26).

Income tax search and demand. The Income Tax Department conducted a search under Section 132 of the Income Tax Act on the Company and one subsidiary during the quarter ended March 31, 2025. During Q1 FY27, the Department issued an order levying tax aggregating Rs 336.14 crore (including surcharge and cess) on the Company and one subsidiary; the standalone order on the Company was Rs 192.42 crore. Appeals have been filed before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals). The Department also issued a notice under Section 158BC on March 17, 2026 initiating block assessment proceedings against one subsidiary. The auditors drew attention to this matter via an Emphasis of Matter paragraph in both the consolidated and standalone review reports.

Standalone profitability decline. Standalone profit after tax fell to Rs 83.49 crore in Q1 FY27 from Rs 112.05 crore in Q1 FY26, and standalone net profit margin declined to 43% from 53%. Standalone other income fell to Rs 121.39 crore from Rs 188.53 crore YoY, with dividend received from subsidiaries at Rs 77.49 crore versus Rs 150.87 crore in Q1 FY26.

Other income volatility. Consolidated other income swung from Rs -55 crore in Q4 FY26 to Rs 47 crore in Q1 FY27, and was Rs 63 crore in Q1 FY26.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong YoY growth in revenue and profit. Total revenue rose 20.0% YoY to Rs 870 crore; revenue from operations rose 24.2% YoY to Rs 822 crore; ARR revenue rose 20.3% YoY to Rs 614 crore; PAT rose 14.8% YoY to Rs 330 crore.

AUM expansion. Total AUM stood at Rs 7,76,755 crore as on June 2026, up 17.0% YoY. ARR AUM rose 19.0% YoY to Rs 3,42,035 crore. Wealth Management AUM rose 18.4% YoY to Rs 6,76,616 crore; Asset Management AUM rose 8.2% YoY to Rs 1,00,139 crore.

Wealth Management momentum. Wealth ARR AUM rose 24.2% YoY to Rs 2,41,896 crore. The 360 ONE Plus proposition grew 39.4% YoY to Rs 99,105 crore; Distribution grew 11.9% YoY to Rs 1,29,849 crore. Wealth ARR net flows were Rs 13,379 crore in Q1 FY27, up from Rs 6,957 crore in Q4 FY26. The lending book grew 67.8% YoY to Rs 12,942 crore.

Alternates growth. Alternative Investment Fund AUM rose 24.7% YoY to Rs 56,294 crore. Private Equity AUM rose from Rs 27,724 crore in Q4 FY26 to Rs 29,494 crore in Q1 FY27; Credit & Real Assets rose from Rs 13,699 crore to Rs 14,575 crore. Mutual Fund AUM rose 9.8% YoY to Rs 14,009 crore.

Client franchise expansion. The company services 8,900+ clients. Clients with AUM of INR 10 crore+ stood at 4,000+ as on June 2026, accounting for ~95% of Wealth AUM (excluding custody). The UHNI segment caters to 4,000+ families with AUM over INR 4,50,000 crore and services 600+ corporate treasuries.

Strategic collaboration with UBS. The company cites an exclusive strategic collaboration with UBS involving bi-directional client referrals, cross-platform product access, and best-practice exchange in Wealth.

Market opportunity. Management states India is witnessing wealth creation through liquidity events, private equity exits, new-economy listings, and the steady financialisation of savings. It notes domestic capital is now scaling into alternatives with real momentum, and that as global investors re-focus on India, 360 ONE Asset is positioned to lead the next phase of growth.

Capital position. Tangible net worth stood at Rs 6,882 crore; tangible return on equity was 19.4%. Net worth (tangible plus goodwill and intangibles) was Rs 9,991 crore. The company declared and paid an interim dividend of Rs 243.78 crore (Rs 6 per share) during Q1 FY27.

Awards. The company cites recognition including India’s Best Wealth Manager at Euromoney Private Banking Awards 2026, Best Private Bank – India at Asian Private Banker’s 5th Awards for Distinction, Best Private Equity House at Global Private Banking Innovation Awards 2026, and Best Private Credit Deal at FinanceAsia Achievement Awards 2025.

3. Key Risks

Tax litigation uncertainty. The Income Tax Department order levying Rs 336.14 crore on the Company and one subsidiary (Rs 192.42 crore standalone) is under appeal. The company states uncertainty exists regarding the outcome, does not believe adjustments are required, and has treated the amount as a contingent liability. A separate Section 158BC block assessment notice dated March 17, 2026 has been issued to one subsidiary, with no outcome communication received as of the financial results date.

Retention pressure. The decline in combined ARR retention to 74 bps and Wealth Management retention to 71 bps represents a risk to recurring revenue growth if sustained.

Asset Management net outflows. Negative ARR net flows of Rs -2,564 crore in Asset Management in Q1 FY27, alongside a 13.9% YoY decline in Discretionary PMS AUM, indicate potential pressure in that segment.

Carry income dependence. Management expects carry income to be ~15% of the overall fee pool. Carry income is inherently lumpy, as evidenced by the swing from Rs 110 crore in Q3 FY26 to Rs 55 crore in Q1 FY27.

Cost inflation. Total costs rose 27.1% YoY, outpacing total revenue growth of 20.0% YoY, compressing operating PBT growth to 21.0% YoY and PAT growth to 14.8% YoY.

Segment losses. HNI and Mass Affluent segments continue to report operating losses before tax.

Regulatory and compliance environment. The company references risk management, compliance, and governance standards as underpinning client trust, in the context of a business subject to SEBI regulations, the Companies Act, and income tax scrutiny.

Forward-looking statement risk. The presentation states it contains forward-looking statements involving known and unknown risks, uncertainties, and other factors beyond the company’s control that could cause actual results to differ materially.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Business Performance

Carry income guidance. Management states it expects carry income to be ~15% of the overall fee pool, with the multi-asset class nature of the alternates business giving it stability. Observed: carry income was Rs 55 crore in Q1 FY27, Rs 87 crore in Q4 FY26, Rs 110 crore in Q3 FY26, Rs 61 crore in Q2 FY26, and Rs 56 crore in Q1 FY26. As a share of ARR revenue, carry was approximately 9% in Q1 FY27 and approximately 18% in Q3 FY26, indicating quarter-to-quarter variability around the stated expectation.

Retention guidance. Management states the combination of Alternates and Public Markets has allowed steady growth with the ability to maintain retentions at 70+ bps. Observed: combined ARR retention was 74 bps in Q1 FY27, 78 bps in Q4 FY26, 81 bps in Q3 FY26, 76 bps in Q2 FY26, and 79 bps in Q1 FY26 — within the stated 70+ bps range but trending toward the lower end in the latest quarter. Asset Management retention was 83 bps in Q1 FY27, above the 70+ bps threshold; Wealth Management retention was 71 bps, at the threshold.

Growth positioning. Management states the company is “well positioned for sustained, profitable growth” as it deepens presence across asset classes, expands geographically, and scales newer businesses. Observed: total revenue grew 20.0% YoY and PAT grew 14.8% YoY in Q1 FY27, while operating PBT declined 9.9% QoQ. The company expanded its subsidiary list during the period, including the acquisition of Quark Solar Private Limited consummated on April 30, 2026 for Rs 9.39 crore, and the addition of 360 ONE Global Asset Management (IFSC) Limited as a step-down subsidiary effective December 23, 2025.

Capital allocation. Management describes a prudent capital allocation strategy led by strong focus on value creation. Observed: the company paid an interim dividend of Rs 6 per share (Rs 243.78 crore) in Q1 FY27; total FY27 dividend till date is Rs 6 per share, versus Rs 12 per share in FY26 and Rs 6 per share in FY25. The company also approved the 360 ONE Employee Stock Appreciation Rights Scheme 2026, proposing to grant up to 11,20,000 ESARs (around 0.28% of paid-up capital) with a 24-month vesting schedule, subject to shareholder approval.

ESAR scheme details. The ESAR price per ESAR is to be determined by the Nomination and Remuneration Committee. The exercise period shall not exceed 36 months from the date of vesting. As of the disclosure date, no ESARs have been granted, vested, or exercised under the scheme. During Q1 FY27, the Nomination and Remuneration Committee did not grant any stock options, and the company allotted 5,46,579 equity shares on exercise of options by employees under existing schemes.

Fund utilization. The company reported no deviation or variation in the utilization of proceeds from preferential issues of warrants dated May 29, 2025 (Rs 97,89,66,568.77, being 25% of warrant issue proceeds) and July 21, 2025 (Rs 527,95,06,792.50, being 25% of warrant issue proceeds). CARE Ratings Limited is the monitoring agency. The Audit Committee and auditors reported nil comments.

ESG rating. ESG Risk Assessments & Insights Limited has assigned a rating of ‘55’ to 360 ONE WAM Limited.

Broker Narrative

Brokers consistently flagged elevated operating costs and competitive intensity as headwinds from the first report through the last, though the specific metrics shifted from HNI hiring costs to a rising cost-to-income ratio and 29% employee cost growth. Tailwinds evolved from broad macro themes like inter-generational wealth transfer and tier-2 expansion toward specific inflow metrics, such as 360 ONE Plus inflows and wealth ARR AUM growth. While the HNI and global platforms remained touted as growth drivers, their timelines stretched, with the HNI platform now expected to break even only by end-FY27. Ultimately, the narrative transitioned from early-stage optimism on new business traction to a more mature focus on structural franchise expansion and eventual operating leverage.

Fears that came true

  • Elevated costs from continued investments in new businesses materialized as a 29% YoY surge in employee costs and a 120bp rise in the cost-to-income ratio, causing a 5% operating profit miss and a disappointing actual return of +3.2% for ICICI Securities.
  • Competitive intensity and a limited talent pool persisted, as newly hired RMs required 24-30 months to reach optimal productivity while costs were incurred upfront, contributing to wealth ARR retention declining to 71bp and disappointing returns like +4.1% vs. +20.2% predicted by Motilal Oswal in Oct 2025.
  • Operating costs from additional ultra HNI headcounts outpaced corresponding revenues, leading to a cost-to-income ratio above estimates and actual returns falling far short of predictions (e.g., +5.0% vs. +21.8% predicted by Motilal Oswal in Jan 2026).

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that the wide product portfolio would counter cyclicality with strong traction in credit products failed, as wealth management ARR retention dropped to 71bp YoY driven specifically by lower lending book retention.
  • Strong growth expectations from the HNI Proposition and Global Business Platform starting FY25 did not materialize on time, with the HNI platform now expected to break even only by end-FY27.
  • Upbeat deal flow and asset management traction failed to prevent asset management net outflows of INR25.6b due to the redemption of a large institutional mandate.
