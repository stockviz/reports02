Arvind Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements: Key Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Elevated and volatile input costs. The quarter was marked by elevated input costs across key raw material categories. In the Textile division, EBITDA margin growth was curtailed by a rise in input cost of approximately ₹19 Cr. Raw material availability and cost escalation remain a concern exerting pressure on margins.
  • Geopolitical and trade disruption. The operating environment was shaped by evolving geo-economic developments, reconfiguration of global trade channels, shifting logistics patterns, and developments in the Middle East influencing sourcing and procurement economics. Geopolitical uncertainties and disruptions in key shipping channels impacted the supply chain.
  • Tariff-related pressure. Residual tariff-related pricing pressures remained a drag on margins, although tariff-related pressures in the USA eased during the quarter.
  • Working capital build. Higher procurement of raw material in anticipation of price escalation led to higher working capital levels.
  • Acquisition-related cost drag. The Dalco-GFT consolidation added ₹10.7 Cr of depreciation from amortisation of intangible assets, ₹10.6 Cr of incremental finance cost on the US$110 Mn acquisition loan, and ~₹23 Cr (net of tax) of one-time transaction expenses as an exceptional item. Dalco-GFT’s EBITDA margin of 15.1% for the ~1.8-month consolidation period was impacted by a sharp increase in raw material costs.
  • Balance sheet leverage. Closing net debt stood at ₹2,158 Cr versus ₹1,172 Cr as of March 31, 2026, driven by debt taken for the Dalco-GFT acquisition.
  • Revenue mix moderation. Garmenting revenue growth was moderated by a higher contribution from value-segment products despite 13% volume growth; management notes product mix changes may alter short-term revenue growth.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record consolidated performance. Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue was ₹2,501 Cr (up 25% YoY) and EBITDA ₹258 Cr (up 39% YoY), with EBITDA margin improving ~104 bps to 10.3%. PAT before exceptional items was ₹80 Cr (up 47% YoY). Excluding Dalco-GFT, revenue and EBITDA were ₹2,344 Cr and ₹234 Cr, up 17% and 26% respectively.
  • Volume momentum across textiles. Denim fabric volumes reached 17.5 Mn metres — the highest in sixteen quarters — up 34%, driven by higher verticalisation. Woven fabric volumes were 31.2 Mn metres, up 7%, despite a seasonally weaker quarter. Garmenting volumes crossed 11 Mn pieces for the first time, up 13%.
  • Advanced Materials Business (AMB) strength. AMB reported its highest-ever quarterly revenue of ₹650 Cr (up 85%) and EBITDA of ₹97 Cr (up 115%), at a 15% margin. India AMB grew 40% to ₹493 Cr revenue with ₹74 Cr EBITDA, supported by Human Protection and Composites. Human Protection benefited from normalisation of defence procurement in India and easing US tariff pressures; Composites saw momentum from renewable energy and mobility applications; the Industrial segment achieved early double-digit growth with capacity expansion due from Q2.
  • Dalco-GFT acquisition. Arvind acquired a ~61% majority stake in Dalco-GFT (USA) — its largest-ever acquisition, at an Enterprise Value of US$136 Mn — adding four segments (Automotive, Geotextiles, Industrial, Furniture & Furnishings) and a TAM of ~US$2.5 Bn in the largest technical textile market. It contributed ₹157 Cr revenue and ₹24 Cr EBITDA in ~1.8 months; on a full-quarter basis, revenue was ₹244 Cr and EBITDA ₹40 Cr (16.3% margin).
  • International expansion. A London (UK) office and representative network were established to strengthen customer engagement in overseas markets; a partnership with a Bangladesh garment manufacturer was also noted.
  • UK-India FTA. Its going live is viewed as a positive trigger to unlock fresh demand shifts to India, alongside improving domestic demand and an early festival season.
  • Balance sheet reinforcement. A ₹500 Cr Qualified Institutions Placement (QIP) was completed with significant over-subscription and marquee investors; proceeds are primarily for debt reduction and balance sheet strengthening. The company cites a rationalised debt profile and steady free cash generation over recent years.
  • Renewable power procurement. Arvind terminated its Power Transfer Agreement and Share Subscription and Shareholders Agreement with Torrent Urja 28 Private Limited (TU28) due to capacity reallocation, holding no equity in TU28, and entered agreements with Torrent Urja 12 (13.30% equity) and Torrent Urja 21 (26.60% equity) to offtake contracted electricity as a captive user in Gujarat, citing significant financial/commercial benefits.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material availability and cost escalation pressuring margins, with mitigation plans still being worked through.
  • Geopolitical uncertainties, Middle East developments, and shipping-channel disruptions affecting supply chains and procurement economics.
  • Residual tariff-related pricing pressures on margins.
  • Elevated working capital from anticipatory raw material procurement.
  • Acquisition integration and financing costs: incremental depreciation, finance costs, and one-time transaction expenses from Dalco-GFT, alongside the resulting increase in net debt.
  • Product mix shifts potentially moderating short-term revenue growth.
  • Forward-looking statements are subject to known and unknown risks and uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ materially.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Growth guidance vs. delivery. Management states Q1 FY27 growth was delivered “in line with our guidance,” with consolidated revenue up 25% and EBITDA up 39% YoY.
  • Capex. Management expects full-year FY27 growth capex of ₹450–500 Cr, with all growth capex projects on track; ₹98 Cr was spent on capex in Q1 FY27.
  • Margins. Management is working on a detailed mitigation plan to keep margins resilient amid raw material cost escalation; observed Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 10.3% (up ~104 bps YoY), while Textile division margin growth was curtailed by ~₹19 Cr of higher input cost.
  • Demand. Management expects resilient demand across Textiles and AMB supported by healthy order books; observed Q1 FY27 volumes grew across denim (+34%), woven (+7%), and garmenting (+13%, crossing 11 Mn pieces).
  • AMB. Management is focusing on higher-value customer programs in defence and allied business; observed AMB delivered record revenue of ₹650 Cr and EBITDA of ₹97 Cr at a 15% margin, with India AMB up 40%.
  • UK-India FTA and domestic demand. Management expects these to create incremental growth opportunities; no quantified impact is provided.
  • Product mix. Management notes product mix changes may alter short-term revenue growth; observed garmenting revenue growth was moderated by higher value-segment contribution despite volume growth.
  • Dividend/commission contingency. In the event of loss or inadequate profits in any financial year from April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2029, payment of commission to Non-Executive Directors shall be as per limits prescribed in Schedule V of the Act; the company aims to sustain positive year-on-year revenue and profit growth.
  • Governance calendar. The AGM will be held Tuesday, September 22, 2026 at 11:00 A.M. IST via video conferencing/audio-visual means; the Record Date for the FY2025-26 dividend is Friday, September 4, 2026; remote e-voting runs September 19–21, 2026 with a cut-off date of September 15, 2026; Mr. Punit Lalbhai and Mr. Kulin Lalbhai are seeking re-appointment as directors. Senior management will attend a meeting with LIC Mutual Fund in Ahmedabad on September 17, 2026, subject to change due to exigencies.
