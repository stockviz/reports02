SHREEPUSHK Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material cost inflation and availability. Management describes continued volatility in raw material prices and availability as the key constraint on operations. Sulphur, which previously traded in the USD 250–300 range, is cited at around USD 1,100, with little expectation of a return to old pricing. Ammonia and sulphur availability was acutely disrupted by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, and although the strait remains closed, material is now being sourced from alternative origins. The company kept its acid plants on low load as a deliberate response: one kilogram of sulphur yields three kilograms of acid, sulphur requires advance payment while acid is sold entirely on credit, and with thin spreads management chose not to block working capital. This is the stated reason chemical volumes fell from 14,837 MT in Q1 FY26 to 9,113 MT in Q1 FY27.

Lower volumes across both segments. Fertiliser volumes declined to 66,527 MT from 76,288 MT year-on-year, and chemical volumes fell 38.6% year-on-year. Management attributes the softness to customers pausing purchases after the West Asia conflict triggered sudden price spikes, with demand returning only gradually as buyers absorbed higher prices.

Global supply chain and geopolitical disruption. The company cites ongoing global supply chain challenges and elevated raw material prices, with the West Asia conflict and Hormuz closure explicitly identified as the trigger for halted dispatches from mid-March and for the cautious stance on commissioning new capacity.

Working capital stress. Management explicitly names working capital stress as one of the reasons for running acid plants at low load, alongside the unfavourable advance-payment-versus-credit terms on sulphur and acid.

Deferred commissioning. Ratnagiri Unit 5 and Unit 6 are at an advanced stage of completion, but the company is taking a measured approach to commencement given raw material volatility, and did not factor Unit 5 and Unit 6 volumes into the current season’s guidance.

Dyes utilisation. Utilisation levels in dyes and chemicals have decreased somewhat because of the global environment, though management frames the priority as securing better value rather than volume.

Sector-level risks cited in the annual disclosure. Raw material price volatility (sulphur, ammonia, phosphoric acid, rock phosphate), geopolitical developments and global supply chain disruptions, evolving environmental and government regulations, and competitive and agricultural risks including changing weather patterns and monsoon variability.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Improved realisations offsetting lower volumes. Despite the volume declines, revenue from operations grew 10.0% year-on-year to Rs. 280.1 crores and 28.4% sequentially. Fertiliser sales value rose 4.0% to Rs. 142 crores and chemical sales value rose 17.1% to Rs. 138 crores. The two segments contributed 51% and 49% respectively.

Profitability recovery. EBITDA rose 9.7% year-on-year to Rs. 31.9 crores at an 11.4% margin, and PAT rose 9.4% to Rs. 22.9 crores at an 8.2% margin. Management contrasts this with the COVID-period trough of roughly 5–5.5% PAT margin and describes a gradual climb back above 8%.

Raw material situation normalising. Management states availability is much better than two to three months earlier, that pre-war raw material price levels have reset, and that customers have absorbed the higher prices. On this basis the company plans to restart Unit 6 and expects trials by end-August or September.

Demand recovery into Q2. Management says demand is becoming more apparent in Q2 and expects Q2 to be better than Q1 and better than Q2 of last year.

China order flow into India. K-acid, H-acid and Vinyl Sulphone prices have risen significantly in the last 15–20 days as China places orders back to India, with plentiful inquiries. K-acid prices are cited as moving from Rs. 550 to Rs. 700-plus, and the company describes itself as the largest K-acid manufacturer in India.

Capacity expansion pipeline. Ratnagiri Unit 5 (66,000 MTPA, Rs. 37 crores capex, fully incurred) and Unit 6 (1,50,000 MTPA fertiliser, Rs. 85 crores planned, Rs. 72 crores incurred) are nearing completion. Meghnagar Unit 8 adds 3,00,000 MTPA of complex fertiliser capacity at Rs. 350 crores planned, with Rs. 60 crores incurred and a target completion of March 2028. Together these add 4,50,000 MTPA of fertiliser capacity and 72,000 MTPA of chemical capacity. Total planned capex is Rs. 512 crores, of which Rs. 209 crores is incurred, funded through internal accruals and preferential issue proceeds, with 25% of preferential issue proceeds directed to Meghnagar.

Renewable energy integration. The 10 MW DC solar project at Nanded is nearing completion, taking total installed solar capacity to 20.6 MW DC from 10.6 MW DC. Solar Power Plant 2 (Nanded, Rs. 35 crores) and Plant 3 (Hisar, Rs. 5 crores) are shown as fully incurred, with Plant 3 commissioned in Q4 FY26.

Land acquisition for future expansion. Approximately 30,000 square metres adjacent to Unit 1 at Lote Parshuram was acquired for Rs. 9.33 crores, described as an auction opportunity at an adjoining plot providing headroom at an established location.

Sector opportunity. The annual disclosure cites growth of India’s chemical sector to a projected US$ 1 trillion by 2040, increasing demand for balanced crop nutrition and higher agricultural output, and captive renewable solar investment to optimise operating costs.

Balance sheet flexibility. Non-lien deposits of Rs. 124.9 crores as on June 30, 2026, described as providing financial flexibility for the capex programme.

3. Key Risks

Raw material price and availability risk. Sulphur at roughly USD 1,100 versus historical USD 250–300, with management stating there is little hope of a return to old pricing. Ammonia and sulphur supply routes remain dependent on alternative origins while Hormuz remains closed.

Working capital and credit terms risk. The structural mismatch between advance payment for sulphur and full credit terms on acid sales constrains throughput when spreads are thin.

Volume versus value trade-off. Management states plainly that volume and value do not move together and that value must come first. This means revenue and margin can improve while volumes fall, as occurred in Q1 FY27, and volume recovery is not assured on the same timeline.

Execution and commissioning timing risk. Unit 5 and Unit 6 commissioning has been deliberately deferred, and management did not include them in current-season volume expectations. Meghnagar Unit 8 has a target completion of March 2028 with Rs. 290 crores of capex still outstanding.

Demand absorption risk. Customers paused purchases during the price spike and are only gradually absorbing higher prices. A renewed price shock could repeat the pause.

Competitive and agricultural risk. Changing weather patterns and monsoon variability, plus competitive pressures, are cited in the annual disclosure.

Regulatory and environmental risk. Evolving environmental and government regulations are cited as a material risk.

Product concentration in K-acid. Management states no one else in India manufactures K-acid, which is a position of strength but also concentrates exposure to that product’s price cycle, which has already moved sharply from Rs. 550 to Rs. 700-plus.

Governance and board transition. Mr. Ramakant Madhav Nayak resigned as Non-Executive and Non-Independent Director effective August 13, 2026, and Mr. Raghav Punit Makharia was appointed as an Additional Non-Executive Non-Independent Director the same day. M/s. S. K. Patodia & Associates retires as Statutory Auditors at the 33rd AGM, with M/s JASS & CO LLP recommended for a five-year term. Mr. Dilip M. Bathija was appointed Cost Auditor for FY 2026-27.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

FY27 revenue guidance was revised upward during the call. Management entered the call with visibility of approximately Rs. 1,250 crores for FY27, a figure that assumed Unit 6 starting in the second fertiliser season. During the call this was revised to Rs. 1,350–1,400 crores, attributed to higher realisations and better profit margins. In response to a question about reaching Rs. 1,300–1,350 crores with a 10% PAT margin, management corrected the margin expectation to 8.5–9%.

PAT margin guidance versus Q1 outcome. Management referenced a return toward the historical 9.5–10% PAT margin range, noting the past three years had fallen to about 5.5% and that Q1 FY27 crossed 8%. Q1 FY27 PAT margin was 8.2%, consistent with the stated trajectory but below the 8.5–9% full-year aspiration.

Q2 guidance versus Q1 run-rate. Management stated Q2 would be better than Q1 and better than Q2 of last year, and separately stated Q2 would be “110% much better than last year Q2.” Q1 FY27 revenue of Rs. 280.1 crores was 10.0% above Q1 FY26, so the Q2 guidance implies an acceleration from the Q1 growth rate.

Unit 5 and Unit 6 guidance versus observed status. Management stated Unit 5 is almost commissioned with capitalisation not yet announced, and Unit 6 is complete with trials expected by end-August or September, implying four to five months of operation for both this year. This is later than the presentation’s target completion dates of August 2026 for Unit 5 and September 2026 for Unit 6, and management confirmed Unit 5 and Unit 6 volumes were not factored into the current season.

Peak revenue guidance. Management indicated theoretical peak revenue of about Rs. 2,000 crores once Unit 6 is live, with Rs. 1,700 crores described as not difficult, and for next year a possibility of crossing Rs. 1,700–1,750 crores with a full twelve months of Unit 6.

Prior-quarter guidance versus Q1 delivery. Management had stated in the prior call that dispatches were halted from mid-March in anticipation of higher prices, and that Q4 was weak because orders slipped into Q1. An analyst noted Q1 FY27 revenue and EBITDA were roughly in line with Q1 FY26 rather than showing the expected uplift from the slipped orders. Management’s response was that most retracted Q4 orders do not carry into the next quarter because buyers do not wait, and pointed to the 10% year-on-year improvement in revenue, EBITDA and PAT achieved despite lower volumes.

FY26 performance versus FY27 starting point. FY26 revenue was Rs. 976.6 crores (+21.1%), EBITDA Rs. 99.5 crores (+18.7%, 10.2% margin) and PAT Rs. 70.1 crores (+19.6%), with ROE of 12.2% and ROCE of 15.3%. Q1 FY27 revenue of Rs. 280.1 crores represents 28.7% of the FY26 full-year revenue base, and the revised FY27 guidance of Rs. 1,350–1,400 crores implies growth of roughly 38–43% over FY26, contingent on Unit 6 commissioning and the realisation environment holding.
