Mastek: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Mastek’s Middle East business is seeing impact due to geopolitical uncertainties, as stated by CEO Umang Nahata in the Q1FY27 press release dated July 21, 2026. CFO Deepak Kedia noted that EBITDA performance of 15.4% was a sequential decline largely due to delayed collection in the Middle East.

On a year-on-year basis, revenue from operations in dollar terms declined 2.4% in Q1FY27 (from $107.4mn in Q1FY26 to $104.8mn), even as rupee revenue grew 7.7%. Operating EBITDA margin declined 71 basis points sequentially from 16.1% in Q4FY26 to 15.4% in Q1FY27. Net profit declined 0.3% sequentially. Utilization net of leave fell to 83.9% in Q1FY27 from 85.7% in Q4FY26 and 87.2% in Q1FY26. Days sales outstanding rose to 75 days from 73 in the prior quarter. The number of clients with annual billing above USD 1mn fell to 69 from 75 a year earlier, and active clients during the quarter declined to 309 from 326 in Q4FY26.

The AMEA segment’s 12-month order backlog declined from $64.5mn in Q1FY26 to $54.1mn in Q1FY27. Segment revenue for AMEA fell to Rs 10,800 lakhs in Q1FY27 from Rs 12,018 lakhs in Q1FY26. North America segment revenue also declined year-on-year to Rs 20,111 lakhs from Rs 21,354 lakhs.

The company recorded exceptional items related to the statutory impact of new Labour Codes notified on November 21, 2025, with an incremental impact of Rs 2,135 lakhs for gratuity and Rs 877 lakhs for long-term compensated absences, primarily due to change in wage definition. In Q4FY26, exceptional losses included Rs 2,373 lakhs for Labour Codes and Rs 363 lakhs for impairment of investment in a subsidiary. Mastek continues to monitor finalization of Central/State Rules and clarifications on other aspects of the Labour Code.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Mastek secured 40+ new AI deals in Q1FY27, with approximately 45% of new order book coming from AI deals. The 12-month order backlog stood at Rs 2,935.2 crore ($310.1mn) as of June 30, 2026, reflecting 25.0% growth in rupee terms and 13.3% growth in dollar terms year-on-year. UK & Europe delivered 6.5% sequential revenue growth in rupee terms, and North America delivered 6.2% sequential revenue growth in rupee terms.

The company entered a strategic Platinum Partnership with Innovaccer on August 3, 2026, making Mastek Innovaccer’s platinum global partner with expertise to implement and scale the Gravity Platform for healthcare organizations across North America, UK, Europe, and Middle East. This supports Mastek’s vision of becoming a leading AI transformation partner for the healthcare industry.

Key wins during the quarter included a $25mn AI-led Salesforce deal in North America; a strategic engagement to digitally transform one of England’s largest councils on Oracle Cloud; a landmark engagement with a US Federal security agency on Salesforce Agentforce; four strategic programmes within the UK’s national health authority Data Directorate; a five-year engagement with one of KSA’s fastest-growing listed hospital groups; a multi-million dollar fixed-price transformation contract with a leading US provider of automatic taping and finishing tools; a multi-year AI-led engagement with a leading US furniture rental provider; an engagement with a leading US online automotive marketplace; and a five-year strategic partnership with KSA’s national transportation and mobility leader.

The company completed the sale of a commercial building at Mahindra World City, SEZ, Chengalpattu, Chennai, on August 17, 2026, receiving aggregate consideration of Rs 60 crores in two tranches per the MOU dated April 24, 2026. The buyer is Caresoft Mobility Private Limited, not belonging to the promoter/promoter group.

Cash, cash equivalents, and fair value of mutual funds stood at Rs 945.4 crore as of June 30, 2026, adding approximately Rs 200 crore in operating cash over the last two quarters. The company added 167 employees during the quarter, bringing total headcount to 4,897. Last twelve months attrition declined to 16.4% from 19.5% a year earlier.

Recognitions during the period included Challenger positions in Avasant Higher Education Digital Services 2026 and Manufacturing Digital Services 2026 RadarViews, recognition in the Avasant Banking Digital Services 2026 RadarView, recognition in Gartner’s Market Guide for Oracle Cloud Infrastructure Professional and Managed Services, Major Contender and Star Performer in the Everest Oracle Cloud Applications Services PEAK Matrix Assessment 2026, Product Challenger in ISG Snowflake Ecosystem Partners 2026, and Top 15 Sourcing Standout in the ISG Index Q2 2026 Booming 15 Leaderboard.

Key Risks

Geopolitical uncertainties in the Middle East are impacting the business and causing delayed collections. The company’s forward-looking statements note risks and uncertainties including competition, growth, pricing environment, recruitment and retention, technology, wage inflation, and law and regulatory policies. The Labour Codes’ finalization of Central/State Rules remains pending, with potential for further accounting effects. Currency fluctuations affect reported results, as evidenced by the divergence between rupee and dollar revenue growth. Client concentration remains notable, with top 5 clients contributing 32.0% and top 10 contributing 44.7% of revenue in Q1FY27.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

CEO Umang Nahata stated that the long-term growth outlook remains positive, with the “Lead with AI” strategy delivering directional change. The company reported 1.8% constant currency revenue growth in Q1FY27. CFO Deepak Kedia stated the company remains focused on operational efficiency, disciplined cost management, and AI-led productivity.

The 12-month order backlog grew 25.0% year-on-year in rupee terms, which management cited as evidence of strong order booking. However, dollar revenue declined 2.4% year-on-year, and sequential EBITDA margin declined 71 basis points. The Middle East impact was explicitly acknowledged as a drag on both revenue and collections. Management’s stated focus on operational efficiency is set against a sequential margin decline and rising DSO.

The AGM held on September 7, 2026, approved all three ordinary resolutions, including adoption of standalone and consolidated financial statements, confirmation of interim dividend and declaration of final dividend for F.Y. 2025-26, and re-appointment of Mr. Umang Nahata as a Director liable to retire by rotation. An Analyst/Institutional Investor Meet with Anand Rathi Share and Stock Brokers Ltd. was scheduled for September 1, 2026, in Mumbai, focusing on publicly available information with no UPSI proposed to be shared.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from cautiously optimistic Add calls expecting demand recovery from AI, cloud, and strong deal velocity, to a final Unrated stance acknowledging persistent revenue conversion lag, margin erosion, and slow AI monetization. While early reports flagged demand softness and competition, they remained positive for two years; the last report confirmed those fears as order backlog growth failed to boost revenue and margins contracted. Themes of AI and deal pipeline persisted but shifted from growth drivers to unresolved challenges.

Fears that came true

  • Demand indicators trailing the start of the year materialized as Q1FY27 CC revenue declined 3% YoY.
  • Pressure on discretionary spending and longer conversion cycles materialized as delayed booking-to-revenue conversion and project transition issues.
  • Increased competition in deals materialized as intense pricing pressure with 20–25% discounts, compressing margins.
  • Decline in EBIT margins for certain companies mirrored MASTEK’s own EBITDA margin fall to 15.4%, down 71 bps QoQ.

Optimism that failed

  • Strong deal velocity and robust bookings failed to drive revenue growth; order backlog grew ~25% YoY but Q1FY27 revenue declined 3% YoY.
  • Acceleration in AI spending and adoption failed to materially improve revenue or profitability; AI contributed 45% of new orders but monetization stayed slow.
  • Favourable supply-side factors supporting operational resilience failed to prevent margin decline, with EBITDA margin down 71 bps QoQ to 15.4%.
