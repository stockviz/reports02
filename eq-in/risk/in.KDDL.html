KDDL — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Margin pressure in the standalone business. Standalone EBITDA margin slipped to 23.2% in Q1FY27 from 25.1% in Q4FY26, and gross profit margin declined to 73.8% from 76.2% in Q1FY26 and 74.9% in FY26. Sequentially, standalone EBITDA (-1.8%), EBIT (-2.1%), PBT (-1.3%) and PAT (-1.5%) all contracted versus Q4FY26 despite higher total income.
  • Cost inflation across expense lines. Standalone employee expenses rose to Rs 35.3 Cr in Q1FY27 (from Rs 29.2 Cr in Q1FY26) and other expenses to Rs 43.0 Cr (from Rs 32.9 Cr). On a consolidated basis, employee expenses rose to Rs 83.7 Cr from Rs 59.9 Cr and other expenses to Rs 88.8 Cr from Rs 62.8 Cr year-on-year.
  • Statutory cost impact. Consolidated results for FY26 include a Rs 2.4 Cr exceptional expense described as the statutory impact of the Labor Codes (Rs 2.45 Cr).
  • Working capital build. Standalone inventories rose to Rs 77.2 Cr in Mar’26 from Rs 56.5 Cr in Mar’25, and trade receivables to Rs 103.3 Cr from Rs 67.6 Cr. Consolidated inventories rose to Rs 792.8 Cr from Rs 657.9 Cr, and consolidated borrowings increased (non-current Rs 73.9 Cr; current Rs 82.6 Cr).
  • Rising finance and depreciation costs. Standalone finance cost rose to Rs 14.3 Cr in FY26 from Rs 10.9 Cr in FY25; consolidated finance cost rose to Rs 42.4 Cr from Rs 31.4 Cr. Consolidated depreciation increased to Rs 122.4 Cr from Rs 86.1 Cr.
  • Full-year margin compression. Consolidated EBITDA margin fell to 16.4% in FY26 from 18.1% in FY25, and consolidated PAT declined to Rs 135.2 Cr from Rs 142.3 Cr, with PAT margin down to 6.1% from 8.4%.
  • Responsible sourcing and value-chain impacts. The company flags challenges in responsible material sourcing and managing socio-environmental impacts across the value chain.
  • Regulatory filing matters. The company refiled its financial results for the period ended 30 September 2025, including a revised submission and a repeat XBRL filing in January 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong top-line momentum. Standalone total income grew 34.4% YoY to Rs 154.4 Cr in Q1FY27; consolidated total income grew 35.7% YoY to Rs 647.0 Cr and 10.7% QoQ. Consolidated revenue rose 36.3% YoY to Rs 633.8 Cr.
  • Profit growth. Standalone PAT rose 63.9% YoY to Rs 19.5 Cr; consolidated PAT rose 50.7% YoY to Rs 44.7 Cr and 29.6% QoQ. Consolidated EBIT grew 37.7% YoY.
  • Segment outlook (5–7 years). Management identifies: ~10–12% CAGR from Swiss, Indian and Japanese brands on rising Japanese demand and growth in handcrafted and enamel work; ~20–25% CAGR in the Swiss mid-to-high-end segment on cost-effective production; a Rs 50–75 Cr China+1 opportunity in the Swiss mid-end market; ~20–25% CAGR in US and European markets for high-precision complex parts; and a Rs 80–100 Cr opportunity in domestic watches/jewellery and exports, supported by sustainability trends. Swiss high-end and mid-end brands are seen as offering premium niche growth potential.
  • Business diversification. Portfolio spans dials and hands, bracelets, cases and related parts, Eigen precision engineering, Ornapac luxury packaging, and Swiss manufacturing. Eigen serves aerospace, automotive, electronics, industrial and alternate energy sectors and is certified to IATF 16949, ISO 9001 and AS 9100D. Ornapac serves luxury watch, jewellery and gifting brands with a design-to-delivery model.
  • Capacity and capability expansion. Commercial production of steel bracelets at Dobbaspet, Karnataka commenced in 2024; the company continues investing in automation, digital design, digital printing and eco-friendly manufacturing.
  • Export orientation. Exports contribute 71% of total turnover, supported by long-standing OEM partnerships.
  • Sustainability initiatives. The company has pledged to plant and nurture one million trees across India by 2030, and cites opportunities in training and skill improvement, innovation management, resource efficiency and supply chain management.
  • Strategic milestones. Acquisitions include Estima AG (2019), the Favre-Leuba brand (2023), and the 2022 IPO of Ethos on BSE and NSE.

3. Key Risks

  • Fluctuations in earnings and the ability to manage growth.
  • Competition in both domestic and international markets.
  • Economic growth conditions in India and abroad.
  • Ability to attract and retain highly skilled professionals.
  • Time and cost overruns on contracts.
  • Ability to manage international operations.
  • Government policies and actions, regulations, and prevailing interest and fiscal costs in the economy.
  • Forward-looking statements are subject to these risks and uncertainties, and actual results could materially differ.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Segment growth guidance vs. current run-rate. Management projects ~10–12% CAGR for Swiss/Indian/Japanese brands and ~20–25% CAGR for Swiss mid-to-high-end and US/European markets over 5–7 years. Observed Q1FY27 performance shows consolidated revenue growth of 36.3% YoY and standalone revenue growth of 39.8% YoY, above the guided long-term ranges, though these are single-quarter figures against multi-year projections.
  • Margin trajectory. Despite strong revenue growth, consolidated EBITDA margin in Q1FY27 (16.76%) was marginally below Q1FY26 (16.86%) and FY26 full-year margin (16.4%) was below FY25 (18.1%), indicating that growth has not yet translated into margin expansion at the consolidated level. Standalone EBITDA margin improved YoY (23.2% vs 22.1%) but declined sequentially from 25.1%.
  • Profitability guidance implied by outlook. The company does not provide explicit numeric profit guidance in the supplied material; the segment outlook is expressed as growth ranges and rupee opportunities. Observed consolidated PAT margin improved YoY to 6.92% in Q1FY27 from 6.23%, but full-year FY26 PAT margin (6.1%) was below FY25 (8.4%).
  • Sustainability commitment. The one-million-trees-by-2030 target is reiterated as an ongoing commitment; no interim progress data is provided in the supplied material.
  • Investor engagement. The company scheduled group meetings with analysts/institutional investors in Mumbai on 6 August 2026, based on publicly available information, with no price-sensitive information to be disclosed.
  • Governance. All 7 resolutions at the 46th AGM on 15 September 2026 were passed with the requisite majority, covering adoption of financial statements, dividend payments, re-appointment of directors, incentive payouts, borrowing authorization and cost auditor remuneration.
