Vardhman Textiles (VTL) — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Fabric business softness. Management stated that improvement in the fabric segment was less than warranted for two reasons. First, during the prior year, the U.S. tariff situation caused the company to miss sampling for a particular season, and as a result U.S. orders did not come to India during that period. Second, there is an inherent lag in passing through yarn price increases to fabric customers; management noted that even at the time of the call, only about 60–70% of the yarn price increase had been passed on, leaving 20–30% not yet recovered.

Volume declines in fabric. Grey fabric production fell 6.9% year-on-year to 462 lakh metres and 11.3% quarter-on-quarter; grey fabric sales fell 6.8% year-on-year and 12.1% quarter-on-quarter. Processed fabric production was down 0.5% year-on-year and 6.6% quarter-on-quarter. Management attributed lower percentage utilisation partly to a new line added in March that could not be fully utilised.

Raw material uncertainty. Management described highly variable rainfall in India, an El Niño condition, and area-to-area variation, noting that such variation generally brings quality concerns and can reduce crop size. Brazil saw a roughly 5% reduction in cotton area; Australia’s crop was expected at about 3.5 million bales with a possible further fall to about 2.5 million bales next year; U.S. West Texas was still facing drought conditions; and China announced a reduction in cotton growing areas. Management also flagged uncertainty from the U.S.–Iran war and crude prices touching $100 before easing to about $87–88, which raised polyester and acrylic prices and, by sympathy, other fibre prices.

Structural raw-material disadvantage (prior period). Management noted that Indian cotton prices had been at a disadvantage versus international prices for the last two years, though this had improved over the last four to five months, with Indian and international prices now aligned.

Margin moderation expected. Management explicitly stated that the strong Q1 margin will moderate, because part of the result reflected trading gains on lower-cost inventory that will not recur once inventories run out and market-priced cotton is consumed.

Synthetic fabric ramp-up risk. The new synthetic woven fabric business (Vardhman Performance Fabric) had capacity of 15 lakh metres per month but utilisation of only about 15–20%, with sampling underway and products new to the company.

Dhar / PM MITRA land and power timing. Management said the government is still developing the land and that power is not expected before June 2027; construction will only start once there is visibility on power, to avoid further delay.

Garment unit scale. The existing garment capacity of about 7,000 shirts per day was described as too small to serve customers viably, with higher costs and lower margins at that scale.

Industry context. Management referenced the prior two years as a very difficult period for spinning, with margins falling as low as 9–10% and conversion available of only about $0.65–0.70 per kg, and an estimated 12–13 million spindles permanently closed in India over three years.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Spinning demand and utilisation. Spinning utilisation was full, margins were good, and overall demand was good, with demand originally deriving from China. Yarn production rose 2.0% year-on-year and yarn sales (including internal transfer) rose 7.2% year-on-year.

China sourcing. Management noted Chinese reserve cotton sales at an average price of about $1.15–1.16 (above $1 even after removing VAT), implying Chinese mills’ margin on today’s yarn prices would be no more than $0.65–0.70 per kg, which supports continued Chinese yarn buying from outside China. India’s monthly yarn exports rose from about 95–97 million kg to about 110 million kg, with the net increase of about 15 million kg primarily from China. China and Bangladesh together take about 60% of India’s yarn exports.

Cotton supply–demand outlook. Management’s view is that after three years, this could be a year where production is lower than consumption, potentially continuing for two to three years with a 2–3% annual drop in cotton crop and 2–3% growth in consumption, implying a 3–4% gap.

Price alignment. Indian cotton prices rose from about INR55,000 a candy to a CCI price close to INR64,000, tracking the New York future’s rise from about $0.67–0.68 to about $0.80 per pound. Indian prices in U.S. cents terms were described as close to $0.87–0.88, with cotton at about $0.91–0.92, aligning Indian and international prices.

Limited global spindle additions. Management stated China’s spindle count fell from a peak of about 116–117 million to about 84–85 million, with Indonesia, Vietnam, Turkey and Bangladesh not adding capacity; only India and Egypt may see small increases. New Indian capacity was about 0.5 million spindles last year, with 700,000–800,000 spindles of announced projects this year.

FTA-driven export opportunity. Management cited India’s natural advantage from FTAs with the U.K. and EU, and saw a genuine two-year export opportunity for Indian manufacturers, benefiting all stages from spinning to garmenting and home textiles, alongside a strong and improving domestic market.

U.S. demand recovery. Management said that over the last month or so, things had started improving, with far better business expected from U.S. customers over the next two to three months, which should improve fabric utilisation.

Synthetic fabric approvals. Two large brand approvals were received in the last month, with production starting in August, and management aims to reach 70–80% capacity utilisation within six months.

Garment capacity expansion. The Board approved expanding garment capacity from 2.2 million to 4.5 million shirts per annum, with an investment of approximately INR125 crore, expected completion by end of FY2026-27. Peak revenue at 100% was indicated at close to INR300 crore. The business is about 30% export and 70% domestic brands.

Capex and green power. Most capex is online: a biomass boiler at Baddi started the prior week, another in Madhya Pradesh expected in about a month, most modernisation completed, and an open-end project of about 55–60 tonnes per day with construction just started and expected available in about 10 months. Investments in solar and wind power were expected to deliver increasing cost advantages over the next six months.

Recycling and customer requirements. Management noted recycling remains in fashion, especially in export markets, with up to 30–40% recycled fibre possible on ring spinning and more on open-end. Customers increasingly favour organised players with scale, compliance, ESG credentials, faster delivery and innovation capability.

3. Key Risks

  • Cotton price and availability risk: Management’s central concern is cotton availability at the right prices; the company continues to request more sustainable visibility from the government on duty-free imports, currently available for a four-month window.
  • Margin normalisation risk: Trading gains embedded in Q1 margins are expected to fade as lower-cost inventory is consumed.
  • Weather and crop risk: El Niño, uneven rainfall, and drought in West Texas create uncertainty over crop size and quality.
  • Geopolitical and input cost risk: The U.S.–Iran war and crude price volatility raised polyester and acrylic prices, with possible sympathetic effects on other fibres.
  • Ordering pattern risk: Fashion cycles are shortening, with customers demanding yarn delivery in about 20 days, fabric in 44–45 days and garments in 60–70 days, and minimum inventory; only vendors able to deliver will win.
  • Project execution risk: Dhar/PM MITRA construction depends on land and power availability, with power not expected before June 2027.
  • New business ramp risk: Synthetic fabric utilisation is low and the margin/ROCE profile is not yet established; management said it would be more certain in about six months.
  • Garment scale risk: The current small garment unit carries higher costs and lower margins.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Q1 FY27 reported performance (standalone): Revenue from operations INR2,648 crore, up 13% year-on-year and 9% quarter-on-quarter; total income INR2,721 crore; EBITDA INR528 crore with a 19.4% margin, up 295 bps year-on-year and 531 bps quarter-on-quarter; PBT INR380 crore, up 43% year-on-year and 81% quarter-on-quarter; PAT INR285 crore, up 41% year-on-year and 59% quarter-on-quarter; EPS INR9.85. Consolidated revenue from operations was INR2,703.08 crore, with profit for the period of INR314.56 crore and EPS of INR10.88.

Guidance versus observed performance:

  • Management attributed the spinning improvement to better yarn sizes, an earlier raw material advantage, and a partial trading gain as yarn prices adjusted to new raw material prices — consistent with the reported 19.4% EBITDA margin.
  • Management stated fabric improvement was less than it should have been, and reported grey fabric production and sales declined year-on-year and quarter-on-quarter, consistent with that assessment.
  • Management said spinning utilisation was full and margins good; yarn production and sales rose year-on-year, consistent with this.
  • Management said fabric volumes were comparable to Q1 last year and about 3–4% lower than Q4, and stated it expected the current quarter to be definitely better than Q1 — reported processed fabric sales were up 0.2% year-on-year and down 4.8% quarter-on-quarter.
  • Management guided that Q2 should reflect the April–May price improvement, confirming that bulk of the improvement should be visible in Q2.
  • Management guided that margins will moderate from Q1 levels but should not return to the prior 9–10% lows, indicating a conversion range of about $0.85–0.90 per kg and a margin range of 13–14%, versus the historical $0.85–1.00 range and the last two to three years’ $0.60–0.70.
  • Management stated the cotton yarn spread in the quarter was about $0.90 per kg on spot cotton and spot yarn.
  • Management guided that yarn sales visibility extends for at least the next three to four months.
  • Management guided that of the announced ~INR3,600 crore capex, only about INR800–900 crore will spill into the next year, with the remainder completed or to be completed this year; power projects to be completed within three months; most modernisation completed; open-end and some modernisation possibly into Q1 next year.
  • Management guided that the Dhar project is still under consideration and no expenditure on Dhar is included in the announced capex.
  • Management guided that the garment expansion would reach full utilisation in about one year, with peak revenue near INR300 crore at 100%.
  • Management guided that synthetic fabric utilisation should reach 70–80% within six months from the current 15–20%.
  • Management guided that ROCE improvement could materialise over a five-year horizon, with the company intending to invest in larger projects when the right opportunity arises, which would reduce idle investments and improve ROCE.

Broker Narrative

The narrative began with a cautious Hold, focused on fabric weakness, high cotton costs, inventory overhangs, and margin pressure, while acknowledging an early yarn export recovery. Over the middle period, brokers turned repeatedly Buy as yarn volumes, cotton-yarn spreads, and margins improved and the stock delivered strong returns. By the final report, the rating reverted to Neutral despite another strong quarter because the same spread driver was seen as unsustainable, fabric demand remained weak, and the announced capex benefits were delayed beyond FY28.

Fears that came true

  • Fabric export-market weakness persisted: fabric revenue declined 4% YoY on lower realizations, synthetic fabric capacity utilization was only 15-20%, and a US sampling season was missed due to tariff disruption, coinciding with the 2025-04-04 call’s actual +28.7% falling short of its predicted +36.3%.
