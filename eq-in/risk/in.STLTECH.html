STLTECH: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Litigation exposure (now largely resolved): STLTECH was involved in UK patent infringement litigation brought by Fujikura LTD and Fujikura Europe Limited concerning Fujikura’s patent EP3796060, related to the Company’s Celesta cable family. This was disclosed in a letter dated December 5, 2025.
  • Contractual risk-sharing obligations: The long-term supply agreement with a leading hyperscaler establishes a reciprocal risk-sharing framework with mutual, capped financial liabilities for both parties in the event of demand shortfalls or supply capacity shortages.
  • Execution and funding demands of expansion: The proposed capacity addition of approximately 50% over existing installed manufacturing capacity requires an investment of approximately ₹3,000 crores, to be financed through internal accruals and/or debt, alongside planned annual investments of approximately ₹1,000 crores over the next three financial years under the Lakshya ambition.
  • ESG rating engagement gap: Crisil ESG Ratings & Analytics Ltd assigned an ESG rating of 59 on August 31, 2026; the company did not engage the rating agency for this rating, which was independently prepared using publicly available data.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • AI-driven optical demand: An expanding optical total addressable market is being driven by AI data center scale and increasing optical content per rack — cited at 4,000 fibers for Hopper, 16,000 fibers for Blackwell, and 64,000 fibers for VERA Rubin.
  • India data center growth: Data center capacity growth in India is projected at a 46% CAGR, reaching 10 gigawatts by 2030.
  • Hyperscaler contract: STL signed a long-term supply agreement with a leading international hyperscaler worth approximately USD 288 million for high-density optical fibre cable products, to be executed from CY27 to CY29, extendable by two further years with mutual consent. Purchase orders are to be released periodically starting from 2027 to 2029.
  • Data center portfolio certification: On September 10, 2026, STL achieved Optical Fiber Nonconductive Plenum (OFNP) US certification for its 48 to 576F Intermittently Bonded Ribbon (IBR) pre-terminated assembly portfolio, positioning it among global AI-ready infrastructure providers for NFPA 262-qualified indoor cable assemblies targeting hyperscale, colocation, and enterprise data center containment zones. This enables faster installation, lowered costs, and compliance for indoor AI data center overhead and underfloor pathways.
  • Capacity expansion: The Board approved capex/capacity addition at an existing manufacturing facility, adding approximately 50% over existing installed capacity by the end of FY29.
  • Technology differentiation: Customer co-development, integrated connectivity solutions, and technology-led differentiation including hollow-core and multi-core fibers.
  • Scale and IP position: STL holds approximately 9% of the global optical fiber market outside China, over 785 patents, 10 advanced manufacturing facilities, and an open order book of more than $2 billion.
  • Litigation resolution: Fujikura’s patent EP3796060 was revoked in its entirety by the Technical Board of Appeal of the European Patent Office. This non-appealable decision formally ends the UK patent litigation concerning the Celesta cable family, and the Company is actively pursuing recovery of legal defence costs from Fujikura.
  • Governance continuity: At the 27th Annual General Meeting on August 19, 2026, all ordinary and special business resolutions passed with the requisite majority, including the re-appointment and remuneration of Managing Director Mr. Ankit Agarwal and the re-appointment of Director Mr. Venkatesh Murthy.

3. Key Risks

  • Demand and supply mismatch risk: The hyperscaler agreement’s reciprocal, capped liability framework explicitly addresses demand shortfalls or supply capacity shortages, indicating these as recognized risks.
  • Financing risk: The ₹3,000 crores capacity expansion and ~₹1,000 crores annual investment plan depend on internal accruals and/or debt.
  • Execution risk on ambitious targets: The Lakshya FY29 goals imply a very large step-up from FY26 baselines, requiring successful capacity build-out and demand conversion.
  • Litigation risk (historical): The Fujikura patent dispute demonstrates exposure to intellectual property litigation in key markets, though the UK matter is now concluded.
  • Order conversion risk: The hyperscaler contract depends on periodic purchase order releases during 2027–2029 rather than firm upfront volumes.

Broker Narrative

The broker shifted from a Buy stance built on OFC market-share gains, margin expansion, and a multi-year digital capex cycle to a Neutral stance as North American demand weakened, revenue and margins fell sharply, and order book contraction persisted. Persisting themes included the secular 5G/FTTx/hyperscaler build-out, broadly steady optical cable prices, and debt/capex discipline; the changed emphasis was from near-term growth optimism to acknowledging a muted demand environment and deteriorating financials.

Fears that came true

  • The North American inventory overhang flagged in May 2023 materialized as softer US demand, with the last report noting muted US revenue and an 11.5% QoQ revenue decline.
  • The flagged order book contraction continued, deepening from a 3.0% YoY decline to a 6.3% YoY decline by the last report.
  • The expectation of a softer H1FY24 played out through the July 2023 DISAPPOINTMENT (-1.4% actual vs +17.2% predicted) and the later sharp EBITDA margin decline to 6.8%.

Optimism that failed

  • The early optimism on global OFC market share gains reversed, as market share in the global ex-China OFC market slipped to 9% in H1FY24 from 10% a year earlier.
  • The margin expansion story failed to sustain, with EBITDA margin collapsing from 13.7% in the first report to 6.8% in the last report.
