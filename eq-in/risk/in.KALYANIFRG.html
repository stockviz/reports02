Kalyani Forge (KALYANIFRG) — Recent Corporate Announcements Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Driveline segment softness: Management acknowledges a recent reduction in the driveline business segment and states it is actively tracking and developing prospective growth areas to diversify the revenue base.
  • Agro decline: Agro sales fell 31% year-on-year, attributed to consolidation of core businesses and the phasing out of some legacy low-margin business.
  • Electrification exposure: The passenger car engine business carries potential exposure to the electrification of vehicles.
  • Input cost and geopolitical pressure: Supply chain disruptions and indirect material price increases (consumables and cutting tools by 15% to 30%) are cited as risks tied to geopolitical disturbances such as the Middle East war.
  • Receivables and working capital: Trade receivables stand at ₹124.21 crore. The company is enforcing recovery policies for legacy accounts, strengthening credit control, and conducting weekly ageing reviews. The Cash Conversion Cycle target is a further reduction to 120 days from 148 days.
  • Compliance and governance lapses: A July 10, 2026 exchange clarification addressed a delay in submitting financial results, attributed to the absence of a CFO (position vacant), inability to obtain the Audit Committee Chairman’s signature before filing (meeting held via video conferencing and concluded in the evening), and the company having remained without a Company Secretary prior to a recent appointment. The company described the missing signatures as procedural and inadvertent, and stated it has since strengthened its compliance framework.
  • Audit qualifications: The statutory auditors’ Limited Review Report on the Q1 FY27 results contains a disclaimer of opinion, citing: stock valuation methodology still being refined (inventory quantities and valuation unascertainable); trade receivables, trade payables, stock with vendors, and bank balances subject to confirmations/reconciliations; property, plant and equipment and other assets (GST input tax credit, GSTR-1 sales) subject to reconciliation; and inability to determine whether adequate internal financial controls exist due to absent documentation. Management refers to a “comprehensive clean audit roadmap.”

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record profitability metrics: Q1 FY27 PAT of ₹4.48 crore (up over 218% YoY from ₹1.41 crore); EPS ₹12.31; EBITDA margin 16.2% (up 640 bps YoY); PBT ₹6.15 crore (up over 203%); PAT margin 6.7%. ROCE crossed the 20% target for the first time, reaching 22% (from 18% in Q4 FY26).
  • Cost savings program: The internal ‘Vriddhi Council’ has realized ₹19.1 crore in savings to date against a ₹50 crore annual target.
  • Working capital improvement: Cash Conversion Cycle improved to 148 days from 168 days in Q4 FY26, described as the best level in five quarters.
  • Deleveraging: Debt to EBITDA improved from 3.53x to 2.51x, now below target levels.
  • New order wins: Engine and wheel hub components from marquee global customers, representing approximately ₹20 crore in incremental annual revenue potential, in sample validation phase. New high-volume products cited include Gen 3 wheel hubs and gear blanks.
  • Segment growth: Cars up 35% YoY, Trucks up 48% YoY (fastest-growing), Industrial up 67% YoY (strongest percentage growth on a smaller base). OEM revenue rose to ₹40.7 crore in Q1 FY27, up 31% YoY and 9% QoQ — the fourth consecutive quarter of OEM revenue growth.
  • Exports revival: Export sales mix revived to 16%, with new high-volume business replacing legacy non-fit business.
  • Low-capex expansion: Second conrod production line commissioned for a key customer program with zero additional capex; wheel hub line expansion executed by redeploying existing machining assets, reducing planned outlay from ₹10 crore to approximately ₹2 crore.
  • Product mix differentiation: Kalyani Forge is described as the only forging company offering Engine + Driveline + Axle components to OEMs, combining hot and warm forging technologies. The driveline and axle segments are described as fuel-agnostic and well-hedged.
  • Capacity: Installed forging capacity of 20,000 tonnes (50–60% utilized) and machining capacity of 1.8 lakh pieces per month (90–95% utilized), with plans to expand machining capacity to 3 lakh pieces per month by end of FY27.

3. Key Risks

  • Audit and financial reporting risk: The disclaimer of opinion on Q1 FY27 results, covering inventory valuation, receivable/payable confirmations, asset reconciliations, and internal financial controls, represents a material reporting risk.
  • Credit and collection risk: Elevated trade receivables (₹124.21 crore) with legacy account recovery efforts underway.
  • Demand cyclicality: Exposure to automotive, agriculture, and industrial equipment sectors, with agro already declining and driveline softening.
  • Technology transition risk: Electrification exposure in the passenger car engine business.
  • Input cost and FX volatility: Raw material price volatility, foreign exchange fluctuations, and indirect material price increases (15–30% in consumables and cutting tools).
  • Regulatory/compliance risk: The recent filing delay and prior vacancies in the CFO and Company Secretary positions.
  • Competitive pressure: Cited in the safe harbour statement as a factor that could cause actual results to differ.
