Relaxo Footwears — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Elevated raw material prices and geopolitical uncertainties. In the Q1 FY27 press release (August 13, 2026), the Chairman and Managing Director noted that the quarter was marked by an “evolving external environment, including elevated raw material prices and geopolitical uncertainties.”
  • Cost pressure visible in the P&L. Cost of materials consumed, including packing material, rose to ₹311.48 crore in Q1 FY27 from ₹247.86 crore in Q1 FY26, even as revenue grew 7.7% year-on-year.
  • Sequential moderation. Revenue declined 6.13% quarter-on-quarter (₹705.05 crore in Q1 FY27 vs ₹751.10 crore in Q4 FY26), EBITDA fell 12.70% QoQ, and PAT fell 18.81% QoQ. EBITDA margin contracted 116 bps QoQ to 15.35%.
  • Full-year FY26 revenue decline. Revenue from operations for FY26 was ₹2,702.16 crore, down 3.13% from ₹2,789.61 crore in FY25, indicating a softer demand environment through the prior year.
  • Capacity recalibration. The company is upgrading and modernizing manufacturing facilities (RFL-I and II) to support future growth and enhance its presence in the fashion-forward footwear segment. As per the Board Meeting outcome of August 13, 2026, production capacity has been recalibrated from 10.5 lakh pairs per day to 9.1 lakh pairs per day.
  • External risk factors cited in the source material include volatile raw material prices (rubber and synthetic materials such as EVA, PU, PVC), supply chain challenges, changing consumer preferences, intense competition from global brands in premium and athleisure segments, shortage of skilled workers, rising labour costs, foreign exchange fluctuations, and input tax credit accumulation under the inverted duty structure.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Q1 FY27 growth across channels. Revenue grew 7.7% YoY to ₹705 crore, EBITDA grew 8.8% YoY to ₹108 crore, and PAT grew 12.4% YoY to ₹55 crore. Management attributed this to “resilient demand across channels, a trusted brand portfolio, our wide distribution network and disciplined execution,” with broad-based growth across all channels.
  • Retail (EBO) expansion. Management described expansion and strengthening of the retail EBO network as a key strategic priority, alongside upgradation and modernization of existing outlets, with an ambition to move closer to the 500-store mark by year end. The company currently operates 429 Exclusive Brand Outlets. This expansion is intended to deepen reach in underpenetrated regions and create a retail platform for the premium product portfolio.
  • Industry growth outlook. The organized Indian footwear market is expected to grow at a 13% CAGR, expanding from ₹674 billion in 2025 to ₹1,995 billion by 2034. The sports and athleisure category is described as the fastest-growing, expected to expand at ~10% CAGR. Global e-commerce footwear is expected to grow at 4.25% CAGR from 2025 to 2035 to $400 billion, with Asia-Pacific holding the largest share at 32%.
  • India’s export position. India has moved up from 6th to 5th largest exporter of footwear by quantity.
  • Other cited tailwinds include rising disposable incomes, a shift toward premium and branded footwear, expansion of e-commerce, q-commerce and omni-channel retail, increasing workforce participation by women, favourable government initiatives such as BIS quality standards and GST rationalization, and potential for export growth.
  • Financial resilience. The company maintains a debt-free balance sheet with no borrowings and no term loans outstanding, and holds ICRA AA (Long Term) and A1+ (Short Term) credit ratings.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material price volatility (rubber, EVA, PU, PVC) and its impact on margins, as reflected in the sharp rise in material costs in Q1 FY27.
  • Geopolitical uncertainties affecting the operating environment.
  • Competitive intensity from global brands in the premium and athleisure segments.
  • Supply chain challenges and dependence on a wide distribution network of ~630 pan-India distributors.
  • Execution risk on retail expansion — the plan to approach 500 EBOs by year end and the modernization of RFL-I and II facilities, which has already reduced stated capacity from 10.5 lakh to 9.1 lakh pairs per day.
  • Demand sensitivity — FY26 revenue declined 3.13% YoY, and Q1 FY27 revenue moderated sequentially, indicating exposure to consumption cycles.
  • Foreign exchange fluctuations and input tax credit accumulation under the inverted duty structure.
  • Talent and cost pressures — shortage of skilled workers and rising labour costs.
  • Governance and succession concentration — the Board approved re-appointments of multiple promoter-family members (Nikhil Dua, Gaurav Kumaar Dua, Ritesh Dua, Nitin Dua, Rahul Dua) for terms from October 1, 2026 to March 31, 2029, and Sushil Batra from April 1, 2027 to March 31, 2029, all subject to shareholder approval.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Management guidance: Management stated it remains “optimistic about the demand environment” and focused on “sustaining the improvement seen over the last few quarters,” citing the strong brand portfolio, expanding retail network, product innovation and wide distribution as positioning the company to capitalise on growth opportunities. It also set an ambition to move closer to the 500-store mark by year end and continues to focus on cost optimization, product mix improvement and operational efficiencies.
  • Observed performance: Q1 FY27 delivered YoY growth in revenue (7.7%), EBITDA (8.8%) and PAT (12.4%), with EBITDA margin at 15.35% versus 15.20% in Q1 FY26 (a 15 bps improvement) and PAT margin at 7.79% versus 7.47% (a 32 bps improvement). This is consistent with the stated focus on steady margin performance through cost optimization and product mix.
  • Where performance diverged from the trend: On a sequential basis, Q1 FY27 was weaker than Q4 FY26 across revenue (−6.13%), EBITDA (−12.70%) and PAT (−18.81%), and EBITDA margin contracted 116 bps QoQ. FY26 full-year revenue of ₹2,702.16 crore was 3.13% lower than FY25, even though FY26 EBITDA margin (13.84%) was 15 bps higher than FY25 (13.69%) and FY26 PAT margin (6.63%) was 52 bps higher than FY25 (6.11%).
  • Capacity guidance vs. action: The stated rationale for modernizing RFL-I and II is to support future growth and enhance presence in the fashion-forward segment; the accompanying action was a reduction in stated production capacity from 10.5 lakh to 9.1 lakh pairs per day, as disclosed in the Board Meeting outcome of August 13, 2026.
  • Dividend: For FY2025-26, the Board recommended a final dividend of ₹3.50 per share (350%).

Broker Narrative

The narrative started with a cautious Neutral in May 2023, citing margin pressure, weak rural demand, rich valuations and operational disruptions, while acknowledging some recovery drivers. By August 2026 the stance had hardened to Sell, with the same core concerns — demand fragility, input costs, valuation and capacity/output disruption — still present. The later report did acknowledge some improvement (gross margin expansion, ASP growth, EBO expansion), but framed it as insufficient to make the recovery durable or justify the valuation.

Fears that came true

  • Weak rural demand and a fragile consumption environment persisted, as later reports still cited a weaker consumption backdrop and constrained volume momentum, with the stock posting large negative actual returns.
  • Rich valuations did not protect downside; the stock fell sharply (e.g., -56.4% from the first report) and the last report still called valuations demanding at ~45x FY28E.
  • Operational disruptions materialised in a different form, with the fashion-footwear transition and plant reconstruction/renovation reducing daily output and capacity.
  • Near-term demand challenges proved more durable than initially hoped, as later reports continued to flag limited visibility on the durability of recovery.
  • Input-cost pressure persisted in the form of elevated input costs and price-hike-driven volume trade-offs, even though gross margin later expanded.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected market-share gains from unorganized players did not translate into sustained outperformance, as later reports highlighted constrained volume momentum and capacity-reducing disruptions, and Buy calls around this optimism ended in DISAPPOINTMENT (e.g., -56.8%).
  • The expectation that easing raw-material costs would drive EBITDA margin recovery toward 15–16% did not hold up as a lasting tailwind, since later reports still cited elevated input costs and the stock kept delivering negative returns.
