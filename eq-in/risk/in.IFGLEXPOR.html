IFGL Refractories — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Input cost inflation and supply chain disruption. The dominant pressure on profitability in Q1 FY27 came from higher raw material costs arising from geopolitical uncertainties and supply chain disruptions, compounded by elevated LPG/fuel costs due to availability constraints during the quarter. Standalone gross margin fell to 43% from 47% a year earlier, and standalone EBITDA declined 17% year-on-year to ₹31 crore despite 7% total income growth to ₹299 crore. Management noted that price increases were implemented across customers and product categories, but the nature of the business means a time lag before cost pass-through, with some elevated costs absorbed in the near term. The company also cited a sharp increase in raw material prices amid the Middle East conflict as a factor in modest domestic growth.

Customer-specific production outages in the UK. British Steel produced at very minimal levels in Q1 due to blast furnace problems. Because Sheffield Refractories’ sales are tied to that customer’s offtake, the Europe segment result swung sharply — from close to breakeven in Q4 to a loss in Q1 — with management describing the quarter as “exceptionally low” for Sheffield. No orders were lost; the impact was a timing difference. Management expects British Steel’s blast furnace to be back in Q2 and Sheffield’s sales to normalise.

Freight costs. A spurt in ocean freights due to the prevailing geopolitical situation had a big impact on UK and China operations.

Loss-making overseas operations. Turning around Hofmann Ceramic and Monocon UK remains a work in progress. Hofmann’s order book was maintained at similar levels to the corresponding period, with new initiatives in product enhancement, customer additions and cost rationalisation beginning to show progress. Monocon UK’s loss narrowed from the previous quarter, but the Europe segment as a whole (Monocon UK plus Sheffield) deteriorated sequentially.

China JV stalled. The proposed joint venture with Marvels Group received communication from the appropriate Government of India authority that the application had been closed due to concerns about the proposed project location at Bhachau, Gujarat, with a fresh application invited for an alternative location. Management confirmed the earlier plan is on hold pending approval; only land-related spending has been incurred so far.

Employee cost step-up. Standalone staff cost rose to ₹28.7 crore (about 10% of revenue), up from ₹23.4 crore in Q4 FY26 though similar to Q1 FY26. Consolidated employee benefit expenses rose to ₹84.5 crore from ₹76.5 crore year-on-year. Management said it is working on this and does not expect standalone staff cost to exceed 10% of revenue.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong end-market demand. India is described as the strongest major steel market, with steel demand forecast to grow 7.4% in 2026 and 9.2% in 2027. US steel production grew 6% year-on-year, reflected in the US subsidiaries’ results. European steel demand is bottoming out with growth expected to revive in 2026 and 2027, supported by published results of major European steel groups.

Consolidated growth ahead of the industry. Consolidated revenue grew 13% year-on-year to ₹515 crore in Q1 FY27, with gross margin stable at 48% and consolidated PAT up 58% year-on-year to ₹17 crore. Standalone exports grew 9% year-on-year, and the domestic-to-export mix stood at 77:23.

Overseas momentum. Monocon and Sheffield Refractories revenues grew double-digit year-on-year despite the headwinds. The US business delivered double-digit revenue growth with cost optimisation supporting margins. Monocon UK and Monocon China continued to enter new geographies with new products. New offices were opened in Australia and, subsequent to the reporting date, IFGL Monocon Saudi Company Ltd. was incorporated in Saudi Arabia on 11 July 2026. Mexico and the USA were flagged as markets for Monocon product introduction, with strong customer feedback.

UK industry visibility improving. British Steel is now in public ownership, alleviating concerns about the longevity of its manufacturing operations and improving long-term visibility for UK group companies. Market sources indicate the administrators of the erstwhile Liberty Steel Aldwarke, now Speciality Steel, are looking to restart Rotherham melt shops around November–December 2026; management met the operational and procurement teams and sees a potential significant boost to Monocon if operations resume. Management was clear that historical unsecured dues are unlikely to be recovered.

Sheffield integration and new products. Sheffield Refractories’ product portfolio integration with IFGL India is progressing at the desired pace, including joint customer visits. Production of Sheffield’s plastic ramming mass has commenced at the IFGL Vizag facility, with marketing trials and customer accreditation ongoing. IFGL is placing extra impetus on new products and categories including bricks, casting flux and Sheffield products, and is working with Monocon’s engineering division to bring advanced refractory application equipment to market.

Capacity additions. Management reiterated that the two new product lines — mag carbon brick and casting flux — can add approximately ₹150–200 crore at peak capacity.

R&D contribution. The Odisha R&D facility (around ₹20 crore spent, per management’s correction of an investor’s figure) has developed new materials supporting Monocon UK products into the foundry market, and designed a tundish SEN for thin slab casters for the US market, manufactured in the US, which contributed to US growth. Recycling of materials into mixes is also being pursued, and the R&D team is being augmented.

Goodwill amortisation ending. The legacy goodwill from the 2017 amalgamation was fully amortised as at 31 March 2026, so reported earnings from FY27 onwards no longer carry the ~₹26.7 crore annual non-cash amortisation charge (₹667 lakh per quarter previously).

3. Key Risks

Macro and input-cost risk. The company states it remains susceptible to various micro and macroeconomic factors beyond its control, and to potential loss or inadequacy of profits in financial years. Geopolitical uncertainty and supply chain disruption are the identified drivers of raw material and fuel cost volatility, with limited ability to pass through costs in real time.

Customer concentration and counterparty risk. The Sheffield/British Steel episode illustrates exposure to individual steelmakers’ operational disruptions. The Liberty Steel/Speciality Steel situation illustrates credit risk on historical dues, which management doubts will be recovered given their unsecured nature.

Tax litigation. Two matters are disclosed. First, a disallowed deduction for depreciation on goodwill arising on amalgamation for AY 2020-21 (₹2,816 lakh; tax impact ₹984 lakh), with reopening notices under section 148 for AY 2018-19 (₹5,006 lakh; tax impact ₹1,732 lakh) and AY 2019-20 (₹3,755 lakh; tax impact ₹1,312 lakh); a writ petition was filed before the Hon’ble High Court on 21 May 2024, and the amounts are shown as contingent liabilities. Second, a challenge to the vires of the Explanation to Section 10AA(1) of the Income Tax Act, admitted by the Division Bench of the Hon’ble High Court at Calcutta on 10 January 2024, involving ₹832 lakh, considered possible in nature based on legal opinion. The auditors issued an Emphasis of Matter on the 10AA matter in both standalone and consolidated review reports, with no adjustment considered pending the Division Bench decision.

Turnaround execution risk. Hofmann Ceramic and Monocon UK remain loss-making, and the objective of reducing losses and achieving breakeven by the end of FY27 depends on measures that are still in progress.

Regulatory/approval risk. The China JV with Marvels Group is on hold pending a fresh application with an alternative location, with no announced timeline.

Foreign exchange translation. Exchange differences on translating foreign operations contributed ₹376 lakh to other comprehensive income in Q1 FY27, versus ₹2,016 lakh in Q1 FY26 and ₹2,000 lakh in Q4 FY26, indicating sensitivity of reported comprehensive income to currency movements.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Margin guidance. Management had previously spoken of achieving a 12% EBITDA margin. On this call, the CFO framed the target as trying to achieve a double-digit consolidated EBITDA margin, dependent on the geopolitical situation and overseas demand. Observed Q1 FY27 performance: standalone EBITDA margin of approximately 11% (₹31 crore on ₹299 crore total income) and consolidated EBITDA margin of approximately 8% (₹40 crore on ₹515 crore total income). The consolidated figure remains below the stated double-digit aspiration.

Domestic growth guidance. Management targets at least double-digit domestic growth for the year, noting that the 7% year-on-year domestic growth in Q1 partly reflects a higher base and that the company is “almost” at double digit. Observed Q1 FY27: domestic revenue up 7% year-on-year, exports up 9%.

Cost pass-through guidance. Management said benefits of pricing actions should flow through progressively over coming quarters, and that price increases are temporary, cost-linked measures rather than sources of additional margin. Observed Q1 FY27: gross margin contraction of 400 basis points year-on-year on a standalone basis, with EBITDA down 17% year-on-year, indicating the pass-through had not yet caught up with input cost inflation in the quarter.

Turnaround guidance. Management targets reducing losses and achieving breakeven at Hofmann Ceramic by the end of FY27, and continues to guide toward breakeven at Monocon UK. Observed Q1 FY27: Monocon UK’s loss narrowed sequentially, but the Europe segment result worsened sequentially, driven by Sheffield’s low offtake; management expects Q2 to be normal for Sheffield.

Sheffield technology transfer. Phase 1 (plastic ramming mass) production has commenced at Vizag, with marketing trials and customer accreditation ongoing. Management cautioned that these products will take time to mature in the market through full trials and accreditation.

Peak revenue from new lines. Management reiterated prior guidance that the mag carbon brick and casting flux lines can add approximately ₹150–200 crore at peak capacity. No figure was given for peak revenue on total current capacity.

Overall outlook. Management stated that, on current visibility, the worst appears to be behind in terms of profitability and the negative impact of raw material pass-through, citing green shoots in the European steel industry, positive first-half results at major steel groups, and restarts of previously closed plants — while cautioning that the environment changes quickly and is difficult to predict.
