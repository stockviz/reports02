BOMDYEING: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Real Estate cyclicality and revenue timing. The real estate business is described as cyclical, with negligible revenue during transitions between project phases. In the quarter ended 30 June 2026, the Real Estate segment reported a loss before tax and finance costs of ₹12.94 crore, compared with a profit of ₹5.46 crore in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 and a loss of ₹4.38 crore in the quarter ended 30 June 2025. For the year ended 31 March 2026, the segment recorded a loss of ₹7.09 crore. The company notes that real estate profits and losses do not accrue evenly over the year, so quarterly results may not be representative of full-year outcomes.

Polyester (PSF) pressure. The PSF division has faced volatile crude oil prices and increased energy costs. The Polyester segment reported a profit before tax and finance costs of ₹8.14 crore in the June 2026 quarter, an improvement from a loss of ₹8.42 crore in the June 2025 quarter, but the segment recorded a full-year loss of ₹41.09 crore for the year ended 31 March 2026. Segment revenue declined to ₹350.79 crore in the June 2026 quarter from ₹360.51 crore a year earlier.

Retail competition and input volatility. The Retail/Textile segment faces volume pressure from unorganized competition and fluctuating commodity prices. Segment revenue was ₹14.71 crore in the June 2026 quarter against ₹14.09 crore a year earlier, with segment profit of ₹2.50 crore.

Cost and margin pressure in the latest quarter. Standalone total expenses rose to ₹422.89 crore in the June 2026 quarter from ₹403.33 crore a year earlier, driven by higher cost of materials consumed (₹356.84 crore versus ₹257.39 crore) and other expenses (₹184.45 crore versus ₹85.65 crore). Profit before tax fell to ₹10.29 crore from ₹11.09 crore a year earlier, and profit after tax from continuing operations was ₹7.00 crore versus ₹13.76 crore. Other expenses for the quarter included ₹89.65 crore incurred towards the THREE ICC project, and employee benefits expense included ₹2.81 crore for the same project.

Inventory write-down. Changes in inventories were arrived at after a write-down of inventories to net realisable value of ₹3.49 crore for the quarter ended 30 June 2026.

Tax and regulatory matters. A Demand-cum-Show Cause Notice was received on 21 August 2026 from the Assistant Commissioner of State Tax, Investigation-C, Mazgaon, Mumbai, under Section 74 of the CGST Act and MGST Act, 2017 for FY 2020-21. The notice concerns GST on infrastructure charges relating to sale of flats and interest on late reversal of input tax credit pertaining to flats sold after the occupation certificate. The notice quantifies GST of ₹1,52,25,088, interest of ₹1,79,69,596 and penalty of ₹1,52,25,088, totalling ₹4,84,19,772. The company had already paid the tax liability of ₹1,52,25,088 under protest, leaving a balance liability of ₹3,31,94,684 for interest and penalty. The company states this has not impacted operations and that it will file a reply within the permissible timeline.

SEBI matter continuing at the Supreme Court. A SEBI order dated 21 October 2022 alleged inflation of revenue and profits in the financial statements for FY 2011-12 to FY 2017-18 and non-disclosure of material transactions relating to MoUs with Scal Services Limited. The order imposed a ₹2.25 crore penalty on the company, restrained it from accessing the securities market for two years, and imposed penalties and restrictions on three present directors. The order recorded that there was no diversion, misutilisation or siphoning of assets and no unfair gain or loss. The company obtained a stay from SAT on 10 November 2022, and on 16 January 2026 SAT set aside the SEBI order by majority decision. SEBI has challenged this in the Supreme Court; the appeals were listed on 13 July 2026, and the court issued notice directing the company and others to file replies. The company maintains the financial statements were validly prepared and all transactions legitimate.

Litigation provisions. Exceptional items of ₹0.74 crore in the June 2026 quarter represent provisions for litigated matters pertaining to real estate.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Real estate project launch. During the year ended 31 March 2026, the company received requisite regulatory approvals to commence its real estate project, THREE ICC, which was launched during the quarter ended 30 June 2026. MahaRERA registrations were received for the third phase, “THREE ICC – Tower A, B and C”. Revenue recognition for the project has commenced on an “over time” basis under Ind AS 115; because the stage of completion has not reached a reasonable level of development, revenue is recognised to the extent of costs incurred, using the percentage of costs incurred relative to total estimated project costs. A sum of ₹153.20 crore previously capitalised as land improvement cost/Capital Work-in-Progress was transferred from Property, Plant and Equipment to Inventories-Work In Progress during the quarter.

Real estate segment revenue and assets. Real Estate segment revenue was ₹42.05 crore in the June 2026 quarter, up from ₹18.70 crore in the March 2026 quarter and nil in the June 2025 quarter. Segment assets rose to ₹773.98 crore from ₹664.09 crore at 31 March 2026 and ₹445.86 crore a year earlier.

PSF recovery. The Polyester segment swung to a profit of ₹8.14 crore in the June 2026 quarter from a loss of ₹8.42 crore a year earlier. Management expects a recovery in domestic and export demand for the PSF business.

Retail mix improvement. Management expects higher profitability in the “Home & You” retail business through an improved product mix.

Balance sheet strength. The company describes its balance sheet as fundamentally strengthened and debt-free. Standalone net capital employed was ₹2,307.38 crore at 30 June 2026, with total segment assets of ₹3,045.95 crore and total segment liabilities of ₹738.57 crore. Finance costs declined to ₹2.76 crore in the June 2026 quarter from ₹3.61 crore a year earlier.

Other income and comprehensive income. Other income was ₹23.12 crore in the June 2026 quarter. Other comprehensive income was positive ₹43.30 crore, driven by net fair value changes on equity instruments of ₹50.78 crore, resulting in total comprehensive income of ₹50.30 crore for the quarter.

3. Key Risks
  • Cyclical real estate demand and uneven profit recognition across quarters, with the company cautioning that quarterly results may not represent full-year performance.
  • Volatility in crude oil prices and energy costs affecting the PSF business, which recorded a full-year segment loss of ₹41.09 crore for FY 2025-26.
  • Volume pressure in retail from unorganized competition and fluctuating commodity prices.
  • GST demand-cum-show cause notice of ₹4,84,19,772 in aggregate, with ₹3,31,94,684 remaining for interest and penalty after payment of tax under protest; the outcome of the reply and adjudication is pending.
  • The SEBI matter before the Supreme Court, following SAT’s majority decision setting aside the SEBI order; the company and others have been directed to file replies.
  • Provisions for litigated real estate matters, reflected in exceptional items.
  • Dependence on continued construction and sales momentum at THREE ICC for real estate revenue recognition, given revenue is currently limited to costs incurred.
  • Inventory net realisable value risk, evidenced by the ₹3.49 crore write-down in the June 2026 quarter.
4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Real estate. Management expects performance in the Real Estate division to surge as sales and construction for THREE ICC gain momentum. Observed performance shows the segment moving from nil revenue in the June 2025 quarter to ₹42.05 crore in the June 2026 quarter, but still recording a segment loss before tax and finance costs of ₹12.94 crore in the June 2026 quarter. Revenue recognition is currently limited to costs incurred because the stage of completion has not reached a reasonable level of development.

PSF. Management expects the PSF business to recover with a potential uptick in performance. Observed performance shows a swing to a segment profit of ₹8.14 crore in the June 2026 quarter from a loss of ₹8.42 crore in the June 2025 quarter, though segment revenue declined year on year and the segment recorded a full-year loss of ₹41.09 crore for FY 2025-26.

Overall positioning. Management states the company is well-positioned to pursue its growth plans and deliver improved operational performance and profitability. Observed performance for the June 2026 quarter shows revenue from operations of ₹410.80 crore (up from ₹377.84 crore a year earlier), but profit before tax of ₹10.29 crore (down from ₹11.09 crore) and profit after tax from continuing operations of ₹7.00 crore (down from ₹13.76 crore), with total comprehensive income of ₹50.30 crore (up from ₹64.08 crore a year earlier on a standalone basis, and ₹50.38 crore consolidated versus ₹64.13 crore).

Managerial continuity. The board approved the re-appointment of Mr. Rajnesh Datt as Manager for a further two-year term from 4 February 2027 to 3 February 2029, subject to member approval, with his current tenure concluding on 3 February 2027. The associated postal ballot material notes remuneration exceeding Section 197 and Schedule V limits due to inadequate profits calculated under Section 198 of the Companies Act, 2013.
