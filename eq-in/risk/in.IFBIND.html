IFB Industries — Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Commodity and forex pressure. Management stated on the 14 August 2026 earnings call that commodity and forex pressures were not yet behind the company. In the Home Appliances Division, the quarter absorbed an impact of ₹58 crores from commodity increases and ₹28 crores from forex. Management said it had not been able to pass these costs on to customers because it needed to remain competitively priced, and that price increases taken across categories in the quarter were not enough to cover the commodity and forex impact. Gross margin fell about 160 basis points year on year on account of material cost increases.

Room air conditioner growth lag. RAC volume growth was about 6–8% in the quarter, below the roughly 20% plus industry growth referenced by an analyst. Management attributed this to a conscious decision not to build stock of older-rated AC products ahead of the rating change; old-rated stock was cleared by around January, and the new-rated products were 10–15% more expensive, removing a price advantage in the market.

Steel Division loss. The Steel Division posted a loss in the quarter. Management attributed this to a rise in raw material (steel) prices of around ₹4,125 per metric ton over six months, with the industry practice being that such increases are passed on to customers from the following quarter. The division was also impacted by lower production due to delayed commissioning of the additional Annealing Furnace, and by raw material cost increases that could not be fully passed on under the agreed pricing mechanism.

Engineering Division after-market material availability. The after-market business faced material availability issues due to BIS restrictions. Management expects these to stabilize by September 2026.

EV/battery project uncertainty. The Engineering Division had bagged a Letter of Intent from Tata worth about ₹150 crores for an EV-related project and acquired land in Gujarat. Following newspaper reports that Tata Sons had decided not to support the battery business, management said it was holding back and in active discussion with Tata Agratas, and was reviewing the situation. Land acquired will also serve the stamping project.

Trade schemes and discounts. An analyst described trade schemes and discounts of about ₹1,800-odd crores as high versus other durable players. Management acknowledged the number needs to be closely examined for leakages and efficiencies, while noting this is how the industry operates.

Home Appliances Division budget shortfall. The division’s performance was impacted by non-achievement of budgeted volume, alongside the commodity and forex impacts noted above.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Revenue and profit growth. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹1,529 crores versus ₹1,311 crores a year earlier, a growth of 16.65%. PBDIT was ₹88.46 crores (5.79% of revenue) versus ₹69.95 crores (5.34%), up 26.46%. PBT was ₹51.54 crores (3.3% of revenue) versus ₹33.93 crores (2.59%). PAT was ₹38.06 crores (2.5% of revenue) versus ₹25.36 crores (1.9%). Home Appliances Division revenues grew 18% for the quarter. The company reported zero net debt, with cash and cash equivalents of ₹409.24 crores (including mutual fund investments) against total borrowings of ₹10.49 crores; ROCE was 17.32% annualized and EPS ₹9.39.

Category-wise volume growth. Front-load washing machines grew about 9–10%, top-load was in excess of 20%, and AC about 6–8%. Refrigerator volume growth in IFB Industries was 14%. Management said it saw growth in all lines and believed market shares were improving, with front-loaders in the top two.

Distribution and channel expansion. Management attributed growth to improved placements, better extraction, and in-store promoters manning counters, and said the number of counters covered by in-store promoters is being increased rather than cut.

Cost initiatives. Cost savings of about ₹42–43 crores flowed through in Q1, against a full-year target of ₹120–150 crores; ₹67 crores was achieved last year. Management said it is on track for the ₹150 crores cost initiative and is not cutting anything that would impact market presence.

SKU rationalization. Front-loader models were reduced from about 55–57 to about 24–25, and washer SKUs from roughly 9,500 to about half. Benefits cited include simpler counter briefing, manageable inventory, easier manufacturing, lower changeover time, and better model selection by gross margin.

Capacity. Air conditioner capacity is about 75,000 units per month, running at about 85% in peak months. Debottlenecking and automation are expected to raise this to 75,000–80,000 per month without adding shifts. Management said there is currently no capacity constraint.

New products and segments. Premium portfolio expansion includes 2-in-1 Washer Refreshers, LavandriMagic washer dryers, and high-capacity top-load washers. BLDC motors entered commercial production with expansion into prospective OEMs across air cooler and chimney applications; the Motor Division aims to exceed 450,000 BLDC motor units for the full fiscal year. Management said products for capacities not currently served would be added over the next 3–6 months, providing an additional growth pillar.

Engineering Division. Q1 sales exceeded budget and grew 17%, with EBITDA at budgeted level. New order bookings reached ₹31 crores against a full-year target of ₹500 crores, with an RFQ pipeline of ₹300 crores (₹69 crores in advanced stage). Planned investments include ₹150 crores in the EV segment and ₹60 crores in brake discs; full-year capex is about ₹110 crores. The division targets around ₹1,000 crores of new business with overall stamping capex of around ₹400 crores, which management said could take the division toward ₹2,000 crores from the present ₹1,000–1,100 crores, contingent on projects materializing.

Steel Division. The Annealing Furnace commissioned in May 2026 has started stabilizing and is expected to support higher volumes and improved product mix in coming quarters. Management indicated ROCE in the Steel Division is around 20-odd percent, in line with other divisions.

Services business. The services business grew about 17% and is a double-digit profitable business at PBT level, covering refrigerator services as well.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued commodity and forex inflation that cannot be passed through, compressing gross margins; management said it does not want to be competitively disadvantaged on price.
  • Competitive intensity across home appliance categories, including RAC, where multiple players operate and pricing power is limited.
  • Dependence of the Engineering Division’s ₹2,000 crores aspiration on the Tata/Agratas battery project proceeding; management said the target is contingent on this project being successful, though other projects are being scouted.
  • Steel Division exposure to raw material price movements and the timing lag before pass-through to customers.
  • BIS-related material availability constraints in the Engineering after-market business.
  • Execution risk in channel expansion and new product launches, which management described as an ongoing effort rather than a completed task.
  • Reliance on revenue growth to hold margins: management said maintaining PBDIT levels depends significantly on the revenue growth achieved.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

Growth guidance. Management had previously guided to about 20% growth. Observed: 18% revenue growth in Q1 FY27 for the Home Appliances Division, with July also described as good, against full-year FY26 growth of about 9.8–10%. Management said it should be able to sustain the same rate in coming quarters and years.

Cost savings guidance. Management guided to ₹120–150 crores of cost savings for the year (with an earlier reference to ₹150 crores). Observed: about ₹42–43 crores realized in Q1, with ₹67 crores realized in the prior full year; management said it is on track.

Margin guidance. Management said PBDIT should be early double digit over time, and that if BOM costs ease, the 160 basis points of gross margin decline could flow straight into the P&L. Observed: PBDIT margin of 5.79% in Q1 FY27 versus 5.34% a year earlier, with gross margin down about 160 basis points year on year.

RAC market share aspiration. Management reiterated an aspiration of 7–10% market share in ACs but declined to give a timeline, saying it could not provide a number. Observed: AC volume growth of about 6–8% in Q1, below the industry growth referenced by an analyst, though management expressed confidence that AC growth can exceed market growth.

Engineering Division order booking. Management guided to a ₹500 crores new order booking target for FY27. Observed: ₹31 crores achieved so far, with a ₹300 crores RFQ pipeline of which ₹69 crores is in advanced stage.

Engineering Division growth. Management said it expects growth similar to Q1 to continue, both in top line and bottom line, subject to global scenarios. Observed: Q1 sales grew 17% and exceeded budget, with EBITDA at budgeted level.

After-market stabilization. Management expects BIS-related material availability issues in the Engineering after-market business to stabilize by September 2026.

Steel Division recovery. Management expects the raw material price increase to be recovered from customers in coming quarters, and the Annealing Furnace commissioned in May 2026 to support higher volumes and improved product mix.

Motor Division. Management aims to exceed 450,000 BLDC motor units for the full fiscal year. No representative from the Motor division was present on the call, and a question on Motor division profitability was deferred to an offline discussion.

IFB Refrigeration (associate). Management declined to discuss the associate’s performance on the call. For context, the associate’s Q1 PBT was ₹80 lakhs, of which IFB Industries’ 41.40% share was ₹0.33 crores; the prior-year corresponding quarter was a loss, and the March quarter had been higher due to deferred tax recognition.

Broker Narrative

The narrative started with Reduce calls focused on weak home-appliance/AC demand, low AC utilisation, intense competition, and a long-drawn margin recovery, with AC utilisation and RAC market-share gains as the hoped-for catalysts. By the last report the stance flipped to Buy, leaning on diversification into EMS/W&C and non-cooling products, while cooling-season weakness and pricing pressure persisted. What persisted was the cooling-demand and margin/competition concern; what changed was the broker’s willingness to underwrite recovery via non-AC businesses.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning that AC/cooling demand was subdued materialised, as the last report cites erratic summer and high base causing revenue declines in air coolers, RACs, and fans, and the 2024-08-06 Reduce call captured a -26.6% actual decline.
  • The early concern about intense competition and weak pricing was realised in the last report’s subdued pricing trends and price erosion in lighting, which accompanied disappointing negative actual returns on the 2024-11-07 and 2025-07-11 Buy calls.
  • The early expectation that margin improvement would be long-drawn was confirmed by raw-material copper/aluminium increases and price erosion, leaving profitability under pressure and later bullish calls with negative actual returns.

Optimism that failed

  • The projected recovery in AC utilisation from Q3 onwards through distribution reach and OEM opportunities did not materialise, with RACs still in revenue decline by the last report.
  • The goal of lifting AC gross margins toward home-appliance levels and capturing a 10%+ RAC market share failed to show up, as cooling revenue and pricing weakened instead.
  • The gross-margin tailwind from lower commodity costs was short-lived, as later copper and aluminium price increases and price erosion in lighting reversed the favourable cost environment.
