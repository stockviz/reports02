Summary Analysis for CANFINHOME (Can Fin Homes Limited)

Can Fin Homes Limited (CFHL), a leading housing finance company (HFC) sponsored by Canara Bank (~30% stake), reported robust Q2/H1 FY26 results (ended Sep 2025) amid steady loan growth and pristine asset quality. Loan book reached ₹39,657 Cr (+8% YoY), PAT at ₹475 Cr (+16% YoY for H1), with NIM at 3.83% and RoE ~17.4%. The company maintains AAA ratings, strong liquidity (LCR 217%), and is advancing digital/IT transformation. Below is a structured analysis of headwinds, tailwinds, growth prospects, and key risks based on the filings (Q2 results, investor presentation, ESOP grant, etc.).

Tailwinds (Positive Catalysts)

  • Strong Financial Momentum: H1 disbursements up 8% YoY to ₹4,560 Cr; NII ₹767 Cr (+16% YoY). NIM expanded to 3.83% (vs. 3.65% YoY), spread to 2.79% on yield stability (~10.09%) and falling funding costs (7.29%). RoA/RoE at 2.32%/17.4% reflect efficiency (cost-income ~18%).
  • Pristine Asset Quality: GNPA 0.94%, NNPA 0.48% (stable/improving); PCR 75% (incl. overlays). Salaried/professionals form 69-70% of AUM, with housing dominant (74-85%).
  • Funding Strength: AAA/Stable ratings (CARE/ICRA); diversified mix (banks 57%, NCDs 22%, NHB 11%); deposits crossed ₹200 Cr. LCR 217%; new NHB refinance ₹1,500 Cr.
  • Operational Scale: 248 branches across 21 states; DSA channel ~79% of sourcing. Sponsor backing ensures stability.
  • Talent & Governance: ESOP grant (1.76L options); new promoter director approved; investor meets signal engagement.
  • Sustainability/ESG: Solar loans, rainwater harvesting, CSR (e.g., pond revival); gender diversity up 9%.

Headwinds (Challenges)

  • Leverage Pressure: DER at 6.61x (improved from 7.19x but elevated); total debt ₹36,107 Cr vs. net worth ₹5,068 Cr. Funding mix bank-heavy (57%) exposes to rate hikes.
  • Provisioning Load: ECL ₹390 Cr + overlays ₹99 Cr = total ₹495 Cr; delinquency pool ~9.6% (SMA+NPA).
  • Margin Squeeze Potential: Yield stable but competition/int rates could compress NIM (historical dip noted in presentation).
  • Slowing Disbursement Growth: Q2 sequential dip; run-down ratio ~4.3% signals maturity profile.
  • Geographic Concentration: South/West dominant (e.g., 68% incremental disbursements); North/Central slower ramp-up.

Growth Prospects

  • AUM Trajectory: Targeting 15-20% CAGR (implied from presentation charts); H1 AUM +8%; focus on salaried/SENP (54% incremental), non-housing (26% of book).
  • Disbursement Upside: Avg. ticket ₹25L (housing)/₹14L (non-housing); direct sourcing/tech to boost. Roadmap-2028 emphasizes pan-India expansion.
  • Digital/Tech Leverage: Core IT rollout Q3 FY26 (LOS/LMS/HRMS); risk mgmt. tools live. Aims for seamless customer journey, fraud prevention.
  • Product Diversification: Top-ups, LAP, CRE growing; rooftop solar loans for green push.
  • Funding Diversification: Deposits growth (7.5% rate); CP A1+ rated. Promoter support for equity/debt.
  • Market Tailwinds: Housing demand (urbanization); HFC sector recovery post-RBI/NHB easing.

Projected FY26 Outlook: 12-15% AUM growth feasible (consensus-aligned), PAT ₹1,700-1,800 Cr, assuming stable macros.

Growth via tech/digital and diversification positions it well; monitor leverage and rates. Trading at ~2.5x FY26 BVPS (premium to peers on RoE/NPAs); attractive for long-term investors.

Broker Narrative

The first report was an early Buy despite near-term pain points (NPA uptick, opex spike, cost-of-funds inflation, NBFC liquidity stress), arguing growth, NIM expansion and provisioning buffers would dominate. The last report remained a Buy with the same core optimism on disbursements, NIM, and asset quality, but the risk language shifted from sector-level liquidity/IndAS optics to company-specific operational frictions: loan-book run-down from quarterly resets, elevated cost/income from branch/IT spending, and higher incremental term-loan rates. Asset quality and operating-expense/funding-cost concerns persisted across both reports, while NIM and liquidity/funding were consistently cited as offsets.

Fears that came true

  • The sequential rise in NPAs materialized: GNPA/NNPA inched up to 0.87%/0.42% by Q4FY26/Q1FY27, and several later Buy calls saw negative actual returns/disappointments.
  • Funding-cost inflation materialized: new term loan rates rose to 7.25-7.50% versus 6.95% a year earlier, pressuring incremental borrowings.
  • The opex worry materialized: the cost/income ratio stayed elevated at ~19-19.5% in FY27 due to IT implementation, branch expansion and manpower addition, echoing the earlier 34.9% opex spike.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected recovery/provisioning-buffer cushion did not lead to steadily improving asset quality: GNPA still rose from 0.85% to 0.87%, and later reports were followed by negative stock returns.
  • The projected steady loan-growth trajectory was not sustained: by Q1FY27 the loan book ran down 4.4% sequentially and grew only 10.8% YoY, well below the ~19% CAGR originally projected.
