Can Fin Homes — Recent Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Regulatory penalty for Fair Practices Code non-compliance. On 19 June 2026 the company received a speaking order from the Reserve Bank of India imposing a monetary penalty of Rs. 2.70 lakh under Section 52A of the National Housing Bank Act, 1987. The violation related to Paragraph 80.4 of the Master Direction – Non-Banking Financial Company – Housing Finance Company (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2021, which requires instalments collected from borrowers to clearly indicate the bifurcation between interest and principal. The violation was observed during a statutory inspection by the National Housing Bank with reference to the company’s financial position as on 31 March 2025. The company states there is no material financial or operational impact from the penalty and that it implemented corrective measures effective 1 April 2026 by incorporating the principal/interest bifurcation in the Statement of Account.

Declining yield and spread compression. Yield on the loan portfolio declined from 10.09% in Q1 FY26 to 9.81% in Q1 FY27, and from 9.99% in Q4 FY26 to 9.81% in Q1 FY27. Spread narrowed to 2.83% in Q1 FY27 from 2.92% in Q4 FY26 and 2.62% in Q1 FY26. Net interest margin at 3.81% in Q1 FY27 was below the 4.19% recorded in Q4 FY26.

Sequential moderation in profitability. Profit after tax of Rs. 268 crore in Q1 FY27 was lower than Rs. 346 crore in Q4 FY26, and return on average assets (2.39%) and return on average equity (17.15%) were both below the Q4 FY26 levels of 3.29% and 23.12% respectively. New approvals (Rs. 2,795 crore) and disbursements (Rs. 2,609 crore) in Q1 FY27 were also below Q4 FY26 levels (Rs. 3,523 crore and Rs. 3,246 crore).

Rising run-down and delinquency pool. Run-down ratio rose to 4.40% in Q1 FY27, the highest in the series shown from Q1 FY23, reflecting higher amortisation and pre-payment relative to opening balances. The total delinquency pool stood at Rs. 3,442 crore (8.01% of outstanding) in Q1 FY27, up from Rs. 3,375 crore (7.99%) in Q4 FY26.

Cost-income ratio elevated. The cost-income ratio was 19.52% in Q1 FY27, higher than 18.33% in Q1 FY26, though marginally below 19.84% in Q4 FY26.

Dependence on DSA sourcing. DSAs accounted for 80% of total business in Q1 FY27, with active DSAs at 1,775. The share of top-20 DSAs was 8% of total business in Q1 FY27, down from 10% in FY24 and FY25.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Year-on-year growth across key metrics. Loan assets grew 11% to Rs. 42,961 crore at June 2026 from Rs. 38,773 crore at June 2025. Disbursements grew 29% to Rs. 2,609 crore, new approvals grew 36% to Rs. 2,795 crore, net interest income grew 18% to Rs. 427 crore, profit before tax grew 22% to Rs. 339 crore and profit after tax grew 20% to Rs. 268 crore.

Falling cost of borrowing. Cost of borrowing declined steadily from 7.47% in Q1 FY26 to 6.98% in Q1 FY27, supporting the spread despite lower yields.

Improved asset quality. Gross NPA ratio improved to 0.87% in Q1 FY27 from 0.98% in Q1 FY26, and net NPA ratio improved to 0.42% from 0.54%. ECL provisions halved year-on-year to Rs. 13 crore from Rs. 26 crore.

Strong provisioning coverage. Total provision available (ECL Stage 3 plus management overlay) was 70% of GNPA in Q1 FY27 versus 61% in Q1 FY26; including provision for the restructured pool, coverage was 81% versus 74%. Total provisions held were Rs. 519 crore as per the ECL model.

Comfortable liquidity. Liquidity Coverage Ratio stood at 250.32% as against the stipulated 100%, with documented undrawn bank lines of Rs. 1,524.55 crore as of 30 June 2026, stated to cover business commitments for the next three months.

High credit ratings. Term loans, NHB borrowings, NCDs and public deposits are rated AAA/Stable by CARE and/or ICRA and IND Ra; commercial papers are rated A1+ by CARE and ICRA. The fixed deposit programme is rated AAA by ICRA with stable outlook.

Technology transformation progress. Risk management and ALM, treasury, finance and accounting (Sprint-1 and Sprint-2), HRMS, Aadhaar Data Vault, DMS and deposits applications have been implemented across Q2 FY26 to Q4 FY26. The Loan Origination System, Loan Management System and reports modules are in pilot phase implemented in Q1 FY27. The company completed the pilot launch of its IBM-led Core Banking Solution across 5 branches.

Strategic roadmap and stated priorities. The presentation sets out “Reinforce” (thrust on growth, asset quality, profitability and liquidity; focus on governance, compliance, audit and risk), “Rebuild” (technology adoption, direct sourcing to enhance the loan portfolio) and “Reach Higher” (expansion of sourcing channels, environment-friendly products). A “Roadmap – 2028” is referenced.

Growth catalysts in sourcing. Business from old branches rose from Rs. 8,308 crore in FY25 to Rs. 8,822 crore in FY26; sales team sourcing rose from Rs. 130 crore to Rs. 883 crore; new branches rose from Rs. 179 crore to Rs. 826 crore. Total sourcing rose from Rs. 8,568 crore in FY25 to Rs. 10,531 crore in FY26, with Q1 FY27 at Rs. 2,609 crore.

Product and inclusion initiatives. The company launched the Rooftop Solar Loan Scheme (3–10 KW systems), introduced “Can Fin Vishwas” for marginalized and vulnerable groups, and runs “PMAY Lantern Nights” under PMAY-U 2.0 to promote the Interest Subsidy Scheme.

ESG rating improvement. CRISIL ESG Ratings & Analytics Limited assigned an ESG rating of 69 (Strong) for FY 2025-26, up from the Crisil ESG 63 shown in the Q1 FY27 presentation. Other ESG ratings shown include 60 (ESG Risk Assessments & Insights), 78.7 (SES ESG Research), 80 (CFC Finlease) and 73+ (NSE Sustainability Ratings and Analytics).

Network and reach. 250 branches/offices across 21 states/union territories as on 30 June 2026, with average business per branch of Rs. 172 crore in Q1 FY27 versus Rs. 167 crore in Q1 FY26.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory and compliance risk: the RBI penalty for Fair Practices Code non-compliance illustrates exposure to regulatory action; the company has stated corrective measures are in place.
  • Asset quality risk: while GNPA and NNPA ratios improved year-on-year, the total delinquency pool rose sequentially to Rs. 3,442 crore (8.01% of outstanding) in Q1 FY27.
  • Margin and yield risk: yield on the loan portfolio has declined for five consecutive quarters (10.09% to 9.81%), and the share of annual reset loans to total AUM fell sharply to 13.18% in Q1 FY27 from 66.58% in Q1 FY26, which affects repricing dynamics.
  • Prepayment/run-down risk: run-down ratio at 4.40% in Q1 FY27 is the highest in the disclosed series, requiring continued disbursement growth to sustain the book.
  • Concentration in sourcing: 80% of business is DSA-sourced, exposing the company to channel dependence.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: the presentation notes that forward-looking statements and projections involve known and unknown risks, uncertainties and other factors including competitive environment, ability to implement strategies, technological changes, and political, economic, regulatory and social conditions in India, which may cause actual results to differ materially.
  • Event scheduling risk: the company notes that the ASHWAMEDH – ELARA INDIA DIALOGUE 2026 meeting schedule may undergo changes due to exigencies on the part of the investor, arranger or the company.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements. The presentation’s strategic themes commit the company to continued thrust on growth, asset quality, profitability and liquidity; unwavering focus on governance and due diligence; no deviation from fundamental ethos; priority to compliance, audit and risk; evolution through technology and digitalization; seamless integration of new processes; thrust on direct sourcing; and expansion of sourcing channels. A “Roadmap – 2028” is referenced. The company states it will continue to maintain required liquidity both on and off balance sheet. The investor meeting intimation states that no unpublished price sensitive information will be shared at the ASHWAMEDH – ELARA INDIA DIALOGUE 2026 on 1 September 2026.

Observed performance against these themes:

  • Growth: disbursements grew 29% and new approvals 36% year-on-year in Q1 FY27, and loan assets grew 11%, consistent with the stated growth thrust. However, sequentially, disbursements and approvals moderated from Q4 FY26.
  • Asset quality: GNPA ratio improved to 0.87% and NNPA to 0.42% year-on-year, and provisioning coverage strengthened to 81% including the restructured pool, consistent with the stated asset quality focus. The delinquency pool, however, rose sequentially.
  • Profitability: PAT grew 20% year-on-year but declined sequentially from Q4 FY26; ROA and ROE were below Q4 FY26 levels.
  • Liquidity: LCR of 250.32% against a stipulated 100%, with undrawn bank lines of Rs. 1,524.55 crore stated to cover three months of commitments, consistent with the stated liquidity priority.
  • Technology: implementation status shows most functional applications completed across Q2 FY26–Q4 FY26, with LOS, LMS and reports in pilot phase in Q1 FY27 and the IBM-led Core Banking Solution pilot across 5 branches, consistent with the stated digitalization thrust.
  • Direct sourcing: the presentation states a thrust on direct sourcing to enhance the loan portfolio, while DSA-sourced business remained at 80% of total business in Q1 FY27.
  • Compliance: the RBI penalty relates to a violation observed in the inspection referencing the financial position as on 31 March 2025; corrective measures were implemented effective 1 April 2026, and the company states it is now in compliance.

Governance and shareholder matters. The 39th AGM was held on 29 July 2026 at 11:00 am through video conferencing/OAVM. Remote e-voting was open from 25 July 2026 (9:00 am) to 28 July 2026 (5:00 pm) via NSDL. The record date was 22 July 2026, with 93,494 shareholders on record. 92 shareholders attended through video conferencing (1 promoter/promoter group and 91 public). All 8 resolutions proposed in the AGM notice were passed with the requisite majority.

Broker Narrative

The first report was an early Buy despite near-term pain points (NPA uptick, opex spike, cost-of-funds inflation, NBFC liquidity stress), arguing growth, NIM expansion and provisioning buffers would dominate. The last report remained a Buy with the same core optimism on disbursements, NIM, and asset quality, but the risk language shifted from sector-level liquidity/IndAS optics to company-specific operational frictions: loan-book run-down from quarterly resets, elevated cost/income from branch/IT spending, and higher incremental term-loan rates. Asset quality and operating-expense/funding-cost concerns persisted across both reports, while NIM and liquidity/funding were consistently cited as offsets.

Fears that came true

  • The sequential rise in NPAs materialized: GNPA/NNPA inched up to 0.87%/0.42% by Q4FY26/Q1FY27, and several later Buy calls saw negative actual returns/disappointments.
  • Funding-cost inflation materialized: new term loan rates rose to 7.25-7.50% versus 6.95% a year earlier, pressuring incremental borrowings.
  • The opex worry materialized: the cost/income ratio stayed elevated at ~19-19.5% in FY27 due to IT implementation, branch expansion and manpower addition, echoing the earlier 34.9% opex spike.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected recovery/provisioning-buffer cushion did not lead to steadily improving asset quality: GNPA still rose from 0.85% to 0.87%, and later reports were followed by negative stock returns.
  • The projected steady loan-growth trajectory was not sustained: by Q1FY27 the loan book ran down 4.4% sequentially and grew only 10.8% YoY, well below the ~19% CAGR originally projected.
