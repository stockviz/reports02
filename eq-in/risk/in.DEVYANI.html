Devyani International — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Cost inflation: Management flagged inflation in raw materials, LPG, and wage costs during Q1 FY27, even as profitability improved.
  • Volatile operating environment and seasonality: The Chairman described the operating environment as volatile with “usual seasonal complexities,” and noted that demand has remained stable so far.
  • Weather-related consumption risk: A forecast of a below-normal season combined with El Niño risk was cited as a reminder that consumption recovery in India “rarely moves in a straight line.”
  • Pizza Hut store rationalisation: Pizza Hut recorded negative net new units in Q1 FY27 (−13), following −12 in Q1 FY26 and 0 in Q4 FY26, with the Yum! Brands portfolio overall contracting by 2 net units in the quarter.
  • Total store count declined sequentially: Total DIL stores moved from 2,256 as at 31 March 2026 to 2,255 as at 30 June 2026, with India down 2 net units (1,857 to 1,855) and Nigeria down 1.
  • International ADS pressure: Thailand KFC average daily sales were flat at THB 55,000 across Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 (down from THB 56,000 in Q1 FY26).
  • Merger execution risk: The Sapphire Foods merger remains subject to regulatory processes, with completion targeted by the end of FY27.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Broad-based positive SSSG: KFC +3.3%, Costa +10.2%, Biryani by Kilo +7.2%, Vaango +7.1%; Pizza Hut delivered sequentially better SSSG.
  • Record profitability: Highest ever quarterly EBITDA of INR 2.55 billion at 16.1% margin; operating EBITDA grew 38% year-on-year to INR 1,511 million (9.6% margin); PBT of INR 229 million was up more than 6x year-on-year and the highest in 8 quarters; PAT of INR 171 million was the highest in 8 quarters.
  • Revenue growth: Consolidated revenues of INR 15,805 million, up 16.5% year-on-year; India revenue INR 10,709 million (+14.9%); KFC India INR 6,842 million (+11.7%); international business INR 5,230 million (+20.7%).
  • Margin expansion: Consolidated gross margin at 69.1% (+0.9% vs Q1 FY26); brand contribution margin at 14.2% vs 13.1%; reported EBITDA margin up 100 bps.
  • Dine-in focused strategy working as planned: Revenue improvement in dine-in and takeaway channels for both KFC and Pizza Hut, supported by dine-in exclusive offers and a two-pronged strategy (offline for recruitment and value; online for convenience and accessibility).
  • Store expansion on track: KFC added 11 net new stores in India and 2 in Thailand; BBK added 3 net; the yearly store opening plan is proceeding as per initial guidance, with the network at 2,255 stores.
  • Merger progress: NSE and BSE approvals received in June 2026, moving the Sapphire Foods merger closer to the next phase of regulatory filings, with completion targeted by end-FY27; a joint CCI application has been filed.
  • Revised merger structure: Following mutual termination of the SFML–Arctic share purchase agreement, the amended Scheme removes the Secondary Sale Transaction as a condition precedent; SFML will receive equity shares of the Transferee Company under the Scheme, and post-Scheme promoter shareholding is revised to 41.99%.
  • Management transition: The new management team is fully in place under Manish Dawar, with early cultural and operational shifts under the “DIL 2.0” initiative.

3. Key Risks

  • Cost inflation in raw materials, LPG, and wages.
  • Weather and El Niño-related consumption uncertainty.
  • Continued net store closures at Pizza Hut and flat-to-negative momentum in parts of the Yum! Brands portfolio.
  • International market softness, including flat Thailand ADS and a net store decline in Nigeria.
  • Execution and regulatory risk associated with the Sapphire Foods merger, including the revised promoter shareholding structure and completion timeline.
  • Forward-looking statements are subject to risks including changes in the industry structure, political and economic environment in India and overseas, tax laws, import duties, litigation, and labour relations.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Store expansion: Management stated the yearly store opening plan is proceeding “as per initial guidance,” and the company is “on-track to deliver store expansion targets for FY2027.” Observed: total store count was essentially flat sequentially (2,256 to 2,255), with KFC India net additions of 11 offset by Pizza Hut’s −13 and other declines.
  • Merger timeline: Management stated the merger is “broadly on track with our stated target of completion by the end of FY27,” with NSE and BSE approvals received in June 2026 and CCI filing underway. Observed: regulatory milestones are progressing, but completion remains pending.
  • Profitability trajectory: Management highlighted “sustainable profitable growth” despite cost inflation. Observed: EBITDA, PBT, and PAT all improved to multi-quarter or record highs in Q1 FY27, with margin expansion across gross margin, brand contribution, and reported EBITDA.
  • Demand environment: Management described demand as stable so far while cautioning on weather-related uncertainty. Observed: positive SSSG across most brands supports the stable demand characterization.
  • Governance: The 35th AGM on 14 August 2026 passed all four resolutions with requisite majority; the auditors’ reports contained no negative qualification or observation.

Broker Narrative

Broker stance flipped from Sell (May 2023) to Buy (Jul 2026), with persistent concerns over raw material inflation, Pizza Hut weakness, and elevated costs offset by growing optimism on KFC’s SSSG, margin recovery, and Devyani-Sapphire merger synergies. Early fear of industry slowdown remained valid for Pizza Hut, but KFC emerged as a bright spot, while the narrative shifted from defensive to aggressive growth driven by store expansion and international performance.

Fears that came true

  • Raw material inflation and gross margin pressure persisted, as the last report still cited food commodity inflation, wage hikes, and higher LPG costs.
  • Industry slowdown and muted SSSG materialized for Pizza Hut, which continued to post negative SSSG of -2.2% and saw 13 net store closures.
  • Decline in average daily sales continued, with Pizza Hut ADS down 3% YoY and KFC ADS stable at INR98k (below the earlier INR106k).
  • Brand contribution drop persisted, with Pizza Hut brand contribution remaining marginally negative.

Optimism that failed

  • Medium-term SSSG targets were missed: KFC’s 3.3% fell short of the 5-6% target, and Pizza Hut’s -2.2% was far below the 7-8% target.
  • Inflation moderation expectation failed, as cost pressures from commodities, wages, and LPG remained elevated in the last report.
