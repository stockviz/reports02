Headwinds and Challenges

Weak consolidated profitability despite revenue growth. In Q1 FY27, consolidated revenue rose 24% year-on-year to ₹508.5 crore, but adjusted EBITDA grew only 18.6% to ₹118.8 crore, reported EBITDA grew just 8.6% to ₹108.8 crore, and profit after tax fell 20% to ₹57.1 crore. Consolidated EBITDA margin contracted to 21.4% from 24.4%, and PAT margin fell to 11.2% from 17.4%. The consolidated results were dragged by a ₹10 crore unrealized forex loss, higher employee cost (₹93.3 crore vs ₹72.5 crore), higher other expenses (₹83.7 crore vs ₹56.8 crore), higher finance cost (₹24.3 crore vs ₹12.2 crore), and lower other income (₹4.0 crore vs ₹20.5 crore).

Standalone margin pressure at the PAT level. Standalone revenue grew 36.7% to ₹509.1 crore and adjusted EBITDA rose 46.7% to ₹144.7 crore, but PAT grew only 21.3% to ₹87.5 crore. PAT margin declined to 17.2% from 19.4%, EBIT margin fell to 25.6% from 27.5%, and finance cost rose sharply to ₹20.0 crore from ₹6.9 crore. A ₹6 crore unrealized forex loss also weighed on reported EBITDA.

French judicial investigation into Huron Graffenstaden SAS. Based on suspicion of exporting certain machines with dual-use technology in violation of European Union laws, investigation agencies in France initiated a judicial investigation against Huron Graffenstaden SAS and certain employees. Interim measures include restriction on the Director General from discharging his duties, control over the export license of certain machines, interim seizure of funds totaling Euro 3.02 million, and seizure of two residential properties owned by Jyoti SAS. The investigation is ongoing.

Erosion of subsidiary net worth and no impairment taken. The company’s subsidiary, which further invested in the operating step-down subsidiary, has seen net worth erosion due to losses over certain years. Management has not provided for impairment, citing expected recovery in the near future and the strategic nature of the investment. The auditor drew attention to this without qualifying the opinion.

Working capital absorption. In FY26, changes in working capital absorbed ₹309.1 crore, and net cash from operating activities was only ₹54.4 crore after an exceptional item outflow of ₹32.9 crore. Inventories stood at ₹1,166.0 crore and trade receivables at ₹599.1 crore as of March 2026.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong order book. Total consolidated order book stood at ₹4,848 crore as of June 30, 2026, up from ₹4,732 crore at March 31, 2026. The order book is diversified across aerospace and defence, auto and auto components, die and moulds, general engineering, EMS, and others.

Meity approval for a large capex program. On August 17, 2026, the company’s ₹1,020.65 crore capital investment proposal over five years at its Rajkot facility received approval under the Electronic Components Manufacturing Scheme of the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology. The investment is for expansion of installed capacity and a backward-integrated manufacturing facility for electronic devices used to build CNC machines. The company would be eligible for a capex incentive of up to 25% on the investments, subject to scheme stipulations.

Capacity expansion. Capacity enhancement to 6,000 machines per annum is already completed. A further enhancement of an additional 10,000 machines per annum is targeted for completion by September 2026.

Product launches and development. The company launched the NX 6240 EY high-performance double column machine in Q1 FY27, targeting railways, commercial vehicles, infrastructure, power, heavy engineering, and die and moulds. Recent product development includes the GU 8 five-axis gantry machining center, HP 4000 and HP 6000 horizontal machining centers, AWT 22 alloy wheel turning machine, BTM 100 twin spindle with gantry automation, ATM 200 inverted turning center, and Tachyon Beta five-axis high dynamics machining center.

Diversified end-user industry mix. Consolidated revenue mix in Q1 FY27 included aerospace and defence (26%), auto and auto components (36%), general engineering (17%), die and moulds (6%), EMS (1%), and others (14%). The EMS share rose from 0% in Q1 FY26 to 1% in Q1 FY27, and aerospace and defence rose from 30% to 26% (with the mix shifting toward auto).

Industry growth drivers. The company cites growth drivers including aerospace and defence (global market expected to reach ~USD 2,037 billion by 2034 at 8.6% CAGR), electric vehicles (global EV market expected to reach ~USD 4,886 billion by 2034 at 20.43% CAGR; India EV market expected to grow to USD 191 billion at ~55% CAGR during 2026–2034), semiconductors (Indian semiconductor market expected to grow from USD 52 billion in FY2024-25 to USD 103.4 billion by 2030 at 13% CAGR), and EMS (India’s electronic components demand projected at $240 billion by 2030; potential CNC machine demand for the EMS industry in India is over 1,00,000 machines within five years).

Global position. India ranks 9th in global machine tool consumption and 4th in production. The company has installed over 1,40,000 machines across more than 60 countries, with 200+ product variants, 29 sales and service centres across 12 states in India, and foreign subsidiaries in France, Germany, Canada, and Turkey.

Key Risks

Geopolitical and regulatory risk in France. The ongoing judicial investigation into Huron Graffenstaden SAS, with restrictions on the Director General, export license controls, and seizure of funds and properties, represents a material regulatory and operational risk to the European business.

Forex risk. Unrealized forex losses of ₹6 crore (standalone) and ₹10 crore (consolidated) in Q1 FY27 affected reported profitability.

Impairment risk on subsidiary investments. The subsidiary’s net worth has eroded, and no impairment has been provided. If the expected recovery does not materialize, impairment charges could follow.

Margin sustainability. Consolidated EBITDA margin declined year-on-year in Q1 FY27 despite revenue growth, with rising employee, other, and finance costs.

Working capital and cash flow risk. FY26 operating cash flow was modest relative to profitability, with significant working capital absorption.

Concentration in end-user industries. Auto and auto components accounted for 36% of Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue, and aerospace and defence for 26%, making performance sensitive to cycles in these sectors.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Capacity expansion guidance. Management states that capacity enhancement to 6,000 machines per annum is already completed, and a further enhancement of an additional 10,000 machines per annum is to be completed by September 2026. Observed performance: the company reported a strong order book of ₹4,848 crore as of June 30, 2026, which provides visibility for the expanded capacity.

Product development guidance. Management highlights ongoing R&D leverage to design and develop new product variants in line with global industry demands. Observed performance: the NX 6240 EY was launched in Q1 FY27, and recent products include the GU 8, HP 4000/6000, AWT 22, BTM 100, ATM 200, and Tachyon Beta.

Meity capex incentive guidance. The company states it would be eligible for a capex incentive of up to 25% on investments under the scheme, subject to stipulations. Observed performance: the approval letter was issued on August 17, 2026, and will be placed before the board for consideration.

Subsidiary recovery guidance. Management is of the opinion that no impairment is warranted in view of expected recovery in the near future of the operating step-down subsidiary’s business. Observed performance: the French judicial investigation and interim restrictions remain ongoing, and the subsidiary continues to make losses.

Financial performance guidance. The investor presentation does not provide explicit numerical revenue or margin guidance for future periods. Observed performance: Q1 FY27 standalone revenue grew 36.7% year-on-year, but consolidated PAT declined 20% year-on-year, with consolidated EBITDA margin contracting to 21.4% from 24.4%.
