CHENNPETRO: Q1 FY2026–27 Performance and Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Lower crude throughput year-on-year. Crude throughput for Q1 FY2026–27 stood at 2.848 MMT, down from 2.981 MMT in the corresponding quarter of the previous year. Sequentially, throughput also declined from 2.930 MMT in Q4 FY2025–26.

Board composition non-compliance. The Statutory Auditors’ reports (both standalone and consolidated) note that the Company did not have the minimum number of Independent Directors, including one Woman Independent Director, as required under the Companies Act, 2013 and SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015, throughout the reporting period. Additionally, the requirement of having two-thirds of Independent Directors on the Audit Committee and the Nomination & Remuneration Committee was not complied with throughout the reporting period. The auditors state that appointments of Independent Directors, including one Woman Independent Director, are under consideration of the Government of India as of the date of reporting.

Finance costs increased. Finance costs rose to ₹52.14 crore in Q1 FY2026–27 from ₹37.04 crore in Q1 FY2025–26 and ₹16.42 crore in Q4 FY2025–26.

Sequential decline in profitability. Profit Before Tax declined from ₹1,890.40 crore in Q4 FY2025–26 to ₹1,365.56 crore in Q1 FY2026–27 on a standalone basis. Profit After Tax declined from ₹1,399.70 crore to ₹1,016.67 crore over the same period.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Navratna Status granted. On 19 June 2026, the Government of India granted Navratna Status to Chennai Petroleum Corporation Limited, as conveyed by the Department of Public Enterprises vide letter PD-26/0001/2025-PD-(14347) dated 19 June 2026. This was disclosed under Regulation 30 of SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015.

Sharp improvement in Gross Refining Margin. Average GRM for April–June 2026 was US$ 8.78 per barrel, compared to US$ 3.22 per barrel in the same period last year. The GRM excludes additional revenue of ₹385.21 crore arising from retrospective price revisions pertaining to the previous financial year.

Return to profitability. The Company reported Profit Before Tax of ₹1,365.56 crore and Profit After Tax of ₹1,016.67 crore for Q1 FY2026–27, compared to a Loss Before Tax of ₹80.10 crore and Loss After Tax of ₹56.62 crore in Q1 FY2025–26.

Highest ever distillate yield. The Company recorded its highest ever distillate yield during the quarter.

High capacity utilisation. Crude throughput of 2.848 MMT translated to a capacity utilisation of 108%, which the Company attributes to efficient plant operations and high reliability.

Revenue growth. Revenue from Operations rose to ₹29,358.75 crore in Q1 FY2026–27 from ₹18,683.36 crore in Q1 FY2025–26.

Retrospective price revision benefit. Pursuant to a retrospective revision in prices effective 16 March 2026 for certain petroleum products, additional revenue of ₹385.21 crore relating to supplies made during March 2026 was recognised during the current quarter.

Consolidated performance. On a consolidated basis, Q1 FY2026–27 Profit Before Tax was ₹1,380.24 crore and Profit After Tax was ₹1,031.35 crore, including share of profit from Joint Ventures/Associates of ₹14.68 crore.

Dividend approvals at AGM. The 60th Annual General Meeting held on 26 August 2026 passed all eight resolutions, including approval of a preference dividend of 6.65% amounting to ₹15.94 crore and a final equity dividend of ₹54/- per equity share for the year 2025–26.

3. Key Risks

Governance and compliance risk. The auditors’ “Other Matters” paragraphs in both standalone and consolidated reports flag non-compliance with board composition requirements under the Companies Act, 2013 and SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015, throughout the reporting period. The auditors’ opinion is not modified in respect of these matters.

Dependence on government action for board appointments. The appointment of Independent Directors, including one Woman Independent Director, is stated to be under consideration of the Government of India as of the date of reporting, leaving the timeline for resolution uncertain.

Throughput volatility. Crude throughput declined both year-on-year (from 2.981 MMT to 2.848 MMT) and sequentially (from 2.930 MMT to 2.848 MMT), indicating potential variability in operational volumes.

Single-segment concentration. The Company operates only in one segment, the Petroleum Sector, and reporting is done on a single segment basis, implying concentration risk.

Reliance on unaudited financial information for consolidated results. The auditors note that the financial results and other financial information of two Jointly Controlled Entities and an Associate, reflecting the Parent’s share of net profit of ₹14.68 crore and total comprehensive income of ₹14.68 crore for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, are unaudited and have been furnished by the Management. The auditors’ opinion on the consolidated statement, insofar as it relates to these entities, is based solely on such unaudited financial statements.

No outstanding listed Non-Convertible Debentures. The Company confirmed there is no outstanding listed Non-Convertible Debentures as on the reporting dates, and there is no default in payment of outstanding Loans/revolving facilities or debt securities.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

The supplied material does not contain explicit forward-looking guidance or quantitative targets from management for future periods. The available evidence of management commentary versus observed performance is as follows:

  • The Company characterised Q1 FY2026–27 as delivering “outstanding operational results” and “sustained operational excellence,” citing crude throughput of 2.85 MMT, capacity utilisation of 108%, and highest ever distillate yield. Observed throughput of 2.848 MMT was, however, lower than the 2.981 MMT recorded in Q1 FY2025–26 and lower than the 2.930 MMT recorded in Q4 FY2025–26.
  • The Company stated that financial results reflected “robust physical performance and improved refining margins.” This is consistent with the observed GRM increase from US$ 3.22 per barrel in Q1 FY2025–26 to US$ 8.78 per barrel in Q1 FY2026–27, and the swing from a loss to a profit.
  • The Company disclosed that the additional revenue of ₹385.21 crore from retrospective price revisions was excluded from the reported GRM, as it pertains to the previous financial year.
  • The CEO/CFO certification for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 states that, to the best of their knowledge and belief, the financial results do not contain any false or misleading statement/figures and do not omit any material fact.
  • The Statutory Auditors issued unmodified opinions on both the standalone and consolidated financial statements for the quarter ended 30 June 2026.
  • The Board meeting to approve the results was held on 23 July 2026, with the Audit Committee recommendation and Board approval both dated 23 July 2026.

Broker Narrative

The first report centered on refining economics: strong demand, high throughput, Russian crude discounts, and deleveraging, with subsidy-sharing and inventory losses as risks. The last report maintains refining recovery—strong cracks, better diesel marketing margins, improving throughput—but shifts subsidy from a headwind to a tailwind as LPG burden recovery begins, and broadens the narrative to include telecom and retail metrics. Persistent themes are refining throughput and marketing margins; changed themes are crude-price-driven inventory effects (now forex depreciation) and the treatment of subsidy policy.

Fears that came true

  • The feared crude price changes and inventory losses materialized, as evidenced by multiple DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes with double-digit negative actual returns in 2024 and 2025.
  • The adverse government policy/subsidy-sharing risk materialized as an LPG subsidy burden that weighed on earnings until its recovery began only in Nov’25.

Optimism that failed

  • The expected Russian crude discounts and inventory gains failed to materialize; instead, crude price swings produced inventory losses and dragged actual returns below forecasts.
