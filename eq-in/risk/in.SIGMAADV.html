Headwinds and Challenges

  • Technology obsolescence risk from rapid advances in AI and autonomous systems.
  • Programme and execution risk tied to long development and qualification cycles.
  • Customer concentration risk.
  • Integration risk from recent acquisitions.
  • Geopolitical and export-control risk.
  • Supply-chain disruption vulnerabilities.
  • Working-capital pressures during rapid expansion.
  • Regulatory and certification requirements.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • A structural supercycle in global aerospace and defence, driven by rising air travel demand, fleet renewal, geopolitical tensions, and increased defence spending such as NATO commitments.
  • In India, the ‘Make in India’ initiative, growing defence production, indigenisation mandates, and export promotion.
  • Growing counter-drone demand.
  • Faster technology transfers from legacy hubs.
  • A multi-year order book exceeding Rs. 8,000 Crore with major international contracts.
  • The Sri City aerospace facility transitioned from ground-breaking to active production in eight months, providing 100,000 machining hours of annual capacity, with plans to expand to nearly 300,000 annual machining hours and scale the campus to 500,000 sq ft over the next 12 months.
  • Management expects the Sri City facility to drive double-digit EBITA expansion and create specialized employment for more than 500 people over the next two to three years.
  • Initial stages of the source-change approval process with Rolls-Royce Aerospace are complete, with approximately 70% of the manufacturing process expected to be undertaken in India once capabilities are fully operational.
  • The company operates through UK subsidiaries Nasmyth and Bromford.

Key Risks

  • The risks listed above, including technology obsolescence, programme and execution risk, customer concentration, acquisition integration, geopolitical and export-control exposure, supply-chain disruption, working-capital pressure during expansion, and regulatory and certification requirements.
  • The Sri City ramp-up depends on commissioning additional aerospace-critical processes, progressing NADCAP accreditation, and increasing the proportion of manufacturing undertaken in India.

Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

  • Management targets an EBITDA improvement of 2–4% by the end of FY 26-27 and a further 4–6% through FY 27-28 via the work transfer program.
  • Sri City operations are guided to commence in Q3 FY 27, followed by NDT capabilities in Q4 FY 27.
  • Management is working toward a first major revenue milestone of Rs. 5,000 Crore across the foreseeable future.
  • Management expects the remaining Sri City campus to be operational in the next 12 months, scaling to 500,000 sq ft and nearly 300,000 annual machining hours, with double-digit EBITA expansion and more than 500 employees over the next two to three years.
  • Observed performance: the Sri City facility was transitioned into active production after eight months, with an initial 75,000 sq ft operational facility delivering 100,000 annual machining hours on a larger planned 500,000 sq ft campus.
  • For FY 2025-26, consolidated revenue from operations reached Rs. 49,187.74 lakhs (standalone revenue was Rs. 15,764.16 lakhs), and consolidated profit after tax touched Rs. 26,558.40 lakhs.
  • More than 90% of the order book is international, totaling around Rs. 8,000 Crore as of April 2026.
