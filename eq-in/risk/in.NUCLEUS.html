Headwinds and Challenges

Margin compression and profitability decline. In the quarter ended June 30, 2026, consolidated revenue was INR 210.4 crore, up 6.4% QoQ and 3.4% YoY, but EBITDA fell to INR 7.6 crore (3.6% margin) from INR 34.6 crore (15.4%) in the prior quarter and INR 33.7 crore (15.5%) a year earlier. Profit after tax was INR 23.9 crore, down 30.9% QoQ and 32.2% YoY. Cost of delivery rose to 76.2% of revenue from 70% QoQ and 70.1% YoY; marketing and sales expenses rose to 10% of revenue from 7.1% QoQ and 6% YoY; general and administrative expenses rose to 10.1% of revenue from 7.5% QoQ and 8.3% YoY.

Order book not converting into near-term revenue. Management acknowledged a mismatch between a rising order book and P&L performance. The order book rose to INR 1,244 crore as of June 30, 2026 (88%, or INR 1,096 crore, from products) from INR 1,044 crore as of March 31, 2026 (86%, or INR 899 crore, from products), yet revenue declined 3% YoY. Management attributed this to long-range contracts (some extending up to five years), AMC components within the order book, and implementation cycles that vary by customer complexity and legacy migration volumes.

Client concentration and competitive risk. Revenue from the top five clients was 28.1% of revenue, down from 29.3% in the prior quarter. Management confirmed the loss of a top NBFC client that had invested in a competitor, describing it as a one-off case, while acknowledging that theoretically any top customer could invest in a competitor.

India revenue decline. India constituted 56% of revenue versus 58% in the prior quarter. Segment profit before tax for India fell to INR 4,667 lakh (consolidated) from INR 6,676 lakh QoQ and INR 7,430 lakh YoY. Management attributed part of this to a one-time event in the prior quarter involving a special capitalization initiative for a large customer.

Rising receivables and DSO. Receivables rose to INR 174.7 crore as of June 30, 2026, from INR 121.1 crore as of March 31, 2026. DSO increased to 63 days from 42 days in the prior quarter.

Attrition. Attrition rose to 3% in the quarter from 2% in the prior quarter.

Pricing pressure. Management stated that pricing remains a challenge amid competition, and that the company has chosen to remain a premium player while competition affects efforts to raise prices.

Cost inflation. Management cited higher marketing expenses from global market investments and increased administrative costs partly due to diesel and power price fluctuations.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong order book momentum. The company recorded its largest order book addition of over INR 600 crore in a single quarter (the March 2026 quarter) and locked in excess of INR 300 crore in the June 2026 quarter. Over the last 15 months, approximately 10 new logos were added.

New logo wins. Management stated the order book increase came significantly from new logos, with a reasonable part from existing customers renewing AMCs at better prices. Three new logos were added in the June 2026 quarter.

AI strategy progress. Management stated the enterprise AI foundation is in place, an organization-wide learning and upskilling initiative is underway, and the full-blown release 9.0 was launched with embedded AI capabilities across the lending life cycle. Management expects AI productivity benefits to flow in as AI usage strengthens internally, though tangible benefits are not yet being measured.

Geographic expansion. Management identified Middle East and Southeast Asia as big bets, noted growing footing in U.S. markets, and stated the company is focusing on high-margin deals in geographies beyond India. The order book increase was largely from FinnOne Neo, mostly from India, Middle East, and America.

Product upgrade milestone. Stanbic IBTC Bank, a member of Standard Bank Group, advanced to FinnAxia 9.0 on August 12, 2026, marking more than a decade of collaboration. The upgrade strengthens integrated payments, collections, liquidity management, and cash management capabilities.

Customer relationship milestones. The company completed five years with Hatton National Bank in Sri Lanka, a FinnAxia customer.

New leadership. Bhavit Godiwala joined as Chief Customer Success Officer with nearly three decades of experience across banking technology, digital transformation, core banking, and transaction banking.

U.S. market opportunity. Management noted the possibility of converting GM Financial’s North American business to Nucleus, and expects to build on a U.S. order won in the prior year in the auto finance segment.

Balance sheet strength. Total cash and cash equivalents were INR 977.9 crore as of June 30, 2026, versus INR 972.4 crore as of March 31, 2026. Other income was INR 17.8 crore, including INR 18.9 crore from investments and deposits and a forex loss of INR 1.1 crore, driven by mark-to-market gains on debt mutual funds from falling interest rates.

Dividend. A final dividend of INR 12.50 per share for FY2026 was approved by shareholders at the AGM on July 27, 2026, and paid on July 28, 2026.

Key Risks

Execution and conversion risk. The order book may not translate into revenue in line with historical patterns due to longer implementation cycles, AMC components, and customer-specific complexity.

Client concentration risk. Top five clients contributed 28.1% of revenue, and the loss of a major NBFC client to a competitor demonstrates vulnerability to customer defection.

Competitive pricing risk. Management acknowledged strict competition affecting pricing power and the ability to raise prices.

Geographic concentration. India constituted 56% of revenue, and India segment profitability declined significantly.

Margin sustainability risk. Management stated it is working on margin control initiatives and focusing on high-margin deals, but could not quantify the impact, expecting effects in subsequent quarters.

Receivables risk. DSO increased to 63 days from 42 days, and receivables rose sharply.

Talent and cost risk. Attrition increased, and the company continues investing in AI-native talent hiring, which may impact costs.

Regulatory and labour code impact. The Labour Codes notified on November 21, 2025, resulted in an increase in gratuity liability of INR 1,590 lakh and compensated absences liability of INR 605 lakh in the year ended March 31, 2026. In the June 2026 quarter, a revision in remuneration structure resulted in a reduction in gratuity liability of INR 699 lakh and compensated absences liability of INR 283 lakh, presented as exceptional items.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

No specific revenue guidance. Management explicitly stated the company does not provide specific revenue or earnings guidance.

Order book versus revenue. Management stated the order book is composed of contracts extending up to five years, with implementation cycles varying by customer, which explains why order book growth has not yet translated into revenue growth. Observed performance: order book rose from INR 1,044 crore to INR 1,244 crore QoQ, while revenue declined 3% YoY.

Sales and marketing investment. Management stated sales and marketing expenses are yielding results in the order book and that investments in strategic markets will continue with calibration. Observed performance: marketing and sales expenses rose to 10% of revenue from 7.1% QoQ and 6% YoY, while order book increased.

Employee benefit expenses. Management stated employee benefit expenses as a percentage of sales should come down as revenue increases, while continuing to invest in AI-native talent. Observed performance: employee benefits expense was INR 15,718 lakh (consolidated) in the June 2026 quarter, up from INR 15,147 lakh QoQ and INR 14,555 lakh YoY.

Margin control. Management stated it is working on margin control initiatives and focusing on high-margin deals, with effects expected in subsequent quarters, but could not quantify the impact. Observed performance: EBITDA margin was 3.6% in the June 2026 quarter versus 15.4% QoQ and 15.5% YoY.

AI productivity benefits. Management stated AI productivity benefits are expected to flow in as AI usage strengthens internally, but tangible benefits are not yet being measured. Observed performance: no quantified AI productivity benefits were reported.

Execution cycle. Management stated the execution cycle for the recent order book would be more or less in line with the existing track record, while noting AMC contracts are long-term and cannot be compared to implementation orders. Observed performance: revenue did not reflect the order book increase in the June 2026 quarter.

Geographic focus. Management identified Middle East and Southeast Asia as big bets and noted growing U.S. footing. Observed performance: Middle East revenue was INR 2,548 lakh (consolidated) in the June 2026 quarter versus INR 2,828 lakh QoQ and INR 2,322 lakh YoY; South East Asia revenue was INR 2,281 lakh versus INR 2,403 lakh QoQ and INR 2,618 lakh YoY.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative shifted from an initial Buy based on a strong order book and expected revenue/margin recovery in FY24 to a Reduce by July 2025 as growth and margin headwinds persisted. Early concerns about wage-cost pressure, muted revenue, and macro volatility remained central; the later report added tariff disruptions, Middle East tensions, and slower deal conversions. Optimism moved from order-book execution and demand recovery to cyclical tailwinds like currency gains and delayed BFSI spending, but actual returns were mostly negative throughout.

Fears that came true

  • The early flag of muted revenue growth after one-time revenue normalization materialized: the final report still cited muted growth and delayed deal conversions, and most actual returns were negative.
  • The early warning that wage hikes and higher headcount would pressure EBIT margins materialized: the last report again cited wage hikes and visa costs as limiting margin expansion.
  • The early macro-volatility risk materialized as tariff disruptions and elevated Middle East geo-political tensions in the last report, correlating with DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation that revenue momentum would pick up from Q2FY24 failed: subsequent actual returns were mostly negative and the final report still described muted growth.
  • The expectation that margins would improve once wage-hike impact normalized failed: the last report said margin expansion remained restrictive due to wage hikes and visa costs.
  • The optimism over a strong order book and healthy demand pipeline did not translate into growth: the final report cited deal signing delays and elongation of existing deal win conversion.
