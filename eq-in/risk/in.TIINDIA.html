In the consolidated segment presentation, Gears and Gear Products revenue declined to ₹115.13 Cr from ₹134.74 Cr sequentially, and segment results fell to ₹14.28 Cr from ₹25.03 Cr.

Semiconductor losses: The Semiconductors segment posted a loss of ₹(49.99) Cr, wider than the ₹(8.70) Cr loss a year earlier, though narrower than the ₹(37.42) Cr loss in Q4 FY2025-26.

Dependence on external auditors for subsidiary review: The statutory auditor’s consolidated review relies on review reports of other independent auditors for 20 subsidiaries and one joint venture, and on unaudited management-furnished information for 11 subsidiaries, 2 discontinued-operation subsidiaries, 1 associate and 2 joint ventures.

Broker Narrative

The broker maintained a Buy rating throughout, but the narrative shifted from an early bet on a broad auto-volume and export recovery to a 2026 story built on domestic engineering strength, GST cuts, and e-mobility optionality. The early EV-loss concern persisted and intensified, while later reports added specific execution delays and unresolved US trade barriers as headwinds. Tailwinds became more granular: EU FTA, Nashik/Phaltan ramp-up, e-3W/e-truck share gains, and CG Power growth replaced the earlier generic TI-2 optionality.

Fears that came true

  • The EV PBIT losses flagged at INR1b not only materialized but widened to INR1.6b in 3QFY26, a period during which several Buy calls ended as DISAPPOINTMENT with actual returns as low as -30.7% and -32.8%.
  • The early concern about low revenue/pressured domestic-cycle businesses materialized in Shanti Gears’ 26% YoY revenue decline amid softening demand, even as the broker stayed Buy.

Optimism that failed

  • The early expectation of an export recovery failed: the final report said EU and US exports remained weak, with India still facing a 50% import duty under US Section 232.
  • The TI-2 new-business ramp-up promised optionality but was only partially delivered, as EV losses widened and a new plant was delayed by 6-9 months.
