RAIN Industries — Q2 2026 Management Commentary Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Carbon segment volumes and deferred shipments. Carbon segment volumes were approximately 10 percent lower year-over-year in Q2 2026. Management attributed a net impact of roughly 10,000–15,000 metric tonnes to deferred shipments, after accounting for puts and takes across product groups. Utilisation remained at 69 percent, broadly in line with Q1 2026 and the calendar year 2025 average.

Product mix and reporting constraints. Lower Carbon calcination volumes were partly offset by higher carbon black oil sales in Carbon distillation. Management has moved away from detailed product-level reporting, citing visibility to competitors, and declined to provide more granularity.

Historical performance concerns. An investor question highlighted that over the last decade and a half the company generated more than ₹130,000 crore of revenue with only minimal cumulative profit after tax, with more than half of operating cash flow consumed by interest costs and returns on capital often below the cost of capital, especially after the RÜTGERS acquisition. Management acknowledged these concerns and attributed the last decade to acquisition-related leverage, challenging market cycles, regulatory disruptions, and elevated financing costs.

HHCR facility. The HHCR facility was developed to position the company in a long-term specialty materials market, but its ramp-up coincided with exceptionally high European energy costs and aggressive pricing pressure from low-cost Asian imports, resulting in significantly lower-than-expected returns. Measures have been implemented to substantially reduce losses from the facility beginning Q2 2026, while the company continues to pursue regulatory relief in Europe.

Indian GPC import restrictions. These restrictions had a significant impact on volumes, plant utilisation, and profitability. The industry has now largely adapted to the revised regulatory framework, reducing uncertainty and restoring greater operating stability.

Working capital build. Working capital increased materially during the quarter, primarily because the company deliberately built safety stocks for Indian calciners in response to Middle East disruptions and related logistics uncertainty. The increase was driven mainly by higher inventory volumes rather than price increases alone. This is why improved earnings did not translate immediately into higher cash generation, and why short-term borrowings did not rise in step with working capital.

Employee expenses. Employee expenses increased approximately 32 percent year-over-year, driven mainly by annual incentive provisions and appreciation of USD and Euro against the Indian Rupee by 10.7 percent and 13.5 percent respectively. Because performance had been weaker over the prior couple of years, incentive payments had not been triggered; the comparison base was unusually low.

Geopolitical and market risks. Global implications of geopolitical conflicts include increased volatility in commodity prices, stock and foreign exchange markets, supply chain disruptions, closure and abandonment of operations, travel restrictions, market volatility, and issues with recoverability of inter-company loans and repatriation of profits between group entities, along with regulatory and sanction compliance requirements for step-down subsidiary Severtar Holding Limited.

Refinancing costs. While the Term Loan is prepayable at any time, refinancing the bonds early would involve meaningful costs, so the market window would need to be sufficiently attractive to justify those costs.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Aluminium market support. Primary aluminium pricing was already relatively healthy before escalation of Middle East events. In the medium term, the market is expected to remain reasonably well supported, with no expectation of excess supply, given continued demand growth and China’s 45 million tonnes per year production cap. Longer-term demand should benefit from electrification, lightweighting, energy-transition infrastructure, packaging growth, and urbanisation. Most incremental production growth outside China is expected from the Middle East, India, and other parts of Asia — important growth markets for RAIN’s carbon products.

Carbon pricing dynamics. Carbon represents roughly 15 percent of aluminium production cost, and carbon pricing is primarily determined by supply-demand balance for carbon products in each geography, raw material availability, logistics, quality requirements, and customer-specific market conditions — not directly by LME pricing.

Utilisation improvement. Utilisation is expected to improve toward the end of the year, particularly with plans to restart one kiln in the U.S. after a year-long outage. Volumes in Q3 are expected to move back toward more normal levels, subject to shipment or logistics disruptions.

Targeted investments. Teams are evaluating a few targeted, relatively small investments that could increase production where required, responding to medium-term market demand without compromising capital discipline.

Constructive CPC and distillation environment. The market has improved from trough levels, though not to pre-war conditions. In CPC, supply-demand balance remains supported by growing aluminium production outside China, China’s production cap, and continued constraints around suitable anode-grade GPC availability. In distillation, regional supply conditions and coal tar availability remain important drivers. The current cycle is seen as having more underlying support from structural demand growth, tighter availability of qualified raw materials, and the need for reliable supply to smelters, compared with the prior CPC price spike driven more by rapid price movement and inventory effects.

China’s shifting role. China was once a net exporter of anode-grade GPC but has become a large net importer, reducing the historical cost advantage of Chinese calcination facilities. China has increasingly focused on producing and selling carbon anodes rather than exporting CPC, contributing to a gradual reduction in CPC exports from China and supporting a more constructive supply-demand environment for established non-Chinese suppliers with reliable feedstock access, blending capability, and customer qualifications.

Competitor exit in U.S. distillation. Any reduction in competitor capacity can create opportunities, though RAIN will remain disciplined, focused on customers and product areas where it can provide reliable supply, strong logistics, and consistent quality.

Advanced Materials segment. Comprises Resins and Downstream Materials, Chemical Intermediates, and Engineered Products (including battery materials). Recent performance supported by improved demand in several speciality end markets, better product mix, and disciplined cost-reduction measures. Newer green products remain an emerging but increasingly important contributor, with materials made using greener and alternative raw materials well received by customers, including those serving consumer-goods markets.

Battery materials strategy. Focused on areas within the anode materials value chain where feedstock access, carbon processing expertise, coating capabilities, and ability to develop tailored materials are relevant. Activities include PETRORES specialty carbon coatings used in battery anode material production, sale and distribution of mesophase carbon micro-beads used in small-device batteries, and development of new solid and liquid carbon materials, formulations, and coatings. A partnership with a major global producer of mesophase carbon micro-beads was recently achieved, and joint development agreements with several major graphite companies are progressing.

India CTP expansion. Permissions were received earlier to undertake a new pitch production and distillation unit in India. The first phase of the distillation unit is expected to commence operations in early 2028, subject to normal project execution and regulatory timelines. The strategic rationale is that India is becoming a more important aluminium and carbon market, and additional local capacity enhances supply reliability, improves logistics flexibility, and strengthens ability to serve regional customers competitively.

Differentiated model. RAIN’s sideways integration across CPC and CTP production, supported by global supply chain, R&D, pilot-plant capabilities, and technical service teams, is viewed as a structural advantage as anode-grade GPC potentially becomes tighter.

Digital tools and AI. Leveraged across demand planning, raw material sourcing, production scheduling, logistics, inventory management, pricing analysis, working capital monitoring, and new product development. These initiatives have already delivered measurable benefits in procurement planning, operational efficiency, inventory discipline, and cost control.

M&A and facility revitalisation. Successful acquisition, staffing, and revitalisation of facilities driven by executive leadership in M&A to optimise operational efficiency and drive growth.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical conflicts causing commodity price, stock, and foreign exchange volatility; supply chain disruptions; closure and abandonment of operations; travel restrictions; and market volatility.
  • Recoverability of inter-company loans and repatriation of profits between group entities.
  • Regulatory and sanction compliance requirements for step-down subsidiary Severtar Holding Limited.
  • Suitable GPC availability and logistics are seen as the more likely value-chain bottleneck over the next one to three years, rather than CPC production capacity alone. Blending and feedstock flexibility help but do not eliminate the need for reliable access to qualified raw materials.
  • Margin sustainability in Advanced Materials will continue to vary by product line, customer demand, raw material costs, and mix.
  • Battery materials require long qualification cycles, customer validation, and careful capital allocation.
  • Emerging technologies such as inert anodes, recycling, and alternative carbon sources could become relevant over the longer term, though they are not expected to materially change the anode-grade CPC demand picture in the next decade.
  • Refinancing economics: early bond refinancing would involve meaningful costs and requires a sufficiently attractive market window.
  • Working capital levels are viewed as close to a peak requirement from a volume perspective; moderation depends on supply chains normalising and inventories being consumed.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Utilisation. Management noted Q2 2026 Carbon segment utilisation of 69 percent, broadly in line with Q1 2026 and the calendar year 2025 average. Management expects utilisation to improve toward the end of the year, particularly with the planned restart of one U.S. kiln after a year-long outage.

Volumes. Q2 2026 Carbon volumes were approximately 10 percent lower year-over-year, with deferred shipments of roughly 10,000–15,000 metric tonnes. Management expects Q3 volumes to move back toward more normal levels, subject to shipment or logistics disruptions.

Margins. Management characterised Q2 2026 performance as benefiting from both mix and market conditions, with some opportunistic margins not necessarily from traditional core products. Management does not view the entire Q2 performance as a new run-rate but also not as purely one-off, and expects earnings going forward to remain within the typical range, with a better operational foundation than during the trough period. Some opportunistic margin benefit should be balanced by higher volumes in Q3.

Working capital. Management views the Q2 2026 working capital build as a temporary and prudent measure rather than a structural change in cash conversion, and expects working capital levels to moderate as supply chains normalise and inventories are consumed.

Cash generation. Improved earnings generated additional operating cash, but a meaningful portion was deployed into inventory and working capital. CAPEX and interest expenses remain broadly in line with last year’s levels, while taxes are expected to be higher this year because of improved profitability.

Refinancing and leverage. The company remains in regular dialogue with banking advisors and debt investors. Second lien notes are trading at a yield below the coupon, indicating the market is recognising an improving credit profile and that refinancing could potentially be achieved at a lower cost than the current coupon. Based on normalised earnings, management believes the company should be able to manage toward a net-debt-to-EBITDA ratio of around 3x over time, balancing the pace of debt reduction with liquidity, working capital needs, refinancing economics, and any high-return capital requirements.

Use of excess cash. First use of cash will be to support working capital for higher production and sales volumes; first priority as safety-stock build unwinds would be to reduce working capital loans. Over the medium term, assuming earnings and cash flows strengthen and no major growth projects are committed, cash would be moved through the system in a tax-efficient manner and applied toward reducing long-term debt, while remaining open to selective projects meeting return thresholds.

Equity raise. At this stage, the company has no plans to raise equity under current market conditions. Preference is to improve cash generation, manage working capital efficiently, refinance at the right time, and reduce leverage in a disciplined manner.

Employee expenses. The year-over-year increase should be viewed as a performance-linked expense normalising with better business results rather than a broad structural increase in fixed employee costs. The actual level in future periods will depend on business performance, incentive plan outcomes, and normal employee cost inflation.

India CTP expansion. First phase of the distillation unit expected to commence operations in early 2028, subject to normal project execution and regulatory timelines. Immediate focus remains on increasing capacity utilisation at existing Carbon plants globally to optimum levels.

Strategic roadmap and communication. Management stated it may not provide every element of a two- to three-year roadmap in the form of fixed targets, but recognises investors value a clearer framework and will endeavour to improve communication of strategic priorities, capital allocation approach, margin drivers, and key milestones over time. The current pre-recorded Q&A format remains the preferred approach, with openness to hosting live calls when they would add meaningful value.

Subsidiary compliance. Management stated that based on internal assessments and external counsel opinions, they believe they are in compliance with applicable laws and regulations and do not foresee any recoverability-related issues on investments concerning subsidiary matters.

Dividend and governance. The Board declared an interim dividend of Rs. 1.00 per equity share (50 percent on a face value of Rs. 2 per share) for the financial year ending December 31, 2026, with August 14, 2026 as the record date. The unaudited standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter and half year ended June 30, 2026 received an unmodified (unqualified) limited review report from the statutory auditors. Shareholders approved a Special Resolution through postal ballot on September 10, 2026, for the re-appointment of Mr. Robert Thomas Tonti as an Independent Director for a 5-year term from October 31, 2026, to October 30, 2031.
