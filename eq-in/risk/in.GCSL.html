asof: 2026-09-18

Headwinds and Challenges

  • The first quarter of FY2027 was marked by heightened geopolitical uncertainties and continued volatility in global financial markets.
  • Mainboard IPO activity slowed sharply: 53 issues raised approximately INR 6,895 crores in the quarter, of which 8 mainboard IPOs accounted for roughly INR 5,000 crores, with the balance from 45 SME issues. This compares with FY26, when 108 mainboard companies raised over INR 1.76 lakh crores — described as a significant and disciplined deceleration.
  • SME issue sizes remained small, though this also insulated the segment from institutional capital volatility.
  • FY25 consolidated margins were depressed by the scale-up of low-margin, high-volume market-making, which inflates revenue at wafer-thin spreads. Consolidated EBITDA fell to INR 13 Mn (0.50% margin) in FY25 from INR 396 Mn (36.33%) in FY24, and PAT dropped to INR 18 Mn (0.70%) from INR 366 Mn (33.58%).
  • Consolidated revenue declined from INR 2,589 Mn in FY25 to INR 1,785 Mn in FY26, even as margins recovered.
  • Consolidated cash flow from operations was negative in each of FY24 (INR 186 Mn outflow), FY25 (INR 310 Mn outflow) and FY26 (INR 283 Mn outflow). Cash and cash equivalents fell from INR 210 Mn in FY24 to INR 9 Mn in FY26.
  • Consolidated borrowings rose, with short-term borrowings of INR 103 Mn and lease liabilities of INR 12 Mn in FY26, alongside finance costs increasing from INR 2 Mn (FY24) to INR 19 Mn (FY26).
  • Inventories rose sharply to INR 1,340 Mn in FY26 from INR 692 Mn in FY25 and INR 510 Mn in FY24.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Domestic participation in equity markets remained robust, supported by a growing investor base and increasing financialisation of savings; secondary-market liquidity was stable, which is important for the SME ecosystem and for market-making and broking services.
  • A gradual shift toward professionally managed investment solutions, with rising interest across mutual funds, PMS and alternative investment platforms.
  • Three new advisory mandates were signed in Q1 FY27, including a listing mandate with a West Bengal-based ferroalloy company, and the DRHP for Sky Alloys and Power Limited was filed on both NSE and BSE.
  • Bahutex received Category-II AIF registration during the quarter, with Gretex as a partner in the fund, extending the Group’s participation in alternative investments.
  • Gretex Share Broking Limited continued to progress towards its proposed listing; its strong contribution in the quarter reaffirmed the strategic value of scaling broking and market-making operations.
  • Gretex Share Broking Limited, a material subsidiary, opened a new branch office at 401-402, SPG Empress, Mithakhali Circle, Navrangpura, Ahmedabad – 380009, in line with its business requirements and expansion plans.
  • Forward pipeline: 10 SME IPOs across both SME exchange platforms and 4 larger mandates on the BSE/NSE mainboards, plus 4 in-principle approved mandates deferred to FY27 under SEBI’s extended IPO-offer validity (to Sep-2026) — a total of 14 IPO pipeline mandates.
  • FY2026 delivered 11 SME IPOs listed, approximately INR 495 crores mobilised, 3 open offers and 20 valuation mandates.
  • Strategic initiatives: plans to launch PMS next year; listing of Gretex Share Broking on the mainboard; increasing mainboard IPO pipeline; building equity research capabilities; exploring tuck-in acquisitions/partnerships for retail broking; and the CAT-II AIF platform.
  • Stated target of a total fund raise of approximately INR 20,000 crores over the next three years through private equity placements and IPOs.
  • Industry growth drivers cited: financialisation of savings, SME formalisation, deepening retail participation, domestic institutional depth, thematic capital allocation, and larger, more complex transactions. Regulatory factors cited include Startup India, Make in India and Atmanirbhar Bharat, SEBI’s SME listing framework, extended IPO offer validity to Sep-2026, tighter net-worth and due-diligence norms rewarding quality lead managers, and Category-I registration (INM000012177).
  • Balance sheet described as strong and near debt-free, with an approximately INR 220 crores equity base and long-term debt-free status.

Key Risks

  • Geopolitical uncertainties and global financial-market volatility affecting the operating environment.
  • Dependence on primary-market issuance volumes, which decelerated materially in Q1 FY27 versus FY26 mainboard activity.
  • Concentration of SME mandates in small individual issue sizes.
  • Margin sensitivity to the mix between low-margin, high-volume market-making and fee-based merchant banking, as demonstrated by the FY25 margin collapse.
  • Negative operating cash flows across FY24–FY26 and a low cash balance at FY26 year-end.
  • Rising inventories and short-term borrowings, with associated finance-cost increases.
  • Execution risk on the stated pipeline (14 IPO mandates, including 4 in-principle approved mandates whose validity extends to Sep-2026) and on strategic initiatives (PMS launch, GSBL listing, AIF, research build-out, retail-broking M&A).
  • Regulatory and compliance obligations across SEBI and exchange requirements, with governance, risk and compliance managed through board-led Audit, Risk and NRC committees, defined KYC/AML and due-diligence processes, and a cybersecurity policy with no material incidents reported in FY26.
