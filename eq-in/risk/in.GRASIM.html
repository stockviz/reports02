Grasim Industries: Recent Developments Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Customs search at Nagda unit. The Superintendent, Office of the Commissioner of Customs, conducted a search under section 105 of the Customs Act, 1962 at Grasim’s Cellulosic Staple Fiber Business manufacturing unit at Birlagram, Nagda, Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh. The search concluded on 12th September 2026 and primarily concerned import-export transactions. As of the date of disclosure, no show cause notice (SCN) or order had been issued or passed.

Cellulosic Fibres volume pressure. Grasim’s CSF sales volumes stood at 202 KT in Q1FY27, down 4% YoY, attributed to lower production on account of planned maintenance and subdued domestic demand. CFY volumes were down 7% YoY due to weak downstream demand in the textile value chain.

Chemicals volume decline. Caustic sales volumes stood at 284 KT, down 6% YoY, due to lower production on account of a captive power plant maintenance shutdown. Chlorine realisations deteriorated further due to lower demand from end-user industries, moderating ECU, which stood at ₹37,955/ton (up 6% YoY).

Input cost inflation in Paints. Industry-wide input costs rose sharply, prompting calibrated price revisions; the overall impact of price hikes stood at 8.8% in Q1FY27.

B2B E-commerce sequential moderation. On a QoQ basis, Birla Pivot revenue moderated amidst elevated market volatility and an inventory optimisation approach pursued by value-conscious customers.

Labour and demand conditions. Labour shortage and subdued demand in the textile value chain were cited among the challenges.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Consolidated performance. Consolidated revenue for Q1FY27 stood at ₹48,716 Cr, up 21% YoY. EBITDA was highest-ever at ₹8,077 Cr, up 26% YoY, driven by revenue-led growth and cost efficiencies. Adjusted PAT stood at ₹2,153 Cr, up 49% YoY.

Standalone performance. Standalone revenue reached an all-time high of ₹11,795 Cr, up 28% YoY, led by Core and Growth businesses. Standalone EBITDA more than doubled to ₹1,094 Cr, up 107% YoY.

Cellulosic Fibres. The China CSF market remained strong, with operating rates at 93% in Q1FY27 versus 82% in Q1FY26, and inventory levels declining to a low of 7 days. Global CSF prices improved for the third consecutive quarter, with Q1FY27 average prices at $1.81/kg, up 19% YoY. Export sales volumes more than doubled YoY. Segment revenue was ₹4,530 Cr, up 12% YoY; EBITDA nearly doubled to ₹632 Cr.

Chemicals. Caustic soda international average spot prices (CFR-SEA) recovered for the second consecutive quarter to $483/ton, up 3% YoY. Specialty Chemicals’ revenue share in the segment improved 200 bps YoY to 30%. Segment revenue was ₹2,640 Cr, up 10% YoY; EBITDA was ₹491 Cr, up 16% YoY.

Cement (UltraTech). Total grey cement capacity (India + Overseas) stood at 205.5 MTPA, with expansion to over 240 MTPA targeted by Mar-28. Total sales volume was 41.3 million tons, up 12.2% YoY; ready-mix concrete volumes were 4.6 Mn m³, up 18% YoY. UltraTech Building Solutions expanded to 5,802 outlets, up 21% YoY. Operating EBITDA/Mt was ₹1,214. Green power mix was 45.6%, with a target of 85% by FY30. Cement revenue was ₹24,648 Cr, up 16% YoY.

Paints (Birla Opus). Revenue was ₹1,661 Cr, up 64% YoY and 17% QoQ. Revenue market share expanded ~30 bps QoQ; including Birla White Putty, combined revenue market share is nearing early teens. First-time billed dealers increased sequentially by over 10%. The business operates over 1,450 exclusive branded franchise retail outlets and launched 10 new products and 95 SKUs, taking the portfolio to 228 products and 1,945+ SKUs. Birla Opus emerged as the second most recalled brand.

B2B E-commerce (Birla Pivot). Revenue was ₹2,548 Cr, up 75% YoY. Annualised revenue run-rate continues to trend above ₹10,000 Cr.

Financial Services (Aditya Birla Capital). Consolidated revenue grew 28% YoY to ₹12,155 Cr. Total lending portfolio (NBFC + HFC) stood at ₹2,19,289 Cr, up 32% YoY. Customer assets across asset management and insurance stood at ₹7,52,745 Cr. The D2C platform ABCD serves over 12 million customers; Udyog Plus has ~2.4 million registrations.

Other businesses. Revenue stood at ₹1,126 Cr, up 30% YoY, with EBITDA nearly doubling to ₹302 Cr. Textiles revenue was ₹690 Cr, up 26% YoY, with EBITDA of ₹45 Cr versus ₹9 Cr in Q1FY26. Renewables revenue was ₹307 Cr, up 59% YoY, with EBITDA up 71% YoY at ₹250 Cr; cumulative installed capacity stood at 1.96 GWp.

Capex and sustainability. Budgeted FY27 capex stands at ₹3,157 Cr across standalone businesses, with nearly 45% earmarked for growth projects. Phase I capacity of 55K TPA is progressing, with environmental clearance under progress for Phase II capacity of 110K TPA. Renewable capacity power share rose to 24% (from 23% in Q1FY26); recycled water share stood at 50%.

Macro tailwinds. Growing infrastructure and housing demand, increasing financialisation, a growing renewable energy sector, and strong macroeconomic growth drivers such as higher IIP and merchandise exports were cited. The Government’s Viksit Bharat initiative was described as a catalyst spurring demand across core industries.

ESG recognition. Crisil ESG Ratings & Analytics Limited assigned an ESG rating of ‘Crisil ESG 65’ under the Strong category for FY 2026, compared to ‘Crisil ESG 61’ for FY 2025. The company noted it did not engage the rating agency for this rating and does not endorse it.

3. Key Risks

  • The customs search at the Nagda Cellulosic Staple Fiber unit relating to import-export transactions; no SCN or order had been issued as of the disclosure date.
  • Continued weak downstream demand in the textile value chain affecting CFY volumes.
  • Chlorine realisation deterioration due to lower demand from end-user industries.
  • Sharp industry-wide input cost inflation in Paints.
  • Market volatility and value-conscious customer inventory optimisation affecting B2B E-commerce sequential revenue.
  • Maintenance-related production disruptions (planned maintenance and captive power plant shutdowns).
  • The company’s forward-looking statements are subject to factors including global and Indian demand-supply conditions, finished goods prices, feedstock availability and prices, cyclical demand and pricing, changes in government regulations, tax regimes, economic developments, and litigation and labour negotiations.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

B2B E-commerce EBITDA break-even. Management states Birla Pivot remains on track to achieve EBITDA break-even by exit of FY27, driven by scale, operating leverage and disciplined execution. Observed performance: Q1FY27 revenue of ₹2,548 Cr, up 75% YoY, with annualised run-rate trending above ₹10,000 Cr, though QoQ revenue moderated.

Paints revenue trajectory. Management states Birla Opus remains focused on sustaining market share gains and delivering on its guided revenue trajectory. Observed performance: Q1FY27 revenue of ₹1,661 Cr, up 64% YoY and 17% QoQ, with market share expanding ~30 bps QoQ.

Cement capacity expansion. Management targets total grey cement capacity of over 240 MTPA by Mar-28. Observed performance: capacity stood at 205.5 MTPA, with Q1FY27 volume growth of 12.2% YoY.

Green power mix. Management targets an 85% green power mix by FY30. Observed performance: Q1FY27 green power mix stood at 45.6%.

Caustic Soda renewable power share. Management expects Caustic Soda’s share of renewable power capacity to reach 45% by exit of FY27. Observed performance: standalone renewable capacity power share to total power requirement increased to 24% (from 23% in Q1FY26).

Customs search. Management states there is no material impact on financials, operations, or other activities, and business operations will continue as usual. The company stated it will continue to provide any further clarification or information required and will comply with legal obligations for disclosures. Observed status: the search concluded on 12th September 2026; no SCN or order has been issued or passed as of the date.

Investor engagement. The company is interacting with the Jefferies India Forum 2026 on 16th September 2026 in Gurgaon through a Group and One to One physical meeting. The latest Investor Presentation and Corporate Presentation are available on www.grasim.com, and no unpublished price sensitive information will be shared.

AGM outcomes. The 79th AGM was held on 21st August 2026 via video conference. All items of business were passed with requisite majority, including adoption of audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY 2026 (99.9637% in favour), declaration of dividend (99.9983% in favour), reappointment of Mr. Kumar Mangalam Birla (94.6468% in favour), reappointment of Mr. Sushil Agarwal (98.4768% in favour), appointment of Deloitte Haskins & Sells Chartered Accountants LLP as Joint Statutory Auditors (99.9888% in favour), ratification of Cost Auditors’ remuneration for FY 2026-27 (99.9983% in favour), and payment of commission to Non-Executive Directors for five years from 1st April 2026 (99.9783% in favour).

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from a defensive Buy on a cyclically weak VSF/chemicals business, anchored on recovery in utilisation, China reopening, specialty chemicals, paints launch, and B2B e-commerce, to a more mature multi-engine growth story where VSF and chemicals had already recovered strongly. By the last report, the focus shifted to scaling paints and B2B, with newer frictions around paints margin pressure, Middle East-driven B2B moderation, and a royalty drag. VSF cyclicality and input-cost/margin risks persisted as themes, but the early caution about VSF margin normalisation was replaced by confidence in improved EBITDA/kg.

Fears that came true

  • The early flag of global demand slowdown and supply-chain disruption materialised in B2B e-commerce after the Middle East crisis, when customers deferred purchases and optimised inventories, coinciding with a -0.9% actual return on the final Buy call.
  • The early concern about higher raw-material costs materialised in the last report as higher-cost raw-material inventory weighed on paints margins, a disappointment after the earlier optimism.

Optimism that failed

  • The initial expectation that chlorine integration would improve to 72% after ongoing projects has not materialised; the latest guidance is for ~68% by FY27 end.
