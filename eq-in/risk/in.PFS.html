PFS Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance vs Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

A muted, “forgettable” quarter. Management described Q1 FY27 as “relatively muted” and, in the words of the Director (Operations), “by all measures, not a very good quarter… a muted and relatively forgettable quarter.” Disbursements were ₹117.25 crores in Q1 FY27 against ₹138 crores in Q1 FY26 and ₹162 crores in Q4 FY26.

Year-on-year deterioration across key metrics. Total income fell to ₹103.31 crores in Q1 FY27 from ₹142.24 crores in Q1 FY26. PAT fell to ₹40.24 crores from ₹136.63 crores. Return on net worth (annualized) dropped to 5.19% from 19.36%, and ROA (annualized) to 3.31% from 9.77%. Yield on assets declined to 10.14% from 11.05%. Net interest income was ₹48.71 crores versus ₹66.18 crores a year earlier.

Shrinking loan book. Loan assets stood at ₹2,946 crores at June 30, 2026, down from ₹3,292 crores at March 31, 2026 and ₹4,313 crores at June 30, 2025.

Disbursement slippage against internal expectations. Management had expected roughly ₹1,000 crores of disbursement in Q1 (and had carried a similar expectation from Q4 FY26). This did not materialize. Reasons cited: infrastructure disbursements occur in phases; some sanctioned loans (notably to gas/oil-producing companies) were delayed because borrowers’ construction schedules slipped amid international market conditions; and the leadership transition — the previous MD’s exit and the new MD’s arrival — meant certain decisions were taken later.

Leadership transition. The MD & CEO resigned effective June 30, 2026 (resignation tendered March 30, 2026). Mr. Rajiv Malhotra was appointed as an Additional Director (Nominee Director of PTC India Limited) on April 8, 2026, and re-designated MD & CEO (Addl. Charge) effective July 1, 2026 to November 30, 2026. This was his maiden investor call.

Investor dissatisfaction. Multiple individual investors challenged management on years of negative growth, the declining loan book, absence of dividend, and the lack of returns, with one asking that the parent company divest its stake. Management acknowledged that “there has been a period which is not very encouraging for investors” and referred to “an overhang of the past.”

Regulatory/compliance matter. As of June 30, 2026, the company did not comply with the minimum infrastructure exposure requirement of 75% prescribed for NBFC-IFC classification. It has intimated RBI and is undertaking measures to restore compliance within the stipulated timeline of September 30, 2026; the request has been accepted by RBI on the Daksh portal.

Cost of funds. An investor noted prior-quarter commentary that cost of funds was high and that the company was not able to raise funds at competitive rates. Management said it does not see why it would be unable to raise funds, and that cost is part of its strategy, with efforts underway to reduce borrowing costs.

Strategic pause on segments. The company is “taking a pause on the FI and SME book” and refocusing on core infrastructure financing.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Sharp rebound in sanctions after Q1. In the first month of Q2 FY27 alone, the company sanctioned more than ₹1,200 crores — described as a record sanction level for the last 13 quarters. Management expects this momentum to continue and translate into stronger disbursements.

AUM target. Management stated it expects AUM to reach approximately ₹5,000 crores by the end of the financial year, from a loan asset base of ₹2,946 crores at June 30, 2026.

Pipeline. Management said there is “a very good pipeline for coming quarters,” with a further pipeline of similar scale to the Q2 sanctions for subsequent months. Delayed Q1 disbursements are expected to convert in the current quarter as borrowers reach the construction stage and contribute their own share.

Core infrastructure focus and India infrastructure story. Management is “bullish on how infrastructure in this country is going to grow over the next decade or more,” citing the company’s two-decade track record. The strategy centers on infrastructure financing as the primary growth engine, across large, medium and small ticket sizes, with a balanced focus on private sector, PSU and government borrowers, and across the broader infrastructure value chain.

Diversified focus areas. The investor presentation lists power infrastructure, renewable energy and energy transition, transportation and logistics, water and urban infrastructure, energy storage, clean mobility, biofuels and waste-to-energy, digital infrastructure, and social and commercial infrastructure.

Deepening existing relationships. A key near-term focus is deepening relationships with existing clients through enhancements and additional financing, leveraging knowledge of their business and credit profiles.

Asset quality improvement. Gross Stage III improved to ₹190 crores in Q1 FY27 from ₹441 crores in Q1 FY26. Provision coverage ratio for Stage III assets improved to 75% from 62%. No new slippages occurred in Q1 FY27. Net Stage III assets after provisioning were ₹47 crores, approximately 2% of net worth.

Capital and liquidity strength. Net worth increased to ₹3,120 crores from ₹3,080 crores in the previous quarter. Capital adequacy ratio was 68.91% in Q1 FY27 (66.63% in Q4 FY26, 64.96% in Q1 FY26). The company maintained a comfortable liquidity and ALM position with positive cumulative net cash flow across all maturity buckets.

Rating action. CRISIL reaffirmed the rating at CRISIL A (Negative)/A1, and the rating was removed from the ‘Watch with Developing Implications’ category.

Funding outlook. The CFO indicated that while no new sanctions are currently in hand, discussions with various institutions are “in a positive direction,” and new additional facilities may be sanctioned before the end of the next quarter. The company is also engaging existing lenders to reduce borrowing costs.

3. Key Risks

Execution risk on the growth pivot. The Q1 disbursement shortfall against internal expectations illustrates the risk that sanctions do not convert to disbursements on schedule. Management attributed delays to phased infrastructure disbursements, borrower construction slippage (including gas/oil borrowers affected by international market conditions), and the leadership transition.

Concentration of stressed assets in a single account. Of the ₹190 crores in Gross Stage III assets, a single account pending admission at NCLT accounts for ₹187 crores. Resolution depends on NCLT admission, expected during the current quarter, after which the IBC process would commence. Timing and recovery outcomes are therefore tied to the tribunal process.

Loan book contraction. The loan book has declined from ₹4,313 crores (June 30, 2025) to ₹2,946 crores (June 30, 2026), and the company is pausing the FI and SME book, making the infrastructure refocus the sole growth lever.

Regulatory classification risk. Non-compliance with the 75% minimum infrastructure exposure requirement for NBFC-IFC classification as of June 30, 2026, with a stipulated restoration timeline of September 30, 2026.

Funding cost and access. Prior commentary on elevated cost of funds and inability to raise funds at competitive rates; new facility sanctions were not yet in hand as of the call, with the CFO expecting possible sanctions before the end of the next quarter.

Leadership continuity. The MD & CEO role is held on additional charge with a defined term through November 30, 2026.

Investor confidence and dividend uncertainty. Management declined to answer when a dividend might be paid, and shareholders on the call expressed loss of hope and asked for divestment by the parent.

Associate investments. Two associates — R.S. India Wind Energy Private Limited and Varam Bio Energy Private Limited — are fully impaired/written off (₹61.12 crores and ₹4.39 crores respectively). VBPL is under liquidation. Their share of profit/loss is considered Nil in consolidated results.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Disbursement guidance vs actual. Management had expected approximately ₹1,000 crores of disbursement in Q1 FY27 (carried over from Q4 FY26 expectations). Actual Q1 FY27 disbursement was ₹117.25 crores. Management attributed the gap to phased infrastructure disbursements, delayed borrower construction (including gas/oil accounts), and the leadership transition, and stated the delayed amounts “will be converting to disbursement in this quarter.”

Sanction momentum vs prior trend. Following the weak Q1, management reported sanctions of more than ₹1,200 crores in the first month of Q2 FY27 — the highest in 13 quarters — and expects the momentum to continue.

AUM guidance. Management targets AUM of approximately ₹5,000 crores by the end of FY27, versus loan assets of ₹2,946 crores at June 30, 2026.

Value creation timeline. Management asked investors for “a couple of quarters, the next 3 quarters,” stating “our numbers will speak for themselves,” while declining to give specific targets or commit on dividend timing.

Asset quality guidance vs observed. The presentation cites significant improvement in asset quality driven by recoveries, a 75% provision coverage ratio for Stage III assets, and no new slippages in Q1 FY27. Gross Stage III fell to ₹190 crores from ₹441 crores year-on-year. The single NCLT account (₹187 crores) remains pending admission, with resolution expected to commence during the current quarter.

Funding guidance. The CFO stated that while no new facility sanctions were in hand as of the call, discussions with institutions are positive and sanctions may be received before the end of the next quarter; the company is also pursuing reductions in borrowing costs with existing lenders.

Compliance guidance. The company stated it is undertaking necessary measures to restore the 75% infrastructure exposure compliance within the stipulated timeline of September 30, 2026, and that RBI has accepted its request on the Daksh portal.
