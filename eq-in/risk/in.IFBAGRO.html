IFB Agro Industries — Recent Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Distillery and IMFL pressures. The Chairman stated that FY 2025-26 business performance improved over the previous year but continued to operate in a challenging environment marked by intense competition. Distillery margins were impacted by stiff price cuts in ENA and Ordinary Denatured Spirit (ODS), even though raw material prices eased marginally owing to FCI rice supply to dedicated ethanol manufacturers. Margins also declined as by-product prices — Distiller’s Dried Grains with Solubles (DDGS) and carbon dioxide (CO2) — fell due to excess supply from ethanol plants. Distillery operations were disrupted by intermittent production stoppages caused by high stock levels, as customers were reluctant to lift material due to what the company described as illegal methods adopted by State Excise authorities.

Excise and pricing environment in West Bengal. The Indian Made Liquor (IML) industry in Bengal saw only nominal 3% growth over two years, with frequent price increases by the Excise Department. Effective December 01, 2025, excise duty rose again, pushing prices higher. The company said this abnormal price increase forced lower-income consumers to shift to smaller pack sizes. Illegal interference by district-level Excise authorities continued, with retailers allegedly threatened and deterred from lifting IFB-branded products. Representations were made to the Excise Commissioner, West Bengal, regarding activities in both Distillery and IML; the company stated action from the Excise Commissioner has been pending since 2020. Under the new regime, representations were submitted to the Chief Secretary seeking an independent enquiry and action against concerned Excise officials, including former Excise Commissioners. The Chairman added that no such issues persist under the new regime.

Aquaculture feed margin pressure. After the Cargill India aquaculture feed acquisition, integration of team and IT systems was completed successfully and the business saw decent growth, but margins remained under pressure due to an abnormal increase in raw material prices that could not be passed on because of price control by the Andhra Pradesh government.

Marine segment seasonality. The company noted that due to the seasonal nature of the Marine business, quarterly results are not strictly comparable to the corresponding quarter.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Ethanol capacity commissioned. The newly set up 25 KLPD ethanol plant at the distillery was successfully commissioned during FY 2025-26. Ethanol sales are expected to begin in FY 26-27 once new tenders are floated by oil marketing companies.

Aquaculture feed acquisition. The company made a strategic entry into aquaculture feed by acquiring the commercial compound shrimp feed and freshwater fish feed business in India from Cargill India Private Limited. The business had annual turnover of Rs. 353 crore as on 31st March 2025 and was acquired for Rs. 110 crore plus working capital, along with distribution network, feed formulations, assets, contracts, business liabilities, licences, employees, and manufacturing facilities at Vijayawada and Rajahmundry, Andhra Pradesh. The acquisition became effective 1st August 2025. The company described it as a significant milestone strengthening its presence in the high-potential aquaculture sector.

Vietnam operations. IFB Vietnam transitioned from development and product validation to initial commercial operations, transforming the product profile from traditional frozen product to value-added shrimp product. The first shipment took place in April 2026. Focus for the current financial year is to grow the business and achieve break-even.

Founder’s legacy. The Chairman referenced the enduring legacy of Founder and Chairman Mr. Bijon Bhushan Nag, who passed away on 28th January 2024 in Singapore, where he had been undergoing treatment since July 2023. The company said it focused over the past year on strengthening its foundation, enhancing resilience, and moving forward with renewed commitment to his values, vision and principles.

3. Key Risks

  • Regulatory and excise risk: Ongoing (though reportedly resolved under the new regime) illegal interference by Excise authorities at district level, pending action since 2020, and frequent excise duty increases affecting demand and pricing.
  • Commodity and by-product price risk: ENA/ODS price cuts, falling DDGS and CO2 prices due to excess supply, and raw material price volatility in aquaculture feed.
  • Price control risk: Inability to pass on raw material cost increases in aquaculture feed due to Andhra Pradesh government price control.
  • Demand mix risk: Consumer down-trading to smaller pack sizes in IML.
  • Operational risk: Intermittent distillery production stoppages due to high stock and customer reluctance.
  • Seasonality: Marine business results vary by quarter.
  • Ethanol offtake timing: Ethanol sales dependent on new tenders from oil marketing companies.
