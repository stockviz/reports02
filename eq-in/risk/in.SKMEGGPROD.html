Headwinds and Challenges

  • Feed cost inflation is the dominant margin pressure. Management stated that the increase in feed cost is the biggest factor influencing the bottom line, with a substantial increase in soya. They indicated the company may have to live with increased feed cost for at least another two to three to six months.
  • Inability to pass on costs. Management explained that this is not a B2C business internationally, so any increase in cost cannot be transferred to the customer; pricing is driven by competition and international market prices, not by the company’s own cost. Contracts do not have feed cost as an input for pricing.
  • Capacity constraints limit volume growth. The company is running at almost full capacity utilization. Management stated there is no room to increase sale volume in the rest of the quarters because of capacity restrictions, and bottom line will be influenced by international egg product market prices and feed cost.
  • Logistics disruption in the quarter. Six to seven containers did not reach destination in time and had to be brought back, and additional product had to be airlifted to Russia. This disturbed capacity utilization and exports. The returned product is being retested and will be deployed in July and August sales.
  • Japan branch approval delayed. Approval was expected by June–July but has been postponed by one or two months because the company was asked to resubmit information due to technical errors. The impact of the branch has still not increased.
  • Russia distributor transition. The company is trying to induct one more distributor in the second half (Q3 and Q4) and must iron out a few things with the existing distributor.
  • International price environment. Management noted inventory positions with all processors remain, and they do not see a very big change in average realization except for exchange rate fluctuation. Egg white may see some improvement in two to three quarters, with signs of higher demand.
  • Branded eggs are lower margin. Management stated the branded egg business is not a high-margin business like egg products, though volume scaling is easier given India’s large demand.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Capacity expansion planned. Management indicated a principal green signal for the next expansion plan, with board approval expected around October. Implementation timeline is one year, and the first two quarters cannot be expected to contribute.
  • Backward integration via EC sheds. The first four EC sheds were completed in the previous project; the remaining sheds are targeted for completion by November–December 2028. Management cited minimum 5% reduction in overall production cost from higher productivity, more consistent productivity, and more efficient feed conversion per egg.
  • Biogas and organic fertilizer. Gas is being consumed by the boiler. Liquid and solid organic fertilizer are being test marketed, with commercial launch planned by September. Management framed this as necessary for sustaining EC house egg production and improving efficiency rather than as a standalone ROCE project.
  • Branded egg foray. The brand division company was merged into the company. Branding consultants have been engaged, and a business model targeting five locations in India is being designed. Management expects a definite plan by end of the financial year, with the strategy ready in six months and revenue contribution from next year. SKM Best Eggs transaction is complete, expected to add about Rs. 40–50 crore turnover this year, with margin development growth from the next financial year.
  • Domestic revenue balancing. Management aims to balance export revenue with domestic revenue over a 10-year horizon, citing limited growth potential in the egg powder market compared to egg sales in India, and the company’s current 100% dependence on export revenues.
  • Russia exports running normally. About 150 tons per month to Russia. The Russia-Ukraine war is not impacting the company now, though the Iran war caused delivery trouble last financial year.
  • New customer onboarded. A new customer who was previously reluctant has come on board, though described as an opportunistic buyer.
  • Strategic goals 2030 and 2035. Management stated a clearly defined growth plan is being worked on in detail, with financial resources available.
  • Realization improvement. Q1 FY2026-27 realization was about Rs. 770, up from Rs. 722 in the prior quarter, primarily from exchange rate. Management expects realization to be above Rs. 700, in the range of Rs. 720–750, depending on product mix.

Key Risks

  • Commodity price exposure. International egg product prices and feed costs drive profitability; the company has limited ability to pass through cost increases.
  • Capacity ceiling. Until expansion is approved and implemented, volume growth is constrained.
  • Execution risk on expansion. Board approval expected around October, with one-year implementation; first two quarters cannot be expected to contribute.
  • Branded egg execution risk. Business plan not yet finalized; margins are lower than egg products; investment budget for branding not yet determined.
  • Japan branch delay. Approval postponed; impact not yet realized.
  • Distributor concentration in Russia. Transition to an additional distributor is still being worked out.
  • Logistics and geopolitical disruption. Prior Iran war caused airlifting of six to seven containers; similar disruptions could recur.
  • Weather-driven supply shifts. Management noted short-term fluctuations such as Europe’s severe summer do not provide leverage to capture new markets or customers on a consistent basis; only prolonged supply disruptions like bird flu create strategic opportunities.
  • Product mix variability. Average realization depends on the combination of products sold in a quarter, making it difficult to predict exact trends.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Realization: Management cited Q1 FY2026-27 realization of about Rs. 770 versus Rs. 722 in the prior quarter, attributing the increase primarily to exchange rate. For the coming quarter, management guided to a range above Rs. 700, around Rs. 720–750, depending on product mix.
  • Feed cost: Management guided that increased feed cost may persist for another two to three to six months, with no ability to pass it on to customers.
  • Capacity: Management stated the company will run at the same full capacity for the rest of the year, with no room to increase sale volume.
  • Expansion: Management guided that the next expansion plan will come up for board approval around October, with one-year implementation and no contribution in the first two quarters.
  • EC sheds: Management guided completion of remaining EC sheds by November–December 2028, with minimum 5% production cost reduction.
  • Biogas/organic fertilizer: Management guided commercial launch of organic fertilizer by September.
  • Branded eggs: Management guided strategy ready in six months, definite plan by end of financial year, revenue from next year, and about Rs. 40–50 crore turnover addition this year from SKM Best Eggs with margin growth from next financial year.
  • Japan branch: Management guided approval expected June–July but delayed by one or two months; contracts established directly until September, with branch in place for H2 (Q3 and Q4).
  • Russia: Management guided about 150 tons per month, with a second distributor expected in H2 (Q3 and Q4).
  • Financial results: Q1 FY2026-27 standalone revenue from operations was Rs. 17,920 lakhs versus Rs. 18,684 lakhs in the preceding quarter and Rs. 17,365 lakhs in the corresponding quarter of the previous year. PBT was Rs. 3,213 lakhs versus Rs. 4,125 lakhs in the preceding quarter and Rs. 2,201 lakhs in the corresponding quarter. Net profit was Rs. 2,403 lakhs versus Rs. 3,172 lakhs in the preceding quarter and Rs. 1,632 lakhs in the corresponding quarter. The board recommended a final dividend of Rs. 1.25 per equity share for FY2025-26.
