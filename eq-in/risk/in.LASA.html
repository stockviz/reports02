Headwinds and Challenges

  • A fire on 18 May 2025 at the company’s mother unit (Plot No. C-4, Lote Parshuram Industrial Area, Ratnagiri) caused significant damage to inventories, property, plant and equipment and the office area, halting all production. Because downstream owned/leased units depended on output from this unit, activities at all subsequent units also ceased. The affected assets were uninsured, and a loss of Rs. 2,057.47 lakhs was recognised as an exceptional item in earlier periods.
  • Operations remained suspended through the quarter ended 30 June 2026. Revenue from operations fell to Rs. 122.67 lakhs for the quarter, compared with Rs. 2,001.18 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2025, and the company reported a net loss of Rs. 357.87 lakhs for the quarter.
  • The company was unable to pay salaries/wages to certain labourers and workers due to the non-operational status of the factory and consequent financial constraints. An unrecognised labour union initiated a strike and protest outside the factory, obstructing ingress and egress. Professionals proposed to be engaged (valuers, chartered engineers, technical experts) expressed reluctance to enter the premises, and a police complaint was lodged; management states it has been unable to gain proper access to inspect and assess damage.
  • Management has been unable to ascertain and quantify the exact extent of loss and damage to Plant & Machinery until a detailed physical inspection and technical evaluation is conducted, although preliminary observations suggest a substantial portion has been severely damaged or destroyed.
  • Management has expressed its inability to commence manufacturing, citing pending statutory clearances and certain operational constraints.
  • GST notices/demand orders aggregating Rs. 3,810.93 lakhs (excluding interest and penalty where unascertainable) relating to various assessment periods are being contested; no provision has been made, and these are disclosed as contingent liabilities.
  • Recognition of Government Grant income on an accrual basis has been discontinued pending restoration of operations and reassessment of compliance with grant conditions.
  • Bank balance confirmations for accounts with book balances of Rs. 5.71 lakhs and fixed deposit confirmations of Rs. 26.42 lakhs as on 30 June 2026 could not be obtained because the accounts and deposits are dormant.
  • Revenue recognition on certain invoices stands suspended under force majeure/supervening impossibility (Section 56, Indian Contract Act, 1872) as goods were destroyed before transfer of risk or ownership; the related customer advance is held as a liability under “Other Current Liabilities – Force Majeure Suspense” pending final settlement.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Management is actively exploring alternative options to enter into contract manufacturing arrangements and/or lease the facility, subject to receipt of necessary regulatory approvals and consents.
  • The company is evaluating its recovery and rebuilding strategy, including potential funding arrangements to restore operations.
  • Under Dr. Omkar Herlekar’s leadership, the company has charted a multi-pronged approach for corporate growth: envisioning a global presence, establishing marketing set-ups across and outside India, focusing heavily on research, developing veterinary APIs and breakthrough innovations through the Catalyst Chemistry division, and utilising backward integration.
  • The statutory auditor issued an unmodified opinion on the unaudited standalone results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, with the limited review report drawing attention to the fire, impairment, going concern, GST, government grant, dormant account and force majeure notes.

Key Risks

  • Continued complete stoppage of production with no confirmed timeline for restart, and dependence on pending statutory clearances and regulatory approvals for any contract manufacturing or leasing arrangement.
  • Inability to quantify fire damage to Plant & Machinery until physical inspection is possible, with access currently obstructed by labour unrest.
  • Labour unrest and law-and-order constraints at the factory premises, including reported lack of effective intervention by authorities despite a police complaint.
  • Uninsured loss with no insurance recovery; financial constraints affecting wage payments.
  • GST demands of Rs. 3,810.93 lakhs under appeal, with no provision recognised; adverse adjudication could affect the financial position.
  • Going-concern uncertainty: management’s inability to commence manufacturing, with recovery dependent on alternative arrangements and potential funding.
  • Discontinued government grant income recognition and uncertainty over compliance with grant conditions.
  • Suspended revenue recognition on invoiced goods destroyed before transfer of risk, with customer advance treatment contingent on future adjudication.
  • Inability to obtain bank and fixed deposit confirmations for dormant accounts.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Management states it is actively exploring contract manufacturing and/or leasing of the facility and evaluating funding arrangements to restore operations; observed performance shows no manufacturing activity in the quarter ended 30 June 2026, with revenue of Rs. 122.67 lakhs against Rs. 2,001.18 lakhs a year earlier and a net loss of Rs. 357.87 lakhs.
  • Management believes the company has a strong case on merits in the GST matters and has made no provision; the demands remain contested before appellate authorities.
  • Management has discontinued recognising Government Grant income on accrual pending restoration of operations and reassessment of grant compliance.
  • Management is of the opinion that a substantial portion of Plant & Machinery was severely damaged/destroyed, but states the exact extent cannot be ascertained until detailed physical inspection and technical evaluation.
  • The board approved the unaudited results and limited review report at its meeting on 14 August 2026; the statutory auditor’s limited review report carries an unmodified conclusion while drawing attention to the fire, impairment, going concern, GST, government grant, dormant accounts and force majeure matters.
  • The 11th AGM is scheduled for 30 September 2026 at 9:30 A.M. through VC/OAVM, with the Register of Members and Share Transfer Books closed from 23 September 2026 to 29 September 2026; resolutions include appointment of Mr. Janardan Savala as Whole-time Director (Executive Director), re-appointment of Mr. Omkar Herlekar, appointment of Mr. Shivam Sharma as secretarial auditor for five consecutive years, donations up to Rs. 2 crore per annum, managerial remuneration limits up to Rs. 3 crore per annum, and secured/unsecured borrowing powers. All seven resolutions set out in the AGM notice were subsequently approved by members with the requisite majority through remote e-voting and e-voting during the AGM.
