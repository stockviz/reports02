Headwinds and Challenges

  • Seasonal revenue swings. Reported revenue softened quarter-on-quarter in Q1 FY27 due to Q4 open enrollment/annual enrollment period (OE/AEP) seasonality. Seasonal revenue was nil in Q1 FY27 versus INR 2,260 million in Q4 FY26. Recurring seasonal revenues are slated to return in Q3 and Q4 FY27.
  • Statutory minimum wage increases. State governments of Karnataka and Telangana notified revised minimum wage rates effective 22 May 2026 and 1 June 2026, respectively. This triggered a re-measurement of gratuity and leave encashment liabilities under the New Labour Codes, resulting in a past service cost of INR 150.89 million recognised as an exceptional item in Q1 FY27. The minimum wage impact for Q1 FY27 was INR 70 million (~40 days impact), with a margin impact estimated at ~1.2% of revenues for FY27.
  • Forex losses. Q1 FY27 included a forex loss of INR 283 million driven by foreign exchange hedges, which weighed on reported EBITDA relative to adjusted EBITDA.
  • Client concentration. Top 3 clients contributed 60.1% of TTM Jun’26 revenues; top 5 contributed 70.1%; top 10 contributed 84.1%. While concentration has reduced over time (top 3 was 72.4% in FY23), it remains elevated.
  • Attrition. Voluntary attrition rate was 28.6% in Q1 FY27 (annualised, for employees with tenure exceeding 90 days), up from 27.6% in Q1 FY26, though down sharply from 38.1% in Q4 FY26.
  • New Labour Codes implementation. The company continues to monitor finalisation, notification and implementation of State-specific rules and further clarifications relating to the New Labour Codes, and will assess the impact of future developments as they arise.
  • Cash conversion impacted by acquisition-related earnouts. Q1 FY27 cash conversion was 69.9%, impacted by acquisition-related earnout payments.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong revenue growth. Q1 FY27 revenue was INR 19,635 million (US$ 207.8 million), up 27.6% YoY (15.2% in constant currency). Organic YoY growth was 27.3% (14.9% in CC terms). Excluding CareSeed, YoY growth was 27.3% (14.9% in CC).
  • Steady-state momentum. Excluding Q4 OE/AEP seasonality, steady-state organic revenue grew 5.1% QoQ in CC terms.
  • Profitability growth. Adjusted EBITDA was INR 4,716 million at 24.0% of revenue, up 27.9% YoY. Adjusted PAT was INR 2,697 million at 13.7% of revenue, up 35.1% YoY. Basic EPS was INR 0.46, up 45.9% YoY; adjusted basic EPS was INR 0.58, up 35.1% YoY.
  • Order book. Secured US$35.3 million of steady-state ACV in Q1 FY27, driven by expansion opportunities/new SOWs across 18 existing clients and 1 new logo.
  • Client additions. Added 27 clients in Q1 FY27 (including CareSeed), increasing total client base to 109. Added 26 new clients via the CareSeed acquisition.
  • CareSeed acquisition. On 11 June 2026, Sagility LLC acquired 100% of CareSeed LLC, a U.S.-based healthcare technology company providing HEDIS reporting and risk adjustment solutions. CareSeed had ~US$5.1 million CY25 revenue, ~95% recurring revenue, 31.4% CY25 EBITDA margin, 30 small & mid payer clients, and 14 team members. The acquisition strengthens Sagility’s position in healthcare quality for Medicare Advantage plans, adds NCQA-certified Forecast and Harvest platforms, and expands mid-market reach with cross-sell opportunities.
  • Sagility Synchrony. Management noted early momentum behind Sagility Synchrony, reinforcing the belief that the future of healthcare operations lies in more integrated, intelligent operating models.
  • Demand environment. Management sees growing demand for outcome-oriented managed services engagements, with healthcare organisations seeking partners combining healthcare expertise with technology, AI, and operational accountability.
  • Debt reduction. Closing debt position of INR 5,670 million in FY26 is to be fully repaid in FY27 (debt repayment of INR 5,670 million planned for FY27). Interest payments decline from INR 543 million in FY26 to INR 287 million in FY27, then nil thereafter.
  • Strong balance sheet and cash generation. Cash and cash equivalents (including investments) were INR 9,674 million at 30 June 2026. Net cash flows from operating activities were INR 3,161 million in Q1 FY27 (69.9% of reported EBITDA); free cash flow was INR 2,416 million (53.4% of reported EBITDA).
  • Recognition. Sagility was recognised as a Major Contender in Healthcare CXM and RCM Intelligent Operations PEAK Matrix Assessments 2026, a Leader in Avasant Clinical and Care Management Business Process Transformation 2026 RadarView, ranked 11th among India’s 100 Best Companies to Work For 2026 by Great Place to Work India, and recognised by ET Edge as Best Organizations for Women.
  • Scale. 47,307 employees at end of Q1 FY27 (up 18.5% YoY); presence in 5 countries with 29 delivery centers.

Key Risks

  • Client concentration risk. Top 3, top 5 and top 10 clients contributed 60.1%, 70.1% and 84.1% of TTM Jun’26 revenues respectively.
  • Regulatory and labour law risk. The New Labour Codes and state-specific minimum wage notifications (Karnataka effective 22 May 2026; Telangana effective 1 June 2026) have already resulted in exceptional past service costs (INR 150.89 million in Q1 FY27; INR 328.23 million in FY26). Further state-specific rules and clarifications are pending, and the company will assess future impacts as they arise.
  • Foreign exchange risk. Q1 FY27 forex loss of INR 283 million driven by hedges; exchange differences on translation of foreign operations and fair value changes on cash flow hedges affect other comprehensive income.
  • Seasonality risk. OE/AEP seasonal revenues are concentrated in Q3 and Q4; their absence in Q1 and Q2 creates quarterly revenue variability.
  • Attrition risk. Voluntary attrition at 28.6% in Q1 FY27 remains a workforce management consideration.
  • Acquisition integration and contingent consideration risk. The CareSeed acquisition includes contingent consideration of up to US$12.5 million linked to achievement of specified performance targets over three years; fair value at acquisition date estimated at US$5.1 million. Goodwill of US$15.7 million was recognised. Earnout payments impacted Q1 FY27 cash conversion.
  • Forward-looking statement risk. The company’s safe harbour notes that future growth prospects involve risks and uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ materially.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Seasonal revenue return. Management stated recurring seasonal revenues are slated to return in Q3 and Q4 FY27. Observed: seasonal revenue was nil in Q1 FY27 (versus INR 2,260 million in Q4 FY26), consistent with the stated seasonality pattern.
  • Minimum wage margin impact. Management estimated a margin impact of ~1.2% of revenues for FY27 from statutory minimum wage hikes. Observed: Q1 FY27 minimum wage impact was INR 70 million (~40 days impact), with the full exceptional past service cost of INR 150.89 million recognised separately.
  • Debt repayment. Management’s go-forward position shows closing debt of INR 5,670 million in FY26 to be fully repaid in FY27, with interest payments of INR 287 million in FY27 and nil thereafter. Observed: borrowings were INR 5,777 million at 30 June 2026 (versus INR 5,776 million at 31 March 2026), with repayment of promoter borrowings (including interest) of INR 112 million in Q1 FY27.
  • Cash generation. Management described robust cash generation. Observed: Q1 FY27 OCF was INR 3,161 million (69.9% of reported EBITDA) and FCF was INR 2,416 million (53.4% of reported EBITDA), with cash conversion impacted by acquisition-related earnout payments.
  • Growth momentum. Management stated the year started on a strong note with healthy business momentum. Observed: Q1 FY27 revenue grew 27.6% YoY and adjusted PAT grew 35.1% YoY, with US$35.3 million of steady-state ACV secured.
  • Investor forum guidance. Officials will attend the 2026 Jefferies India Forum at The Oberoi, Gurgaon on Friday, September 18, 2026, for meetings with multiple investors. Management stated the company will reiterate industry and company-specific developments already in the public domain, and no unpublished price-sensitive information will be shared.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative stayed structurally bullish from first to last, retaining Buy ratings and anchoring on management guidance, US healthcare domain expertise, and payer-led growth as offsets to policy and wage risks. However, the tone shifted from treating Medicaid cuts as an opportunity and emphasising BroadPath-led cross-selling momentum to a more defensive stance acknowledging negative Q1 seasonality, slower provider traction, and a higher AI-cannibalisation estimate. Persisting themes across both reports were wage inflation, US healthcare policy stress, and the risk of delayed synergy benefits.

Fears that came true

  • Minimum-wage hike margin pressure materialised: Karnataka/Telangana wage hikes caused ~120bps margin impact, with full impact in Q2FY27, and the first Buy call’s actual return was -10.1% (DISAPPOINTMENT).
  • AI-led revenue cannibalisation materialised and intensified: the 1-2% FY26 headwind flagged in May 2025 was raised to a 2-3% FY27 headwind in July 2026.
  • US Medicaid/Medicare spending-cut apprehension materialised as a stressed US healthcare market with membership volatility, pricing pressure, client cost takeouts, and policy-driven reductions in discretionary spending and outsourcing.
  • BroadPath-related margin/synergy risks did not reverse: BroadPath showed negative Q1 seasonality, provider-segment traction slowed, and ‘delay in synergy benefits’ remained a key risk.

Optimism that failed

  • BroadPath acquisition-led inorganic growth and cross-selling traction faded, as BroadPath was hit by negative Q1 seasonality and provider-segment traction slowed over two quarters.
  • The expectation that Medicaid spending-cut pressure would create cost-optimisation-led deal opportunities did not offset the damage; the last report instead describes clients imposing cost takeouts and reducing discretionary outsourcing.
  • The FY25 margin improvement did not prove durable: adjusted EBITDA margin slipped from 25.7% in FY25 to 23.8% in Q1FY27, with FY27 guidance of 24-25% below the FY25 level.
