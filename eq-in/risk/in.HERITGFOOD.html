Heritage Foods: Recent Corporate Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

The June 2026 quarter shows margin pressure relative to the prior-year period. On a standalone basis, total income rose to ₹12,980.42 million from ₹11,241.79 million a year earlier, but total expenses grew faster, to ₹12,651.97 million from ₹10,735.59 million. Profit before exceptional items and tax consequently fell to ₹328.45 million from ₹506.20 million, and standalone profit for the period declined to ₹247.70 million from ₹377.58 million. Basic and diluted earnings per share fell to ₹2.67 from ₹4.07.

The cost build-up is visible across line items. Cost of materials consumed rose to ₹9,743.79 million from ₹7,826.57 million, employee benefit expenses to ₹870.04 million from ₹806.52 million, finance costs to ₹80.39 million from ₹41.54 million, and depreciation and amortisation to ₹248.06 million from ₹179.72 million. Sequentially, finance costs also rose from ₹52.48 million in the March 2026 quarter.

On a consolidated basis, the same pattern appears. Total income was ₹13,445.03 million against ₹11,420.27 million, while total expenses were ₹13,109.54 million against ₹10,857.68 million. Consolidated profit for the period fell to ₹249.71 million from ₹405.46 million, with EPS of ₹2.72 versus ₹4.37. The consolidated results also absorbed a share of loss of an associate and joint venture of ₹13.12 million in the prior-year quarter, with no such item in the June 2026 quarter.

The consolidated statement includes one subsidiary whose interim results were not reviewed by its auditors, reflecting total revenues of ₹195.73 million, a net loss after tax of ₹0.94 million, and total comprehensive loss of ₹0.94 million for the quarter. The auditors’ conclusion was not modified in respect of this matter.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

The company is deepening its control of its subsidiaries. On 16 July 2026, the Board approved the acquisition of an additional 20% equity stake in Peanutbutter and Jelly Limited (PBJL), raising its shareholding from 51% to 71%. The consideration is ₹7.20 crore in cash, for 3,116 equity shares of face value ₹10 each, with completion expected within 30 days of Board approval subject to conditions precedent. PBJL, incorporated on 23 February 2022, retails healthy desserts under the brand “GET AWAY” — protein ice creams, keto desserts, vegan ice pops and low-calorie kulfis — through e-commerce, quick-commerce and retail chains. Its turnover has grown from ₹14.80 crore in FY 2023-24 to ₹18.08 crore in FY 2024-25 and ₹33.65 crore in FY 2025-26. The company describes the acquisition as aligned with strategic growth initiatives in the healthy desserts and guilt-free indulgence category, expected to bring synergistic benefits and enhance market reach in ice-cream and frozen desserts.

Separately, the Board approved the acquisition of the remaining 5.60% equity stake in Heritage Novandie Foods Limited (HNFL), raising the company’s shareholding from 94.40% to 100% and making HNFL a wholly owned subsidiary. HNFL manufactures flavoured yogurt products under the brand “Livo” for Heritage Foods, with paid-up share capital of ₹15,99,06,780 and FY 2025-26 turnover of ₹4.05 crore. Neither acquisition constitutes a related party transaction, and no governmental or regulatory approvals are required for the PBJL acquisition.

The company had already consolidated control of HNFL earlier: in May 2025 it entered a Share Purchase Agreement to acquire 71,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each from the other joint venture partner for ₹85.00 million, completing the transaction on 16 June 2025 and acquiring controlling interest.

The consolidated segment framework identifies Dairy, Renewable Energy and Feed as reportable segments, while the standalone framework identifies Dairy and Renewable Energy. Consolidated segment revenue totalled ₹13,889.39 million for the June 2026 quarter, with segment results of ₹404.87 million, segment assets of ₹20,287.11 million and segment liabilities of ₹8,955.78 million.

Key Risks

The results carry a prior-period exceptional item relating to indirect tax. Exceptional items for the year ended 31 March 2026 included ₹69.33 million received towards refund of differential GST liability deposited under protest on the classification of flavoured milk following a favourable order, and ₹32.02 million reversed from a previously accrued liability towards potential interest obligations associated with the matter. The March 2026 quarter and year also included a provision of ₹5.37 million for impairment loss on investment. The June 2026 quarter itself recorded no exceptional items.

The consolidated accounts rely, in part, on unreviewed interim financial results for one subsidiary, and the group includes an associate, SKIL Raigam Power (India) Limited, alongside subsidiaries Heritage Nutrivet Limited, Heritage Novandie Foods Limited and Peanut Butter and Jelly Limited.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

The shareholder votes at the 34th AGM on 23 July 2026 show strong support for the resolutions placed before members, including adoption of the standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY 2025-26 and declaration of a final dividend of 50%, i.e. ₹2.50 per equity share of face value ₹5. The re-appointment of Mr. Muthu Raju Paravasa Raju Vijay Kumar as Non-Executive Independent Director drew the largest dissent, with 1,20,501 votes against (0.2139% of votes polled), including 1,19,458 votes against from public institutions. The director retiring by rotation saw 1,048 votes against (0.0019%). All five resolutions were passed with the requisite majority.

The dividend declaration follows a year in which standalone profit for FY 2025-26 was ₹1,397.01 million and consolidated profit was ₹1,501.38 million, even as the June 2026 quarter showed a year-on-year decline in profitability. The company’s stated rationale for the PBJL stake increase — expansion, diversification and stronger presence in healthy desserts — is supported by PBJL’s rising turnover trajectory across the last three financial years, though the subsidiary’s contribution to group results remains small relative to the dairy business.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from an optimistic Buy in Aug 2023, which treated milk price inflation as temporary and expected H2FY24 deflation, to a cautious Hold in Jul 2026 as procurement prices stayed elevated and margins contracted. Persistent themes included milk procurement cost pressure, VAP/ice cream growth, and distribution expansion, while later reports placed greater emphasis on premiumisation and near-term margin pain.

Fears that came true

  • Early-flagged milk procurement inflation persisted, with prices rising ~7% YoY to INR 46.6/ltr and the Apr–May 2026 mini flush failing to materialise.
  • Repeatedly flagged margin contraction materialised, as Q1FY27 EBITDA fell 16.3% YoY and margins contracted sharply.

Optimism that failed

  • Expected input price deflation in H2FY24 from a normal monsoon and flush season failed, as procurement prices remained elevated and the 2026 mini flush did not materialise.
