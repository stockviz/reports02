Agri-Tech (India) Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Persistent losses. The company reported a net loss of ₹2.85 lakhs for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, compared with a loss of ₹10.18 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2025 and a loss of ₹26.75 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026. For the year ended 31 March 2026, the net loss was ₹94.08 lakhs.
  • Weak operating profitability. Profit from operations before other income, finance costs and exceptional items was negative ₹2.85 lakhs for the June 2026 quarter, negative ₹10.18 lakhs for the June 2025 quarter, and negative ₹111.48 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2026.
  • Expenses exceeding income. Total expenses of ₹23.25 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter exceeded total income of ₹20.40 lakhs. Other expenditure alone was ₹13.95 lakhs, and employee benefit expense was ₹5.16 lakhs.
  • Qualified auditor conclusion. The statutory auditors, K. P. Sahasrabudhe & Co., issued a qualified conclusion on the unaudited standalone financial results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026. The qualification relates to inter-corporate loans/advances granted to related parties on which no interest has been charged during the quarter, consistent with the position adopted in the audited standalone financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2026. The auditors state this is not in compliance with Section 186 of the Companies Act, 2013, and that under Ind AS 109 such financial assets are required to be recognised and subsequently measured at fair value; in the absence of interest recognition, the corresponding financial asset valuation as at 30 June 2026 is misstated. The impact has not been quantified because the matter is sub judice before the Honourable Supreme Court of India.
  • Stalled CIRP proceedings. The company has not provided for interest on loans given to Tech India Nirman Ltd. The CIRP proceedings against that company were stalled, and the case is sub judice with the Honourable Supreme Court of India.
  • Single product segment. The company deals in one major product segment: horticulture crops.
  • Volatile quarterly performance. Revenue from operations rose to ₹20.40 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter from ₹11.53 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter and ₹7.94 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter, but the company still recorded a loss, indicating cost pressures.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Revenue growth. Revenue from operations for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 was ₹20.40 lakhs, higher than ₹11.53 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2025 and ₹7.94 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026.
  • Narrowing quarterly loss. The net loss for the June 2026 quarter (₹2.85 lakhs) was smaller than the loss in the June 2025 quarter (₹10.18 lakhs) and substantially smaller than the loss in the March 2026 quarter (₹26.75 lakhs).
  • Reduced total expenses. Total expenses fell to ₹23.25 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter from ₹32.70 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter and were only slightly higher than ₹21.71 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter.
  • Lower employee benefit expense. Employee benefit expense declined to ₹5.16 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter from ₹7.70 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter and ₹10.89 lakhs for the year ended 31 March 2026.
  • No investor complaints. No investor complaints were received during the quarter and none were pending at the end of the quarter.
  • Stable share capital. Paid-up equity share capital remained at ₹594.00 lakhs (face value ₹10 per share) across the periods presented.
  • Auditor reappointment. The 33rd Annual General Meeting held on 16 September 2026 approved the appointment of M/s. KP Sahasrabudhe & Co. as Statutory Auditors for five consecutive years from FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31, providing continuity in audit oversight.
  • Governance continuity. The AGM also approved adoption of the audited financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2026 and re-appointment of Mrs. Jeevanlata Kagliwal as Director.

3. Key Risks

  • Related-party lending and non-recognition of interest. The auditors’ qualified conclusion highlights that inter-corporate loans/advances to related parties carry no interest, which the auditors state is not in compliance with Section 186 of the Companies Act, 2013, and that the financial asset valuation is misstated under Ind AS 109. The impact is unquantified.
  • Litigation uncertainty. The CIRP proceedings against Tech India Nirman Ltd. were stalled and the case is sub judice before the Honourable Supreme Court of India. The outcome and its impact on the company’s loans to that entity remain uncertain.
  • Continued losses. The company has reported losses across the quarters presented and for the year ended 31 March 2026, with no tax expense recognised in any period presented.
  • Concentration risk. The company operates in a single major product segment, horticulture crops.
  • No dividend or return information. The financial results show no dividend-related disclosures, and the company continues to report negative earnings per share (basic and diluted: negative ₹0.05 for the June 2026 quarter, negative ₹0.17 for the June 2025 quarter, negative ₹0.33 for the March 2026 quarter, and negative ₹1.45 for the year ended 31 March 2026).

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • No explicit forward-looking guidance. The announcements do not contain specific management guidance on future revenue, profitability, or growth targets. The only forward-looking element is the AGM approval of the Statutory Auditors’ appointment for five consecutive years from FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31.
  • Observed performance versus prior periods. Revenue from operations improved to ₹20.40 lakhs in the June 2026 quarter from ₹11.53 lakhs in the June 2025 quarter and ₹7.94 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter. The net loss narrowed to ₹2.85 lakhs from ₹10.18 lakhs and ₹26.75 lakhs in the respective comparative periods. Total expenses declined to ₹23.25 lakhs from ₹32.70 lakhs in the March 2026 quarter.
  • Auditor qualification persists. The qualified conclusion for the June 2026 quarter is consistent with the position adopted in the audited standalone financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2026, indicating the related-party interest matter remains unresolved.
  • No quantified impact. The auditors state that the impact of the related-party interest matter on the statement has not been quantified and could not be ascertained by them because the matter is sub judice before the Honourable Supreme Court of India.

Broker Narrative

The narrative evolved from company/operational specifics in the first report — BSNL execution, furloughs, wage/subcontracting costs, Comviva seasonality, BFSI offshoring — to macro/political/regulatory risks in the last report, including Trump tariffs, H1-B visa fees, outsourcing tax, TCS layoffs, and muted budgets. The persistent thread was a Buy bias (one Hold) with repeated confidence in order-book/deal strength and cost optimization, although currency moved from a cross-currency headwind to a tailwind and the final report explicitly separated short-term uncertainty from a favorable digital/GenAI and M&A-driven medium/long-term outlook.

Fears that came true

  • The early fear of weak US and continental European markets materialized into the last report’s macro uncertainty and muted manufacturing/retail budgets, and every actual return was negative (-22% to -53%).

Optimism that failed

  • The first report’s expectation of an ‘absence of wage hike and visa costs’ was contradicted by the later USD 100,000 H1-B fee hike and proposed 25% outsourcing tax.
  • The repeated optimism around strong deal wins and BSNL/order-book execution failed to materialize in share-price terms, as all reports ended as DISAPPOINTMENT with negative actual returns.
