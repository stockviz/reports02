GHCL Textiles: Q1 FY27 Announcements and ESOS 2026

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Raw material volatility: Management notes that sufficient cotton inventory leaves the company “protected from raw material volatility,” implying that cotton price swings remain a live risk for the business.
  • Cotton import duty expiry: The 11% cotton import duty waiver, effective from 1 June, runs only until 30 October 2026. Its lapse would alter the landed cost of imported cotton.
  • Export exposure: Exports contributed 11.0% of revenue in Q1 FY27 (Rs. 45 crores), up from 6.1% in Q1 FY26, exposing the company to overseas demand and tariff conditions.
  • Dependence on yarn: Yarn still accounted for 84% of revenue in Q1 FY27, so overall performance remains tied to spinning spreads even as fabric share rises.
  • Execution risk on expansion: Phase 2 of knitting (25 machines) and the 11 MW green energy project are still to be commissioned, with timelines set for FY27 and Q3 FY27 respectively.
  • Dilution consideration: The ESOS 2026 scheme covers up to 45,00,000 equity shares, approximately 4.70% of paid-up equity share capital as of 31 March 2026.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong Q1 FY27 results: Revenue rose 52% year-on-year to Rs. 410 crores (Rs. 270 crores in Q1 FY26); EBITDA rose 116% to Rs. 70 crores; PAT rose 191% to Rs. 39 crores. Sequentially, revenue was up 9% and PAT up 42% over Q4 FY26.
  • Vertical integration: Fabric’s share of revenue rose to 16% in Q1 FY27 from 9% in Q1 FY26, with yarn falling to 84% from 91%. Management describes this as the success of its value-addition strategy.
  • Volume growth: Yarn sales volume rose to 10.5 thousand MT in Q1 FY27 from 8.4 thousand MT in Q1 FY26; knitted fabric rose to 898 MT from 247 MT; griege fabric rose to 59 lakh meters from 36 lakh meters. Capacity utilisation held at 99%.
  • Knitting expansion: Phase 1 (15 machines) is operational; Phase 2 (25 machines) is expected to be commissioned in FY27, supporting planned fabric capacity expansion.
  • Green energy: 65 MW of renewable capacity currently meets around 70% of energy needs, with an additional 11 MW under development (targeted Q3 FY27), intended to lower energy costs and strengthen ESG performance. The stated goal is 75 MW, catering to up to 75% of energy requirements.
  • Trade agreements: The India-UK CETA removes an 8–12% tariff on yarn, fabric and garments, with the UK FTA commencing 15 July. Other agreements cited include Oman (zero duty from 3 June 2026), New Zealand, the EU (implementation in 2027), and a US tariff reset to ~10%, with FTAs under negotiation with Canada, Peru, Israel and the GCC.
  • Sector conditions: The yarn-cotton spread expanded in Q1 FY27, supporting better spinning margins; global retailer commentary points to a constructive demand environment, particularly in value and off-price channels; acreage and output are expected to rise.
  • Expansion pipeline: MoUs signed with the Tamil Nadu Government for Rs. 1,035 crores of investment, of which ~Rs. 675 crores is already invested. The 25,000 spindles commissioned in June 2025 are expected to generate Rs. 250 crores of revenue for a full year. Plans include weaving and dyed fabric capacities.
  • Employee alignment: The GHCL Textiles ESOS 2026, covering up to 45,00,000 equity shares, was approved by the Board and passed by postal ballot with the requisite majority on 10 September 2026. It excludes promoters, promoter group, independent directors, directors holding more than 10% of outstanding equity shares, and non-executive directors, with a per-grantee annual limit of 3,00,000 options.

3. Key Risks

  • Commodity and spread risk: Performance is tied to cotton prices and yarn-cotton spreads, which management identifies as having improved in Q1 FY27 but which can reverse.
  • Policy and tariff risk: The cotton import duty waiver is time-limited to 30 October 2026, and the benefits of FTAs depend on implementation timelines (for example, the EU FTA in 2027) and on negotiations still ongoing with Canada, Peru, Israel and the GCC.
  • Project execution risk: Phase 2 knitting and the 11 MW renewable project remain under progress, with commissioning dates subject to delivery and installation.
  • Concentration in yarn: With 84% of revenue from yarn, the company remains exposed to spinning segment cyclicality despite the fabric mix shift.
  • Forward-looking statement risk: The presentation itself notes that forward-looking statements are subject to known and unknown risks, uncertainties and assumptions, and that actual results could differ materially.
  • Equity dilution: The ESOS 2026 scheme, at up to 4.70% of paid-up capital, represents potential dilution for existing shareholders.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Margin guidance: Management states long-term EBITDA margins shall be in the 15–18% range. Observed Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 17.0%, within that band, and up 500 bps year-on-year from 12.0% and 310 bps sequentially from 13.9%. The company also cites an industry-leading average EBITDA margin of ~14% over the long term.
  • Capacity expansion guidance: Phase 2 commissioning of knitting machines “remains on track” and is expected in FY27; Phase 1 (15 machines) was commissioned in Q4 FY26 as planned. The 11 MW green energy project is guided for Q3 FY27 and remains under progress.
  • Revenue guidance on new spindles: The 25,000 spindles commissioned in June 2025 are expected to generate Rs. 250 crores of revenue for a full year; production ramp-up is described as completed.
  • Vertical integration guidance: Management expects scale, mix and integration upside to support higher RoCE, and states these initiatives “will more than double the revenue and will be margin accretive.” Observed progress shows fabric revenue share at 15.6% in Q1 FY27 versus 11.7% for FY26 and 9.3% in Q1 FY26.
  • Green energy guidance: The stated target is to enhance the green energy portfolio to 75 MW from 65 MW, catering to up to 75% of energy requirements; currently 65 MW meets around 70%.
  • ESOS guidance: The scheme was approved by the Board and subsequently passed by shareholders via postal ballot on 10 September 2026, with the grant covering up to 45,00,000 equity shares and a per-grantee annual limit of 3,00,000 options.
