URJA Global Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements and Financial Review

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Audit qualifications and documentation gaps. The statutory auditors’ limited review reports for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 flag several unresolved matters. No documentary evidence was made available for investments in mines projects, and project progress classified as capital work in progress amounts to Rs. 46,35,28,484 as on June 30, 2026. No documentary evidence was available for loans and advances granted by the company. The auditors note that the GST department raided the company’s premises on July 20, 2021 and took all records, so documents relating to projects, terms of agreement and signed balance confirmations are not available. In the absence of these documents, recoverability of loans and advances, the impact on carrying value of investments, and the consequential impact on profit are not determinable.

GST input tax credit issues. The company has not reversed GST input tax credits against dues of Rs. 34,24,65,614 as on June 30, 2026, arising from non-payment to sundry creditors within the stipulated time under the second proviso to section 16(2) of the CGST Act, 2017. The auditors state that non-reversal will result in availment of the wrong amount of GST input credits against GST liability.

Related-party and interest accrual questions. Amounts of Rs. 4,48,73,816 were lent to individuals and corporate entities; management states none fall in the related-party category, so no half-yearly disclosures under Regulation 23(9) of the LODR Regulations are required. The auditors also note failure to accrue interest on a loan from promoter company Nandanvan Commercial Pvt Ltd (outstanding Rs. 25,00,82,633 as on June 30, 2026, no interest accrued since inception), on loans and advances to persons and body corporates worth Rs. 4,12,12,928 since inception, and on loans and advances worth Rs. 36,60,888 for Q1 FY2026-27.

Offsetting and receivables concerns. Debtors worth Rs. 36,45,63,651 and loans and liabilities worth Rs. 35,99,25,808 were set off under the same “Debtors Control ledger account.” The auditors note that offsetting of receivables and payables is generally prohibited under Ind AS 32 and that Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013 requires proper classification and disclosure. Ledger confirmations from the concerned parties had not been received. Total outstanding receivables were Rs. 47.27 crores as on June 30, 2026, of which Rs. 41.54 crores had an aging of more than 180 days; the auditors suggest making provisions to write off trade receivables and note that no ledger confirmation was received against trade receivables and trade payables.

Regulatory and tax proceedings. A SEBI show-cause notice dated March 19, 2024 concerned non-compliances in disclosures under Regulations 30 and 34 of the LODR Regulations. SEBI imposed a penalty of Rs. 90 lakh on Urja Global Ltd and 15 related entities, including directors, officers, key managerial persons and promoters, for failing to comply with disclosure norms and in respect of non-disclosure of MOU/agreement/joint venture agreements entered with certain entities; the company has filed an appeal.

On the Income Tax Portal, various notices were observed, including reassessment, recovery proceedings, and adjustments of income under section 143(1)(a). Due dates for replies to almost all of these notices had lapsed and no replies had been submitted. The auditors note this could result in adverse orders, creation or enhancement of tax demands, recovery actions, interest and penalties, adjustment of refunds, and appellate or remedial proceedings. Outstanding tax liability per the Income Tax Portal was Rs. 6,23,98,520 for various years, excluding interest.

Legal notices and demand orders include: a demand-cum-show cause notice for Rs. 29,87,063 for FY 2019-20 dated May 31, 2024 (matter in progress); a demand order dated August 27, 2024 for FY 2020-21 (October 2020 to March 2021) with penalties of Rs. 17,21,54,640 under section 122(1)(ii) and Rs. 17,02,05,654 under section 122(1)(vii) of the CGST/DGST Act, 2017, against which an appeal was filed on November 28, 2024; a demand order for FY 2017-18 to FY 2022-23 disallowing input tax credit of Rs. 3,89,07,213 with an equal penalty, plus a penalty of Rs. 3,89,07,213 on a director, against which a writ petition was filed; a demand order for Rs. 1,91,35,890 under sections 122(1)(ii), 122(1)(vii) and 125 of the CGST/TGST Act, 2017, against which the company decided to file an appeal; a demand order dated January 28, 2025 for Rs. 14,97,31,686 for recovery of ineligible ITC with an equal penalty and a Rs. 50,000 penalty on a director and the CFO (case in process); a demand order under section 73 for Rs. 30,49,630 for FY 2018-19 dated April 30, 2024 from the Assistant Commissioner, Rajendranagar, Telangana, with an appeal to GSTAT in process; an intimation notice for FY 2021-22 requesting reversal/payment of ITC of Rs. 12,44,57,710 with interest and penalty; a demand notice of Rs. 12,47,310 for AY 2024-25 under the Income Tax Act; a search under section 67(2) of the CGST Act, 2017 conducted on July 3, 2024 at the registered office, to which management responded within the time limits; and an order dated December 24, 2025 for FY 2018-19 to 2020-21 levying tax of Rs. 44,14,99,371 and an equal penalty under section 74 for short payment of tax, excess availment/utilization of ITC, wrong availment/utilization of fake ITC without actual receipt/supply of goods or services, and non-payment of late fee.

Business environment headwinds. The company faces intense competition from domestic and international players causing pricing pressures and margin challenges, rapid technological advancements requiring continuous R&D investment, dependence on imported raw materials and components exposing it to supply chain disruptions and currency fluctuations, changes in government policies or regulatory frameworks, and economic uncertainties or inflationary pressures affecting consumer spending.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Government policy support. The company points to government support through initiatives such as PM-KUSUM, the PM Surya Ghar Scheme, and the Atmanirbhar Bharat vision, along with India’s target of achieving 500 GW of non-fossil fuel-based energy capacity by 2030.

Demand drivers. Rising EV adoption and increasing investments in charging infrastructure, growing demand for lithium-ion batteries and energy storage solutions, and increasing public awareness of clean energy and sustainable transportation are cited as opportunities.

Fundraising for growth. On August 5, 2026, the board proposed raising funds aggregating up to USD 500 million by way of issuance of securities, including convertible instruments, warrants and debt instruments, through Qualified Institutional Placement, FCCB issue, rights issue, further public offering, private placement basis or any combination thereof, in one or more tranches, subject to member approval. The stated purpose is to provide financial flexibility for the company’s future growth and funding requirements.

Employee stock option scheme. The board approved the UGL ESOP Scheme – 2026 “Urja For All,” with an option pool of 56,00,000 options exercisable into an equal number of equity shares of face value Re. 1 each. The scheme is administered by the Nomination and Remuneration Committee and implemented through the direct route by fresh allotment. The scheme may be extended to employees of subsidiary companies incorporated in India or outside India. M/s Corporate Professionals Capital Private Limited, a category I merchant banker, was appointed for implementation up to the stage of obtaining in-principle approval from recognized stock exchanges under the SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations, 2021. Vesting commences one year from the grant date and extends up to a maximum of five years from the grant date; after vesting, options may be exercised wholly or partly within a maximum exercise period of three years from the respective vesting date.

Board and management changes. Mr. Vijay Kumar Singhal was appointed Chief Financial Officer and Key Managerial Personnel effective August 5, 2026; he is a qualified Chartered Accountant with over 10 years of experience in finance, accounting, taxation, auditing, banking, financial reporting, budgeting, internal financial controls and statutory compliances. Dr. Anuradha Tomar was appointed Additional Non-Executive Independent Director effective August 5, 2026 for an initial term of five years, subject to member approval; she is an academician and researcher with 15 years of experience, holds a Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering from IIT Delhi, has been granted 9 patents and authored more than 90 publications, and works in energy management, microgrid operation and control, and solar photovoltaic applications. Mr. Mohan Jagdish Agarwal was re-appointed Managing Director for a further five years effective November 13, 2026, subject to shareholder approval. Mr. Yogesh Kumar Goyal was re-appointed Whole-time Director for a further five years effective August 5, 2026, subject to shareholder approval. Dr. Gopalsetty Prasad Rao’s continuation as Non-Executive Independent Director upon attaining age 75 on July 1, 2027 was approved by the board, subject to shareholder approval at the 34th AGM.

Governance calendar. The 34th Annual General Meeting will be held on Tuesday, September 22, 2026 at 11:00 A.M. through video conferencing/other audio-visual means. M/s Siddiqui & Associates, Practising Company Secretaries, was appointed scrutinizer for the remote e-voting and e-voting at the AGM.

3. Key Risks

  • Recoverability of loans and advances and the carrying value of investments in mines projects cannot be determined due to missing documentation, with consequential impact on profit not determinable.
  • Non-reversal of GST input tax credits of Rs. 34,24,65,614 as on June 30, 2026 could result in availment of wrong GST input credits against GST liability.
  • Offsetting of debtors and liabilities under a single “Debtors Control ledger account” is generally prohibited under Ind AS 32 and raises Schedule III classification and disclosure concerns; ledger confirmations were not received.
  • Receivables of Rs. 47.27 crores, of which Rs. 41.54 crores aged more than 180 days, with no ledger confirmations against trade receivables and trade payables; the auditors suggest write-off provisions and proper collection/payment SOPs.
  • Failure to accrue interest on promoter company loan and on loans and advances to persons and body corporates, with reference to sections 185 and 186 of the Companies Act, 2013.
  • SEBI penalty of Rs. 90 lakh on the company and 15 related entities, including directors, officers, key managerial persons and promoters, for disclosure non-compliances; appeal filed.
  • Multiple GST and income tax demand orders and notices, including a December 24, 2025 order levying tax and an equal penalty totaling Rs. 44,14,99,371 each, and an intimation for FY 2021-22 requesting reversal/payment of ITC of Rs. 12,44,57,710 with interest and penalty.
  • Lapsed deadlines for replies to Income Tax Portal notices, with potential adverse orders, enhanced demands, recovery actions, interest, penalties, refund adjustments and appellate proceedings.
  • Outstanding tax liability per the Income Tax Portal of Rs. 6,23,98,520 for various years, excluding interest.
  • Business risks: intense competition and pricing pressure, rapid technological change requiring continuous R&D, dependence on imported raw materials and components with supply chain and currency exposure, policy and regulatory changes, and economic uncertainty or inflation affecting consumer spending.
  • The consolidated results include three subsidiaries — Urja Batteries Limited, Urja Digital World Limited and Sahu Minerals & Properties Limited — whose interim financial results were not reviewed by their auditors and were certified by management; these reflect total revenue from operations of Rs. 68.20 lakhs and total comprehensive loss of Rs. 18.77 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Management remains optimistic about growth prospects, supported by India’s focus on renewable energy, electric mobility, energy storage, and sustainable development. The company expects to continue focusing on expanding its product portfolio, strengthening market reach, improving operational efficiencies, and enhancing customer value.

For FY 2025-2026, standalone total revenue increased by 15.07% to Rs. 5,923.54 lakhs and profit after tax increased by 32.43% to Rs. 190.86 lakhs. On a consolidated basis, revenue stood at Rs. 6,759.02 lakhs with a profit after tax of Rs. 134.76 lakhs. The company has three material subsidiaries: Urja Batteries Limited, Sahu Minerals and Properties Limited, and Urja Digital World Limited.

The statutory auditors issued a qualified opinion regarding unreversed GST input tax credits of approximately Rs. 34.25 lakhs and missing documentary evidence for investments in mines projects and loans and advances. The board’s proposal to raise up to USD 500 million and the adoption of the UGL ESOP Scheme – 2026 indicate management’s stated intent to fund future growth and align employees with long-term value creation, while the audit qualifications and pending tax and regulatory proceedings represent matters that remain unresolved as of the August 5, 2026 board meeting.
