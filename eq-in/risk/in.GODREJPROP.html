Headwinds and Challenges

Weak quarterly financial performance. In Q1 FY27, total income fell 16% year-on-year to INR 1,337 crores, profit before tax declined 44% to INR 480 crores, and net profit after tax declined 42% to INR 350 crores. Adjusted EBITDA margin dropped to 41.7% from 58.1% in Q1 FY26. Sequentially, total income was down 66% from Q4 FY26.

Operating cash flow volatility. Q1 FY27 operating cash flow was INR 399 crores, down sharply from INR 4,631 crores in Q4 FY26. The company attributes this to deliveries falling to 0.9 million sq. ft. in Q1 FY27 from 7.4 million sq. ft. in Q4 FY26, which reduced collections by approximately INR 3,600 crores quarter-on-quarter, while construction outflows rose 54% year-on-year to INR 2,244 crores. Net cashflow for the quarter was negative INR 1,193 crores.

Rising leverage and borrowing costs. Net debt increased to INR 7,637 crores as of June 30, 2026, from INR 6,414 crores as of March 31, 2026 and INR 4,637 crores as of June 30, 2025. The net debt-to-equity ratio rose to 0.39 from 0.33 and 0.26 at those respective dates. Average borrowing cost (year-to-date) increased to 7.15% from 7.05% in FY26.

Regulatory penalty. On July 3, 2026, the company received a certified copy of an order dated June 8, 2026 from the Chhattisgarh Real Estate Regulatory Authority imposing a penalty of INR 10 lakh under Section 3(1) read with Section 59 of RERA, relating to alleged advertisement of ‘Project – Greenvale Estate’ prior to obtaining RERA registration. The company states the advertisements were circulated by third parties without its knowledge or consent, allegedly misusing the Godrej brand, and that it initiated legal measures and takedown actions. The company obtained the project’s registration certificate from CGRERA on June 25, 2026, is evaluating legal remedies, and states there is no material impact on financial or operational activities.

Delivery-dependent revenue recognition. The company notes that deliveries depend on receipt of regulatory approvals and can be delayed beyond initial expectations, and that revenue recognition depends on project work completion, the underlying business model, economic interest, and accounting method.

Quarterly variability in operating cash flow. Management states that operating cash flow will vary quarterly due to volatility in collections, which depend on bookings, construction progress, milestones achieved, and deliveries, whereas outflows are largely evenly spread over the year with an increasing trend due to growing scale.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Scale and market position. Godrej Properties describes itself as India’s largest residential real estate developer by booking value, booking volume, and cash collections in both FY25 and FY26, with approximately 258 million sq. ft. of developable area across India and 126 projects as of June 30, 2026. It has delivered approximately 79 million sq. ft. since FY18.

Q1 FY27 sales momentum. Booking value rose 22% year-on-year to INR 8,651 crores on 3,738 units and 6.2 million sq. ft. This was the sixth consecutive quarter with booking value above INR 7,000 crores and the twelfth consecutive quarter above INR 5,000 crores. Collections rose 18% year-on-year to INR 4,348 crores. Key launches included Godrej Vanantara, Bengaluru (INR 3,237 crores), Godrej Samaris, Gurugram (INR 1,248 crores), and Godrej Brooklyn Avenue, Hyderabad (INR 317 crores).

Business development pipeline. In Q1 FY27, the company added three new projects with approximately 8.0 million sq. ft. of saleable area and expected booking value of INR 9,500 crores: Greater Noida DMIC, NCR (5.78 million sq. ft., INR 7,000 crores), Noida Sector 150 (1.09 million sq. ft., INR 2,000 crores), and Chennai Plotted-2 (1.18 million sq. ft., INR 500 crores), all 100% owned.

Revenue recognition pipeline. The company expects booking value of INR 40,000 crores to be delivered and recognized in P&L by FY28, representing a more than 2x increase over the approximately INR 15,000 crores recognized in FY25–FY26. Major projects expected to be delivered by FY28 include Godrej Reserve (MMR), Godrej Aristocrat (Gurugram), Godrej Zenith (Gurugram), Godrej MSR City (Bengaluru), Godrej Woodscapes (Bengaluru), and others.

Cash flow and free cash flow targets. The company expects to deliver INR 52,000–55,000 crores of collections and INR 20,000–22,000 crores of operating cash flow cumulatively for FY27 and FY28, which it states will enable it to be free cash flow positive by FY28. It expects approximately INR 20,000 crores of operating cash flow between Q2 FY27 and Q4 FY28.

Return on equity target. GPL intends to deliver 20% ROE from FY28 while retaining market share leadership.

Execution scale-up. Construction and related outflow increased 54% year-on-year in Q1 FY27, which the company states reflects an increased pace of execution expected to be seen in deliveries in FY28. Of 52 projects acquired between FY21 and FY25, 51 have been launched; 7 of 18 projects acquired in FY26 have also been launched.

Geographic expansion. The company is expanding into new micro-markets within core cities and testing new markets through plotted development projects, having recently entered Indore, Panipat, Raipur, Baroda, and Coimbatore.

Sustainability positioning. GPL ranks #1 globally in the Real Estate and Management sector on the S&P Global Dow Jones Best-in-Class Indices for 2025 and #1 globally with a score of 100/100 in GRESB 2025. It was included in TIME World’s Most Sustainable Companies 2026 as the only Indian real estate company on the list, received CDP ‘A’ list inclusion in 2025, and received SBTi validation of near-term, long-term, and Net Zero goals in January 2026. As of FY26, 100% of projects in the reporting boundary are certified or under certification for green building rating systems.

Brand and lifestyle differentiation. The company opened On Court, one of India’s largest indoor padel facilities with four international-standard courts developed in partnership with PadelPark India, at its 145-acre Godrej City township in Panvel. Godrej City Panvel also announced a new phase of 3 and 4 bed Sky Villas near its 9-hole golf course. Management notes the facility caters to both competitive players and newcomers, aiming to support Padel All Stars in introducing the sport to more people while building an active lifestyle ecosystem at the township.

Capital access. The company cites the largest QIP ever in Indian real estate (INR 6,000 crores) in December 2024 and the lowest bank funding rates in the sector.

Key Risks

  • Regulatory and approval risk: Deliveries depend on receipt of regulatory approvals and can be delayed beyond initial expectations. The CGRERA penalty illustrates exposure to RERA compliance issues, including third-party misuse of the Godrej brand in advertisements.
  • Execution and delivery risk: Revenue recognition depends on project work completion, business model, economic interest, and accounting method. The company’s FY28 delivery and revenue recognition targets are subject to these dependencies.
  • Cash flow timing risk: Operating cash flow varies quarterly due to collection volatility tied to bookings, construction progress, milestones, and deliveries. Q1 FY27 demonstrated this with a sharp sequential decline.
  • Leverage risk: Net debt and net debt-to-equity have risen, and average borrowing costs increased year-to-date.
  • Market and macroeconomic risk: The company’s forward-looking statements note that actual results might differ materially due to changes in industry structure, significant changes in political and economic environment in India and overseas, tax laws, import duties, litigation, and labor relations.
  • Joint venture cash access risk: The company notes timing differences in cash collection from customers in project SPVs and pending transfer to GPL due to non-availability of RERA limits and restrictions in agreements with JV partners whereby GPL cannot withdraw cash until particular milestones are achieved.
