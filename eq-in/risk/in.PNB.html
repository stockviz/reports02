Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from a cautious Neutral (2023) worried about rising deposit costs, margin compression and wage-driven opex to a Buy (2026) as funding costs fell, NIM expanded and opex relief emerged from lower PSLC/AS-15 costs. Retail/SME/RAM credit growth and controlled slippages remained consistent bullish themes throughout. The biggest reversal was on treasury: initially cited as a tailwind from falling bond yields, by 1QFY27 treasury profits had collapsed 52% YoY and became a headwind. New concerns about ECL transition, CASA erosion and early-stage SMA-2 stress replaced the original cost-of-deposit fears.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning on higher funding costs/bulk deposit mix materialized as CASA contracted to 36.7% by 1QFY27, and this funding pressure was associated with the May-2024 DISAPPOINTMENT (-23.8% actual return).
  • The flagged stagnation in loan yields showed up in low-yielding corporate advances being run off and NII growth of just 2.1% YoY in 1QFY27, a period also preceded by negative actual returns in mid-2024 (e.g., -14.8% in June-2024).

Optimism that failed

  • The early optimism on healthy treasury performance from moderating bond yields failed: treasury profits fell 52% YoY to INR 7.7b, dragging total other income down 17.7% YoY.
  • The expected deposit-accretion tailwind from the INR 2,000 note discontinuance did not provide a durable CASA improvement, as CASA dipped 29 bps YoY to 36.7% by 1QFY27.
