HARSHA Engineers International — Q1 FY27 Earnings Call Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Raw material cost inflation. Average raw material costs rose around 8% in Q1 FY27 (described elsewhere as 6–8%), across brass, copper, zinc, steel and polymer. While most systems follow a pass-through mechanism, management noted a one-to-two quarter lag before recovery, so the cost increase weighed on Q1 margins and should be passed through in subsequent quarters.

Foreign exchange losses. The India Engineering business absorbed an FX loss of around Rs. 4 crores, attributed to technical accounting under IndAS — foreign currency fluctuations relating to FX cash flow hedges are parked in other comprehensive income until realized and settled, and the loss arose on realization of cash flow hedges in the current quarter. Management does not expect a material adverse impact going forward. Separately, Harsha Romania recorded a one-time FX loss of around Rs. 2 crores from adverse movement between the local currency and Europe.

Indirect material inflation. Oil, chemicals and packing material costs rose due to inflationary pressure, linked to the war that broke out during Q1, adding approximately Rs. 3 crores to Q1 costs.

Romania underperformance. Harsha Romania remained in negative territory with continued operating losses, despite slight top-line growth. Management noted that excluding FX, operations did not significantly improve, partly due to rising copper prices. The shift from semi-finished castings toward more cages has not yet produced significant numbers, though the pipeline is improving. A strategic change in top management was made, with a new CEO brought in. Management indicated Romania may still post some losses in the current financial year.

Advantek losses. Harsha Advantek reported a PAT loss of about Rs. 4.3 crores in Q1 FY26 (as stated in the call) and is in a ramp-up phase, with Q1 FY27 EBITDA of 9%, below the India blended margin.

Large-size cage ramp-up constraints. Q1 large-size cage sales were around Rs. 10 crores, which management called an aberration, partly because large-size capacity at the new facility is still struggling to ramp up and respond to demand.

Margin compression. Consolidated EBITDA margin was 15.9% in Q1 FY27. India Engineering saw EBITDA and PAT growth YoY but margin degrowth QoQ. Foreign business EBITDA margin fell from 1.5% to 0.3% YoY despite 20% revenue growth, with operating leverage not yet flowing through.

Japan segment slow conversion. The Japan-based customer segment grew 25% YoY in Q1 (Rs. 21 crores vs Rs. 16.5 crores), but management guided only modest ~10% full-year growth, citing a slow development and conversion process.

Solar EPC volatility. Solar revenue was Rs. 36.3 crores in Q1, down sharply QoQ (described as down 65% QoQ) though up 120% YoY, reflecting the project-based, fourth-quarter-weighted nature of the business.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong consolidated growth. Consolidated Q1 FY27 revenue grew 25% YoY. Engineering business consolidated supply was Rs. 421 crores versus Rs. 382 crores in the prior quarter and Rs. 349 crores in the same quarter last year. Consolidated EBITDA was Rs. 69.8 crores versus Rs. 77 crores QoQ and Rs. 65.3 crores YoY.

India Engineering momentum. The India Engineering business (HEIL plus Harsha Advantek) posted around 21% YoY top-line growth and 6% QoQ growth, notable because the last quarter is historically the strongest.

Cages demand. Continued good demand, traction and offtake in India and abroad, driven by customer offtake matching their growth plans, demand from MNC customers’ recently set up Indian facilities, and other major Indian bearing customers. Export from India in Q1 FY27 was Rs. 139 crores, up 22% YoY and 11% QoQ. Growth was described as broad-based, with industrial demand strengthening and European demand recovering.

Bushing growth. Q1 Bushing sales were around Rs. 34 crores, up about 35% YoY, with strong order visibility and pipeline. Management is confident of ~30% growth this year against Rs. 127 crores last year. Growth is supported by conversion, expected to continue at least a couple of years, plus wallet share gains.

Stamping growth. Q1 Stamping sales were around Rs. 90 crores, up about 31% YoY. New products developed or under development include white goods (AC compressor components), railways, seals and other automotive stamping products. Management targets ~30% growth this year against Rs. 60 crores last year.

Large-size cages. Despite Q1 sales of around Rs. 10 crores, management expects ~50% growth for the year against Rs. 49 crores last year, citing order book and pipeline. The driver is capturing a bigger wallet share, as current wallet share with customers in larger-size cages is low.

Japan customers. Q1 FY27 sales around Rs. 21 crores, up about 25% YoY; full-year expectation is modest ~10% growth over Rs. 72 crores.

Advantek scale-up. Q1 sales around Rs. 30 crores, up 7% QoQ, with visibility of progressive quarterly increases. Annual sales target is around Rs. 140 crores plus, against Rs. 43 crores achieved in FY26. Advantek is expected to be PAT positive by end of FY27. A Phase-2 expansion has been announced for Bushing, Stampings and large-size cages.

China stability and expansion. Harsha China is expected to grow around 10% in the current financial year against sales of about Rs. 120 crores in FY26, with EBITDA margin of 12–14% and PAT around 6%. A Brownfield expansion has commenced after securing debt funding at an attractive rate, focused on steel cages to expand product portfolio and market reach. Commissioning is expected in Q3 of next financial year, with full impact from FY2029 onwards.

New application areas. Work is ongoing in wind energy (Bushing technology upgrades and cage size/geometric capability), railways (stamping components and cages), aerospace and defense (stamping components with Tier-1/Tier-2 Indian defense companies, at a developmental stage), and EV-related products (partly stamping, largely through bearing cages improving performance).

Inorganic optionality. Management said it will evaluate opportunities that enhance capability within precision engineering if the right opportunity arises, though this is not a mandate.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material price volatility across brass, copper, zinc, steel and polymer, with pass-through lag of one to two quarters; management said it has “no idea” whether RM prices will normalize, expecting them to settle soon.
  • Continued losses at Harsha Romania, where breakeven depends on the key customer buying semi-finished castings returning to previous levels, and on increasing cage sales from around 20–25% to 30–35% of the mix; overheads remain high.
  • Foreign exchange exposure, including accounting-driven hedge realization effects in India and local-currency versus Europe borrowing effects in Romania.
  • Ramp-up execution risk at the new large-size cage facility and at Bhayla, where civil construction was on hold due to heavy rain in Ahmedabad.
  • Customer concentration: top 10 customers account for almost 80% of India revenue, spread over more than 80 plants worldwide; top four customers keep changing rank.
  • Slow conversion in the Japan customer segment.
  • Project-based volatility in the solar EPC business, which is fourth-quarter weighted and does not involve manufacturing.
  • Margin sensitivity: management guided sustainable India margins of 20–22%, below the 24.4% standalone Engineering margin in FY26, citing metal price increases and Bhayla ramp-up costs.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Consolidated growth. Management reaffirmed confidence in strong India growth in the higher-teens and consolidated sales growth of low-to-medium teens, with bottom line growing more strongly. In Q1 FY27, consolidated revenue grew 25% YoY, above the full-year guided range, and management explicitly said 20% for FY27 is “a very tough stretch,” reiterating mid-to-high teens in India and low-teens overall.

India Engineering margins. Management guided a sustainable margin range of 20–22%, against a Q1 standalone Engineering margin described by an analyst as around 24% (management called 24% “a little high”). Management expects EBITDA margin to improve and match last year’s 18.7% consolidated level if metal prices stabilize, against the 16% reported in Q1.

Bushing. Q1 growth of ~35% YoY versus full-year guidance of ~30% over FY26’s Rs. 127 crores.

Stamping. Q1 growth of ~31% YoY versus full-year guidance of ~30% over FY26’s Rs. 60 crores.

Large-size cages. Q1 sales of ~Rs. 10 crores versus full-year guidance of ~50% growth over FY26’s Rs. 49 crores; management called Q1 an aberration tied to capacity ramp-up constraints.

Japan customers. Q1 growth of ~25% YoY versus full-year guidance of only ~10% over FY26’s Rs. 72 crores, with management citing slow conversion and expecting a run rate around Rs. 80 crores.

Advantek. Q1 sales ~Rs. 30 crores with 9% EBITDA; full-year target ~Rs. 140 crores plus and PAT positive by end of FY27, against Rs. 43 crores in FY26 and a Q1 FY26 PAT loss of ~Rs. 4.3 crores.

Foreign subsidiaries. Management targets combined losses reducing to lower single digits (Rs. 2–4 crores range discussed) versus Rs. 10 crores in FY26, with China continuously profitable and Romania tapering losses. The combined net loss of China and Romania was disclosed as Rs. 3 crores for the quarter. Management said it is too early to confirm a turn to positive and asked for one to two quarters for a better picture.

CapEx. FY27 CapEx guidance of Rs. 50–80 crores, with combined major expansions (Bhayla and China Phase-2) plus regular CapEx expected at Rs. 180–200 crores over 1.5 to 2 years. Q1 FY27 consolidated CapEx incurred was Rs. 37 crores. China Phase-2 commissioning guided for Q3 FY28, with full impact from FY2029; Bhayla Phase-2 building construction expected to complete by end of the year or the last quarter, with machinery orders already being procured. Bhayla is targeted to generate roughly Rs. 300–400 crores of revenue by the third year across combined phases.

Working capital. Consolidated working capital cycle improved to around 116 days from 130 days in the previous quarter.

Solar. FY27 revenue outlook of Rs. 200-odd crores with 7–8% EBITDA margin; Q1 revenue was Rs. 36.3 crores with positive EBITDA of Rs. 2.82 crores.

Subsidiary revenue growth. Management indicated Romania and China together would grow less than 10%, India more than 15%, averaging 12–15%.

Broker Narrative

The narrative shifted from cyclical industrial exposure and China/Europe weakness with Solar EPC margin dilution in early 2024 to defense import dependence and Atmanirbhar Bharat tailwinds by 2026. Early themes of subsidiary losses and muted bearing demand faded, replaced by geopolitical indigenization concerns. The bull case moved from foreign subsidiary recovery and cage outsourcing to government defense procurement policies.

Fears that came true

  • Weak demand from China/Europe and Romania/China subsidiary losses materialized, correlating with the DISAPPOINTMENT outcome on 2024-02-09 and the -15.3% return in 2024-08-08.
  • India Engineering revenue decline and muted large-size bearing offtake persisted, aligning with the negative early-2024 actual returns.
  • Gross margin compression from lower-margin Solar EPC business materialized, as flagged by the 348bps YoY fall.

Optimism that failed

  • Solar EPC revenue growth failed to improve margins, as gross margin fell 348bps due to its higher share.
  • Foreign subsidiary profitability recovery (China turnaround, Romania wind revival) did not sustain as a theme by 2026.
  • Long-term levers (bearing cage outsourcing, Japan wallet share, EV plastic cages) were superseded by defense import headwinds.
