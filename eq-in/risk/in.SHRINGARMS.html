SHRINGARMS: Recent Corporate Developments

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Margin compression despite revenue growth. In Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026), revenue from operations grew 64.9% year-on-year to INR 548.5 crores, but profitability grew much more slowly. Gross profit rose 17.5% to INR 57.0 crores, EBITDA rose 18.7% to INR 48.9 crores, and profit after tax rose 19.3% to INR 34.0 crores. Margins contracted sharply: gross profit margin fell from 14.6% in Q1 FY26 to 10.4% in Q1 FY27 (a decline of about 420 basis points), EBITDA margin fell from 12.4% to 8.9% (about 350 basis points), and PAT margin fell from 8.6% to 6.2% (about 240 basis points).

Sequential decline. Compared with Q4 FY26, Q1 FY27 revenue fell 24.4% (from INR 725.6 crores to INR 548.5 crores) and gross profit fell 11.7% (from INR 64.5 crores to INR 57.0 crores). EBITDA, however, rose 9.4% quarter-on-quarter.

Working capital intensity and cash flow strain. Management describes operations as working capital intensive. The FY26 cash flow statement shows a sharp deterioration: changes in working capital of negative INR 408.0 crores, cash generated from operations of negative INR 246.5 crores, and net cash from operating activities of negative INR 281.7 crores, against positive operating cash flow in FY23. This was accompanied by large increases in inventories (from INR 228.1 crores in FY25 to INR 439.4 crores in FY26) and trade receivables (from INR 87.8 crores to INR 235.7 crores). The FY26 net increase in cash was funded mainly by financing activities (INR 410.3 crores).

Borrowing limits may be insufficient. The company has stated that existing borrowing limits may not be adequate to meet its financial requirements given the scale of its business and future growth plans. A proposal to increase borrowing limits up to INR 1,000 crores under Section 180(1)(c) is being put to shareholders at the 17th Annual General Meeting scheduled for Monday, September 28, 2026, at 3:00 p.m. IST via video conferencing or other audio-visual means.

Capacity ramp-up risk. Commercial production from the new capacity resumed on February 23, 2026, with 57% capacity utilization at that point. The company states production is expected to ramp up in the coming quarters. The new capacity was added in February 2026, and blended capacity for the full year is stated as 2,625 kg, implying 87% actual capacity utilization.

Gold price and cost exposure. The company hedges against gold price fluctuations by purchasing an equivalent quantity of gold at a similar price immediately after fulfilling client orders, uses Gold Metal Loans, commodity futures, and the Advanced Gold Model (about 30% of bullion requirements). It also relies on outsourced production to complement in-house capacity.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Strong top-line momentum. Q1 FY27 revenue grew 64.9% year-on-year to INR 548.5 crores. Over a longer horizon, revenue from corporate clients grew from INR 950 crores in FY23 to INR 1,102 crores in FY24, INR 1,430 crores in FY25, and INR 2,246 crores in FY26, a compound annual growth rate of about 57%. Total revenue from operations rose from INR 950.2 crores in FY23 to INR 2,245.8 crores in FY26.

Bridal jewellery entry. The company entered the bridal jewellery segment, which management describes as gaining strong momentum in Q1 FY27. The investor presentation cites bridal jewellery as accounting for 23–25% of total wedding costs in India, with an average of 30 to 250 grams of gold consumed in marriages. The company sees its legacy partnerships with leading institutional jewellery brands, design expertise in chains, bracelets, and rings, in-house design strength, and integrated end-to-end manufacturing as advantages in this high-ticket segment.

Organised retail formalisation. The presentation cites compulsory hallmarking, GST compliance, and consumer demand for transparency as drivers of consolidation toward organised retail. Leading brands are estimated to add 400–440 new retail outlets across domestic and global markets in the near to mid-term, which the company expects to drive additional demand. The organised wholesale gold jewellery market is projected to reach a value of INR 489 billion by CY29, representing a CAGR of 9.0% from CY24 to CY29.

Distribution expansion. The company has opened branch offices in Delhi and Pune, with Pune acting as a coordination hub for Marathwada and Vidarbha and Delhi for North India. Third-party facilitators have been onboarded in Nagpur, Agra, Durg, Ahmedabad, and Pune, displaying approximately 5 kg of inventory each to showcase designs. The company is targeting unorganised and underserved Tier-2 to Tier-4 markets.

Capacity expansion. The company invested approximately INR 15 crores via internal accruals to expand capacity from 2,500 kg to 4,000 kg, upgrade machinery, and improve efficiency. Installed capacity rose from 2,500 kg in FY23 to 2,900 kg in FY24 and FY25, and 4,000 kg in FY26. Actual production was 2,500 kg in FY23, 2,900 kg in FY24, and 2,900 kg in FY25.

Fundraising capacity. The company completed its Initial Public Offering of 2,43,00,000 equity shares at INR 165 per equity share, aggregating to INR 4,009.50 million, with shares listed on NSE and BSE on September 17, 2025. Management states that listing enabled growth funds. Unutilized net IPO proceeds of INR 2.54 million were held in the company’s Kotak Bank Account as of June 30, 2026. The company is also seeking shareholder approval to increase borrowing limits up to INR 1,000 crores to support growing business operations, procurement of gold, inventory management, retail expansion, and strengthening working capital.

Client relationships. As of FY26, the company served 35 corporate clients, 1,061 wholesalers, and 237 retailers. Corporate customers include Malabar Gold, Titan, Reliance Retail, Novel Jewel, Joyalukkas, PN Gadgil Jewellers, and Kalyan Jewellers (UAE). The company has 15+ collections and 10,000+ active SKUs, with 30 in-house designers and 316 skilled karigars, plus 73 additional karigars through long-term partnerships.

3. Key Risks

Margin sustainability. The sharp year-on-year margin contraction in Q1 FY27 raises the question of whether the company can sustain profitability as it scales, particularly given the working capital intensity of the business.

Cash flow and liquidity. The FY26 operating cash flow was negative INR 281.7 crores, with working capital consuming INR 408.0 crores. The company relies on financing activities to fund growth. The proposed increase in borrowing limits to INR 1,000 crores reflects the need for external funding.

Capacity utilization. The new capacity resumed production on February 23, 2026, at 57% utilization. Failure to ramp up utilization could weigh on returns from the approximately INR 15 crores invested in expansion.

Gold price and supply chain. The company’s hedging mechanisms (Gold Metal Loans, commodity futures, Advanced Gold Model) and reliance on outsourced production and bullion suppliers expose it to gold price volatility and supply chain disruptions.

Dependence on corporate clients. The business is heavily oriented toward corporate clients, whose expansion plans and purchasing decisions drive demand. The presentation notes that leading brands plan to add 400–440 new outlets, but this is an estimate and not guaranteed.

Regulatory and compliance. The company has issued reminders for shareholders to update KYC details and dematerialize physical securities pursuant to SEBI circulars. The AGM notice includes proposals to increase remuneration for key managerial personnel, including Chairman and Managing Director Mr. Chetan Natvarlal Thadeshwar, Executive Director Mr. Viraj Chetan Thadeshwar, and Whole-Time Director Mr. Balraj Chetan Thadeshwar.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Management commentary. Chairman and Managing Director Mr. Chetan N Thadeshwar stated that FY27 started on a positive note with revenues and PAT delivering steady growth in Q1, supported by healthy volume growth. He cited encouraging traction across both business verticals, robust momentum in bridal jewellery, and confidence in its long-term potential. He stated the focus remains on deepening partner relationships, expanding product offerings, sharpening assortment, strengthening product innovation, and responding to evolving partner needs. Looking ahead, he cited significant headroom to scale the franchise as the jewellery ecosystem evolves and organised players gain relevance, with focus on expanding distribution footprint, strengthening product capabilities, and building operational capacity for sustainable growth.

Observed performance. Revenue growth of 64.9% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 is consistent with the stated positive momentum. However, PAT growth of 19.3% lagged revenue growth, and all three margins (gross profit, EBITDA, and PAT) contracted year-on-year. The quantity sold was 356 kg in Q1 FY27 versus 376 kg in Q1 FY26, a decline of about 5.3%, even as revenue grew, indicating that revenue growth was driven by price or product mix rather than volume. This contrasts with the management reference to “healthy volume growth across the business.”
