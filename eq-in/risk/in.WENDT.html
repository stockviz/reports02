WENDT (India) Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Machines and Accessories segment remains loss-making. On a standalone basis, the segment reported a loss of Rs. 190 lakhs in Q1 FY26-27 (quarter ended 30 June 2025) and a loss of Rs. 350 lakhs for the full year ended 31 March 2026. On a consolidated basis, the segment loss was Rs. 330 lakhs in Q1 FY26-27 and Rs. 1,360 lakhs for FY25-26. Although the segment improved to a profit of Rs. 66 lakhs (standalone) and a loss of Rs. 149 lakhs (consolidated) in the quarter ended 30 June 2026, it remains the weakest of the reported segments.
  • Overseas subsidiaries are loss-making. The auditors’ consolidated review report notes that the two subsidiaries incorporated outside India (Wendt Grinding Technologies Ltd, Thailand and Wendt GmbH, Germany) reflected total revenue of Rs. 1,137 lakhs and a total net loss after tax of Rs. 139 lakhs for the quarter ended 30 June 2026.
  • Sequential moderation in the largest segment. Standalone Super Abrasives segment results declined from Rs. 849 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 to Rs. 802 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2026, and consolidated Super Abrasives results declined from Rs. 851 lakhs to Rs. 762 lakhs over the same period.
  • Precision Components declined sequentially. Standalone Precision Components revenue fell from Rs. 856 lakhs in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 to Rs. 757 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2026, with segment results improving only modestly from Rs. 68 lakhs to Rs. 83 lakhs.
  • Employee benefit costs rose. Consolidated employee benefits expense increased from Rs. 1,219 lakhs in Q1 FY26-27 to Rs. 1,518 lakhs in Q1 FY27-28.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong year-on-year growth. Standalone sales for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 were Rs. 6,077 lakhs, 31% higher year-on-year; standalone PAT was Rs. 800 lakhs, 62% higher year-on-year. Consolidated sales were Rs. 7,093 lakhs, 37% higher year-on-year, with consolidated PAT of Rs. 618 lakhs, 63% higher year-on-year.
  • Domestic demand broad-based. Domestic sales of Rs. 4,925 lakhs were 38% higher year-on-year, attributed to higher sales to major user industries including auto, auto ancillaries, blade, bearings, ceramics and resellers.
  • Export growth. Exports of Rs. 1,152 lakhs were 7% higher year-on-year, attributed to higher offtake from the US, Singapore, Thailand, Canada, Australia and Spain.
  • Sequential improvement. Standalone sales rose 7% quarter-on-quarter with PAT up 7%; consolidated sales rose 8% with PAT up 21% against the quarter ended 31 March 2026.
  • Machines and Accessories turnaround. The segment moved from losses in prior periods to a standalone profit of Rs. 66 lakhs in the quarter ended 30 June 2026, with consolidated segment loss narrowing to Rs. 149 lakhs.
  • Balance sheet expansion. Standalone total assets rose from Rs. 26,302 lakhs (30 June 2025) to Rs. 27,967 lakhs (30 June 2026); consolidated total assets rose from Rs. 29,075 lakhs to Rs. 31,729 lakhs over the same period.
  • Shareholder returns. The 44th AGM approved a final dividend of Rs. 10 per equity share for FY2025-26 and confirmed an interim dividend of Rs. 20 per equity share.
  • ESG recognition. Crisil ESG Ratings & Analytics Limited, a SEBI-registered ESG Rating Provider, assigned an ESG rating to the Company, with the rating report submitted to BSE and NSE (communication received 3 August 2026).

3. Key Risks

  • Segment concentration. Super Abrasives dominates revenue and profitability — standalone segment revenue of Rs. 4,152 lakhs out of Rs. 6,077 lakhs total in Q1 FY27-28, and segment results of Rs. 802 lakhs out of Rs. 951 lakhs total. Weakness in this segment would disproportionately affect overall performance.
  • Persistent losses in Machines and Accessories. Despite improvement, the segment recorded a full-year standalone loss of Rs. 350 lakhs and consolidated loss of Rs. 1,360 lakhs in FY25-26.
  • Overseas subsidiary losses. The two foreign subsidiaries recorded a combined net loss after tax of Rs. 139 lakhs for the quarter ended 30 June 2026.
  • Foreign exchange exposure. Consolidated other comprehensive income includes exchange differences on translating foreign operations, which moved from a gain of Rs. 63 lakhs in Q1 FY26-27 to a loss of Rs. 23 lakhs in Q1 FY27-28.
  • Input cost and inventory volatility. Standalone changes in inventories swung from a credit of Rs. 293 lakhs in Q1 FY26-27 to a charge of Rs. 259 lakhs in Q1 FY27-28; consolidated changes in inventories moved from a credit of Rs. 369 lakhs to a charge of Rs. 30 lakhs.
  • Actuarial/defined benefit obligation volatility. Remeasurements of the defined benefit obligation produced other comprehensive losses of Rs. 21 lakhs (standalone) and Rs. 23 lakhs (consolidated) in Q1 FY27-28.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

The supplied material does not contain explicit forward-looking management guidance or projections. The announcements instead report observed performance and the Board’s approved actions:

  • The Board approved the unaudited financial results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 at its meeting on 24 July 2026, and the Chairman announced the Company’s performance for that quarter at the 44th AGM held the same day.
  • The statutory auditors (Price Waterhouse Chartered Accountants LLP) issued limited review reports on both the standalone and consolidated results with no qualifications or material misstatements noted.
  • The auditors’ report on the FY2025-26 financial statements and the Secretarial Audit Report contained no qualifications, observations or comments with an adverse effect on the functioning of the Company.
  • The AGM transacted adoption of the standalone and consolidated audited financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2026, declaration of the final dividend, re-appointment of Mr. Muthiah Venkatachalam as a rotating director, and ratification of the Cost Auditor’s remuneration for FY2026-27.
  • The Company stated it would publish an extract of the consolidated results with a QR code in English and Kannada newspapers within the stipulated time, and make detailed results available on its website and the stock exchange websites.
  • E-voting results were to be declared within two working days of the AGM and placed on the Company’s website and disseminated to the stock exchanges.
