SMLT: Recent Corporate Announcements — Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Aluminium flipping coils under pressure. Management describes the segment as “subdued during the quarter, reflecting difficult market conditions and increasing constraints in aluminium scrap availability.” The UAE’s June 2026 ban on aluminium scrap exports, along with similar restrictions emerging across more countries, is tightening global scrap availability as major markets retain recycled metal domestically, while rising primary aluminium prices are pushing scrap prices higher. Segment volumes fell 23% year-on-year and realizations declined 31% quarter-on-quarter, with revenue at ₹4 crore for Q1 FY27.

Margin compression. EBITDA (excluding other income and exceptional items) declined 12% year-on-year and 38% quarter-on-quarter to ₹1.55 crore, with EBITDA margin at 2.81% versus 3.80% in Q1 FY26 and 4.04% in Q4 FY26. Finance costs rose 110% year-on-year to ₹0.26 crore.

Biotechnology overcapacity. Substantial capacity additions across the ethanol industry have resulted in under-utilisation of several plants in certain regions. Management states it remains disciplined in evaluating further investments and will pursue opportunities only where industry utilisation and commercial viability provide sufficient clarity. ₹50 lakh has been invested to date, primarily in a pilot R&D facility in Nagpur.

Compliance lapses. The AGM proceedings for the meeting held 18 August 2026 were submitted to the exchanges on 19 August 2026 at 9:29 A.M., beyond the twelve-hour requirement from the 1:14 P.M. conclusion. The company attributed this to an inadvertent oversight in its internal process and stated it has strengthened compliance monitoring and timeline-based review. Separately, the Q1 FY27 results filed on 13 August 2026 contained an error — figures were swapped between Cost of Material Consumed and Purchase of Stock-in-Trade, and the results were described as audited when they were limited reviewed. Revised financials were filed on 18 August 2026, with no material change to PBT or PAT.

Governance continuity. Mr. Dwadasi Venkata Giri, Independent Director and Chairperson of the Stakeholder Relationship Committee, Audit Committee, Corporate Social Responsibility Committee and Nomination and Remuneration Committee, was absent from the 31st AGM.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Steel demand outlook. Management cites India’s long-term steel outlook as encouraging, supported by continued investments in infrastructure, railways, housing, manufacturing and government-led capital expenditure. Steel manufacturers continue to expand capacity, and improving macroeconomic conditions plus investments in logistics and connectivity are expected to strengthen the domestic steel value chain.

Cored wires momentum. Order flow momentum continued into Q1 FY27, with volumes growing 7% year-on-year to 1,834 tonnes. Ferro titanium is highlighted as an area of particular strength, supported by technical expertise and relatively limited competition. Recent global supply disruptions have underscored the importance of reliable service, product quality and inventory availability — areas where the company maintains an advantage through quality standards and resilient supply capabilities.

Welding division scaling. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹5.1 crore with volumes up 94% year-on-year to 458 tonnes, despite the seasonally weaker first quarter. Over the past year, more than 1,600 tonnes of flux-cored welding consumables have been sold. A dealer-led distribution model now spans 10+ dealers across more than five states, with rising repeat orders. Certifications including RDSO and BIS are strengthening access to institutional and government opportunities. The stated target is ₹25 crore in annual sales for FY27, against annual production capacity of 3,600 tonnes and 7 grades across carbon steel, stainless steel and hardfacing. The segment is positioned as an import substitution opportunity, with India’s flux cored wire imports averaging ~14,500+ TPA over three years and SML’s initial announced quantity at 1,200+ TPA.

Capacity and infrastructure. Manufacturing capacity (double shift) stands at 10,000 TPA cored wires, 15,000 TPA aluminium flipping coils and 3,600 TPA flux cored wires, across three Bhilai facilities with cumulative plot area and land availability for ~4,50,000 sq. ft. of future expansion. Warehousing includes 1,500 sq. mt. for cored wires (2,000+ tonnes, ~3 months’ buffer), 2,800 sq. mt. for aluminium flipping coils (4,000+ tonnes, ~4 months’ buffer) and 1,000 sq. mt. for flux cored wires.

Sustainability positioning. About 75% of aluminium flipping coil production comes from aluminium scrap, which requires only 5% of the energy needed for ore reduction. 400 KW of renewable energy is in use, with captive consumption expected to reduce electricity expenses by approximately 50%.

3. Key Risks

  • Raw material availability and pricing: Aluminium scrap export restrictions in the UAE and other markets, combined with rising primary aluminium prices, are tightening scrap availability and raising input costs for the flipping coils business.
  • Segment concentration: The company operates in a single segment, “Cored Wires,” per the results notes.
  • Margin sensitivity: EBITDA margin compression of 99 bps year-on-year and 123 bps quarter-on-quarter indicates limited cushion if input costs or realizations move adversely.
  • Execution risk in new businesses: The welding division’s ₹25 crore FY27 target depends on continued volume growth, dealer expansion and certification-driven institutional access. The biotechnology venture faces industry-wide overcapacity and remains subject to further evaluation.
  • Compliance and disclosure risk: The delayed AGM proceedings submission and the restatement of Q1 FY27 figures (including the audited/limited-reviewed misstatement) point to internal process gaps, which the company states it has addressed.
  • Customer concentration: Top 5 clients contribute 40% of sales, though 85% of business is repeat and 60 relationships exceed 10 years.
  • Foreign currency exposure: ₹6.34 lakh of unrealised gains on foreign currency transactions as at 30 June 2026.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

Welding — guidance vs. actual. Management aims for ₹25 crore in annual sales in FY27. Q1 FY27 actual revenue was ₹5.1 crore (up 89% year-on-year per the business update; the P&L summary shows welding revenue of ₹5 crore with 94% volume growth to 458 tonnes). The company notes Q1 is seasonally weaker, and over 1,600 tonnes have been sold in the past year. The stated ₹25 crore target implies a significant step-up from the Q1 run-rate.

Biotechnology — measured approach. Earlier objective was integrating technology solutions with ethanol distilleries to improve fermentation efficiency and reduce operating costs. Field experience has reinforced the need for a measured approach; further investments will be pursued only where industry utilisation and commercial viability provide sufficient clarity. ₹50 lakh invested to date in the Nagpur pilot R&D facility.

Cored wires — momentum confirmed. Management’s commentary on continued order flow momentum is supported by the 7% year-on-year volume growth to 1,834 tonnes in Q1 FY27.

Aluminium flipping coils — no growth guidance, weakness observed. Management states it will monitor developments closely and remain focused on commercial viability and disciplined operations. Observed performance shows a 23% year-on-year volume decline and 31% quarter-on-quarter realization decline.

Overall financial trajectory. Q1 FY27 revenue from operations was ₹55.21 crore (up 19% year-on-year, down 11% quarter-on-quarter); PBT ₹2.07 crore (up 33% year-on-year, down 4% quarter-on-quarter); PAT ₹1.33 crore (up 25% year-on-year, down 11% quarter-on-quarter); EPS ₹0.97. The five-year trend shows revenue rising from ₹178.42 crore in FY22 to ₹192.25 crore in FY26, with PAT peaking at ₹29.76 crore in FY23 and declining to ₹4.61 crore in FY26. EBITDA margin fell from 10.6% in FY22 to 4.1% in FY26. Equity rose from ₹77.94 crore in FY22 to ₹124.27 crore in FY26, with debt-to-equity at 0.29 in FY22 declining to zero in FY25 and FY26.
