Sudarshan Chemical Industries – Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Geopolitical and macro pressure. Management described a Middle East crisis that has driven energy cost spikes across Europe and India, increased raw material and logistics costs, and lengthened the logistics cycle by roughly two weeks. Customers across industries have responded by delaying purchases and avoiding stock build-up.

Raw material and input cost inflation. Raw material prices rose substantially, with additional supply constraints for specific materials such as urea in India. Price increases taken in the market were described as pass-through of cost inflation rather than demand-supply gains, and management indicated some pricing would be reversed if raw material and oil prices soften.

Segment demand softness. Coatings markets in the US and Europe (decorative and automotive) were described as subdued. Plastics customers moved to hand-to-mouth supply amid high polymer prices. Volume-driven printing inks continued a long-term decline due to digitalisation, though regulated packaging and specialty applications were growing.

RIECO execution issues. RIECO had a difficult quarter, with revenue from operations at ₹38 crore. Execution was affected by delays in customer-side readiness and subcontracting manpower availability, which led to an EBITDA drop. Management expects normalisation from the current quarter and a positive contribution before year end.

Integration and systems complexity. The acquired business operated in silos with no unified culture or harmonised reporting. The group still runs more than four different SAP systems and about 130 applications, making financial information difficult to obtain. An interim MIS is in place, with the One SAP “Project Integra” targeted to go live during the financial year.

Restructuring charge pending quantification. A wholly owned subsidiary entered an agreement with an employee representative body for an employee restructuring programme after 30 June 2026. The impact was not quantified in the Q1 results; management expects clarity on quantification by the end of Q2.

Governance signal from voting. At the 75th AGM on 18 August 2026, the resolution re-appointing Mr. Amitabha Mukhopadhyay as Non-Executive and Non-Independent Director passed with 93.44% votes in favour, with 6.56% against. Public institutions recorded 14.68% against on this resolution, a higher dissent level than on the other three resolutions, each of which passed with over 99.9% support.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Scale and footprint. The combination of Sudarshan, Clariant and Heubach created a pigment platform with 19 manufacturing sites in 11 countries across five continents, more than 1,600 products, over 4,000 global customers and turnover close to €1 billion. Management highlighted that 55–60% of assets are based in Asia as a competitive advantage.

Turnaround of the acquired group. EBITDA for the acquired group went from near zero at acquisition to ₹146 crore reported (₹128 crore business EBITDA) in Q1 FY27. Net debt fell from a peak of ₹922 crore at takeover to ₹531 crore in under 18 months, a reduction of about 60%.

Value capture and cost reduction. Management stated that profitability improvement is substantially driven by cost reduction and value capture, with the full benefit not yet in the numbers. Value capture is expected to be continuous, with the major portion in FY2027–FY2028 and some flowing into FY2029.

Recovery of lost business. The acquired business lost significant business due to lack of focus and insolvency issues. Management sees scope to regain this business, which underpins confidence in growth beyond market rates.

Capacity headroom. No new volume capex is needed to support the indicated growth numbers; only moderate, ROI-driven capex for special initiatives such as backward integration is contemplated.

Portfolio shift to higher-value categories. High-performance pigments and pigment dispersions have grown substantially in the product mix, and specialty applications such as agro and digital inks are seeing good growth.

Divestment of non-core asset. The sale of step-down subsidiary VP4 Frankfurt GmbH to Celanese Corporation, USA, allows the company to sharpen focus on its core pigments business. VP4 Frankfurt manufactures intermediates such as Diketene primarily for Nutrinova and Mitsui & Co. Japan, and became part of Sudarshan through the Heubach acquisition. Nutrinova will continue to supply Sudarshan with required materials after completion.

Group holding simplification. The board accorded in-principle approval on 12 August 2026 to acquire 1,62,16,847 equity shares (70.26%) of Sudarshan Colorants India Limited (formerly Heubach Colorants India Limited) from Sudarshan Europe B.V. and the Swiss step-down subsidiaries, so the stake will be held directly. The transaction is an internal restructuring with no impact on control or business operations.

Shareholder returns and capital actions. The 75th AGM approved a final dividend of ₹5.00 per equity share (250% of face value ₹2.00) for FY2025-26. Mr. Rajesh Rathi exercised 9,80,000 warrants at ₹1,019.75 each, paying the remaining 75% (about ₹75 crore) and receiving 9,80,000 equity shares on 10 June 2026; his holding rose from 5.15% to 6.32% and promoter group holding from 8.19% to 9.32%.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical and energy cost volatility affecting manufacturing costs, logistics and customer buying behaviour.
  • Raw material price and supply risk, including constraints on specific materials, with pricing reversals possible if input costs fall.
  • Demand risk in core end markets – subdued US and European coatings, hand-to-mouth plastics buying, and structural decline in volume-driven printing inks.
  • Execution risk at RIECO, where recovery depends on subcontractor manpower availability and customer site readiness.
  • Integration and reporting risk from multiple SAP systems and applications until Project Integra goes live.
  • Unquantified restructuring charge related to the employee restructuring programme, with quantification expected by end of Q2.
  • Currency translation exposure given the large international business; foreign exchange movements affected standalone and consolidated other expenses.
  • Guidance risk – management explicitly chose not to revise guidance despite a strong Q1, citing geopolitical uncertainty and a wait-and-watch stance until after Q2.

4. Management Guidance vs Observed Performance

FY27 guidance for the acquired group reaffirmed. Management maintained guidance of ~€700 million sales and ~€35 million EBITDA for the acquired group, despite Q1 being “much stronger” than expected. The decision not to revise was attributed to the geopolitical situation, with a commitment to reconsider after Q2.

Q1 FY27 observed performance (One Sudarshan consolidated). Revenue from operations was ₹2,642 crore, up 5.4% year-on-year. Reported EBITDA was ₹266 crore and business EBITDA was ₹247 crore, with around 60%+ year-on-year EBITDA growth. PBT excluding RPS impact was ₹164 crore. EPS for the quarter (not annualised) was ₹12.3, ROCE 22.7%, net debt ₹531 crore, net debt-to-equity 0.2, and annualised net working capital 23.6%.

Segment detail. Pigments segment revenue was ₹2,604.4 crore with EBITDA of ₹268.8 crore. The “Others” segment (including RIECO) recorded revenue of ₹37.7 crore and negative EBITDA of ₹9.5 crore.

Legacy Sudarshan. Management indicated legacy Sudarshan should sustain performance in the region of 12–13% growth. Standalone revenue from operations for the quarter was ₹677.4 crore against ₹531.2 crore in the prior-year quarter.

Acquired group. Grew 5% despite the geopolitical situation. Business EBITDA rose from ₹65 crore to ₹128 crore; reported EBITDA rose from ₹78 crore to ₹146 crore. The ₹18 crore difference between reported and business EBITDA was attributed to inventory increase impact.

Gross margin. Q1 gross margin was described as one of the highest at about 54%, with roughly 2% movement from the prior quarter attributed mainly to cost reduction in yields, utilities and production, and only minor one-off items. Management expects gross margin to remain in the 50%+ range.

Acquired group margin trajectory. Management indicated the acquired group could move to high single digits or low double digits, and noted the business historically delivered double digits consistently. The FY29 aspiration is ₹12,000–14,000 crore revenue and ₹1,400–1,500 crore EBITDA with positive cash, a target management said has been held since day one.

Debt reduction. Management stated the acquisition debt will be repaid faster as the net debt position improves, with a stated aspiration to reach a debt-free level.

Volume vs value. Management did not confirm a volume decline in Q1, citing the broad product range (products priced from €1 to €130–140) and a modest 6% growth for the quarter. Growth is expected to come from a mix of business regain and value capture, with profitability and gross margin protection prioritised over pure volume or value growth.

ESG rating. Crisil ESG Ratings & Analytics Limited voluntarily assigned a rating of ‘Crisil ESG 53’ and a Core ESG rating of ‘Crisil Core ESG 55’ to the company on 10 September 2026, based on FY2025-26 data. The company did not engage Crisil for this rating; it was independently determined using public disclosures and publicly available information.

Broker Narrative

The narrative moved from a cautious Accumulate built on post-capex organic growth, specialty-mix gains and a demand revival after cost shocks, to a confident Buy built on Heubach integration, synergy capture and deleveraging. Persistent themes were margin/demand pressure, raw-material/logistics inflation, muted global demand and debt reduction. The key shift was that Chinese dumping and slow ramp-up of newly commissioned capex receded, replaced by integration execution, value-capture levers and Heubach margin recovery as the main swing factors.

Fears that came true

  • Margin/PAT pressure was never fully repaired: the last report’s consolidated EBITDA margin was 9.8% versus 12.3% in the first report, and continued cost inflation coincided with Buy calls that delivered negative actual returns of -32.1% and -28.8%.
  • The expected demand slowdown/slower capacity ramp-up materialised, with subdued US/European decorative and automotive coatings and hand-to-mouth plastics buying, matching the repeated DISAPPOINTMENT/negative outcomes after 2024.
  • The early warning on raw-material/logistics/energy volatility proved correct, as the last report still cited energy and raw-material inflation, logistics lead-time stretches and Middle East conflict-driven cost increases.

Optimism that failed

  • The strong demand revival anticipated after Q4FY23 did not sustain, as the last report still flagged subdued coatings demand and structural decline in printing and inks volumes.
  • The expected softening of input costs reversed, with crude/toluene/benzene moderation giving way to persistent energy and raw-material inflation.
  • The projected revenue ramp-up from the Rs7.5bn specialty capex was not evident by the last report, which instead relied on Heubach guidance and synergies for growth.
