TruCap Finance Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Financial stress and deteriorating asset quality. The company has been operating under continued financial stress, with asset quality deteriorating sharply. Gross NPA rose to 49.90% as of June 30, 2026, from 25.24% as of March 31, 2026, and 1.42% as of June 30, 2025. Net NPA stood at 31.50% as of June 30, 2026, compared with 15.63% as of March 31, 2026, and 0.90% as of June 30, 2025. The overall provision coverage ratio was 37.00% as of June 30, 2026.

Losses. The company reported a net loss after tax of ₹1,039.65 lakhs for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, compared with a loss of ₹1,771.28 lakhs in the quarter ended March 31, 2026, and a loss of ₹886.97 lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2025. For the year ended March 31, 2026, the standalone loss after tax was ₹11,042.33 lakhs. Total income for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was ₹1,218.12 lakhs, down from ₹4,198.85 lakhs in the quarter ended June 30, 2025.

Security cover breach. The company’s security cover on secured borrowings became inadequate from the quarter ended December 31, 2025, driven by lower business growth arising from resource limitations and the ongoing rundown of the loan book. The statutory auditor’s security cover certificate concluded that the asset cover available to holders of secured non-convertible debentures as of June 30, 2026 was lower than the minimum asset cover requirement stipulated in the Debenture Trust Deed.

Defaults and standstill. The company defaulted on repayment of borrowings, and its account with various lenders turned into a non-performing asset. A standstill on all payments to lenders was declared effective October 01, 2025. Credit rating downgrades to ‘D’ by CARE and Infomerics were noted. The company also faced acceleration of repayment obligations under certain listed debt securities, termination of the Gold Loan Business slump sale, and termination of transactions with MCG.

Going concern uncertainty. The auditor’s review report on both standalone and consolidated results drew attention to the breach of security cover and other loan covenants, continued financial stress including deteriorating asset quality, the restructuring plan presented to existing lenders, and potential equity infusion. The increased loss, in addition to these conditions, indicates conditions that may cast significant impact on the going concern of the company. The going concern is dependent upon the restructuring plan and potential equity infusion.

Other challenges. Elevated gross NPAs, potential attrition of employees and customers during the transition, heightened competition in gold and MSME lending, compliance intensity under new gold-lending directions, sensitivity of gold-backed portfolios to bullion price corrections, and macroeconomic uncertainties such as rising inflation and energy price shocks were noted.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Macro and sector opportunities. Growth prospects are supported by India’s position as the fastest-growing major economy, a substantial MSME credit gap estimated at approximately ₹25 lakh crore, the rapid formalization of small-business cash flows through GST and digital payments, and the growth of the organised gold loan market. The rapid transition to electric vehicles in the three-wheeler segment—where EVs account for over 60% of registrations supported by government schemes—presents opportunities in EV financing.

Restructuring and capital infusion. A joint meeting with lenders was held on July 06, 2026, to decide the future course of action and discuss the proposed restructuring plan. As of the date of approval of the financial results, 60% of lenders by value have expressed support for the proposed restructuring plan, and 26% of lenders have yet to revert. The company is hopeful of getting through the proposed restructuring plan. Potential capital infusion and debt restructuring plans under discussion aim to recapitalize the balance sheet and restore operational capabilities.

Penal interest reversal. Consequently, penal interest amounting to ₹287.11 lakhs, which had been recognised in earlier periods, was reversed during the current period, considering the anticipated waiver/relaxation of such penal interest under the proposed restructuring plan.

3. Key Risks

  • Going concern risk dependent on the restructuring plan and potential equity infusion.
  • Security cover inadequacy on secured borrowings and non-convertible debentures.
  • Default on debt obligations and declaration of standstill on all payments to lenders effective October 01, 2025.
  • Credit rating downgrades to ‘D’ by CARE and Infomerics.
  • Elevated gross NPAs (49.90% as of June 30, 2026) and net NPAs (31.50%).
  • Potential attrition of employees and customers during the transition.
  • Sector-level competition in gold and MSME lending.
  • Compliance intensity under new gold-lending directions.
  • Sensitivity of gold-backed portfolios to bullion price corrections.
  • Macroeconomic uncertainties such as rising inflation and energy price shocks.
  • Restructuring plan uncertainty: 26% of lenders by value have yet to revert on the proposed restructuring plan.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance. Management states that FY 2026-27 will be a year of stabilisation and rebuilding. Immediate priorities outlined include resolving capital and ownership matters (including the outcome of the impending open offer and completion of the proposed restructuring plan with lenders involving contemplated debt-to-equity conversion), preservation of asset quality and intensive collections, retention of the branch network, employee base, and lender relationships, and a calibrated resumption of disbursements in gold loans and MSME business loans as funding normalizes.

Observed performance. The quarter ended June 30, 2026 showed a net loss of ₹1,039.65 lakhs (standalone) and ₹1,058.04 lakhs (consolidated), with GNPA at 49.90% and NNPA at 31.50%. Total income declined to ₹1,218.12 lakhs from ₹4,198.85 lakhs in the prior-year quarter. The debt-equity ratio rose to 7.67 times as of June 30, 2026, from 6.29 times as of March 31, 2026, and 3.22 times as of June 30, 2025. Net worth declined to ₹4,318.93 lakhs as of June 30, 2026, from ₹5,328.48 lakhs as of March 31, 2026, and ₹14,224.16 lakhs as of June 30, 2025. The auditor noted that the going concern is dependent upon the restructuring plan and potential equity infusion, and the security cover certificate confirmed the asset cover was below the minimum requirement as of June 30, 2026.

Other developments. The Board appointed M/s Dinesh Jain & Co., Chartered Accountants, as Internal Auditors for the financial year ending March 31, 2027, and appointed Mr. Shailendra Roy as Interim Chief Financial Officer and Key Managerial Personnel with effect from August 14, 2026. The 32nd Annual General Meeting is scheduled for Tuesday, September 29, 2026, to be held through video conferencing. A notice was sent to shareholders on July 22, 2026, regarding transfer of unclaimed dividend for FY 2018-2019 to the Investor Education and Protection Fund, due to be transferred on October 23, 2026, if no valid claim is received by October 10, 2026.
