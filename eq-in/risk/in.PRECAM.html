PRECAM: 34th AGM Voting Analysis

Headwinds and Challenges

The 34th Annual General Meeting, held on July 30, 2026 via video conferencing, brought several governance and remuneration matters to shareholders that indicate areas of strain.

The most notable challenge relates to executive remuneration. Resolution 5 sought approval for payment of remuneration to Executive Directors in excess of the limits specified under Section 197 and Schedule V of the Companies Act, 2013 for the Financial Year 2025-26. This indicates that remuneration paid during FY2025-26 exceeded statutory limits tied to profits, requiring shareholder ratification.

Resolutions 6, 7, and 8 sought approval for remuneration to Mr. Yatin S. Shah (Chairman and Managing Director), Mr. Ravindra R. Joshi (Whole-Time Director and CFO), and Mr. Karan Y. Shah (Whole-Time Director) respectively, in the event of inadequacy or absence of profits for FY2026-27. The need to seek such approvals in advance suggests the company is preparing for a scenario where profits may be insufficient to support executive remuneration under normal statutory provisions.

Public Institutional investors demonstrated significant dissent on these remuneration resolutions. On Resolution 5, Public Institutions cast 87,688 votes against (99.13% of their votes polled) versus only 767 in favour. The same pattern repeated on Resolutions 6, 7, and 8, with Public Institutions opposing at 99.13% against in each case. This represents a clear governance concern raised by institutional shareholders regarding executive compensation practices.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

The AGM saw strong shareholder participation, with 77.10% of outstanding shares voted. The total votes polled were 73,233,056 out of 94,985,835 shares.

All eight resolutions passed with the requisite majority. The adoption of audited standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY2025-26 (Resolution 1) received near-unanimous support, with 99.99% of votes in favour.

The declaration of a final dividend of ₹1 per equity share of ₹10 each for FY2025-26 (Resolution 2) was approved with 99.99% support, indicating confidence in the company’s financial position and commitment to shareholder returns.

The re-appointment of Mr. Karan Y. Shah as a director retiring by rotation (Resolution 3) passed with 99.99% approval.

The ratification of Cost Auditors’ remuneration for FY2026-27 (Resolution 4) also passed with 99.99% support.

Key Risks

The concentrated ownership structure presents a governance risk. Promoter and Promoter Group held 62,092,363 shares and voted 62,091,363 shares (99.9984% of their holding) in favour of all resolutions. Their votes represented approximately 84.8% of total votes polled on most resolutions. This dominance means promoter interests can effectively determine outcomes regardless of public shareholder dissent.

The institutional dissent on remuneration resolutions highlights potential misalignment between management compensation and shareholder expectations. While these resolutions passed due to promoter support, the near-unanimous opposition from Public Institutions signals governance concerns that could affect investor confidence.

The need to seek approval for remuneration in the event of inadequacy or absence of profits for FY2026-27 suggests uncertainty about future profitability. This forward-looking contingency planning indicates the company may be anticipating challenging business conditions.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

The AGM materials provide limited direct guidance on business performance. However, certain inferences can be drawn from the resolutions presented.

The approval of remuneration in excess of Section 197 limits for FY2025-26 indicates that actual remuneration paid exceeded what profits would normally support. This suggests either lower-than-expected profitability or higher executive compensation relative to profits during FY2025-26.

The proactive seeking of approval for remuneration in the event of inadequacy or absence of profits for FY2026-27 indicates management’s awareness of potential profitability challenges in the coming year. This could reflect conservative planning or anticipation of continued pressure on margins.

The dividend declaration of ₹1 per share for FY2025-26, while approved, represents a modest payout. Without additional financial data in the source material, the relationship between this dividend and the company’s earnings cannot be assessed.

The company’s cost auditors’ remuneration for FY2026-27 was ratified, indicating ongoing cost audit requirements consistent with its manufacturing operations.

The 34th AGM was conducted entirely through video conferencing and other audio-visual means, with 45 attendees (4 promoters/promoter group representatives and 41 public shareholders). The record date was July 23, 2026, with book closure from July 24 to July 30, 2026.
