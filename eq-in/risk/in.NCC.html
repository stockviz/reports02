NCC Limited: Recent Developments and Outlook

Headwinds and Challenges

Execution across NCC’s project portfolio continues to be shaped by factors outside the company’s direct control. Management points to fund allocation by clients, client approvals, land availability, utility shifting, and billing milestones as the key variables influencing project progress. While NCC’s own execution capability is described as unchanged, the timing of client funding and approvals determines how quickly work converts into revenue in any given quarter or year.

The operating environment is described as broadly stable but still uncertain, which is why management chose to issue formal FY27 guidance rather than commit to a higher growth trajectory despite a strong Q1. The first quarter delivered 12% revenue growth, but the full-year revenue guidance of 8–10% implies a moderation in the remaining quarters.

Specific project-level issues include:

  • BharatNet / OFC pricing: Optical fibre cable prices have risen, and the BharatNet contract is fixed-price. Management states this may not result in a loss but will result in lower profit if current prices persist. NCC has raised the issue with the client and ministry, and notes that cable availability—not just price—is a concern for timely completion of the overall BharatNet programme. The balance order to be executed on BharatNet is ₹6,500 crore.
  • Ken-Betwa land acquisition: Media reports of protests and disruption at the Daudhan Dam site were addressed by management, which stated the reported site is 20–30 km from NCC’s actual site and that local administration is engaging with agitators. NCC has fully mobilised at the site, with ₹116.35 crore executed against a total order value of ₹3,390 crore.
  • Commodity prices: Steel and cement prices are stable, but petroleum-linked commodities, aluminium, copper, and OFC cables have seen increases. About 81% of contracts carry price escalation clauses, providing partial compensation.
  • Receivables and state payments: Payment cycles vary by state and by project within the same state. The Vizag Urban outstanding was ₹271 crore as of June 2026, expected to be fully recovered by December 2026. AP Capital City old project receivables stood at ₹142 crore, expected in the current or early next quarter. Telangana Mission Bhagiratha receivables (~₹180 crore) are sub judice; ₹50 crore has been received, with the balance expected at 15% per month.
  • JJM exposure: Outstanding for UP water projects was ₹1,043 crore as of June 2026; overall JJM unbilled revenue was ₹2,771 crore. NCC collected ₹610 crore in Q1 and a further ₹413 crore in July 2026. Substantial completion of JJM projects is expected in the current year, contingent on continued payment flow.
  • Unbilled revenue: Rose to ₹7,414 crore (38% of annualised revenue) from ₹6,675 crore. About ₹780–800 crore was certified in the first week of July. Management expects this to smoothen over the next two quarters as BSNL/BharatNet billing begins.
  • Debt increase: Standalone debt rose to ₹2,410 crore (net ₹2,008 crore) from ₹2,251 crore at the start of the quarter. Consolidated debt rose to ₹4,020 crore (net ₹3,513 crore). The increase is mainly attributable to smart meter project debt (₹1,461 crore cumulative) and HoldCo capex. Debt-equity stood at 0.31 standalone versus 0.30 in March 2026 and 0.24 a year earlier.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Order book visibility: Consolidated order book stood at ₹81,214 crore as of 30 June 2026 (standalone ₹71,312 crore; subsidiaries ₹9,902 crore), providing book-to-bill of about 3.5x. New orders of ₹3,889 crore were booked in Q1, rising to ₹4,542 crore including July 2026. A further ₹430.19 crore of Buildings Division orders were received in August 2026.
  • Diversification: The order book spans seven business divisions—Buildings (₹22,357 crore, 28%), Transportation (₹16,344 crore, 20%), Electrical T&D (₹13,312 crore, 16%), Mining (₹13,400 crore, 16%), Water & Railways (₹10,994 crore, 14%), and Irrigation (₹4,806 crore, 6%).
  • Government infrastructure momentum: Management cites continued government-led infrastructure investment across Central and State programmes, supported by the Viksit Bharat 2047 vision.
  • Smart meter annuity: The Smart Meter SPVs (two in Maharashtra under RAY and Marathwada, one in Bihar in NCC’s parent name) are expected to complete the capex phase by March 2027, after which O&M revenue begins. Approximately 7–8 million meters are to be installed across the three projects; about 45% are already installed (over 50% in Maharashtra, lower in Bihar). Management targets an IRR of 18% on total capital. O&M revenue is approximately ₹1 crore per lakh meters in operation. No further equity investment is required beyond the ~₹460 crore already invested as of March 2026.
  • Private sector entry: Private clients now constitute about 4% of the order book, with NCC gradually entering private projects.
  • Bid pipeline: Prospective pipeline of about ₹2.5 lakh crore as of Q1 FY27.
  • Client mix: Central government 14%, state government 19%, PSUs and state government entities 60%, ADB/AIIB/banks 5%, private 4%.
  • Ken-Betwa: First river interlinking project in India; NCC has fully mobilised equipment and expects progress to improve as the season starts.

Key Risks

  • Execution delays and cost overruns tied to client funding, approvals, land, and utility shifting.
  • Fixed-price BharatNet contract exposed to OFC price and availability risk.
  • Sub judice Telangana Mission Bhagiratha receivables (~₹180 crore, ~5% of total receivables).
  • Input cost fluctuations, particularly petroleum-linked commodities, aluminium, copper, and OFC.
  • Interest rate movements and the changing contracting landscape (PPP, HAM, annuity modes) affecting future debt profiles.
  • Macroeconomic and market conditions, regulatory and policy developments, talent availability, geopolitical dynamics, and evolving ESG priorities.
  • Working capital intensity: 95 days as of Q1 FY27 (improved from 97 days), with trade receivables at 68 days (down from 73 days).
  • Unbilled revenue at 38% of annualised revenue, though expected to smoothen.

Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

FY27 guidance: Order inflows of ₹22,000–25,000 crore; revenue growth of 8–10%; EBITDA margin of 8.5–9%.

Q1 FY27 observed performance: - Consolidated revenue ₹5,842 crore, up 12% Y-o-Y (standalone ₹4,952 crore, up 12%)—the highest Q1 turnover in NCC’s history. - Consolidated EBITDA margin 9.38% (standalone 9.01%), above the full-year guidance band. - Consolidated PAT ₹216.40 crore (standalone ₹187.31 crore); consolidated PAT including the ANN.pdf figure of ₹229 crore. - Order inflow ₹3,889 crore in Q1, plus July 2026 orders taking the total to ₹4,542 crore—running below the annualised pace implied by the ₹22,000–25,000 crore guidance. - EPS ₹2.98 standalone versus ₹3.03 in Q1 FY26. - Capex ₹170 crore against a full-year budget of ₹500 crore.

Management acknowledged that Q1 growth of 12% exceeds the full-year guidance of 8–10%, attributing the conservative guidance to uncertainty around fund allocation and the pace at which client-side factors will play out. On debt, management indicated standalone debt by March 2027 would be “more or less the same” as March 2026 levels, contingent on JJM collections. Depreciation is expected to rise from Q3 FY27 as TBM operations begin. Interest cost is expected to remain at a similar percentage of revenue, with possible relief if rate corrections materialise.

Broker Narrative

The initial narrative centered on a strong ₹50,244 crore order book, margin expansion from easing raw materials, and deleveraging, but by the last report the focus shifted to execution challenges involving multiple authorities, utility shifting, and design-change delays, with new concerns about commodity, freight, and insurance cost pressures from West Asia disruptions. Government infra tailwinds persisted but were reframed around power-sector demand, urban infrastructure, private capital, and overseas projects with better margins rather than domestic capex-driven margin expansion.

Fears that came true

  • Project delays materialized as the last report explicitly cites execution challenges involving multiple authorities, utility shifting, clearances, and frequent design changes resulting in delays and working-capital build-up, aligning with deeply negative actual returns across nearly all reports.
  • Raw material volatility evolved into confirmed margin pressure, with the last report flagging potential near-term FY27 pressure on margins from higher commodity, freight, and insurance costs due to West Asia-related disruptions.
  • Competitive bidding risk contributed to widespread DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes, as even buy-rated calls with predicted returns of +15% to +39% produced actual returns between -1% and -54%.

Optimism that failed

  • Margin expansion of 50 bps from easing raw material costs failed entirely; instead, the last report warns of margin pressure from higher commodity, freight, and insurance costs, and actual returns were negative in nearly every report.
  • The ₹50,244 crore order book and projected ~18% revenue CAGR did not translate into positive stock performance, with 25+ out of 27 reports showing negative actual returns and DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes.
  • Deleveraging and balance sheet improvement (gross debt reduced from ₹1,184 crore to ₹980 crore) did not drive returns, as the stock delivered deeply negative actual returns across virtually all broker calls regardless of rating.
