JPPOWER: Recent Developments, Risks and Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Regulatory and tariff disputes (UPPCL). Uttar Pradesh Power Corporation Ltd. has raised recovery claims against the Vishnuprayag Hydro Electric Plant for amounts it assesses as excess paid on account of income tax and secondary energy charges for FY 2007-08 to 2019-20 and FY 2014-15 to 2019-20. As of 30 June 2026 the amount stood at Rs. 47,316 lakhs (Rs. 47,148 lakhs as on 31 March 2026), with Rs. 40,347 lakhs held back (Rs. 39,183 lakhs as on 31 March 2026). UPERC disallowed the Company’s claims by order dated 12 June 2020; the Company has appealed to APTEL. UPPCL and the Company agreed that recovery be made from monthly power sale invoices over seven years from FY 2021-22 to FY 2027-28, with carrying cost at SBI TMCLR plus 350 basis points. No provision has been considered necessary pending the appeal.

Sand mining demands and litigation. The Company received show cause/demand notices totalling Rs. 8,57,342 lakhs (including amounts estimated from show cause notices of Rs. 509,455 lakhs) up to 30 June 2026, from district offices of the Director Mines & Geology, Andhra Pradesh, alleging illegal extraction, storage, transportation and sale of sand. Demand notices issued between 28 August 2024 and 30 June 2026 aggregate Rs. 347,887 lakhs (Rs. 221,654 lakhs as on 31 March 2026). The Company has challenged these before the Andhra Pradesh High Court, which has stayed the demand notices; GST authorities have also issued summons and notices. DMG has filed FIRs against the Company and its officials. The Company’s contracts were sub-contracted on a back-to-back basis, DMG issued a “No due certificate” and released the sub-contractor’s bank guarantees. The sub-contractor balance remains subject to confirmation and reconciliation as on 30 June 2026.

Corporate guarantee exposure to Jaiprakash Associates Limited (JAL). JAL was admitted into CIRP by NCLT Allahabad by order dated 3 June 2024; Adani Enterprises Limited was accepted as the successful resolution applicant by order dated 17 March 2026. The Company had given a corporate guarantee to SBI of USD 1,500 lakhs (equivalent to Rs. 123,915 lakhs at Rs. 82.61 per USD as on 3 June 2024) against loans by SBI to JAL. SBI assigned its fund-based outstanding dues of JAL (including the claim against the guarantee) to NARCL, and DRT Delhi permitted substitution of NARCL for SBI by order dated 25 September 2025. The Company filed a claim of USD 1,500 lakhs with the RP of JAL, taken on record to the extent of Rs. 51,173 lakhs on a provisional basis. The Company relies on Clause 6.12 of the Framework Agreement dated 18 April 2019, under which lenders expressly agreed to release and return the corporate guarantee. During the quarter ended 30 June 2026, NARCL Trust filed an application before NCLT Allahabad under Section 7 of the IBC against the Company alleging default on the guarantee; the matter is pending adjudication. The auditors note non-provision against the guarantee and non-compliance with Ind AS 113 fair valuation of the guarantee.

Recompense claim by lenders. Under the 2019-20 Framework Agreement, lenders have a right of recompense subject to availability of free cash flow and other conditions. ICICI Bank, as lead banker, claimed Rs. 5,69,651 lakhs by demand letter dated 9 January 2026. The Company has challenged the amount and asked ICICI Bank to explain its basis; management believes nothing is payable as on 30 June 2026 given the present free cash flow situation and RBI guidelines, and that any part payment/cost would be recoverable under PPAs. The auditors note non-provisioning against this claim.

Coal mining viability. During the quarter the Company initiated steps to surrender the Amelia (North) and Bandha North coal mines due to sustained financial and operational unviability, subject to lender approval, with a request submitted to the Nominated Authority, Ministry of Coal, on 20 May 2026 under Clause 24.3.1(i) of the CMDPA. A provision of Rs. 193.63 crores against expenditure incurred (including intangibles) has been made as an exceptional item. Regulatory evaluation and compensation assessment are at initial stages.

Tax transition effects. The Company opted for the new tax regime under Section 115BAA effective FY 2025-26. MAT credit entitlement of Rs. 140.78 crores recognised in Q4 FY 2025-26 was reversed, and deferred tax liability of Rs. 248.87 crores was reversed due to remeasurement at new rates (net impact Rs. 108.09 crores). Tax provision for the current quarter is therefore not comparable with previous periods.

Subsidiary going-concern uncertainty. Sangam Power Generation Company Limited has accumulated losses eroding more than 50% of its net worth as on 30 June 2026, and its claim against UPPCL is pending before the Supreme Court. The holding company’s investment of Rs. 55,212 lakhs carries an impairment provision of Rs. 33,025 lakhs.

Seasonality at VHEP. Water availability at Vishnuprayag is normally higher in the first half; based on past data, the first two quarters contribute 75-80% of annual generation, with 25-20% in the last two quarters.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Revenue growth. Standalone revenue from operations rose to Rs. 1,77,570 lakhs in Q1 FY 2026-27 from Rs. 1,58,316 lakhs in Q1 FY 2025-26 and Rs. 1,38,643 lakhs in Q4 FY 2025-26. Consolidated revenue from operations was also Rs. 1,77,570 lakhs for the quarter, with consolidated total income at Rs. 1,80,649 lakhs.

Power segment performance. Consolidated power segment revenue was Rs. 1,77,570 lakhs for Q1 FY 2026-27, and the power segment result before finance charges, depreciation, exceptional items and tax was Rs. 78,607 lakhs, up from Rs. 64,376 lakhs in Q1 FY 2025-26 and Rs. 21,294 lakhs in Q4 FY 2025-26.

Coal segment contribution. The coal segment contributed revenue of Rs. 23,973 lakhs and a segment result of Rs. 694 lakhs in Q1 FY 2026-27.

Profitability. Standalone profit before exceptional items and tax was Rs. 57,049 lakhs; after an exceptional item of Rs. 19,363 lakhs, profit before tax was Rs. 37,686 lakhs and net profit after tax was Rs. 46,895 lakhs. Consolidated net profit after tax was Rs. 46,884 lakhs, compared with Rs. 27,813 lakhs in Q1 FY 2025-26. Basic and diluted EPS on a consolidated basis was Rs. 0.52 for the quarter.

Capital employed. Consolidated capital employed rose to Rs. 16,23,459 lakhs as on 30 June 2026 from Rs. 15,76,178 lakhs as on 31 March 2026, with power segment assets at Rs. 16,84,180 lakhs.

Resolution progress at JAL. The acceptance of Adani Enterprises Limited’s resolution plan for JAL by NCLT order dated 17 March 2026 is a development in the erstwhile promoter company’s insolvency process, relevant to the guarantee exposure.

Tariff framework. Revenue for JBTPP and JNSTPP is accounted on the basis of the Multi Year Tariff control period 2024-25 to 2028-29, subject to true up/final assessment; VHEP revenue is on provisional tariff subject to true up.

3. Key Risks

  • Contingent liability crystallisation: The corporate guarantee dispute (NARCL’s Section 7 application before NCLT Allahabad), the ICICI Bank recompense claim of Rs. 5,69,651 lakhs, the UPPCL recovery of Rs. 47,316 lakhs, and the DMG sand mining demands of Rs. 8,57,342 lakhs all remain unresolved, with no provisions made in the financial results.
  • Auditor qualifications: The limited review reports on both standalone and consolidated results are qualified in respect of non-provision against the corporate guarantee and the recompense claim, and note non-compliance with Ind AS 113 fair valuation of the corporate guarantee. The same matters were qualified in the audit report for the year ended 31 March 2026, and the review report for the quarter ended 30 June 2025 was modified in respect of the guarantee matter.
  • Going-concern uncertainty at SPGCL: Accumulated losses have eroded more than 50% of SPGCL’s net worth, and its claim against UPPCL is pending before the Supreme Court.
  • Coal mine surrender: Compensation receivable and final settlement for the Amelia (North) and Bandha North mines are at initial stages and subject to government and lender approval.
  • Mining taxation uncertainty: Following the Supreme Court’s 25 July 2024 judgment and 14 August 2024 order on state taxation of mineral rights, the impact on the Company is currently unascertainable.
  • Sub-contractor account reconciliation: Balances with the sand mining sub-contractor are unconfirmed and unreconciled as on 30 June 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Recompense claim. Management believes nothing is payable as on 30 June 2026 based on the present free cash flow situation and RBI guidelines, and that any part payment or cost would be recovered under PPAs. The auditors record that the impact cannot be ascertained at this stage and that no provision has been made.

Corporate guarantee. Management states the impact is unascertainable pending claims and its application to SBI for release of the guarantee under the Framework Agreement. The auditors note the guarantee has not been fair valued under Ind AS 113.

UPPCL dues. Management considers the Rs. 40,347 lakhs deducted/retained by UPPCL as recoverable and good, consistent with PPA terms, and has made no provision. The auditors note the amount is shown as recoverable and considered good by management.

Sand mining demands. Management, supported by legal advice and a Board note that the demands are without cogent basis, believes no provision is needed and there will be no impact on the state of affairs on final decision. The Andhra Pradesh High Court has stayed the demand notices.

Coal mine surrender. Management expects no material impact on final settlement on the state of affairs and results; compensation, if any, will be accounted for when settled or received. A provision of Rs. 193.63 crores has already been recognised as an exceptional item in the current quarter.

Observed performance against prior periods. Revenue and power segment results improved year-on-year and sequentially. However, the quarter’s net profit was affected by the one-time exceptional item of Rs. 193.63 crores and by the tax transition adjustments (MAT credit reversal of Rs. 140.78 crores and deferred tax liability reversal of Rs. 248.87 crores, net impact Rs. 108.09 crores), which management states makes the tax provision not comparable with previous periods. The Q4 FY 2025-26 standalone result was a loss before exceptional items and tax of Rs. 806 lakhs and a net loss after tax of Rs. 2,335 lakhs, while Q1 FY 2026-27 returned to profit.
