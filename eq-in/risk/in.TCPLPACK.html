TCPL Packaging — Q1 FY27 Announcements: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks and Guidance

1. Headwinds and challenges

  • Global uncertainty weighing on exports. Management described export growth as steady year-on-year but remained cautious on the near-term outlook “given the continuing uncertainty in the global operating environment.” Exports had been weak in the prior year, and the Q1 improvement was partly described as a normalization from a particularly poor comparable quarter.
  • Raw material price pass-through lag. Price increases initiated on inputs take “over a quarter” to pass through to customers because of existing stocks and stocks on order. Management noted the EBITDA margin could not have been maintained if the increases had not been passed on.
  • Flexible packaging is structurally lower-margin. Management stated that flexible packaging is generally a lower-margin business than cartons, so as its share grows it can dilute company-level EBITDA margins, even though return on capital profiles are similar.
  • Slow adoption of mono-material / recyclable packaging. The Innofilms single-layer recyclable line, merged into the parent, faced early machine issues that were resolved, but the bigger concern is that brand owners have not adopted mono-material packaging at the pace expected. Post-COVID pressure on growth and margins caused sustainability targets to be extended, deferred or forgotten. India does not currently mandate recyclable packaging, reducing pressure on brand owners.
  • Plastic ban newsflow. A recent notification concerning pan masala/gutka packaging was described as misleading and related to a structural change in that packaging rather than a new plastics ban. TCPL is not a major supplier to the pan masala segment, so any impact on core business is marginal and short term.
  • Capacity constraints in flexible packaging. The existing flexible facility is operating at almost full utilization, with utilization varying month to month, which triggered the new line addition.
  • Folding carton utilization is uneven by plant. Overall utilization is around 70%-plus, but some plants are choked while others have headroom, requiring area expansion in a couple of factories to make room for next year’s capex.
  • No formal policy framework yet for battery materials PLI. Management declined to speculate on PLI eligibility for the new subsidiary because the government has not formalized a scheme or opened applications.

2. Tailwinds and growth prospects

  • Record quarterly performance. Consolidated total income rose 16% year-on-year to INR 495 crore, EBITDA grew 17% to INR 88 crore with margins at 18%, cash profit rose 56% to INR 76 crore, and PAT grew nearly 79% to INR 40 crore.
  • Broad-based demand. Growth was driven by strong domestic demand, with both Folding Cartons and Flexible Packaging performing well. Domestic growth was at a good double-digit clip, higher than export growth; volumes grew at a high single-digit rate, with a mix tilted slightly more toward value than volume.
  • Flexible packaging expansion. A high-speed manufacturing line is being added, increasing capacity by about 30% at a capex of INR 50-60 crore, expected operational by January or February next year. It will serve similar customers and sectors while targeting new clients.
  • Folding carton expansion readiness. Space is being developed in a couple of factories for next year’s capex, and in some plants capacity can be added within a quarter to 1.5 quarters’ notice, as in Chennai.
  • Chennai ramp-up. Management expressed satisfaction with the Chennai plant ramp-up, with utilization “getting there” toward the 70%-odd level, and noted that two to three lines could be added quickly given available land and readiness.
  • Structural packaging tailwinds. Consumption-led growth, premiumization, rising demand for sustainable packaging and continued outsourcing by branded customers were cited as favorable structural drivers.
  • Export sentiment and FTAs. Customers in Europe and the UK are positive on sourcing from India. FTAs do not materially help cartons, which already moved at zero duty, but flexibles benefit from a slight duty reduction, improving competitiveness versus Vietnam or Turkey. Management sees good scope for further export expansion.
  • Mono-material positioning. The recyclable packaging line is performing well and justifying the investment, serving as a marketing differentiator. High-value fully recyclable packaging is being sold as exports, building a base abroad ahead of eventual Indian regulation.
  • New line of business: lithium-ion battery separator films. The Board approved entry into manufacturing lithium-ion battery separator films through a proposed subsidiary, a critical component of Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC) batteries used in electric vehicles, grid-scale energy storage and other applications. The business builds on TCPL’s capabilities in specialized films, polymer processing, precision manufacturing, R&D, process control and quality assurance.
  • Project scale and phasing. Proposed investment of approximately INR 125 crore over the next 18 months, funded through a mix of internal accruals and debt, with commercial production targeted for Q4 FY28 (January or February 2028). Initial capacity of 70 million square meters per annum, supporting 6-8 GWh of cell production, with a vision to scale to approximately 500 million square meters per annum (~50 GWh equivalent) over 5-7 years.
  • Revenue potential. Phase 1 is expected to generate a topline of INR 150-200 crore with good double-digit margins. At the 500 million square meter scale, management estimated roughly INR 1,100-1,200 crore, potentially INR 1,200-1,300 crore, though prices at that time are unknown.
  • Domestic whitespace. There is currently no lithium-ion battery separator capacity online in India and no announced plans by others, though lead-acid separator makers and other parties are looking at the space. International supply is fragmented and concentrated in China, Korea and Japan, with China the lead player.
  • Policy backdrop. Significant investments underway in India across EVs, energy storage systems and domestic battery manufacturing, supported by policy initiatives aimed at increasing localization across the battery value chain, are expected to drive demand for locally manufactured separator films.
  • Qualification timeline. Separator qualification is understood to be slightly faster than for anode and cathode materials. FY28-29 is expected to be a year of qualification, testing and initial commercial supply, with scale-up dependent on how quickly cell makers set up and scale their factories.

3. Key risks

  • Execution and technology risk in a new field. The separator business requires capabilities beyond packaging. Management expressed confidence based on TCPL’s track record of entering specialized fields (tobacco cartons, folding carton, paper cups, cylinder engraving, flexible packaging, tipping paper, shrink sleeve, ink) and stated the technology is being developed from various sources with in-house R&D, with no external technology transfer. The battery business was described as “very unforgiving.”
  • Customer qualification and demand timing. Commercial supply depends on cell makers’ own timelines. Management expects at least a year to scale up and is prepared to “play the long game.”
  • Phase 2 complexity and capex. Phase 1 involves coating and conversion; moving backward into base film manufacturing entails more capex than coating. Subsequent capacity additions cannot simply be extrapolated by multiplying Phase 1 capex, and will be more than a linear multiple. Base film line lead times are not expected to be as long as two to three years seen in BOPP-type films, but this is not certain.
  • Upfront cost drag. Significant upfront cost is being incurred on land intended to cover 5-6 years of requirements in one shot, which affects near-term asset turn comparisons.
  • Margin mix risk. Growth in lower-margin flexible packaging could reduce company-level EBITDA margins, though carton growth and a healthy overall mix have maintained the margin profile over the last couple of years.
  • Macro and geopolitical exposure. Management flagged that another war or similar disruption could affect demand, while noting Indian domestic demand appears to be recovering.
  • Policy dependency. PLI benefits for battery materials are uncertain pending formalization. Adoption of recyclable packaging in India depends on government mandates.
  • Competitive intensity. The international separator market has many players, primarily in China, Korea and Japan, with China dominant and not concentrated among one or two companies. Chinese cell manufacturing capacity exceeds 1 terawatt, implying enormous separator requirements.
  • Opex build for the new business. Management indicated that even a top-heavy team would not be a significant drag relative to TCPL’s INR 1,500-2,000 crore topline and is already budgeted.

4. Management guidance versus observed performance

  • Growth guidance. Management said the historic trend is expected to continue and that guidance is challenging given factors out of control, but the outlook from where they sit is “quite positive” for domestic and exports, with margins typically following the top line. Observed: Q1 FY27 delivered 16% total income growth, 17% EBITDA growth, 18% EBITDA margin, 56% cash profit growth and nearly 79% PAT growth, described as a record quarter and the first double-digit growth after five or six quarters.
  • Export outlook. Management went into the quarter “a little pessimistic” but the quarter “turned out to be alright,” with steady year-on-year export growth and continued near-term caution. Observed: exports grew, though less than domestic, and the prior-year comparable was particularly weak.
  • Flexible packaging capacity. Management said the existing facility is at optimal utilization and a new line would be added. Observed: utilization is almost fully utilized with month-to-month variation; the new line adds about 30% capacity at INR 50-60 crore, targeted for January or February next year.
  • Folding carton capacity. Management said utilization is around 70%-plus with plant-level variation and that capacity can be added within a quarter to 1.5 quarters’ notice. Observed: Chennai ramp-up is progressing toward the 70%-odd level, with two to three lines addable quickly given available land.
  • Capex guidance. FY27 capex budget is about INR 100 crore excluding the separator project, rising to INR 100-150 crore including separator spend, with this year’s separator outlay mainly land cost and the balance in the next year. FY28 capex is expected to be at least similar or possibly more, depending on how the carton business develops.
  • Separator project guidance. Investment of approximately INR 125 crore over 18 months, commercial production targeted Q4 FY28, Phase 1 capacity of 70 million square meters per annum supporting 6-8 GWh, Phase 1 topline of INR 150-200 crore with good double-digit margins and double-digit return on investment, and a long-term vision of 500 million square meters (~50 GWh) over 5-7 years with revenue potential of roughly INR 1,100-1,300 crore. Management stated the return arithmetic meets the company’s criteria and could be more attractive than existing returns, exceeding the stated 20% ROCE threshold. Observed: the project was approved by the Board on August 11, 2026, with the subsidiary proposed to be incorporated and 100% owned, and no commercial production yet.
  • Mono-material adoption. Management expected faster brand owner adoption when the investment was made; observed adoption has lagged, though the line is now performing well and justifying the investment.
  • Interest cost comparison. Management clarified that the lower interest cost versus the prior-year quarter reflects a one-time mark-to-market forex hit in that earlier period, making the current quarter more normalized.
