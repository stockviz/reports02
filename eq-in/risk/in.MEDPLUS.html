MedPlus Health Services: Recent Developments

Headwinds and Challenges

Margin compression. Q1 FY27 gross margin fell to 24.5%, down 160 bps year-on-year and 200 bps quarter-on-quarter. Operating EBITDA declined 10.6% year-on-year to ₹651 million (3.5% margin), and PAT fell 21.7% year-on-year to ₹331.7 million. Management attributed roughly 100 bps of the gross margin impact to a lower private label mix (pharma and non-pharma combined) and about 70 bps to lower inventory provisions in the sequential comparison.

Private label share contraction. Private label constituted 20% of Q1 FY27 revenue (10.7% pharma, 9.3% non-pharma), a 1.5% decline versus Q1 FY26. Management explained that early aggressive pushing of private label may have deterred some customers, prompting a shift toward serving brand demand; absolute private label pharma sales were roughly flat year-on-year (₹197 crores vs ₹200 crores). Non-pharma private label revenue also dipped sequentially.

Labour cost inflation. Minimum wage changes effective 1 June drove a 60% increase in Karnataka and over 25% in Telangana. Q1 captured only one month of this impact; full-quarter effects lie ahead. The company stopped new retention-bonus enrolments effective 1 March and is reviewing non-statutory incentives.

Franchisee model friction. Of 52 closures in the quarter (including 9 relocations and 27 franchisee closures), closed franchisee stores averaged only 0.7 years of age. Management cited expectation mismatches among first-time entrepreneur franchisees and noted it has adjusted support, including first-year fee assistance and faster breakeven help.

Inventory provisioning on private label. Annual provisioning runs roughly 0.8–1% of total sales, predominantly linked to private label, since made-to-order products carry obsolescence risk on MedPlus’s books (unlike branded products, over 90–95% of which can be returned to manufacturers). Q1 FY27 provisioning was ~₹11–12 crores versus ~₹4.5–5 crores in Q4 FY26, which had benefited from a release.

Regulatory action. On 5 August 2026, subsidiary Optival Health Solutions received three one-day drug license suspension orders for stores in Vijayapura, Sindagi, and Hanuman Talkies Road, Karnataka, under Rule 65 of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act. Potential revenue loss was stated at ₹0.24 lacs, ₹0.17 lacs, and ₹0.23 lacs respectively.

Quick commerce competition. Management acknowledged quick commerce scaling in some cities but stated it would not subsidise delivery, noting most such businesses are cash-burning.

Diagnostics underperformance versus ambition. Diagnostics revenue grew to ₹370.8 million (from ₹302.9 million) with operating EBITDA of ₹65.9 million, but management said the business has not reached desired levels, with limited B2B success and B2C membership stagnating around 210,000–220,000.

Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Store network expansion. 222 gross openings and 146 net additions in Q1 FY27 brought the network to 5,476 stores and 2.9+ million sq ft (versus 4,813 stores and 2.5+ million sq ft a year earlier). Management reiterated guidance of 800 net new stores in FY27, with roughly 50:50 company-owned versus franchisee as an internal estimate; in Q1, 131 of 146 net additions were franchisee stores. 125 net additions were beyond Tier-One.

Mature store economics. Stores older than 12 months generated ₹16,436 million (94% of pharmacy revenue) at a 10.4% store-level EBITDA margin and 15.3% revenue growth year-on-year, with 63.0% store-level operating ROCE. Stores older than 24 months had a 10.6% store-level margin. On allocating all non-store costs, these stores produced ₹707 million operating EBITDA (4.3% margin).

Private label recovery plan. Management aims to arrest the private label decline this quarter and rebuild at roughly 0.3–0.5% per quarter, targeting recovery of the 100 bps gross margin impact over a couple of quarters, starting with non-pharma.

Membership monetisation. Active plan members rose to ~44–45 lakh from ~39–40 lakh a year earlier, and the plan price was raised from ₹99 to ₹149 about a month before the call, expected to contribute ₹10–11 crores.

Discount structure tweak. Effective 7 July, the 20% discount on purchases above ₹1,000 was reduced to 19% for a large number of customers (non-UPI payment modes), with early traction noted.

Diagnostics growth. Diagnostics operating EBITDA rose to ₹65.9 million from ₹41.3 million year-on-year, with plan sales per day rising from 552 in April to 557 in May and 644 in June.

Geographic expansion. The company operates in 13 states and 1 union territory across 850+ cities, with recent entry into Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, and Kerala, and a stated plan to grow contiguously before moving further north and west.

Franchisee-led long-term scaling. Management views franchising as the route to a 20,000–40,000 store opportunity, with higher return on investment despite lower margins.

Balance sheet strength. Closing cash and bank balance was ₹6,218.8 million; operating cash flow was ₹1,252 million (192.2% of operating EBITDA); net working capital was 54 days. Management said it is evaluating dividend or buyback options for Board consideration.

Subsidiary consolidation. MedPlus is acquiring the residual 0.01% stake (17,986 equity shares of ₹10 face value) in Optival Health Solutions for ₹1.10 crore, taking its holding from 99.99% to 100% and making Optival a wholly owned subsidiary, subject to documentation and dematerialisation/transmission formalities.

Governance. All six resolutions at the 20th Annual General Meeting held on 17 August 2026 were approved with the requisite majority.

Key Risks

  • Sustained margin pressure from private label mix, franchisee dilution (management noted ~50 bps gross margin impact), and permanent labour cost increases, particularly with only one month of the wage hike captured in Q1.
  • Franchisee attrition, given closures concentrated among young franchisee stores and expectation mismatches.
  • Private label inventory obsolescence, with provisioning guided up to ~5% as a considered ceiling and 0.8–1% of sales currently.
  • Regulatory/licensing actions against subsidiary stores, as illustrated by the three Karnataka suspensions.
  • Competitive intensity from quick commerce and continued discounting across offline and online pharmacy.
  • Execution risk in new categories, evidenced by the diagnostics business not meeting internal ambitions.
  • Promoter leverage: promoter debt was stated at approximately ₹1,150 crores including interest, with no current timeline for reduction; decisions rest with the family office.
  • Capex strategy uncertainty: the Board-approved capex for a food park, oil extraction unit, and wellness services facility was put on hold after negative market and investor reaction, with no updated plan on utilisation of balance sheet funds.

Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

  • Store additions: Guidance of 800 net new stores for FY27 was reiterated, with Q1 delivering 146 net additions (versus 218 in the prior quarter). Management maintained the target despite the slower sequential pace.
  • Annual operating plan: Management said the ~₹400-odd crores consolidated operating EBITDA plan remains unchanged for now, citing efforts to restore private label and cost actions, while noting the plan is a guide rather than a binding commitment and could be exceeded.
  • Private label: Management aspires to recover the 100 bps gross margin impact over a couple of quarters with 0.25–0.3% quarterly growth in overall private label share, after Q1 showed a 1.5% year-on-year decline.
  • Full-year outlook: Management deferred firmer full-year guidance to Q2 FY27, asking for one quarter to assess outcomes of corrective actions before projecting.
  • Diagnostics: Described as profitable but below the level management wanted, with B2B and B2C subscription traction lagging.
  • Capital returns: Management said it is evaluating dividend/buyback options and would inform the market next quarter if there is an outcome.
  • Optival stake acquisition: Expected to complete at the earliest, subject to documentation and dematerialisation/transmission formalities.

Broker Narrative

The first report was a detailed, optimistic Buy: it acknowledged private-label/margin and mature-store risks but leaned on FY25 store additions, EBITDA margin expansion, and private-label traction. Over the seven reports, Buy/Add calls repeatedly produced negative actual returns, and by the last report the broker had narrowed the headwind to ‘weaker execution impacting revenue and EBITDA’ while still retaining a Buy. The early pharmacy/retail growth drivers were effectively replaced in the last report by unrelated-looking tailwinds around T&D order books, high-voltage switchgear capex, and exports, marking a clear narrative shift away from MedPlus’s core business.

Fears that came true

  • The early warning of operating-margin pressure from private-label mix, discounts, and advertising spend materialized, as the last report explicitly cited weaker execution impacting revenue and EBITDA and the final Buy produced a -24.2% actual return.
  • The Q4FY24 mature-store slowdown/RoCE decline was not overcome: subsequent calls kept disappointing, including -31.6% after the May 2025 Buy, before the broker finally admitted weaker execution.

Optimism that failed

  • The expectation of gradual operating margin improvement in FY25 failed to materialize, with the last report instead flagging weaker execution impacting revenue and EBITDA.
  • The optimism that private-label traction and mature-store margins would support growth did not translate into shareholder returns, as the stock delivered negative actual returns on every 2025 Buy/Add call.
