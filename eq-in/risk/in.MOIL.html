MOIL: Recent Developments and Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Regulatory and tax demands. MOIL received a short recovery letter from the Executive Engineer, Wainganga Division Balaghat on 29 August 2026 demanding ₹16,08,16,712 towards water tax for the period April 2008 to September 2025, including 24% interest and 1% service charges, based on a CAG audit para. The company states the demand has no immediate operational impact and is examining the letter to take appropriate legal steps, including filing an appeal within the prescribed time.

Environmental clearance penalty at Tirodi Mine. A penalty was imposed by the Collector, Balaghat amounting to ₹1,677.09 lakh, plus an additional demand of ₹54.54 lakh towards economic benefits, for environmental clearance capacity expansion violations at Tirodi Mine (order dated 30 January 2025). The company disclosed the entire ₹1,731.63 lakh as a contingent liability. A Supreme Court interim stay was recalled on 18 November 2025. The statutory auditor’s review report notes that, upon recall of the stay, the obligation stands reinstated; considering the company’s suo-moto acceptance of the violation and its assessment of the minimum payable amount at ₹465.06 lakh, the auditor considers a provision of ₹519.60 lakh (including ₹54.54 lakh towards economic benefits) should be recognised, with the balance ₹1,212.03 lakh disclosed as a contingent liability.

Plant shutdowns. The Electrolytic Manganese Dioxide (EMD) Plant and Ferro Manganese (FMP) Plant remained under major repairs during the quarter ended 30 June 2026. Management represented that the shutdown is temporary, undertaken for technology enhancement, process improvement and inventory optimisation, with production expected to resume upon completion of the proposed activities.

Labour Codes. The company has not carried out an assessment of potential financial and operational impact arising from the new Labour Codes effective from 21 November 2025, and no provision or liability has been recognised. No disclosures have been made regarding possible impact, implementation status, or expected implications on operations, employee benefits and related costs. Management’s opinion is that the impact will not be material.

Accounting classification matters noted by the auditor. Revenue recognition includes amounts collected towards Royalty, District Mineral Fund (DMF) and National Mineral Exploration Trust (NMET) on behalf of third parties, done as per industry practice and based on expert opinion obtained. Capital expenditure of ₹115.76 lakh towards exploration for the proposed Joint Venture with Chhattisgarh Mineral Development Corporation (CMDC) was recognised as Work-in-Progress – Exploration (CG) and, in the auditor’s view, should have been classified under Other Non-Current Assets. Capital expenditure of ₹765.28 lakh (MOIL’s share) towards exploration for the proposed Joint Venture with Gujarat Mineral Development Corporation (GMDC), pending incorporation of the JV entity, was recognised as Investment and, in the auditor’s view, should have been classified under Other Non-Current Assets.

Investor complaints. For the quarter ended 30 June 2026: opening 1, received during the quarter 2, resolved during the quarter 2, pending as on 30 June 2026 – 1.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Q1 FY27 performance. For the quarter ended 30 June 2026, revenue from operations rose 7% to ₹370.88 crore from ₹348.06 crore; total revenue rose 6% to ₹391.13 crore from ₹370.52 crore; PBT rose 75% to ₹111.62 crore from ₹63.82 crore; PAT rose 70% to ₹87.62 crore from ₹51.51 crore. Manganese ore production was 507,605 MT, up 1% from 502,260 MT; manganese ore sales were 369,049 MT, up 4% from 356,196 MT.

Segment performance. Mining products segment revenue was ₹36,007.96 lakh for Q1 FY27 versus ₹32,153.41 lakh in Q1 FY26, with segment results of ₹8,718.37 lakh versus ₹3,201.68 lakh. Manufactured products revenue was ₹846.01 lakh versus ₹2,927.12 lakh, with segment results of ₹12.25 lakh versus ₹567.04 lakh. Power revenue was ₹663.20 lakh versus ₹616.52 lakh, with segment results of ₹406.71 lakh versus ₹367.23 lakh.

Joint ventures and exploration. MOIL, along with Madhya Pradesh State Mining Corporation Limited (MPSMCL), incorporated a Joint Venture Company named MOIL MPSMCL Mining Limited on 4 June 2026; the JVC is yet to commence business as on 30 June 2026, and the initial subscription towards share capital was made on 27 July 2026, after the quarter end. A Memorandum of Understanding has been signed with Chhattisgarh Mineral Development Corporation (CMDC) to explore options for mining manganese and associated minerals in different districts of Chhattisgarh. A proposed Joint Venture with Gujarat Mineral Development Corporation (GMDC) is pending incorporation.

Management commentary. MOIL management stated that the results reflect the dedication and hard work of the entire workforce, and that operational discipline and commitment have enabled growth across key metrics. Management said MOIL remains focused on improving operational efficiency and meeting the growing requirements of the domestic steel industry.

Investor engagement. The company is attending the Anand Rathi G-200 Summit 2026: Bharat – The Next Engine of Global Growth on Monday, 21 September 2026 in Mumbai for group meet and one-on-one meet pursuant to Regulation 30 of SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015. The schedule may undergo a change due to exigencies on the part of Analysts/Investors/Company.

3. Key Risks

  • The water tax demand of ₹16,08,16,712 (April 2008 to September 2025), which the company is examining and may appeal.
  • The Tirodi Mine environmental clearance penalty matter, where the auditor considers a provision of ₹519.60 lakh should be recognised and ₹1,212.03 lakh disclosed as a contingent liability, following recall of the Supreme Court stay on 18 November 2025.
  • Continued shutdown of the EMD and FMP plants, with resumption contingent on completion of technology enhancement, process improvement and inventory optimisation activities.
  • Unassessed impact of the new Labour Codes effective 21 November 2025, with no provision, liability or disclosures made; management considers the impact immaterial.
  • Accounting classification matters flagged by the auditor regarding exploration capital expenditure for the CMDC and GMDC joint ventures.
  • Revenue recognition treatment of Royalty, DMF and NMET collections made on behalf of third parties.
  • One investor complaint pending as on 30 June 2026.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements. Management stated that MOIL remains focused on improving operational efficiency and meeting the growing requirements of the domestic steel industry. On the EMD and FMP plant shutdowns, management represented that the shutdown is temporary in nature and has been undertaken for technology enhancement, process improvement and inventory optimisation, with production expected to resume upon completion of the proposed activities. On the water tax demand, the company stated it is examining the demand letter and shall take appropriate legal steps, including filing an appeal within the prescribed time. On the Labour Codes, management’s opinion is that the impact will not be material.

Observed performance. In Q1 FY27, revenue from operations grew 7%, total revenue grew 6%, PBT grew 75%, PAT grew 70%, manganese ore production grew 1% and manganese ore sales grew 4% over Q1 FY26. The mining products segment drove profitability, while the manufactured products segment saw revenue decline to ₹846.01 lakh from ₹2,927.12 lakh and segment results fall to ₹12.25 lakh from ₹567.04 lakh, consistent with the EMD and FMP plants remaining under major repairs during the quarter. The JVC with MPSMCL was incorporated on 4 June 2026 but had not commenced business as on 30 June 2026, and no consolidated financial statements were prepared for the quarter on that basis.

Dividend. The Board of Directors has not recommended a final dividend for the financial year 2025-26, as dividends have been fully paid through two interim dividends.

Annual General Meeting. The 64th AGM will be held on Friday, 18 September 2026 at 12:30 P.M. through Video Conferencing or Other Audio-Visual Means. Remote e-voting is available from 14 September 2026 (9:00 am) to 17 September 2026 (5:00 p.m.), with a cut-off date of 11 September 2026.

Pricing. The company has fixed or revised prices of different grades of Manganese Ore and other products effective from 01.09.2026. Prices of all Ferro grades with Mn-44% and above, all other Ferro grades below Mn-44%, Chemical grades, SMGR grades, and Fines have been continued for September 2026 at the prevailing prices of August 2026. The basic price of EMD at ₹1,80,000/- PMT and EMD flakes at ₹1,71,000/- have also been continued for September 2026.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative shifted from a purely technical Sell thesis in 2020 (flagging bearish chart patterns, metal and auto sector weakness, and Nifty trend-line breaches with zero tailwinds) to a fundamental Buy thesis by 2025 (emphasizing record production, a projected volume ramp to 3.5m tonnes, demand-supply gap bridging, and infrastructure upgrades despite acknowledging severe near-term headwinds). While long-term demand and volume-growth confidence grew, the last Buy call ended in DISAPPOINTMENT (actual -15.3%), and persistent headwinds like a 63% EBITDA decline and price cuts suggest the optimistic thesis remains unproven.

Fears that came true

  • Metal sector inherent weakness flagged in 2020 materialized as recurring DISAPPOINTMENT outcomes with severe negative actual returns (e.g., -48.3%, -37.5%, -33.2%, -27.8%) across nearly half the 18-report history.
  • The 2025 report’s flagged 21.4% y/y volume decline, 29% revenue drop, and 63% EBITDA decline all materialized in Q1 FY26, correlating directly with the DISAPPOINTMENT outcome of the 2025-07-30 Buy call.
  • Manganese-ore price reductions flagged for May-Jun 2025 materialized, pressuring earnings and contributing to the last report’s DISAPPOINTMENT outcome.
  • Persistent bearish technical signals (Nifty trend-line breaches, lower bottoms, sectoral divergence) from the first report were validated by repeated negative actual returns across multiple subsequent reports.
  • The anticipated ~15% wage revision expected from Jul 2027 remains an unresolved cost-pressure risk flagged in the last report.

Optimism that failed

  • Record production of 0.502m tonnes and projected volume ramp to 2.1m tonnes in FY26 failed to prevent DISAPPOINTMENT in the last report (actual -15.3%), as volume growth did not translate into positive returns.
  • Expected global manganese-ore price hikes in Aug-Sep 2025 failed to offset earlier May-Jun price cuts in time, as Q1 FY26 revenue fell 29% y/y and the last report was DISAPPOINTMENT.
  • Optimism about lump-dominated sales (~4.3x lump-to-fine price spread) aiding realizations was undermined by the actual 29% y/y revenue decline in Q1 FY26.
  • Demand-growth tailwinds (domestic demand rising to ~18m tonnes by FY30-31, railway connectivity improvements) have yet to translate into sustained positive stock performance, with 8 of 18 reports ending in DISAPPOINTMENT.
