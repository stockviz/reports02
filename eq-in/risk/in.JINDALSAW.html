JINDALSAW — Q1 FY27 Corporate Announcements Analysis

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Middle East conflict and export disruption. The conflict that began in February 2026 has created serious logistical constraints, with a notable decline in export sales in Q1 FY27. Exports constitute approximately 30% of the order book, and a majority of these are to the MENA region. Force Majeure has been invoked. Execution of export orders to the Middle East is on hold. The closure of key maritime routes across the MENA region has caused supply chain disruptions and operational constraints for the Abu Dhabi operations.

Abu Dhabi operations. Jindal Saw Gulf LLC delivered approximately 34,000 MT of corrosion-resistant Ductile Iron pipes in Q1 FY27, compared to approximately 48,000 MT in the previous quarter, with the financial performance of the Abu Dhabi company also affected.

Water pipe business weakness. The water pipe business in India, primarily Ductile, continued to face challenges in FY 26 and Q1 FY27, despite a backlog of order book for over a year.

API license suspension. In January 2026, the American Petroleum Institute temporarily restricted the Company from using the API monogram on seamless pipes after compliance issues were identified during a routine audit. The license was reinstated in June 2026. During the suspension period, the Company allocated its API manufacturing facilities for Non-API products. The suspension affected the joint venture Jindal Hunting Energy Services Limited, which reported revenue of Rs. 50 million and a loss after tax of Rs. 44 million for the quarter ended June 30, 2026.

Jindal ITF litigation. JITF had initially won an arbitration award of ₹1,891 crores plus interest and taxes on January 27, 2019, which NTPC challenged in the Delhi High Court. The Delhi High Court set aside the award on January 30, 2025. JITF filed an appeal in the Division Bench of the Delhi High Court; arguments from both sides are complete and the order has been reserved. The auditors drew attention to this matter in both the standalone and consolidated review reports.

Margin compression. Standalone EBITDA to total income fell to 9.1% in Q1 FY27 from 16.8% in Q1 FY26 and 10.7% in Q4 FY26. Standalone PAT to total income fell to 2.9% from 10.9% in Q1 FY26. Consolidated net profit margin was 2.03% in Q1 FY27 versus 10.13% in Q1 FY26. Standalone PBT declined to ₹145.01 crores in Q1 FY27 from ₹306.98 crores in Q1 FY26. Consolidated PAT declined to ₹90.79 crores from ₹415.47 crores.

Production and sales declines. Standalone Iron & Steel Pipes production fell to ~3,71,000 MT in Q1 FY27 from ~3,98,000 MT in Q4 FY26 and ~3,89,000 MT in Q1 FY26. Pellet production fell to ~2,90,000 MT from ~3,56,000 MT in Q4 FY26. Pellet sales fell to ~2,82,000 MT from ~3,87,000 MT in Q4 FY26.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Order book. The standalone order book for Pipes and Pellets stands at approximately US$1,171 million, comprising ~1.8 million MT of Iron & Steel Pipes (~$1,164 million) and Pellets (~$7 million). Export orders total ~0.75 million MT (~41% of pipe order book quantity) and ~30% of order book value. An export job-work order for 0.62 million MT for the water sector was secured in Q2 FY26. Execution of the balance order book is projected to span the next 9–12 months. The UAE entity has an additional order book of ~US$188 million (~1,77,000 MT), up from US$180 million (~171,000 MT) in the previous quarter, independent of the parent’s standalone order book.

New projects in UAE and KSA. Jindal Seamless Pipe Manufacturing LLC, a wholly-owned step-down subsidiary, is establishing a 300,000 TPA seamless pipe facility in the KEZAD Economic Zone, Abu Dhabi, with ~4 lakh sq. mt. of leased developed land, equity capital injected, advanced negotiations for long-lead items with initial orders placed, and financial closure at an advanced stage; commercial production is scheduled for FY 2028-29. In KSA, Jindal Saw and Buhur Investment Company launched a joint venture (51% step-down subsidiary) for HSAW and LSAW pipe production lines with installed capacity of 300,000 TPA each; land parcels identified, lease arrangements being finalized, long-lead item negotiations advanced with initial orders placed, and initial equity infused; commercial production scheduled for FY 2028-29. A Ductile Iron pipe facility JV agreement in KSA has been signed and other corporate actions are in process. These projects are in development stage and not affected by the ongoing Middle East conflict.

API license reinstatement. The API license was reinstated in June 2026 for all API licenses, authorising the Company to affix the API monogram on its Seamless Pipes.

Credit ratings. In June 2026, CARE Ratings reaffirmed ‘CARE A1+’ for short-term debt facilities including commercial paper and ‘CARE AA (Outlook Stable)’ for long-term debt facilities. In October 2025, Brickwork Ratings reaffirmed ‘BWR AA with Stable Outlook’ for Non-Convertible Debentures of Rs 5,000 million subscribed by LIC in March 2021.

Debt reduction and coverage. Standalone finance costs fell to ₹70.72 crores in Q1 FY27 from ₹131.01 crores in Q1 FY26. Standalone debt-equity ratio improved to 0.20 from 0.29 a year earlier. Standalone debt service coverage ratio rose to 14.59 from 1.72. Consolidated debt-equity improved to 0.29 from 0.42.

Capex for efficiency. The Company is incurring capital expenditures on debottlenecking to enhance operational efficiency and productivity, with incremental gains expected over a period.

Business diversification. The Company describes a diversified business model across locations, markets, products, industries, and customers, with balanced domestic and export markets, and growing revenue contribution from drinking water supply and sanitation projects in India and globally. Oil & Gas exposure accounts for approximately one fourth of total revenue.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued geopolitical instability in the MENA region affecting export operations, order execution, and Abu Dhabi operations, with Force Majeure invoked.
  • Logistical constraints and closure of key maritime routes disrupting supply chains.
  • Litigation risk from the JITF v/s NTPC matter, where the Division Bench order is reserved and the single judge’s setting aside of the ₹1,891.08 crore award is under appeal; the auditors drew attention to the assessment of the expected outcome and its impact on the carrying value of investment in JITF (₹1,689.15 crores) and on JITF’s going concern.
  • Regulatory/compliance risk illustrated by the January 2026 API monogram restriction on seamless pipes.
  • Weakness in the domestic water pipe (Ductile) business despite a backlog of order book for over a year.
  • Margin and profitability pressure, with standalone EBITDA margin at 9.1% in Q1 FY27 versus 16.8% in Q1 FY26.
  • Joint venture losses, including Jindal Hunting Energy Services Limited (loss after tax of Rs. 44 million) and consolidated share of JV losses of ₹5.37 crores in Q1 FY27.
  • Forward-looking statements are subject to risks including general market, macro-economic, governmental and regulatory trends, currency and interest rate movements, competitive pressures, technological developments, changes in financial conditions of third parties, and legislative developments.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Performance

Order book execution timeline. Management projects execution of the balance order book to span the next 9–12 months. Observed performance shows Q1 FY27 standalone Iron & Steel Pipes sales of ~3,62,000 MT (up from ~3,26,000 MT in Q1 FY26 but down from ~3,87,000 MT in Q4 FY26), with export operations impacted by MENA instability and Middle East export order execution on hold.

New project timelines. Commercial production for the UAE seamless pipe facility and the KSA saw pipe facilities is scheduled for FY 2028-29. These projects are in development stage; no production has been observed.

Capex efficiency gains. Management expects incremental efficiency gains over a period from debottlenecking capex. Observed Q1 FY27 margins declined year-on-year and quarter-on-quarter, with standalone EBITDA margin at 9.1%.

API license. The restriction imposed in January 2026 was reinstated in June 2026, consistent with the Company’s stated allocation of API facilities to Non-API products during the suspension period. The JV Jindal Hunting Energy Services Limited attributed its Q1 FY27 loss to the suspension.

JITF outcome. Management believes, based on independent legal counsel advice, that JITF has an extremely strong case leading to an ultimate favourable outcome, that JITF will meet all its liabilities, and that surplus assets will exceed shareholder investments, so no adjustments were made in the standalone and consolidated financial results. The Division Bench has heard the matter and reserved its order; no outcome has been observed.

Middle East conflict. Management states the conflict that began in February 2026 created serious logistical constraints and a notable decline in Q1 FY27 export sales, with Force Majeure invoked. Observed Abu Dhabi volumes fell to ~34,000 MT from ~48,000 MT in the previous quarter, and the UAE entity’s order book nevertheless rose to US$188 million from US$180 million.

Investor meeting. Company officials are scheduled to meet institutional investors physically on Tuesday, 01 September 2026, 10:00 AM IST onwards in Mumbai, with discussions based on publicly available information and no unpublished price sensitive information intended to be discussed.

Broker Narrative

Initially Not Rated, the broker focused on softer Abu Dhabi/GCC deliveries, higher acquisition debt, and only modest FY25 volume/price guidance, while highlighting OCTG, exports, margin-accretive mix, and debt pre-payment. By the February 2025 Buy report, the narrative had shifted to explicit optimism, but steel-price deflation and weak global demand/imports were now acknowledged as the main drags, with margin recovery expected from domestic demand and cost control rather than the earlier export/order-book catalysts.

Fears that came true

  • The early expectation that steel prices would only stabilize proved wrong: lower steel realizations weighed on topline and EBITDA margins, coinciding with a -13.9% actual return in the July-2024-to-January-2025 stretch.
  • The initial overseas-delivery softness/geopolitical caution broadened into weak international market demand and high imports, which the last report cited as exacerbating margin contraction.

Optimism that failed

  • Expected margin expansion from VAP mix, backward integration, and Sathvahana ramp-up failed to materialize, as the last report recorded EBITDA margin contraction of 162bps YoY and 95bps QoQ.
  • The export-growth optimism tied to DI pipes/GCC/MENA/NEOM was undercut by weak international demand and high imports, so that driver did not provide the anticipated support.
  • The coke oven battery expansion’s promised margin sustainability did not hold, as margins contracted sharply despite the expected production-cost benefit.
