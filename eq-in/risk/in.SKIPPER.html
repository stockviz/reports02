SKIPPER — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Weak order intake in the prior year: FY26 ended with lower-than-expected order inflow due to subdued domestic bidding and geopolitical/tariff-related uncertainties in several export markets. This constrained Q1 FY27 execution, particularly in the Engineering segment.
  • Export disruption: Geopolitical developments in West Asia caused delays and temporary holds, with exports declining by approximately 50% YoY to Rs 1,620 million in the quarter. High shipping prices led customers to defer liftings to avoid additional freight costs.
  • Segment softness: The Polymer segment saw muted Q1 revenue due to sharp commodity price fluctuations on both sides, which made trade cautious and led to destocking. The Engineering segment was soft, attributed to last year’s weak order intake, especially exports.
  • Seasonality and local factors: Q1 and Q2 are seasonally the slowest execution quarters due to monsoons; Q1 FY27 was additionally impacted by West Bengal elections and export freight disruptions.
  • Capacity expansion deferment: The ongoing 75,000-ton capacity expansion saw a few months’ deferment, with commissioning now expected by the end of Q2.
  • Talent constraint: Management identified recruiting and retaining quality technical manpower — across manufacturing and projects — as the biggest challenge given the demand spurt.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Record order book and pipeline: Highest-ever unexecuted order book of over INR 9,200 crores, with fresh Q1 inflows of approximately INR 1,674 crores and an all-time-high bidding pipeline of INR 35,000 crores.
  • Domestic bidding revival: Management expects INR 90,000 crores to INR 1 lakh crores of bids in the Indian transmission market in FY27, versus muted TBCB bidding of INR 50,000–60,000 crores last year. Intrastate projects are increasingly moving to the TBCB route, and new developers/foreign capital platforms (e.g., CubeGrid, AnantGrid) are entering the sector. Roughly 40% of the INR 9 lakh crores of earmarked capex has been bid so far.
  • Export momentum: Management expects more than a 50% jump in export order inflow versus last year (about INR 1,100 crores), led by North America and Australia, with traditional markets of Middle East, Africa and Latin America also contributing. Qualification audits were completed with developed-market customers including USA, Finland and Australia; subsidiaries were established in Brazil and UAE, with the USA entity expected to become operational shortly.
  • New orders: Fresh orders of Rs 1,305 crores were secured for domestic and international T&D projects, including transmission towers and monopoles for North America and two 765 kV transmission line projects from a reputed domestic developer in Maharashtra.
  • Sector demand drivers: Accelerating renewable energy integration, HVDC corridors, interstate transmission infrastructure, grid expansion and rising electrification in India and globally; in the US, data centers are reportedly not getting clearance where grids lack sufficient power.
  • Capacity leadership: The 75,000-ton expansion will take total manufacturing capacity to 450,000 tons per annum, strengthening the position as the country’s largest power T&D structure manufacturer.
  • Balance sheet strength: Completion of a INR 433.5 crores preferential equity raise from marquee global and domestic long-only institutional investors (92,23,402 equity shares at INR 470 per share including a securities premium of INR 469 per share, allotted 31 July 2026, following board approval on 3 June 2026 and shareholder approval at the Extra-Ordinary General Meeting on 26 June 2026), and a CRISIL long-term credit rating upgrade to A+ stable in July.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical and tariff uncertainty: Export dispatches and revenue recognition were temporarily impacted; shipping cost volatility is causing customers to defer shipments even where orders exist.
  • Commodity price volatility: Sharp movements in steel and aluminum prices affect trade behavior and destocking; steel saw a temporary spike before normalizing to pre-February levels by May.
  • Execution and order conversion timing: Some projects may enter the order book but have very long execution cycles, so order inflows do not necessarily translate immediately into revenue.
  • Manpower availability: Quality technical manpower constraints could affect the ability to meet manufacturing and site requirements.
  • Dependence on bidding activity: Growth expectations rest on domestic TBCB bidding and export order finalization materializing as anticipated.
  • Legacy contract drag: Low-margin legacy contracts, though now less than 5% of the order book and largely to be executed this year, weighed on margins previously.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Performance

  • Revenue growth: Management reaffirmed approximately 15% FY27 revenue growth despite Q1 growth of about 4.5–5%, citing H1 seasonality, West Bengal elections and export freight disruptions, and expects FY27 to be H2-weighted. Q1 FY27 revenue was a record INR 1,310 crores, up 4.5% YoY.
  • Margins: Q1 FY27 EBITDA rose 10% to INR 140 crores with margin expansion of 60 bps to 10.7%, moving toward the long-term aspirational margin of 12%. Management described the improvement as structural, supported by the near-absence of legacy low-margin contracts and operational leverage.
  • Profitability: PBT grew 27% and PAT increased 26% to INR 56.5 crores. Management guidance includes approximately 30% FY27 PAT growth.
  • Finance costs: Q1 finance costs improved to 3.6% of revenue from 4.2% last year, attributed to better working capital management and operational leverage rather than the fundraise (received in early August). Management guides FY27 finance cost at 3.2%–3.5% of revenue post-fundraise, with the entire proceeds used for debt repayment.
  • Order inflow: Management guides INR 7,000 crores-plus FY27 order inflow (of which about INR 1,100 crores from exports), targeting a closing order book above INR 10,000 crores; Q1 inflows were approximately INR 1,674 crores.
  • Order book execution: Of the INR 9,200 crores order book, approximately INR 5,000 crores is expected to be executed in FY27, with orders typically executable over 2–2.5 years.
  • Segment guidance: Polymer is guided to 20% top-line growth for the full year with some margin improvement, driven by both volume and value, after a muted Q1. Infra is expected to deliver higher growth for the year, with Q1 already showing good growth.
  • Export mix: Management maintains a long-term aspiration of exports reaching 50% of overall order inflow, expecting exports to be about 15% of total inflows this year and 20%–25% next year on a higher order inflow target.
  • Capacity: The 75,000-ton expansion is expected to be commissioned by the end of Q2, with no impact expected on the 15% revenue growth target.
  • Next year: Management indicated that with increasing order inflow and capacity, a higher revenue growth target is possible for next year, as this year’s guidance is constrained by last year’s lower order intake.
  • AGM outcomes: All four agenda items at the AGM held 15 September 2026 were passed with the requisite majority, including adoption of financial statements, a dividend declaration of 10% (Rs. 0.10 per equity share), re-appointment of Director Mr. Siddharth Bansal, and ratification of Cost Auditors’ remuneration for FY2026-27. The record date for e-voting and dividend was 4 September 2026.

Broker Narrative

The narrative stayed consistently bullish, with both reports reiterating Buy and emphasizing strong order book, capacity expansion and export opportunities. The first report’s broad themes were later sharpened into quantified drivers such as NEP T&D capex, the 765kV shift, doubling capacity to 600,000 MTPA, and a 50% export revenue target. Risk coverage expanded from generic working-capital/competition concerns to include fixed-price contract margin exposure, polymer PVC price volatility, and geopolitical/export execution risks.

Fears that came true

  • High working capital requirements were flagged in the first report and remained a stated ‘working capital intensity’ risk in the last report, while the 2024 Buy calls produced negative returns of -30.3% and -13.1%.
  • Intense competition was flagged early and persisted as ‘high competition in tender-based competitive bidding (TBCB) business’ in the last report, accompanying the same disappointing outcomes.
