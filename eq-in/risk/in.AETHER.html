Aether Industries — Recent Corporate Announcements: Summary

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Fire incidents and insurance recovery. An accidental fire occurred on 29 November 2023 at the company’s Manufacturing Facility-I at Sachin, Surat. The company assessed damage to property, plant and equipment (net of accumulated depreciation) at Rs. 299.68 million and wrote off the damaged property during the quarter ended 31 March 2026. During the quarter ended 30 June 2026, the insurance company finalised the claim and paid a balance settlement of Rs. 260.05 million, comprising Rs. 225.05 million for asset loss and Rs. 35.00 million for financial loss/profit (FLOP). Separately, a fire incident occurred on 11 March 2026 at the company’s external warehouse at Hojiwala Industrial Estate, Sachin, Surat; based on a preliminary assessment, the company recognised an inventory loss of Rs. 70.00 million in the statement of profit and loss for the quarter and year ended 31 March 2026, and the insurance claim assessment is in progress, with any recovery to be recognised only upon reasonable certainty of receipt.

Environmental compliance and cost pressures. The BRSR identifies water stewardship and effluent liquidation as a risk: specialty chemical production generates highly concentrated industrial wastewater streams, and stringent local PCB pollution compliance demands zero-liquid-discharge discipline. Mitigation involves MVR-based ZLD systems and an advanced Soil Biotechnology plant, but this results in increased parasitic power consumption and higher monthly utility expenditure. Air emission parameters rose year on year — NOx at 61.10 ppm (2026) versus 42.30 ppm (2025), SOx at 42.70 ppm versus 19.30 ppm, and particulate matter at 119.70 mg/Nm3 versus 42.70 mg/Nm3.

Climate transition and carbon pricing. The BRSR flags climate risk from high dependency on fossil-fuel utilities and expanding carbon pricing frameworks as a transition risk.

Cyber and data privacy exposure. Migration to paperless networks and remote cloud servers is identified as opening vulnerabilities to intellectual property theft and cyber-attacks, with ongoing cash outflows required for firewalls and encryption controls.

Hazardous waste regulation. Chemical synthesis inherently generates by-products subject to tight hazardous waste rules, including mandatory 180-day storage caps for specialised components.

Human capital profile. Attrition remains notable in certain categories: the turnover rate for permanent workers was 20.62% in FY2026 (unchanged from 20.62% in FY2025, down from 45.21% in FY2024). Female representation is limited — 30 of 817 permanent employees (3.67%) and no female workers among 632. Gross wages paid to females were 4.98% of total wages in FY2025-26 versus 5.11% in FY2024-25. Spending on employee well-being measures fell to 0.34% of total revenue in FY2025-26 from 5.39% in FY2024-25.

Governance concentration. Related-party transactions show loans and advances to related parties at 90.99% of total loans and advances (down from 98.94%) and investments in related parties at 100.00% of total investments. Purchases from related parties were 2.97% of total purchases (down from 4.80%). The company has no specified Board committee for sustainability decision-making; executive directors are responsible. No independent external assessment of policy working was carried out.

Sourcing shift. Inputs directly sourced from MSMEs/small producers fell to 1.54% in 2026 from 40% in 2025, while sourcing directly from India rose to 91.28% from 65%.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Dow collaboration on silicones. On 31 July 2026, Aether and Dow Chemical International Pvt. Ltd. announced a collaborative research program to develop new manufacturing technologies for silicones, with all research and pilot-scale development at Aether’s R&D and pilot facilities in Surat. The agreement establishes a framework for a long-term strategic partnership, including potential commercialisation and industrial-scale manufacture. Dr. Aman Desai described Aether as Dow’s exclusive research partner for the program, and the release cites India’s rapidly expanding silicone materials market across construction, mobility and transportation, electronics, healthcare, and personal care.

Converge® polyol platform. The BRSR describes the Converge® platform as binding up to 40% CO2 by weight in polyol manufacturing, with first commercialisation in conjunction with H.B. Fuller and Saudi Aramco Technologies Company. The targeted market is stated as 850 KTA (850,000 MT per year) growing at a CAGR of 5%, within an overall CASE industry polyol market of more than 10,000 KTA.

Plastic upcycling partnership. Aether partnered with Novoloop on a sustainable plastic management project using Life-cycling™ technology to convert post-consumer plastic waste into high-purity monomers for virgin-quality thermoplastic polyurethane, with a pilot plant described as laying groundwork for a circular plastic economy.

Renewable energy scale-up. Solar capacity has been scaled to 31 MW of rooftop solar across operating sites. Total electricity consumption from renewable sources was 92.98 TJ in 2026 versus 111.65 TJ in 2025, and renewable sources represented 19.67% of energy consumed. Total energy consumption fell to 472.74 TJ in 2026 from 993.99 TJ in 2025, with energy intensity per rupee of turnover at 0.50 versus 1.25.

Emissions reduction. Total Scope 1 emissions were 7,696.08 tCO2e in 2026 versus 59,326.91 in 2025; Scope 2 were 17,768.27 tCO2e versus 123,673.45. Scope 1 and 2 emissions per rupee of turnover were 27.08 versus 231.56. Scope 3 emissions were 123.04 tCO2e versus 561.77.

Water and waste efficiency. Water withdrawal fell to 34,111 KL in 2026 from 1,83,505 KL in 2025, with water intensity per crore rupee of turnover at 36.29 versus 232.20. Total waste generated was 4,399.25 MT versus 5,064.23 MT, with waste intensity per crore rupee of turnover at 4.67 versus 6.00. Solvent recovery was 100.00%.

ESG recognition. Aether holds a Silver Medal sustainability rating from EcoVadis, is a member of the UN Global Compact Network India and the Indian Chemical Council, was awarded a ‘CRISIL ESG 51’ rating by CRISIL ESG Ratings & Analytics Limited, and was rated 61 points out of 100 by NSE Sustainability Ratings & Analytics.

Financial scale. Standalone turnover for FY2025-26 was Rs. 9,401.69 million; net worth was Rs. 24,093.15 MM. Exports contributed 17.88% of total turnover (including sales in SEZ and deemed exports), with a presence in 16 states nationally and 20 countries internationally.

3. Key Risks

  • Operational disruption from fire and hazardous operations, with one incident still subject to an in-progress insurance claim assessment (11 March 2026 warehouse fire, Rs. 70.00 million inventory loss recognised).
  • Environmental regulatory risk from wastewater, hazardous waste storage caps, and air emission compliance, with mitigation carrying higher utility costs.
  • Climate transition risk from fossil-fuel utility dependency and carbon pricing.
  • Cyber-security and IP theft risk from cloud migration and paperless operations.
  • Concentration in related-party loans, advances and investments (90.99% and 100.00% respectively).
  • Governance structure: no specified Board committee for sustainability; no independent external assessment of policy working.
  • Workforce risks: worker attrition at 20.62%, low female representation, and a sharp reduction in well-being spending as a percentage of revenue.
  • Value chain assessment gaps: no procedures for sustainable sourcing; no Life Cycle Assessments conducted yet; environmental impact assessments of value chain partners not undertaken; health and safety and working conditions assessments covered only 2.00% of value chain partners.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

Guidance and forward-looking statements. Management states that CRAMS and CEM together now contribute 60% of revenue, with expectations to reach 70% over the next couple of years. Sales grew 27% year on year and 7% quarter on quarter; CEM grew approximately 75% year on year and CRAMS approximately 20% year on year. The R&D centre completed installation of 18 additional fume hoods and an NMR machine. The Dow agreement establishes a framework for potential commercialisation and industrial-scale manufacture of technologies developed through the program. The BRSR states the company is systematically scaling value chain engagement programs to cover top segments by business value, is designing a formalised curriculum-driven sustainability training program, and intends to commence Life Cycle Assessments for some products soon, with a phased deployment roadmap prioritising Converge® polyol technologies and polymer up-cycling pilots in Surat.

Observed performance. Consolidated revenue from operations for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 was Rs. 3,265.57 million, against Rs. 3,051.22 million in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 and Rs. 2,566.29 million in the quarter ended 30 June 2025; consolidated profit for the period was Rs. 627.49 million, versus Rs. 540.07 million and Rs. 470.21 million respectively. Standalone revenue from operations for the quarter ended 30 June 2026 was Rs. 2,565.06 million, against Rs. 2,373.14 million and Rs. 2,148.26 million; standalone profit for the period was Rs. 283.14 million, versus Rs. 379.36 million and Rs. 201.43 million. Standalone revenue for the year ended 31 March 2026 was Rs. 9,401.69 million, with profit for the year of Rs. 1,736.10 million. The wholly owned subsidiary, Aether Speciality Chemicals Limited, reported total revenues of Rs. 700.51 million and total profit after tax of Rs. 149.83 million for the quarter ended 30 June 2026.

Governance and organisational actions. The Board meeting on 31 July 2026 approved re-appointment of Mr. Ashwin Desai as Managing Director and Ms. Purnima Desai, Mr. Rohan Desai and Dr. Aman Desai as Whole-time Directors, each for a further five years effective 1 October 2026, subject to shareholder approval. CA Riddhi Chitaliya was appointed Internal Auditor and M/s. PAAA & Associates Cost Auditor for FY 2026-27. Six employees were designated Senior Managerial Personnel effective 1 August 2026, covering HR, engineering/CAPEX, logistics and import-export, procurement, EHS, and environmental functions. The 14th AGM was convened for 11 September 2026 via video conferencing/OAVM; the voting results and Scrutinizer’s Report show all eight resolutions passed with the requisite majority. A physical meeting with multiple institutional investors was held on 23 September 2026 in Mumbai, with no unpublished price sensitive information intended to be discussed or disclosed.

Broker Narrative

The broker narrative evolved from a cautious Add rooted in macro/cyclical risks (Red Sea disruption, China dumping, European CDMO costs, demand slowdown, destocking) and generic India-shift optimism (China plus one, Japanese innovators, battery chemicals) to a confident Buy focused on company-specific execution: Chinese price competition, high capex/debt for Site 5, validation/qualification dependencies, and a named pipeline of CEM/CRAMS contracts, electronic chemicals, and R&D scaling. The China competition and India-as-sourcing-hub themes persisted, but optimism became more concrete with specific customers (Milliken, Baker Hughes), revenue potentials, and FY30 mix targets. All six calls were successful, and the later report shifted from broad macro tailwinds to quantifiable growth drivers and financial targets.
