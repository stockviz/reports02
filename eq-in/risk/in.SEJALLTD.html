Sejal Glass Limited — Recent Corporate Announcements

1. Headwinds and Challenges

  • Margin pressure in Q1 FY27. Management described the quarter’s margin as held back by several one-off and cost factors: an annual performance appraisal and yearly increments, a newly signed labour union agreement at the Taloja plant (an incremental people-cost impact of around INR 1 crore in the quarter), a roughly 1% increase in cost of goods consumed due to diesel and energy surcharges, and logistics disruption in the UAE in June due to war.
  • Geopolitical situation. Management repeatedly linked its guidance to the geopolitical environment, noting that the UAE situation was “not in control” when earlier guidance was issued and that “something is happening on the geopolitical situations” still. Stabilisation was framed as a precondition for reaching the upper end of growth expectations.
  • Geographical concentration. A participant observed heavy reliance on UAE sales, with profitability largely derived from UAE. Management acknowledged that dependency on the UAE is high and that geographical diversification is needed.
  • Capacity underutilisation in India. Tempering capacity utilisation in Q1 was around 77% at Silvassa, 55% at Taloja, 15% at Erode, and 71% in the UAE. Low utilisation at Taloja and Erode means fixed costs on power and manpower are not fully absorbed.
  • Order conversion timing. In the UAE, order book does not translate immediately into revenue because product sizes must be released by end customers after site readiness and architect design approvals; the product is tailor-made and cannot be pre-built.
  • Working capital and credit discipline. Management cited debtor credit as a constraint on scaling, noting working capital days of around 98 in India and 85 in the UAE, and stated a preference for conservative growth to avoid bad debts (bad debts kept under 0.5% over the last three years).
  • Scaling constraints. Management cited the need to scale skilled labour, production, logistics, teams, human resources, IT infrastructure, automation, and risk alignment as factors limiting the pace of growth.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

  • Strong Q1 FY27 performance. Consolidated revenue of INR 117.95 crores, up 52.88% year-on-year; India revenue up 67.03% to INR 36.43 crores; UAE revenue up 47.31% to INR 81.52 crores. Consolidated EBITDA rose over 44% year-on-year to INR 18 crores, and consolidated PAT grew more than 63% year-on-year to INR 7.22 crores.
  • Order book expansion. The UAE order book increased from approximately AED 50 million to around AED 72 million, reflecting incremental inflow of nearly AED 22–27 million, with execution commencing in June and July and expected to continue over the next two quarters. India secured orders exceeding INR 50 crores from developers including Godrej, L&T, Prestige, and Raheja, with execution planned over the next six months.
  • Significant order wins announced July 22, 2026. The UAE subsidiary secured five architectural glass orders aggregating approximately AED 27.6 million (equivalent to INR 71 crores) across hospitality, residential, and healthcare projects in the UAE. India orders aggregated approximately INR 50 crores across commercial, residential, and institutional projects. These involve insulated glass units, solar-control glazing, and laminated safety glass, with execution expected over 2026–2027.
  • Industry outlook. The architectural glass industry outlook remains encouraging, supported by investment in premium residential development, commercial infrastructure, and energy-efficient buildings.
  • Product mix enhancement. Focus on increasing the contribution of value-added solutions such as insulated glass, laminated glass, and digitally printed glass. In Q1, product-wise sales were 34% IG, 32% lamination, 31% solid glass, and 3% other mix.
  • New verticals. Railway glass is currently under 1% of revenue, with the company participating more aggressively in tenders and approved as a vendor for the Kapurthala, Raibareli, and Chennai railway manufacturing facilities. Fire-rated products are expected to start in Q3. Management targets 10% of revenue from these verticals next year.
  • UAE capacity expansion. A third tempering line and fire-rated technology are in installation, with commercial production expected in Q3. Total capex is around AED 15 million, funded roughly 50-50 by internal accruals and proposed long-term bank debt of around AED 7 million. Total tempering capacity will be around 24 lakh sq mtr per annum.
  • Geographic diversification. The UAE business is starting the Africa market and targeting Europe; India exports to Cyprus and Israel. Exports are 6% of India revenue and domestic 94%.
  • Seasonal support. Management expects Q2 and Q3 to benefit from the festive season (Navratri, Ganesh Utsav, Diwali, Christmas) and smoother construction activity after September.
  • Revenue mix rebalancing. UAE contribution was about 75% last year; management expects 60-40 this year and 50-50 in the coming year. India was 31% last year and is 40% this quarter.
  • Capacity headroom. Management indicated maximum revenue of around INR 600 crores from present capacity, rising by more than INR 75 crores with the UAE third line and the Taloja and Erode units.
  • Tax shield. No income tax is payable in India due to carry-forward losses, with four to five years still available to absorb them; UAE corporate tax is 9%, giving a blended effective tax rate of 8–9%.

3. Key Risks

  • Geopolitical and logistics risk affecting UAE operations, cited as a factor in the June logistics cost hit and in the conservatism of guidance.
  • Geographical concentration risk from heavy dependence on UAE for revenue and profitability, which management is addressing through Africa and Europe expansion and India rebalancing.
  • Execution and timing risk from customer-dependent size releases on tailor-made architectural glass, which delays conversion of order book into revenue.
  • Credit and bad debt risk in a business that extends credit to developers; management noted it avoids poor-quality clients and has kept bad debts under 0.5% over three years.
  • Competitive structure. The architectural glass segment has no listed comparable; Asahi India Glass derives about 90% of revenue from automobiles, and Saint-Gobain focuses on float and coated glass manufacturing. The segment includes many organised, semi-organised, and unorganised players and SMEs.
  • Client concentration. In India, 15 clients contribute more than 70% of business, with around 200 regular clients contributing 30%. Management intends to move toward 50% from the top 15 clients and 50% from 100 or more clients.
  • Debt and funding. India debt is around INR 52 crores, comprising working capital debt of around INR 14 crores and term loan of around INR 38 crores. Cash reserves are minimal as working capital limits are used. The UAE capex is proposed to be funded roughly half by around AED 7 million of long-term bank debt.
  • Capacity ramp risk on new lines. The third UAE tempering line is expected to run at only 15–20% utilisation in its first period after a Q3 start, rather than immediately reaching existing-line levels.

4. Management Guidance Versus Observed Business Performance

  • Revenue growth guidance. Management stated FY27 revenue growth guidance of “minimum 25% and upper side 40%,” with 25% described as the minimum that is “100% going to achieve.” This was revised upward in tone from the earlier “around 25% or more” guidance, with management citing the improved UAE situation and a larger India order book. Observed Q1 FY27 performance showed consolidated revenue growth of 52.88% year-on-year, above both ends of the stated range.
  • Margin guidance. Management guided to EBITDA margin improvement of approximately 1% over upcoming quarters and PAT margin of 9–10% for FY27, with the 9% figure expected in Q3 and Q4 as efficiency improves. Observed Q1 FY27 PAT margin was around 6.5%, below the guided range, which management attributed to appraisal costs, the Taloja labour agreement, higher energy and diesel-linked costs, and UAE logistics disruption.
  • Quarterly progression. Management guided Q2 revenue of INR 140–145 crores, representing 15–20% growth over Q1’s INR 117.95 crores, with margin improvement weighted toward Q3 and Q4.
  • Capacity utilisation targets. Management targets Silvassa at 85–90% by year end, Taloja at 75%, Erode at 25–30%, and UAE at 85% (with the third line at 15–20% utilisation after its Q3 start). Observed Q1 utilisation was 77% Silvassa, 55% Taloja, 15% Erode, and 71% UAE.
  • UAE monthly run-rate. Management confirmed it remains on track with prior guidance of AED 31 million by Q1 and AED 35 million by Q2, and reported July at approximately AED 11.87 million, above the roughly AED 10–10.5 million monthly level seen over the prior four quarters.
  • Glasstech profitability. Management stated Glasstech had not yet been PAT profitable and expected PAT profitability in Q2.
  • FY28 guidance. Management said it is too early to guide FY28 but expects at least 25% year-on-year growth, and over a three-to-four-year horizon expects minimum 25% and maximum 40% growth annually.
  • Order book guidance. Management had previously guided India order book of around INR 50 crores and UAE around AED 70 million, and confirmed these levels, describing the UAE order book as roughly INR 175 crores as on the call date, with execution timelines of 6–9 months.
  • Capex and funding guidance. Management stated there is no fund-raising plan, with capex funded from internal profit and some debt, and confirmed no demerger plans for the real estate business, which operates separately with its own team, capital, and CEO.
