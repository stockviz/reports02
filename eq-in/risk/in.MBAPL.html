MBAPL Q1 FY27: Headwinds, Tailwinds, Risks, and Guidance vs. Performance

1. Headwinds and Challenges

Monsoon delay and sowing disruption. The delayed onset of the Southwest Monsoon temporarily impacted land preparation and sowing across key agricultural regions. By end-June, cumulative rainfall was somewhat below the long-period average, and Kharif sowing stood at around 183 lakh hectares against 236.5 lakh hectares in the corresponding period last year. Since phosphatic fertilizers (DAP, NPK, SSP) are applied mainly during land preparation and early crop establishment, the sowing delay shifted fertilizer application to later in the season.

Raw material cost inflation and supply chain disruption. Geopolitical developments in West Asia, particularly around the Strait of Hormuz, created uncertainty across global fertilizer supply chains. Given India’s dependence on imported raw phosphate, phosphoric acid, sulfur, and ammonia, these disruptions led to higher freight costs, delays in vessel movements, and elevated procurement costs. Ammonia and sulfur prices increased significantly, while phosphoric acid and sulfuric acid remained at elevated levels. Management noted these challenges have existed since January and continue, with rock phosphate prices still at similar levels, though ammonia prices have started coming down.

Industry-wide production constraints. The quarter saw a temporary supply-side constraint that impacted raw material availability and production schedules. Per FAI data, SSP production declined 7% YoY and NPK production declined 28% YoY during April and May 2026.

Low capacity utilization. Q1 FY27 saw SSP utilization around 45% and NPK/DAP utilization around 43%, attributed to raw material availability issues affecting the entire industry.

Flat revenue. Q1 FY27 revenue growth was relatively flat at approximately 1–2%, reflecting quantity challenges during the quarter.

2. Tailwinds and Growth Prospects

Improving monsoon and sowing momentum. The Southwest Monsoon gained momentum during July, with improved rainfall and accelerated Kharif sowing across key agricultural states, expected to drive fertilizer consumption during the peak application period.

Resilient underlying demand. Despite production declines, SSP sales grew 28% YoY and NPK sales increased 33% YoY during April–May 2026, indicating strong underlying demand with production moderation driven by supply-side factors rather than consumption weakness. Management stated there have been no demand constraints for domestic players.

Supportive government policy. The government announced higher MSPs for all 14 Kharif crops for the FY26-27 marketing season, with higher MSPs for soybean, groundnut, cotton, and pulses expected to improve farm profitability and purchasing power. The government also approved nutrient-based subsidy rates for Kharif 2026, providing pricing visibility. Continued focus on fertilizer availability, comfortable inventories, higher natural gas allocation to fertilizer manufacturers, and restoration of gas allocation to urea plants to 100% support supply stability. Management also expressed hope for supportive NBS revisions for the October–March period.

Dhule expansion. The integrated fertilizer complex at Dhule, Maharashtra is central to the growth strategy. The SSP fertilizer plant and integrated sulfuric acid plant were commissioned and stabilized. During the quarter, MBAPL acquired an additional 52,600 square meters of adjoining land, increasing the total land bank to approximately 6.38 lakh square meters. The next phase is scheduled for commissioning by October 2026, adding 3,30,000 MTPA of DAP-NPK capacity and 99,000 MTPA of phosphoric acid capacity. The final phase is targeted for October 2027, adding 3,30,000 MTPA of DAP-NPK, 66,000 MTPA of phosphoric acid, and 3,96,000 MTPA of sulfuric acid capacity.

Market expansion and product portfolio. The Dhule complex is expected to strengthen presence in Western and Southern India, enhancing market access, optimizing logistics, and expanding distribution. New complex fertilizer grades introduced — 15:15:15, 9:24:24, and 16:20:0:13 — complement the existing portfolio.

Sourcing diversification and green ammonia. Rock phosphate is primarily sourced from Egypt and Jordan, which follow a different shipping route, limiting the impact of the West Asia crisis. A Green Ammonia supply agreement has been entered into, with supplies expected to commence from April 1, 2029.

Corporate actions. A 1:5 stock split reduced face value from ₹10 to ₹2, effective 3 July 2026, aimed at enhancing retail investor affordability and broadening participation. The EGM held August 28, 2026 approved the issuance of equity shares to Non-Promoter Category persons on a preferential basis and amendment to the Main Object Clause of the Memorandum of Association, both passing with the requisite majority.

3. Key Risks

  • Continued uncertainty in the geopolitical situation in West Asia and its effect on raw material availability, freight, and procurement costs.
  • Elevated raw material prices, particularly rock phosphate, with ammonia prices having started to decline.
  • Monsoon variability and its impact on sowing and fertilizer application timing.
  • Execution risk on the Dhule expansion phases, including the October 2026 and October 2027 commissioning targets.
  • Funding structure for the planned next expansion (beyond Dhule) remains under discussion and has not been finalized; options include pure equity, debt plus equity, or debt with internal accruals.
  • Dependence on continued government policy support, including NBS rates and MSP levels.

4. Management Guidance vs. Observed Business Performance

Q1 FY27 observed performance. Revenue from operations was ₹416 crore. EBITDA was ₹66 crore, up 16% YoY. PAT was ₹33 crore, up 17% YoY. EPS was ₹0.75 versus ₹0.64 in the prior-year quarter. Fertilizer production was 99,224 MT and sales were 101,583 MT, with sales exceeding production.

Management’s explanation of the margin expansion. Management attributed stronger EBITDA and PAT primarily to lower-cost raw material inventory procured in the March quarter before prices rose; this benefit has since been exhausted. Revenue remained flat due to quantity challenges.

Forward guidance. Management expects production and capacity utilization to improve in Q2 as raw material issues have largely been resolved. Following commissioning of the 330,000 MTPA NPK/DAP expansion at Dhule in October 2026, a significant improvement in performance is expected from Q3 onwards. For the new Dhule NPK/DAP plant, management targets around 60% capacity utilization, while existing facilities are expected to operate at around 90% utilization. Imported fertilizers will continue to be supplied based on market demand. Overall, management expects more than a 50% increase in turnover in the coming months, with revenue expected to witness a quantum jump and EBITDA performance maintained. Management affirmed its earlier FY27 revenue guidance and stated confidence in achieving, and even outperforming, the three-year target of ₹3,500 crore revenue and ₹15.6 lakh MT capacity by FY28. Management declined to provide an exact forecast, stating only that performance may be better than Q1.

Guidance versus observed performance. Q1 FY27 revenue growth was flat at approximately 1–2%, while management maintains full-year revenue growth guidance and expects a quantum jump in revenue over the remaining three quarters. The Q1 margin expansion was driven by a one-time benefit from lower-cost inventory rather than sustained pricing or cost advantages, and management expects to maintain EBITDA performance going forward. The utilization ramp-up from Q1 levels of approximately 43–45% to targeted levels of 60% (new Dhule plant) and 90% (existing facilities) represents the key operational bridge between observed Q1 performance and management’s forward expectations.
